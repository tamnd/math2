## Definition

Consider an optical system that is rotationally symmetric about its axis.
Let $h$ be the normalised height of the object point above the axis.
Let $(\rho, \theta)$ be normalised polar coordinates in the exit pupil, with $\theta$ measured from the direction of $h$.
By the symmetry, the [[wave aberration|VG0GPBKP84]] $W$ is a function of $h^2$, $\rho^2$ and $h \rho \cos\theta$, and it has an expansion
$$W = \sum W_{klm} \, h^k \rho^l \cos^m \theta .$$
The terms of total degree $k + l = 4$ are the *primary* or *Seidel aberrations*:

1. spherical aberration $W_{040} \rho^4$;
2. coma $W_{131} h \rho^3 \cos\theta$;
3. astigmatism $W_{222} h^2 \rho^2 \cos^2\theta$;
4. field curvature $W_{220} h^2 \rho^2$;
5. distortion $W_{311} h^3 \rho \cos\theta$.

## Remarks

The terms of degree $2$ are a change of focus $W_{020}\rho^2$ and a tilt $W_{111} h\rho\cos\theta$.
They only move the ideal image, so they are not counted as aberrations of the image quality.
A spherical mirror imaging a point on its axis has only spherical aberration among the five, since $h = 0$ kills the other four terms.
A system with only a distortion term $W_{311}$ forms sharp images, so distortion is an aberration of position, not of sharpness.
In terms of ray aberrations the Seidel terms are of third order in the aperture and field, so they are also called third-order aberrations.
The coefficients are often written as the Seidel sums $S_{\mathrm{I}}, \dots, S_{\mathrm{V}}$, computed surface by surface from paraxial ray data.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Write the error of the wavefront as a polynomial in the field height and in the position in the lens opening.
Symmetry about the axis allows only a few kinds of terms.
The first five terms that really damage or deform the image are the Seidel aberrations.
