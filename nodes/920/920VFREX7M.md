## Definition

Let $X$ be a [[compact|0TVJZYB6SH]] [[Hausdorff space|0S1FJ8VEX9]].
The isomorphism classes of complex [[vector bundles|P4DZM975BV]] of finite rank on $X$ form a commutative monoid under the direct sum (Whitney sum) $\oplus$.
The group $K^0(X)$, also written $K(X)$, is the [[Grothendieck group|5FDMY7PDYY]] of this monoid.
If $X$ has a base point $x_0$, the *reduced group* $\tilde{K}^0(X)$ is the kernel of the map $K^0(X) \to K^0(\set{x_0}) \cong \Z$ given by restriction to $x_0$.
For $n \ge 1$, $K^{-n}(X) = \tilde{K}^0(S^n \wedge X_+)$, where $X_+$ is $X$ with a disjoint base point added, $S^n$ is the $n$-sphere and $\wedge$ is the [[smash product|2ZJ2G308QV]].
*Topological K-theory* is the sequence of groups $K^{-n}(X)$, $n \ge 0$.

## Remarks

Using real vector bundles instead of complex ones gives real K-theory $KO(X)$.
The tensor product of bundles makes $K^0(X)$ a commutative ring.
By the [[Bott periodicity theorem|WN2DCBSX1C]], $K^{-n-2}(X) \cong K^{-n}(X)$, and this defines $K^n(X)$ for all integers $n$.

Example: for a point, a vector bundle is a finite-dimensional complex vector space, so $K^0(\text{pt}) \cong \Z$ through the dimension.
For the $2$-sphere $S^2$, $K^0(S^2) \cong \Z \oplus \Z$.
It is generated by the trivial line bundle $1$ and the class $H$ of the tautological line bundle on $\mathbb{CP}^1 \cong S^2$, with the relation $(H - 1)^2 = 0$ in the ring $K^0(S^2)$.

Non-example: the monoid of isomorphism classes is not itself $K^0(X)$.
In it, the trivial line bundle has no inverse, because the rank of a direct sum is the sum of the ranks.
Also, two bundles $E$ and $F$ with $E \oplus 1 \cong F \oplus 1$ have the same class in $K^0(X)$ even when $E \not\cong F$.
For real bundles and $KO$, the tangent bundle of $S^2$ and the trivial bundle of rank $2$ are such a pair.
