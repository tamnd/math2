## Summary

The formula $(\forall x \, P(x)) \to \exists y \, Q(y)$ hides its quantifiers inside an implication.
It means the same as $\exists x \, \exists y \, (P(x) \to Q(y))$, where all the quantifiers stand at the front and the rest is plain propositional logic.
A formula of that second shape is in prenex normal form, and in classical logic every formula can be brought into this shape.

Exactly, a [[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] is in prenex normal form when it has the form
$$Q_1 x_1 \, Q_2 x_2 \cdots Q_n x_n \, \psi,$$
where $n \ge 0$, each $Q_i$ is $\forall$ or $\exists$, each $x_i$ is a variable, and $\psi$ has no quantifiers.
The [[formal definition|1NKD8P3PS0]] states this, and the [[prenex normal form theorem|ME9J33BFNC]] says that every formula is [[logically equivalent|0JMR2HM2N2]] to a prenex formula with the same [[free variables|9CNGDRAMGC]].

## Motivation

A formula with quantifiers deep inside connectives, such as $\neg \forall x \, P(x) \lor \exists y \, Q(y)$, is hard to compare with other formulas and hard to classify.
In prenex form the pattern of $\forall$ and $\exists$ stands at the front, where you can read it off at once.
Proofs about all formulas can then treat the quantifier prefix and the propositional part separately.

The pattern of the prefix is a measure of complexity.
"For every $\varepsilon$ there is a $\delta$ such that for every $x$" has three alternating blocks, and each alternation makes a statement harder to check.
This count gives the classes $\Sigma_n$ and $\Pi_n$ of the [[arithmetical hierarchy|7CVJCQKY41]].
Prenex form is also the first step towards [[Skolem normal form|GCPQGMJYEV]], Herbrand's theorem and resolution provers.

## Notation

The string $Q_1 x_1 \cdots Q_n x_n$ is the prefix, and $\psi$ is the matrix.
The prefix type lists the quantifiers alone, such as $\forall \exists$.
For the classes $\Sigma_n$ and $\Pi_n$, a block of equal quantifiers counts once: a prenex formula is $\Sigma_n$ when its prefix has $n$ blocks and starts with $\exists$, and $\Pi_n$ when it has $n$ blocks and starts with $\forall$.
Some books also require the variables $x_1, \dots, x_n$ to be distinct.

## Remarks

### Pulling quantifiers out

The conversion uses a short list of classical equivalences, each with the side condition that $x$ is not free in $\chi$:
$$(Q x \, \varphi) \land \chi \equiv Q x \, (\varphi \land \chi), \qquad (Q x \, \varphi) \lor \chi \equiv Q x \, (\varphi \lor \chi), \qquad \chi \to Q x \, \varphi \equiv Q x \, (\chi \to \varphi).$$
Two rules change the quantifier to its dual:
$$\neg \forall x \, \varphi \equiv \exists x \, \neg \varphi, \qquad (\forall x \, \varphi) \to \chi \equiv \exists x \, (\varphi \to \chi),$$
and the same with $\forall$ and $\exists$ swapped.
The antecedent rule follows from the negation rule, because $\alpha \to \beta$ means $\neg \alpha \lor \beta$.

### Three worked conversions

First, $(\forall x \, P(x)) \to \exists y \, Q(y)$.
The $\forall x$ leaves the antecedent as $\exists x$, and the $\exists y$ leaves the consequent unchanged, so the result is $\exists x \, \exists y \, (P(x) \to Q(y))$; [[the implication example|ZVR8TNBHNH]] checks it in small structures.

Second, $\neg \forall x \, P(x)$ becomes $\exists x \, \neg P(x)$.

Third, continuity of $f$ at every point becomes $\forall a \, \forall \varepsilon \, \exists \delta \, \forall x \, \mu$ with a quantifier-free $\mu$, as worked in [[the prenex form of continuity|BZ1J60WBEB]].
Its prefix type $\forall \forall \exists \forall$ has three blocks, so it is a $\Pi_3$ form.

### What is not prenex

A leading quantifier is not enough.
[[A formula that begins with a quantifier|XZF94M3GFF]], such as $\forall x \, (P(x) \to \exists y \, R(x, y))$, still has $\exists y$ inside the implication.
The formula $\forall x \, P(x) \land \forall y \, Q(y)$ is not prenex either, although every quantifier in it is universal; its prenex form is $\forall x \, \forall y \, (P(x) \land Q(y))$.
A quantifier-free formula, on the other hand, is prenex with an empty prefix.

### First properties

The prenex form is not unique.
$\forall x \, P(x) \land \exists y \, Q(y)$ is equivalent both to $\forall x \, \exists y \, (P(x) \land Q(y))$ and to $\exists y \, \forall x \, (P(x) \land Q(y))$, depending on which quantifier you pull out first.
The negation of a $\Sigma_n$ prenex formula is equivalent to a $\Pi_n$ prenex formula: change each quantifier to its dual and negate the matrix.
A universal sentence, one with only $\forall$ in its prefix, stays true in substructures, and an existential sentence stays true in extensions.
The theorem needs classical logic and nonempty domains, as its [[statement|ME9J33BFNC]] explains.

### Watch out

The most common slip is to move a quantifier out of an antecedent without changing it.
$(\forall x \, P(x)) \to Q(c)$ is not $\forall x \, (P(x) \to Q(c))$: in the domain $\{ 1, 2 \}$ with $P = \{ 1 \}$ and $Q$ false at $c$, the first is true and the second fails at $x = 1$.
The correct form is $\exists x \, (P(x) \to Q(c))$.
The second slip is to ignore the side condition.
In $P(x) \land \exists x \, Q(x)$ the free $x$ of $P(x)$ would be captured, so first rename: $\exists y \, (P(x) \land Q(y))$.
The third slip is to merge quantifiers: $\forall x \, P(x) \lor \forall x \, Q(x)$ is not $\forall x \, (P(x) \lor Q(x))$, as the even and odd numbers show.

### History

Skolem used prenex formulas in his 1920 work on the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem.
Hilbert and Ackermann's textbook of 1928 presented the conversion to prenex form as a standard tool of the predicate calculus.
Later, Kleene and Mostowski classified the formulas of arithmetic by their prenex prefixes, which gave the arithmetical hierarchy.

### Try it

Put $\exists x \, P(x) \to \forall y \, Q(y)$ in prenex form.
The $\exists x$ leaves the antecedent as $\forall x$, and $\forall y$ leaves the consequent unchanged, so one answer is $\forall x \, \forall y \, (P(x) \to Q(y))$.

What is the prefix type of a prenex form of $\neg \exists x \, \forall y \, R(x, y)$?
Two negation rules give $\forall x \, \exists y \, \neg R(x, y)$, so the type is $\forall \exists$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The formula $\forall x \, \exists y \, (x < y \land y < x + 2)$ has all its quantifiers at the start, so it is in prenex form.
The formula "if everything is $P$, then $c$ is $Q$" is not, because "everything" sits inside the "if".
You can rewrite it as "there is an $x$ such that if $x$ is $P$, then $c$ is $Q$".
That means the same thing, and now the quantifier is at the front.
In classical logic you can always pull the quantifiers to the front like this.

### L2 human

We put $\varphi = \forall x \, P(x) \to \forall x \, Q(x)$ in prenex form.
The two bound variables share a name, so we rename the second:
$$\varphi \equiv \forall x \, P(x) \to \forall y \, Q(y).$$
The $\forall x$ stands in the antecedent, and $x$ is not free in $\forall y \, Q(y)$, so it moves out as $\exists x$:
$$\varphi \equiv \exists x \, \big( P(x) \to \forall y \, Q(y) \big).$$
The $\forall y$ stands in the consequent, and $y$ is not free in $P(x)$, so it moves out unchanged:
$$\varphi \equiv \exists x \, \forall y \, \big( P(x) \to Q(y) \big).$$
Moving $\forall y$ out first gives $\forall y \, \exists x \, (P(x) \to Q(y))$, another prenex form of $\varphi$.
The typical use is to read off the complexity: the first form is $\Sigma_2$ and the second $\Pi_2$, so the same formula sits in both classes.
Watch out for the second step, where $\forall$ turns into $\exists$ because $\alpha \to \beta$ means $\neg \alpha \lor \beta$.

### L3 human

The [[prenex normal form theorem|ME9J33BFNC]] reduces every classical formula to a quantifier prefix over a propositional matrix.
The number of alternations in the prefix measures complexity: $\Sigma_n$ and $\Pi_n$ in arithmetic, and the levels of the polynomial hierarchy for quantified Boolean formulas.
Preservation theorems link the shape of the prefix to semantics; the Łoś-Tarski theorem says that a theory preserved under substructures has a universal axiomatization.
In intuitionistic logic the prenex theorem fails, so constructive proof theory measures complexity by other means.
In [[Skolem normal form|GCPQGMJYEV]] the existential quantifiers of the prefix are replaced by new function symbols.
