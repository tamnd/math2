## Summary

The Higgs mechanism is the way gauge fields acquire mass when a gauge symmetry is [[spontaneously broken|F5PJ0Q4RCF]] by a scalar field.
The would-be massless [[Goldstone bosons|H028V9A14M]] disappear: they can be removed by a gauge transformation.
They become the extra polarization of the now massive gauge fields.
In the [[Standard Model|NVDNDPCKTV]] it gives mass to the $W$ and $Z$ bosons, while the photon stays massless.
It was found in 1964 by several groups, including Englert and Brout, Higgs, and Guralnik, Hagen and Kibble.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A gauge symmetry normally forces its force-carrying particles to be massless, like the photon.
The Higgs mechanism adds a field that fills all of space with a nonzero value in its lowest-energy state.
The gauge particles moving through this background are slowed down as if they had mass.
The directions in which the background could slide for free, which would otherwise be massless particles, are eaten by the gauge particles.
What is left over is one or more massive scalar particles, such as the Higgs boson found in 2012.
