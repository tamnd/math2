## Statement

Let $(M, g)$ be a four-dimensional [[Lorentzian manifold|BY4QYYS788]].
Let $E^{ab}$ be a symmetric [[tensor field|WX302T93TW]].
Suppose that, in every coordinate system, its components at each point depend only on $g_{ab}$ and its first and second partial derivatives there.
Suppose that $E^{ab}$ is divergence-free, $\nabla_a E^{ab} = 0$, for every metric $g$.
Then there are constants $\alpha$ and $\lambda$ such that
$$E^{ab} = \alpha \, G^{ab} + \lambda \, g^{ab},$$
where $G^{ab}$ is the [[Einstein tensor|P13KTBC7MH]].

## Remarks

The Euler–Lagrange expression of any diffeomorphism-invariant Lagrangian density of the metric is divergence-free.
So, in four dimensions, every such Lagrangian theory of the metric alone whose field equations are of second order gives the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] with a [[cosmological constant|WVKKDY9V61]].
In this sense the [[Einstein–Hilbert action|JPE3A1SN7V]] is essentially the only choice.
Lovelock proved the result in the early 1970s.
The dimension four is necessary: in dimension $D \ge 5$ further divergence-free tensors exist, such as the one obtained from the Gauss–Bonnet term $R^2 - 4R_{ab}R^{ab} + R_{abcd}R^{abcd}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Lovelock's theorem says that Einstein's equations are, in a precise sense, forced on us.
Suppose gravity in four dimensions is described only by a spacetime metric.
Suppose the equations involve at most second derivatives of it and respect the conservation of energy and momentum.
Then they must be Einstein's equations, possibly with a cosmological constant.

### L2 human

The theorem is the standard map of how to modify general relativity.
Any alternative theory must break one of its hypotheses.
It may add new fields such as a scalar field, allow higher derivatives, or work in more than four dimensions.
It may also give up locality or change the geometry, for example by allowing torsion.
Scalar–tensor theories, $f(R)$ gravity, Lovelock gravity in higher dimensions and massive gravity each break one of these assumptions.
