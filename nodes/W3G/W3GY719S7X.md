## Summary

The Weyl equation is the relativistic wave equation for a massless particle of spin $1/2$.
Its solutions are functions with two complex components, and the equation is $(\partial_t \pm \boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \nabla)\psi = 0$, built from the [[Pauli matrices|XAQCW3D4QQ]].
The two signs give the right-handed and the left-handed equation, which are exchanged by a parity transformation.
The massless [[Dirac equation|XNZX7VXXR2]] splits into one Weyl equation of each kind.
Weyl fermions are the building blocks of the Standard Model before symmetry breaking.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A massless particle moves at the speed of light, and its spin can only point along its direction of motion or against it.
The Weyl equation describes such a particle with a wave function of two components, one for each spin direction.
For each wave, only one of the two spin directions solves a given Weyl equation, so each equation describes particles of a single handedness.
Each component of a solution also solves the ordinary wave equation.
