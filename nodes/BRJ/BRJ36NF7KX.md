## Summary

In optics, the cubic [[nonlinear Schrödinger equation|GEJHRXC176]] describes the slowly varying envelope of a light pulse or beam in a medium with the [[Kerr effect|Y7WQY4568B]].
The distance of propagation plays the role of time, and dispersion or diffraction plays the role of the kinetic term.
For pulses in optical fibres it is one-dimensional and integrable, which explains [[optical solitons|XQBA9P8QRE]].
For beams in bulk media it is two-dimensional and describes [[self-focusing|A68PFZJ3ZW]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A short pulse of light in a fibre is a packet of waves with an envelope, its overall shape.
Two things change the envelope as it travels: different colours move at different speeds, and the bright part shifts its own phase through the Kerr effect.
The nonlinear Schrödinger equation is the simplest equation that keeps both effects.
It is the same equation that appears for water waves and for Bose–Einstein condensates, a sign of a common structure behind them.
