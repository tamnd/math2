## Statement

Let two phases $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of a pure substance meet along a curve $p = p(T)$.
Assume the transition is of second order in the [[Ehrenfest classification|5XVRB6MTK4]].
So the molar [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]] $s$ and the molar volume $v$ are continuous across the curve.
Let $c_p$ be the molar [[heat capacity|SYYGZ18FF5]] at constant pressure.
Let $\alpha_p = \frac{1}{v}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial T}\right)_p$ be the [[thermal expansion coefficient|2NRW3XGP3T]].
Let $\kappa_T = -\frac{1}{v}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial p}\right)_T$ be the isothermal [[compressibility|1BPF2AA6VR]].
These three quantities have finite jumps $\Delta c_p$, $\Delta \alpha_p$, $\Delta \kappa_T$ from $\alpha$ to $\beta$.
Then, at each point of the curve where the jumps in the denominators are non-zero,
$$\frac{dp}{dT} = \frac{\Delta c_p}{T v \, \Delta \alpha_p} = \frac{\Delta \alpha_p}{\Delta \kappa_T}.$$

## Remarks

In each phase $ds = \frac{c_p}{T}\, dT - v \alpha_p\, dp$, using the [[Maxwell relation|MAHDE6JZYT]] $(\partial s/\partial p)_T = -(\partial v/\partial T)_p$.
Since $s_\alpha = s_\beta$ all along the curve, the differentials agree along it, and subtracting gives $\frac{\Delta c_p}{T}\, dT = v\, \Delta\alpha_p\, dp$, the first equation.
In the same way $dv = v\alpha_p\, dT - v\kappa_T\, dp$ and $v_\alpha = v_\beta$ give $\Delta\alpha_p\, dT = \Delta\kappa_T\, dp$, the second equation.
They play the role of the [[Clausius–Clapeyron relation|4XEQ5FA5P9]] when the latent heat and the volume jump vanish.
Many real continuous transitions, such as the lambda transition of liquid helium, have a heat capacity that diverges instead of jumping.
There the hypotheses fail and the equations do not apply.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some phase transitions happen without any latent heat or sudden change of volume.
In a second-order transition in Ehrenfest's sense, only quantities such as the heat capacity jump.
The Ehrenfest equations say how the transition temperature shifts with pressure in terms of these jumps.

### L2 human

The derivation copies the proof of the Clapeyron equation one derivative higher: instead of equal chemical potentials, one uses equal entropies and equal volumes along the curve.
Combining the two equations gives the consistency relation $\Delta c_p = T v \frac{(\Delta\alpha_p)^2}{\Delta\kappa_T}$, which can be tested on experimental data.
Landau theory predicts finite jumps of this kind, but for many real systems fluctuations change the behaviour near the transition.
