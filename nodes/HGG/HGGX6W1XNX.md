## Summary

Bimetric gravity is a class of theories of gravity that use two metric tensors on the same spacetime instead of one.
In Rosen's bimetric theory of 1940 the second metric is a fixed flat background.
In the modern bimetric theory of Hassan and Rosen both metrics are dynamical and interact through a potential.
The modern theory is closely related to [[massive gravity|Y4SHDZSCZ4]]: it describes one massless and one massive graviton and avoids the Boulware–Deser ghost.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A metric is a rule for measuring distances and times.
Bimetric theories give spacetime two such rules at once.
Usually matter feels only one of them, and the second one acts on matter only through its interaction with the first.
In the modern versions both rules evolve, and the theory contains two kinds of gravitons, one massless and one massive.
