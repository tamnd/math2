## Statement

Let $f, g \colon \R^n \to \R$ be quadratic functions, that is, $f(x) = x^T A x + 2 a^T x + \alpha$ and $g(x) = x^T B x + 2 b^T x + \beta$ with real symmetric $n \times n$ matrices $A, B$, vectors $a, b \in \R^n$ and numbers $\alpha, \beta \in \R$.
Assume that there is $\hat x \in \R^n$ with $g(\hat x) > 0$.
Then the following are equivalent:

1. $f(x) \ge 0$ for every $x \in \R^n$ with $g(x) \ge 0$;
2. there is $\lambda \ge 0$ such that $f(x) - \lambda g(x) \ge 0$ for every $x \in \R^n$.

## Remarks

The implication from 2 to 1 is immediate: if $g(x) \ge 0$, then $f(x) \ge \lambda g(x) \ge 0$.
For the converse, one first treats [[quadratic forms|0RBQS1JJCR]], where the key fact is that the set $\set{(x^T A x, x^T B x) : x \in \R^n}$ is convex (a theorem of Dines); a separating line then gives $\lambda$.
The general case reduces to the homogeneous one by adding one variable.
The S-lemma is due to Yakubovich.

Condition 2 says that a certain $(n+1) \times (n+1)$ matrix is positive semidefinite, so it turns the implication 1 into a [[semidefinite programming|2RTN2X4R2N]] constraint.
This is why the minimum of a quadratic function over one quadratic constraint, such as the trust-region subproblem, can be computed exactly.

The assumption $g(\hat x) > 0$ cannot be dropped.
Take $n = 1$, $g(x) = -x^2$ and $f(x) = x$.
Then $g(x) \ge 0$ only for $x = 0$, where $f(0) = 0$, so 1 holds.
But for $\lambda = 0$ the function $f - \lambda g = x$ takes negative values, and for $\lambda > 0$ the function $x + \lambda x^2$ equals $-1/(4\lambda)$ at $x = -1/(2\lambda)$, so 2 fails.
The equivalence also fails in general when there are two or more constraints $g_1, g_2, \dots$ in place of $g$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose one quadratic condition always forces another quadratic quantity to be nonnegative.
The S-lemma says that this can always be certified in the simplest way: the second quantity minus a nonnegative multiple of the first is nonnegative everywhere.
So a statement "for all $x$ with ..., it holds that ..." becomes a search for one number $\lambda$.

### L2 human

The S-lemma is the quadratic analogue of Farkas' lemma for linear inequalities.
It is a case where Lagrangian duality has no gap even though the problem need not be convex.
In control theory it is the basis of the S-procedure, which replaces constraints on all trajectories by linear matrix inequalities.
In optimization it shows that the trust-region subproblem and other problems with one quadratic constraint have exact semidefinite relaxations.
