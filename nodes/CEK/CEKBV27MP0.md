## Summary

The Rayleigh number is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] that measures how strongly buoyancy drives a fluid layer heated from below.
It compares this drive with the damping by viscosity and heat conduction.
It is the control parameter of [[Rayleigh–Bénard convection|GKVRHF9E43]].
Below a critical value the fluid stays at rest and heat is conducted; above it, convection cells appear.
At very large values the convection becomes turbulent.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heat a pan of liquid from below.
The warm liquid at the bottom is lighter and wants to rise, but viscosity resists the motion and conduction smooths out the temperature difference.
The Rayleigh number compares the push with the resistance.
When it is small, nothing moves; when it passes a threshold of about $1708$ for a layer between two rigid plates, the liquid starts to roll in regular cells.
