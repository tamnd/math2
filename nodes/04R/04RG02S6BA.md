## Statement

Let $G$ be a [[cyclic group|0KVGH0J5B3]].
Then $G$ is [[abelian|082GHNPJ53]].

## Remarks

If $g$ generates $G$, then any two elements are $g^a$ and $g^b$ for integers $a, b$, and $g^a g^b = g^{a+b} = g^b g^a$ by [[the laws of exponents|00DRK90Z6K]].
The converse is false: the Klein four-group is abelian but not cyclic.
The group $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ is abelian but not cyclic.

## Explanations

### L1 human

All elements of a cyclic group are powers of one element.
Two powers of the same element always commute, because the exponents just add.
Thus the order of the factors never matters in a cyclic group.
