## Definition

Consider a rotationally symmetric optical system in air made of thin lenses, where the $i$-th lens has focal length $f_i$ and [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] $n_i$.
The *Petzval sum* of the system is
$$P = \sum_i \frac{1}{n_i f_i}.$$
Assume the system has no astigmatism, and work to the order of the [[Seidel aberrations|VHH260SY86]].
Then the image of a plane object perpendicular to the axis lies on a surface of revolution, the *Petzval surface*.
Its curvature at the axis has absolute value $\abs{P}$.
When $P > 0$, the surface is concave toward the lens.
*Petzval field curvature* is this curvature of the image field, and the system is free of it when $P = 0$.

## Remarks

A single thin lens with $n = 1.5$ and $f = 100$ mm has $P = 1/150$ per mm, so its Petzval surface has radius $n f = 150$ mm.
Two thin lenses with $n_1 f_1 = -n_2 f_2$ satisfy the *Petzval condition* $P = 0$.
If $n_1 \ne n_2$, their total power $1/f_1 + 1/f_2$ is nonzero, so the pair can form a real image on a flat field.
With equal indices $n_1 = n_2$, the condition $P = 0$ forces $1/f_1 + 1/f_2 = 0$, and two thin lenses in contact then have no power at all.
In the Seidel expansion, field curvature is the term $W_{220} h^2 \rho^2$; astigmatism splits the image into two surfaces, and the Petzval surface is the one obtained when astigmatism is removed.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each lens contributes to the curvature of the image field an amount inversely proportional to its index times its focal length.
Adding up these contributions gives the Petzval sum.
Lens designers flatten the field by combining positive and negative lenses of different glasses so that the sum cancels while the lens still focuses.
