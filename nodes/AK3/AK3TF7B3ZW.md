## Summary

The Fresnel number is the dimensionless number $a^2 / (L \lambda)$.
Here $a$ is the size of an aperture, $L$ the distance to the observation plane and $\lambda$ the wavelength.
It tells which approximation of diffraction applies.
For a Fresnel number much smaller than $1$ the pattern is in the [[Fraunhofer|D7YGBADRV9]] regime.
For a Fresnel number of order $1$ or more it is in the [[Fresnel|M8669NXXJS]] regime.
For very large values geometrical optics is accurate.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light passing through a hole looks different near the hole and far from it.
Close to the hole you see a sharp shadow of the hole with fringes at its edge; far away you see a spread-out pattern that no longer resembles the hole.
The Fresnel number measures where you are between these extremes.
For a hole of radius $1$ mm, green light of wavelength $500$ nm and a screen $1$ m away, it equals $2$.
