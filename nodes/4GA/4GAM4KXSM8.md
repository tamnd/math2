## Summary

Buckling is the sudden sideways deflection of a slender structure, such as a column, plate or shell, under compressive load.
It happens at a critical load where the straight or flat equilibrium becomes unstable and a bent shape becomes possible.
So it is a case of [[elastic instability|50W7AQMS5Z]].
The critical load can be far below the load that would crush the material, so buckling often decides the strength of slender parts.
For columns the basic result is [[Euler's critical load|E8JCKAE09C]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stand on an empty aluminium drink can and it holds your weight.
Tap its side while you stand on it and it folds at once.
The metal is not crushed; the wall simply finds a bent shape that is easier than staying straight.
That jump to a bent shape under compression is buckling.
A long thin stick buckles under a much smaller push than a short thick one of the same material.
