## Definition

Let a transparent medium be placed in a static uniform [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] $\mathbf{B}$.
Let linearly [[polarized|PJ8ZSJ0QDW]] light travel through it in a direction $\hat{\mathbf{k}}$ over a path of length $d$, and let $B_\parallel = \mathbf{B} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{k}}$.
The *Faraday effect* is the rotation of the direction of linear polarization caused by the field.
Its angle is $\beta = V B_\parallel d$, where $V$, the *Verdet constant*, depends on the medium and the wavelength.

## Remarks

The field makes the two circular polarizations propagate with different [[refractive indices|9VGK0SP6EQ]], as in [[optical activity|6037NA1P98]].
The sense of rotation is fixed by $\mathbf{B}$, not by $\hat{\mathbf{k}}$.

Example: light that passes through the medium and is reflected back sees the rotation $\beta$ twice in the same sense, a total of $2\beta$.
With $\beta = 45^\circ$, the returning light is orthogonal to the incoming light, and a polarizer then blocks it; this is the principle of the optical isolator.

Non-example: the rotation in a sugar solution is optical activity, not the Faraday effect.
It needs no magnetic field and cancels on a round trip.
