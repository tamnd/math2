## Proof

We compute the [[truth table|05RSK4Q657]] of $\neg(p \land \neg p)$ and check that its column contains only T.

1. By the [[principle of bivalence|5DYNDW9526]], the variable $p$ is T or F, so the table has two rows.
2. Row $p = \mathrm{T}$.
   By the definition of [[negation|029JBBF693]], $\neg p$ is F.
3. In this row $p \land \neg p$ is $\mathrm{T} \land \mathrm{F}$.
   By the definition of [[conjunction|025YZN3VJW]], a conjunction with a false part is false, so $p \land \neg p$ is F.
4. Row $p = \mathrm{F}$.
   Here the first part $p$ of the conjunction is false, so $p \land \neg p$ is F by the definition of conjunction.
5. By steps 3 and 4, $p \land \neg p$ is F in both rows.
   By the definition of negation, $\neg(p \land \neg p)$ is T in both rows.

| $p$ | $\neg p$ | $p \land \neg p$ | $\neg(p \land \neg p)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| T | F | F | T |
| F | T | F | T |

6. The two rows cover every valuation, so $\neg(p \land \neg p)$ is a [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]]. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

A conjunction is true only when both of its parts are true.
The parts $p$ and $\neg p$ always have opposite truth values, so in each row one of them is false.
So $p \land \neg p$ is always false, and its negation is always true.

## Variants

The [[natural deduction proof of the law of noncontradiction|RN3Y6804V7]] uses no truth values at all.
It shows that the law also holds in [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]], where the truth-table method does not apply.
