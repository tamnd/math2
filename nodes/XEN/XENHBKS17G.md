## Definition

Let $A$ be a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] over a commutative ring $R$.
$A$ is *anticommutative* if $xx = 0$ for all $x \in A$.

## Remarks

Expanding $(x + y)(x + y) = 0$ gives $xy = -yx$ for all $x, y \in A$.
Conversely, $xy = -yx$ for all $x, y$ gives $2xx = 0$, so the two conditions agree when $2$ is invertible in $R$.
Example: $\R^3$ with the cross product, since $v \times v = 0$ for every vector $v$.
Non-example: the field $\mathbb{F}_2$ as an algebra over itself satisfies $xy = -yx$, because it is commutative and $-1 = 1$, but $1 \cdot 1 = 1 \neq 0$.
Some authors call an algebra with $xy = -yx$ anticommutative and one with $xx = 0$ alternating.
