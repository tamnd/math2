## Summary

Jeans escape is the thermal loss of gas from the top of a planetary atmosphere.
The exobase is the level above which collisions are rare.
There, molecules in the fast tail of the [[Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution|3QSVWQJX11]] that move upward faster than the [[escape velocity|DJGG5SN382]] leave the planet.
The escape flux depends on the escape parameter, the ratio of gravitational binding energy to thermal energy, and falls off roughly exponentially with it.
It explains why light gases such as hydrogen and helium escape from small or hot bodies while heavier gases stay.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The molecules of a gas move at many different speeds, and a few are much faster than average.
High up in an atmosphere the air is so thin that a fast molecule moving upward rarely hits anything.
If it is faster than the escape velocity, it leaves the planet for good.
Light molecules move faster at a given temperature, so they escape more easily.
This is part of why the Earth has kept little hydrogen and helium in its atmosphere while the Moon has no thick atmosphere at all.
