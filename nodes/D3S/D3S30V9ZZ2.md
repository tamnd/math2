## Proof

We prove each direction by the rules of [[natural deduction|6KT3BJZ7D1]], we mark the one classical step, and we show that it cannot be removed.
We read $\neg\varphi$ as $\varphi \to \bot$, as in Lean, where `Not p` is `p → False`.
To prove $\neg\varphi$, we assume $\varphi$, derive $\bot$, and discharge the assumption; this is the introduction rule of [[negation|029JBBF693]].
From $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ we may infer $\bot$; this is the elimination rule of negation, which is modus ponens.

Part 1: introduction, $p \to \neg\neg p$.

1. Assume $p$, since we prove an implication.
2. Assume $\neg p$, since we prove $\neg\neg p$, which is $\neg p \to \bot$.
3. From steps 1 and 2, infer $\bot$ by the elimination rule of negation.
4. Discharge step 2 and conclude $\neg\neg p$ by the introduction rule of negation.
5. Discharge step 1 and conclude $p \to \neg\neg p$ by the introduction rule of implication.
6. No step uses a classical principle, so this direction holds in [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]].

Part 2: elimination, $\neg\neg p \to p$.

7. Assume $\neg\neg p$.
8. By the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]], $p \lor \neg p$.
   This is the classical step, `Classical.em p` in Lean.
9. Case $p$: then $p$ holds, and this case is done.
10. Case $\neg p$: with the assumption $\neg\neg p$ of step 7, the elimination rule of negation gives $\bot$.
    From $\bot$ infer $p$ by the rule of [[explosion|TTKZGJEEK7]], `absurd` in Lean.
11. Both cases give $p$, so $p$ holds by the elimination rule of [[disjunction|0F72FK8EKB]], that is, by [[proof by cases|0WCP0PVET6]] (`Or.elim`).
12. Discharge step 7 and conclude $\neg\neg p \to p$.

Part 3: the classical step of part 2 cannot be removed.

13. Assume $\neg(p \lor \neg p)$.
14. Assume $p$.
    Then $p \lor \neg p$ by the introduction rule of disjunction (`Or.inl`), and with step 13 we get $\bot$.
15. Discharge step 14 and conclude $\neg p$.
16. From step 15, $p \lor \neg p$ by the introduction rule of disjunction (`Or.inr`), and with step 13 we get $\bot$.
17. Discharge step 13 and conclude $\neg\neg(p \lor \neg p)$.
    Steps 13 to 17 use no classical principle.
18. Suppose that $\neg\neg\varphi \to \varphi$ were provable intuitionistically for every formula $\varphi$.
    With $\varphi = p \lor \neg p$ and step 17, the law of excluded middle would then be provable intuitionistically.
    It is not, so elimination is not intuitionistically provable for all formulas.

Part 4: three negations reduce to one, intuitionistically.

19. Assume $\neg\neg\neg p$, and assume $p$.
20. By part 1, $p$ gives $\neg\neg p$, and with $\neg\neg\neg p$ the elimination rule of negation gives $\bot$.
21. Discharge the assumption $p$ and conclude $\neg p$.
    So $\neg\neg\neg p \to \neg p$ holds without any classical step, and part 1 with $\neg p$ in place of $p$ gives the converse. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Introduction is a direct argument: a proof of $p$ refutes every refutation of $p$.
Elimination has to get $p$ back from the mere impossibility of $\neg p$, and that needs a case split on $p \lor \neg p$.
Part 3 shows that the two classical principles are interchangeable: double negation elimination proves excluded middle, and excluded middle proves double negation elimination.

## Variants

A [[Kripke model|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]] shows directly that elimination fails intuitionistically.
Take two worlds $w_0 \le w_1$, and let $p$ hold at $w_1$ only.
At $w_0$ the formula $\neg p$ fails, because $p$ holds at the later world $w_1$, and at $w_1$ the formula $\neg p$ fails because $p$ holds there.
So $\neg\neg p$ holds at $w_0$ while $p$ does not, and $\neg\neg p \to p$ fails at $w_0$.
The classical result alone has the shorter [[proof by a truth table|A35WJBK3PW]].
