## Summary

Gradient descent is an iterative method for minimising a differentiable function.
At each step it moves a little in the direction opposite to the [[gradient|0ABK9V9Y64]].
Since the negative gradient is the direction of fastest local decrease, a small enough step lowers the value of the function.
For convex functions with Lipschitz gradient, the values converge to the minimum at rate $O(1/k)$.
It is the starting point of most first-order methods in optimization and machine learning.
It should not be confused with the method of steepest descent for asymptotics of integrals.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine standing on a hillside in fog and wanting to reach the valley.
You feel which way the ground slopes down most steeply and take a step that way, then repeat.
The step size matters: too small and progress is slow, too large and you may jump past the valley.
For $f(x) = x^2$ with step size $1/4$, the update is $x \mapsto x - \tfrac14 \cdot 2x = x/2$, so the iterates halve at every step and tend to the minimiser $0$.
