## Summary

The Ffowcs Williams–Hawkings equation describes the sound produced by a flow around solid surfaces that move, such as rotor blades and propellers.
It extends [[Lighthill's acoustic analogy|J31W7DJ90H]] to a fluid that contains moving boundaries.
Generalised functions concentrated on the surface turn the boundary into explicit sound sources.
It is the standard basis of the prediction of rotor and fan noise.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A helicopter rotor makes noise in two simple ways: each blade pushes air aside as it passes, and each blade pushes on the air to make lift.
A third source is the turbulence in the air around the blades.
The Ffowcs Williams–Hawkings equation writes the sound as the sum of these three parts.
The first two are called thickness noise and loading noise, and they can be computed from the shape and motion of the blades and the forces on them.
