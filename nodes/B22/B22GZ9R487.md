## Example

Let $\lambda > 0$, $\mu > 0$ and let $c \ge 1$ be an integer.
In the M/M/c queue, in [[Kendall's notation|9HTFATQNJ2]], customers arrive as a Poisson process of rate $\lambda$, and there are $c$ servers.
Service times are independent [[exponential|0E99F51GVB]] random variables of rate $\mu$.
The number $Q(t)$ of customers in the system is a [[birth–death process|E8DAD7052E]] on $\set{0, 1, 2, \dots}$.
Its birth rates are $\lambda_n = \lambda$ and its death rates are $\mu_n = \min(n, c)\, \mu$, since $\min(n, c)$ servers are busy when $n$ customers are present.

Let $E = \lambda / \mu$ be the offered load and $\rho = E / c$ the load per server.
The balance equations $\lambda \pi_{n} = \mu_{n+1} \pi_{n+1}$ give
$$\pi_n = \pi_0 \frac{E^n}{n!} \ (0 \le n \le c), \qquad \pi_n = \pi_0 \frac{E^c}{c!} \rho^{\,n - c} \ (n \ge c).$$
These sum to a finite value exactly when $\rho < 1$, so a [[stationary distribution|0KRH5157WP]] exists if and only if $\lambda < c \mu$, and then
$$\pi_0 = \Big( \sum_{k=0}^{c-1} \frac{E^k}{k!} + \frac{E^c}{c!} \frac{1}{1 - \rho} \Big)^{-1}.$$
The probability that an arriving customer must wait is $P(Q \ge c) = \frac{E^c}{c!} \frac{\pi_0}{1 - \rho}$, the [[Erlang C formula|8R094GG276]].
The mean waiting time in the queue is $W_q = P(Q \ge c) / (c\mu - \lambda)$, and [[Little's law|JN2YME6F4A]] gives the mean number waiting, $L_q = \lambda W_q$.

For $c = 2$, $\lambda = 1$ and $\mu = 1$: $E = 1$, $\rho = 1/2$, $\pi_0 = (1 + 1 + 1)^{-1} = 1/3$, the probability of waiting is $1/3$, $W_q = 1/3$, and the mean number in the system is $L_q + E = 4/3$.
For $c = 1$ the formulas reduce to those of the [[M/M/1 queue|GTFRPF5J2K]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bank has $c$ tellers, customers arrive at random, and each service takes a random, memoryless time.
As long as the tellers together serve faster than customers arrive, the line stays stable.
The formulas give the chance of having to wait and the average wait.
They show that one fast shared line with $c$ tellers works better than $c$ separate lines with one teller each.
