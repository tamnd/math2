## Summary

The certainty equivalent of a gamble is the sure amount of money that a decision maker values exactly as much as the gamble.
Under the [[expected utility hypothesis|8C2N37XPJ8]], it is the amount whose utility equals the expected utility of the gamble.
The gap between the expected value of the gamble and its certainty equivalent is the risk premium.
It measures how much the agent dislikes the risk.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ask a person: what sure amount would make you give up this lottery ticket?
That amount is the certainty equivalent of the ticket.
With the utility $u(x) = \sqrt{x}$, a coin flip for $0$ or $100$ euros has expected utility $5$, and $\sqrt{25} = 5$, so the certainty equivalent is $25$ euros.
The person would give up $50 - 25 = 25$ euros of expected value to avoid the risk.
