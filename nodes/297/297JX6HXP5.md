## Summary

The Barnes–Hut algorithm is a tree method that computes the gravitational forces in an [[N-body simulation|DRCDVJS6PT]] approximately in about $N \log N$ operations instead of $N^2$.
It groups particles into the cells of a hierarchical subdivision of space.
Each cell that is far enough away is replaced by a single point at its centre of mass.
It was introduced by J. Barnes and P. Hut in 1986 and is a standard method in astrophysics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

From far away, a whole galaxy pulls on you almost like a single star of the same total mass placed at its centre.
The algorithm uses this idea at every scale.
Space is cut into a cube, the cube into eight smaller cubes, and so on, until each small cube holds at most one particle.
To find the force on one particle, walk down this [[tree|062D9KXJB1]].
A cube that looks small from the particle counts as one point mass.
A cube that looks large is opened, and its children are examined.
In two dimensions the same idea uses squares cut into four.
