## Statement

Let $(M, g)$ be a four-dimensional, [[asymptotically flat|3HTF65XMF7]] spacetime containing a black hole.
Assume that its domain of outer communications is globally hyperbolic.
Assume also that its [[event horizon|D8NZ32Z3SQ]] is connected and non-degenerate.

1. (Israel) Let $(M, g)$ be a static [[vacuum solution|JRV1T69FYJ]].
Then its domain of outer communications is isometric to the region $r > 2m$ of a [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]] for some $m > 0$.
2. (Israel) If $(M, g)$ is a static [[electrovacuum solution|GAZKQEMJJ6]], then its domain of outer communications is isometric to that of a [[Reissner–Nordström metric|8YYV4NRRMB]].
3. (Carter, Robinson) Let $(M, g)$ be a stationary and axisymmetric vacuum solution.
Then its domain of outer communications is isometric to that of a [[Kerr metric|X3FZC85THY]] with $\abs{a} < m$.
4. (Mazur, Bunting) Let $(M, g)$ be a stationary and axisymmetric electrovacuum solution.
Then its domain of outer communications is isometric to that of a [[Kerr–Newman metric|NM34PFSE8A]] with $a^2 + Q^2 < m^2$.

## Remarks

The domain of outer communications is the region outside the black hole and white hole regions.
Stationary means that there is a [[Killing vector field|5AKNNW3W4Z]] that is timelike near infinity, and static means moreover that it is hypersurface orthogonal.
Israel's proof for the static case uses integral identities on the level sets of the norm of the Killing field.
In the axisymmetric case the Einstein equations reduce to a harmonic map problem from a two-dimensional domain to a hyperbolic space.
Uniqueness then follows from a divergence identity of Robinson, later generalised by Mazur and Bunting.
Axial symmetry can be derived from stationarity by Hawking's rigidity theorem, but that theorem assumes the metric is real analytic.
Removing analyticity is an open problem with partial results.
In dimensions higher than four uniqueness fails: there are rotating black rings with the same conserved charges as black holes of spherical horizon.

## Explanations

### L1 human

These theorems say that a black hole left alone has very few possible shapes.
If it does not rotate and carries no charge, it is exactly the Schwarzschild black hole; with charge it is the Reissner–Nordström one; with rotation it is Kerr or Kerr–Newman.
They are the precise results behind the slogan that black holes have no hair.

### L2 human

The theorems classify the end states of gravitational collapse in Einstein–Maxwell theory, assuming the collapse settles down.
Together with rigidity they give the [[no-hair theorem|RAEHHEPX7F]].
They are the reason why the Kerr family is the central object of black hole physics.
Its stability, studied in the [[black hole stability problem|6R0BJ6WS5A]], is then the question of whether all collapse settles to Kerr.
