## Definition

Let a [[lattice model|PAEME5EQRM]] be given by partition functions $Z_\Lambda(\beta, K)$ on finite boxes $\Lambda$ of a lattice.
Here $\beta$ is the inverse temperature and $K$ is a list of coupling parameters.
Its free energy per site is
$$f(\beta, K) = -\lim_{\Lambda} \frac{1}{\beta \abs{\Lambda}} \log Z_\Lambda(\beta, K),$$
the limit taken along boxes that grow to the whole lattice (the [[thermodynamic limit|FPYQWE8X12]]).
The model is called *exactly solvable* on a region of parameters when $f(\beta, K)$ is given there by an explicit closed formula.
Such a formula may be an elementary expression, an explicit integral or series, or the solution of explicit algebraic or integral equations.

## Remarks

This is a working definition of physics, not a formal one: what counts as closed form is a matter of convention.
Often one also asks for exact correlation functions or the spontaneous magnetization.
Example: the [[Ising model|R0KGM32QN6]] on $\Z$ with coupling $J$ and no field is exactly solvable.
With periodic boundary conditions on $N$ sites, $Z_N = (2 \cosh \beta J)^N + (2 \sinh \beta J)^N$, so $f = -\beta^{-1} \log(2 \cosh \beta J)$.
The two-dimensional Ising model without field is exactly solvable by [[Onsager's solution|JJ4ZH5YD80]].
Non-example: no closed formula is known for the free energy of the Ising model on $\Z^3$, so it is not known to be exactly solvable.
