## Summary

A deformation of a body is a map that sends each material point to its new position.
It goes from the reference configuration to the current configuration.
It is injective and preserves orientation, so matter neither overlaps nor turns inside out.
Its difference from the identity is the displacement, and its derivative, the [[deformation gradient|HTYRR2WDQ6]], measures local stretching and rotation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a rubber sheet, draw a grid on it, and stretch it.
The deformation is the rule that tells where each grid point ends up.
For example, the map $(X_1, X_2, X_3) \mapsto (2X_1, X_2, X_3)$ doubles all lengths in the first direction and leaves the other directions alone.
A rigid motion, a rotation followed by a translation, is also a deformation, but one that changes no lengths.
