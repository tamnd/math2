## Proof

We apply the Fitting decomposition to $S = T - \lambda \id_V$, and then we read the multiplicity of $\lambda$ off the characteristic polynomial of each summand.

*The kernel and the splitting.*

1. Let $S = T - \lambda \id_V$.
   By the [[definition|07PNDWA7CE]], $V_\lambda$ is the union of the kernels $\ker S^j$ for $j \ge 1$.
2. By part 1 of the [[Fitting decomposition|77SACA9CVQ]], the kernels $\ker S^j$ are constant from some index $k \le n$ on.
3. The kernels increase with $j$, so each $\ker S^j$ lies in $\ker S^k = \ker S^n$.
   Hence $V_\lambda = \ker S^n$.
4. Let $K = \ker S^n = V_\lambda$ and $I = \operatorname{im} S^n$.
   The Fitting decomposition gives $V = K \oplus I$, with $S$-invariant summands.
5. We have $T = S + \lambda \id_V$, and $\lambda \id_V$ maps every subspace into itself.
   So $K$ and $I$ are $T$-invariant, which is the first claim of part 3.

*The characteristic polynomial.*

6. Join a basis of $K$ and a basis of $I$ to a basis of $V$.
   By step 5, the matrix of $T$ in this basis is block diagonal, with the matrices of $T|_K$ and $T|_I$ as blocks.
7. By the [[determinant of a block triangular matrix|0J9TVTNB4K]], $p_T = p_{T|_K} \cdot p_{T|_I}$.
8. Let $d = \dim K$.
   The Fitting decomposition says that $S|_K$ is nilpotent, so $p_{S|_K} = t^d$ by the [[characterizations of nilpotent maps|FSKECQK1GW]].
9. On $K$ we have $t \id_K - T|_K = (t - \lambda) \id_K - S|_K$.
   Taking determinants gives $p_{T|_K}(t) = p_{S|_K}(t - \lambda) = (t - \lambda)^d$.
10. The Fitting decomposition also says that $S|_I$ is invertible.
    So if $w \in I$ and $T w = \lambda w$, then $S w = 0$, and $w = 0$.
11. Hence $\lambda$ is not an eigenvalue of $T|_I$.
    Since [[eigenvalues are the roots of the characteristic polynomial|053QVHF6N7]], the polynomial $q = p_{T|_I}$ has $q(\lambda) \ne 0$.
12. Steps 7, 9 and 11 give $p_T = (t - \lambda)^d q$ with $q(\lambda) \ne 0$.

*The multiplicity.*

13. By the [[factor theorem|0K3MWA7DMC]], $t - \lambda$ does not divide $q$.
14. Suppose that $(t - \lambda)^{d+1}$ divides $(t - \lambda)^d q$.
    Cancelling $(t - \lambda)^d$ in the integral domain $F[t]$ shows that $t - \lambda$ divides $q$, which contradicts step 13.
15. So the largest power of $t - \lambda$ that divides $p_T$ is $(t - \lambda)^d$.
    By the [[definition of the algebraic multiplicity|0S7A0EMPF4]], $m_\lambda = d = \dim V_\lambda$, which is part 2.
16. With step 9, $p_{T|_{V_\lambda}} = (t - \lambda)^{m_\lambda}$, which completes part 3.

*The remaining parts.*

17. Part 4 is step 4 together with step 10.
    An injective endomorphism of the finite-dimensional space $I$ is invertible.
18. The map $S|_K$ is nilpotent on a space of dimension $m_\lambda$, so $(S|_K)^{m_\lambda} = 0$ by the characterizations of nilpotent maps.
19. Hence $V_\lambda \subseteq \ker S^{m_\lambda}$.
    For $j \ge m_\lambda$, the kernel $\ker S^j$ lies between $\ker S^{m_\lambda}$ and $V_\lambda$, so it equals $V_\lambda$, which completes part 1.
20. For part 5, let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue.
    An eigenvector lies in $\ker S \subseteq V_\lambda$, so $V_\lambda \ne 0$.
21. Conversely, if $V_\lambda \ne 0$, then $m_\lambda = \dim V_\lambda \ge 1$ by part 2.
    So $\lambda$ is a root of $p_T$, and it is an eigenvalue.

## Idea

The Fitting decomposition of $T - \lambda$ splits $V$ into the generalized eigenspace and a part where $T - \lambda$ is invertible.
On the first part, $T - \lambda$ is nilpotent, so the characteristic polynomial there is a power of $t - \lambda$.
On the second part, $\lambda$ is not a root at all.
So the whole factor $(t - \lambda)^{m_\lambda}$ of $p_T$ comes from the first part, and its degree is the dimension of that part.

## Variants

Over an algebraically closed field, one can [[triangularize|0363JMQ42Y]] $T$ with $\lambda$ in the first $m_\lambda$ diagonal places and count dimensions in the triangular form.
Axler, Chapter 8, turns the statement around and defines the multiplicity of $\lambda$ as $\dim V_\lambda$, so that no determinant is needed.
One can also use the [[primary decomposition|49ZHBMYKVE]] with the factor $(t - \lambda)^{m_\lambda}$ of $p_T$, together with the fact that the characteristic polynomial of a nilpotent map is a power of $t$.
