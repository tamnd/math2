## Proof

We apply the Fitting decomposition to $S = T - \lambda \id_V$ and read off the characteristic polynomial.

1. Let $S = T - \lambda \id_V$. By the [[definition|07PNDWA7CE]], $V_\lambda$ is the union of the kernels $\ker S^j$ for $j \ge 1$.
2. By part 1 of the [[Fitting decomposition|77SACA9CVQ]], the kernels $\ker S^j$ are constant from some $k \le n$ on.
3. The kernels increase with $j$. So each $\ker S^j$ lies in $\ker S^k = \ker S^n$, and $V_\lambda = \ker S^n$.
4. Let $K = \ker S^n = V_\lambda$ and $I = \operatorname{im} S^n$. By the Fitting decomposition, $V = K \oplus I$, with $S$-invariant summands.
5. $T = S + \lambda \id_V$, and $\lambda \id_V$ preserves every subspace. So $K$ and $I$ are $T$-invariant. This proves the first claim of part 3.
6. Join a basis of $K$ and a basis of $I$ to a basis of $V$. In it, the matrix of $T$ is block diagonal.
7. The blocks are the matrices of $T|_K$ and $T|_I$. By the [[block determinant|0J9TVTNB4K]], $p_T = p_{T|_K} \cdot p_{T|_I}$.
8. Let $d = \dim K$. By the Fitting decomposition, $S|_K$ is nilpotent. By the [[nilpotent characterizations|FSKECQK1GW]], $p_{S|_K} = t^d$.
9. $t \id_K - T|_K = (t - \lambda) \id_K - S|_K$. So $p_{T|_K}(t) = p_{S|_K}(t - \lambda) = (t - \lambda)^d$.
10. By the Fitting decomposition, $S|_I$ is invertible. So $T w = \lambda w$ with $w \in I$ gives $S w = 0$, and then $w = 0$.
11. So $\lambda$ is not an eigenvalue of $T|_I$. By [[eigenvalues as roots|053QVHF6N7]], $q = p_{T|_I}$ has $q(\lambda) \ne 0$.
12. By steps 7, 9 and 11, $p_T = (t - \lambda)^d q$ with $q(\lambda) \ne 0$.
13. By the [[factor theorem|0K3MWA7DMC]], $t - \lambda$ does not divide $q$.
14. If $(t - \lambda)^{d+1}$ divides $(t - \lambda)^d q$, then cancelling $(t - \lambda)^d$ in $F[t]$ gives $t - \lambda$ divides $q$. This contradicts step 13.
15. So the largest power of $t - \lambda$ that divides $p_T$ is $(t - \lambda)^d$. By the [[definition of multiplicity|0S7A0EMPF4]], $m_\lambda = d$. This proves part 2.
16. With step 9, $p_{T|_{V_\lambda}} = (t - \lambda)^{m_\lambda}$. This completes part 3.
17. Part 4 is steps 4 and 10, since an injective endomorphism of the finite-dimensional space $I$ is invertible.
18. $S|_K$ is nilpotent on a space of dimension $d = m_\lambda$. By the nilpotent characterizations, $(S|_K)^{m_\lambda} = 0$.
19. So $V_\lambda \subseteq \ker S^{m_\lambda}$. For $j \ge m_\lambda$, $\ker S^j$ lies between $\ker S^{m_\lambda}$ and $V_\lambda$, so it equals $V_\lambda$. This completes part 1.
20. For part 5, if $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue, an eigenvector lies in $\ker S \subseteq V_\lambda$. So $V_\lambda \ne 0$.
21. If $V_\lambda \ne 0$, then $m_\lambda = \dim V_\lambda \ge 1$. So $\lambda$ is a root of $p_T$, and it is an eigenvalue.

## Idea

The Fitting decomposition of $T - \lambda$ splits $V$ into the generalized eigenspace and a part where $T - \lambda$ is invertible.
On the first part, $T - \lambda$ is nilpotent, so the characteristic polynomial is a power of $t - \lambda$.
On the second part, $\lambda$ is not a root.
So the whole factor $(t - \lambda)^{m_\lambda}$ of $p_T$ comes from the first part, and its degree is the dimension.

## Variants

Over an algebraically closed field, one can triangularize $T$ with the eigenvalue $\lambda$ in the first $m_\lambda$ diagonal places, and count dimensions in the triangular form.
This is the route of Axler, Chapter 8.
One can also use the [[primary decomposition|49ZHBMYKVE]] with $p = t - \lambda$, and the fact that the characteristic polynomial of a nilpotent map is a power of $t$.
