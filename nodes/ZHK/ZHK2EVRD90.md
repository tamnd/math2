## Summary

A hyperelastic material is an elastic material whose stress is the derivative of a stored energy, its [[strain energy density function|3E9K1DRNYN]].
It is a special kind of [[Cauchy elastic material|DV71K7X1JD]] in which no energy is lost or created around a closed cycle of deformations.
Hyperelastic models describe rubber and many soft tissues at large strains.
Their equilibria are critical points of an energy, so the calculus of variations applies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Rubber can be stretched to several times its length and still spring back.
Hyperelastic models describe this by giving one energy function: the energy stored by each small piece of rubber for each way it can be deformed.
The force is the rate of change of this energy.
Because the energy comes back when the load is removed, loading and unloading follow the same curve.
