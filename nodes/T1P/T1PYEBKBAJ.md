## Summary

Elastodynamics is the study of time-dependent motion of elastic solids, above all of elastic waves.
The linear theory adds the inertia term to the equilibrium equations of [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]].
Then the displacement obeys a hyperbolic system of partial differential equations.
In an isotropic body this system carries two kinds of bulk waves, the faster [[P-waves|EEPJJHC5JZ]] and the slower [[S-waves|GZ7E9GHQYB]].
Boundaries add surface and guided waves.
It is the mathematical basis of seismology, ultrasonic testing and the vibration of structures.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hit one end of a long steel bar with a hammer.
The far end does not move at once: a disturbance travels along the bar at about five kilometres per second.
Elastodynamics describes such travelling disturbances in solids.
Unlike air, a solid resists shear, so it carries both squeezing waves and shaking waves, and they travel at different speeds.
