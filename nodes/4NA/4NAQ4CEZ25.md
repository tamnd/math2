## Example

*Euler's disk* is a heavy metal disc spun on its edge on a flat, smooth mirror.
It rolls on its rim around a circle, with the disc tilted at an angle $\alpha$ to the table.
As it loses energy, $\alpha$ decreases, and the point of contact runs around faster and faster.
The motion stops abruptly after a finite time, with a whirring sound of rising pitch.

The motion before the end is close to [[rolling without slipping|PPW75DGKDR]]: the material point of the disc at the contact is nearly at rest.
For such a rolling disc, the rate of [[precession|CFE9SNEHMP]] of the contact point around the vertical grows without bound as $\alpha$ tends to $0$.
So the energy loss, though small, drives the disc towards a singular end of the motion in finite time.

## Remarks

Which mechanism of energy loss dominates, the viscosity of the air below the disc or rolling and sliding friction at the contact, has been debated in the physics literature.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Euler's disk is a toy, a thick polished disc on a mirror, named after Leonhard Euler.
Like a coin spun on a table, it rolls around on its edge, leaning more and more, with a faster and faster whirr, and then suddenly stops.
It shows how rolling motion and slow energy loss combine to give an abrupt finish.
