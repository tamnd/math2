## Summary

The Q factor, or quality factor, is a dimensionless number that measures how weakly an oscillator is [[damped|RJX78YZ6XD]].
A high Q means that the oscillator rings for many cycles before its energy is lost, and that its resonance is sharp.
For the [[damped harmonic oscillator|0RNXVVTJTJ]] it equals $1/(2\zeta)$, where $\zeta$ is the damping ratio.
It is used for mechanical oscillators, electric circuits, lasers and musical instruments alike.
See the [[definition|K6ZBJR0BBB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Strike a tuning fork and it hums for a long time; strike a block of wood and the sound dies at once.
The tuning fork has a high Q factor and the wood a low one.
Roughly, Q is $2\pi$ times the energy stored divided by the energy lost in one cycle.
A Q of $1000$ means the oscillation keeps going for hundreds of cycles.
A system with Q at most $1/2$ does not oscillate at all.
