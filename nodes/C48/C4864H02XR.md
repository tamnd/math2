## Example

The *Lagrange top* is a heavy rigid body turning about a fixed point $O$ in a uniform gravitational field.
It is symmetric about an axis through $O$.
In the principal body frame at $O$, its moments of inertia are $I_1 = I_2$ and $I_3$, and its centre of mass is at $\ell e_3$ with $\ell \ne 0$.
Let $m$ be its mass, $g$ the gravitational acceleration, $\omega$ the angular velocity and $\gamma$ the upward unit vertical, both in the body frame.
The motion is given by the Euler–Poisson equations
$$I\dot\omega = (I\omega) \times \omega + m g \ell\, \gamma \times e_3, \qquad \dot\gamma = \gamma \times \omega,$$
where $I = \operatorname{diag}(I_1, I_1, I_3)$.

It is an [[integrable system|N0N7CMRCMH]] on the $6$-dimensional phase space of the heavy top, with three integrals in involution:

1. the energy $H = \tfrac12 \omega^T I \omega + m g \ell\, \gamma_3$;
2. the vertical component $L \cdot \gamma$ of the angular momentum $L = I\omega$;
3. the spin $\omega_3$ about the symmetry axis.

The check for the third: the third component of $(I\omega) \times \omega$ is $I_1\omega_1\omega_2 - I_1\omega_2\omega_1 = 0$, and the third component of $\gamma \times e_3$ is $0$, so $I_3\dot\omega_3 = 0$.

## Remarks

With the three integrals, the cosine $u = \gamma_3$ of the angle between the axis and the vertical satisfies $\dot u^2 = f(u)$ for a polynomial $f$ of degree $3$.
So $u$ oscillates between two roots of $f$: the symmetry axis turns about the vertical, which is [[precession|CFE9SNEHMP]], while its tilt oscillates between two values, which is [[nutation|QP24MG6QFS]].
The motion is solved by elliptic functions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Lagrange top is the ordinary spinning top, a symmetric body spinning on its tip under gravity.
Its axis slowly circles around the vertical and nods up and down while it does so.
Its motion can be solved exactly, because it has three conserved quantities: energy, the vertical part of the angular momentum and the spin about its own axis.
