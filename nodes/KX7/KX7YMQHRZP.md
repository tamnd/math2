## Proof

Call $M$ *weakly homogeneous* if for $A \subseteq B$ in $\operatorname{Age}(M)$, every [[embedding|SCRB74SWQN]] $A \to M$ extends to an embedding $B \to M$.
All structures below are countable, and "f.g." means finitely generated.

### Step A: weak homogeneity

1. An ultrahomogeneous $M$ is weakly homogeneous. Given $f \colon A \to M$, embed $B$ by some $h$; then $f \circ h^{-1}$ on $h(A)$ extends to an automorphism $\sigma$, and $g = \sigma \circ h$.
2. Conversely, let $M$ be countable and weakly homogeneous, and let $p$ be an isomorphism of f.g. substructures.
3. Enumerate $M$ and extend $p$ by the [[back-and-forth method|6F2DB4HTJR]]. Alternately add the next element to the domain, then to the range.
4. Each step applies weak homogeneity to the f.g. substructure generated by the current domain and one new point.
5. The union is an automorphism extending $p$.

### Step B: uniqueness

6. Let $M, N$ be countable, weakly homogeneous, with the same age $\mathcal{K}$.
7. The substructure of $M$ generated by $\emptyset$ is in $\mathcal{K}$, so it embeds into $N$. The embedding maps it onto the substructure of $N$ generated by $\emptyset$.
8. Go back and forth: weak homogeneity of $N$ extends a partial isomorphism forward, that of $M$ extends it backward.
9. The union is an isomorphism $M \cong N$.

### Step C: existence

10. List representatives $A_0, A_1, \dots$ of the types in $\mathcal{K}$, repeating if there are finitely many.
11. Fix a bijection $\pi \colon \N \times \N \to \N$ with $\pi(k, j) \ge k$.
12. Build $D_0 \subseteq D_1 \subseteq \cdots$ in $\mathcal{K}$, with $D_0 = A_0$.
13. When $D_k$ is built, list the countably many tasks $(A, B, f)$ with $A \subseteq B$ from the list and $f \colon A \to D_k$ an embedding. Call them $t_{k, j}$.
14. At stage $n = \pi(k, j)$, use AP for $f \colon A \to D_n$ and $A \subseteq B$ to get $D' \supseteq D_n$ with $f$ extended to $B$.
15. Use JEP to embed $A_{n+1}$ into some $D_{n+1} \supseteq D'$.
16. Let $M = \bigcup_n D_n$. Every f.g. substructure of $M$ lies in some $D_n$, so it is in $\mathcal{K}$ by HP.
17. Each $A_n$ embeds in $M$, so $\operatorname{Age}(M) = \mathcal{K}$.
18. A task $f \colon A \to M$ has finitely generated image, inside some $D_k$; it is some $t_{k, j}$, handled at stage $\pi(k, j)$.
19. So $M$ is weakly homogeneous, and ultrahomogeneous by step A.

### Step D: the converse

20. Let $M$ be countable and ultrahomogeneous. Every age has HP and JEP.
21. Countably many f.g. substructures exist, since $L$ and $M$ are countable.
22. For AP, take $A \subseteq B$, $A \subseteq C$ embedded in $M$ by $h_B, h_C$.
23. Extend $h_B \circ h_C^{-1}$ on $h_C(A)$ to an automorphism $\sigma$. The substructure generated by $h_B(B) \cup \sigma h_C(C)$ is an amalgam.

## Idea

Weak homogeneity is a one-point condition that back-and-forth turns into ultrahomogeneity and uniqueness.
Existence builds a chain that meets every extension task; AP is what makes each task solvable.

## Variants

One can replace the bookkeeping in step C by the Baire category theorem on a Polish space of structures with universe $\N$: the generic structure is the Fraïssé limit.
