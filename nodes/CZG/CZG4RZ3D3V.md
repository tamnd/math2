## Summary

Milankovitch cycles are the slow, quasi-periodic changes of the Earth's orbit and of the tilt and direction of its axis.
They change the distribution of sunlight over latitude and season.
The three main cycles are those of the [[eccentricity|0HVX863SAJ]] of the orbit, of the tilt of the axis, and of the [[precession|G0HP8ED7NW]] of the axis.
Milutin Milanković computed their effect on insolation and proposed that they pace the ice ages.
Records of ocean sediments show the same periods.
The cycles are now accepted as the pacemaker of the glacial cycles of the last few million years.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Earth's path around the Sun is not fixed.
Over tens of thousands of years the orbit becomes more or less oval, and the tilt of the axis rocks between about $22$ and $24.5$ degrees.
The axis also wobbles slowly like a spinning top.
These changes hardly alter the total sunlight the Earth receives, but they change how it is shared between summer and winter and between north and south.
When northern summers are cool, snow can survive the summer and ice sheets grow.
For example, the tilt cycle has a period of about $41\,000$ years, a rhythm that dominates the ice ages before about one million years ago.
