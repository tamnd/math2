## Summary

Bremsstrahlung, German for braking radiation, is [[electromagnetic radiation|AC6R5A3M1C]] emitted by a charged particle deflected by the electric field of another charged particle.
In astrophysics the main case is free electrons passing ions in a hot ionised gas, called free-free emission.
Let $h$ be the Planck constant and $k_B$ the Boltzmann constant.
Thermal bremsstrahlung from gas at temperature $T$ has a nearly flat spectrum up to a cutoff near $h\nu \approx k_B T$.
It is the main X-ray emission of the hot gas in clusters of galaxies and the main radio emission of hot ionised regions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A charged particle that speeds up, slows down or turns gives off light.
When a fast electron flies past a positive ion, the attraction bends its path, and it radiates during the bend.
In a hot gas there are countless such encounters, and the sum of their flashes is a continuous glow.
The hotter the gas, the higher the frequencies the glow reaches; gas at tens of millions of kelvin glows in X-rays.
