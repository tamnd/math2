## Summary

A gravitational wave is a ripple in the curvature of [[spacetime|63ZKC7AFX2]] that travels at the speed of light.
In [[linearized gravity|AH62KSWA6Y]] it is a solution of a wave equation for the perturbation of the flat metric, with two independent polarisations.
Gravitational waves are emitted by accelerated masses, most strongly by pairs of compact objects such as black holes in close orbit.
They carry away energy and angular momentum.
They were predicted by Einstein in 1916 and first detected directly by LIGO in 2015.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When masses move, the curvature of spacetime around them changes, and the change spreads outwards as a wave.
A passing wave stretches distances in one direction and squeezes them in the perpendicular direction, then the other way round.
Picture a ring of floating particles: it is deformed into an ellipse, back to a circle, and into an ellipse at right angles.
The effect is tiny: waves from merging black holes far away change the length of a $4$-kilometre detector arm by much less than the size of a proton.
