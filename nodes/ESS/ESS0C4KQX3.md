## Summary

The Faraday effect is a rotation of the plane of linear [[polarization|PJ8ZSJ0QDW]] of light in a medium.
It is caused by a static [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] along the direction of travel.
The angle of rotation is proportional to the field strength and to the path length.
Michael Faraday discovered it in 1845, and it was the first evidence of a link between light and magnetism.
Because it is not reciprocal, it is used in optical isolators, which let light pass in one direction only.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put a piece of special glass inside a coil and pass polarized light through it along the axis of the coil.
When current flows, the polarization of the light comes out turned by a small angle.
Reverse the current, and it turns the other way.
Unlike the rotation in sugar water, sending the light back through the glass doubles the turn instead of undoing it.
