## Summary

A spin network is a [[graph|00C0BR7D7H]] whose edges are labelled by irreducible representations of $SU(2)$ and whose vertices are labelled by intertwiners.
The representations are given by spins $j = \frac12, 1, \frac32, \dots$.
Roger Penrose introduced spin networks around 1971 as a combinatorial model of space.
In [[loop quantum gravity|9C3ECSDRTP]], spin networks embedded in space give a basis of the gauge-invariant quantum states of geometry.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a network of lines meeting at points, with a number on each line.
The numbers are spins, the same half-whole numbers that label the angular momentum of a quantum particle.
At each meeting point the spins must be combined so that the total "rotation" balances out, like forces at a joint.
In loop quantum gravity each line carries a quantum of area and each meeting point a quantum of volume.
For example, a single closed loop with spin $\frac12$ is the simplest spin network.
