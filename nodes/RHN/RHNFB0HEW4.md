## Summary

Right ascension is the longitude coordinate of the [[equatorial coordinate system|QBEJJPMWV1]].
It is the angle measured eastward along the celestial equator from the March equinox to the hour circle of an object.
It is usually given in hours, minutes and seconds, and together with [[declination|5HD86E1TEH]] it fixes a direction in the sky.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Right ascension is the sky's version of geographic longitude.
It is counted in hours because the sky seems to turn once in about a day: $24$ hours make a full circle, so one hour is $15^\circ$.
Stars with right ascension $6^{\mathrm h}$ cross the meridian about six hours of star time after the March equinox does.
For example, Sirius has right ascension close to $6^{\mathrm h} 45^{\mathrm m}$.
