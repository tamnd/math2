## Proof

1. Assume, for a contradiction, that $\mathrm{Def}(x, y)$ is a formula of $\mathcal{L}$ with the property of the [[statement|WWC9SZXDYK]].
2. Let $B(z, y)$ be the formula $\forall x \le z\, \neg \mathrm{Def}(x, y) \wedge \forall w < y\, \exists x \le z\, \mathrm{Def}(x, w)$.
3. In $\mathcal{M}$, $B(\underline{c}, \underline{n})$ says: $n$ is the least number defined by no formula with code at most $c$.
4. The [[diagonal lemma with parameters|P3XFQC6ACX]], for $\mathsf{Q}$ in $\mathcal{L}$, gives a formula $\beta(y)$ with $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \forall y\,(\beta(y) \leftrightarrow B(\ulcorner \beta \urcorner, y))$.
5. Let $b$ be the code of $\beta$. The formula $\beta$ has exactly one free variable $y$.
6. At most $b + 1$ numbers are at most $b$. So at most $b + 1$ formulas have code at most $b$.
7. A formula with one free variable defines at most one number, by the definition of "defines".
8. By steps 6 and 7, at most $b + 1$ numbers are defined by formulas with code at most $b$.
9. So some number is not defined by such a formula. Let $m$ be the least such number.
10. By steps 1, 3 and 9, $\mathcal{M} \models B(\underline{b}, \underline{m})$.
11. Let $n < m$. Then $n$ is defined by a formula with code at most $b$, so the first conjunct of $B(\underline{b}, \underline{n})$ fails.
12. Let $n > m$. Then $w = m$ makes the second conjunct of $B(\underline{b}, \underline{n})$ fail, by step 9.
13. $\mathcal{M}$ expands $\mathcal{N}$, so it satisfies $\mathsf{Q}$. By step 4 and [[soundness|4A84VHDTHV]], $\mathcal{M} \models \beta(\underline{n}) \leftrightarrow B(\underline{b}, \underline{n})$ for every $n$.
14. Steps 10 to 13 give $\mathcal{M} \models \forall y\,(\beta(y) \leftrightarrow y = \underline{m})$. So $\beta$ [[defines|CY1TXHF6KQ]] $m$.
15. The code of $\beta$ is $b$. So by step 1, $\mathcal{M} \models \mathrm{Def}(\underline{b}, \underline{m})$.
16. By step 10, $\mathcal{M} \models \forall x \le \underline{b}\, \neg \mathrm{Def}(x, \underline{m})$. With $x = b$ this gives $\mathcal{M} \models \neg \mathrm{Def}(\underline{b}, \underline{m})$.
17. Steps 15 and 16 contradict each other. So no formula $\mathrm{Def}$ with the property exists.

## Idea

The proof formalizes the Berry phrase "the least number not definable in fewer than $c$ symbols".
The length bound becomes a bound on codes.
The diagonal lemma with parameters makes the bound equal to the code of the defining formula itself.
A counting step shows that the least undefined number exists, and the formula then defines it.

## Variants

1. One can bound the length of formulas in place of their codes. Step 6 then counts strings of bounded length over a finite alphabet.
2. In Boolos's proof of incompleteness, "defines in $\mathcal{M}$" is replaced by a definability relation that is representable in arithmetic.
3. A shorter proof reduces to [[Tarski's undefinability theorem|YRV6SYK2BY]], since truth is definable from definability.
