## Summary

The Burnside ring $A(G)$ of a finite group $G$ is a commutative ring built from finite $G$-sets.
Its addition is disjoint union and its multiplication is Cartesian product.
As an abelian group it is free on the classes of the transitive $G$-sets $G/H$, one for each conjugacy class of subgroups $H$.
It records the ways a group can act on finite sets.
It maps to the [[representation ring|RKTX9MN7Q3]] by sending a $G$-set to its permutation representation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A finite group can act on many finite sets.
Such actions can be added, by putting two sets side by side, and multiplied, by taking pairs.
The Burnside ring allows formal differences of actions as well, as the integers do for counting numbers.
Every action splits into orbits, and each orbit looks like the set of cosets $G/H$ of a subgroup.
For the trivial group, a $G$-set is just a finite set, so the Burnside ring is $\Z$, counting elements.
