## Summary

A reference ellipsoid is an oblate ellipsoid of revolution chosen to approximate the shape of the Earth.
It is fixed by two numbers, usually the semi-major axis $a$ and the [[flattening|NE1PZM0HBN]] $f$.
It is the surface on which geodetic coordinates are defined, and the reference for heights and gravity formulas.
Widely used examples are GRS 80 and WGS 84, both with $a = 6378137$ m.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take an ellipse and spin it around its short axis: the surface you get is a squashed sphere.
Choosing its two radii well gives a surface that fits the Earth to within about $100$ m everywhere.
That is much better than any sphere, which misses by up to about $10$ km.
Because the formula is simple, one can compute exact distances and coordinates on it.
