## Definition

A process of a thermodynamic system is *isentropic* if the [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]] of the system is the same at all times of the process.

## Remarks

A [[reversible|F3DHZ45H6A]] [[adiabatic process|W1RS8DA2H6]] is isentropic, since $dS = \delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}} / T = 0$ along it.
The reversible adiabatic expansion of an ideal gas, with $p V^\gamma$ constant, is an example.
The free expansion of a gas in an insulated container is adiabatic but not isentropic: its entropy increases.
Many engineering texts define an isentropic process as a reversible adiabatic process.
