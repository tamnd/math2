## Statement

Let $K$ be a field, let $k \ge 1$ be an integer, and let $c_0, \dots, c_{k-1} \in K$.
Let $S$ be the set of all sequences $(u_n)_{n \ge 0}$ in $K$ that satisfy the [[linear recurrence with constant coefficients|081THG13JZ]]
$$u_{n+k} = c_0 u_n + c_1 u_{n+1} + \dots + c_{k-1} u_{n+k-1} \qquad (n \ge 0).$$
Let $p(x) = x^k - c_{k-1} x^{k-1} - \dots - c_1 x - c_0$, let $U_n = (u_n, u_{n+1}, \dots, u_{n+k-1})^{\mathsf T}$ be the state vector, and let
$$M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & & & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 \\ c_0 & c_1 & c_2 & \cdots & c_{k-1} \end{pmatrix}.$$
We use the convention $0^0 = 1$.

1. **Dimension.** $S$ is a [[space of solutions|05BDYVQQ0T]] of [[dimension|05G2G0P224]] $k$ over $K$. The map $u \mapsto U_0 = (u_0, \dots, u_{k-1})$ is an isomorphism from $S$ onto $K^k$.
2. **Matrix powers.** For every $u \in S$ and every $n \ge 0$, $U_{n+1} = M U_n$ and $U_n = M^n U_0$.
3. **Closed form.** Suppose that $p$ has $k$ distinct roots $r_1, \dots, r_k$ in $K$. Let $V$ be the [[Vandermonde matrix|5T4DY28JSK]] with the columns $(1, r_j, r_j^2, \dots, r_j^{k-1})^{\mathsf T}$, and let $D = \operatorname{diag}(r_1, \dots, r_k)$. Then $M = V D V^{-1}$, the sequences $(r_j^n)_{n \ge 0}$ form a basis of $S$, and every $u \in S$ is
$$u_n = a_1 r_1^n + \dots + a_k r_k^n, \qquad (a_1, \dots, a_k)^{\mathsf T} = V^{-1} U_0.$$
4. **[[Binet's formula|0404D8TN7Y]].** The Fibonacci numbers, with $F_0 = 0$, $F_1 = 1$ and $F_{n+2} = F_{n+1} + F_n$, satisfy
$$F_n = \frac{\varphi^n - \psi^n}{\sqrt 5}, \qquad \varphi = \frac{1 + \sqrt 5}{2}, \quad \psi = \frac{1 - \sqrt 5}{2}.$$

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- *The roots must be distinct.* The recurrence $u_{n+2} = 4 u_{n+1} - 4 u_n$ has $p(x) = (x - 2)^2$. The sequence $u_n = n 2^n$, which starts $0, 2, 8$, is a solution, since $8 = 4 \cdot 2 - 4 \cdot 0$. It is not a multiple $a 2^n$ of the only geometric solution, because $u_0 = 0$ forces $a = 0$. Here $M$ is not [[diagonalizable|07SQCNV233]].
- *The roots must lie in $K$.* Over $K = \mathbb{R}$, the recurrence $u_{n+2} = -u_n$ has $p(x) = x^2 + 1$ with no real root. Its solution space still has dimension $2$, by part 1, but no nonzero real geometric sequence solves it. Over $\mathbb{C}$, the roots $\pm i$ give the basis $i^n$, $(-i)^n$.
- *The convention $0^0 = 1$.* When $c_0 = 0$, the number $0$ is a root of $p$, and the basis sequence $0^n$ is $1, 0, 0, \dots$. Without the convention, part 3 needs $c_0 \ne 0$.
- *The coefficients must be constant.* Part 1 also holds for $u_{n+k} = c_0(n) u_n + \dots + c_{k-1}(n) u_{n+k-1}$, with the same proof. In part 2, $M^n$ becomes a product of $n$ different matrices, and the roots of one polynomial no longer give the solutions.

### Consequences

- If $\abs{r_1} > \abs{r_j}$ for all $j \ne 1$ and $a_1 \ne 0$, then $u_n / (a_1 r_1^n) \to 1$. So the dominant root gives the growth rate.
- Since $\abs{\psi} < 1$, the term $\psi^n / \sqrt 5$ has absolute value less than $1/2$. So $F_n$ is the integer nearest to $\varphi^n / \sqrt 5$, and $F_{n+1} / F_n \to \varphi$.
- Induction on $n$ with $\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1$ gives $\varphi^n = F_n \varphi + F_{n-1}$ for $n \ge 1$.

### Converse

The converse of part 3 is true.
If $S$ has a basis of $k$ geometric sequences $(r_j^n)$, then $p$ has $k$ distinct roots in $K$.
The reason: $(r^n)$ solves the recurrence exactly when $r^n p(r) = 0$ for all $n$, that is, when $p(r) = 0$.
Two geometric sequences with the same ratio are equal, so a basis needs $k$ different ratios, and these are $k$ distinct roots of $p$.
In matrix language, $M$ is diagonalizable over $K$ exactly when $p$ has $k$ distinct roots in $K$, because the [[minimal polynomial of a companion matrix|XYR7GPH20K]] equals its characteristic polynomial.

### Generalizations

- If $K$ has characteristic $0$ and a nonzero root $r$ of $p$ has multiplicity $m$, then $n^j r^n$ for $0 \le j < m$ are solutions. All these sequences together form a basis of $S$ when $c_0 \ne 0$ and $p$ splits over $K$. The proof uses the Jordan form of $M$ instead of a diagonalization.
- An inhomogeneous recurrence $u_{n+k} = c_0 u_n + \dots + c_{k-1} u_{n+k-1} + g(n)$ has the solutions $u = u^{\mathrm p} + h$, with one particular solution $u^{\mathrm p}$ and $h \in S$.
- The continuous analogue is the system $x' = A x$, solved by [[linear systems of ODEs by eigenvectors|DH7CQXQYJX]].
- Every linear recurrence $U_{n+1} = A U_n$ with a $k \times k$ matrix $A$ is a [[linear discrete dynamical system|2F1B461YN7]]. The companion form is the special case of one scalar sequence.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A recurrence such as "each Fibonacci number is the sum of the two before it" computes every term from the first few terms.
So a solution is fixed by its first $k$ terms, and the solutions form a space with $k$ free parameters.
Some solutions are especially simple: they multiply by the same number $r$ at each step, like $1, 2, 4, 8, \dots$.
When there are $k$ such simple solutions, every solution is a mixture of them.
For the Fibonacci numbers, this gives a formula with the golden ratio $\varphi \approx 1.618$.

### L2 human

Worked example: solve $u_{n+3} = 2 u_{n+2} + u_{n+1} - 2 u_n$ with $u_0 = 0$, $u_1 = 1$, $u_2 = 2$.

1. The polynomial is $p(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 - x + 2 = (x - 1)(x + 1)(x - 2)$, with the distinct roots $1, -1, 2$.
2. By part 3, $u_n = a_1 + a_2 (-1)^n + a_3 2^n$.
3. The first three terms give the Vandermonde system $a_1 + a_2 + a_3 = 0$, $a_1 - a_2 + 2 a_3 = 1$ and $a_1 + a_2 + 4 a_3 = 2$.
4. The first and third equations give $3 a_3 = 2$, so $a_3 = 2/3$. The first and second give $2 a_2 - a_3 = -1$, so $a_2 = -1/6$. Then $a_1 = -1/2$.
5. So $u_n = -\tfrac12 - \tfrac16 (-1)^n + \tfrac23 2^n$.
6. Check: the recurrence gives $u_3 = 4 + 1 - 0 = 5$ and $u_4 = 10 + 2 - 2 = 10$. The formula gives $-\tfrac12 + \tfrac16 + \tfrac{16}{3} = 5$ and $-\tfrac12 - \tfrac16 + \tfrac{32}{3} = 10$.

The typical use is to find the growth of a sequence that a counting argument defines by a recurrence.
Here $u_n \approx \tfrac23 2^n$ for large $n$.
The trap is a repeated root: then $a_1 r^n + a_2 r^n$ is only one solution, and the second one is $n r^n$.

### L3 human

The shift $T(u)_n = u_{n+1}$ is a linear operator on the space of all sequences, and $S$ is the kernel of $p(T)$.
So $S$ is a $T$-invariant subspace, and $M$ is the matrix of $T$ restricted to $S$ in the basis of the solutions that start with the standard basis vectors.
Then $p(x) = \prod (x - r_j)^{m_j}$ gives $S = \bigoplus_j \ker (T - r_j)^{m_j}$, which is the primary decomposition of $T$ on $S$.
The same structure appears for linear differential equations, with $d/dt$ in place of the shift.
For the Fibonacci matrix, the computation of [[Fibonacci numbers by diagonalizing the Fibonacci matrix|AC3XJ3DC9X]] is part 3 with $k = 2$.
Generating functions give the same closed forms, because $\sum u_n z^n$ is a rational function with denominator $1 - c_{k-1} z - \dots - c_0 z^k$.
