## Summary

The Zeldovich–von Neumann–Döring model, or ZND model, describes the structure of a steady planar [[detonation|FBR0PYYJJ7]].
A non-reacting [[shock wave|FRVV0NHBXT]] compresses the fresh gas to a high-pressure state, the von Neumann spike, and a reaction zone of finite length follows it.
Through the reaction zone the pressure falls and the heat release accelerates the gas until the burnt state is reached.
The model was proposed independently by Zeldovich, von Neumann and Döring in the early 1940s.
It refines the [[Chapman–Jouguet theory|ZZCNP3N36B]], which treats the detonation as a single jump.
See the [[definition|12EPT5G5QV]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Zoom into a detonation and it is not a single jump.
First comes a sharp shock that squeezes and heats the fresh gas, without burning it yet.
Then, a short distance behind, the hot gas burns, releasing heat and expanding.
The ZND model follows the gas through these two stages, one after the other, in a frame moving with the shock.
