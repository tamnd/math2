## Summary

The angular momentum of a particle about a point is the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]] of its position relative to that point with its [[momentum|37Q4BTJMXW]].
It measures the amount of rotation of the motion about the point, and its direction is the axis of that rotation.
Its rate of change equals the torque of the forces, so it is conserved when the torque vanishes, for instance under a central force.
This conservation explains why planetary orbits lie in a plane and obey Kepler's second law.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Angular momentum is the rotational cousin of momentum.
A figure skater who pulls in her arms spins faster, because her angular momentum stays the same while her mass comes closer to the axis.
A stone of mass $1$ kg swung on a string of length $1$ m at $2$ m/s has angular momentum $2$ kg m²/s about the hand.
A body moving straight towards or away from the point has no angular momentum about it.
