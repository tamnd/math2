## Definition

Let $(M, g)$ be a [[Riemannian manifold|7CBT4SM83Y]], the configuration space of a mechanical system with [[kinetic energy|C9YCMAPKMT]] $T = \tfrac12 g(v, v)$.
Let $V \colon M \to \R$ be a smooth [[potential energy|DF7DKHTQ7A]] and let $E \in \R$.
Let $M_E = \set{q \in M : V(q) < E}$, an open subset of $M$.
The *Jacobi metric* for the energy $E$ is the [[Riemannian metric|Q61VFHCH2J]] on $M_E$
$$g_E = 2\big(E - V(q)\big)\, g.$$

## Remarks

The factor $2(E - V)$ is positive on $M_E$, so $g_E$ is a Riemannian metric, conformal to $g$.
Some authors use $(E - V)\,g$ instead; a constant factor does not change the geodesics.
Along a motion of energy $E$ one has $\tfrac12 g(\dot q, \dot q) = E - V$, and the $g_E$-length of the path equals the abbreviated action $\int 2T\, dt$.
By [[Maupertuis's principle|ZXY0AYKMXZ]], the paths of motions of energy $E$ are, up to reparametrisation, the [[geodesics|NSYMQMHPDN]] of $g_E$.

Example: a free particle, $V = 0$, with $E > 0$ gives $g_E = 2E\, g$, a constant multiple of $g$, so the paths are the geodesics of $g$.

Example: uniform gravity on $\R^2$, with $g = m(dx^2 + dy^2)$ and $V = mgy$, gives $g_E = 2m(E - mgy)(dx^2 + dy^2)$ on the half-plane $y < E/(mg)$.
Its geodesics are, up to reparametrisation, arcs of parabolas with vertical axis and vertical segments, the trajectories of projectiles.

Non-example: on the set where $V \ge E$, the tensor $2(E - V)g$ is not positive definite, so it is not a Riemannian metric there.
This region is not reached by motions of energy $E$, except at turning points on its boundary.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Jacobi metric reweights the usual lengths in configuration space by how much kinetic energy the system would have at each point.
With this reweighting, the paths of motion at fixed energy become geodesics.
