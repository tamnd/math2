## Summary

The phase space formulation of quantum mechanics works with functions on [[phase space|CPEC2NW2KF]] instead of operators on a Hilbert space.
A state is described by its [[Wigner function|563Y1P5HR8]], an observable by its [[Weyl symbol|RFNGJ09WDR]], and the product of operators by the [[Moyal product|J3A8CFHZZM]].
It is equivalent to the Hilbert space formulation.
It makes the comparison with classical statistical mechanics direct, since the Moyal bracket tends to the Poisson bracket as $\hbar \to 0$.
It is the model case of [[deformation quantization|3MKSJ1J04K]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In classical statistical mechanics one has a density on the plane of positions and momenta, and it flows along the classical motion.
The phase space formulation keeps this picture for quantum mechanics.
The density is replaced by the Wigner function, which may be negative in small regions.
Its evolution looks like the classical Liouville equation with extra terms proportional to powers of $\hbar^2$.
For a harmonic oscillator these extra terms vanish, and the Wigner function simply rotates in the plane.
