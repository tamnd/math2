## Summary

Fracture toughness is the resistance of a material to the growth of a crack.
In linear elastic [[fracture mechanics|SNRXGRZWR5]] it is the critical value $K_{Ic}$ of the mode I [[stress intensity factor|8D619PN75J]].
At this value a crack under plane strain starts to grow.
It is a material constant, measured on standard cracked specimens, and it is used to compute the largest crack a part can tolerate.
Ductile metals have a high fracture toughness and glasses and ceramics a low one.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two materials can be equally strong when flawless, yet behave very differently once they contain a crack.
Glass breaks with a small scratch, while steel with the same scratch keeps carrying its load.
Fracture toughness puts a number on this difference.
If a part with a crack of length $2a$ is loaded with stress $\sigma$, the crack grows once $\sigma \sqrt{\pi a}$ reaches the fracture toughness.
