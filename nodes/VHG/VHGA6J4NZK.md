## Summary

This topic is a part of [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].
It covers research and practice in the teaching of logic, mathematical language, sets and proof, from primary school to university.
The pages treat what convinces students, why students find proof hard, how classes argue, how curricula place proof, and the main teaching approaches.
General theories of learning are in [[mathematics education|9622VKAWMZ]], and the proof methods themselves are in the other topics of this area.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Teaching foundations of mathematics|~5a0c5672]]: the place of logic, sets and proof in curricula, and the main works on the field.
2. [[Philosophy and mathematics in education|~5b9b7014]]: the views of mathematics held by teachers and students, and their effect on proof.
3. [[Teaching logic|~a70622e7]]: logic in school and at university, and how students read conditional statements.
4. [[Mathematical language in education|~8f166c1a]]: symbols, definitions, special meanings of words, and learners in a second language.
5. [[Reasoning and proof in the classroom|~718ed082]]: argumentation in class, proving tasks and the roles of the teacher.
6. [[Teaching sets and relations|~9ec6076d]]: the New Math and the Venn diagram in school.
7. [[Proof schemes|~968d6007]]: external, empirical and deductive standards of conviction.
8. [[Transition-to-proof courses|~a913431e]]: the university course on logic, sets, proof methods and induction.
9. [[Toulmin model of argumentation|~889806ec]]: claim, data, warrant, backing, qualifier and rebuttal.
10. [[Student difficulties with proof|~5a5258b8]]: the research on how and why students fail to prove.
11. [[Proof comprehension and validation|~b200f4e2]]: how students and mathematicians read and judge proofs.
12. [[Moore method and inquiry-based learning|~492bc465]]: courses in which students prove the theorems themselves.
13. [[Proof in school curricula|~9d753695]]: the NCTM standards, the Common Core and the A level.
14. [[Generic and pre-formal proofs|~66a80e3a]]: proofs by a generic example, by actions and by pictures.
15. [[Concept image and concept definition|~ce2f3661]]: the mental picture of a concept versus its definition.
16. [[Proof assistants in teaching|~03ceb7ae]]: Lean, Rocq and Isabelle in proof courses.

### Prerequisites

1. [[Mathematical proof|0B94TKFQJX]]: what a proof is, in the sense of mathematicians.
2. [[The language of mathematical statements|WMY13MXY3W]]: connectives, conditionals and quantifiers.
3. [[Basic proof methods|958EF46EYJ]]: the methods that students learn to use.
4. [[Writing and reading proofs|STSM6PR7TX]]: the practice of writing and reading proofs, which this topic studies from the side of the learner.
5. [[Mathematics education|9622VKAWMZ]]: the general field of research, for its theories and methods.
