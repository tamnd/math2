## Example

Let $\sigma$ be a signature, and use first-order logic without equality, so $=$ is not a symbol.
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a $\sigma$-structure with domain $M$.
Its *double* $\mathcal{M}'$ has domain $M' = M \times \set{0, 1}$ and the projection $\pi(a, i) = a$, with:

- $R^{\mathcal{M}'}(b_1, \dots, b_n)$ exactly when $R^{\mathcal{M}}(\pi b_1, \dots, \pi b_n)$;
- $f^{\mathcal{M}'}(b_1, \dots, b_n) = (f^{\mathcal{M}}(\pi b_1, \dots, \pi b_n), 0)$, and $c^{\mathcal{M}'} = (c^{\mathcal{M}}, 0)$.

We show that every formula without $=$ has the same truth in $\mathcal{M}'$ and in $\mathcal{M}$.

1. For each term $t$ and assignment $s$ into $M'$, the value of $t$ in $\mathcal{M}$ under $\pi \circ s$ is $\pi$ of its value in $\mathcal{M}'$ under $s$. Reason: [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $t$, since $\pi$ of a variable value is the composed value, and $\pi$ commutes with each $f$ by definition.
2. Claim: $\mathcal{M}' \models \varphi[s]$ exactly when $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[\pi \circ s]$, for every formula $\varphi$ without $=$. We prove it by induction on $\varphi$.
3. Atomic $R(t_1, \dots, t_n)$: by the definition of $R^{\mathcal{M}'}$ and step 1, both sides say that $R^{\mathcal{M}}$ holds of the values of the $t_i$ under $\pi \circ s$.
4. Connectives: both sides are computed from their parts by the same truth table.
5. $\exists x \, \psi$: a witness $b \in M'$ in $\mathcal{M}'$ gives the witness $\pi b$ in $\mathcal{M}$. Conversely a witness $a \in M$ gives the witness $(a, 0)$, since $\pi \circ (s[x \mapsto (a, 0)]) = (\pi \circ s)[x \mapsto a]$. The case $\forall x \, \psi$ is the same.
6. So $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{M}'$ satisfy the same sentences.
7. Take $\mathcal{M}$ with one element. Then $\mathcal{M}'$ has two elements. A sentence that says "there are at least two elements" would be false in $\mathcal{M}$ and true in $\mathcal{M}'$, against step 6.

So no sentence of logic without equality says that there are at least two elements.

## Remarks

The same argument with $M \times K$ for any nonempty set $K$ shows that no set of sentences without $=$ bounds the size of models from above.
With equality the sentence $\exists x \, \exists y \, \neg (x = y)$ does the job, as in the [[counting sentences|GVBGZT47CY]].
So $=$ adds expressive power, and it cannot be defined from the other symbols in general.
The proof is also a model of an induction on formulas whose atomic case uses an induction on terms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Without the symbol $=$, logic cannot tell two copies of the same thing apart.
Take any structure and copy each element twice, with the copies behaving exactly like the original.
Every statement without $=$ is true in the copy exactly when it is true in the original.
So "there are at least two things" cannot be said: a one-element structure and its two-element copy agree on everything.

### L2 human

We test the doubling on the signature $\set{<}$ and $\mathcal{M} = (\set{0, 1}, <)$.

1. $M' = \set{(0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1)}$, and $(a, i) < (b, j)$ exactly when $a < b$.
2. In $\mathcal{M}$, the sentence $\forall x \, \neg (x < x)$ is true, and it is true in $\mathcal{M}'$, since $(a, i) < (a, i)$ would need $a < a$.
3. The two elements $(0, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ of $\mathcal{M}'$ are not related by $<$ either way, so $\mathcal{M}'$ is not a total order.
4. Totality needs $=$ to state: $\forall x \, \forall y \, (x < y \lor x = y \lor y < x)$. Without $=$, the doubled structure cannot be told apart from $\mathcal{M}$.

The typical use of this construction is to show that a property needs equality.
A trap: in logic without equality, a structure may still interpret some relation symbol as identity, but no sentence can force it to.
