## Example

Work in [[first-order logic|086QKPF0A6]] without equality, so $=$ is not a symbol, over a signature $\sigma$.
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a $\sigma$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]] with domain $M$.
Its double $\mathcal{M}'$ has domain $M' = M \times \{ 0, 1 \}$, and we write $\pi(a, i) = a$ for the projection.
Each symbol of $\sigma$ looks only at the first coordinates:
$$R^{\mathcal{M}'}(b_1, \dots, b_n) \iff R^{\mathcal{M}}(\pi b_1, \dots, \pi b_n), \qquad f^{\mathcal{M}'}(b_1, \dots, b_n) = \big( f^{\mathcal{M}}(\pi b_1, \dots, \pi b_n), 0 \big),$$
and a constant $c$ goes to $(c^{\mathcal{M}}, 0)$.
We show that every formula without $=$ means the same in $\mathcal{M}'$ as in $\mathcal{M}$.

1. Terms.
   For each term $t$ and each assignment $s$ into $M'$, the value of $t$ in $\mathcal{M}$ under $\pi \circ s$ is $\pi$ of the value of $t$ in $\mathcal{M}'$ under $s$.
   This follows by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $t$: it holds for a variable by the definition of $\pi \circ s$, and $\pi$ commutes with each $f$ by the definition of $f^{\mathcal{M}'}$.
2. The claim for formulas: $\mathcal{M}' \models \varphi[s]$ exactly when $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[\pi \circ s]$, for every formula $\varphi$ without $=$.
   We prove it by induction on $\varphi$.
3. Atomic case $R(t_1, \dots, t_n)$.
   By the definition of $R^{\mathcal{M}'}$ and step 1, both sides say that $R^{\mathcal{M}}$ holds of the values of the $t_i$ under $\pi \circ s$.
4. Connectives.
   Both sides are computed from their parts by the same truth table.
5. Quantifier case $\exists x \, \psi$.
   A witness $b \in M'$ gives the witness $\pi b$ in $\mathcal{M}$.
   Conversely, a witness $a \in M$ gives the witness $(a, 0)$, since $\pi \circ (s[x \mapsto (a, 0)]) = (\pi \circ s)[x \mapsto a]$.
   The case $\forall x \, \psi$ is the same with "every" for "some".
6. So $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{M}'$ make the same sentences true.
7. Now let $\mathcal{M}$ have one element, so $\mathcal{M}'$ has two.
   A sentence that says "there are at least two elements" would be false in $\mathcal{M}$ and true in $\mathcal{M}'$, against step 6.

So no sentence of logic without equality says that there are at least two elements.

## Remarks

With equality, the sentence $\exists x \, \exists y \, \neg (x = y)$ says it at once, as in the [[counting sentences|GVBGZT47CY]].
The argument breaks down at exactly one place: the atomic formula $x = y$ is true of $(a, 0)$ and $(a, 0)$ but false of $(a, 0)$ and $(a, 1)$, while $\pi$ sends both pairs to $(a, a)$.
So step 3 fails for $=$, and $=$ really adds expressive power.

Replace $\{ 0, 1 \}$ by any nonempty set $K$, and the same proof works for $M \times K$.
So without equality no set of sentences bounds the size of models from above: every structure has arbitrarily large structures that make the same sentences true.
The proof also shows a common pattern, an induction on formulas whose atomic case rests on an induction on terms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Without the symbol $=$, logic cannot tell two copies of the same thing apart.
Take any structure, and make two copies of each element that behave exactly like the original.
Every statement without $=$ is true in the copy exactly when it is true in the original.
So "there are at least two things" cannot be said: a one-element structure and its two-element copy agree on everything.

### L2 human

We test the doubling on the signature $\{ < \}$ and $\mathcal{M} = (\{ 0, 1 \}, <)$.
The double has four elements,
$$M' = \{ (0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1) \},$$
and $(a, i) < (b, j)$ holds exactly when $a < b$.
The sentence $\forall x \, \neg (x < x)$ is true in $\mathcal{M}$, and it stays true in $\mathcal{M}'$, since $(a, i) < (a, i)$ would need $a < a$.
But $(0, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ are not related by $<$ in either order, so $\mathcal{M}'$ is not a total order.
That causes no contradiction, because totality needs $=$ to state: $\forall x \, \forall y \, (x < y \lor x = y \lor y < x)$.
The typical use of the doubling is to show that a property needs equality.
Watch out: in logic without equality a structure may still read some relation symbol as identity, but no set of sentences can force it to.
