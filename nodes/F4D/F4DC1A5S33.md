## Example

Let $G = (\mathbb{Z}, +)$ be the [[additive group of integers|0BSCJA5G53]].
We show that the [[subgroup generated|0RP7VWQ0YN]] by $4$ and $6$ is the set $2\mathbb{Z}$ of even integers.

1. $2 = 6 - 4$ is a word in the generators $4$ and $6$. So $2 \in \langle 4, 6 \rangle$.
2. A subgroup that contains $2$ contains every integer multiple $2k$, by closure under sums and negatives. So $2\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \langle 4, 6 \rangle$.
3. The set $2\mathbb{Z}$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$, because $2k - 2l = 2(k - l)$ and $0 = 2 \cdot 0$.
4. It contains $4 = 2 \cdot 2$ and $6 = 2 \cdot 3$. The subgroup $\langle 4, 6 \rangle$ is the smallest subgroup that contains $4$ and $6$, so $\langle 4, 6 \rangle \subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}$.
5. By steps 2 and 4, $\langle 4, 6 \rangle = 2\mathbb{Z}$.

In particular, $1 \notin \langle 4, 6 \rangle$, so the set $\{4, 6\}$ does not generate $\mathbb{Z}$.

## Remarks

The number $2$ is the greatest common divisor of $4$ and $6$.
This is a special case of a general rule: $\langle a, b \rangle = \gcd(a, b)\mathbb{Z}$ for all integers $a$ and $b$.
The rule is part of the theorem on the [[subgroups of the integers|0SRATCFF1W]], and it is the group form of [[Bézout's identity|0DQVHMYAGA]].
The elements of $\langle 4, 6 \rangle$ are the sums $4x + 6y$ with $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$, because $\mathbb{Z}$ is abelian.
For example, $10 = 4 \cdot 1 + 6 \cdot 1$ and $-2 = 4 \cdot 1 + 6 \cdot (-1)$.
In the same way, $\langle 4, 9 \rangle = \mathbb{Z}$, because $\gcd(4, 9) = 1$ and $1 = 9 \cdot 1 - 4 \cdot 2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start with the numbers $4$ and $6$, and allow adding and subtracting them as often as we like.
We get $6 - 4 = 2$.
From $2$ we get every even number, for example $2 + 2 + 2 = 6$ and $-2 - 2 = -4$.
We never get an odd number, because sums and differences of even numbers are even.
So $4$ and $6$ generate exactly the even numbers.

### L2 human

We find the generated subgroup in two steps.

1. Find a small element. The Euclidean algorithm gives $6 = 1 \cdot 4 + 2$ and $4 = 2 \cdot 2 + 0$. So $\gcd(4, 6) = 2$, and $2 = 6 - 4$.
2. Check that the multiples of this element form a subgroup that contains both generators.

The typical use is a fast computation of $\langle a, b \rangle$ in $\mathbb{Z}$: compute $\gcd(a, b)$.

A trap: $\langle 4, 6 \rangle$ is not the set $\{4x + 6y : x, y \geq 0\}$.
That set misses $2$, because $4x + 6y \geq 4$ when $x, y \geq 0$ are not both $0$.
Negative coefficients are needed.
