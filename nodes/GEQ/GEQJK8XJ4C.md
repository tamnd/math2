## Summary

The Budyko–Sellers model is a one-dimensional [[energy balance model|M182YC20JJ]] in which the temperature depends on latitude.
Its albedo jumps from a low to a high value where the surface is cold enough to be covered by ice.
Mikhail Budyko and William Sellers introduced such models independently in 1969.
The temperature-dependent albedo creates the ice–albedo feedback: cooling extends the ice, the ice reflects more sunlight, and the reflection causes further cooling.
Because of this positive feedback the model can have several stable climates for the same sunlight, including a completely ice-covered one.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ice and snow are bright: they reflect most of the sunlight that falls on them, while dark ocean absorbs most of it.
So if the Earth cools a little, ice spreads, the planet reflects more sunlight, and it cools further.
This chain is the ice–albedo feedback.
The Budyko–Sellers model puts it into an equation for the temperature at each latitude, with a rule that the ground is icy where it is colder than a fixed threshold.
It predicts that a large enough drop in sunlight could make the ice spread all the way to the equator.
