## Summary

The holographic principle says that quantum gravity in a region has no more degrees of freedom than fit on its boundary.
The maximal entropy is a quarter of the boundary area measured in Planck units, so information has a finite density per unit area.
It was proposed by Gerard 't Hooft in 1993 and developed by Leonard Susskind in 1995.
Its starting point is the [[Bekenstein–Hawking entropy|ANNJ7JR36T]] of black holes, which grows with the horizon area and not with the volume.
The [[AdS/CFT correspondence|92T9TPF13Y]] is the best-understood realization of the principle.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In ordinary physics, the amount of information that fits in a box grows with the volume of the box.
Black holes break this rule: the entropy of a black hole is proportional to the area of its horizon.
Since packing too much matter into a box makes a black hole, the most information a region can hold is limited by the area of its surface.
The holographic principle takes this seriously: the physics inside a region can be described by a theory living on its boundary.
This is like a hologram, which stores a three-dimensional image on a flat film.
For a sphere of radius one metre the bound is about $10^{70}$ bits, a huge but finite number.
