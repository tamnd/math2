## Summary

A generalized Kac–Moody algebra, also called a Borcherds algebra, is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] defined by generators and relations from a real symmetric matrix.
It is built like a [[Kac–Moody algebra|HD13X0CNBD]], but it allows simple roots of norm zero or of negative norm.
Such simple roots are called imaginary simple roots.
Borcherds introduced these algebras and used one of them, the [[monster Lie algebra|VTZX4WVWHS]], in his proof of the monstrous moonshine conjecture.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a Kac–Moody algebra every simple root behaves like a root of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$: it has positive length and a reflection attached to it.
Borcherds allowed some simple roots to have length zero or negative squared length.
These roots have no reflection, and the relations attached to them are weaker.
The resulting Lie algebras still have a character formula and a denominator identity, which can be used to prove identities between modular functions.
