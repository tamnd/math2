## Proof

We use the [[transpose of a product|09GK5PS824]], $(XY)^T = Y^T X^T$, and $(X^T)^T = X$.

Part 1 (equivalence relation).
We check the three conditions of an [[equivalence relation|0SGTMBEWRF]].

1. Reflexive: $A = I^T A I$, and the identity matrix $I$ is invertible.
2. Symmetric: let $A' = P^T A P$ with $P$ invertible. Multiply by $(P^{-1})^T$ on the left and by $P^{-1}$ on the right.
3. This gives $(P^{-1})^T A' P^{-1} = (P^{-1})^T P^T A P P^{-1} = (P P^{-1})^T A = A$. Here $(P^{-1})^T P^T = (PP^{-1})^T = I$ by the transpose of a product.
4. So $A = Q^T A' Q$ with $Q = P^{-1}$ invertible, and $A'$ is congruent to $A$.
5. Transitive: let $A' = P^T A P$ and $A'' = Q^T A' Q$. Then $A'' = Q^T P^T A P Q = (PQ)^T A (PQ)$.
6. The product $PQ$ of invertible matrices is invertible, with inverse $Q^{-1} P^{-1}$. So $A''$ is congruent to $A$.

Part 2 (rank).

7. $P$ and $P^T$ are invertible, since $(P^T)^{-1} = (P^{-1})^T$ by step 3.
8. By the [[rank of a product|02FDWY55AS]], multiplication by an invertible matrix keeps the rank. So $\rank(P^T A P) = \rank(AP) = \rank A$.

Part 3 (symmetry and skew-symmetry).

9. $(P^T A P)^T = P^T A^T (P^T)^T = P^T A^T P$, by the transpose of a product.
10. If $A^T = A$, step 9 gives $(A')^T = P^T A P = A'$. So $A'$ is symmetric.
11. If $A^T = -A$, step 9 gives $(A')^T = -P^T A P = -A'$. So $A'$ is skew-symmetric.

Part 4 (the alternating property).

12. For $x \in F^n$, put $z = Px$. Then $x^T A' x = x^T P^T A P x = (Px)^T A (Px) = z^T A z$.
13. By the hypothesis, $z^T A z = 0$. So $x^T A' x = 0$ for all $x$.

Part 5 (determinant).

14. By the [[multiplicativity of the determinant|04CPJ6AF00]] and the [[determinant of the transpose|0ZZQ2E1MTE]], $\det(P^T A P) = \det P \cdot \det A \cdot \det P = (\det P)^2 \det A$.

This proves all five parts.

## Idea

Congruence is built from multiplication by invertible matrices, which can be undone and composed.
Transposes turn products around, and this makes $P^T$ and $P$ fit together.
Each invariant then follows from the rule for products: rank, transpose, quadratic values and determinant.

## Variants

Part 1 also follows from the [[change of basis rule|0SPYAGE7T0]]: two matrices are congruent exactly when they represent one form in two bases, and "represent the same form" is an equivalence relation.
