## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2i \\ -2i & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ act on $\mathbb{C}^2$ with the standard inner product $\ip{x}{y} = x_1 \overline{y_1} + x_2 \overline{y_2}$.

1. The [[conjugate transpose|0EX045E2BP]] of $A$ is $A$ itself, so $A$ is [[Hermitian|0EKZCX4K3P]], and the operator $x \mapsto Ax$ is [[self-adjoint|0MB8HQW495]].
2. The characteristic polynomial is $(1 - t)^2 - (2i)(-2i) = (1 - t)^2 - 4$. Its roots are $t = 3$ and $t = -1$. Both are real.
3. For $t = 3$, the matrix $A - 3I = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 2i \\ -2i & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ kills $(i, 1)$: $-2i + 2i = 0$ and $2 - 2 = 0$.
4. For $t = -1$, the matrix $A + I = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2i \\ -2i & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ kills $(1, i)$: $2 - 2 = 0$ and $-2i + 2i = 0$.
5. The two eigenvectors are orthogonal: $\ip{(i, 1)}{(1, i)} = i \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot \overline{i} = i - i = 0$.
6. Each has length $\sqrt{2}$. So $u_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(i, 1)$ and $u_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, i)$ form an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{C}^2$.
7. Let $U = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} i & 1 \\ 1 & i \end{pmatrix}$ have the columns $u_1$ and $u_2$. Then $U$ is [[unitary|0D6RMJ10M9]], and
$$U^* A U = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

## Remarks

- Step 2 shows [[real eigenvalues|0C4NT4GT3X]], and step 5 shows [[orthogonal eigenvectors|0JHGAV9H25]], as the general theorems predict.
- The spectral decomposition is $A = 3 u_1 u_1^* - u_2 u_2^*$ with $u_1 u_1^* = \tfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \\ -i & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $u_2 u_2^* = \tfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -i \\ i & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Indeed $\tfrac{3}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \\ -i & 1 \end{pmatrix} - \tfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -i \\ i & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2i \\ -2i & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
- For $x = (a, b)$, the quadratic form is $x^* A x = \abs{a}^2 + \abs{b}^2 + 2 \operatorname{Re}(2i \, \overline{a} b)$. It is real for every $x$, as [[the quadratic form statement|WAYMK9GR5S]] says. At $x = (1, 1)$ it equals $2$.
- The matrix is not symmetric: $A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2i \\ 2i & 1 \end{pmatrix} \neq A$. Over $\mathbb{C}$ the right condition is $A^* = A$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This complex matrix equals its own conjugate transpose.
It stretches one direction by $3$ and flips another direction, with factor $-1$.
The two directions are perpendicular for the complex inner product.
In these two directions the matrix acts by the plain numbers $3$ and $-1$.

### L2 human

The computation follows the standard recipe for a Hermitian matrix.

1. Find the eigenvalues from the characteristic polynomial. For a Hermitian matrix they are real, here $3$ and $-1$.
2. Find a unit eigenvector for each eigenvalue.
3. Check orthogonality with the complex inner product, which conjugates the second vector.
4. Put the unit eigenvectors as columns of $U$. Then $U^* A U$ is diagonal.

A trap: in step 3, without the conjugation, the product would be $i \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot i = 2i \neq 0$, and the vectors would look not orthogonal.
