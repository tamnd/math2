## Summary

The effective population size $N_e$ of a population is the size of an ideal [[Wright–Fisher model|SR4F169EKF]] population that shows the same amount of [[genetic drift|AFJ02FJ1KR]].
Real populations have unequal sex ratios, fluctuating sizes and unequal numbers of offspring, and these make drift stronger than the census size suggests.
The number $N_e$ summarises all of this in one quantity, and it is usually smaller than the census size.
It sets the time scale of drift and appears in many formulas, such as $\theta = 4 N_e \mu$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A herd of $100$ animals in which only $10$ males breed loses genetic variety much faster than a herd of $100$ where everyone breeds equally.
The effective size says how large an ideal, evenly breeding population would have to be to lose variety at the same speed.
For the herd with $10$ breeding males and $90$ breeding females, the effective size is $4 \cdot 10 \cdot 90 / 100 = 36$.
So the herd behaves genetically like a herd of $36$ ideal animals.
