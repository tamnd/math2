## Summary

Backtracking line search is the simplest practical rule to choose a step length in a [[line search|B6T5HA74QW]] method.
It starts with a trial step and shrinks it by a fixed factor until the Armijo sufficient decrease condition holds.
It needs only function values, and it stops after finitely many trials whenever the direction is a descent direction.
It is the usual step rule in gradient descent and in damped Newton methods.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Try a bold step first.
If the function did not go down enough, halve the step and try again.
Keep halving until the decrease is good enough.
For $f(x) = x^2$ at $x = 1$, take the direction $-2$ and the trial step $1$.
This lands at $-1$, where $f$ is still $1$, so the step is halved.
The halved step lands at $0$, which is accepted.
