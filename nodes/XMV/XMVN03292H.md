## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be an [[alphabet|RABRP2KG81]] and let $\vdash, \dashv$ be two symbols not in $\Sigma$, the left and right end markers.
A *two-way deterministic finite automaton* (2DFA) is a tuple $A = (Q, \Sigma, \delta, s, t, r)$ where:

1. $Q$ is a finite set of states, and $s, t, r \in Q$ are the start, accept and reject states, with $t \ne r$;
2. $\delta \colon Q \times (\Sigma \cup \set{\vdash, \dashv}) \to Q \times \set{L, R}$ is the transition function;
3. for every state $q$, $\delta(q, \vdash)$ has the form $(q', R)$ and $\delta(q, \dashv)$ has the form $(q', L)$, so the head never leaves the marked input.

On an input [[string|1P6EP01HZF]] $w = a_1 \cdots a_n$, the tape holds $a_0 a_1 \cdots a_n a_{n+1}$ with $a_0 = {\vdash}$ and $a_{n+1} = {\dashv}$.
A configuration is a pair $(q, i)$ with $q \in Q$ and $0 \le i \le n + 1$; it starts at $(s, 0)$.
If $q \notin \set{t, r}$ and $\delta(q, a_i) = (q', D)$, the next configuration is $(q', i - 1)$ when $D = L$ and $(q', i + 1)$ when $D = R$.
The computation stops when it enters $t$ or $r$.
The 2DFA *accepts* $w$ when the computation enters $t$; it rejects $w$ when it enters $r$ or never stops.

## Remarks

Example: over $\Sigma = \set{a, b}$, a 2DFA can accept the words whose last letter is $a$.
It moves right in state $s$ until it reads $\dashv$, and then moves left once into a state $c$.
In state $c$ it goes to $t$ if it reads $a$, and to $r$ otherwise.
Non-example: a rule $\delta(q, \dashv) = (q', R)$ breaks clause 3, since it would move the head beyond the right end marker.
Every [[DFA|W65B6BKZRY]] gives a 2DFA that always moves right and decides at $\dashv$.
Conversely, the language accepted by a 2DFA is always a [[regular language|18WVBDCS38]], so 2DFAs and DFAs have the same power.
The nondeterministic version, 2NFA, also accepts exactly the regular languages.
