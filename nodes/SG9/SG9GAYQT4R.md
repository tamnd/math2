## Summary

Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates are coordinates on the Schwarzschild spacetime adapted to radial light rays.
They replace the Schwarzschild time $t$ by a null coordinate, so that the metric stays regular across the horizon $r = 2m$.
They show that the singularity of the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]] at $r = 2m$ comes from the coordinates and not from the geometry.
They are the simplest way to see the interior of a [[black hole|YHJ0PDNY8T]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the usual Schwarzschild coordinates, the formula for the metric blows up at the horizon, and a falling object seems to take infinite time to reach it.
The trouble is the clock used, not spacetime itself.
Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates use the arrival of inward light rays as the clock.
Each inward light ray then has a constant value of this new time coordinate, and the metric has no problem at the horizon.
In these coordinates one sees that light and matter can cross the horizon inwards but never come back out.
