## Summary

Convective available potential energy, or CAPE, is the work per unit mass that buoyancy does on a rising air parcel.
The work is counted from the parcel's level of free convection to its level of neutral buoyancy.
It is the integral of the parcel's buoyancy over the layer in which the parcel is warmer than its environment.
It measures how much energy is available to deep moist convection, and large values indicate the risk of strong thunderstorms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bubble of air that is warmer than the air around it rises, like a hot-air balloon.
CAPE adds up this upward push over the whole height through which the bubble stays warmer than its surroundings.
It is measured in joules per kilogram of air.
If a parcel is $3$ K warmer than air at $300$ K over a depth of $10$ km, the push is about $0.1$ m/s$^2$ and CAPE is about $1000$ J/kg.
Values of a few thousand J/kg are typical of days with severe storms.
