## Summary

A dimension group is a partially ordered abelian group that is directed, unperforated, and has the Riesz interpolation property.
By the Effros–Handelman–Shen theorem, the countable dimension groups are exactly the inductive limits of groups $\Z^n$ with the coordinatewise order.
The countable ones are exactly the ordered $K_0$-groups of separable [[AF algebras|4Y7VENNBVK]], and they carry the invariant in [[Elliott's classification of AF algebras|Y9KSMY8A1Q]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The group $\Z^n$ with the order "every coordinate is at least as large" is the simplest example of a dimension group.
A dimension group is what one gets by gluing such groups along positive maps, in a growing sequence.
For a matrix algebra $M_k(\C)$, the positive part of $K_0$ records the ranks of projections, and so $K_0$ is $\Z$ with its usual order.
For a limit of finite-dimensional algebras the ordered $K_0$-group is the limit of such groups, and so it is a dimension group.
An example that is not $\Z^n$ is the group of dyadic rationals $\Z[1/2]$ with the order of the real line, which comes from the CAR algebra.
