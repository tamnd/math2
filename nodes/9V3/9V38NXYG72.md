## Definition

Let $A$ be a set with a [[total order|0FRXBVVR03]], and let $A^*$ be the set of finite words over $A$.
Order $A^*$ by the [[lexicographic order|0B68KNPGCG]], in which a proper prefix of a word is smaller than the word.
A nonempty word $w \in A^*$ is a *Lyndon word* if $w < v$ for every nonempty proper suffix $v$ of $w$.
Let $w$ be a Lyndon word of length at least $2$.
Let $v$ be the longest proper suffix of $w$ that is a Lyndon word.
Write $w = uv$; this is the *standard factorization* of $w$, and $u$ is also a Lyndon word.

Let $k$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]] and let $L(A)$ be the [[free Lie algebra|NNAXK7BM3H]] over $k$ on $A$.
For each Lyndon word $w$ define $P_w \in L(A)$ by $P_a = a$ for $a \in A$ and $P_w = [P_u, P_v]$ when $w = uv$ is the standard factorization.
The *Lyndon basis* of $L(A)$ is the family $(P_w)$, where $w$ runs over the Lyndon words.
A standard theorem states that this family is a basis of $L(A)$.

## Remarks

Example: let $A = \set{a, b}$ with $a < b$.
The Lyndon words of length at most $3$ are $a$, $b$, $ab$, $aab$ and $abb$.
For $aab$, the proper suffixes $ab$ and $b$ are both larger than $aab$, and the longest Lyndon suffix is $ab$, so $P_{aab} = [a, [a, b]]$.
For $abb$, the suffix $bb$ is not a Lyndon word, so the standard factorization is $ab \cdot b$ and $P_{abb} = [[a, b], b]$.

Non-example: $abab$ is not a Lyndon word, since its suffix $ab$ is a proper prefix and hence smaller than $abab$.

If $A$ has $q$ elements, the number of Lyndon words of length $n$ is
$$\frac{1}{n} \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d)\, q^{n/d},$$
where $\mu$ is the [[Möbius function|0FANE7P0DY]].
By the basis theorem, this is also the dimension of the homogeneous component of degree $n$ of $L(A)$, which is Witt's formula.
An equivalent definition: a nonempty word is a Lyndon word if and only if it is strictly smaller than all its nontrivial cyclic rotations.
