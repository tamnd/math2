## Summary

A Lie algebra is a [[vector space|000AYPA9AJ]] with a bilinear product $[x, y]$, the Lie bracket, that is alternating and satisfies the Jacobi identity.
The commutator $AB - BA$ of square matrices is the basic example.
Every [[Lie group|7JBRGCVJRB]] has a Lie algebra, its tangent space at the identity.
This algebra records most of the structure of the group near the identity.
Lie algebras are also studied for their own sake, and the semisimple ones over $\C$ have a complete classification.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Matrices can be multiplied, but the product depends on the order.
The bracket $[A, B] = AB - BA$ measures this failure to commute.
A Lie algebra keeps only this bracket and its two main rules: $[x, x] = 0$ and the Jacobi identity.
For example, the vectors of $\R^3$ with the cross product $x \times y$ form a Lie algebra.
One can think of a Lie algebra as the space of infinitesimal motions of a group of symmetries.
