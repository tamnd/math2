## Definition

Let $G$ be a [[group|G5S1M9]] and let $[G, G]$ be its [[commutator subgroup|0ASGSTXSBW]], the subgroup generated by all $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ with $a, b \in G$.
The group $G$ is *perfect* if $[G, G] = G$.

## Remarks

Equivalently, the [[abelianisation|0AQZV0905N]] $G/[G, G]$ is trivial, that is, every homomorphism from $G$ to an abelian group is trivial.
Example: the alternating group $A_5$ is perfect, since $[A_5, A_5]$ is a nontrivial normal subgroup (as $A_5$ is not abelian) and $A_5$ is simple.
Non-example: the symmetric group $S_n$ for $n \ge 2$ is not perfect, since the sign map $S_n \to \set{\pm 1}$ is a nontrivial homomorphism to an abelian group, so $[S_n, S_n] \subseteq A_n \ne S_n$.
Every non-abelian simple group is perfect, by the same argument as for $A_5$.
For a ring $R$ with identity, the elementary group $E(R)$ is perfect, since $e_{ij}(r) = [e_{ik}(r), e_{kj}(1)]$ for distinct $i, j, k$.
