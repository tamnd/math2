## Summary

This topic is a part of [[linear algebra|GN8GYSDZFX]], and it builds the dictionary that turns linear maps into matrices and back.
Once we fix a basis of the domain and a basis of the target, a linear map becomes a table of numbers whose columns are the coordinates of the images of the basis vectors.
Composition of maps then becomes the product of matrices, which explains why matrices multiply the way they do.
Each matrix carries four subspaces: the column space, the row space, the null space and the left null space.
Their dimensions are all fixed by one number, the rank, and the fact that row rank equals column rank gets several proofs.
The rank obeys the rank-nullity count and a family of inequalities for sums and products, and every matrix of rank $r$ splits into $r$ matrices of rank one.
The second half of the story asks what happens when we change the bases.
The matrix of an endomorphism changes to $P^{-1}AP$, so the matrices of one map form a similarity class, and the rank, trace, determinant and characteristic polynomial are invariants of that class.
The topic ends with the trace of a linear map and with the Fredholm alternative, which decides when $Ax = b$ has a solution.
Later topics build on this one: [[dual spaces|9K7FFF26QR]] gives the transpose a meaning without bases, [[eigenvalues|6R1D6QPY3S]] looks for a basis in which the matrix is simple, and [[inner product spaces|1D4JTZ9VS3]] returns to the orthogonality of the four subspaces.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Matrix of a linear map|0YQSGNYE3K]]: you write a map as a matrix and compute images by $[f(v)]_C = [f]_{C,B}[v]_B$.
2. [[Linear map of a matrix|06HMCTAQH3]]: you go back from a matrix to a map and see that $\Hom(V, W) \cong M_{m \times n}(F)$.
3. [[Matrix of a composition|05Q1B7MH0M]]: you see why the matrix product is defined the way it is, and that $\End(V) \cong M_n(F)$ as algebras.
4. [[Column space|03AJKXH1AV]]: you find the image of $x \mapsto Ax$ and a basis of it from the pivot columns of $A$.
5. [[Row space|069MCGX635]]: you see why row operations keep the row space and read a basis from an echelon form.
6. [[Null space|0PHDYNR24D]]: you solve $Ax = 0$ and build a basis from the free variables.
7. [[Four fundamental subspaces|73DVECPAT6]]: you put the four subspaces in one picture, with dimensions $r$, $n - r$, $r$ and $m - r$.
8. [[Rank of a matrix|0ZC9YTXZRY]]: you compute one number in several ways, from pivots, from columns and from minors.
9. [[Row rank equals column rank|0TZAG60ZYT]]: you see three proofs of the basic fact about rank and compare them.
10. [[Rank-nullity theorem for matrices|TR48YQGCCA]]: you count pivot and free columns to get $\operatorname{rank} A + \operatorname{nullity} A = n$.
11. [[Rank of a product of matrices|02FDWY55AS]]: you bound the rank of $AB$ and meet the inequalities of Sylvester and Frobenius.
12. [[Rank factorization and rank-one matrices|5JGAG62VN8]]: you write $A = CR$ and split a matrix into a sum of rank-one matrices $uv^{\mathsf{T}}$.
13. [[Change of basis matrix|0MKRHZB3WS]]: you translate coordinates from one basis to another and learn to check the direction of the matrix.
14. [[Change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]]: you compute the new matrix of a map as $Q^{-1}AP$, or $P^{-1}AP$ for an endomorphism.
15. [[Similar matrices|0E89HQATM8]]: you recognize two matrices of one endomorphism and see that similarity is an equivalence relation.
16. [[Invariants that do not decide similarity|J4Q3B9ZNHF]]: you test the standard invariants and see two matrices that share them all and are still not similar.
17. [[Trace of a linear map|0KZDNGA561]]: you define the trace of a map by any of its matrices and find the rank of a projection from its trace.
18. [[Fredholm alternative|4HH8C9V8JQ]]: you decide whether $Ax = b$ has a solution by testing $b$ against the null space of $A^{\mathsf{T}}$.

### Prerequisites

The reader should know [[linear maps|9080WQTZ3M]], in particular the kernel, the image, the rank-nullity theorem and the space $\Hom(V, W)$.
The reader also needs bases, coordinates and dimension from [[vector spaces, span, independence, bases and dimension|MVH97H8B2J]].
The computations use matrix products, the transpose and the trace from [[matrices and the operations on them|Y1ESNQ2V52]], and row reduction with pivots and free variables from [[systems of linear equations and row reduction|DXSXFVDMXQ]].
Change of basis needs the invertible matrices of [[invertible matrices and matrix factorizations|P8J6QR66TJ]].

The topic on [[dual spaces and duality|9K7FFF26QR]] explains the transpose as the dual map and gives one more proof that row rank equals column rank.
