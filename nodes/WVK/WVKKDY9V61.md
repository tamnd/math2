## Summary

The cosmological constant $\Lambda$ is a constant term $\Lambda g$ that can be added to the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]].
It acts like an energy density of empty space with negative pressure, which drives an accelerated expansion of the universe when $\Lambda > 0$.
Einstein introduced it in 1917 to allow a static universe.
In the standard model of cosmology, a positive $\Lambda$ accounts for the observed accelerated expansion.
It is the simplest model of dark energy.

## Explanations

### L1 human

General relativity allows one extra number in its equations, the cosmological constant.
A positive value means that empty space itself carries a small amount of energy that pushes the universe apart instead of pulling it together.
In a universe with only this energy, distances grow exponentially in time.
Observations of distant supernovae from the late 1990s showed that the expansion of our universe is speeding up, as a positive cosmological constant predicts.
