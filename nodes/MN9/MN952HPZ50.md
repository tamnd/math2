## Statement

Consider the [[XY model|J53AT590R9]] on $\Z^2$ with nearest-neighbour coupling $J = 1$ at inverse temperature $\beta \ge 0$.
Let $\langle \cdot \rangle_\beta$ be an infinite-volume [[Gibbs state|DG9F3W6MZM]] obtained as a limit of finite-box Gibbs states.
Let $G_\beta(x) = \langle \cos(\theta_0 - \theta_x) \rangle_\beta$ be the spin-spin [[correlation function|ENWMTRJRND]] for $x \in \Z^2$.

1. There is $\beta_0 > 0$ such that for every $\beta < \beta_0$ there are constants $C, m > 0$ with $G_\beta(x) \le C e^{-m \abs{x}}$ for all $x$.
2. For every $\beta$, $G_\beta(x) \to 0$ as $\abs{x} \to \infty$.
3. (Fröhlich–Spencer, 1981) There is $\beta_1$ such that for every $\beta > \beta_1$ there are constants $C, \eta > 0$ with $G_\beta(x) \ge C \abs{x}^{-\eta}$ for all $x \ne 0$.

So the correlations decay exponentially at high temperature and only like a power of the distance at low temperature.
At no temperature is there spontaneous magnetization.

## Remarks

Part 1 follows from a high-temperature expansion of $e^{\beta \cos(\theta_x - \theta_y)}$.
Part 2 is a form of the [[Mermin–Wagner theorem|5C0QQFXSD7]]: slow spin waves cost little energy in two dimensions and destroy long-range order.
Part 3 is the hard part.
Fröhlich and Spencer rewrite the model as a Coulomb gas of vortices, and a multiscale analysis shows that at low temperature vortices bind into neutral pairs.
Berezinskii and, independently, Kosterlitz and Thouless predicted this picture in the early 1970s, with vortex-antivortex unbinding as the mechanism of the transition.
The spin-wave approximation predicts the decay $G_\beta(x) \approx \abs{x}^{-1/(2\pi\beta)}$ at low temperature.
The dimension matters: on $\Z^d$ with $d \ge 3$ the XY model has true long-range order at low temperature.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a flat sheet of tiny compass needles, order at long distances is impossible at any temperature.
Still, something changes as the sheet is cooled.
When hot, the needles forget each other quickly, with a correlation that dies off exponentially.
When cold, they remember each other over long distances, with a correlation that dies off only like a power.
The change happens when swirling defects called vortices stop roaming freely and pair up.

### L2 human

This is the standard example of a phase transition without symmetry breaking.
It shows that the Mermin–Wagner theorem does not rule out a transition, only a nonzero magnetization.
The Fröhlich–Spencer proof uses a duality to an integer-valued height model and a Coulomb gas, and it is a landmark of rigorous renormalization.
The same mechanism appears in thin superfluid films and in the roughening transition of crystal surfaces.
