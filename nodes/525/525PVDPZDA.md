## Summary

Additive and combinatorial number theory is the study of sets of integers, and of subsets of abelian groups, through their sums.
Its central object is the sumset A + B, the set of all sums a + b with a in A and b in B.
The area asks which integers are sums of elements of a given set, how large a sumset must be, and what structure a set with few sums has.
Goldbach's letter to Euler of 1742 and Waring's book of 1770 started the classical theory, and the circle method of Hardy and Littlewood in the 1920s became its main tool.
This topic collects the additive and combinatorial number theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Elementary number theory, analytic number theory, enumerative combinatorics and extremal and probabilistic combinatorics have their own topics.
