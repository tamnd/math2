## Summary

Stellar structure is the study of how pressure, density, temperature and luminosity vary inside a star.
In the simplest model the star is a static, spherically symmetric ball of gas.
Then these quantities obey a system of four [[ordinary differential equations|0NT64JV96A]] in the radius.
The system expresses [[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]], conservation of mass, conservation of energy and the transport of energy by radiation or convection.
Its solutions, called stellar models, explain the main sequence, the [[mass–luminosity relation|AN3ZQVNG7Z]] and the evolution of stars.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A star is a ball of hot gas that is squeezed by its own gravity and held up by the pressure of the gas.
The centre is dense and very hot, and the surface is thin and much cooler.
Energy made by nuclear reactions near the centre leaks outward, carried by light or by rising and sinking gas.
For the Sun, the centre is at about fifteen million kelvin and the surface at under six thousand kelvin.
The equations of stellar structure describe how all these quantities change from the centre to the surface.
