## Summary

The Adams operations $\psi^k$, for $k \ge 1$, are ring endomorphisms of a [[lambda-ring|E2KA3XP47F]], in particular of K-groups.
They are built from the exterior power operations by Newton's formula, so that $\psi^k(L) = L^k$ for the class of a line bundle.
Adams introduced them in topological K-theory and used them in his solution of the [[vector fields on spheres|3ES79NWRS7]] problem.
In algebraic K-theory their eigenspaces split rational K-groups into pieces of fixed weight.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The exterior powers $\lambda^n$ do not respect sums: $\lambda^2(x + y)$ has a cross term $xy$.
The Adams operation $\psi^k$ is the combination of exterior powers that behaves like "take the $k$-th power of each eigenvalue".
If a vector bundle splits as a sum of line bundles $L_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus L_r$, then $\psi^k$ of it is $L_1^{k} + \cdots + L_r^{k}$.
So $\psi^k$ is additive and multiplicative, which makes it far easier to compute with than $\lambda^k$.
For example $\psi^2(x) = x^2 - 2 \lambda^2(x)$.
