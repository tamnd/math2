## Summary

A Poisson algebra is a vector space with two products, an associative product and a Lie bracket.
They are linked by one rule: bracketing with a fixed element is a [[derivation|3J89REV9WQ]] of the associative product.
The main examples are algebras of smooth functions on a [[Poisson manifold|A2KQ49Y4NF]], in particular on a symplectic manifold, with the [[Poisson bracket|2S0MXDT4YH]].
Poisson algebras are the algebraic setting of classical Hamiltonian mechanics, and deformation quantization turns them into noncommutative associative algebras.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In classical mechanics, observables such as position, momentum and energy are functions on phase space.
They can be multiplied as functions, and they also have a Poisson bracket that describes how one quantity changes along the flow of another.
A Poisson algebra keeps the rules that link these two operations.
For functions $f, g$ of $(x, y)$ in the plane, the bracket $\set{f, g} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial g}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}$ gives the standard example.
