## Definition

Let an object have [[apparent magnitude|R8BRMEQ4DN]] $m$ and [[absolute magnitude|D7EAZ5F0YT]] $M$ in the same passband.
The *distance modulus* of the object is $\mu = m - M$.

## Remarks

If no light is absorbed on the way, the definition of $M$ gives
$$\mu = 5 \log_{10} \frac{d}{10\ \mathrm{pc}}, \qquad d = 10^{\mu/5 + 1}\ \mathrm{pc}.$$
With interstellar extinction $A$ in magnitudes, $m - M = 5\log_{10}(d / 10\ \mathrm{pc}) + A$.
Example: the Large Magellanic Cloud has $\mu \approx 18.5$, so $d \approx 10^{4.7}$ pc $\approx 50$ kpc.
Non-example: the difference of the apparent magnitudes of two stars is not a distance modulus; it compares two objects, not one object with itself at $10$ pc.
The symbol $\mu$ is the usual notation, though it also denotes proper motion.
