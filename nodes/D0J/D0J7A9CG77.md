## Example

Take a cart of mass $M > 0$ that moves on a horizontal line, with position $x(t)$.
A massless rigid rod of length $\ell > 0$ is hinged on the cart and carries a point mass $m > 0$ at its free end.
Let $\theta(t)$ be the angle of the rod from the upward vertical, and let $g > 0$ be the acceleration of gravity.
The only input is a horizontal force $u(t)$ on the cart.
The Lagrangian $L = \frac{1}{2} M \dot x^2 + \frac{1}{2} m \left( \dot x^2 + 2 \ell \dot x \dot\theta \cos\theta + \ell^2 \dot\theta^2 \right) - m g \ell \cos\theta$ gives the equations of motion
$$(M + m)\, \ddot x + m \ell \left( \ddot\theta \cos\theta - \dot\theta^2 \sin\theta \right) = u,$$
$$\ell\, \ddot\theta + \ddot x \cos\theta - g \sin\theta = 0.$$
There are two degrees of freedom, $x$ and $\theta$, and one input, so the cart-pole is an [[underactuated system|RJSM2ZNV61]].

The upright position $\theta = 0$, $\dot\theta = 0$, $\dot x = 0$ with $u = 0$ is an equilibrium.
The [[linearisation|09ZWYRQ41J]] at it is $(M + m) \ddot x + m \ell \ddot\theta = u$ and $\ell \ddot\theta + \ddot x = g \theta$.
Eliminating $\ddot x$ gives
$$\ddot\theta = a\, \theta - b\, u, \qquad a = \frac{(M + m) g}{M \ell}, \quad b = \frac{1}{M \ell}.$$
With $u = 0$ the solutions include $\theta(t) = \theta_0 e^{\sqrt{a}\, t}$, so the upright position is not [[Lyapunov stable|0F2SYCKDT2]].
With the feedback $u = k_1 \theta + k_2 \dot\theta$, the angle obeys $\ddot\theta + b k_2 \dot\theta + (b k_1 - a) \theta = 0$.
If $k_2 > 0$ and $k_1 > a / b = (M + m) g$, both coefficients are positive, so both roots of $s^2 + b k_2 s + (b k_1 - a)$ have negative real part and $\theta(t) \to 0$.

## Remarks

This feedback keeps the rod upright in the linearised model, but the cart may drift.
The full linearised system in the state $(x, \dot x, \theta, \dot\theta)$ is [[controllable|A9WFM77Z4P]].
So a linear feedback on all four state variables makes the upright equilibrium asymptotically stable for the linearised system, and locally for the nonlinear one.
The simplest version fixes the hinge and applies a torque $\tau$ at it; then $m \ell^2 \ddot\theta = m g \ell \sin\theta + \tau$, which is the [[pendulum equation|0SHPXM2HWH]] turned upside down.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Balancing a broom upright on the palm of your hand is an inverted pendulum.
Left alone, the broom falls, because any small tilt makes gravity pull it further away from the vertical.
By moving the hand under the broom in the direction of the tilt, you can keep it up.
Control engineers use the pendulum on a cart as a standard test: one motor pushing the cart must keep both the cart and the rod in check.
