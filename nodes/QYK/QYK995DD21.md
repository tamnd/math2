## Summary

Finite-time thermodynamics studies thermodynamic processes and cycles that must be completed in a finite time.
Heat and matter then flow at finite rates, driven by finite differences of temperature or chemical potential, and this causes losses.
It asks for the best performance under such rate constraints, for example the largest power of a [[heat engine|S7XFW04T3V]].
Its basic model is the endoreversible engine, which leads to the [[Curzon–Ahlborn efficiency|G30CMRM06T]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Classical thermodynamics says what is possible if one waits forever.
A Carnot engine is the most efficient engine, but it would run so slowly that it gives almost no power.
Finite-time thermodynamics puts the clock back in: heat needs a temperature gap to flow quickly, and that gap wastes some of the available work.
For example, an engine run for maximum power between $1200$ K and $300$ K reaches the efficiency $0.5$ in the simplest model, not the Carnot value $0.75$.
