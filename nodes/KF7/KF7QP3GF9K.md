## Summary

The Taylor microscale is a length scale of a [[turbulent|VY307F8Z0S]] flow defined from the ratio of the velocity fluctuations to their spatial derivatives.
It is the curvature scale of the velocity correlation function at zero separation.
It lies between the size of the large eddies and the [[Kolmogorov length|NYRS1BJKRQ]], and it is not the size of any particular eddies.
The Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}_\lambda$ built with it is the standard way to state how turbulent a flow is in experiments and simulations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Measure the wind speed at two nearby points: if they are very close, the readings almost agree, and the agreement falls off as the points move apart.
For small separations, the agreement drops off like a parabola, and the Taylor microscale is the distance that sets the width of that parabola.
It was introduced by G. I. Taylor in the 1930s.
It is convenient because it can be estimated from measurements of velocity and its gradient at a single point.
