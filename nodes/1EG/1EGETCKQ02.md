## Summary

An overlapping generations model is an economic model in discrete time in which agents live for finitely many periods, usually two.
A new generation is born every period.
At each date, young and old agents live side by side and trade with each other.
The best-known versions are those of Paul Samuelson and Peter Diamond.
These models show that a [[competitive equilibrium|YTBPB52K27]] with infinitely many agents and dates need not be [[Pareto efficient|JREVYHFY4T]].
They are used to study pensions, public debt and money.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a society where everyone lives two periods: young, then old.
The young work and save; the old live on their savings.
Each period the old generation leaves and a new young one arrives, so the population is always a mix of two generations.
The savings of the young become the capital that the next young generation works with.
For example, with log utility and patience factor $\beta = 1$, each young person saves exactly half of the wage.
