## Summary

Quantum superposition is the principle that a normalised linear combination of state vectors of a quantum system is again a state vector of the system.
It holds because pure [[quantum states|4NQN47A92F]] are rays in a complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]], a vector space.
The relative phases of the coefficients matter and produce interference, while an overall phase does not.
It is the quantum form of the [[superposition principle|0F2VBPXNP9]] for linear equations, here the linear Schrödinger equation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If a system can be in state $A$ and can be in state $B$, it can also be in a combination of both.
Such a combination is not "$A$ or $B$, we do not know which": it is a new state with its own predictions.
For a spin, the equal combination $(\text{up} + \text{down})/\sqrt{2}$ gives "up" or "down" half of the time in a vertical measurement.
But in a horizontal measurement it gives the same result every time, which no random choice between up and down can do.
