## Summary

A tempered representation is an irreducible [[unitary representation|74BPYJNZTM]] of a locally compact group that is weakly contained in the [[left regular representation|PR8C073VWY]].
For a semisimple Lie group this means that its matrix coefficients are almost square integrable.
For a unimodular group of type I, the tempered representations are those needed to decompose $L^2(G)$.
They form the support of the [[Plancherel measure|QESEAKKABM]].
In the Langlands classification every irreducible admissible representation of a real reductive group is built from tempered ones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The regular representation of $G$ on $L^2(G)$ contains, in a continuous sense, a whole family of irreducible pieces; these pieces are the tempered representations.
A representation is tempered when its matrix coefficients decay at infinity almost as fast as square integrable functions.
For $\mathrm{SL}(2, \R)$ the discrete series and the unitary principal series are tempered.
The trivial representation is not: its matrix coefficient is the constant $1$, which does not decay at all.
