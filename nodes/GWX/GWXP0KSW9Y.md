## Summary

A universality class is a set of critical points that share the same [[critical exponents|SQ41AZGZ2P]] and the same scaling functions.
Very different systems, such as a uniaxial magnet and a fluid at its liquid–gas critical point, can belong to the same class.
The class depends only on a few features: the dimension of space, the symmetry of the order parameter, and whether the interactions are short-range.
In the [[renormalization group|5XTSCZCF7Z]] picture, a universality class corresponds to a fixed point and its basin of attraction.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Near a critical point the small details of a material stop mattering.
What counts is the large-scale picture, and only a few broad features shape it.
So iron magnets, a fluid near its critical point and an alloy can show exactly the same power laws.
All the systems that behave alike in this way form one universality class.
For example, the Ising model on the square lattice and on the triangular lattice have the same exponent $\beta = 1/8$, so they lie in one class.
