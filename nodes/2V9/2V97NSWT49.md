## Summary

The Fourier series of a square integrable function on a [[compact group|EVHCE5VR6Y]] expands it along the irreducible [[unitary representations|74BPYJNZTM]] of the group.
Its coefficients are operators, one for each irreducible representation, and its terms are combinations of [[matrix coefficients|J85Q7QGGHS]].
By the [[Peter–Weyl theorem|6VC9QE39XH]] the series converges to the function in $L^2$.
For the circle group it is the classical [[Fourier series|G12KXDHPPS]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

On the circle, a periodic function is a sum of waves $e^{i n \theta}$, each with a complex coefficient.
On a compact group such as the rotation group $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, the waves are replaced by irreducible representations, which can be matrices of any size.
The coefficient for a representation of dimension $d$ is a $d \times d$ matrix.
The function is then recovered as a sum over all irreducible representations, just as a periodic function is recovered from its Fourier series.
