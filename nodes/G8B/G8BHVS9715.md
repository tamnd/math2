## Statement

Fix concept names $A_1, A_2, \dots$ and role names $r_1, \dots, r_n$.
Let $\mathcal{L}_n$ be the [[multimodal|ERG3KAQWYF]] language with atoms $p_1, p_2, \dots$, primitive connectives $\top, \bot, \neg, \wedge, \vee$, and primitive modalities $\Diamond_i$ and $\Box_i$ for $i \le n$; $\varphi \to \psi$ abbreviates $\neg\varphi \vee \psi$.
Define $\tau$ from [[ALC|TXQ80B8ZSA]] concepts to $\mathcal{L}_n$ by
$$
\tau(A_j) = p_j,\ \tau(\top) = \top,\ \tau(\bot) = \bot,\ \tau(\neg C) = \neg\tau(C),
$$
$$
\tau(C \sqcap D) = \tau(C) \wedge \tau(D),\ \tau(C \sqcup D) = \tau(C) \vee \tau(D),\ \tau(\exists r_i.C) = \Diamond_i \tau(C),\ \tau(\forall r_i.C) = \Box_i \tau(C).
$$
To an interpretation $\mathcal{I}$ assign the [[Kripke model|6BQ71KQ6QT]] $M_{\mathcal{I}} = (\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}, r_1^{\mathcal{I}}, \dots, r_n^{\mathcal{I}}, V)$ with $V(p_j) = A_j^{\mathcal{I}}$.

1. For every concept $C$, every interpretation $\mathcal{I}$ and every $d \in \Delta^{\mathcal{I}}$: $d \in C^{\mathcal{I}}$ if and only if $M_{\mathcal{I}}, d \models \tau(C)$.
2. Every Kripke model with $n$ relations and nonempty domain is $M_{\mathcal{I}}$ for exactly one interpretation $\mathcal{I}$ of these names, and $\tau$ is a bijection from concepts onto $\mathcal{L}_n$.
3. Hence $C$ is satisfiable iff $\tau(C)$ is satisfiable in some Kripke model, and $\models C \sqsubseteq D$ iff $\tau(C) \to \tau(D)$ is valid, iff $\mathsf{K}_n \vdash \tau(C) \to \tau(D)$.

The proof is in [[the proof of the correspondence|SGNK5FWABD]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. The translation of $\exists r_i.C$ uses the same relation $r_i^{\mathcal{I}}$ as the modality $\Diamond_i$. With a single modality for several roles, $\exists r_1.A \sqcap \forall r_2.\neg A$ would translate to the unsatisfiable $\Diamond p \wedge \Box\neg p$, while the concept is satisfiable.
2. The concept constructors must be those of $\mathcal{ALC}$. Number restrictions such as ${\ge} 2\, r.\top$ have no translation, because they are not invariant under bisimulation, see [[number restrictions are not ALC concepts|5KQCT1HMC3]].
3. Part 3 uses the completeness of $\mathsf{K}_n$ for the class of all Kripke models; without it one gets only the semantic statement.

### Consequences

1. Concept satisfiability in $\mathcal{ALC}$ has the same complexity as satisfiability in $\mathsf{K}_n$, PSPACE-complete, since $\tau$ and its inverse take linear time.
2. $\mathcal{ALC}$ has the finite model property and the tree model property, because $\mathsf{K}_n$ has them.
3. Tableau calculi for $\mathsf{K}_n$ are decision procedures for $\mathcal{ALC}$, and conversely.
4. A concept $C$ is satisfiable with respect to a TBox $\mathcal{T}$ iff $\tau(C)$ is satisfiable in a model where every $\tau(D) \to \tau(E)$ with $D \sqsubseteq E \in \mathcal{T}$ holds at every world.

### Converse

Part 2 is the converse direction: every $\mathsf{K}_n$ formula is $\tau(C)$ for a unique concept $C$, and every Kripke model comes from an interpretation.
So $\mathcal{ALC}$ and $\mathsf{K}_n$ have the same expressive power over the same structures.

### Generalizations

1. Inverse roles correspond to converse modalities, as in tense logic, and nominals correspond to hybrid logic.
2. Role hierarchies and transitive roles correspond to inclusion and $\mathsf{K4}$ axioms on the modal frames.
3. ABox assertions correspond to formulas at named worlds, as in hybrid logic with nominals and the satisfaction operator.

## History

Schild proved the correspondence in 1991 in "A correspondence theory for terminological logics".
In the same year Schmidt-Schauß and Smolka proved PSPACE-completeness for $\mathcal{ALC}$ directly, which matches, through the correspondence, Ladner's earlier result for $\mathsf{K}$.
The correspondence led description logic research to adopt the methods of modal logic, and modal logicians to study logics with features from description logic.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A concept such as "has a child who is a doctor" says that some object linked by "has child" is a doctor.
A modal formula "possibly $p$" says that some world linked by the accessibility relation makes $p$ true.
These are the same idea with different words: objects are worlds and roles are accessibility relations.
So a question about concepts is a question about modal formulas.

### L2 human

1. Translate each concept name to an atom and each role to its own modality.
2. Translate $\exists r.C$ to $\Diamond_r$ and $\forall r.C$ to $\Box_r$.
3. Read the interpretation as a Kripke model with the same domain and relations.
4. Check by induction on concepts that membership in $C^{\mathcal{I}}$ is truth of $\tau(C)$.

The typical use is to transfer a decidability or complexity result between the two areas.
A trap: TBox reasoning corresponds to global consequence, not to local validity, and this changes the complexity from PSPACE to EXPTIME.
