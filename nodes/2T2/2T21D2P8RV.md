## Example

Let $W = \{w, u, v\}$ with $R = \{(w, u), (w, v)\}$.
So $R[w] = \{u, v\}$ and $R[u] = R[v] = \emptyset$.
Let $S_w = \{(u, u), (v, v), (u, v)\}$, and let $S_u$ and $S_v$ be empty.
Let $p$ be true only at $u$, and $q$ true only at $v$.
We first check that $(W, R, \{S_x\})$ is a Veltman frame, with the conditions of [[interpretability logic|BGKZTSQN3W]].

1. $W$ is finite and non-empty.
2. $R$ is irreflexive: none of the pairs $(w, w)$, $(u, u)$, $(v, v)$ lies in $R$.
3. $R$ is [[transitive|0GJZW9JFZQ]]: a chain $xRyRz$ needs $y \in \{u, v\}$ with an $R$-successor, and neither $u$ nor $v$ has one. So there is nothing to check.
4. $S_w$ is a relation on $R[w] = \{u, v\}$, and it contains $(u, u)$ and $(v, v)$, so it is reflexive on $R[w]$.
5. $S_w$ is transitive: the compositions are $(u, u)(u, v) = (u, v)$, $(u, v)(v, v) = (u, v)$, and the compositions with an identity pair, which give back the other pair; all lie in $S_w$.
6. $S_u$ and $S_v$ are relations on $R[u] = R[v] = \emptyset$, so the empty relation is reflexive and transitive there.
7. The condition "$xRyRz$ implies $y S_x z$" holds, because by step 3 there is no chain $xRyRz$.

Now we compute two formulas at $w$.

8. $w \Vdash p \rhd q$: the only $R$-successor of $w$ where $p$ holds is $u$. We have $u S_w v$ and $v \Vdash q$, so $u$ has the needed partner.
9. $w \nVdash \square(p \to q)$: we have $wRu$, $u \Vdash p$ and $u \nVdash q$, so $u \nVdash p \to q$.

So $p \rhd q \to \square(p \to q)$ fails at $w$, and by the soundness of $\mathsf{IL}$ for Veltman models it is not a theorem of $\mathsf{IL}$.

## Remarks

The model also satisfies the frame conditions of $\mathsf{ILM}$ and $\mathsf{ILP}$.

- The condition of M, "$x S_y z R t$ implies $x R t$", holds: the middle world $z$ of $x S_w z$ lies in $\{u, v\}$, which has no $R$-successors.
- The condition of P, "$y R y' R x S_y z$ implies $x S_{y'} z$", holds: there is no chain $y R y' R x$.

So $p \rhd q \to \square(p \to q)$ is not a theorem of $\mathsf{ILM}$ or $\mathsf{ILP}$ either.
The example shows that the converse of axiom J1 fails: interpretability is weaker than provable implication.
In arithmetic this matches the fact that $T + A$ can interpret $T + B$ without proving $B$.
For example, $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ interprets $\mathsf{PA} + \neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, but it does not prove $\neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, because it is a consistent theory.
The interpretation comes from J5 with $A = \neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, since the formalized second incompleteness theorem gives $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA}) \to \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA} + A)$.

If we remove the pair $(u, v)$ from $S_w$, then $w \nVdash p \rhd q$, since the only $S_w$-partner of $u$ is $u$, where $q$ fails.
If we add $(v, u)$ to $S_w$, the frame stays a Veltman frame, and $w \Vdash q \rhd p$ holds as well.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a root world $w$ that looks at two worlds $u$ and $v$.
At $u$ the statement $p$ holds, and at $v$ the statement $q$ holds.
From $w$, the world $u$ is allowed to "jump" to $v$.
"$p$ interprets $q$" holds at $w$, because every $p$-world seen from $w$ can jump to a $q$-world.
But "$p$ implies $q$ in every seen world" fails, because at $u$ we have $p$ without $q$.

### L2 human

We evaluate J3 with $r = q$ at $w$, as a further check of the model.

1. $w \Vdash p \rhd q$ by step 8.
2. $w \Vdash q \rhd q$: the only successor where $q$ holds is $v$, and $v S_w v$.
3. $w \Vdash (p \vee q) \rhd q$: the successors with $p \vee q$ are $u$ and $v$, with partners $v$ and $v$.

So J3 holds at $w$ in this instance, as soundness says it must.
The typical use of such a model is to separate two formulas that look similar.
A trap: the jump relation $S_w$ belongs to the world $w$; a different root world could have a different relation on its successors.
