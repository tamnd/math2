## Summary

The Ross–Macdonald model describes the transmission of malaria between humans and mosquitoes by two coupled differential equations.
It tracks the infected fraction of humans and the infected fraction of mosquitoes.
Ronald Ross introduced it in the early twentieth century, and George Macdonald extended it in the 1950s.
Its threshold quantity shows that reducing mosquito numbers or mosquito survival can stop transmission without killing every mosquito.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Malaria does not pass from person to person; it needs a mosquito in between.
A mosquito bites an infected person and becomes infected, and later it bites a healthy person and passes the parasite on.
So the model has two groups, humans and mosquitoes, and each group infects only the other.
Because the mosquito bites twice in one cycle, the biting rate appears squared in the threshold.
For example, halving the biting rate cuts the threshold quantity by a factor of $4$.
