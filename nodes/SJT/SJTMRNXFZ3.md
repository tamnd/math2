## Summary

The potential temperature of a parcel of air is the temperature it would have at a reference pressure, usually $1000$ hPa.
The parcel is imagined to be brought there without exchange of heat.
It removes the cooling by expansion and the warming by compression that a parcel feels when it rises or sinks.
It is conserved in dry adiabatic motion, so it labels air parcels and measures the static stability of the atmosphere.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air that rises expands and cools, and air that sinks is compressed and warms, even when no heat is added or removed.
So the plain temperature is a poor label for a parcel of air that moves up and down.
The potential temperature fixes this: it is how warm the air would be if it were moved to sea-level pressure.
Air at $500$ hPa with temperature $253$ K has a potential temperature of about $309$ kelvin.
So in this sense it is warmer than air at $1000$ hPa with temperature $300$ K.
When the potential temperature increases with height, the atmosphere is stable.
Then a lifted parcel sinks back.
