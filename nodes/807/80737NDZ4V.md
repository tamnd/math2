## Summary

The Lamé parameters $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are the two constants that describe a homogeneous isotropic linear elastic material.
In terms of them the stress–strain law reads $\sigma = \lambda (\tr \varepsilon) I + 2\mu \varepsilon$.
The second parameter $\mu$ is the [[shear modulus|J2GWW1NPTC]].
Every other isotropic elastic constant, such as [[Young's modulus|T8ENJX1905]] or [[Poisson's ratio|NWYZ9QBA84]], is a function of $\lambda$ and $\mu$.
They are the natural constants in the [[Lamé equations|GQ3K59MRJP]] for the displacement.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An isotropic material behaves the same in every direction, like glass or most metals on a large scale.
Its elastic response is fixed by two numbers.
One of them, $\mu$, tells how hard it is to shear the material, as when sliding the top of a block sideways.
The other, $\lambda$, adds the extra resistance to changing volume.
For steel, $\mu$ is about $80$ GPa and $\lambda$ is about $120$ GPa.
