## Definition

Let a homogeneous lossless medium fill the half-space $z > 0$, and let $k > 0$ be the wavenumber of free waves of angular frequency $\omega$ in it.
Let $A \neq 0$ be a constant, $\beta > k$ real, and $\kappa = \sqrt{\beta^2 - k^2} > 0$.
Consider the function
$$u(x, y, z, t) = A \, e^{i(\beta x - \omega t)} \, e^{-\kappa z}.$$
It solves the Helmholtz equation $\nabla^2 u + k^2 u = 0$, and it is called an *evanescent wave* in this region.
An *evanescent field* is a superposition of such waves.
More generally, it is a field near a surface that oscillates along the surface and decays exponentially away from it.

## Remarks

Check: substituting gives $-\beta^2 + \kappa^2 + k^2 = 0$, which holds by the choice of $\kappa$.
The wave vector is formally the complex vector $(\beta, 0, i\kappa)$, whose "length squared" $\beta^2 - \kappa^2$ equals $k^2$.
Example: consider [[total internal reflection|GS6CP71X4K]] from index $n_1$ to $n_2$ at angle $\theta_i$ above the critical angle, with vacuum wavenumber $k_0$.
The field in medium $2$ has $\beta = n_1 k_0 \sin\theta_i > n_2 k_0 = k$ and decay rate $\kappa = k_0\sqrt{n_1^2 \sin^2\theta_i - n_2^2}$.
Non-example: if $0 \le \beta < k$, the same ansatz needs $\kappa$ purely imaginary, and $u$ is a propagating [[plane wave|NM024G378A]] that carries power away from the surface.
