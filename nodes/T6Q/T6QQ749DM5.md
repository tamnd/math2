## Summary

This topic is a part of [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].
It covers the standard false proofs, the logical errors that occur in proofs, and famous proofs that were wrong.
Each false proof is given with the exact step that fails, the reason, and the correct rule.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Mathematical fallacies|S2BW0FDHV8]]: the idea of an invalid proof and of a howler.
2. [[Division by zero fallacies|V7601AG5G3]]: cancelling a factor that is zero.
3. [[Fallacies with roots and powers|75ZEQ21AG2]]: rules for roots, powers and logarithms outside their range.
4. [[Fallacies with infinite series and integrals|KDXKVT5K1E]]: finite rules used for limits.
5. [[Geometric fallacies|5CJ4KD4D96]]: facts read from a wrong figure.
6. [[Circular reasoning|JPHE53PABT]]: a proof that uses its own conclusion.
7. [[Affirming the consequent and denying the antecedent|J7KFCFQRBT]]: invalid forms of conditional reasoning.
8. [[Proof by example|ZX6ERT3SW7]]: a general claim from finitely many cases.
9. [[Quantifier shift fallacy|CRW82NARQV]]: exchanging "for all" and "there exists".
10. [[Non-reversible steps in solving equations|641ZP4RA5S]]: extraneous solutions and implication versus equivalence.
11. [[Famous incorrect proofs|1CV4HTA6C8]]: accepted proofs that turned out to be wrong.
12. [[Proof by intimidation and handwaving|D8A1K052XV]]: informal names for invalid proof styles.

### Prerequisites

The reader should know the [[basic proof methods|958EF46EYJ]] and the [[language of mathematical statements|WMY13MXY3W]].
The errors in proofs by induction, such as the [[all horses are the same colour|AJ2FDJJGTH]] paradox, are treated with [[induction and well-ordering as proof methods|EEH2F76CYQ]].
