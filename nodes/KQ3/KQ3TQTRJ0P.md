## Definition

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space with independent standard [[Wiener processes|22735FGJV6]] $W$ in $\R^d$ and $V$ in $\R^m$.
Let the signal $X$ in $\R^n$ and the observation $Y$ in $\R^m$ solve the [[stochastic differential equations|B219G9SHJA]]
$$dX_t = f(X_t) \, dt + \sigma(X_t) \, dW_t, \qquad dY_t = h(X_t) \, dt + dV_t, \qquad Y_0 = 0.$$
Here $f \colon \R^n \to \R^n$ and $\sigma \colon \R^n \to \R^{n \times d}$ are globally Lipschitz.
The map $h \colon \R^n \to \R^m$ is bounded and continuous, and $X_0$ is independent of $W$ and $V$.
Let $\mathcal{Y}_t$ be the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $Y_s$ for $s \le t$.
For a bounded measurable $\varphi$, let $\pi_t(\varphi) = E[\varphi(X_t) \mid \mathcal{Y}_t]$ be the [[conditional expectation|0TTDGTEFMN]].
Let $\mathcal{L}$ be the [[generator|VPA41PWRR1]] of $X$:
$$\mathcal{L}\varphi = \sum_{i} f_i \, \partial_i \varphi + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i,j} (\sigma \sigma^{T})_{ij} \, \partial_i \partial_j \varphi.$$
The *Kushner equation* (or Kushner–Stratonovich equation) is the equation
$$d\pi_t(\varphi) = \pi_t(\mathcal{L}\varphi) \, dt + \big(\pi_t(h \varphi) - \pi_t(h) \, \pi_t(\varphi)\big)^{T} \big(dY_t - \pi_t(h) \, dt\big),$$
required for every twice continuously differentiable $\varphi$ with compact support.
Here $\pi_t(h)$ and $\pi_t(h\varphi)$ are taken componentwise.

## Remarks

Under the hypotheses above, the conditional law $\pi_t$ of $X_t$ given $\mathcal{Y}_t$ satisfies the Kushner equation.
The process $I_t = Y_t - \int_0^t \pi_s(h) \, ds$ is the innovation process; it is a standard Wiener process with respect to $(\mathcal{Y}_t)$.
When $\pi_t$ has a smooth density $p_t$, the equation takes the form
$$dp_t = \mathcal{L}^{*} p_t \, dt + p_t \, \big(h - \pi_t(h)\big)^{T} \big(dY_t - \pi_t(h) \, dt\big),$$
where $\mathcal{L}^{*}$ is the formal adjoint of $\mathcal{L}$, the Fokker–Planck operator.
Example: if $h = 0$, the observations carry no information, the correction term vanishes, and $\pi_t$ is the law of $X_t$; the equation becomes the weak form of the [[Fokker–Planck equation|FVQWR7NS00]].
Non-example: the [[Zakai equation|FQ8TVSR74H]] $d\rho_t(\varphi) = \rho_t(\mathcal{L}\varphi) \, dt + \rho_t(h\varphi)^{T} dY_t$ is linear and is solved by an unnormalized conditional measure $\rho_t$, which is not a probability measure in general.
Normalizing it, $\pi_t(\varphi) = \rho_t(\varphi)/\rho_t(1)$, gives the solution of the Kushner equation.
For linear $f$ and $h$, constant $\sigma$ and Gaussian $X_0$, the law $\pi_t$ is Gaussian, and its mean and covariance follow the Kalman–Bucy filter.
