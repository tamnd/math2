## Summary

The thermal expansion coefficient of a substance is the relative change of its volume per unit increase of temperature at constant pressure.
It is usually written $\alpha$ or $\beta$.
It measures how strongly a material expands when heated, and it appears in the relation between the heat capacities and in the Joule–Thomson coefficient.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Most things get a little bigger when they get warmer.
The thermal expansion coefficient tells by what fraction the volume grows for each degree of warming, with the pressure kept fixed.
For a gas near room temperature it is about $1/300$ per kelvin, much more than for a liquid or a solid.
Water is unusual: between $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ it shrinks when heated, so its coefficient is negative there.
