## Definition

Let a charged particle move past another charged particle and be accelerated by its Coulomb field.
Assume the two are not bound before or after the encounter.
*Bremsstrahlung* is the [[electromagnetic radiation|AC6R5A3M1C]] emitted by the particle because of this acceleration.
*Thermal bremsstrahlung* is the bremsstrahlung of a gas of free electrons and ions.
In it the electrons have a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution of velocities at a temperature $T$.

## Remarks

Example: let an electron of charge $-e$ and mass $m_e$ pass an ion of charge $Ze$ at distance $b$.
Near closest approach its acceleration has size about $Z e^2 / (4\pi\varepsilon_0 m_e b^2)$, where $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity.
By the [[Larmor formula|EHYKJXF899]] it radiates power proportional to the square of this acceleration.
Non-example: radiation from an electron captured by an ion into a bound state is recombination radiation (free-bound), not bremsstrahlung, because the electron is bound after the event.
Consider thermal bremsstrahlung of electrons of density $n_e$ and ions of density $n_i$ and charge $Ze$.
Its emissivity per unit frequency is proportional to $Z^2 n_e n_i T^{-1/2} e^{-h\nu/(k_B T)}$ times a slowly varying Gaunt factor.
Its total power per unit volume is proportional to $Z^2 n_e n_i T^{1/2}$.
Collisions between identical particles, such as electron–electron, give no dipole radiation, so in a non-relativistic plasma electron–ion encounters dominate.
