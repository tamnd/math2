## Example

In $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the dot product, apply the [[Gram-Schmidt process|A1Y41YZFB6]] to $v_1 = (1, 1, 0)$, $v_2 = (1, 0, 1)$ and $v_3 = (0, 1, 1)$.

1. $u_1 = v_1 = (1, 1, 0)$, and $u_1 \cdot u_1 = 2$.
2. $v_2 \cdot u_1 = 1$, so $u_2 = (1, 0, 1) - \frac12(1, 1, 0) = (\frac12, -\frac12, 1)$. Also $u_2 \cdot u_2 = \frac14 + \frac14 + 1 = \frac32$.
3. $v_3 \cdot u_1 = 1$ and $v_3 \cdot u_2 = 0 - \frac12 + 1 = \frac12$. So $u_3 = v_3 - \frac12 u_1 - \frac{1/2}{3/2} u_2 = v_3 - \frac12 u_1 - \frac13 u_2$.
4. Componentwise, $u_3 = (0 - \frac12 - \frac16,\ 1 - \frac12 + \frac16,\ 1 - 0 - \frac13) = (-\frac23, \frac23, \frac23)$, and $u_3 \cdot u_3 = \frac43$.
5. Orthogonality: $u_1 \cdot u_2 = \frac12 - \frac12 = 0$, $u_1 \cdot u_3 = -\frac23 + \frac23 = 0$ and $u_2 \cdot u_3 = -\frac13 - \frac13 + \frac23 = 0$.
6. Normalization: $e_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}(1, 1, 0)$, $e_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt6}(1, -1, 2)$ and $e_3 = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(-1, 1, 1)$.
7. Part 4 of the [[Gram-Schmidt theorem|0WXBGBTA3D]]: $v_1 \cdot e_1 = \sqrt2$, $v_2 \cdot e_2 = \frac{3}{\sqrt6} = \sqrt{\frac32}$ and $v_3 \cdot e_3 = \frac{2}{\sqrt3}$. These are $\norm{u_1}$, $\norm{u_2}$ and $\norm{u_3}$.
8. [[Coordinates|09DZ8CD1AK]] of $v_3$: $v_3 \cdot e_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}$, $v_3 \cdot e_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt6}$ and $v_3 \cdot e_3 = \frac{2}{\sqrt3}$. The squares add to $\frac12 + \frac16 + \frac43 = 2 = \norm{v_3}^2$.

## Remarks

The result is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$, and $e_1, e_2$ span the same plane as $v_1, v_2$.
Let $A$ be the matrix with columns $v_1, v_2, v_3$.
Then $\det A = -2$, and the [[Gram matrix|00SPJSDT3Z]] $G = A^\top A$ has $\det G = 4$.
This agrees with $\norm{u_1}^2\norm{u_2}^2\norm{u_3}^2 = 2 \cdot \frac32 \cdot \frac43 = 4$, the squared volume of the box spanned by $v_1, v_2, v_3$.
If we change the order to $v_3, v_2, v_1$, the first vector becomes $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(0, 1, 1)$, so the orthonormal basis changes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We start with three vectors that point in different directions but are not perpendicular.
We keep the first one, straighten the second against the first, and straighten the third against the first two.
Then we scale all three to length $1$.
The result is a new set of axes for space.

### L2 human

The same computation gives the QR decomposition $A = QR$.

1. $Q$ has the columns $e_1, e_2, e_3$ of step 6.
2. $R$ is upper triangular with entries $r_{ij} = v_j \cdot e_i$ for $i \leq j$.
3. The diagonal is $\sqrt2$, $\sqrt{3/2}$, $2/\sqrt3$, and its product is $2 = |\det A|$.
4. The entries above the diagonal are $r_{12} = v_2 \cdot e_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}$, $r_{13} = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}$ and $r_{23} = \frac{1}{\sqrt6}$.

The typical use is to find an orthonormal basis of a subspace given by a basis.
A trap: in step 3 the coefficient of $u_2$ is $\frac{v_3 \cdot u_2}{u_2 \cdot u_2} = \frac13$, not $v_3 \cdot u_2 = \frac12$.
