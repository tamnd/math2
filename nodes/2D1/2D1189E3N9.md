## Example

In $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the dot product, we apply the [[Gram-Schmidt process|A1Y41YZFB6]] to the basis $v_1 = (1, 1, 0)$, $v_2 = (1, 0, 1)$ and $v_3 = (0, 1, 1)$, and we check that the output is an orthonormal basis.

1. Keep the first vector: $u_1 = v_1 = (1, 1, 0)$, with $u_1 \cdot u_1 = 2$.
2. The second vector has $v_2 \cdot u_1 = 1$, so its coefficient is $\tfrac12$ and $u_2 = (1, 0, 1) - \tfrac12(1, 1, 0) = (\tfrac12, -\tfrac12, 1)$.
3. Its squared length is $u_2 \cdot u_2 = \tfrac14 + \tfrac14 + 1 = \tfrac32$.
4. The third vector has $v_3 \cdot u_1 = 1$ and $v_3 \cdot u_2 = 0 - \tfrac12 + 1 = \tfrac12$, so its coefficients are $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac{1/2}{3/2} = \tfrac13$.
5. Subtract both shadows entry by entry:

$$u_3 = v_3 - \tfrac12 u_1 - \tfrac13 u_2 = \left(0 - \tfrac12 - \tfrac16, \; 1 - \tfrac12 + \tfrac16, \; 1 - 0 - \tfrac13\right) = \left(-\tfrac23, \tfrac23, \tfrac23\right).$$

6. Its squared length is $u_3 \cdot u_3 = \tfrac49 + \tfrac49 + \tfrac49 = \tfrac43$.
7. Orthogonality: $u_1 \cdot u_2 = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12 = 0$, $u_1 \cdot u_3 = -\tfrac23 + \tfrac23 = 0$ and $u_2 \cdot u_3 = -\tfrac13 - \tfrac13 + \tfrac23 = 0$.
8. Normalization: dividing by $\sqrt2$, $\sqrt{3/2}$ and $\tfrac{2}{\sqrt3}$ gives

$$e_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1, 1, 0), \qquad e_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}(1, -1, 2), \qquad e_3 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(-1, 1, 1).$$

9. Check the lengths: $\tfrac{1 + 1}{2} = 1$, $\tfrac{1 + 1 + 4}{6} = 1$ and $\tfrac{1 + 1 + 1}{3} = 1$.
10. Part 4 of the [[Gram-Schmidt theorem|0WXBGBTA3D]]: $v_1 \cdot e_1 = \sqrt2$, $v_2 \cdot e_2 = \tfrac{3}{\sqrt6} = \sqrt{3/2}$ and $v_3 \cdot e_3 = \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3}$, which are $\norm{u_1}$, $\norm{u_2}$ and $\norm{u_3}$.
11. The [[coordinates|09DZ8CD1AK]] of $v_3$ are $v_3 \cdot e_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$, $v_3 \cdot e_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}$ and $v_3 \cdot e_3 = \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3}$, and their squares add up to $\tfrac12 + \tfrac16 + \tfrac43 = 2 = \norm{v_3}^2$.

So $(e_1, e_2, e_3)$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$, and $e_1, e_2$ span the same plane as $v_1, v_2$.

## Remarks

The lengths $\norm{u_j}$ also measure volume.
Let $A$ be the matrix with columns $v_1, v_2, v_3$; expanding along the first row gives $\det A = 1(0 - 1) - 1(1 - 0) + 0 = -2$.
The [[Gram matrix|00SPJSDT3Z]] $G = A^{\mathsf{T}} A$ therefore has $\det G = 4$, and this agrees with $\norm{u_1}^2\norm{u_2}^2\norm{u_3}^2 = 2 \cdot \tfrac32 \cdot \tfrac43 = 4$, the squared volume of the box spanned by $v_1, v_2, v_3$.

The output depends on the order of the input.
If we start from $v_3, v_2, v_1$, the first output vector is $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(0, 1, 1)$, which is not in the list above, so the orthonormal basis changes.
Only the first vector of each run is fixed in advance: it is the first input divided by its length.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We start with three arrows that point in different directions but are not at right angles.
We keep the first one, straighten the second one against the first, and straighten the third one against the first two.
Then we scale all three to length $1$.
The result is a new set of axes for space, at right angles to each other.

### L2 human

The same numbers give the QR decomposition $A = QR$ of the matrix $A$ with columns $v_1, v_2, v_3$.
The matrix $Q$ has the columns $e_1, e_2, e_3$ of step 8, and $R$ is upper triangular with entries $r_{ij} = v_j \cdot e_i$ for $i \leq j$:
$$R = \begin{pmatrix} \sqrt2 & \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \\ 0 & \sqrt{3/2} & \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6} \\ 0 & 0 & \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3} \end{pmatrix}.$$
Check one column: $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} e_1 + \sqrt{3/2}\, e_2 = \tfrac12(1, 1, 0) + \tfrac12(1, -1, 2) = (1, 0, 1) = v_2$.
The product of the diagonal entries is $\sqrt2 \cdot \sqrt{3/2} \cdot \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3} = 2 = \abs{\det A}$.
Watch out: in step 4 the coefficient of $u_2$ is $\frac{v_3 \cdot u_2}{u_2 \cdot u_2} = \tfrac13$, not $v_3 \cdot u_2 = \tfrac12$, because $u_2$ is not a unit vector.
