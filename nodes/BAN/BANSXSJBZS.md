## Summary

In mathematics, the Riemann zeta function is a function in complex analysis, which is also important in number theory. It is often denoted $\zeta(s)$ and is named after the mathematician Bernhard Riemann. When the argument $s$ is a [[real number|08J2M1JYAG]] greater than one, the zeta function satisfies the equation

$$\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n^s} \, .$$

It can therefore provide the sum of various convergent [[infinite series|0NM626FH6Q]], such as $\zeta(2) = \frac{1}{1^2} +$$\frac{1}{2^2} +$$\frac{1}{3^2} + \ldots \, .$ Explicit or numerically efficient formulae exist for $\zeta(s)$ at integer arguments, all of which have real values, including this example. This article lists these formulae, together with tables of values. It also includes derivatives and some series composed of the zeta function at integer arguments.
