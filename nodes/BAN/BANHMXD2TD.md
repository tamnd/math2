## Summary

The electric dipole moment of a charge distribution is the vector that measures how its positive and negative [[charges|6XPSV47R56]] are separated.
It is the first moment of the charge density.
For a neutral distribution it is the leading term of the field far away, which decays like the inverse cube of the distance.
It also governs the torque and the energy of the distribution in a uniform external field.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a charge $+q$ and a charge $-q$ a short distance apart; this pair is a dipole.
Its dipole moment is the arrow from the negative to the positive charge, scaled by $q$.
For charges $+q$ at $(0,0,d/2)$ and $-q$ at $(0,0,-d/2)$ the moment is $(0, 0, qd)$.
Many molecules, such as water, carry a dipole moment because their electrons sit closer to one end.
In an electric field a dipole turns to line up with the field, like a compass needle.
