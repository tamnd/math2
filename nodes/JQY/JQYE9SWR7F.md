## Summary

A whispering-gallery wave is a wave that travels around a concave curved surface while staying close to it.
Examples are sound along the inside wall of a circular room and light along the rim of a glass sphere.
In optics, a dielectric disk, ring or sphere supports whispering-gallery modes, light trapped near the rim by repeated total internal reflection.
These modes have very large azimuthal mode numbers and can have extremely high quality factors.
They are used in microresonators for lasers, sensors and frequency combs.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the dome of St Paul's Cathedral in London, a whisper against the wall can be heard well on the far side of the gallery.
The sound clings to the curved wall and travels around it instead of spreading across the room; Lord Rayleigh explained this effect.
Light does the same inside a tiny glass sphere or disk: it skims around the rim, bouncing at grazing angles and never escaping.
A resonance happens when a whole number of wavelengths fits around the circumference.
