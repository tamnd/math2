## Summary

Fluid dynamics is the part of [[fluid mechanics|H27M44BV4M]] that studies fluids in motion.
It seeks the velocity, pressure and density of a flow from the laws of conservation of mass, momentum and energy.
Its central equations are the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] for viscous fluids and the Euler equations for inviscid fluids.
It covers aerodynamics, hydrodynamics, weather, ocean currents and blood flow.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Fluid dynamics asks how a fluid moves and why.
Picture smoke rising from a candle: first it rises in a smooth column, then it breaks into swirls.
Fluid dynamics describes both regimes with the same equations, which balance the pressure, the friction and the inertia of the fluid.
A simple example is water flowing slowly through a straight pipe: the speed is largest at the centre and zero at the wall, with a parabolic profile.
