## Definition

Fix a photometric passband and a reference flux $F_0 > 0$ in that band.
Let $F > 0$ be the flux of an object received at the observer in that band.
The *apparent magnitude* of the object is
$$m = -2.5 \log_{10} \frac{F}{F_0}.$$

## Remarks

For two objects with fluxes $F_1$ and $F_2$, $m_1 - m_2 = -2.5 \log_{10}(F_1/F_2)$, which does not depend on $F_0$.
Here $\log_{10} x = \ln x / \ln 10$, with $\ln$ the [[natural logarithm|04DXAFN2ES]].
Example: if $F_1 = 100 F_2$, then $m_1 - m_2 = -2.5 \cdot 2 = -5$, so the brighter object has the magnitude smaller by $5$.
A factor $10^{0.4} \approx 2.512$ in flux is one magnitude.
Non-example: the total power emitted by a star, its luminosity, is not an apparent magnitude; the apparent magnitude also depends on the distance.
In the Johnson visual band $V$, the zero point is set so that the star Vega has magnitude close to $0$.
