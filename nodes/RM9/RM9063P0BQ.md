## Definition

Let a linear elastic material have [[elasticity tensor|SX32X1D537]] $C$, and let $e_1, e_2$ be two orthogonal unit vectors.
Consider the simple shear [[strain|8QS6QMJF5Z]] $\varepsilon = \tfrac{\gamma}{2} (e_1 \otimes e_2 + e_2 \otimes e_1)$ with engineering shear strain $\gamma \ne 0$, and let $\sigma = C : \varepsilon$ be the resulting [[stress|KG2XWEMJ30]].
The *shear modulus* of the material for the pair $(e_1, e_2)$ is
$$G = \frac{\sigma_{12}}{\gamma}.$$
For an isotropic material $G$ does not depend on the pair and equals the second [[Lamé parameter|80737NDZ4V]] $\mu$.

## Remarks

Example: for an isotropic material, $\tr \varepsilon = 0$, so $\sigma = 2\mu \varepsilon$ and $\sigma_{12} = \mu \gamma$; hence $G = \mu$.

Non-example: the ratio $\sigma_{11}/\varepsilon_{11}$ in uniaxial stress is Young's modulus, not the shear modulus; it measures stretching, and for isotropic materials it equals $2G(1 + \nu)$, where $\nu$ is Poisson's ratio.

Common notations are $G$, $\mu$ and $S$.
For anisotropic materials $G$ depends on the plane and the direction of shear.
