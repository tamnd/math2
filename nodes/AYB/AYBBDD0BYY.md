## Summary

Prospect theory is a descriptive model of choice under risk, proposed by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky in 1979.
Outcomes are judged as gains and losses relative to a reference point.
They are valued by a function that is concave for gains, convex for losses and steeper for losses than for gains.
Probabilities enter through a weighting function instead of linearly, which overweights small probabilities and underweights large ones.
It explains patterns such as the [[Allais paradox|E4NVGHXJQC]] that the [[expected utility hypothesis|8C2N37XPJ8]] cannot explain.

## Explanations

### L1 human

People feel a loss of $100$ euros more strongly than a gain of $100$ euros.
They also judge money as a change from where they stand now, not as a total fortune.
And they treat a $1\%$ chance as larger than it is, which is why lottery tickets and insurance both sell well.
Prospect theory puts these three observations into one formula for the value of a gamble.
