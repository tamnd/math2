## Summary

The Hosoya index of a graph is the number of its [[matchings|0MJ04A50QW]], the empty matching included.
Haruo Hosoya introduced it in 1971 as a topological index in [[chemical graph theory|AE724Y8CQX]].
It correlates with boiling points and other properties of saturated hydrocarbons.
It is also the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients of the matching polynomial of the graph.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A matching is a choice of bonds no two of which share an atom.
The Hosoya index counts all such choices, including choosing no bond at all.
For a path of three atoms there are three choices: no bond, the left bond, or the right bond.
For longer paths the counts are the Fibonacci numbers, so the index grows exponentially with the size of the molecule.
