## Summary

The Randall–Sundrum models, introduced by Lisa Randall and Raman Sundrum in 1999, are five-dimensional brane-world models whose extra dimension is warped.
The bulk is a slice of five-dimensional [[anti-de Sitter space|EQX6M75VB1]], and our four-dimensional world lives on a [[brane|DJAATX710N]] at its edge.
In the first model (RS1) there are two branes.
The exponential warp factor between them offers an explanation of why gravity is so much weaker than the other forces.
In the second model (RS2) there is one brane and the extra dimension is infinite, yet gravity on the brane is approximately four-dimensional.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In [[Kaluza–Klein theory|E55CCQ3K4S]], an extra dimension is hidden by making it tiny.
Randall and Sundrum found another way: the extra dimension is curved so that distances and energies shrink exponentially as one moves along it.
Two four-dimensional "walls" sit at the ends of the extra dimension.
If we live on the wall where energies are scaled down, a natural gravitational energy scale can look much smaller to us.
A modest distance between the walls, about $35$ times the curvature length, already gives a factor of about $10^{-15}$.
