## Summary

A Γ-convergence derivation of a plate model obtains a two-dimensional plate theory as a limit of three-dimensional elasticity.
The limit is taken as the thickness of the body tends to zero.
The elastic energy of a thin three-dimensional body is rescaled by a power of the thickness, and its [[Γ-limit|NW9MM370A5]] is computed.
The limit functional is the energy of the plate model, and its minimizers are the limits of the three-dimensional minimizers.
Different energy scalings give different theories, such as membrane theory, nonlinear bending theory and the [[Föppl–von Kármán equations|JHEQAM6Z8M]].
This replaces the kinematic assumptions of classical [[plate theory|782XMC21FH]] by a rigorous limit theorem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A sheet of paper is a three-dimensional object, yet engineers describe it by a two-dimensional surface that can bend and stretch.
The question is which two-dimensional energy is the right one.
The Γ-convergence approach answers it by taking the full three-dimensional energy of a slab of thickness $h$ and letting $h$ go to zero.
How the energy is scaled with $h$ decides what survives in the limit.
If the total energy is of order $h^3$, stretching is forbidden in the limit and only bending costs energy.
This is what happens when a sheet of paper is rolled into a cylinder.
