## Example

Let $E = (e_1, e_2, e_3)$ be the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$, with the standard [[orientation|0QJJAQ6ZYZ]], whose positive bases we call right-handed.
We compare $B_1 = (e_2, e_3, e_1)$ and $B_2 = (e_2, e_1, e_3)$ with $E$.

1. The change of basis matrix from $E$ to $B_1$ has the columns $e_2, e_3, e_1$:
$$P_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
2. Only the entry $1$ in row 1 survives in the expansion along row 1, and it sits in column 3 with sign $(-1)^{1+3} = +1$:
$$\det P_1 = 1 \cdot \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 1 > 0.$$
3. So by the [[definition|0Q8BZXVT1Z]], $B_1$ has the orientation of $E$, and it is right-handed.
4. The matrix $P_2$ with columns $e_2, e_1, e_3$ is the identity with its first two columns exchanged.
   One exchange changes the sign of the determinant, so $\det P_2 = -1 < 0$.
5. So $B_2$ has the opposite orientation, and it is left-handed.
6. We check both answers with the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]].
   For three vectors of $\mathbb{R}^3$ the determinant with columns $u, v, w$ equals $(u \times v) \cdot w$.
7. For $B_1$ we get $e_2 \times e_3 = e_1$, and $e_1 \cdot e_1 = 1 > 0$.
8. For $B_2$ we get $e_2 \times e_1 = -e_3$, and $(-e_3) \cdot e_3 = -1 < 0$.
   Both signs agree with steps 2 and 4.

## Remarks

A cyclic shift of three vectors is a product of two exchanges, so it keeps the orientation, and one exchange reverses it.
All six orders of $e_1, e_2, e_3$ split this way: the three cyclic shifts of $(e_1, e_2, e_3)$ are right-handed, and the three orders with one exchange are left-handed.
The right-hand rule describes the positive class in pictures.
Point the thumb of the right hand along $u$ and the index finger along $v$; for a right-handed basis $(u, v, w)$ the middle finger points to the same side of the plane of $u$ and $v$ as $w$.

If we replace $e_1$ by $-e_1$ in $B_1$, the determinant changes sign, so $(e_2, e_3, -e_1)$ is left-handed.
Replacing all three vectors by their negatives multiplies the determinant by $(-1)^3 = -1$, so $(-e_1, -e_2, -e_3)$ is left-handed too.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hold the thumb, the index finger and the middle finger of your right hand so that they make right angles with each other.
The standard axes $x$, $y$, $z$ follow these three fingers.
The order $y$, $z$, $x$ still fits the right hand, after you turn the hand.
The order $y$, $x$, $z$ only fits the left hand.

### L2 human

The determinant of the change of basis matrix gives the orientation in one computation.
In $\mathbb{R}^3$ the cross product gives a second check, since $(u \times v) \cdot w$ is the same determinant.
Watch out: the same three vectors in another order can have the other orientation, so the order must always be stated.
