## Statement

Let $F$ be a [[field|0QA8CHH8MG]], and let $A$ be a nonzero $m \times n$ matrix over $F$.
Then the [[rank|0EWQEYRVA8]] of $A$ is the largest integer $r$ such that $A$ has a nonzero [[minor|0SD528P0EK]] of order $r$.
In other words, $\operatorname{rank} A \ge r$ if and only if some $r \times r$ submatrix of $A$ has nonzero determinant.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The zero matrix has rank $0$ and no nonzero minor, so the statement excludes it or uses the convention that the empty minor is $1$.
The field is needed: over $\mathbb{Z}$, the matrix $(2)$ has the nonzero minor $2$ but is not invertible, and the usual notion of rank splits into several notions.

### Consequences

- For a square $n \times n$ matrix, $\operatorname{rank} A = n$ if and only if $\det A \ne 0$; this is the [[invertibility criterion|FBSAPTP1PR]].
- $\operatorname{rank} A \le r - 1$ if and only if every minor of order $r$ vanishes.
- So the matrices of rank less than $r$ form the zero set of polynomials, and rank can only go down under limits over $\mathbb{R}$.
- The [[rank of the adjugate|9CJME5W96T]] follows from this criterion.

### Converse

The statement is an equality of two numbers, so its converse is the same statement.

### Generalizations

The ideal generated by the $r \times r$ minors is a determinantal ideal; these belong to commutative algebra.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To find the rank of a matrix, look for the largest square piece with nonzero determinant.
Its size is the rank.

### L2 human

Take $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. Expansion along row 3 gives $\det A = 1 \cdot (12 - 12) - 0 + 1 \cdot (4 - 4) = 0$, so the rank is less than $3$.
2. Rows $1, 3$ and columns $1, 2$ give the minor $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = -2 \ne 0$, so the rank is at least $2$.
3. So $\operatorname{rank} A = 2$.
4. Check by elimination: $R_2 - 2R_1 = 0$ and $R_3 - R_1 = (0, -2, -2)$, which leaves two pivots.

Finding a nonzero minor proves a lower bound; an upper bound needs all minors of the next order to vanish.
For large matrices, elimination is much faster than checking minors.
A trap: one zero minor of order $r$ says nothing; the rank is less than $r$ only when all minors of order $r$ vanish.

### L3 human

The criterion shows that rank is lower semicontinuous, and that the matrices of rank at most $r$ form an affine variety.
