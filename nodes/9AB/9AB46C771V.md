## Summary

El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is an irregular oscillation of the coupled ocean and atmosphere of the tropical Pacific.
Its warm (El Niño) and cold (La Niña) phases recur every two to seven years.
ENSO models are mathematical models that explain and predict this oscillation.
The simplest are conceptual models with one or two variables, such as the delayed oscillator and the recharge oscillator.
Then come intermediate models such as the Zebiak–Cane model, and full coupled climate models.
The conceptual models show how an oscillation arises from two feedbacks.
One is a positive feedback between winds and sea surface temperature; the other is a delayed negative feedback carried by ocean waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an El Niño year the eastern tropical Pacific becomes unusually warm, and weather changes around the world.
The warming weakens the trade winds, and weaker winds let the warm water spread further east, so the warming feeds itself.
But the changed winds also send slow waves through the ocean, and after some months these waves bring cold water back and end the event.
ENSO models turn this story into equations.
The simplest has a single variable, the temperature anomaly $T$, which grows on its own but is pushed back by its own value from some months earlier.
