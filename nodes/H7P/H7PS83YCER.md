## Summary

An exact solution in general relativity is a spacetime, together with its matter fields, that satisfies the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] exactly, without approximation.
Usually it is given by an explicit formula for the metric in some coordinates.
Exact solutions are found by imposing symmetries or algebraic conditions that reduce the equations to a manageable system.
The [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]], the [[Kerr metric|X3FZC85THY]] and the [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric|ECJMS4VDTF]] are the best known, and they model black holes and the expanding universe.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Einstein's equations are hard nonlinear equations, and in general one can only solve them approximately or on a computer.
In special situations, such as a perfectly round star or a universe that looks the same everywhere, the symmetry makes it possible to write down the solution as a formula.
These exact solutions are the main source of intuition about gravity: black holes, gravitational waves and the Big Bang were all first understood through them.
The simplest exact solution is flat Minkowski space, where there is no matter and no curvature.
