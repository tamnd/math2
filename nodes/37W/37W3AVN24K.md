## Proof

Write $n = b \times c$ and $V = [a, b, c] = a \cdot n$.
We prove part 2 in both directions and then use the same decomposition for part 1.

**Part 2: dependent implies $V = 0$.**

1. Assume $x a + y b + z c = 0$ with $(x, y, z) \neq (0, 0, 0)$, by the definition of dependence.
2. Take the [[dot product|0S52TNNTH9]] with $n$. By linearity, $x (a \cdot n) + y (b \cdot n) + z (c \cdot n) = 0$.
3. Since $n$ is [[perpendicular to $b$ and $c$|0T4QHJXSD4]], $b \cdot n = c \cdot n = 0$.
4. So $x V = 0$. If $x \neq 0$, then $V = 0$.
5. If $x = 0$, then $y b + z c = 0$ with $(y, z) \neq (0, 0)$, so $b$ and $c$ are parallel.
6. Then $n = 0$ by the [[length theorem|SAZ353S8W0]], and $V = a \cdot 0 = 0$.

**Part 2: $V = 0$ implies dependent.**

7. Case $n = 0$. Then $b$ and $c$ are parallel by the [[length theorem|SAZ353S8W0]], say $b = t c$ or $c = t b$.
8. So $1 \cdot b - t c = 0$ or $1 \cdot c - t b = 0$, and $a$, $b$, $c$ are dependent.
9. Case $n \neq 0$. We first show that $b$, $c$, $n$ are independent. Assume $\alpha b + \beta c + \gamma n = 0$.
10. The dot product with $n$ gives $\gamma \|n\|^2 = 0$, by step 3, so $\gamma = 0$.
11. Then $\alpha b + \beta c = 0$. Since $n \neq 0$, $b$ and $c$ are not parallel, so $\alpha = \beta = 0$.
12. Three [[independent|0CRMGPAH83]] vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$ form a [[basis|096V7H13H9]], so $a = \lambda b + \mu c + \nu n$ for some numbers $\lambda, \mu, \nu$.
13. The dot product with $n$ gives $V = \nu \|n\|^2$, by step 3. Since $V = 0$, $\nu = 0$.
14. So $a = \lambda b + \mu c$, and $a$, $b$, $c$ are dependent.

**Part 1.**

15. If $n = 0$, then $b \parallel c$, the box is flat, and its volume is $0 = |V|$, by step 6.
16. Let $n \neq 0$. The base is the parallelogram on $b$ and $c$, with area $\|n\|$, by the [[length theorem|SAZ353S8W0]].
17. By step 12, $a = (\lambda b + \mu c) + \nu n$, a part in the base plane plus a part along $n$.
18. The part along $n$ is perpendicular to the base, by step 3, and its length $|\nu| \, \|n\|$ is the height.
19. By step 13, $\nu = V / \|n\|^2$, so the height is $|V| / \|n\|$.
20. The volume of the [[parallelepiped|0SQV2VJTGY]] is base times height, $\|n\| \cdot |V| / \|n\| = |V|$.

## Idea

The cross product $b \times c$ is a normal vector of the base, and its length is the area of the base.
The dot product with $a$ multiplies that area by the signed height of $a$ over the base.
So the triple product is the volume, and it vanishes exactly when $a$ has no height, that is, when $a$ lies in the base plane.

## Variants

The part $\nu n$ in step 17 is the [[projection|NA6Q7ME9G0]] of $a$ along $n$, since $a - \nu n$ is perpendicular to $n$.
Seen this way, the height in step 18 is the length of a projection, which is how the motivation on the [[scalar triple product|34VJYG6GVD]] page computes it.

With the [[determinant formula|059Q2XWJDC]], part 2 follows from the fact that a $3 \times 3$ determinant vanishes exactly when its rows are dependent.
