## Definition

Let $F$ be a field and let $(V_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of [[vector spaces|05EGHEQZAW]] over $F$.
The *external direct sum* $\bigoplus_{i \in I} V_i$ is the set of families $(v_i)_{i \in I}$ with $v_i \in V_i$ for each $i$ and $v_i = 0$ for all but finitely many $i$.
It is a vector space over $F$ with the componentwise operations
$$(v_i)_i + (w_i)_i = (v_i + w_i)_i, \qquad a (v_i)_i = (a v_i)_i.$$
For $j \in I$, the *canonical injection* $\iota_j \colon V_j \to \bigoplus_{i \in I} V_i$ sends $v$ to the family with $v$ in place $j$ and $0$ in the other places.

## Notation

For a finite family we write $V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_k$, and its vectors are tuples $(v_1, \dots, v_k)$.
For copies of one space, $V^{(I)} = \bigoplus_{i \in I} V$.
Some books write $\coprod_{i} V_i$ to stress that the direct sum is a coproduct.

## Remarks

The set is a subspace of the [[product|0EX2HPXPJ6]] $\prod_{i \in I} V_i$: a sum of two finitely supported families is finitely supported.
For finite $I$, the direct sum equals the product.
For infinite $I$, it is a proper subspace of the product when infinitely many $V_i$ are nonzero.
Each $\iota_j$ is an injective linear map, and $\bigoplus_i V_i$ is the [[internal direct sum|0XYYT5KNTX]] of the images $\iota_j(V_j)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The external direct sum builds a new space out of several spaces, side by side.
A vector of the new space chooses one vector in each space, with only finitely many nonzero choices.
For two spaces, this is just the space of pairs.

### L2 human

For $I = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ and $V_i = \R$, the direct sum $\R^{(I)}$ is the space of sequences with only finitely many nonzero terms.
The sequence $(1, 0, 2, 0, 0, \dots)$ belongs to it.
The sequence $(1, 1, 1, \dots)$ does not, although it belongs to the product.
