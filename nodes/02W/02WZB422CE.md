## Definition

The external direct sum of a family of vector spaces is the space of families that pick one vector from each space, with only finitely many of them nonzero.
Let $F$ be a field and let $(V_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of [[vector spaces|05EGHEQZAW]] over $F$.
The *external direct sum* $\bigoplus_{i \in I} V_i$ is the set of families $(v_i)_{i \in I}$ with $v_i \in V_i$ for each $i \in I$ and $v_i = 0$ for all but finitely many $i$.
It is a vector space over $F$ with the componentwise operations
$$(v_i)_i + (w_i)_i = (v_i + w_i)_i, \qquad a (v_i)_i = (a v_i)_i \quad (a \in F).$$
For $j \in I$, the *canonical injection* $\iota_j \colon V_j \to \bigoplus_{i \in I} V_i$ sends $v$ to the family with $v$ in place $j$ and $0$ in the other places.

## Notation

For a finite family we write $V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_k$, and its vectors are tuples $(v_1, \dots, v_k)$.
For copies of one space $V$, we write $V^{(I)} = \bigoplus_{i \in I} V$.
Some books write $\coprod_i V_i$, to stress that the direct sum is a coproduct.

## Remarks

The condition $v_i \in V_i$ says that each place $i$ holds a vector of its own space, so the spaces never mix.
The condition of finite support is the one that distinguishes the direct sum from the [[product|0EX2HPXPJ6]] $\prod_{i \in I} V_i$, which allows all families.
The direct sum is a subspace of the product.
The zero family has no nonzero entries, the nonzero entries of a sum lie among the nonzero entries of the two terms, and a scalar multiple has no new nonzero entries.
So the componentwise operations of the product stay inside the direct sum, and every vector space axiom holds because it holds in each $V_i$, entry by entry.

For example, take $I = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ and $V_i = \R$ for each $i$.
The sequence $(1, 0, 2, 0, 0, \dots)$ lies in $\R^{(I)}$, since only two of its entries are nonzero.
The sequence $(1, 1, 1, \dots)$ fails the finite support condition, so it lies in the product $\R^I$ but not in the direct sum.

For a finite index set the finite support condition holds for every family, so the direct sum equals the product.
Each $\iota_j$ is an injective linear map, and $\bigoplus_i V_i$ is the [[internal direct sum|0XYYT5KNTX]] of the images $\iota_j(V_j)$.
The concept page on the [[external direct sum|0S7W6EG32V]] gives more examples and the universal property.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The external direct sum builds a new space out of several spaces, side by side.
A vector of the new space chooses one vector in each space, and only finitely many of the choices may be nonzero.
For two spaces, this is just the space of pairs, such as the pair $((1, 2), 5)$ in $\R^2 \oplus \R$.

### L2 human

We add two vectors of $\R^{(I)}$ for $I = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$, entry by entry:
$$(1, 0, 2, 0, 0, \dots) + (0, 3, -2, 0, 0, \dots) = (1, 3, 0, 0, 0, \dots).$$
The first term has nonzero entries in places $1$ and $3$, the second in places $2$ and $3$, and the sum has nonzero entries only among places $1$, $2$ and $3$.
This is why finite support survives addition.
Watch out: the sum can have fewer nonzero entries than the terms, as in place $3$ here, but never more places than the two terms together.
