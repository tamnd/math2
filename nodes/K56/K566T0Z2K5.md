## Definition

Let $c > 0$ be the colonisation rate and $e > 0$ the local extinction rate.
The *Levins metapopulation model* is the [[ordinary differential equation|0NT64JV96A]]
$$p'(t) = c \, p(t) \left(1 - p(t)\right) - e \, p(t),$$
where $p(t) \in [0, 1]$ is the fraction of habitat patches occupied by the species at time $t$.

## Remarks

The term $c p (1 - p)$ counts colonisations of empty patches by migrants from occupied patches, and $e p$ counts local extinctions.
The [[equilibrium points|0N8NGKDZDZ]] are $p = 0$ and $p^* = 1 - e/c$.
Example: with $c = 0.5$ and $e = 0.1$, $p^* = 0.8$, and indeed $0.5 \cdot 0.8 \cdot 0.2 - 0.1 \cdot 0.8 = 0.08 - 0.08 = 0$.
If $c > e$, every solution with $p(0) \in (0, 1]$ tends to $p^*$; if $c \le e$, every solution tends to $0$.
The equation can be written $p' = (c - e) \, p \left(1 - p/p^*\right)$, which is a logistic equation for the fraction $p$.
Non-example: an equation for the number of individuals in a single patch, such as $N' = rN(1 - N/K)$, is not a metapopulation model, since it has no patches.
