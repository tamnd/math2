## Summary

A cluster expansion writes the logarithm of a partition function as a power series.
Its terms are sums over connected clusters of particles or of other objects.
In the classical form, due to Mayer, the pressure of a gas is expanded in powers of the activity.
Its coefficients are integrals of products of [[Mayer f-functions|T4FZCAQC0D]] over connected graphs.
When the series converges it proves that the system is in a single phase with analytic thermodynamic functions and fast decay of correlations.
Abstract versions for polymer systems are a main tool of rigorous statistical mechanics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a dilute gas, most molecules are alone, some meet in pairs, fewer meet in groups of three, and so on.
The cluster expansion accounts for these groups one size at a time.
The logarithm turns the partition function, a sum over all arrangements, into a sum over connected groups only.
The first correction to the ideal gas law comes from pairs.
For hard spheres of diameter $\sigma$, it adds $2 \pi \sigma^3 / 3$ times the density squared to $p / (k_B T)$, with $p$ the pressure.
