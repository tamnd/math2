## Summary

General covariance is the property of a physical theory that its equations keep their form under arbitrary smooth changes of coordinates.
Equivalently, they keep their form under [[diffeomorphisms|0KZZ70ZHXP]] of spacetime.
It is achieved by writing all laws as equations between [[tensor fields|WX302T93TW]] on a manifold, without fixed background structures such as a preferred coordinate system.
It was one of Einstein's guiding principles for [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]], and in that theory it implies that coordinates have no physical meaning by themselves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Coordinates are labels that we put on events, like street addresses on houses.
A good law of physics should not depend on which labelling we choose.
General covariance asks that the equations look the same in every system of labels, however curved or twisted.
For example, the statement that a vector field has zero divergence can be written in Cartesian or in polar coordinates, and it means the same thing in both.
