## Summary

E-theory, introduced by Connes and Higson, assigns an abelian group $E(A, B)$ to each pair of separable C*-algebras, built from asymptotic morphisms.
Like [[KK-theory|1W9Q7W378J]], it has a composition product and contains K-theory and K-homology.
Unlike KK-theory, it is half-exact in both variables for every extension of C*-algebras, not only for semisplit ones.
There is a natural map $KK(A, B) \to E(A, B)$, and it is an isomorphism when $A$ is nuclear.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A $*$-homomorphism must respect sums, products and adjoints exactly.
An asymptotic morphism is a continuous family of maps that respects them better and better as a parameter goes to infinity.
Such families arise naturally from extensions of C*-algebras and from deformations in quantization.
E-theory counts asymptotic morphisms between stabilized suspensions up to homotopy, which gives a theory with good exactness properties.
