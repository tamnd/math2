## Summary

The projective Hilbert space of a complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] $\mathcal{H}$ is its set of complex lines through $0$, the [[projective space|ZT9A6JK8QF]] of $\mathcal{H}$.
In quantum mechanics its points are the pure [[quantum states|4NQN47A92F]], because nonzero vectors on the same line give the same probabilities.
When $\mathcal{H} = \C^{n+1}$ it is the [[complex projective space|7GJ4STCNNM]] $\mathbb{CP}^n$.
The overlap $\abs{\ip{\psi}{\varphi}}^2 / (\norm{\psi}^2 \norm{\varphi}^2)$ depends only on the two lines, and it gives the transition probability between two pure states.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A state vector carries a little more information than the state: its length and an overall phase.
Neither changes any prediction, so one identifies all nonzero multiples of a vector.
What is left is a line through the origin, a "ray", and the projective Hilbert space is the set of all rays.
For a single spin, $\mathcal{H} = \C^2$, and the rays form a sphere, often drawn as the Bloch sphere.
