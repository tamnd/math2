## Statement

Let $F$ be an [[algebraic number field|MWBDFS2KJ5]], let $\mu(F)$ be its finite group of [[roots of unity|0WX11EA3F3]], and let $m$ be its order.
A noncomplex [[place|BT5V3TSNY6]] of $F$ is a finite place or a real place.
For each noncomplex place $v$, let $F_v$ be the completion of $F$ at $v$.
Let $\mu(F_v)$ be the finite group of roots of unity of $F_v$, and let $m_v$ be its order.
Let $(\cdot, \cdot)_v \colon K_2(F) \to \mu(F_v)$ be the homomorphism induced by the [[norm residue symbol|GBDFSDV23C]] of degree $m_v$ on $F_v$.
Then the sequence
$$K_2(F) \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{v \text{ noncomplex}} \mu(F_v) \longrightarrow \mu(F) \longrightarrow 1$$
is [[exact|JB7ZBFBA9C]], where the first map is $x \mapsto ((x)_v)_v$ and the second map sends $(\zeta_v)_v$ to $\prod_v \zeta_v^{m_v/m}$.

## Remarks

Here $K_2(F)$ is [[K2|W7T56A6YF8]] of $F$, and the first map lands in the direct sum because for given $a, b \in F^\times$ almost all symbols $(a, b)_v$ are trivial.
That the composite of the two maps is trivial is the reciprocity law $\prod_v (a, b)_v^{m_v/m} = 1$, a consequence of global class field theory.
The deep part, due to Moore, is exactness in the middle: the reciprocity law is the only relation among the local symbols.
The kernel of the first map is the wild kernel of $F$.

Example: for $F = \Q$, $\mu(\Q) = \set{\pm 1}$, so $m = 2$, and for an odd prime $p$, $\mu(\Q_p)$ has order $p - 1$.
The trivial composite contains Hilbert's product formula $\prod_v (a, b)_v = 1$ for the quadratic Hilbert symbols, which is equivalent to quadratic reciprocity with its supplements.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For each completion of a number field there is a local symbol, which measures whether one number is a norm for an extension generated by a root of the other.
The product of all these local symbols, suitably adjusted, is always $1$; this is a reciprocity law.
Moore's theorem says that this is the only global constraint: any family of local values whose product is $1$ comes from $K_2$ of the field.

### L2 human

For $F = \Q$ the sequence refines Tate's computation of $K_2(\Q)$ by tame symbols.
It is a K-theoretic form of the Hilbert reciprocity law, and it identifies the cokernel of the global symbol map with $\mu(F)$.
The kernel of the first map, the wild kernel, is a finite group studied in the arithmetic of $K_2$ of rings of integers.
