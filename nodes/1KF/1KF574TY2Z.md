## Definition

Let $n \ge 1$ be the number of populations, and let $\R_{\ge 0}^n$ be the set of vectors in $\R^n$ with nonnegative entries.
A *population model* is a [[dynamical system|BBZP1R872N]] with state space $\R_{\ge 0}^n$.
The entry $x_i$ of the state $x$ is the size or density of population $i$.
It has one of two forms:

1. continuous time: an [[ordinary differential equation|0NT64JV96A]] $x'(t) = f(x(t))$, where $f \colon \R_{\ge 0}^n \to \R^n$ satisfies $f_i(x) \ge 0$ whenever $x_i = 0$;
2. discrete time: a [[recurrence relation|0FYX44GP92]] $x_{t+1} = F(x_t)$, where $F$ maps $\R_{\ge 0}^n$ into itself.

The condition in each form makes sure that sizes that start nonnegative stay nonnegative.

## Remarks

Example: $N' = r N$ with $r \in \R$ is a population model with $n = 1$; here $f(N) = rN$ and $f(0) = 0 \ge 0$.
Non-example: $N' = N - 1$ fails clause 1, since $f(0) = -1 < 0$, and the solution with $N(0) = 0$ becomes negative.
Many models also have a stochastic form, in which births and deaths are random events.
Models that keep track of ages or stages, and not only of total sizes, are called structured population models.
