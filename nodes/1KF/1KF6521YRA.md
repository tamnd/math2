## Summary

The Heath–Jarrow–Morton (HJM) framework models the whole curve of instantaneous forward rates at once, instead of only the [[short rate|7TZ87AS1MW]].
It was introduced by David Heath, Robert Jarrow and Andrew Morton in 1992.
Its central result is that absence of arbitrage fixes the drift of the forward rates in terms of their volatilities.
The model is then specified by choosing the volatility structure, and it fits today's yield curve by construction.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The forward rate curve tells, for each future date, the interest rate that can be locked in today for borrowing over a tiny period at that date.
The HJM framework lets every point of this curve move randomly over time.
One cannot choose the drift of these moves freely: if one did, some bond trades would make money without risk.
So the modeller picks only how volatile each forward rate is, and the drifts follow.
