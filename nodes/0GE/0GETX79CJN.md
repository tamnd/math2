## Summary

Let $G$ be a [[group|G5S1M9]] and let $H$ be a [[subgroup|0X1JXT47GA]] of $G$.
The normal core of $H$ in $G$ is the intersection of all the [[conjugates|0H11ZPX4ND]] of $H$:
$$\operatorname{core}_G(H) = \bigcap_{g \in G} gHg^{-1}.$$
It is a [[normal subgroup|0CB0DX5BVV]] of $G$, it is contained in $H$, and it contains each normal subgroup of $G$ that is contained in $H$.
Thus it is the largest normal subgroup of $G$ inside $H$.
It is also the kernel of the action of $G$ on the left cosets of $H$.

## Motivation

A subgroup $H$ that is not normal does not give a quotient group $G/H$.
We can still ask for the best normal subgroup that we can find inside $H$.
The normal core is this subgroup.
It also answers a second question.
The group $G$ acts on the set $G/H$ of left cosets by $g \cdot xH = gxH$.
The elements of $G$ that fix every coset are exactly the elements of the normal core.
Thus the normal core measures how much information this action loses.

## Notation

We write $\operatorname{core}_G(H)$.
Some books write $H_G$ or $\operatorname{Core}_G(H)$.
Conjugation is $gHg^{-1} = \{ghg^{-1} : h \in H\}$; some books use $g^{-1}Hg$, and this gives the same intersection.
A subgroup $H$ with $\operatorname{core}_G(H) = \{e\}$ is called core-free.

## Remarks

### Examples

- If $H$ is normal in $G$, then each conjugate $gHg^{-1}$ equals $H$, so $\operatorname{core}_G(H) = H$.
- In $S_3$, the subgroup $\{e, (1\,2)\}$ is core-free. Its three conjugates are the three subgroups of order $2$, and their intersection is $\{e\}$ ([[a core-free subgroup of S3|E7SWCC1JR5]]).
- In $S_4$, the subgroup $P = V_4 \cup (1\,2)V_4$ of order $8$ has core $V_4$, where $V_4$ is the [[Klein four-group|0PMRQJVTME]] of double transpositions ([[the core of a subgroup of order 8 in S4|ZDXFVNH5XV]]).
- In the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] $S_n$ with $n \ge 2$, the stabiliser $H$ of the point $n$ is core-free. The conjugates of $H$ are the stabilisers of the points $1, \ldots, n$, and only $e$ fixes every point.
- If $G$ is a [[simple group|0YMYMNQ0QZ]] and $H \ne G$, then $H$ is core-free. The core is normal in $G$, so it is $\{e\}$ or $G$, and it cannot be $G$.

### Non-examples

- If $H$ is not normal in $G$, then $\operatorname{core}_G(H)$ is not $H$. It is a proper subgroup of $H$.
- A subgroup that is normal in $H$ need not be the core. In $S_4$, the subgroup $K = \{e, (1\,2), (3\,4), (1\,2)(3\,4)\}$ is normal in $P$, but it is not normal in $S_4$ ([[a normal subgroup of P that is not the core|CXK7VSCTNG]]).
- The [[normaliser|0YGN3VAA0D]] is a different subgroup. For $H = \{e, (1\,2)\}$ in $S_3$, the normaliser is $H$, but the core is $\{e\}$.

### Basic properties

- $\operatorname{core}_G(H)$ is a subgroup, because an [[intersection of subgroups|0ZTGMDNVTB]] is a subgroup.
- It is normal in $G$. For $a \in G$, we get $a\operatorname{core}_G(H)a^{-1} = \bigcap_{g} (ag)H(ag)^{-1}$. As $g$ runs over $G$, so does $ag$, so this intersection is again $\operatorname{core}_G(H)$.
- It lies in $H$, because $H = eHe^{-1}$ is one of the conjugates.
- If $N \trianglelefteq G$ and $N \subseteq H$, then $N = gNg^{-1} \subseteq gHg^{-1}$ for each $g$. So $N \subseteq \operatorname{core}_G(H)$.
- It is the kernel of the action of $G$ on $G/H$. The element $g$ fixes $xH$ if and only if $x^{-1}gx \in H$, that is, $g \in xHx^{-1}$.
- If $[G : H] = n$ is finite, then $G/\operatorname{core}_G(H)$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_n$. Thus the [[index|0DTXBQPA2S]] of the core is finite and divides $n!$.
- If $[G : H] = p$ is the smallest prime that divides $|G|$, then $\operatorname{core}_G(H) = H$, and so $H$ is normal ([[smallest prime index|0FEBE9NC0E]]).
- The core depends on $G$. If $H \le K \le G$, then $\operatorname{core}_G(H) \le \operatorname{core}_K(H)$, and the two can differ.

### Common errors

- A normal subgroup of $H$ is not the same as a normal subgroup of $G$ inside $H$. The core asks for normality in $G$.
- The core is an intersection, so it can be small. It is not the subgroup generated by the conjugates; that subgroup is the [[normal closure|5WBSBAP4PD]].
- For a subgroup of finite index, it is enough to intersect the finitely many distinct conjugates. There are $[G : N_G(H)]$ of them, where $N_G(H)$ is the normaliser.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A subgroup can fail to be normal.
Then we can look for the biggest normal subgroup that fits inside it.
To find it, move the subgroup around by conjugation, and keep only the elements that stay inside every moved copy.
The elements that remain form the normal core.
For example, in the group of all permutations of $\{1, 2, 3\}$, the subgroup $\{e, (1\,2)\}$ moves to $\{e, (1\,3)\}$ and to $\{e, (2\,3)\}$.
Only $e$ is in all three copies, so the core is $\{e\}$.

### L2 human

We compute the core of a cyclic subgroup of order $4$ in $S_4$.
Compose permutations from right to left.

1. Let $H = \langle (1\,2\,3\,4) \rangle = \{e, (1\,2\,3\,4), (1\,3)(2\,4), (1\,4\,3\,2)\}$.
2. Conjugation by $(3\,4)$ sends $(1\,2\,3\,4)$ to $(1\,2\,4\,3)$. So $(3\,4)H(3\,4)^{-1} = \{e, (1\,2\,4\,3), (1\,4)(2\,3), (1\,3\,4\,2)\}$.
3. Conjugation by $(2\,3)$ sends $(1\,2\,3\,4)$ to $(1\,3\,2\,4)$. So $(2\,3)H(2\,3)^{-1} = \{e, (1\,3\,2\,4), (1\,2)(3\,4), (1\,4\,2\,3)\}$.
4. The three subgroups have no common element except $e$. The three double transpositions are different, and the six $4$-cycles are different.
5. Thus $\operatorname{core}_{S_4}(H) = \{e\}$. There is no need to compute more conjugates, because the intersection is already $\{e\}$.
6. Consequence: $S_4$ acts on the $6$ cosets of $H$, and only $e$ fixes all of them. So $S_4$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_6$ by this action.

The typical use of the core is to turn a subgroup of small index into a normal subgroup.
If $H$ has index $n$, then the core has index at most $n!$.
For example, a [[simple group|0YMYMNQ0QZ]] $G$ with a subgroup of index $n > 1$ satisfies $|G| \le n!$, because the core is $\{e\}$.

A trap is to compute only one or two conjugates and stop too early.
The intersection of two conjugates can be larger than the core.
In $S_4$, let $K$ be the stabiliser of the point $4$, a copy of $S_3$.
The conjugate $(3\,4)K(3\,4)^{-1}$ is the stabiliser of $3$, and $K \cap (3\,4)K(3\,4)^{-1} = \{e, (1\,2)\}$.
But the core of $K$ is $\{e\}$, because the stabilisers of all four points meet only in $e$.

### L3 human

A transitive action of $G$ on a set $X$ is the same as the action on the cosets of a point stabiliser $H$.
The action is faithful exactly when $H$ is core-free.
So the faithful transitive permutation representations of $G$ correspond to the conjugacy classes of core-free subgroups.
The [[normal closure|5WBSBAP4PD]] is the dual notion: the smallest normal subgroup that contains a subgroup.
In Galois theory, let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension with group $G$, and let $E$ be an intermediate field with $H = \operatorname{Gal}(L/E)$.
Then $\operatorname{core}_G(H)$ is the group of the [[normal closure|6XM4CCVTKE]] of $E$ over $K$ inside $L$.
The bound $[G : \operatorname{core}_G(H)] \le n!$ shows that a group with a subgroup of finite index also has a normal subgroup of finite index.
This is a basic step in the study of residually finite groups and of profinite completions.
