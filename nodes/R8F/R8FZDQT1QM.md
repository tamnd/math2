## Statement

Let $R$ be a [[ring|0B1261B5ED]] with identity, and let $R[t, t^{-1}]$ be the [[Laurent polynomial ring|QMY5JTZZB8]] over $R$.
Let $K_0$ be [[K0 of a ring|Q14JS2MA1C]], $K_1$ be [[K1 of a ring|SXVNJY4XFY]], and $NK_1$ be the [[NK group|TV0GX7AQWT]] in degree $1$.
Then there is a natural isomorphism
$$K_1(R[t, t^{-1}]) \cong K_1(R) \oplus K_0(R) \oplus NK_1(R) \oplus NK_1(R).$$
Let $P$ be a finitely generated projective module, and let $P[t, t^{-1}] = P \otimes_R R[t, t^{-1}]$.
The summand $K_0(R)$ is embedded by sending $[P]$ to the class of the multiplication by $t$ on $P[t, t^{-1}]$.
The two summands $NK_1(R)$ come from $R[t]$ and from $R[t^{-1}]$.
In particular, if $R$ is regular Noetherian, then $K_1(R[t, t^{-1}]) \cong K_1(R) \oplus K_0(R)$.

## Remarks

The proof studies the projective $R[t]$-modules that become free after inverting $t$, and the localization sequence for $R[t] \to R[t, t^{-1}]$.
The cokernel of $K_1(R[t]) \oplus K_1(R[t^{-1}]) \to K_1(R[t, t^{-1}])$ is identified with $K_0(R)$ through the multiplication by $t$.

Example: let $F$ be a field.
The ring $F[t, t^{-1}]$ is Euclidean, so its $K_1$ is its group of units, which is $\set{c t^m : c \in F^\times, m \in \Z} \cong F^\times \times \Z$.
This agrees with $K_1(F) \oplus K_0(F) = F^\times \oplus \Z$, and the factor $\Z$ is generated by the class of $t$.

For a group $G$, applied to the group ring $R = \Z G$, the theorem gives a decomposition of the Whitehead group of $G \times \Z$ that contains the reduced group $\tilde K_0(\Z G)$ as a summand.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem computes the invertible matrices over Laurent polynomials, up to elementary operations, from data about the coefficient ring.
Besides the obvious part $K_1(R)$, there is a new part $K_0(R)$, coming from the new unit $t$.
For bad rings there are two more correction terms, one for positive and one for negative powers of $t$.

### L2 human

This is the degree one case of the fundamental theorem of algebraic K-theory, which gives the same pattern for $K_n$ in every degree.
Bass used the cokernel above to define negative K-groups, so that the pattern continues below degree zero.
In topology the formula for the Whitehead group of $G \times \Z$ controls the torsion of h-cobordisms over manifolds with fundamental group $G \times \Z$.
