## Summary

The Rosseland approximation, or diffusion approximation, treats [[radiative heat transfer in an optically thick medium|FYYQNDT6X2]] like heat conduction.
When radiation is absorbed and re-emitted many times over short distances, the radiative heat flux becomes proportional to the temperature gradient, as in [[Fourier's law|9YJZ1PP2Y8]].
The radiative conductivity is $16 \sigma T^3 / (3 \beta_R)$, where $\beta_R$ is the Rosseland mean extinction coefficient.
It is used for stellar interiors, where it defines the Rosseland mean opacity, and for insulating materials and glass melts.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Deep inside a star, light cannot travel far before it is absorbed and sent out again in a random direction.
The energy creeps outward in a random walk, much like heat spreading through a solid.
So instead of following every ray, one can describe radiation by a heat conduction law with a special conductivity.
This conductivity grows like the cube of the temperature, so radiation carries heat very well in hot, thick media.
