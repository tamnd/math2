## Definition

*Aerodynamics* is the branch of [[fluid dynamics|F2JRWJNYN4]] that studies the motion of a gas relative to a solid body.
It studies in particular the forces and moments that this motion produces on the body.
In the usual setting a rigid body moves at constant velocity through a gas at rest.
Equivalently, a uniform stream of speed $U$ meets a body at rest.
The resultant force on the body is split into the *drag* $D$, parallel to the stream, and the *lift* $L$, perpendicular to it.
Let $\rho$ be the density of the free stream and $S$ a reference area of the body.
The lift and drag coefficients are
$$C_L = \frac{L}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 S}, \qquad C_D = \frac{D}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 S}.$$

## Remarks

Example: the flow of air at $70\ \mathrm{m/s}$ around the wing of a light aircraft is a problem of aerodynamics; it is nearly incompressible because its [[Mach number|GFHJ858AN1]] is about $0.2$.
Non-example: the flow of water around a ship's hull belongs to hydrodynamics, since the fluid is a liquid.
The coefficients depend mainly on the shape, the angle of attack, the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]] and the Mach number.
Aerodynamics is divided by the Mach number into subsonic, transonic, supersonic and hypersonic flow.
