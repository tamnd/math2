## Summary

Dimensional regularization makes divergent [[Feynman diagram|BS6HVNMTWC]] integrals finite by computing them in a spacetime of dimension $d$, treated as a complex variable.
The integrals become [[meromorphic functions|0ZZ7ZYDR8W]] of $d$, and the divergences of four-dimensional integrals reappear as poles at $d = 4$.
It preserves Lorentz invariance and gauge invariance, which is why it is the standard tool for the [[renormalization|1X0W3AEBEJ]] of gauge theories.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some integrals of quantum field theory diverge in four dimensions but converge in fewer dimensions.
Dimensional regularization computes them in a general dimension $d$, where they are given by a formula with the [[gamma function|0H5SYBSED9]].
This formula makes sense for every complex $d$ except at some poles.
Near $d = 4$, the divergence shows up as a term like $1/(4 - d)$, which renormalization then removes.
