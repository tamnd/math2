## Definition

Let $(M, g)$ be a 4-dimensional [[Lorentzian manifold|D1SYF8F8RQ]].
It is a *vacuum solution* if its [[Einstein tensor|P13KTBC7MH]] vanishes:
$$G_{ab} = R_{ab} - \tfrac12 R \, g_{ab} = 0,$$
where $R_{ab}$ is the [[Ricci tensor|K83HM845A8]] and $R$ the scalar curvature of $g$.

## Remarks

Taking the trace gives $R - 2R = -R = 0$, so the condition is equivalent to $R_{ab} = 0$: vacuum solutions are the Ricci-flat Lorentzian 4-manifolds.
They are the solutions of the [[Einstein field equations|7C2WP33E10]] with $T_{ab} = 0$ and $\Lambda = 0$.
Example: the Schwarzschild metric on $r > 2M$ has $R_{ab} = 0$, although its Riemann tensor is not zero.
Non-example: the Reissner–Nordström metric with charge $Q \ne 0$ is not a vacuum solution, since its electromagnetic field has a nonzero stress–energy tensor and so $R_{ab} \ne 0$.
When a cosmological constant is present, the analogous condition $R_{ab} = \Lambda g_{ab}$ defines the lambdavacuum solutions, such as de Sitter space.
For a vacuum solution the Riemann tensor equals the Weyl tensor.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A vacuum solution is a spacetime with no matter, whose Ricci curvature is zero.
