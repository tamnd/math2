## Summary

The galaxy two-point correlation function $\xi(r)$ measures the excess probability, over a random distribution, of finding two galaxies at separation $r$.
It is the simplest statistic of galaxy clustering and the spatial analogue of an [[autocorrelation function|YCZHYY8VE5]].
Its Fourier transform is the galaxy power spectrum, and on scales of a few megaparsecs it is close to a power law.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pick a galaxy and count its neighbours at distance $r$; then compare with what you would get if galaxies were sprinkled at random with the same average density.
If you find twice as many, $\xi(r) = 1$; if you find just as many, $\xi(r) = 0$.
Galaxies cluster, so $\xi(r)$ is large at small distances and drops towards $0$ at large distances.
Observed galaxies follow roughly $\xi(r) = (r/r_0)^{-1.8}$ with $r_0$ near $5 \, h^{-1}$ megaparsecs, where $h$ is the Hubble constant in units of $100$ km/s/Mpc.
