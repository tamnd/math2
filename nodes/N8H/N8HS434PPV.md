## Summary

A quantum phase transition is a sudden change of the ground state of a quantum many-body system as a parameter of its Hamiltonian is varied.
It happens at zero temperature.
It is driven by quantum fluctuations instead of thermal ones.
At the transition the ground-state energy density is not analytic, and at a continuous transition the energy gap above the ground state closes.
The [[transverse-field Ising model|PJZ35X5QHM]] is the standard example.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cool a material to absolute zero, so that it sits in its lowest-energy state.
Now slowly turn a knob, such as a magnetic field or the pressure.
At a certain value the nature of the lowest-energy state can change abruptly, for instance from magnetic to non-magnetic.
That is a quantum phase transition: no heat is involved, only the competition between terms of the energy that do not commute.
In a chain of quantum spins, a coupling that aligns neighbours competes with a transverse field that flips spins, and the two balance at one value of the field.
