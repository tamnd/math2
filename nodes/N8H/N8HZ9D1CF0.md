## Summary

A geodetic datum is a reference frame that ties coordinates to actual places on the Earth.
It fixes a [[reference ellipsoid|R9XX7TNJCH]] and the way this ellipsoid sits relative to the Earth.
The same point has different [[geodetic coordinates|6QBV95NG6E]] in different datums, sometimes differing by hundreds of metres.
Converting between datums is done by a [[Helmert transformation|HKPQ1565TS]] or a similar map.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Coordinates mean nothing until one says what they are measured from.
A datum is that agreement: which ellipsoid, where its centre is, and how its axes point.
Old national datums placed the ellipsoid to fit one country well.
Modern datums, such as WGS 84 used by GPS, place the centre at the Earth's centre of mass.
A point plotted with the wrong datum can land in the wrong street.
