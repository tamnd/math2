## Example

An invertible matrix need not have an invertible corner block, and then its Schur complement does not exist.
Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, with $p = q = 1$ and blocks $A = (0)$, $B = (1)$, $C = (1)$ and $D = (0)$.
Then
$$M \cdot M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 1 & 0 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 0 \\ 1 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot 1 & 1 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 0 \end{pmatrix} = I_2,$$
so $M$ is [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]] with $M^{-1} = M$.
The block $A = (0)$ is not invertible, so $A^{-1}$ does not exist, and the [[Schur complement|CA1J41NWGS]] $M/A$ is not defined.
The block $D = (0)$ is not invertible either, so $M/D$ is not defined.
So neither form of the [[block inverse formula|5QTK7JWXF3]] applies, although $M^{-1}$ exists.
After the exchange of the two rows, which is left multiplication by $P = M$, we get $PM = I_2$, whose blocks are invertible, and the formula applies to $PM$.

## Remarks

### The lesson

The formula is a statement about $M$ together with a choice of block.
Invertibility of $M$ alone does not give an invertible diagonal block.

### Watch out

The opposite failure also occurs.
For $M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ the block $A = (1)$ is invertible, so $M/A = 1 - 1 = 0$ is defined, but it is $0$, and this shows that $M$ is singular.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Schur complement needs the corner block to be invertible.
Here the corner is $0$, so we cannot even start, although the whole matrix has an inverse.
This is the block version of a zero pivot.

### L2 human

The same issue appears in the [[LU decomposition|0RKG5YW66Y]]: the matrix $M$ has no LU decomposition because its first pivot is $0$.
In both cases a row exchange fixes the problem.
