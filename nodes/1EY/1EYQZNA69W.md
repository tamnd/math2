## Summary

The Weyl chambers of a root system are the regions into which the hyperplanes orthogonal to the roots cut the ambient Euclidean space.
The Weyl group permutes the chambers simply transitively, and the chambers correspond one to one to the bases of the root system.
The closure of a chosen chamber is a fundamental domain for the Weyl group, and dominant weights are the weights in it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw the mirror lines orthogonal to all roots of a root system.
They cut the space into wedge-shaped regions, the Weyl chambers.
For the hexagon system $A_2$ in the plane, three mirror lines cut the plane into six equal wedges with angle $60^\circ$.
Reflections in the mirrors move each wedge onto each other wedge, in exactly one way.
