## Proof

We prove the two directions separately, and in the second we use the set of all consequences of $\Gamma$ as the refuting valuation.
We use the notation of [[the statement|2FQB3NQF8A]], and the conditions (R), (M) and (C) of a [[consequence relation|J78Y1M4GND]].

First we show that $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ implies the semantic side.

1. Assume $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$, and let $T$ be a closed set with $b_T(\gamma) = 1$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$.
   By the definition of $b_T$, this means $\Gamma \subseteq T$.
2. Monotonicity (M) applied to $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ and $\Gamma \subseteq T$ gives $T \vdash \varphi$.
3. Since $T$ is closed, $T \vdash \varphi$ gives $\varphi \in T$, that is, $b_T(\varphi) = 1$.

Next we show that the semantic side implies $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$.

4. Assume the semantic side, and put $T = \{\psi \in L : \Gamma \vdash \psi\}$.
5. We show that $T$ is closed.
   Let $T \vdash \chi$; then $\Gamma \cup T \vdash \chi$ by (M).
   Each $\psi \in T$ has $\Gamma \vdash \psi$, so cut (C) with $\Delta = T$ gives $\Gamma \vdash \chi$, that is, $\chi \in T$.
6. We show that $\Gamma \subseteq T$.
   For $\gamma \in \Gamma$, reflexivity (R) gives $\Gamma \vdash \gamma$, so $\gamma \in T$.
7. By step 5, $b_T \in B$, and by step 6, $b_T(\gamma) = 1$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$.
8. The semantic side now gives $b_T(\varphi) = 1$, so $\varphi \in T$, that is, $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$.

The two directions together give the equivalence of the theorem.

## Idea

The closed theories themselves serve as two-valued valuations, with "true" read as "belongs to the theory".
Monotonicity and closure give soundness: a theory that contains the premises contains their consequences.
For the other direction, when $\Gamma \nvdash \varphi$, the theory of all consequences of $\Gamma$ contains $\Gamma$ and not $\varphi$, so it is the refuting valuation.
Reflexivity puts $\Gamma$ into that theory, and cut makes it closed.

## Variants

For a logic of a matrix $(V, D)$, you can use the maps $b_v$ with $b_v(\varphi) = 1$ iff $v(\varphi) \in D$, one for each matrix valuation $v$.
Then the equivalence is just the definition of matrix consequence, read with "designated" as "true", as in [[the example for Ł3|MAYHP6P8NC]].
For multiple-conclusion consequence relations there is an analogous reduction to two-valued valuations, under a suitable form of cut.
