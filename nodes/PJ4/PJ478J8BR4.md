## Proof

We use the notation of [[the statement|2FQB3NQF8A]], and (R), (M) and (C) from the definition of a [[consequence relation|J78Y1M4GND]].

Part A: from $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ to the semantic side.

1. Assume $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$. Let $T$ be closed, with $b_T(\gamma) = 1$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$.
2. By the definition of $b_T$, this means $\Gamma \subseteq T$.
3. By monotonicity (M), $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ and $\Gamma \subseteq T$ give $T \vdash \varphi$.
4. Since $T$ is closed, $T \vdash \varphi$ gives $\varphi \in T$, that is, $b_T(\varphi) = 1$.

Part B: from the semantic side to $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$.

5. Assume the semantic side. Let $T = \set{\psi \in L : \Gamma \vdash \psi}$.
6. $T$ is closed. Let $T \vdash \chi$. Each $\psi \in T$ has $\Gamma \vdash \psi$, and $\Gamma \cup T \vdash \chi$ by (M). Cut (C) with $\Delta = T$ gives $\Gamma \vdash \chi$, so $\chi \in T$.
7. $\Gamma \subseteq T$: for $\gamma \in \Gamma$, reflexivity (R) gives $\Gamma \vdash \gamma$.
8. By step 7, $b_T(\gamma) = 1$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$. By step 6, $b_T \in B$.
9. The semantic side then gives $b_T(\varphi) = 1$, so $\varphi \in T$, that is, $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$.

Parts A and B together give the equivalence of the theorem.

## Idea

The closed theories themselves serve as two-valued valuations: "true" means "belongs to the theory".
Monotonicity and closure give soundness, and the theory of all consequences of $\Gamma$ is the counter-valuation when $\Gamma \nvdash \varphi$.

## Variants

For a logic of a matrix $(V, D)$, one can use the valuations $b_v$ with $b_v(\varphi) = 1$ iff $v(\varphi) \in D$.
Then the equivalence is the definition of matrix consequence, read with "designated" as "true"; see [[the example for Ł3|MAYHP6P8NC]].
For multiple-conclusion consequence relations, SEP gives the analogous result with bivaluations that separate premises from conclusions.
