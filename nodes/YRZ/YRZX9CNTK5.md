## Statement

Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]], let $n \ge 2$ be an integer invertible in $F$, and let $\zeta \in F$ be a primitive $n$-th [[root of unity|0WX11EA3F3]].
For $a, b \in F^\times$, let $A_\zeta(a, b)$ be the $F$-algebra generated by two elements $x, y$ with the relations
$$x^n = a, \qquad y^n = b, \qquad yx = \zeta xy.$$
Then $A_\zeta(a, b)$ is a [[central simple algebra|5HRKJBAY2V]] of dimension $n^2$ over $F$.
There is a unique group homomorphism
$$R_{n, \zeta} \colon K_2(F) \to \operatorname{Br}(F), \qquad \set{a, b} \mapsto [A_\zeta(a, b)],$$
from [[K2|W7T56A6YF8]] of $F$ to the [[Brauer group|BEW48TA9WZ]] of $F$.
Its image lies in the subgroup of elements of order dividing $n$, and it vanishes on $nK_2(F)$, so it induces $K_2(F)/n \to \operatorname{Br}(F)[n]$.

## Remarks

By [[Matsumoto's theorem|E0W8X5Y5SE]], it is enough to check that $(a, b) \mapsto [A_\zeta(a, b)]$ is a Steinberg symbol.
Bimultiplicativity holds in the Brauer group: $A_\zeta(a, b) \otimes A_\zeta(a', b)$ is Brauer equivalent to $A_\zeta(aa', b)$, and similarly in $b$.
For the relation $\set{a, 1 - a}$, consider the main case where $X^n - a$ is irreducible and let $\alpha^n = a$.
Then $A_\zeta(a, b)$ is the [[cyclic algebra|D6BM80QYNE]] of $F(\alpha)/F$ with $u^n = b$, and $1 - a = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1 - \zeta^i \alpha)$ is a norm from $F(\alpha)$, so $A_\zeta(a, 1 - a)$ is split.

Example: $F = \R$, $n = 2$, $\zeta = -1$.
The algebra $A_{-1}(-1, -1)$ is Hamilton's quaternions $\mathbb{H}$, a division algebra, so $R_{2, -1}(\set{-1, -1}) \ne 0$ in $\operatorname{Br}(\R) \cong \Z/2\Z$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two nonzero numbers $a$ and $b$ and a root of unity $\zeta$ define a noncommutative algebra, which generalizes the quaternions.
The proposition says that this construction respects the rules of symbols in $K_2$.
So it gives a homomorphism from $K_2$ of the field to the Brauer group, which classifies such algebras.

### L2 human

The algebras $A_\zeta(a, b)$ are called symbol algebras or power norm residue algebras; for $n = 2$ they are the quaternion algebras $(a, b)_F$.
The Merkurjev–Suslin theorem says that the induced map $K_2(F)/n \to \operatorname{Br}(F)[n]$ is an isomorphism.
Over a local field the Brauer class of $A_\zeta(a, b)$ is determined by the norm residue symbol of $a$ and $b$.
