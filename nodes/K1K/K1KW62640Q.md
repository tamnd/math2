## Definition

A [[normal-form game|5MZTZRC3CZ]] $G = (N, (S_i), (u_i))$ is *zero-sum* if
$$\sum_{i \in N} u_i(s) = 0 \quad \text{for every strategy profile } s \in S.$$
A finite two-player zero-sum game is given by one $m \times n$ matrix $A$, with $u_1(j, k) = A_{jk}$ and $u_2(j, k) = -A_{jk}$.

## Remarks

For [[mixed strategies|P01KKHB155]] $x \in \Delta(S_1)$ and $y \in \Delta(S_2)$ the payoff of player 1 is $x^T A y$.
By [[von Neumann's minimax theorem|GJ8VTBFNP2]], $\max_x \min_y x^T A y = \min_y \max_x x^T A y$; this common number is the *value* of the game.
A pair $(x^*, y^*)$ is a [[Nash equilibrium|745WYP9YJ0]] exactly when $x^*$ and $y^*$ are optimal strategies, that is, they attain the outer max and the outer min.
Equilibria are interchangeable, and all of them give player 1 the value.
Example: matching pennies has $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and $u_1 + u_2 = 0$ in all four cells.
Non-example: the prisoner's dilemma is not zero-sum, since $u_1(C, C) + u_2(C, C) = 6 \ne 0$.
A game whose payoffs add up to the same constant in every cell is *constant-sum*; it behaves like a zero-sum game.
