## Summary

Polar motion is the motion of the Earth's rotation axis relative to the crust.
It is given by two coordinates of the rotation pole in a terrestrial frame, measured in seconds of arc.
Its main parts are the free [[Chandler wobble|9RESF83PY2]], with a period of about $433$ days, and an annual oscillation forced by the seasons.
A slow drift is added to them.
Polar motion must be known to convert between terrestrial and celestial coordinates.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The point where the spin axis pierces the Earth near the North Pole is not fixed.
It wanders around a circle of a few metres, roughly once every fourteen months, and the circle grows and shrinks.
This happens because the Earth is not a perfect rigid ball: air, water and ice move around, and the Earth's axis of spin is not exactly its axis of symmetry.
Astronomers first noticed it as small changes in the latitudes of observatories.
