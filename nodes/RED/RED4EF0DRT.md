## Statement

Consider stars in [[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]] and thermal equilibrium, described by the equations of [[stellar structure|SBKFDNEMFF]].
The *Vogt–Russell theorem* asserts that the structure of such a star is determined by its mass and by the distribution of its chemical composition.
In particular its radius, luminosity and effective temperature are determined by these data.

## Remarks

The reason is a count of conditions.
The equations of stellar structure are four first-order differential equations in the radius.
The four boundary conditions at the centre and at the surface, with the total mass and the composition, leave no free parameter in the generic case.
Example: for zero-age main-sequence stars of one uniform composition, the theorem predicts that position on the main sequence depends only on mass.
This matches the narrow main sequence in the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram.
Despite its name the statement is not a theorem in the mathematical sense.
The boundary value problem is nonlinear, and for some masses and compositions it has more than one solution, so uniqueness can fail.
Rotation, magnetic fields and the history of the star are also ignored by the statement.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rule says that if you know how heavy a star is and what it is made of, you know everything else about it.
That includes its size, its brightness and its colour.
This explains why stars of the same mass and age look alike.
It is a very good rule of thumb, but it is not always exactly true.

### L2 human

The principle explains why the main sequence is a curve and not a scattered cloud: for one composition, mass is the only free parameter.
As a star ages, nuclear burning changes its composition, and the same principle says that the star then moves along an evolutionary track fixed by its initial mass and composition.
Mathematically it is a claim of existence and uniqueness for a nonlinear boundary value problem, and only existence near known solutions and local uniqueness can be expected in general.
