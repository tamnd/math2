## Summary

The Stommel box model describes the thermohaline circulation with two well-mixed boxes of sea water, at low and at high latitude.
The boxes are joined by a flow driven by their difference in density.
Henry Stommel introduced it in 1961.
Temperature and salinity act on the density in opposite directions.
The model shows that this competition can give two stable states of circulation for the same forcing.
It is the simplest model of the possible collapse of the Atlantic overturning circulation, and an example of [[saddle-node bifurcations|MAP5J6HK0T]] and hysteresis in climate.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture two tanks of sea water, a warm and salty one for the tropics and a cold one for the polar seas, joined by pipes.
Cold water is heavy and salty water is heavy, so the two effects fight over which tank has the denser water.
The denser water sinks and flows toward the other tank, and the flow carries heat and salt with it.
If the polar tank is made fresher, for example by melting ice, the flow can weaken and then suddenly reverse.
Making the polar tank salty again does not simply undo the change: the system can stay in its new state.
