## Summary

The Lambda-CDM model is the standard model of cosmology.
It is a spatially flat [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker|ECJMS4VDTF]] universe containing a positive [[cosmological constant|WVKKDY9V61]] $\Lambda$, cold dark matter, ordinary matter and radiation.
With a handful of parameters it fits the cosmic microwave background, the large-scale distribution of galaxies and the accelerated expansion seen in distant supernovae.
Today about $70\%$ of its energy density is in $\Lambda$ and about $30\%$ in matter.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Lambda-CDM is the name of the recipe that cosmologists use for the universe.
"Lambda" is the cosmological constant, a dark energy that makes the expansion speed up.
"CDM" means cold dark matter: invisible matter that moves slowly and clumps under gravity to seed galaxies.
Early on radiation dominated, then matter, and now the cosmological constant does, so the expansion first slowed down and then started to accelerate.
