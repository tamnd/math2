## Definition

The dual basis of a basis is the family of linear forms that read off the coordinates in that basis.
Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$.
Let $B = (b_1, \dots, b_n)$ be a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $V$.
The *dual basis* of $B$ is the family $(b_1^*, \dots, b_n^*)$ of elements of the [[dual space|0M856Q1AMZ]] $V^*$ defined by
$$b_i^*(b_j) = \delta_{ij} \qquad (1 \le i, j \le n),$$
where $\delta_{ij} = 1$ if $i = j$ and $\delta_{ij} = 0$ if $i \ne j$.

## Notation

Upper indices $b^1, \dots, b^n$ are common in physics.
Mathlib writes `Module.Basis.dualBasis`.

## Remarks

The definition prescribes the values of each $b_i^*$ on the basis $B$ only.
That is enough, because a linear map can take any values on a basis and is then unique, by the [[theorem on maps determined by a basis|0R8X9XA83A]].
So each $b_i^*$ exists and is unique, and by linearity
$$b_i^*(a_1 b_1 + \dots + a_n b_n) = a_1 \delta_{i1} + \dots + a_n \delta_{in} = a_i.$$

The condition that $B$ is a basis is needed.
If the family is dependent, say $b_2 = 2b_1$, then $b_1^*(b_2) = 2 b_1^*(b_1) = 2$ for every linear form, which contradicts $b_1^*(b_2) = 0$.
If the family does not span, the values on it do not fix a form.

For the standard basis of $F^2$, the dual basis is $e_1^*(x, y) = x$ and $e_2^*(x, y) = y$.
The [[dual basis|0ZD2CF6ZS0]] page shows that the dual basis is a basis of $V^*$, works out a non-standard basis, and explains why $b_i^*$ depends on all of $B$ and not only on $b_i$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each form of the dual basis reads off one coordinate.
The form $b_1^*$ gives the first coordinate in the basis $B$, the form $b_2^*$ gives the second, and so on.
The condition $b_i^*(b_j) = \delta_{ij}$ says exactly this, since the coordinates of $b_j$ itself are a $1$ in place $j$ and zeros elsewhere.
