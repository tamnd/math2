## Summary

The Fourier number $\mathrm{Fo} = \alpha t / L^2$ is the dimensionless time of heat conduction.
Here $\alpha$ is the [[thermal diffusivity|CS4ZZX092Z]], $t$ is time, and $L$ is a length of the body.
It compares the time elapsed with the time heat needs to diffuse across the body.
For bodies of the same shape with the same dimensionless initial and boundary data, solutions of the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]] depend on time only through $\mathrm{Fo}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heat needs time to spread through a body, and the time grows with the square of the size.
A cake twice as thick takes roughly four times as long to heat through.
The Fourier number measures elapsed time in units of this natural heating time.
When it is much smaller than $1$, the heat has only reached a thin layer near the surface.
When it is much larger than $1$, the heat has spread through the whole body.
