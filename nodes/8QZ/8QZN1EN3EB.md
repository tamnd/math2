## Summary

Nonimaging optics is the part of [[geometrical optics|90F3NWRHVD]] that designs systems to transfer light from a source to a target.
The aim is efficient transfer, not the formation of an image.
Typical goals are to concentrate sunlight onto a small absorber or to spread the light of a lamp evenly over a surface.
Its basic tool is the conservation of [[etendue|M8FEBN0QSE]], which gives an upper bound on how strongly light can be concentrated.
Designs such as the compound parabolic concentrator reach this bound in two dimensions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A camera lens must send each point of a scene to one point of the film.
A solar concentrator does not care about the picture; it only needs to gather as much sunlight as possible onto a small cell.
Dropping the need for an image allows much better collection.
There is still a limit: light cannot be squeezed into a smaller area without spreading over a wider range of directions.
