## Statement

Consider a [[Yang–Mills theory|A0J2688KZF]] in four dimensions with gauge group $SU(N)$, $N \ge 2$, and gauge coupling $g$.
Couple it to $n_f$ Dirac fermions in the fundamental representation.
Then, at one loop in perturbation theory, the beta function of $g$ is
$$\beta(g) = \mu \frac{dg}{d\mu} = -\frac{g^3}{16\pi^2}\left(\frac{11}{3}N - \frac{2}{3}n_f\right).$$
So $\beta(g) < 0$ for small $g > 0$ exactly when $n_f < 11N/2$.
In that case the running coupling $g(\mu)$ tends to $0$ as the energy scale $\mu$ tends to infinity.

## Remarks

The coefficient comes from the one-loop vacuum polarization of the gauge field.
Gluon and ghost loops give $+\tfrac{11}{3}N$ to the bracket, and each fermion flavour gives $-\tfrac23$.
For a general compact simple group the bracket is $\tfrac{11}{3}C_2(G) - \tfrac43 \sum_f T(R_f)$, with $C_2$ the adjoint Casimir and $T$ the index of the fermion representations.

Solving the equation with $b_0 = \tfrac{11}{3}N - \tfrac23 n_f > 0$ gives $g(\mu)^2 = 8\pi^2/(b_0 \log(\mu/\Lambda))$ for a constant scale $\Lambda$.
For quantum chromodynamics, $N = 3$ and the bound is $n_f \le 16$; with the six known quark flavours it holds.
In quantum electrodynamics the sign is the opposite, so QED is not asymptotically free.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In most theories, forces get stronger when particles come very close and collide at high energy.
In the theory of quarks and gluons it is the other way round.
At very high energy the quarks behave almost like free particles, while at low energy the force grows strong and confines them inside protons and neutrons.

### L2 human

Asymptotic freedom was found by Gross and Wilczek and by Politzer in 1973.
It explains the scaling seen in deep inelastic scattering and makes perturbative QCD reliable at high energies.
In four dimensions, theories without non-abelian gauge fields are not asymptotically free, as Coleman and Gross showed.
The equation for the running coupling is the [[Callan–Symanzik equation|RAHKB0ZHG8]] applied to the gauge coupling.
