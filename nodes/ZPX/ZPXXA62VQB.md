## Summary

The lapse rate is the rate at which the temperature of the atmosphere decreases with height.
In the lower atmosphere it is on average about 6.5 kelvin per kilometre.
A parcel of dry air that rises without exchanging heat cools at the dry adiabatic lapse rate $g/c_p$, about 9.8 K/km.
Comparing the actual lapse rate with the adiabatic one decides whether the air is stable.
If it is not, convection starts, and with it clouds and storms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Mountain tops are colder than valleys.
The lapse rate says how much colder per kilometre of height: about 6.5 degrees on average.
Rising air expands because the pressure is lower above, and expanding air cools, by about 10 degrees per kilometre if it is dry.
If the surrounding air cools faster than that with height, rising air stays warmer than its surroundings and keeps rising, and the atmosphere is unstable.
