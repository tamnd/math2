## Summary

Nambu mechanics is a generalization of [[Hamiltonian mechanics|MPM0RHWCN6]] proposed by Yoichiro Nambu in 1973.
In it the motion is driven by two or more Hamiltonians instead of one.
The [[Poisson bracket|2S0MXDT4YH]] of two functions is replaced by a bracket of three or more functions, such as the Jacobian determinant on $\R^3$.
All the Hamiltonians are conserved, and the flow preserves volume, as in [[Liouville's theorem|9T7D9ZRWYY]].
The free rigid body is the standard example.
Its equations of motion have this form, with energy and squared angular momentum as the two Hamiltonians.
See the [[definition|VGGCXQWQQV]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In ordinary mechanics, one function, the energy, decides how the system moves.
In Nambu's version on three-dimensional space, two functions $H_1$ and $H_2$ decide it together.
The velocity is the cross product of their gradients, so it is perpendicular to both gradients.
Then the point moves along the curve where both $H_1$ and $H_2$ stay constant, the intersection of two level surfaces.
For a spinning body, these are a sphere of fixed angular momentum and an ellipsoid of fixed energy.
