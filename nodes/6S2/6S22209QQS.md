## Summary

A Petri net is a model of concurrent systems made of places, which hold tokens, and transitions, which consume and produce tokens.
Its underlying graph is a [[bipartite graph|0ZVQKTRKVQ]] with places on one side and transitions on the other.
The state of the net is a marking, the number of tokens in each place.
A transition may fire when its input places hold enough tokens.
Petri nets describe resources, synchronisation and concurrency, and questions such as reachability of a marking are central in verification.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw circles (places) and bars (transitions), with arrows from circles to bars and from bars to circles.
Put dots (tokens) in the circles.
A bar can fire when each circle with an arrow into it has a dot.
Firing removes those dots and puts one dot in each circle that the bar points to.
For example, a producer bar adds a token to a buffer place, and a consumer bar takes one out; the consumer cannot fire when the buffer is empty.
