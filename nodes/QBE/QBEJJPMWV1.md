## Summary

The equatorial coordinate system is the [[celestial coordinate system|YT0G7RE2GM]] built on the celestial equator, the projection of the Earth's equator onto the sky.
Its coordinates are [[declination|5HD86E1TEH]], the angle north or south of the celestial equator, and [[right ascension|RHNFB0HEW4]], measured eastward from the March equinox.
Star catalogues use it because the coordinates of a star change only slowly.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Project the Earth's equator and poles outward onto the sky.
Declination is then the "latitude" of a star and right ascension is its "longitude".
Unlike longitude on the Earth, right ascension is counted from a point in the sky, not from a city, so it does not turn with the Earth.
Polaris lies near declination $+89^\circ$, almost at the north celestial pole.
