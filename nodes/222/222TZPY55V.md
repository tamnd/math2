## Definition

Let $\Lambda \subset \R^2$ be a Bravais lattice with reciprocal lattice $\Lambda^*$, so that the [[Brillouin zone|EKTERAYFJ2]] with opposite edges identified is the torus $T = \R^2 / \Lambda^*$.
Let $k \mapsto H(k)$ be a smooth family of Hermitian Bloch Hamiltonians, with $H(k + G)$ unitarily equivalent to $H(k)$ for $G \in \Lambda^*$.
Let $E_m$ be a band of the [[band structure|QP0TK2M29V]] that does not touch any other band.
Let $u_k$ be a normalized eigenvector of $H(k)$ for $E_m(k)$, chosen smoothly on an open set of $k$.
The Berry connection and Berry curvature of the band are
$$\mathcal{A}(k) = i\langle u_k, \nabla_k u_k \rangle, \qquad \mathcal{F}(k) = \partial_{k_1}\mathcal{A}_2 - \partial_{k_2}\mathcal{A}_1 = -2 \operatorname{Im} \langle \partial_{k_1} u_k, \partial_{k_2} u_k \rangle.$$
The *TKNN invariant* (or Chern number) of the band is
$$C = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{T} \mathcal{F}(k) \, d^2 k.$$

## Remarks

The curvature $\mathcal{F}$ does not depend on the phase of $u_k$, so it is defined on all of $T$ even when no global smooth choice of $u_k$ exists.
The number $C$ is an integer: it is the first [[Chern number|PAD37GKDPD]] of the line bundle over $T$ formed by the eigenvectors.
Sign conventions for $\mathcal{F}$ and $C$ differ between sources.
For a band insulator with the Fermi energy in a gap, the Hall conductance is $\sigma_{xy} = \frac{e^2}{h} \sum_{m \text{ filled}} C_m$.

Example: the two-band model $H(k) = d(k) \cdot (\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z)$ with Pauli matrices $\sigma_j$ and $d(k) = (\sin k_1, \sin k_2, M + \cos k_1 + \cos k_2)$ on $\Lambda = \Z^2$.
The lower band has $\abs{C}$ equal to the degree of $d/\abs{d} \colon T \to S^2$ in absolute value.
For $0 < M < 2$, the direction $(0, 0, 1)$ is reached at $k = (0,0), (\pi, 0), (0, \pi)$ with local degrees $+1, -1, -1$, so $\abs{C} = 1$; for $M > 2$ it is also reached at $(\pi, \pi)$ with local degree $+1$, so $C = 0$.

Non-example: at $M = 2$ the vector $d(k)$ vanishes at $k = (\pi, \pi)$, the two bands touch, and $C$ is not defined.
