## Example

Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Take rows $1$ and $2$ and columns $2$ and $1$, in this order, and form the $2 \times 2$ array
$$B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 4 \end{pmatrix}.$$
We show that $\det B$ is not a [[minor|0SD528P0EK]] of $A$, because a minor keeps its columns in increasing order.

1. By the [[2 by 2 formula|0ZN2J42SK7]], $\det B = 2 \cdot 4 - 1 \cdot 5 = 3$.
2. The minor with rows $1, 2$ and columns $1, 2$ uses the columns in increasing order:
$$\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 5 \end{pmatrix} = 5 - 8 = -3.$$
3. The array $B$ is this submatrix with its two columns swapped.
   A swap of two columns changes the sign of the determinant, as for rows in the [[effect of row operations|09YB3WQG38]], so $\det B = -(-3) = 3$.
4. The nine minors of order $2$ of $A$, for the row pairs $\{1, 2\}$, $\{1, 3\}$, $\{2, 3\}$ and the column pairs in the same order, are
$$-3, -6, -3, \qquad -6, -11, -4, \qquad -3, -2, 2.$$
   For example, rows $1, 3$ and columns $1, 3$ give $1 \cdot 10 - 3 \cdot 7 = -11$.
5. None of the nine numbers is $3$, so $\det B$ is not a minor of $A$.

## Remarks

The definition of a minor fixes the order of the chosen rows and columns, and this fixes the sign.
With any other order, the determinant is the true minor times the sign of the permutation that reorders the rows and the columns.
Here the column permutation is one swap, with sign $-1$.
If we reorder both the rows and the columns by a swap, the two signs cancel: rows $2, 1$ and columns $2, 1$ give the array with rows $(5, 4)$ and $(2, 1)$, whose determinant $5 - 8 = -3$ is the minor again.

The order matters most in a cofactor expansion, where a wrong order of columns gives a wrong sign in one term and so a wrong total.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When you cut a small square out of a table to form a minor, keep its columns in the order they had.
Swapping two columns of a square changes the sign of its determinant.
Here the shuffled piece gives $3$, while the correct minor is $-3$.

### L2 human

The two numbers $3$ and $-3$ have the same size, so a check of size alone cannot catch the mistake.
The safe habit is to list the chosen rows and columns in increasing order before you write the submatrix.
If a formula needs another order, for example in the general Laplace expansion, then write the sign of the reordering explicitly.
