## Summary

A Vogan diagram is a [[Dynkin diagram|CS73JZQS66]] with extra marks that records a [[real form|59DHW3BZTW]] of a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]].
The marks come from a Cartan involution acting on the roots of a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra.
Vogan diagrams give a short route to the classification of real simple Lie algebras.
They play the role for compact Cartan subalgebras that [[Satake diagrams|KY3M36JCE8]] play for maximally split ones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start with the Dynkin diagram of a complex Lie algebra, a set of dots joined by lines.
A real form of the algebra is encoded by two kinds of marks on it.
Some pairs of dots are joined by a curved arrow, which says that a symmetry swaps them.
Some of the other dots are painted black, which says that the matching root is "noncompact".
For example, one white dot is the compact algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, and one black dot is $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\R)$.
