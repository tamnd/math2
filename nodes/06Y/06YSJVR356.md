## Definition

The geometric multiplicity of an eigenvalue is the dimension of its space of eigenvectors.
Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and let $T \colon V \to V$ be a linear map.
Let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $T$.
The *geometric multiplicity* of $\lambda$ is the [[dimension|03GSZ8RTZJ]]
$$\dim E_\lambda, \qquad E_\lambda = \ker(T - \lambda \operatorname{id}_V),$$
of the [[eigenspace|0G0RFPG8HR]] $E_\lambda$.
For a square matrix $A$ over $F$, it is the geometric multiplicity of $\lambda$ for the map $x \mapsto Ax$.

## Remarks

The finite dimension makes the number finite, and the condition that $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue makes $E_\lambda \ne 0$, so the number is at least $1$.
Since every basis of $E_\lambda$ is a list of independent eigenvectors and every independent list of eigenvectors for $\lambda$ lies in $E_\lambda$, the number is the largest size of an independent list of eigenvectors for $\lambda$.
For an $n \times n$ matrix, rank-nullity gives $\dim E_\lambda = n - \operatorname{rank}(A - \lambda I)$, which is how we compute it.

For the [[shear|HTAWRAC3QE]] $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ the matrix $A - I = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $1$, so the geometric multiplicity of $1$ is $2 - 1 = 1$.
The [[algebraic multiplicity|0FY42N02DV]] of $1$ is $2$, which shows that the two numbers differ in general.
They always satisfy $1 \le \dim E_\lambda \le a(\lambda)$, by the theorem on [[the dimension of an eigenspace|FWY8A9M5HK]].
A matrix is [[diagonalizable|07SQCNV233]] if and only if $p_A$ splits and the two multiplicities agree for each eigenvalue.
The [[concept page|099TWVJ909]] has the worked examples.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The geometric multiplicity counts the independent directions that the map stretches by $\lambda$.
For the identity of space it is $3$, since every direction is kept.
For a shear of the plane it is $1$, since only the horizontal direction is kept.
When it is smaller than the algebraic multiplicity, the matrix cannot be made diagonal.
