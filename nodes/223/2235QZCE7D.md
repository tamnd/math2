## Definition

Consider a single-species [[population model|1YEMFQ1H32]] $N' = N \, g(N)$, where $N \ge 0$ is the population size and $g \colon [0, \infty) \to \R$ is the per capita growth rate.
The model has an *Allee effect* if $g$ is strictly increasing on some interval $[0, a]$ with $a > 0$.
The Allee effect is *strong* if moreover $g(0) < 0$, and *weak* if $g(0) \ge 0$.

## Remarks

Example: let $0 < A < K$ and $r > 0$, and let $g(N) = r \left(\frac{N}{A} - 1\right)\left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right)$.
Then $g(0) = -r < 0$ and $g'(0) = r\left(\frac{1}{A} + \frac{1}{K}\right) > 0$, so $g$ increases near $0$ and the effect is strong.
The [[equilibrium points|0N8NGKDZDZ]] are $0$, $A$ and $K$; the equilibria $0$ and $K$ are stable and $A$ is unstable.
Solutions with $N(0) < A$ tend to $0$, and those with $A < N(0)$ tend to the [[carrying capacity|X23CRWHRCH]] $K$.
Non-example: the logistic rate $g(N) = r(1 - N/K)$ is strictly decreasing, so the logistic model has no Allee effect.
The number $A$ in a strong Allee effect is called the Allee threshold.
