## Summary

The Solow–Swan model is the basic model of long-run economic growth.
Output is produced from capital and labour, a fixed share of output is saved and invested, and capital wears out at a fixed rate.
Capital per effective worker then follows a one-dimensional [[ordinary differential equation|0NT64JV96A]] that converges to a unique steady state.
The model shows that saving alone cannot sustain growth of output per worker; long-run growth comes from technological progress.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of an economy as a stock of machines that workers use.
Each year part of what is produced is saved to build new machines, while old machines wear out and new workers need machines too.
When there are few machines, each new one adds a lot of output, so the stock grows.
When there are many, the extra output is small and only covers the wear and the new workers, so the stock per worker stops growing.
With output $k^{1/2}$ per worker, saving rate $1/5$ and losses at rate $1/20$, the stock per worker settles at $k = 16$, where $\frac{1}{5} \cdot 4 = \frac{1}{20} \cdot 16$.
