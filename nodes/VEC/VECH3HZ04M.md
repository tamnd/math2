## Statement

No process can have as its sole result the transfer of [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] from a body to a body of higher [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]].

## Remarks

"Sole result" is the key phrase.
A refrigerator does move heat from a cold body to a hot one, but it needs [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] from outside, so the transfer is not its only effect.
The statement is a form of the second law of thermodynamics and is a law of nature, not a theorem.
It is equivalent to the Kelvin–Planck statement: a machine that violates one of them can be combined with an ordinary engine or refrigerator to violate the other.
Rudolf Clausius formulated it in the 1850s.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heat never flows by itself from a colder object to a hotter one.
To push heat "uphill", as a fridge does, one must spend energy from somewhere else.

### L2 human

The Clausius statement is the most direct formulation of the irreversibility of heat flow.
With a reversible Carnot cycle it gives Carnot's theorem: no engine between two reservoirs is more efficient than a reversible one.
From this one builds the thermodynamic temperature scale, the Clausius theorem and finally the entropy.
