## Definition

Let $G$ be a [[group|G5S1M9]], and let $H$ and $K$ be [[subgroups|0X1JXT47GA]] of $G$.
The product of $H$ and $K$ is the subset $HK = \{hk : h \in H, k \in K\}$ of $G$.

## Notation

When the operation of $G$ is addition, the product is written $H + K = \{h + k : h \in H, k \in K\}$.
The same formula defines the product $AB$ of any two subsets $A$ and $B$ of $G$.

## Remarks

The set $HK$ contains $H$ and $K$, because $h = he$ and $k = ek$.
The set $HK$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $HK = KH$, by the argument on the [[concept page|0PT52XQZD7]].
It need not be a subgroup.
In the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] $S_3$, two distinct subgroups of order $2$ have a product with $4$ elements, and $4$ does not divide $6$.
When $HK$ is a subgroup, it is the [[subgroup generated by|0RP7VWQ0YN]] $H \cup K$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To multiply two subgroups, multiply each element of the first by each element of the second.
The result contains both subgroups.
It can fail to be a subgroup, because a product $hkh'k'$ need not have the form $h''k''$.
In an abelian group, the product of two subgroups is always a subgroup.
