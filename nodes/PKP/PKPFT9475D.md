## Definition

*Spherical astronomy* is the branch of astronomy that determines the directions of celestial objects as seen by an observer.
Each direction is represented by a point of the [[celestial sphere|W7GD4WR497]].
Its subject is the description of these points in coordinate systems and the transformations between such systems.
It also studies how the points change with time and with the position of the observer.
Its main tool is [[spherical trigonometry|SXQGHWHFRF]].

## Remarks

Example: a typical problem of spherical astronomy is to compute the altitude of a star above the horizon.
The data are its right ascension, its declination, the time and the latitude of the observer.
It uses the spherical law of cosines in the triangle formed by the celestial pole, the zenith and the star.
Non-example: computing the mass of a star from the orbit of a companion is not spherical astronomy, because it needs dynamics and not only directions; it belongs to [[celestial mechanics|RCC89W11XQ]].
The field is often called positional astronomy, and its high-precision part is called astrometry.
