## Summary

Mach's principle is the idea that the inertia of a body is determined by all the matter in the universe, not by an absolute space.
The same should hold for the distinction between rotating and non-rotating frames.
It is named after Ernst Mach, who criticised Newton's notion of absolute space, and the name was given by Einstein.
It guided Einstein towards [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]], but it has no single precise formulation, and general relativity satisfies it only in part.
Frame dragging, where a rotating mass makes nearby inertial frames rotate, is often seen as a Machian effect of general relativity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Newton argued with a spinning bucket of water: the water surface curves when it rotates, so rotation seems to be absolute.
Mach asked: rotating relative to what?
His answer was that it rotates relative to the distant stars, and that without them there might be no inertia at all.
Mach's principle is this vague but influential idea that the far-away matter of the universe sets which motions count as unaccelerated.
