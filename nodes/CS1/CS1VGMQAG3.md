## Summary

Chapman–Enskog theory derives the equations of fluid dynamics from the [[Boltzmann equation|MH6CSWD7F0]] by an expansion in the [[Knudsen number|22V2W7KB1D]].
At leading order the gas is in local equilibrium and obeys the compressible [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]].
At the next order it obeys the compressible [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]], with viscosity and heat conductivity computed from the molecular collisions.
It was developed independently by Sydney Chapman and David Enskog around 1916 and 1917.
See the [[definition|TX0C8EQE7S]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A gas whose molecules collide very often is almost in equilibrium at every place.
Then its state is described by a few numbers at each point: density, velocity and temperature.
Chapman–Enskog theory computes the small departure from equilibrium caused by gradients of these numbers.
This departure carries momentum and heat, and it explains viscosity and heat conduction from molecular collisions.
For example, it predicts that the viscosity of a dilute gas does not depend on its density.
