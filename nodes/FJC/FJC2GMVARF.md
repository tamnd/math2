## Summary

Compressible flow is the flow of a fluid in which changes of density are significant and must be computed together with the velocity and pressure.
It is the regime of gases at speeds comparable to the [[speed of sound|SN4J6KP03C]], and its main parameter is the [[Mach number|GFHJ858AN1]].
Its equations, the compressible Euler and Navier–Stokes equations, couple mass, momentum and energy through an equation of state.
New phenomena appear that have no counterpart in [[incompressible flow|2JZ0C15A9C]], such as shock waves and choking.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air can be squeezed, but at walking speed one never notices it.
When air moves at a sizable fraction of the speed of sound, the pressure differences are large enough to change its density, and the flow behaves differently.
For example, in a nozzle a slow flow speeds up where the pipe narrows, but a supersonic flow speeds up where the pipe widens.
Engineers usually start to treat air as compressible above about a third of the speed of sound.
