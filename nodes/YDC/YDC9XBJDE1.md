## Summary

The S-matrix, or scattering operator, is an operator of [[scattering theory|CSHWP5PK4P]].
It sends the free state of a system long before a collision to its free state long after.
Its matrix elements between incoming and outgoing states give the probability amplitudes of all possible outcomes of a collision.
Conservation of probability makes it a [[unitary operator|MM0EJ2GDE4]] when the theory is asymptotically complete.
In quantum field theory it is the main observable of particle physics, and the LSZ reduction formula expresses it through correlation functions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a collision as a black box: free particles go in, free particles come out.
The S-matrix is the table that says, for each way of going in, how likely each way of coming out is, as a complex amplitude.
If nothing interacts, nothing changes, and the S-matrix is the identity.
For a particle scattering off a spherically symmetric potential, each angular momentum channel is only changed by a phase $e^{2i\delta_l}$, where $\delta_l$ is the phase shift of that channel.
