## Summary

A formally real Jordan algebra is a real [[Jordan algebra|6C2MCVB8C7]] in which a sum of squares vanishes only when every term is zero.
The condition copies the defining property of a [[formally real field|X32TSWJSYT]].
Jordan, von Neumann and Wigner introduced these algebras as models for the observables of quantum mechanics, and [[classified|AE979FBMPD]] the finite-dimensional ones.
In finite dimension, formally real Jordan algebras are the same as [[Euclidean Jordan algebras|N184N4TVZ2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

For real numbers, $a_1^2 + \cdots + a_n^2 = 0$ forces every $a_i$ to be $0$.
A formally real Jordan algebra keeps this rule for its own squares.
Real symmetric matrices are a good picture.
The trace of $X^2$ is the sum of the squares of the entries of $X$.
So a sum of squares of symmetric matrices can only be $0$ if each matrix is $0$.
This positivity is what makes these algebras suitable for describing measurable quantities in physics.
