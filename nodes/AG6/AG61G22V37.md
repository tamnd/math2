## Proof

We show that an invariant formula follows from its modal consequences, and then use compactness to replace these consequences by one modal formula.
We write $\mathfrak{M} \models \alpha[w]$ for first-order truth with $x$ read as $w$, and $\mathfrak{M}, w \models \varphi$ for modal truth.
Two results are cited without proof: the [[compactness theorem|1NR3HAWG9D]], and the fact that every structure has an $\omega$-saturated [[elementary extension|CGH8C8EA9N]] (see [[saturated model|RMTZ5J64FX]]).
A structure is $\omega$-saturated if every set of formulas with finitely many parameters that is finitely satisfiable in it is satisfiable in it.

### Part A: from right to left

1. By structural induction on $\varphi$, $\mathfrak{M} \models \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)[w]$ holds exactly when $\mathfrak{M}, w \models \varphi$.
   The variable case is $P(x)$, the Boolean cases commute, and the case $\lozenge$ is the truth clause of $\lozenge$ written in first-order logic.
2. By step 1 and [[bisimulation invariance|TKFFTAX30E]], $\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$ is invariant under bisimulation, and so is every formula equivalent to it.

### Part B: a world with the same modal theory

Let $\alpha(x)$ be invariant under bisimulation.

3. Let $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha) = \{\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi) : \alpha \models \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)\}$ be the set of modal consequences of $\alpha$.
   Steps 4 to 16 show that $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha) \models \alpha$.
4. Let $\mathfrak{M} \models \mathrm{MOC}(\alpha)[w]$.
   Let $T(w) = \{\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi) : \mathfrak{M}, w \models \varphi\}$ be the translated modal theory of $w$.
5. We claim that $T(w) \cup \{\alpha\}$ is satisfiable.
   If not, compactness gives formulas $\varphi_1, \ldots, \varphi_n$ true at $w$ with $\alpha \models \neg \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \varphi_n)$.
6. That formula is $\mathrm{ST}_x(\neg(\varphi_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \varphi_n))$, so it lies in $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha)$, and by step 4 it is true at $w$.
7. But each $\varphi_i$ is true at $w$, so by step 1 the formula of step 6 is false at $w$.
   This contradiction proves the claim.
8. Let $\mathfrak{N} \models (T(w) \cup \{\alpha\})[v]$.
   Every modal formula true at $w$ is true at $v$, and if $\varphi$ is false at $w$, then $\neg \varphi$ is true at $w$ and so at $v$; hence $w$ and $v$ have the same modal theory.
9. Take $\omega$-saturated elementary extensions $\mathfrak{M}^*$ of $\mathfrak{M}$ and $\mathfrak{N}^*$ of $\mathfrak{N}$, by the cited fact.
10. Elementary extensions keep the truth value of every first-order formula at old elements.
    By step 1, $w$ in $\mathfrak{M}^*$ and $v$ in $\mathfrak{N}^*$ still have the same modal theory.

### Part B, continued: saturation and compactness

11. We claim that an $\omega$-saturated model $\mathfrak{K}$ is m-saturated.
    Let $u$ be a world and $\Sigma$ a set of modal formulas such that every finite subset of $\Sigma$ is true at some successor of $u$.
12. Let $\Gamma(y) = \{R(u, y)\} \cup \{\mathrm{ST}_y(\psi) : \psi \in \Sigma\}$, a set of formulas in $y$ with the one parameter $u$.
    By the assumption on $\Sigma$ and step 1, $\Gamma(y)$ is finitely satisfiable in $\mathfrak{K}$.
13. By $\omega$-saturation some element realizes $\Gamma(y)$.
    It is a successor of $u$ at which all of $\Sigma$ is true, which proves the claim.
14. So $\mathfrak{M}^*$ and $\mathfrak{N}^*$ are m-saturated, and $w$ and $v$ have the same modal theory in them.
    By the m-saturated case of the [[Hennessy-Milner theorem|DBDNYXA7ME]], $\mathfrak{M}^*, w \mathrel{\underline{\leftrightarrow}} \mathfrak{N}^*, v$.
15. We have $\mathfrak{N} \models \alpha[v]$ by step 8, so $\mathfrak{N}^* \models \alpha[v]$ because the extension is elementary.
16. By the invariance of $\alpha$ and step 14, $\mathfrak{M}^* \models \alpha[w]$.
    Because $\mathfrak{M}^*$ is an elementary extension of $\mathfrak{M}$, also $\mathfrak{M} \models \alpha[w]$, so $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha) \models \alpha$.
17. By compactness, a finite subset $\{\mathrm{ST}_x(\chi_1), \ldots, \mathrm{ST}_x(\chi_k)\}$ of $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha)$ already implies $\alpha$.
    Let $\varphi = \chi_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \chi_k$, with $\varphi = \top$ when $k = 0$.
18. Then $\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi) \models \alpha$ by step 17, and $\alpha \models \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$ because each $\mathrm{ST}_x(\chi_i)$ lies in $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha)$.
    So $\alpha$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$.

## Idea

A bisimulation-invariant formula is implied by its modal consequences.
Any world that satisfies them has the same modal theory as some world where $\alpha$ holds, and saturation upgrades "same modal theory" to "bisimilar".
Invariance then carries $\alpha$ across the bisimulation, and compactness shrinks the infinite set of modal consequences to a single modal formula.

## Variants

A second proof uses unravelings and locality.
One replaces both models by bisimilar tree models, and Gaifman locality shows that $\alpha$ depends only on a neighborhood of bounded radius around the root.
This route bounds the modal depth of $\varphi$ in terms of the quantifier rank of $\alpha$, and it is the route that also works over finite models, as in Otto's proof of Rosen's theorem.
The saturation argument also works for the guarded fragment and guarded bisimulation, with guarded types in place of modal theories.
