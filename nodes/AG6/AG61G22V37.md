## Proof

We write $\mathfrak{M} \models \alpha[w]$ for first-order truth with $x$ interpreted as $w$, and $\mathfrak{M}, w \models \varphi$ for modal truth.
Two results are cited without proof: the [[compactness theorem|1NR3HAWG9D]], and the fact that every structure has an $\omega$-saturated [[elementary extension|CGH8C8EA9N]] (see [[saturated model|RMTZ5J64FX]]).
A structure is $\omega$-saturated if every set of formulas with finitely many parameters that is finitely satisfiable in it is satisfiable in it.

Part A: from right to left.

1. By structural induction on $\varphi$, $\mathfrak{M} \models \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)[w]$ iff $\mathfrak{M}, w \models \varphi$. The variable case is $P(x)$ for $p$, the Boolean cases commute, and the case $\lozenge$ is the truth clause of $\lozenge$ written in first-order logic.
2. By step 1 and [[bisimulation invariance|TKFFTAX30E]], $\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$ is invariant under bisimulation. A formula equivalent to it is then invariant too.

Part B: from left to right.
Let $\alpha(x)$ be invariant under bisimulation.

3. Let $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha) = \set{\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi) : \alpha \models \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)}$, the set of modal consequences of $\alpha$. We first show $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha) \models \alpha$.
4. Let $\mathfrak{M} \models \mathrm{MOC}(\alpha)[w]$. Let $T(w) = \set{\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi) : \mathfrak{M}, w \models \varphi}$, the translated modal theory of $w$.
5. Claim: $T(w) \cup \set{\alpha}$ is satisfiable. Assume not. By compactness there are $\varphi_1, \ldots, \varphi_n$ true at $w$ with $\alpha \models \neg \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \varphi_n)$.
6. The formula $\neg \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \varphi_n)$ is $\mathrm{ST}_x(\neg(\varphi_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \varphi_n))$, so it lies in $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha)$. So it is true at $w$, by step 4.
7. But each $\varphi_i$ is true at $w$, by step 1 and the choice of $\varphi_i$. This contradicts step 6, so the claim holds.
8. Let $\mathfrak{N} \models (T(w) \cup \set{\alpha})[v]$. Every modal formula true at $w$ is true at $v$. If $\varphi$ is false at $w$, then $\neg \varphi$ is true at $w$, so $\varphi$ is false at $v$. So $w$ and $v$ satisfy the same modal formulas.
9. Take $\omega$-saturated elementary extensions $\mathfrak{M}^*$ of $\mathfrak{M}$ and $\mathfrak{N}^*$ of $\mathfrak{N}$, by the cited fact.
10. Elementary extensions preserve the truth of every first-order formula at old elements. By step 1, $w$ in $\mathfrak{M}^*$ and $v$ in $\mathfrak{N}^*$ have the same modal theories as before, so they satisfy the same modal formulas.
11. Claim: an $\omega$-saturated model $\mathfrak{K}$ is m-saturated. Let $u$ be a world and $\Sigma$ a set of modal formulas such that every finite subset of $\Sigma$ is true at some successor of $u$.
12. Let $\Gamma(y) = \set{R(u, y)} \cup \set{\mathrm{ST}_y(\psi) : \psi \in \Sigma}$, a set of formulas in $y$ with the one parameter $u$. By step 11 and step 1, $\Gamma(y)$ is finitely satisfiable in $\mathfrak{K}$.
13. By $\omega$-saturation some element realizes $\Gamma(y)$. It is a successor of $u$ at which all of $\Sigma$ is true. This proves the claim.
14. By steps 10, 11 and 13, $\mathfrak{M}^*$ and $\mathfrak{N}^*$ are m-saturated, and $w$ and $v$ satisfy the same modal formulas in them. By the m-saturated case of the [[Hennessy-Milner theorem|DBDNYXA7ME]], $\mathfrak{M}^*, w \mathrel{\underline{\leftrightarrow}} \mathfrak{N}^*, v$.
15. $\mathfrak{N} \models \alpha[v]$ by step 8, so $\mathfrak{N}^* \models \alpha[v]$ because the extension is elementary.
16. By the invariance of $\alpha$ and step 14, $\mathfrak{M}^* \models \alpha[w]$. Because $\mathfrak{M}^*$ is an elementary extension of $\mathfrak{M}$, $\mathfrak{M} \models \alpha[w]$.
17. Steps 4 to 16 show $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha) \models \alpha$.
18. By compactness, a finite subset $\set{\mathrm{ST}_x(\chi_1), \ldots, \mathrm{ST}_x(\chi_k)}$ of $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha)$ already implies $\alpha$. Let $\varphi = \chi_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \chi_k$, with $\varphi = \top$ when $k = 0$.
19. Then $\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi) \models \alpha$ by step 18, and $\alpha \models \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$ because each $\mathrm{ST}_x(\chi_i)$ lies in $\mathrm{MOC}(\alpha)$. So $\alpha$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$.

## Idea

A bisimulation-invariant formula is implied by its modal consequences: any world that satisfies them is modally equivalent to a world where $\alpha$ holds.
Saturation turns modal equivalence into bisimilarity, and invariance carries $\alpha$ across.
Compactness then reduces the infinite set of modal consequences to a single modal formula.

## Variants

A second proof uses unravellings and locality: one replaces both models by bisimilar tree models, and Gaifman locality shows that $\alpha$ depends only on a neighbourhood of bounded radius around the root.
This route gives an explicit bound on the modal depth of $\varphi$ in terms of the quantifier rank of $\alpha$, and it is the route that also works over finite models, as in Otto's proof of Rosen's theorem.
The saturation argument also works for the guarded fragment and guarded bisimulation, with guarded types in place of modal theories.
