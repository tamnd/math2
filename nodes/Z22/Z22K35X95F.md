## Example

Let $\varphi(x, y)$ be a quantifier-free formula of [[Heyting arithmetic|RXHJPVMPJH]].
*Markov's rule* says that if $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \neg \neg \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$, then $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$.
We derive it with the [[A-translation|6CCK0TT69P]] for $A = \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$, using the lemmas of the [[proof of conservativity|ANXVZG97MG]].

1. The formula $\neg \neg A$ is $(\exists y\, \varphi \to \bot) \to \bot$.
2. By Lemmas F2 and F3, HA proves its translation:
   $$(\exists y\, \varphi^A \to \bot \vee A) \to \bot \vee A.$$
3. We prove the premise.
   Assume $\exists y\, \varphi^A$, and let $y$ be an eigenvariable with $\varphi^A(x, y)$.
4. Lemma F4 gives $\varphi(x, y) \vee A$.
   In the first case ${\exists}\mathrm{I}$ gives $A$, so $A$ holds in both cases.
5. The rule ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ closes the case, since $y$ is not free in $A$.
   Then ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ and ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ give $\exists y\, \varphi^A \to \bot \vee A$.
6. The rule ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\bot \vee A$, and ${\bot}\mathrm{E}$ in the first case gives $A$.

So HA proves $\exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$, and HA is closed under Markov's rule.

## Remarks

Markov's rule is a rule, not an axiom.
The corresponding axiom, Markov's principle $\neg \neg \exists y\, \varphi \to \exists y\, \varphi$, is not provable in HA, although it is true in the standard model.
The [[Dialectica interpretation|HZ17ES9PDY]] validates Markov's principle, and Kleene's [[realizability|4H35VQ16DC]] validates it when the metatheory is classical.

The quantifier-free matrix matters.
Let $\psi(x)$ be $\neg \exists z\, T(x, x, z) \vee \exists z\, T(x, x, z)$, with Kleene's predicate $T$, and let $y$ be a variable that does not occur in $\psi$.
HA proves $\neg \neg \exists y\, \psi(x)$, since it proves $\neg \neg (B \vee \neg B)$ for every $B$.
HA does not prove $\exists y\, \psi(x)$, which is equivalent to $\psi(x)$, because a proof would decide the halting problem.

When $x$ is a numeral, the existence property of HA gives more: a numeral $n$ with $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \varphi(\overline{n})$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a computer program searches for a number with a property that a calculation can check.
A proof that "the search cannot run forever" does not, at first sight, look like a proof that "the search finds a number".
Markov's rule says that, in Heyting arithmetic, the first kind of proof can always be turned into the second kind.
For example, a proof that "it is impossible that no prime is larger than $100$" becomes a proof that "some prime is larger than $100$".

### L2 human

We run the argument for $\varphi(y) = (y \cdot y = 49)$.
Suppose HA proves $\neg \neg \exists y\, (y \cdot y = 49)$, and let $A = \exists y\, (y \cdot y = 49)$.
The translation gives HA a proof of
$$(\exists y\, (y \cdot y = 49 \vee A) \to \bot \vee A) \to \bot \vee A.$$
From $y \cdot y = 49 \vee A$ we get $A$ in both cases, so the premise holds, and HA proves $\bot \vee A$ and hence $A$.
The existence property then gives the numeral $7$, and HA proves $7 \cdot 7 = 49$ by computation.

The typical use is to remove a double negation from a theorem of HA that the negative translation produced.
Watch out: the rule holds for HA, but HA does not prove Markov's principle, so you cannot use the rule under an assumption.
