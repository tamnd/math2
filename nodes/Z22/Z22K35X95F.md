## Example

Let $\varphi(x, y)$ be a quantifier-free formula of [[Heyting arithmetic|RXHJPVMPJH]].
*Markov's rule* says: if $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \neg \neg \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$, then $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$.
We derive it with the [[A-translation|6CCK0TT69P]] for $A = \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$, using the lemmas of the [[proof of conservativity|ANXVZG97MG]].

1. The formula $\neg \neg A$ is $(\exists y\, \varphi \to \bot) \to \bot$.
2. By Lemmas F2 and F3, HA proves its translation $(\exists y\, \varphi^A \to \bot \vee A) \to \bot \vee A$.
3. We prove the premise. Assume $\exists y\, \varphi^A$, and let $y$ be an eigenvariable with $\varphi^A(x, y)$.
4. Lemma F4 gives $\varphi(x, y) \vee A$. In the first case ${\exists}\mathrm{I}$ gives $A$, so $A$ holds in both cases.
5. The rule ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ closes the case, since $y$ is not free in $A$. Then ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ and ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ give $\exists y\, \varphi^A \to \bot \vee A$.
6. The rule ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\bot \vee A$, and ${\bot}\mathrm{E}$ in the first case gives $A$.

So HA proves $\exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$, and HA is closed under Markov's rule.

## Remarks

Markov's rule is a rule, not an axiom.
The corresponding axiom, Markov's principle $\neg \neg \exists y\, \varphi \to \exists y\, \varphi$, is not provable in HA, although it is true in the standard model.
The [[Dialectica interpretation|HZ17ES9PDY]] validates Markov's principle, and Kleene's [[realizability|4H35VQ16DC]] validates it when the metatheory is classical.
If $\varphi$ may contain unbounded quantifiers, the rule fails: for $\varphi = \forall z\, \neg T(x, x, z) \vee \exists z\, T(x, x, z)$ the double negation is provable but the formula without it is not.
With $x$ fixed to a numeral, the existence property of HA then gives a numeral $n$ with $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \varphi(\overline{n})$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a computer program searches for a number with a property that can be checked by calculation.
A proof that "the search cannot run forever" does not, by itself, look like a proof that "the search finds a number".
Markov's rule says that, in Heyting arithmetic, the first kind of proof can always be turned into the second kind.
For example, a proof that "it is impossible that no prime is larger than $100$" becomes a proof that "some prime is larger than $100$".

### L2 human

We run the argument for $\varphi(y) = (y \cdot y = 49)$.

1. Suppose HA proves $\neg \neg \exists y\, (y \cdot y = 49)$.
2. With $A = \exists y\, (y \cdot y = 49)$, HA proves $(\exists y\, (y \cdot y = 49 \vee A) \to \bot \vee A) \to \bot \vee A$.
3. From $y \cdot y = 49 \vee A$ we get $A$ in both cases, so the premise holds.
4. So HA proves $\bot \vee A$, hence $A$.
5. The existence property then gives the numeral $7$, with $\mathsf{HA} \vdash 7 \cdot 7 = 49$.

The typical use is to remove a double negation from a theorem of HA obtained by the negative translation.
A trap is to confuse the rule with the axiom: the rule holds for HA, but HA does not prove Markov's principle.
