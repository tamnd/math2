## Example

Let $Q(x, y, z) = x^2 + 4xy + 2xz + 5y^2 + 8yz + 2z^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$.
We [[diagonalize|0Z3FEWZ8HA]] it by [[completing squares|XNCV97HTZ8]], and then we check the result with matrices.

1. The terms with $x$ fit in one square:
   $$x^2 + 4xy + 2xz = (x + 2y + z)^2 - (2y + z)^2.$$
2. Since $(2y + z)^2 = 4y^2 + 4yz + z^2$, we get $Q = (x + 2y + z)^2 + y^2 + 4yz + z^2$.
3. The terms with $y$ that are left also fit in one square, $y^2 + 4yz = (y + 2z)^2 - 4z^2$, so
   $$Q = (x + 2y + z)^2 + (y + 2z)^2 - 3z^2.$$
4. Put $u = x + 2y + z$, $v = y + 2z$ and $w = z$.
   Then $Q = u^2 + v^2 - 3w^2$.
5. The change is invertible: back substitution gives $z = w$, $y = v - 2w$ and $x = u - 2v + 3w$.
6. Check at three vectors.
   At $(1, 0, 0)$ we get $Q = 1$ and $(u, v, w) = (1, 0, 0)$, which gives $1$.
   At $(0, 1, 0)$ we get $Q = 5$ and $(u, v, w) = (2, 1, 0)$, which gives $4 + 1 = 5$.
   At $(0, 0, 1)$ we get $Q = 2$ and $(u, v, w) = (1, 2, 1)$, which gives $1 + 4 - 3 = 2$.
7. In matrices, [[the correspondence theorem|VN9AG8JKYT]] gives the symmetric matrix $A$ of $Q$, and step 5 gives $x = P(u, v, w)^{\mathsf{T}}$:
   $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 5 & 4 \\ 1 & 4 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
8. The columns of $AP$ are $A(1, 0, 0)^{\mathsf{T}} = (1, 2, 1)$, $A(-2, 1, 0)^{\mathsf{T}} = (0, 1, 2)$ and $A(3, -2, 1)^{\mathsf{T}} = (0, 0, -3)$.
   Multiplying by $P^{\mathsf{T}}$ on the left gives
   $$P^{\mathsf{T}} A P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

## Remarks

The columns of $P$ are the new basis vectors $(1, 0, 0)$, $(-2, 1, 0)$ and $(3, -2, 1)$, and they are orthogonal for the form.
The diagonal form has two positive coefficients and one negative coefficient, so the [[signature|07EZA80NEH]] of $Q$ is $(2, 1, 0)$.
As a further check, $\det A = 1(10 - 16) - 2(4 - 4) + 1(8 - 5) = -3$, and $\det(P^{\mathsf{T}} A P) = -3$ too, since $\det P = 1$.

The diagonal entries $1, 1, -3$ are not the eigenvalues of $A$.
The characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^3 - 8\lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3$, which is $3$ at $\lambda = 0$, $-8$ at $\lambda = 1$ and $-2$ at $\lambda = -1$.
So it has one root in $(-1, 0)$, one in $(0, 1)$ and one above $1$: two positive and one negative, as [[Sylvester's law|0WFED3K3DQ]] predicts.

If the coefficient of $z^2$ were $5$ in place of $2$, the last coefficient would be $5 - 4 - 1 = 0$, and the form would become $u^2 + v^2$, of rank $2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We remove the cross terms one variable at a time.
First we put all terms with $x$ into one square, and then all terms with $y$ that are left.
At the end only a multiple of $z^2$ is left over.

### L2 human

The typical use is to find the signs of a form without computing eigenvalues.
The result $u^2 + v^2 - 3w^2$ shows that $Q$ takes both signs.
It is positive at $(1, 0, 0)$, and it is $-3$ at the vector with $(u, v, w) = (0, 0, 1)$, which is $(x, y, z) = (3, -2, 1)$.
Check:
$$Q(3, -2, 1) = 9 - 24 + 6 + 20 - 16 + 2 = -3.$$
Watch out: the formulas for $u, v, w$ give the rows of $P^{-1}$, not the new basis vectors.
To get the basis, invert the substitution as in step 5 and read off the columns of $P$.
