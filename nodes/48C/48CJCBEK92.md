## Summary

The birth–death model of speciation is a random model of how the number of species in a group grows.
Each lineage independently splits into two at a constant speciation rate and dies out at a constant extinction rate.
The number of lineages is then a linear [[birth–death process|E8DAD7052E]], and the history forms a random [[phylogenetic tree|RK58WSHJSG]].
It is the standard null model for the shape and timing of phylogenies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of each species as a little clock that may ring at random times.
One kind of ring splits the species into two daughter species, another kind makes it go extinct.
Each species has its own clocks, and they run at the same rates for all species.
When splitting is faster than extinction, the group grows on average exponentially, but by chance a young group can still die out.
