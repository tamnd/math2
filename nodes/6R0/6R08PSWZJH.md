## Summary

The Darrieus–Landau instability is the hydrodynamic instability of a [[premixed flame|5P38MRV478]] front.
It comes from the expansion of gas across the flame.
Burnt gas is lighter than fresh gas, and the flow deflected by a wrinkled front makes the wrinkles grow.
In the model of a flame as a thin front, every wrinkle grows, at a rate proportional to its wavenumber.
It was found independently by Georges Darrieus and Lev Landau, and it explains why large flames are usually wrinkled or cellular.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a flat flame moving through a gas mixture, with burnt gas behind it.
The burnt gas is hot, so it takes up several times more room than the fresh gas.
If the flame front gets a small bump, the streamlines of the gas spread out in front of the bulge and slow the gas there.
The bulge then moves ahead even faster, so the bump grows.
In real flames, diffusion of heat smooths out very small bumps, so only wrinkles above a certain size grow.
