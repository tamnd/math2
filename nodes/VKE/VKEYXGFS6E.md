## Summary

The Pancharatnam–Berry phase is a phase that light acquires when its polarization is taken around a closed loop of states.
It depends only on the loop on the [[Poincaré sphere|J1T8NZCVTB]], not on how fast the loop is traversed.
Up to sign, it equals half the solid angle enclosed by the loop.
Pancharatnam found it for polarized light in 1956, and Berry described geometric phases in general quantum systems in 1984.
It is the optical case of [[holonomy|WHDZ3MK56B]] and is used to build flat optical elements with a geometric phase.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Change the polarization of a beam step by step and finally bring it back to the state it started in.
The beam is then in the same state, yet its phase has moved by an amount that remembers the path taken.
The amount is half the area that the path encloses on the Poincaré sphere.
It is like carrying an arrow around a loop on a globe while keeping it as straight as possible.
At the end the arrow has turned, by an angle equal to the enclosed area.
For a loop that encloses one quarter of the sphere, the solid angle is $\pi$ and the phase is $\pi/2$.
