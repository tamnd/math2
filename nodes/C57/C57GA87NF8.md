## Definition

Let $G$ be a [[locally compact group|AHV05ZBCYW]] with a left [[Haar measure|VY0HXD3GC4]] $\mu$.
For $f \in L^1(G)$ and $g \in L^2(G)$, let $\lambda(f)g = f * g$, where
$$(f * g)(x) = \int_G f(y)\, g(y^{-1}x) \, d\mu(y).$$
Then $\lambda(f)$ is a bounded operator on $L^2(G)$ with $\norm{\lambda(f)} \le \norm{f}_1$.
The map $\lambda$ is the integrated form of the [[left regular representation|PR8C073VWY]].
The *reduced group C\*-algebra* $C^*_r(G)$ is the closure of $\set{\lambda(f) : f \in L^1(G)}$ in the operator norm of the algebra $B(L^2(G))$ of bounded operators on $L^2(G)$.

## Remarks

The set $\set{\lambda(f)}$ is a subalgebra closed under adjoints, since $\lambda(f)^* = \lambda(f^*)$ with $f^*(x) = \Delta(x^{-1})\overline{f(x^{-1})}$, where $\Delta$ is the [[modular function|63TX60RTRQ]] of $G$.
So $C^*_r(G)$ is a [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]].
If $G$ is discrete, $\mu$ is counting measure and one may use the finitely supported functions in place of $L^1(G)$.

Example: let $G$ be a finite group.
Then $L^2(G) = \C^G$ is finite-dimensional, so the subalgebra $\lambda(\C^G)$ is already closed.
Since $\lambda(f)\delta_e = f$, the map $\lambda$ is injective, and $C^*_r(G)$ is the group algebra $\C[G]$ acting on itself by left multiplication, of dimension $\abs{G}$.

Example: for $G = \Z$, the Fourier transform turns $\lambda(f)$ into multiplication by $\hat f(z) = \sum_n f(n) z^n$ on $L^2(\mathbb{T})$.
The norm closure of these operators is the algebra of multiplications by continuous functions, so $C^*_r(\Z) \cong C(\mathbb{T})$.
Non-example: the closure of the same operators in the [[weak operator topology|4XM1VN1C1N]] is larger.
It is the algebra of multiplications by functions in $L^\infty(\mathbb{T})$, the [[group von Neumann algebra|2N7CR03Q1X]] of $\Z$.
It is not $C^*_r(\Z)$, because the definition takes the closure in the operator norm.

The reduced algebra is the image of the full [[group C*-algebra|JKANN4VBYH]] under the extension of $\lambda$.
This quotient map $C^*(G) \to C^*_r(G)$ is an isomorphism exactly when $G$ is an [[amenable group|CEAT9BESKK]].
