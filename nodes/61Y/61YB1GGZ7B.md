## Summary

A Wilson loop is the trace of the [[holonomy|WHDZ3MK56B]] of a gauge field around a closed curve, in a chosen representation of the gauge group.
It does not change under gauge transformations, and the Wilson loops of all closed curves, in suitable representations, determine the connection up to gauge equivalence.
Kenneth Wilson used their expectation values to state a criterion for the confinement of quarks: an area law for large loops signals confinement.
They also appear in knot theory, where Witten obtained the Jones polynomial from Wilson loops in [[Chern–Simons theory|F28G886PWD]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A gauge field tells how an internal arrow, such as the phase of a quantum particle, turns as the particle moves.
Carry the arrow around a closed loop and compare it with where it started: the net turn is the holonomy.
The Wilson loop records this net turn as a single number that does not depend on how one labels the arrows at each point.
For electromagnetism it is the phase $e^{iq\Phi/\hbar}$, up to sign convention, where $q$ is the charge of the particle and $\Phi$ is the magnetic flux through the loop.
This phase is the origin of the Aharonov–Bohm effect.
