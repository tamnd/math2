## Summary

Fermi normal coordinates are local coordinates around a whole timelike curve in [[spacetime|63ZKC7AFX2]], adapted to an observer moving along it.
The time coordinate is the proper time of the observer.
The space coordinates are measured along geodesics orthogonal to the curve, in the directions of a non-rotating frame.
Along a geodesic they make the metric equal to the Minkowski metric, with vanishing first derivatives.
So they realise the [[equivalence principle|QS7YCQK8K7]] along a whole world line, not only at one event.
The corrections to the flat metric at second order are given by the curvature, which describes tidal forces.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine the laboratory of a freely falling astronaut.
The astronaut uses the time of their own clock and lays out three perpendicular rulers, which they keep non-rotating.
Fermi normal coordinates are the positions and times measured this way.
Close to the astronaut, spacetime looks flat in these coordinates; tides appear only as small corrections that grow with the square of the distance.
