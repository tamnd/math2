## Summary

The semidirect product $\mathfrak n \rtimes_\varphi \mathfrak h$ is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] built from two Lie algebras $\mathfrak n$ and $\mathfrak h$.
It also needs an action $\varphi$ of $\mathfrak h$ on $\mathfrak n$ by derivations.
In the result $\mathfrak n$ is an ideal, $\mathfrak h$ is a subalgebra, and the bracket of $\mathfrak h$ with $\mathfrak n$ is given by the action.
It is the Lie algebra analogue of the [[semidirect product|0H3WZXCZNF]] of groups, and it gives exactly the split [[extensions|8264A9XM74]].
The Lie algebra of the Euclidean motion group, $\R^3 \rtimes \mathfrak{so}_3(\R)$, is an example.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take two Lie algebras and let the second one act on the first by infinitesimal symmetries.
The semidirect product puts them side by side and uses the action to define brackets between them.
For example, rotations act on translations of space, and together they give the Lie algebra of all rigid motions.
If the action is zero, one gets the plain direct sum.
