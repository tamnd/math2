## Statement

Let $S$ be a finite set, and let $\Phi = (\Phi_A)$ be a translation-invariant interaction on $\Z^d$.
Here $A$ runs over the finite subsets of $\Z^d$ and $\Phi_A$ is a real function of the spins in $A$.
Assume $\Phi$ is absolutely summable: $\sum_{A \ni 0} \norm{\Phi_A}_\infty < \infty$.
Let $\Lambda_n = \set{-n, \dots, n}^d$ and $H_n(\sigma) = \sum_{A \subseteq \Lambda_n} \Phi_A(\sigma)$ for $\sigma \in S^{\Lambda_n}$.
Then the following limits exist:
$$p(\beta) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{\abs{\Lambda_n}} \ln \sum_{\sigma \in S^{\Lambda_n}} e^{-\beta H_n(\sigma)}, \qquad \beta \in \R,$$
$$s(e) = \lim_{\delta \downarrow 0} \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{\abs{\Lambda_n}} \ln \# \set{\sigma \in S^{\Lambda_n} : \abs{H_n(\sigma) / \abs{\Lambda_n} - e} < \delta}, \qquad e \in \R,$$
with $\ln 0 = -\infty$.
The [[microcanonical|TDGEGWHXCT]] entropy density $s$ is concave and upper semicontinuous, and it is related to the [[canonical|G1P1A0N7NZ]] pressure $p$ by
$$p(\beta) = \sup_{e \in \R} \big( s(e) - \beta e \big), \qquad s(e) = \inf_{\beta \in \R} \big( p(\beta) + \beta e \big).$$

## Remarks

Proof idea: approximate additivity of $\ln$ of counts over disjoint boxes, up to boundary terms, gives the existence of the limits and the concavity of $s$.
The first identity is Laplace's method: the sum defining $Z$ is dominated by the energies that maximize $s(e) - \beta e$.
The second follows from the first by the duality of the [[Legendre transformation|ZQYMWVT1TT]] for concave upper semicontinuous functions.
In large deviations language, $\ln \abs{S} - s(e)$ is the rate function of the energy per site under the uniform measure, and the first identity is Varadhan's lemma.
Example: for independent spins with $\Phi_{\set{x}}(\sigma) = -h \sigma_x$ and $S = \set{-1, +1}$, one has $p(\beta) = \ln (2 \cosh \beta h)$, and $s$ is the binary entropy of the fraction of up spins.
Equivalence at the level of measures, rather than of thermodynamic functions, needs extra hypotheses and can fail at first-order phase transitions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The microcanonical ensemble fixes the energy, and the canonical ensemble fixes the temperature.
For a large system the two descriptions give the same thermodynamics, because in the canonical ensemble the energy per particle is almost certain.
This theorem makes the claim exact for lattice systems: the entropy and the free energy determine each other.

### L2 human

The two thermodynamic functions are Legendre transforms of each other, as in classical thermodynamics.
So one may compute in the most convenient ensemble, usually the canonical one, and translate.
Where $s$ has a linear piece, a whole interval of energies corresponds to one value of $\beta$; this is the signature of a first-order phase transition with phase coexistence.
For systems with long-range interactions, such as gravitating particles, $s$ need not be concave and the ensembles can genuinely differ.
