## Definition

Let a [[reference frame|HNXK6CJ5B2]] turn about a fixed origin relative to an inertial frame.
Its angular velocity $\omega(t) \in \R^3$ is a differentiable function of time $t$.
Let $\dot\omega = d\omega/dt$ be its [[angular acceleration|7M4AZSA2MN]].
Let a particle of mass $m > 0$ have position $r \in \R^3$ in the rotating frame, measured from the origin.
The *Euler force* on the particle is the [[fictitious force|ZN2Z2HJY9Z]]
$$F_{\mathrm{E}} = -m\, \dot\omega \times r,$$
where $\times$ is the [[cross product|0SEDGXG9S3]].

## Remarks

Example: let the frame turn about the $z$-axis with $\omega(t) = (0, 0, t)$, so $\dot\omega = (0, 0, 1)$.
For $m = 2$ and $r = (3, 0, 0)$ one gets $F_{\mathrm{E}} = -2\, (0, 0, 1) \times (3, 0, 0) = (0, -6, 0)$.
This points against the direction in which the frame speeds up its turning.

Non-example: if $\omega$ is constant, then $\dot\omega = 0$ and the Euler force is zero, even though the centrifugal force is not.
The Euler force is also called the azimuthal force or transverse force.
