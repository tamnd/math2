## Summary

Spherical astronomy is the part of astronomy that describes where celestial objects appear in the sky.
It also describes how these positions change with time and with the place of the observer.
It treats every object as a point on the [[celestial sphere|W7GD4WR497]] and computes with the tools of [[spherical trigonometry|SXQGHWHFRF]].
It is the base of timekeeping, navigation by the stars and the making of star catalogues.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Look at the night sky and forget how far away the stars are: they seem to sit on a huge dome around you.
Spherical astronomy is the mathematics of that dome.
It gives each star two angles, like latitude and longitude on the Earth, and tells how those angles change as the Earth turns and moves around the Sun.
For example, it answers when a given star will be highest in the sky tonight in a given city.
