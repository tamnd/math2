## Summary

Parastatistics is a family of possible exchange statistics for identical particles that generalizes Bose and Fermi statistics.
It was introduced by H. S. Green in 1953 through trilinear commutation relations for creation and annihilation operators.
Each paraboson or parafermion field has an integer order $p \ge 1$, and order $1$ gives ordinary bosons or fermions.
For parafermions of order $p$, at most $p$ particles can occupy the same state.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For ordinary particles, the state of many identical particles is either fully symmetric (bosons) or fully antisymmetric (fermions) under swaps.
Parastatistics allows more general patterns, described by representations of the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] of higher dimension.
A simple consequence is a relaxed exclusion principle: parafermions of order $2$ allow two particles per state instead of one.
No elementary particle is known to obey parastatistics, but the idea is used to test the assumptions behind the spin-statistics connection.
