## Statement

The tautology $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has Frege proofs of size polynomial in $n$, whose lines have depth about $\log n$.
Ajtai's theorem says that if every line must have depth at most a fixed number, such as $3$, then the size grows faster than every polynomial in $n$.

Fix a [[Frege system|J2BAR09TPF]] $\mathcal F$ over $\neg, \land, \lor$, and fix a depth $d \ge 1$.
Let $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ be the CNF of the [[pigeonhole formulas|RJ571F7FF0]], with $n + 1$ pigeons and $n$ holes.

*Superpolynomial form (Ajtai).* For every polynomial $q$ there is $n_0$ such that, for every $n \ge n_0$, no depth-$d$ $\mathcal F$-proof of $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has size at most $q(n)$.

*Exponential form (Krajíček, Pudlák and Woods; Pitassi, Beame and Impagliazzo).* There are $\varepsilon_d > 0$ and $n_0$ such that, for every $n \ge n_0$, every depth-$d$ $\mathcal F$-proof of $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has size at least $2^{n^{\varepsilon_d}}$.

The proof system is [[bounded-depth Frege|41QJ1WEMJ3]] of depth $d$, and the measure is size in symbols.
The constant $\varepsilon_d$ decreases exponentially in $d$.
The same bounds hold for depth-$d$ refutations of the CNF $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$, with every line of depth at most $d$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The depth must be bounded.
Without a bound the theorem fails, since [[Buss's theorem|VRX23FZV21]] gives polynomial-size Frege proofs with lines of depth that grows like $\log n$.
The depth must be fixed before $n$ grows.
The constants $n_0$ and $\varepsilon_d$ depend on $d$, and the bound $2^{n^{\varepsilon_d}}$ becomes weaker as $d$ grows, so the theorem says nothing about families whose depth grows with $n$.
The number of pigeons must be close to $n$.
For $2n$ pigeons and $n$ holes, Paris, Wilkie and Woods gave proofs of bounded depth and quasi-polynomial size $n^{O(\log n)}$ in 1988, so the exponential form fails there.

### What it gives you

Frege systems are superpolynomially stronger than every fixed depth of bounded-depth Frege, since the pigeonhole formulas separate them.
The theory $I\Delta_0(R)$ does not prove the pigeonhole principle for a new relation symbol $R$.
This follows by the translation of Paris and Wilkie of $I\Delta_0(R)$ proofs into polynomial-size bounded-depth Frege proofs; see [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]].
For resolution, whose lines are clauses, [[Haken's theorem|4PCEYEW3JG]] gives the stronger size bound $2^{\Omega(n)}$.

### The converse

The bound cannot be raised above $2^{O(n \log n)}$, since $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has a resolution refutation of that size, and its lines have depth $1$.
So for each fixed $d$, the least size of depth-$d$ proofs lies between $2^{n^{\varepsilon_d}}$ and $2^{O(n \log n)}$.

### Generalizations

The same method gives exponential lower bounds in bounded depth for the onto and functional versions of the pigeonhole principle, and for the counting principles modulo $p$.
Ben-Sasson proved exponential lower bounds in bounded depth for the [[Tseitin formulas|JXTCC6KXNG]] of expander graphs.
For proof systems with counting gates, such as $\mathrm{AC}^0[p]$-Frege, no superpolynomial lower bound is known.

## History

Miklós Ajtai proved the superpolynomial form in 1988, in "The complexity of the pigeonhole principle".
His proof used a nonstandard model of arithmetic and a forcing construction, together with a combinatorial lemma on restrictions.
Krajíček, Pudlák and Woods, and independently Pitassi, Beame and Impagliazzo, replaced the model theory by a switching lemma for matchings and obtained the exponential form.
Their journal versions appeared in 1995 and 1993.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Bounded-depth Frege proofs use formulas with a fixed number of alternations of "and" and "or".
Ajtai's theorem says that such proofs of the pigeonhole principle must be very long.
For example, for depth $3$ the length grows faster than $n^{10}$, faster than $n^{100}$, and faster than every fixed power of $n$.

### L2 human

We compare three proof systems on the same formulas $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$.

1. Resolution, of depth $1$: size at least $2^{cn}$ for a constant $c > 0$, by Haken's theorem.
2. Depth-$d$ Frege for a fixed $d$: size at least $2^{n^{\varepsilon_d}}$, by the exponential form of this theorem.
3. Frege, with no bound on depth: size polynomial in $n$, by Buss's theorem.

The typical use of the theorem is to separate bounded-depth Frege from Frege, and to prove independence results for weak theories of arithmetic.
A trap: the bound for depth $d$ gives no bound for Frege systems in general.

### L3 human

The theorem is the proof analogue of the fact that [[parity is not in AC0|W6T7X0QCFH]].
In both, a random restriction collapses every small circuit of depth $d$ to a shallow decision tree, by a switching lemma.
For proofs, the restrictions are partial matchings, and the decision trees ask where a pigeon goes or which pigeon a hole holds.
The collapse of a whole proof to shallow trees then contradicts its last line.
