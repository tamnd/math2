## Definition

A language $L \subseteq \set{0,1}^*$ is in *RP* if there are a polynomial $p$ and a [[probabilistic Turing machine|K99S0Q0814]] $M$ such that:

1. on every input $x$ and for every outcome of its coin flips, $M$ halts within $p(\abs{x})$ steps and outputs $0$ or $1$;
2. for every $x \in L$, $\Pr[M(x) = 1] \ge 1/2$;
3. for every $x \notin L$, $\Pr[M(x) = 1] = 0$.

The class *co-RP* is the class of complements of languages in RP.

## Remarks

Example: let $L$ be the set of triples $(A, B, C)$ of $n \times n$ matrices over $\F_2$ with $AB \ne C$.
The machine picks a uniform $r \in \F_2^n$ and accepts if $A(Br) \ne Cr$.
If $AB = C$, it never accepts; if $AB \ne C$, the matrix $D = AB - C$ has a nonzero row, so $Dr \ne 0$ for at least half of the vectors $r$.
So $L$ is in RP.

Non-example: a machine that accepts some $x \notin L$ with probability $1/100$ fails clause 3, even if its error is small.
Such two-sided error is allowed in [[BPP|PTD7E1PKV4]] but not in RP.

The constant $1/2$ in clause 2 can be replaced by any constant in $(0, 1)$, or by $1 - 2^{-q(n)}$ for a polynomial $q$, by independent repetition.
Clauses 2 and 3 show that an accepting run exists exactly for $x \in L$, so an accepting sequence of coin flips is a certificate of membership and $\mathrm{RP} \subseteq \mathrm{NP}$.
