## Definition

Let a tissue occupy the interval $[0, L]$ with $L > 0$, and let $c \colon [0, L] \to [0, \infty)$ be a strictly decreasing morphogen concentration.
Let $T_1 > T_2 > 0$ be two thresholds.
The *French flag model* assigns to a cell at position $x$ the fate
$$\text{fate}(x) = \begin{cases} \text{blue} & \text{if } c(x) > T_1, \\ \text{white} & \text{if } T_2 < c(x) \le T_1, \\ \text{red} & \text{if } c(x) \le T_2. \end{cases}$$

## Remarks

Because $c$ is [[strictly decreasing|0A7KV2WB7A]], each fate occupies an interval, and the boundaries lie at the points where $c$ crosses $T_1$ and $T_2$.
Example: take a source at $x = 0$, diffusion coefficient $D$ and decay rate $k$, on a long domain.
At steady state $c(x) = c_0 e^{-x/\lambda}$ with $\lambda = \sqrt{D/k}$, since then $D c'' = k c$.
The boundaries lie at $x = \lambda \ln(c_0 / T_1)$ and $x = \lambda \ln(c_0 / T_2)$.
Non-example: a constant concentration $c(x) = c_0$ is not strictly decreasing, and it gives all cells the same fate, so no pattern forms.
With $m$ thresholds the same rule gives $m + 1$ bands.
The model is different from Turing patterns, where the pattern arises from self-organisation rather than from a gradient set up by a localised source.
