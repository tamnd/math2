## Summary

Taylor dispersion is the enhanced spreading of a solute along a pipe or channel.
It comes from the combined action of a shear flow and diffusion across the flow.
At long times the average concentration over a cross-section obeys an [[advection–diffusion equation|DG49VBBT91]].
Its effective diffusivity can be far larger than the molecular one.
The effect is named after G. I. Taylor, who analysed it for flow in a pipe.
It matters in chromatography, in microfluidics and in the transport of substances in blood vessels and rivers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a pipe, fluid at the centre moves faster than fluid near the wall.
A band of dye is therefore stretched along the pipe: the middle runs ahead and the edges lag behind.
At the same time diffusion moves dye molecules across the pipe between fast and slow layers, so no molecule stays fast or slow for long.
The net result is that the band moves at the mean speed and spreads along the pipe like a diffusing cloud, but much faster than diffusion alone would spread it.
