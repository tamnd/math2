## Summary

Seismology is the science of earthquakes and of the elastic waves that travel through the Earth.
Its mathematical core is the theory of waves in [[linearly elastic|6W00ZECYT0]] media.
Inverse problems then recover the sources and the interior from recorded ground motion.
Almost everything known about the deep interior of the Earth, such as the liquid outer core and the solid inner core, comes from seismology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When rock breaks in an earthquake, it sends out waves, much like a stone thrown into a pond.
Some waves squeeze the rock ([[P-waves|EEPJJHC5JZ]]), some shake it sideways ([[S-waves|GZ7E9GHQYB]]), and some run along the surface.
Instruments called seismometers record these waves all over the world.
From the arrival times seismologists find where the earthquake happened and how fast waves travel at each depth.
For example, the fact that S-waves never cross the outer core shows that it is liquid.
