## Summary

A heat exchanger moves heat from a hot fluid stream to a cold one through a separating wall, without mixing the fluids.
Heat exchanger theory models it by an energy balance for each stream and a local rate law that uses an overall [[heat transfer coefficient|J821AD6YTE]].
From this model come the two standard design methods, the [[log mean temperature difference|62ESNKESQV]] method and the [[NTU method|KFNKGT1QQ9]].
The flow arrangement, such as parallel flow, counterflow or crossflow, changes how much heat a given exchanger can transfer.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a car radiator, hot engine coolant flows through tubes and outside air flows past them.
Heat passes through the tube walls from the coolant to the air, and the two fluids never touch.
Heat exchanger theory answers questions such as how large the exchanger must be to cool the coolant by twenty degrees.
Of the usual layouts, letting the two fluids flow in opposite directions turns out to transfer the most heat for a given size.
