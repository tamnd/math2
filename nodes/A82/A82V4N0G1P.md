## Example

Consider the claim "there is a unique real number $x$ with $x^2 = 4$".
We show that this [[existence and uniqueness statement|ACSC75R7R5]] is false, although a solution exists.

1. Existence holds: $x = 2$ gives $2^2 = 4$.
2. Uniqueness says: for all real $x$ and $y$, if $x^2 = 4$ and $y^2 = 4$, then $x = y$.
3. Take $x = 2$ and $y = -2$.
   Then $x^2 = 4$ and $y^2 = (-2)^2 = 4$, but $x \neq y$.
4. So uniqueness fails, and the claim "there is a unique real $x$ with $x^2 = 4$" is false.
5. The true statement is "there are exactly two real numbers $x$ with $x^2 = 4$, namely $2$ and $-2$".

## Remarks

A change of domain restores uniqueness.
Among the positive real numbers, $x^2 = 4$ has exactly one solution, $x = 2$.
If $x, y > 0$ and $x^2 = y^2$, then $(x - y)(x + y) = 0$ and $x + y > 0$, so $x = y$.
This is why $\sqrt{4}$ denotes $2$: the [[square root|0F7FQS6XY6]] $\sqrt{a}$ is the unique non-negative $x$ with $x^2 = a$.

So the phrase "the square root of $4$" names one number only when "non-negative" is understood.
The phrase "a square root of $4$" is correct for both $2$ and $-2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two numbers have the square $4$: $2 \cdot 2 = 4$, and $(-2) \cdot (-2) = 4$.
So "the number whose square is $4$" does not name one number.
Among positive numbers, only $2$ works.
So "the positive number whose square is $4$" names exactly one number.

### L2 human

Decide which of two claims is true over $\mathbb{R}$.

1. Claim A: there is a unique real $x$ with $x^3 = 8$.
   Existence: $x = 2$ works.
   Uniqueness: $x \mapsto x^3$ is strictly increasing, so two different numbers have different cubes.
   Claim A is true.
2. Claim B: there is a unique real $x$ with $x^2 = 8$.
   Existence: $x = 2\sqrt{2}$ works.
   But $x = -2\sqrt{2}$ also works, so Claim B is false.

Make this check before you write "the solution".
For a positive value $c$, the equation $x^n = c$ has two real solutions when $n$ is even, and one when $n$ is odd.
Watch out: a calculator returns one value of $\sqrt{8}$, and a student takes it as the only solution of $x^2 = 8$.
