## Definition

Let a [[rotating reference frame|HNXK6CJ5B2]] have [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]] $\Omega(t)$, expressed in the axes of the frame.
Let a particle of mass $m > 0$ have [[velocity|K160D2Z0CS]] $v$ relative to the rotating frame, also in those axes.
The *Coriolis force* on the particle is
$$F_{\mathrm{Cor}} = -2m\, \Omega \times v.$$
It is one of the terms of the [[fictitious force|ZN2Z2HJY9Z]] in the rotating frame.

## Remarks

Example: on the Earth the angular speed is $\abs\Omega \approx 7.29 \times 10^{-5}$ rad/s; take a body at the North Pole moving horizontally with speed $10$ m/s.
There $\Omega$ is vertical, so the Coriolis force is horizontal, perpendicular to $v$ and to the right of the motion, and its size per unit mass is $2 \abs\Omega \cdot 10 \approx 1.46 \times 10^{-3}$ m/s$^2$.
Non-example: the centrifugal force $-m\,\Omega \times (\Omega \times X)$ is a fictitious force but not the Coriolis force.
It depends on the position $X$ and not on the velocity, and it acts even on a body at rest in the frame, where the Coriolis force is $0$.
Since $F_{\mathrm{Cor}} \cdot v = 0$, the Coriolis force does no work.
A body moving parallel to $\Omega$ feels no Coriolis force.
