## Example

The claim is that $x + \frac{1}{x} \ge 2$ for every real number $x > 0$.
We first find a proof in scratch work, which goes backward from the claim, and then we write the final proof, which goes forward from a known fact.
This is the method of [[scratch work and the final proof|E97XXFW9MV]].

The scratch work transforms the claim.

1. Since $x > 0$, multiplying by $x$ keeps the direction of an inequality, and so does dividing by $x$.
   So the claim is equivalent to $x^2 + 1 \ge 2x$.
2. Subtracting $2x$ from both sides gives the equivalent inequality $x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0$.
3. The left side is $(x - 1)^2$, and a square of a real number is at least $0$, so the last inequality is true.
4. Each step was an equivalence, so the chain can run in the other direction, and the reversed chain is the proof.

The final proof reads as follows.

> Let $x$ be a real number with $x > 0$.
> The square of a real number is non-negative, so $(x - 1)^2 \ge 0$.
> Expanding the square gives $x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0$, and adding $2x$ to both sides gives $x^2 + 1 \ge 2x$.
> Since $x > 0$, we may divide both sides by $x$ and keep the direction of the inequality.
> This gives $x + \frac1x \ge 2$.
> $\square$

We check the final proof.

1. It starts from a fact that holds without the claim, $(x - 1)^2 \ge 0$.
2. Each step follows from the line before it, with its reason: expansion, addition of $2x$, division by $x > 0$.
3. It ends with the claim, and it uses the hypothesis $x > 0$ exactly at the division.

Check: $x = 2$ gives $2 + \frac12 = 2.5 \ge 2$, and $x = 1$ gives equality, $1 + 1 = 2$.

## Remarks

The final proof hides how it was found, and a reader may ask why it starts with $(x - 1)^2$.
The scratch work answers that question, but the proof does not need the answer to be correct.

The proof also tells when equality holds.
Equality $x + \frac1x = 2$ forces $(x - 1)^2 = 0$, so $x = 1$.

The hypothesis $x > 0$ is needed.
For $x = -1$ we get $x + \frac1x = -2 < 2$, and in the scratch work, multiplying by a negative $x$ would reverse the inequality.
The claim is the [[inequality of arithmetic and geometric means|0DRF0F3XC9]] for the two numbers $x$ and $\frac1x$, whose product is $1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To find a proof, we often start at the end and ask what would make the claim true.
Here the claim is that a positive number plus its reciprocal is at least $2$, for example $4 + \frac14 = 4.25$.
Working backward, we find that the claim comes down to "a square is never negative".
In the written proof we tell the story in the other order: we start from the square and arrive at the claim.
The backward search is the draft, and the proof is the clean copy.

### L2 human

We apply the same method to the claim $a^2 + b^2 \ge 2ab$ for all real numbers $a$ and $b$.
In scratch work, subtracting $2ab$ turns the claim into $a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \ge 0$, and the left side is $(a - b)^2$.
So the proof can start from that square.

> Let $a$ and $b$ be real numbers.
> Then $(a - b)^2 \ge 0$, because a square is non-negative.
> Expanding gives $a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \ge 0$, and adding $2ab$ to both sides gives $a^2 + b^2 \ge 2ab$.
> $\square$

Check: $a = 3$ and $b = 1$ give $10 \ge 6$.
The same method proves most inequalities that reduce to a sum of squares.
Watch out for a backward step that is not an equivalence, such as multiplying by a number of unknown sign, because the reversed chain then fails at that step.
