## Definition

Let $G = (N, \Sigma, P, S)$ be a [[context-free grammar|CTCY2R8Q77]].
The grammar $G$ is in *Greibach normal form* if every rule in $P$ has one of the following forms:

1. $A \to a A_1 A_2 \cdots A_k$, where $A \in N$, $a \in \Sigma$, $k \ge 0$ and $A_1, \dots, A_k \in N$;
2. $S \to \varepsilon$, allowed only if $S$ does not occur on the right side of any rule.

## Remarks

Example: the rules $S \to aSB$, $S \to aB$, $B \to b$ form a grammar in Greibach normal form.
Each right side is one terminal followed by nonterminals ($SB$, $B$, or nothing), so clause 1 holds; it generates $\set{a^n b^n : n \ge 1}$.
Non-example: the rule $S \to Sa$ fails clause 1, because its right side starts with a nonterminal; such a rule is called left-recursive.
Every [[context-free language|Q56EWEQYE2]] is generated by a grammar in Greibach normal form.
In such a grammar, a word of length $n \ge 1$ has derivations of exactly $n$ steps.
