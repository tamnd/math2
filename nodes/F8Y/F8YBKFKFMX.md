## Summary

The Richter magnitude scale, or local magnitude $M_L$, measures the size of an earthquake by a logarithm.
It is the logarithm of the largest amplitude recorded on a standard seismograph, corrected for distance.
Charles Richter introduced it in 1935 for earthquakes in southern California.
It was the first instrumental magnitude scale, and the idea of a logarithmic magnitude came from it.
It saturates for large earthquakes, and for these it has been replaced by the [[moment magnitude scale|8QWGDVEH46]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Richter wanted one number for the size of an earthquake, the same whichever station reported it.
He measured the biggest swing of the pen on a standard instrument and allowed for the fact that waves weaken with distance.
Because swings range from tiny to huge, he took the logarithm: each step of one magnitude is a ten times larger swing.
A small quake felt by people nearby is around magnitude $3$; a damaging one may be $6$ or more.
