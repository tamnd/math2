## Summary

This topic is a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]].
It covers formal proofs as combinatorial objects: cut elimination, normalization, the subformula property, refined sequent systems and proof-theoretic semantics.
The sub-area studies how proofs can be transformed, which normal forms they have, and what these normal forms show about the logic.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Proof theory|N4EDJNMTEF]]: an overview of the field, from Hilbert's program to proof complexity.
2. [[Cut rule|~5bc7d378]]: the rule for the use of a lemma, in its two forms.
3. [[Cut-elimination theorem|AYRZ98Y0NW]]: Gentzen's Hauptsatz, with the mix proof and the rank-reduction proof.
4. [[Subformula property|~694ac127]]: what cut-free proofs contain, and why this gives decision procedures.
5. [[Normalization theorem for natural deduction|~977456e4]]: Prawitz's detour reductions and normal proofs.
6. [[Midsequent theorem|~f03b8ca3]]: the sharpened Hauptsatz for prenex sequents, and Herbrand's theorem.
7. [[Inversion lemma|~42f9e538]]: which rules can be read backwards without loss.
8. [[Contraction-free sequent calculi|~e8b3934d]]: Kleene's G3 calculi and Dyckhoff's LJT.
9. [[Complexity of cut elimination|~29155909]]: the upper bound by a tower of exponentials, and a matching lower bound.
10. [[Proof-theoretic semantics|~50806e9d]]: meaning given by rules, harmony and tonk.
11. [[Hypersequent calculus|~b56e1b30]]: sequents in parallel, and the communication rule.
12. [[Display calculus|~c1a4d015]]: Belnap's display property and his general cut-elimination theorem.
13. [[Labelled sequent calculus|~aff1be18]]: Kripke semantics written into the rules.
14. [[Deep inference|~110b1691]]: rules applied inside formulas.
15. [[Focusing|~bc719aec]]: proof search with phases of invertible and non-invertible rules.
16. [[Identity of proofs|~8cb6c275]]: the open problem of when two proofs are the same.
17. [[Epsilon substitution method|~de0d8cf6]]: Hilbert's method for consistency proofs with epsilon-terms.

### Prerequisites

- [[Proof systems for first-order logic|TTCRX3NFNY]], with the [[sequent calculus LK and LJ|PZX9J3VEMT]] and [[natural deduction|PAQ4ZX2TX1]].
- [[The propositional calculus|JDK63589SA]] and [[first-order languages and syntax|PSRDJXRW53]], for formulas, substitution and subformulas.
- [[Structures, satisfaction and truth|NJDME6F845]], for the soundness arguments.
