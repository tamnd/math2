## Summary

Second sound is the propagation of heat as a wave, with a definite speed, instead of by diffusion.
It is a temperature and entropy wave, while ordinary sound is a pressure and density wave.
It was first found in [[superfluid|4PZBRJ78X4]] helium-4, and later in some very pure crystals at low temperature.
The [[Cattaneo–Vernotte equation|FFD8ZJBMMD]] gives a simple continuum model of such waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Normally a heat pulse spreads out and fades, like a drop of ink in water.
In superfluid helium a heat pulse instead travels as a sharp wave, much like a sound pulse, and can even bounce off walls.
In the two-fluid picture of the superfluid, a normal part and a superfluid part move in opposite directions.
The total density hardly changes, but the share of the warm normal part does, so the temperature oscillates.
This oscillation of temperature is what travels as second sound.
