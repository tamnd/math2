## Proof

We move $A$ across the inner product with the identity $\ip{Ax}{y} = \ip{x}{A^* y}$, prove part 1 from it, and get the other parts by replacing $A$ with $A^*$ and by taking complements.
Let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over $\mathbb{K} = \R$ or $\C$, with [[conjugate transpose|0EX045E2BP]] $A^* = (\overline{a_{ji}})$.
On $\mathbb{K}^n$ and $\mathbb{K}^m$ we use $\ip{x}{y} = \sum_j x_j \overline{y_j}$.

1. For $x \in \mathbb{K}^n$, entry $i$ of $Ax$ is $\sum_j a_{ij} x_j$, by the definition of the [[matrix-vector product|0PDVQEP0ZC]]. For $y \in \mathbb{K}^m$, entry $j$ of $A^* y$ is $\sum_i \overline{a_{ij}} y_i$.
2. Hence $\ip{Ax}{y} = \sum_i \sum_j a_{ij} x_j \overline{y_i} = \sum_j x_j \overline{\sum_i \overline{a_{ij}} y_i} = \ip{x}{A^* y}$. The reasons are that finite sums can be reordered and that $\overline{\overline{a}\, y} = a\, \overline{y}$.
3. Let $y \in \operatorname{Nul}(A^*)$. For every $x$, step 2 gives $\ip{Ax}{y} = \ip{x}{0} = 0$. Every vector of the [[column space|0F0RPPJ2TT]] has the form $Ax$, so $y \in \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$.
4. Let $y \in \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$, and take $x = A^* y$. Step 2 gives $\ip{x}{x} = \ip{x}{A^* y} = \ip{Ax}{y} = 0$, since $Ax \in \operatorname{Col}(A)$. Positivity gives $A^* y = 0$, so $y$ lies in the [[null space|0PQ2GGY1QV]] $\operatorname{Nul}(A^*)$.
5. Steps 3 and 4 give $\operatorname{Nul}(A^*) = \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$.
6. The [[double orthogonal complement|0PXN9G4MX1]] in $\mathbb{K}^m$ gives $\operatorname{Col}(A) = (\operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp)^\perp = \operatorname{Nul}(A^*)^\perp$. This proves part 1.
7. Conjugating and transposing twice gives back each entry, so $(A^*)^* = A$. Steps 5 and 6 for the $n \times m$ matrix $A^*$ then give $\operatorname{Nul}(A) = \operatorname{Col}(A^*)^\perp$ and $\operatorname{Col}(A^*) = \operatorname{Nul}(A)^\perp$. This proves part 2.
8. Over $\R$ every entry is its own conjugate, so $A^* = A^{\mathsf{T}}$. The columns of $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ are the rows of $A$, so $\operatorname{Col}(A^{\mathsf{T}})$ is the [[row space|0EP73HK4C4]] $\operatorname{Row}(A)$, and parts 1 and 2 give part 3.
9. Part 4. By the definition of the column space, $Ax = b$ has a solution exactly when $b \in \operatorname{Col}(A)$. By part 1 this holds exactly when $b \in \operatorname{Nul}(A^*)^\perp$, that is, when $\ip{b}{y} = 0$ for every $y$ with $A^* y = 0$.
10. Part 5, existence. Let $Ax = b$. The [[orthogonal decomposition|0SP7P0G7KQ]] for the subspace $\operatorname{Col}(A^*)$, together with part 2, gives $\mathbb{K}^n = \operatorname{Col}(A^*) \oplus \operatorname{Nul}(A)$. So $x = x_r + x_0$ with $x_r \in \operatorname{Col}(A^*)$ and $A x_0 = 0$.
11. Then $A x_r = Ax - A x_0 = b - 0 = b$, so $x_r$ is a solution in $\operatorname{Col}(A^*)$.
12. Part 5, uniqueness. Let $x_r$ and $x_r'$ be solutions in $\operatorname{Col}(A^*)$. Then $x_r - x_r'$ lies in $\operatorname{Col}(A^*)$, and $A(x_r - x_r') = b - b = 0$, so it also lies in $\operatorname{Nul}(A) = \operatorname{Col}(A^*)^\perp$.
13. Part 4 of the [[basic properties|T5Q51M5BAK]] gives $\operatorname{Col}(A^*) \cap \operatorname{Col}(A^*)^\perp = \{0\}$, so $x_r = x_r'$.
14. Part 5, solution set. If $Ax = b$, then $A(x - x_r) = 0$, so $x \in x_r + \operatorname{Nul}(A)$. Conversely, $A(x_r + x_0) = b + 0 = b$ for every $x_0 \in \operatorname{Nul}(A)$.

This proves parts 1 to 5.

## Idea

The identity $\ip{Ax}{y} = \ip{x}{A^* y}$ moves $A$ from one side of the inner product to the other.
So a vector orthogonal to every output $Ax$ is a vector that $A^*$ sends to zero, and conversely.
Everything else follows by taking complements twice and by swapping the roles of $A$ and $A^*$.

## Variants

Over $\R$ there is a proof by rows.
A vector $x$ lies in $\operatorname{Nul}(A)$ exactly when each row of $A$ has dot product $0$ with $x$, so $\operatorname{Nul}(A) = \operatorname{Row}(A)^\perp$ by part 3 of the basic properties.
Applying the same argument to $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ gives $\operatorname{Nul}(A^{\mathsf{T}}) = \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$, and the [[four fundamental subspaces theorem|73DVECPAT6]] uses this argument.
