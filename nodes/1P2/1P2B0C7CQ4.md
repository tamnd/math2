## Statement

For a [[normal modal logic|JS8W7PA6JK]] $\Lambda$, the satisfiability problem asks, given a formula $\varphi$, whether $\neg\varphi \notin \Lambda$.
That is, it asks whether $\varphi$ holds at some world of some model of $\Lambda$.
The size of $\varphi$ is its number of symbols.

1. Upper bound. The satisfiability problems of $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{T}$, $\mathsf{D}$, $\mathsf{K4}$ and $\mathsf{S4}$ are in [[PSPACE|JWC9CE07Q2]].
2. Lower bound (Ladner). If $\mathsf{K} \subseteq \Lambda \subseteq \mathsf{S4}$, then the satisfiability problem of $\Lambda$, and its provability problem, are PSPACE-hard.
3. So the satisfiability problems of $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{T}$, $\mathsf{D}$, $\mathsf{K4}$ and $\mathsf{S4}$ are PSPACE-complete.
4. $\mathsf{S5}$. A formula of size $n$ is $\mathsf{S5}$-satisfiable exactly when it is satisfiable in a model with at most $n$ worlds and the universal relation. The satisfiability problem of [[S5|VC3A2VWXND]] is [[NP-complete|JGZWHWA8P8]].
5. Universal modality. For $\mathsf{K}$ with a universal modality, or for $\mathsf{K}$ with global assumptions, satisfiability is [[EXPTIME|T9G5S33CA0]]-complete.

Provability is the complement of satisfiability of the negation, so parts 1 to 3 also hold for provability.
For part 4, provability in $\mathsf{S5}$ is coNP-complete.
The proof, cited with an outline, is in [[the proof of the complexity theorem|H2FYKJQAGC]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. The upper bound $\Lambda \subseteq \mathsf{S4}$ in part 2 is needed. $\mathsf{S5}$ contains $\mathsf{K}$ but is NP-complete, so it is not PSPACE-hard unless $\mathrm{NP} = \mathrm{PSPACE}$.
2. A second logic above $\mathsf{K}$ but not below $\mathsf{S4}$ is $\mathsf{K} + \square\bot$. There $\square\psi$ is always true and $\lozenge\psi$ always false, so its satisfiability problem is propositional [[satisfiability|K3WXSG7WBX]], which is in NP.
3. The lower bound $\mathsf{K} \subseteq \Lambda$ holds for every normal modal logic. It says that the theorem is about normal logics.
4. Part 5 needs the extra operator. Without it, $\mathsf{K}$ is in PSPACE by part 1. So $\mathsf{K}$ with the universal modality is not in PSPACE unless $\mathrm{PSPACE} = \mathrm{EXPTIME}$, which is open.

### Consequences

1. Validity of a modal formula in $\mathsf{K}$ or $\mathsf{S4}$ cannot be decided in polynomial time unless $\mathrm{P} = \mathrm{PSPACE}$.
2. The [[finite model property theorem|XNPJK6B921]] gives countermodels of size $2^n$; part 1 shows that they never need to be stored at once.
3. A single box already makes satisfiability as hard as [[true quantified Boolean formulas|WV2N01B4YV]].

### Generalizations

1. Multi-agent logics (Halpern and Moses 1992): $\mathsf{K}_m$, $\mathsf{T}_m$ and $\mathsf{S4}_m$ are PSPACE-complete for $m \ge 1$; $\mathsf{S5}_m$ and $\mathsf{KD45}_m$ are PSPACE-complete for $m \ge 2$, and $\mathsf{KD45}$ is NP-complete. See [[multimodal logic|ERG3KAQWYF]].
2. With a common knowledge operator and $m \ge 2$ agents, the multi-agent logics become EXPTIME-complete, by the same paper.
3. The bounded depth case: for formulas of fixed modal depth, $\mathsf{K}$-satisfiability is in NP. The reason is that the tree models of step 2 of the proof have polynomial size.
4. Not every normal modal logic is decidable: Isard proved in 1977 that some finitely axiomatized normal modal logic is undecidable, see [[Harrop's theorem|VSYQ1AZ9G7]].

## History

Ladner proved parts 1 to 4 for $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{T}$, $\mathsf{S4}$ and $\mathsf{S5}$ in 1977.
He used a tableau algorithm for the upper bound and a reduction from quantified Boolean formulas for the lower bound.
The same methods give the upper bounds for $\mathsf{D}$ and $\mathsf{K4}$.
Halpern and Moses extended the results to the logics of knowledge and belief with several agents in 1992.
The EXPTIME bounds for the universal modality are treated in chapter 6 of Blackburn, de Rijke and Venema, with the earlier literature in its notes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A computer can decide whether a modal statement can be true.
For the basic logics it needs only a moderate amount of memory.
But in the worst case it needs much more time than for ordinary propositional logic.
For S5, where every world sees every world, the problem is no harder than ordinary propositional satisfiability.

### L2 human

We see why $\mathsf{S5}$ is easier than $\mathsf{K}$, with $\varphi = \lozenge p \wedge \lozenge \neg p \wedge \square(p \vee q)$.

1. In $\mathsf{S5}$, every world sees every world, so a box or diamond does not depend on the world.
2. We need a world with $p$ and a world with $\neg p$; the box asks for $p \vee q$ at both.
3. Two worlds suffice: $u$ with $p$, and $v$ with $\neg p$ and $q$. In general, one world for each diamond, plus the actual world, is enough.
4. In $\mathsf{K}$, diamonds can be nested, as in $\lozenge(\lozenge p_1 \wedge \lozenge \neg p_1)$. Then the required worlds form a tree, and its size can grow exponentially with the depth.

The typical use is to choose the logic of a system by its cost.
A trap: an exponential model does not mean exponential space, since a search can keep one branch at a time.

### L3 human

The PSPACE bound is the modal form of the bound for quantified Boolean formulas.
Each modal level plays the role of a quantifier, and a depth-first tableau keeps one branch, of polynomial length.
The S5 bound comes from the collapse of the frame to a single cluster.
After a guess of polynomially many worlds, this makes the modal part propositional.
The EXPTIME-complete logics, such as propositional dynamic logic and the logics with common knowledge, have an operator for the transitive closure of a relation.
See chapter 6 of Blackburn, de Rijke and Venema.
