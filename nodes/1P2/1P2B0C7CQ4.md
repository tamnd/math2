## Statement

For a [[normal modal logic|JS8W7PA6JK]] $\Lambda$, the satisfiability problem asks, given a formula $\varphi$, whether $\neg\varphi \notin \Lambda$.
Equivalently, it asks whether $\varphi$ holds at some world of some model of $\Lambda$.
The size of $\varphi$ is its number of symbols.
We write $\mathsf{T}$ for $\mathsf{KT}$, $\mathsf{D}$ for $\mathsf{KD}$, $\mathsf{S4}$ for $\mathsf{KT4}$ and $\mathsf{S5}$ for $\mathsf{KT5}$.

1. Upper bound: the satisfiability problems of $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{T}$, $\mathsf{D}$, $\mathsf{K4}$ and $\mathsf{S4}$ are in [[PSPACE|JWC9CE07Q2]].
2. Lower bound: if $\mathsf{K} \subseteq \Lambda \subseteq \mathsf{S4}$, then the satisfiability problem of $\Lambda$ and its provability problem are PSPACE-hard.
3. Hence the satisfiability problems of $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{T}$, $\mathsf{D}$, $\mathsf{K4}$ and $\mathsf{S4}$ are PSPACE-complete.
4. The logic [[S5|VC3A2VWXND]]: a formula of size $n$ is $\mathsf{S5}$-satisfiable exactly when it is satisfiable in a model with at most $n$ worlds and the universal relation, and $\mathsf{S5}$-satisfiability is [[NP-complete|JGZWHWA8P8]].
5. The universal modality: for $\mathsf{K}$ with a universal modality, or for $\mathsf{K}$ with global assumptions, satisfiability is [[EXPTIME|T9G5S33CA0]]-complete.

Provability of $\varphi$ is the complement of satisfiability of $\neg\varphi$, so parts 1 to 3 also hold for provability, and provability in $\mathsf{S5}$ is coNP-complete.
The proof, cited with an outline, is in [[the proof of the complexity theorem|H2FYKJQAGC]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The bound $\Lambda \subseteq \mathsf{S4}$ in part 2 is needed.
$\mathsf{S5}$ contains $\mathsf{K}$ but is NP-complete, so it is not PSPACE-hard unless $\mathrm{NP} = \mathrm{PSPACE}$.
The logic $\mathsf{K} + \square\bot$ also lies above $\mathsf{K}$ and not below $\mathsf{S4}$.
In it $\square\psi$ is always true and $\lozenge\psi$ always false, so its satisfiability problem is propositional [[satisfiability|K3WXSG7WBX]], which is in NP.

The bound $\mathsf{K} \subseteq \Lambda$ holds for every normal modal logic, so it only says that the theorem is about normal logics.

Part 5 needs the extra operator, because without it $\mathsf{K}$ is in PSPACE by part 1.
So $\mathsf{K}$ with the universal modality is not in PSPACE unless $\mathrm{PSPACE} = \mathrm{EXPTIME}$, which is open.

### What it gives you

Validity in $\mathsf{K}$ or $\mathsf{S4}$ cannot be decided in polynomial time unless $\mathrm{P} = \mathrm{PSPACE}$.
The [[finite model property theorem|XNPJK6B921]] gives countermodels of size $2^n$, and part 1 shows that a search never needs to store one at once.
A single box already makes satisfiability as hard as [[true quantified Boolean formulas|WV2N01B4YV]].

### The converse

PSPACE-hardness does not force a logic into the interval between $\mathsf{K}$ and $\mathsf{S4}$.
The provability logic $\mathsf{GL}$ is PSPACE-complete, but it is not contained in $\mathsf{S4}$, since Löb's axiom fails at a reflexive point where $p$ is false.
Nor is every normal modal logic in PSPACE: some finitely axiomatized normal modal logics are undecidable, as noted with [[Harrop's theorem|VSYQ1AZ9G7]].

### Generalizations

Halpern and Moses treated logics with several agents in 1992, as in [[multimodal logic|ERG3KAQWYF]].
The logics $\mathsf{K}_m$, $\mathsf{T}_m$ and $\mathsf{S4}_m$ are PSPACE-complete for $m \ge 1$.
The logics $\mathsf{S5}_m$ and $\mathsf{KD45}_m$ are PSPACE-complete for $m \ge 2$, while $\mathsf{KD45}$ with one agent is NP-complete.
With a common knowledge operator and $m \ge 2$ agents, these logics become EXPTIME-complete.
For formulas of a fixed modal depth, $\mathsf{K}$-satisfiability is in NP, because the tree models in the proof then have polynomial size.

## History

Ladner proved in 1977 that $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{T}$ and $\mathsf{S4}$ are PSPACE-complete and that $\mathsf{S5}$ is NP-complete.
He used a tableau algorithm for the upper bound and a reduction from quantified Boolean formulas for the lower bound.
The same methods give the upper bounds for $\mathsf{D}$ and $\mathsf{K4}$.
Halpern and Moses extended the results to logics of knowledge and belief with several agents in 1992.
The EXPTIME bounds for the universal modality are treated in chapter 6 of Blackburn, de Rijke and Venema, with the earlier literature in its notes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A computer can decide whether a modal statement can be true.
For the basic logics it needs only a moderate amount of memory.
But in the worst case it seems to need much more time than for ordinary propositional logic.
For S5, where every world sees every world, the problem is no harder than ordinary propositional satisfiability.

### L2 human

We see why $\mathsf{S5}$ is easier than $\mathsf{K}$, with $\varphi = \lozenge p \wedge \lozenge \neg p \wedge \square(p \vee q)$.

1. In $\mathsf{S5}$ every world sees every world, so the value of a box or a diamond does not depend on the world.
2. We need a world with $p$ and a world with $\neg p$, and the box asks for $p \vee q$ at both.
3. Two worlds suffice, $u$ with $p$ and $v$ with $\neg p$ and $q$, and in general one world for each diamond, plus the actual world, is enough.
4. In $\mathsf{K}$ diamonds can be nested, as in $\lozenge(\lozenge p_1 \wedge \lozenge \neg p_1)$, so the required worlds form a tree whose size can grow exponentially with the depth.

The typical use is to choose the logic of a system by its cost.
Watch out: an exponential model does not mean exponential space, since a search can keep one branch at a time.

### L3 human

The PSPACE bound is the modal form of the bound for quantified Boolean formulas.
Each modal level plays the role of a quantifier, and a depth-first tableau keeps one branch, of polynomial length.
The $\mathsf{S5}$ bound comes from the collapse of the frame to a single cluster.
After a guess of polynomially many worlds, this makes the modal part propositional.
The EXPTIME-complete logics, such as [[propositional dynamic logic|VJ78A0Q22R]] and the logics with common knowledge, have an operator for the transitive closure of a relation.
Chapter 6 of Blackburn, de Rijke and Venema gives the proofs.
