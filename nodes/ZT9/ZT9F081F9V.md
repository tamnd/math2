## Definition

Let $G$ be a proper $p \times m$ matrix of real rational functions of $s$.
For $\abs{s}$ large it has a convergent expansion
$$G(s) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} M_k s^{-k}$$
with matrices $M_k \in \R^{p \times m}$.
The matrices $M_0, M_1, M_2, \ldots$ are the *Markov parameters* of $G$.
The *Hankel matrix* of $G$ is the infinite block matrix $H$ whose block in row $i$ and column $j$ is $M_{i + j - 1}$ for $i, j \ge 1$:
$$H = \begin{pmatrix} M_1 & M_2 & M_3 & \cdots \\ M_2 & M_3 & M_4 & \cdots \\ M_3 & M_4 & M_5 & \cdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}.$$
For $r \ge 1$, the finite Hankel matrix $H_r$ is its upper left part with $r \times r$ blocks.

## Remarks

If $(A, B, C, D)$ is a [[realization|WDVJXWV3RF]] of $G$, then $(sI - A)^{-1} = \sum_{k \ge 1} A^{k-1} s^{-k}$ for large $\abs{s}$ gives $M_0 = D$ and $M_k = C A^{k-1} B$ for $k \ge 1$.
Then $H_r = \mathcal{O}_r \mathcal{C}_r$, where $\mathcal{O}_r$ has block rows $C, CA, \ldots, CA^{r-1}$ and $\mathcal{C}_r$ has block columns $B, AB, \ldots, A^{r-1}B$.
The [[rank|0ZC9YTXZRY]] of $H$ is finite and equals the dimension of a minimal realization of $G$.

Example: $G(s) = 1/(s + 1) = s^{-1} (1 + s^{-1})^{-1} = \sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{k-1} s^{-k}$, so $M_0 = 0$ and $M_k = (-1)^{k-1}$.
This agrees with $C A^{k-1} B = (-1)^{k-1}$ for $A = -1$, $B = C = 1$.
The Hankel matrix has entries $(-1)^{i+j}$, so each row is $\pm$ the first one, and it has rank $1$.

Non-example: the sequence $M_k = 1/k!$, $k \ge 1$, is not the sequence of Markov parameters of any rational $G$.
Its Hankel matrix has infinite rank, by Kronecker's theorem, since the series $\sum_{k \ge 1} z^k / k! = e^z - 1$ is not a rational function.

In discrete time, with $G$ a function of $z$, the Markov parameters $M_k$ are the values $h(k)$ of the impulse response of the system.
