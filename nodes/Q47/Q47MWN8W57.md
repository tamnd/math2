## Proof

We compute each entry of the two products by a Laplace expansion, along a row for $A \operatorname{adj}(A)$ and along a column for $\operatorname{adj}(A) A$.
Let $A = (a_{kl})$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over a commutative ring $K$, and let $C_{kl}$ be its [[cofactors|0W2QAVJQ1W]].
For $n = 1$ the convention $\operatorname{adj}(a) = (1)$ gives $(a)(1) = (1)(a) = a I_1$, so we assume $n \ge 2$.

*The product $A \operatorname{adj}(A)$.*

1. By the definition of the [[adjugate|0GMPYM0NSR]], $\operatorname{adj}(A)_{jk} = C_{kj}$.
   So, by the definition of the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]], for all $i$ and $k$,
$$\big(A \operatorname{adj}(A)\big)_{ik} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} C_{kj}.$$
2. If $i = k$, this sum is $\det A$, by the [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]] along row $i$.
3. Let $i \ne k$, and let $A'$ be the matrix obtained from $A$ by replacing row $k$ with a copy of row $i$.
4. The cofactor $C'_{kj}$ of $A'$ is computed with row $k$ deleted, and row $k$ is the only row where $A'$ and $A$ differ.
   So $C'_{kj} = C_{kj}$ for all $j$.
5. Row $k$ of $A'$ has entries $a_{ij}$.
   By the Laplace expansion of $A'$ along row $k$ and step 4,
$$\det A' = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} C_{kj}.$$
6. Rows $i$ and $k$ of $A'$ are equal, so $\det A' = 0$ by the rule for [[two equal rows|0K4VQB7HWR]].
7. By steps 1 to 6, entry $(i, k)$ of $A \operatorname{adj}(A)$ is $\det A$ if $i = k$ and $0$ if $i \ne k$.
   So $A \operatorname{adj}(A) = \det(A)\, I_n$, where $I_n$ is the [[identity matrix|06DW9BAWB1]].

*The product $\operatorname{adj}(A) A$.*

8. In the same way, for all $i$ and $k$,
$$\big(\operatorname{adj}(A) A\big)_{ik} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} C_{ji} a_{jk}.$$
9. If $i = k$, this sum is $\det A$, by the [[Laplace expansion along a column|06AMCJMMVM]], here column $i$.
10. Let $i \ne k$, and let $A''$ be the matrix obtained from $A$ by replacing column $i$ with a copy of column $k$.
11. The cofactor $C''_{ji}$ of $A''$ is computed with column $i$ deleted, and column $i$ is the only column where $A''$ and $A$ differ.
    So $C''_{ji} = C_{ji}$ for all $j$.
12. Column $i$ of $A''$ has entries $a_{jk}$.
    By the Laplace expansion of $A''$ along column $i$ and step 11,
$$\det A'' = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{jk} C_{ji}.$$
13. Columns $i$ and $k$ of $A''$ are equal, so the transpose of $A''$ has two equal rows.
    By the [[transpose rule|0ZZQ2E1MTE]] and the rule of step 6, $\det A'' = \det (A'')^{\mathsf{T}} = 0$.
14. By steps 8 to 13, $\operatorname{adj}(A) A = \det(A)\, I_n$.
    Together with step 7, $A \operatorname{adj}(A) = \operatorname{adj}(A) A = \det(A)\, I_n$.

## Idea

An entry of $A \operatorname{adj}(A)$ pairs one row of $A$ with the cofactors of a row.
For the same row, this is the Laplace expansion of $\det A$.
For different rows, it is the expansion of a matrix with two equal rows, so it is $0$.
The cofactors of row $k$ do not see row $k$, which is why replacing that row leaves them unchanged.

## Variants

The second product can be reduced to the first.
Since deleting row $j$ and column $i$ of $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ gives the transpose of the matrix obtained from $A$ by deleting row $i$ and column $j$, the transpose rule gives $\operatorname{adj}(A^{\mathsf{T}}) = (\operatorname{adj} A)^{\mathsf{T}}$.
Applying step 7 to $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ and transposing then gives $\operatorname{adj}(A) A = \big(A^{\mathsf{T}} \operatorname{adj}(A^{\mathsf{T}})\big)^{\mathsf{T}} = \det(A)\, I_n$.
Steps 3 to 6 can also be phrased with linear forms.
For fixed $k$, the map that sends a row vector $x$ to $\sum_j x_j C_{kj}$ is the determinant of $A$ with row $k$ replaced by $x$.
This linear form vanishes on every row of $A$ other than row $k$.
