## Definition

The formulas of the *full Lambek calculus* $\mathsf{FL}$ are built from atoms and the constants $1$ and $0$ by the connectives $\cdot$, $\backslash$, $/$, $\wedge$ and $\vee$.
A sequent is $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Pi$, where $\Gamma$ is a finite list of formulas, possibly empty, and $\Pi$ is one formula or empty.
$\Delta$ and $\Sigma$ are lists too.
The axioms are $A \Rightarrow A$, $\Rightarrow 1$ and $0 \Rightarrow$, and the rules follow.

1. Cut: from $\Gamma \Rightarrow A$ and $\Delta, A, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, \Gamma, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$.
2. $1\mathrm{L}$: from $\Delta, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, 1, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$.
   $0\mathrm{R}$: from $\Gamma \Rightarrow$ infer $\Gamma \Rightarrow 0$.
3. ${\cdot}\mathrm{L}$: from $\Delta, A, B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, A \cdot B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$.
   ${\cdot}\mathrm{R}$: from $\Gamma \Rightarrow A$ and $\Delta \Rightarrow B$ infer $\Gamma, \Delta \Rightarrow A \cdot B$.
4. ${\backslash}\mathrm{L}$: from $\Gamma \Rightarrow A$ and $\Delta, B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, \Gamma, A \backslash B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$.
   ${\backslash}\mathrm{R}$: from $A, \Gamma \Rightarrow B$ infer $\Gamma \Rightarrow A \backslash B$.
5. ${/}\mathrm{L}$: from $\Gamma \Rightarrow A$ and $\Delta, B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, B / A, \Gamma, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$.
   ${/}\mathrm{R}$: from $\Gamma, A \Rightarrow B$ infer $\Gamma \Rightarrow B / A$.
6. ${\wedge}\mathrm{L}$: from $\Delta, A, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, A \wedge B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$, and with $B \wedge A$.
   ${\wedge}\mathrm{R}$: from $\Gamma \Rightarrow A$ and $\Gamma \Rightarrow B$ infer $\Gamma \Rightarrow A \wedge B$.
7. ${\vee}\mathrm{L}$: from $\Delta, A, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ and $\Delta, B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, A \vee B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$.
   ${\vee}\mathrm{R}$: from $\Gamma \Rightarrow A$ infer $\Gamma \Rightarrow A \vee B$, and with $B \vee A$.

## Notation

The connective $\cdot$ is called *fusion*.
The extensions of $\mathsf{FL}$ add structural rules.

1. $\mathsf{FL}_e$ adds left [[exchange|YAFD18WYJ8]]: from $\Delta, A, B, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, B, A, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$.
2. $\mathsf{FL}_w$ adds left [[weakening|VHRXX0T5XT]], from $\Delta, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, A, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$, and right weakening, from $\Gamma \Rightarrow$ infer $\Gamma \Rightarrow A$.
3. $\mathsf{FL}_c$ adds left [[contraction|V0MZVC2XA9]]: from $\Delta, A, A, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ infer $\Delta, A, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$.
4. Combinations such as $\mathsf{FL}_{ew}$ add several of these rules.

An *FL-algebra* is a [[residuated lattice|S9Z6PKTXAS]] $(L, \wedge, \vee, \cdot, \backslash, /, 1)$ with an extra element $0$, where $x \cdot y \leq z$ if and only if $y \leq x \backslash z$, if and only if $x \leq z / y$.
A valuation $v$ sends atoms to elements and extends to all formulas by the operations.
The sequent $A_1, \dots, A_n \Rightarrow \Pi$ is *valid* under $v$ if $v(A_1) \cdot \ldots \cdot v(A_n) \leq v(\Pi)$, where the empty product is $1$ and $v$ of the empty $\Pi$ is $0$.

## Remarks

### Algebraic completeness

Claim: a sequent is derivable in $\mathsf{FL}$ if and only if it is valid under every valuation in every FL-algebra.

1. Soundness: we show by induction on derivations that each axiom is valid and each rule preserves validity.
   Let $g$, $d$, $s$ be the products of $\Gamma$, $\Delta$, $\Sigma$.
2. The axioms say $a \leq a$, $1 \leq 1$ and $0 \leq 0$.
3. Cut: from $g \leq a$ and $d \cdot a \cdot s \leq \pi$, monotonicity of fusion gives $d \cdot g \cdot s \leq d \cdot a \cdot s \leq \pi$.
4. ${\backslash}\mathrm{R}$ is residuation: $a \cdot g \leq b$ gives $g \leq a \backslash b$.
5. ${\backslash}\mathrm{L}$: residuation gives $a \cdot (a \backslash b) \leq b$, so $g \cdot (a \backslash b) \leq a \cdot (a \backslash b) \leq b$, and then $d \cdot g \cdot (a \backslash b) \cdot s \leq d \cdot b \cdot s \leq \pi$.
6. ${\vee}\mathrm{L}$ uses $d \cdot (a \vee b) \cdot s = (d \cdot a \cdot s) \vee (d \cdot b \cdot s)$, which holds since fusion is residuated and so preserves joins.
   The other rules follow from monotonicity and the lattice laws in the same way.
7. Completeness: let $A \equiv B$ mean that $A \Rightarrow B$ and $B \Rightarrow A$ are derivable.
   Cut makes $\equiv$ an equivalence relation, and the logical rules make it a congruence.
8. On the classes, put $[A] \leq [B]$ if $A \Rightarrow B$ is derivable, and define the operations by $[A] \cdot [B] = [A \cdot B]$ and so on.
   This is the [[Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra|MGCVQXXQJH]] of $\mathsf{FL}$.
9. Residuation holds: from $A \cdot B \Rightarrow C$, cut with $A, B \Rightarrow A \cdot B$ gives $A, B \Rightarrow C$, and ${\backslash}\mathrm{R}$ gives $B \Rightarrow A \backslash C$.
   Conversely, cut of $B \Rightarrow A \backslash C$ with $A, A \backslash C \Rightarrow C$ gives $A, B \Rightarrow C$, and ${\cdot}\mathrm{L}$ gives $A \cdot B \Rightarrow C$.
10. The rules for $\wedge$, $\vee$, $1$ make $[A \wedge B]$ a meet, $[A \vee B]$ a join and $[1]$ a unit of fusion.
    So the classes form an FL-algebra.
11. Under the valuation $v(p) = [p]$, induction on formulas gives $v(A) = [A]$.
    If $A_1, \dots, A_n \Rightarrow C$ is valid under $v$, then $A_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot A_n \Rightarrow C$ is derivable.
12. Cut with $A_1, \dots, A_n \Rightarrow A_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot A_n$, which follows from axioms by ${\cdot}\mathrm{R}$, gives $A_1, \dots, A_n \Rightarrow C$.
    For $n = 0$ we cut $\Rightarrow 1$ with $1 \Rightarrow C$, and for an empty right side we cut $\Gamma \Rightarrow 0$ with the axiom $0 \Rightarrow$.

The same proof shows that $\mathsf{FL}_e$, $\mathsf{FL}_w$ and $\mathsf{FL}_{ew}$ are complete for the FL-algebras that are commutative, that satisfy $0 \leq x \leq 1$, or both.
By the claims in the definitions of [[exchange|YAFD18WYJ8]] and [[weakening|VHRXX0T5XT]], these are exactly the conditions under which the structural rules are sound.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The full Lambek calculus is a list of rules for building proofs from hypotheses.
It has rules for each connective and no rule that copies, discards or reorders a hypothesis.
Each such rule can be added on its own, and each choice gives another logic.

### L2 human

We derive $A \cdot (B \cdot C) \Rightarrow (A \cdot B) \cdot C$ in $\mathsf{FL}$, which shows that fusion is associative.

1. The axioms $A \Rightarrow A$ and $B \Rightarrow B$ give $A, B \Rightarrow A \cdot B$ by ${\cdot}\mathrm{R}$.
2. With $C \Rightarrow C$, ${\cdot}\mathrm{R}$ gives $A, B, C \Rightarrow (A \cdot B) \cdot C$.
3. ${\cdot}\mathrm{L}$ on $B, C$ gives $A, B \cdot C \Rightarrow (A \cdot B) \cdot C$.
4. ${\cdot}\mathrm{L}$ on $A, B \cdot C$ gives $A \cdot (B \cdot C) \Rightarrow (A \cdot B) \cdot C$.

The typical use is to read a sequent as an inequality between products, which steps 1 to 4 make exact.
A trap: in ${\backslash}\mathrm{L}$, the list $\Gamma$ that proves $A$ is placed to the left of $A \backslash B$, and in ${/}\mathrm{L}$ it is placed to the right of $B / A$.
