## Summary

Geodetic coordinates locate a point near the Earth by its geodetic latitude $\varphi$, its longitude $\lambda$ and its ellipsoidal height $h$.
They are measured with respect to a [[reference ellipsoid|R9XX7TNJCH]].
The latitude is the angle between the equatorial plane and the normal line to the ellipsoid, not the line to the centre.
They are the coordinates printed on maps and given by satellite receivers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Latitude says how far north or south you are, longitude how far east or west, and height how far up.
On a squashed Earth, "up" means along the line perpendicular to the ellipsoid.
This line does not pass through the centre of the Earth, except at the equator and the poles.
So the geodetic latitude differs a little from the angle seen from the centre, by at most about $0.19^\circ$.
For example, the North Pole has $\varphi = 90^\circ$, any longitude, and $h = 0$.
