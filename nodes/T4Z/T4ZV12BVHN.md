## Summary

A Newtonian fluid is a fluid whose viscous stress depends linearly on the [[strain rate tensor|P1KWMQG4NZ]].
Its [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] is a constant of the material that does not depend on how fast the fluid is sheared.
Water, air and most simple liquids and gases are Newtonian to high accuracy.
The [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] are the equations of motion of a Newtonian fluid.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a Newtonian fluid, pushing twice as hard on a layer of fluid makes it slide twice as fast.
Water behaves like this: stir it slowly or quickly and it feels equally thin.
Ketchup does not: it barely flows until you shake it, and then it runs, so it is not Newtonian.
Cornflour mixed with water does the opposite and stiffens when hit, so it is not Newtonian either.
