## Summary

Tissot's indicatrix describes the local distortion of a [[map projection|Y2C8D9VC7D]] at a point.
It is the image of an infinitely small circle on the Earth, which is an ellipse on the map.
Its semi-axes $a \ge b$ are the largest and smallest scale factors at that point.
The projection keeps angles where the ellipse is a circle, and keeps areas where $ab = 1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw many small equal circles all over a globe and look at them on a flat map.
Each circle becomes an ellipse, and its shape tells how the map distorts there.
A stretched ellipse means shapes are distorted; a big or small ellipse means areas are wrong.
On the Mercator map the ellipses stay circles but grow towards the poles.
On an equal-area map they keep their area but get thinner and longer.
