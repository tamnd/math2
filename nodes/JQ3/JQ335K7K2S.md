## Proof

Let $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$ be a derivable sequent of prenex formulas.

**Preparation.**

1. By the [[cut-elimination theorem|AYRZ98Y0NW]], $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$ has a cut-free derivation $D$.
2. By [[completeness of atomic initial sequents|Q4T4YY7XJ9]], each axiom $A \Rightarrow A$ of $D$ can be replaced by a cut-free derivation from axioms $p \Rightarrow p$ with $p$ atomic.
3. We make $D$ *regular*: take each ${\forall}$R and ${\exists}$L step after all such steps above it, and replace its eigenvariable by a new variable in its premise derivation.
4. The conclusion of such a step does not contain its eigenvariable, so it is unchanged.
   The steps above already have new eigenvariables, so the substitution lemma of [[the mix proof|8HV015W88G]] applies and gives a derivation.
5. Now each eigenvariable belongs to one step, and it occurs only in the derivation of the premise of that step. In particular, it does not occur in the sibling derivations of other premises.
6. $D$ is cut-free, so every formula of $D$ is a subformula of a formula of $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$, up to substitution of terms. So every formula of $D$ is prenex.
7. A prenex formula whose main symbol is a connective is quantifier-free. So the principal and active formulas of a propositional rule in $D$ are quantifier-free.

**The invariant.**
For a sequent $S$ of $D$, write $S^\circ_L$ and $S^\circ_R$ for the lists of quantifier-free formulas of its antecedent and succedent, in order.
Write $S^q_L$ and $S^q_R$ for the lists of the other formulas, which begin with a quantifier.
Say that $\Theta$ is *free for* $S$ when no eigenvariable of a step in the derivation of $S$ occurs in $\Theta$.
We show, by induction on the derivation of $S$ in $D$, that there are lists $X_S$ and $Y_S$ of quantifier-free formulas with three properties.

- (I0) Every formula of $X_S$ and $Y_S$ occurs in the derivation of $S$.
- (I1) The sequent $M_S = X_S, S^\circ_L \Rightarrow S^\circ_R, Y_S$ has a derivation with only propositional and structural rules.
- (I2) For all lists $\Theta$ and $\Xi$ free for $S$, the sequent $S^q_L, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, S^q_R$ is derived from $X_S, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, Y_S$ by quantifier and structural rules only.

8. Axiom $p \Rightarrow p$: let $X_S$ and $Y_S$ be empty. Then $M_S$ is the axiom, and (I2) needs no step since $S^q_L$ and $S^q_R$ are empty.
9. ${\forall}$L, from $S' = A[t/x], \Gamma' \Rightarrow \Delta'$ to $S = \forall x\, A, \Gamma' \Rightarrow \Delta'$, when $A[t/x]$ is quantifier-free: let $X_S = X_{S'}, A[t/x]$ and $Y_S = Y_{S'}$.
10. Then $M_S$ is $M_{S'}$ up to exchange, which gives (I1). (I0) holds since $A[t/x]$ occurs in $S'$.
11. For (I2), let $\Theta$ and $\Xi$ be free for $S$. The formula $A[t/x]$ lies in $S'$, so by step 5 it is free for $S'$.
12. (I2) for $S'$, with $A[t/x], \Theta$ in place of $\Theta$, derives $\Gamma'^q, A[t/x], \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, \Delta'^q$ from $X_S, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, Y_S$ up to exchange.
13. Then ${\forall}$L and exchanges give $\forall x\, A, \Gamma'^q, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, \Delta'^q$, which is $S^q_L, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, S^q_R$.
14. ${\forall}$L when $A[t/x]$ begins with a quantifier: let $X_S = X_{S'}$ and $Y_S = Y_{S'}$. Then $M_S = M_{S'}$, and (I2) for $S'$ followed by ${\forall}$L gives (I2) for $S$.
15. ${\exists}$R is the mirror image of ${\forall}$L, with $Y_S = Y_{S'}, A[t/x]$ in the quantifier-free case.
16. ${\forall}$R, from $S' = \Gamma' \Rightarrow \Delta', A[y/x]$ to $S = \Gamma' \Rightarrow \Delta', \forall x\, A$, when $A[y/x]$ is quantifier-free: let $X_S = X_{S'}$ and $Y_S = Y_{S'}, A[y/x]$.
17. (I0) and (I1) hold as in step 10. For (I2), let $\Theta$ and $\Xi$ be free for $S$, so $y$ does not occur in them.
18. The formula $A[y/x]$ lies in $S'$, so it is free for $S'$ by step 5, since $y$ belongs to the step below $S'$.
19. (I2) for $S'$, with $\Xi, A[y/x]$ in place of $\Xi$, gives $\Gamma'^q, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, \Delta'^q, A[y/x]$ up to exchange.
20. The variable $y$ is not free in $\Gamma'$, $\Delta'$ or $\forall x\, A$ by the eigenvariable condition, and not in $\Theta$ or $\Xi$ by step 17.
21. So ${\forall}$R applies and gives $\Gamma'^q, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, \Delta'^q, \forall x\, A$, which is (I2) for $S$.
22. ${\forall}$R when $A[y/x]$ begins with a quantifier: keep $X_{S'}$ and $Y_{S'}$, and apply ${\forall}$R after (I2) for $S'$. Step 20 again checks the eigenvariable condition.
23. ${\exists}$L is the mirror image of ${\forall}$R.
24. A propositional rule with one premise: by step 7 it acts on quantifier-free formulas only. Keep $X_{S'}$ and $Y_{S'}$.
25. The same rule, applied to $M_{S'}$ with exchanges, gives $M_S$. Since $S^q = S'^q$ and the eigenvariables are the same, (I2) for $S'$ is (I2) for $S$.
26. ${\wedge}$R, from $S_1 = \Gamma' \Rightarrow \Delta', A$ and $S_2 = \Gamma' \Rightarrow \Delta', B$: let $X_S = X_1, X_2$ and $Y_S = Y_1, Y_2$, with $X_i = X_{S_i}$ and $Y_i = Y_{S_i}$.
    Then (I0) holds by (I0) for $S_1$ and $S_2$.
27. For (I1), weaken $M_{S_1}$ by $X_2$ and $Y_2$ and weaken $M_{S_2}$ by $X_1$ and $Y_1$. Then ${\wedge}$R and exchanges give $M_S$.
28. For (I2), let $\Theta$ and $\Xi$ be free for $S$. By (I0) and step 5, the lists $X_2$ and $Y_2$ are free for $S_1$.
29. (I2) for $S_1$, with $X_2, \Theta$ and $\Xi, Y_2$, derives $\Gamma'^q, X_2, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, Y_2, \Delta'^q$ from $X_S, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, Y_S$ up to exchange.
30. The lists $\Gamma'^q$ and $\Delta'^q$ lie in $S_2$, so they are free for $S_2$. (I2) for $S_2$ gives $\Gamma'^q, \Gamma'^q, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, \Delta'^q, \Delta'^q$ up to exchange.
31. Contractions give $\Gamma'^q, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, \Delta'^q$, which is (I2) for $S$. Rule ${\vee}$L is treated in the same way.
32. ${\to}$L, from $S_1 = \Gamma_1 \Rightarrow \Delta_1, A$ and $S_2 = B, \Gamma_2 \Rightarrow \Delta_2$: let $X_S = X_1, X_2$ and $Y_S = Y_1, Y_2$. Rule ${\to}$L on $M_{S_1}$ and $M_{S_2}$ gives $M_S$ up to exchange.
33. For (I2), apply (I2) for $S_1$ with $X_2, \Theta$ and $\Xi, Y_2$, then (I2) for $S_2$ with $\Gamma_1^q, \Theta$ and $\Xi, \Delta_1^q$.
    These lists are free by step 5, since $X_2$ and $Y_2$ occur in the derivation of $S_2$, and $\Gamma_1^q$ and $\Delta_1^q$ in $S_1$.
34. This gives $\Gamma_1^q, \Gamma_2^q, \Theta \Rightarrow \Xi, \Delta_1^q, \Delta_2^q$ up to exchange, and no contraction is needed.
35. A structural rule whose active formulas are all quantifier-free: apply it to $M_{S'}$, and keep $X_{S'}$, $Y_{S'}$ and (I2).
36. Any other structural rule: keep $X_{S'}$, $Y_{S'}$ and $M_{S'}$ up to exchange, and apply the rule after (I2) for $S'$.

**The midsequent.**

37. Let $S$ be the end sequent $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$, and let $M = M_S$. Then $M$ is quantifier-free, and (I1) derives it with propositional and structural rules.
38. The lists $S^\circ_L$ and $S^\circ_R$ lie in $S$, so they are free for $S$ by step 5.
39. (I2) with $\Theta = S^\circ_L$ and $\Xi = S^\circ_R$ derives $S^q_L, S^\circ_L \Rightarrow S^\circ_R, S^q_R$ from $M$ with quantifier and structural rules.
40. Exchanges give $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$. The derivation of (I1) followed by this chain has the three properties of the theorem.

## Idea

Each quantifier step is postponed until all propositional steps are done.
The lists $X_S$ and $Y_S$ record the instances that the quantifier steps above $S$ have used.
Regularity makes sure that the postponed ${\forall}$R and ${\exists}$L steps still satisfy their eigenvariable conditions.
Prenex form makes sure that no propositional rule ever needs a quantified formula.

## Variants

The usual textbook proof permutes quantifier rules downward step by step, by induction on the number of propositional rules below quantifier rules.
The proof above builds the lower chain directly, and it uses weakening in the case of ${\wedge}$R and ${\vee}$L.
