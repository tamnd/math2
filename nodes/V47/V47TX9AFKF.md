## Summary

The Reynolds decomposition writes a fluctuating quantity of a [[turbulent|VY307F8Z0S]] flow as the sum of its mean and a fluctuation with mean zero.
The mean is taken by time averaging, by spatial averaging or by an ensemble average over many realisations.
Osborne Reynolds introduced it in 1895 to derive equations for the mean flow.
It is the starting point of the statistical theory of turbulence and of most engineering turbulence models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The speed of the wind at one spot jumps up and down all the time, but over ten minutes it has a clear average.
The Reynolds decomposition splits the speed into that steady average and the quick ups and downs around it.
By construction the ups and downs average to zero.
For example, a wind that spends equal time at $9$ and at $11$ metres per second has mean $10$ and fluctuations $\pm 1$.
