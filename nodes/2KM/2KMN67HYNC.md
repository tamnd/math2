## Summary

Chemical equilibrium is the state of a reacting mixture in which the amounts of all species no longer change.
In it the forward and reverse reactions balance.
At fixed temperature and pressure it is the state of least [[Gibbs free energy|RBG5ZBM35Y]].
This means that the stoichiometric sum of the [[chemical potentials|WVH4PVW64P]] is zero.
For ideal mixtures this condition becomes the [[law of mass action|PQ4D33P7EE]], with an equilibrium constant that depends on temperature through the [[van 't Hoff equation|MET15VK1F8]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

When nitrogen and hydrogen react to make ammonia, some ammonia also breaks back down.
After a while both reactions run at the same speed, and the amounts of the three gases stop changing.
The mixture then still contains all three gases: the reaction has not run to the end, it has reached a balance.
Changing the temperature or the pressure moves the balance, as [[Le Chatelier's principle|GS9W0JDBW8]] describes.
