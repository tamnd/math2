## Definition

Consider a fluid in a simple shear flow $\mathbf{u}(x, y, z) = (u(y), 0, 0)$, in which layers of fluid parallel to the plane $y = 0$ slide over each other.
Let $\tau$ be the shear stress, the tangential force per unit area that one layer exerts on its neighbour.
Let $\dot\gamma = du/dy$ be the shear rate.
The *dynamic viscosity* of the fluid is
$$\mu = \frac{\tau}{\dot\gamma},$$
and its *kinematic viscosity* is $\nu = \mu / \rho$, where $\rho$ is the density.
In general $\mu$ may depend on the temperature, the pressure and the shear rate.

## Remarks

The shear rate is twice the off-diagonal entry $E_{12}$ of the [[strain rate tensor|P1KWMQG4NZ]].
Example: a film of oil of thickness $h = 1\ \mathrm{mm}$ between a fixed plate and a plate moving at $U = 0.1\ \mathrm{m/s}$ has $\dot\gamma = U/h = 100\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$; if the stress needed is $\tau = 10\ \mathrm{Pa}$, then $\mu = 0.1\ \mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$.
Non-example: a fluid at rest has $\dot\gamma = 0$, and its stress gives no information on $\mu$; viscosity only shows up when the fluid deforms.
The SI unit of $\mu$ is $\mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$, and that of $\nu$ is $\mathrm{m^2/s}$.
Inviscid models set $\mu = 0$.
