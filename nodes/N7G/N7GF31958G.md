## Definition

Consider a closed system at fixed temperature $T$ and pressure $p$ containing species $A_1, \dots, A_k$ that can take part in a reaction $\sum_{i=1}^k \nu_i A_i = 0$.
Here $\nu_i$ is the stoichiometric coefficient of $A_i$, positive for products and negative for reactants.
The amounts change as $dn_i = \nu_i\, d\xi$, where $\xi$ is the extent of reaction.
Let $\mu_i$ be the [[chemical potential|WVH4PVW64P]] of $A_i$ and $G$ the [[Gibbs free energy|RBG5ZBM35Y]] of the system.
The system is in *chemical equilibrium* if $G$, as a function of $\xi$ at fixed $T$ and $p$, is at its minimum.
When this minimum lies in the interior of the allowed range of $\xi$, the condition is
$$\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial \xi}\right)_{T, p} = \sum_{i=1}^k \nu_i \mu_i = 0.$$

## Remarks

For $\mathrm{N_2} + 3\,\mathrm{H_2} \rightleftharpoons 2\,\mathrm{NH_3}$, the coefficients are $\nu = (-1, -3, 2)$, and equilibrium means $2\mu_{\mathrm{NH_3}} = \mu_{\mathrm{N_2}} + 3\mu_{\mathrm{H_2}}$.
A non-example: a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen at room temperature does not change in practice.
But $2\mu_{\mathrm{H_2O}} - 2\mu_{\mathrm{H_2}} - \mu_{\mathrm{O_2}}$ is strongly negative, so $G$ is not minimal.
The mixture is only kinetically stable, not in chemical equilibrium.
With several independent reactions there is one such condition for each reaction.
The quantity $\sum_i \nu_i \mu_i$ is the reaction Gibbs energy $\Delta_r G$; its negative is called the affinity.
Chemical equilibrium is part of [[thermodynamic equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]], together with thermal and mechanical equilibrium.
