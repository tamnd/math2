## Definition

Let $\mu > 0$.
A *Kepler orbit* with gravitational parameter $\mu$ is the path traced by a solution $r \colon I \to \R^3 \setminus \set{0}$, on an interval $I$, of the [[Kepler problem|TYXD1T6FRX]]
$$r'' = -\frac{\mu\, r}{\norm{r}^3}.$$
For two bodies of masses $m_1, m_2$ moving by [[Newton's law of gravitation|29WRN5J730]], the relative position $r = x_2 - x_1$ moves on a Kepler orbit with $\mu = G (m_1 + m_2)$.

## Remarks

If the angular momentum $h = r \times r'$ is not zero, the motion lies in the plane through $0$ orthogonal to $h$.
In polar coordinates $(\rho, \theta)$ in that plane, with $\theta$ measured from the periapsis, the path is the [[conic section|00HJCMC2CD]]
$$\rho = \frac{p}{1 + e \cos \theta}, \qquad p = \frac{\norm{h}^2}{\mu},$$
with a focus at $0$ and [[eccentricity|0HVX863SAJ]] $e \ge 0$.
It is an ellipse for $e < 1$, a parabola for $e = 1$ and a hyperbola for $e > 1$.
If $h = 0$, the motion is along a line through $0$, a radial Kepler orbit.

Example: $r(t) = R (\cos \omega t, \sin \omega t, 0)$ with $R > 0$ and $\omega^2 R^3 = \mu$.
Then $r'' = -\omega^2 r = -\mu\, r / R^3 = -\mu\, r / \norm{r}^3$, so the circle of radius $R$ is a Kepler orbit with $e = 0$.

Non-example: a solution of $r'' = -\mu\, r / \norm{r}^3 - \varepsilon\, r / \norm{r}^4$ with $\varepsilon \ne 0$ does not solve the equation of the Kepler problem, and in general its path is a rosette that does not close.
