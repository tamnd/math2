## Definition

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $A = (a_{ij})_{1 \le i, j \le n}$ be an $n \times n$ [[matrix|0VD9ZSWWJG]] with entries in $\Z$.
The matrix $A$ is a *generalized Cartan matrix* if:

1. $a_{ii} = 2$ for every $i$;
2. $a_{ij} \le 0$ for all $i \neq j$;
3. $a_{ij} = 0$ if and only if $a_{ji} = 0$.

## Remarks

Example: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has diagonal entries $2$, off-diagonal entries $-2 \le 0$, and no zero entries, so it is a generalized Cartan matrix.

Non-example: $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ satisfies the first two clauses, but $a_{21} = 0$ while $a_{12} = -1 \neq 0$, so it fails clause 3.

A generalized Cartan matrix $A$ is *symmetrizable* if there is an invertible diagonal matrix $D$ with positive entries such that $DA$ is symmetric.
It is *indecomposable* if no reordering of the indices brings it into block diagonal form with two or more blocks.
The [[Cartan matrices|H59F1XBDZP]] of finite-dimensional semisimple complex Lie algebras are exactly the generalized Cartan matrices whose principal minors are all positive.
