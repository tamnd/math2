## Summary

The focal mechanism of an earthquake describes the orientation of the fault and the direction of slip on it.
It is given by three angles, strike, dip and rake, and is drawn as a "beach ball" diagram.
It is found from the pattern of first motions or waveforms of seismic waves, which a [[double-couple source|S5TXH6MZ05]] radiates in a characteristic four-lobed pattern.
Focal mechanisms show whether a region is being stretched, compressed or sheared.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a fault slips, the ground in some directions is first pushed away from the source and in others first pulled toward it.
Stations around the world record which way the first wave moved.
The push and pull directions fall into four quarters, separated by two perpendicular planes.
One of these planes is the fault; the other is a mirror companion, and the seismic waves alone cannot tell which is which.
The beach ball diagram shades the push quarters dark and the pull quarters white.
