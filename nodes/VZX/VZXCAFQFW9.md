## Summary

Mechanical similarity is a scaling symmetry of systems whose [[potential energy|DF7DKHTQ7A]] is a homogeneous function of the positions.
Let the potential be homogeneous of degree $k$.
Enlarge a trajectory by a factor $\alpha$ and stretch its time scale by the factor $\alpha^{1 - k/2}$; the result is again a trajectory.
This yields relations between sizes, times and energies of motions without solving the equations of [[Newton's laws|KNBP19FG6K]].
Kepler's third law and the independence of the period of a harmonic oscillator from its amplitude are two consequences.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose you know one orbit of a planet and want an orbit twice as large.
Mechanical similarity says: take the same shape, double its size, and let the planet run along it more slowly, so that each lap takes $2^{3/2} \approx 2.8$ times as long.
For a mass on a spring the time factor is $1$, so a wide swing takes as long as a small one.
For falling under constant gravity, falling four times as far takes twice as long.
