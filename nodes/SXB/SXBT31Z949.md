## Definition

Let $I$ be a countable set of states, and let $\varepsilon \colon I \to \R$ assign an energy $\varepsilon_i$ to each state $i$.
Let $T > 0$ be the temperature, $k_B$ the Boltzmann constant and $\beta = 1 / (k_B T)$.
Assume that $Z = \sum_{i \in I} e^{-\beta \varepsilon_i}$ is finite.
The *Boltzmann distribution* is the [[probability distribution|0DVDCMA2DK]] on $I$ given by
$$p_i = \frac{e^{-\beta \varepsilon_i}}{Z}, \qquad i \in I.$$

## Remarks

The factor $e^{-\beta \varepsilon_i}$ is the Boltzmann factor, and $Z$ is the partition function.
For two states, $p_i / p_j = e^{-\beta (\varepsilon_i - \varepsilon_j)}$.
Example: the levels $\varepsilon_n = n \hbar \omega$, $n \ge 0$, of a quantum harmonic oscillator give $Z = 1 / (1 - e^{-\beta \hbar \omega})$.
So $p_n = (1 - e^{-\beta \hbar \omega}) e^{-n \beta \hbar \omega}$, a geometric distribution.
Non-example: the levels $\varepsilon_n = -1/n^2$, $n \ge 1$, accumulate at $0$, so $\sum_n e^{-\beta \varepsilon_n} = \infty$ and no Boltzmann distribution exists.
For a continuous set of states one replaces the sum by an integral against a reference measure.
