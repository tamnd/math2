## Summary

A reductive Lie algebra is a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] whose adjoint representation is completely reducible.
In characteristic $0$ this means that it is the direct sum of an abelian Lie algebra and a [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]].
The main examples are $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ and the Lie algebras of compact Lie groups.
Reductive Lie algebras are the natural setting for much of representation theory, since $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ is reductive but not semisimple.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A reductive Lie algebra is "almost semisimple": it is a semisimple part plus a commutative part that does not interact with it.
The algebra of all $n \times n$ matrices is the standard example.
It splits into the scalar matrices, which commute with everything, and the trace zero matrices, which form a semisimple algebra.
