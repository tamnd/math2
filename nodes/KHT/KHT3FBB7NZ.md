## Summary

A mechanical system is in equilibrium when the forces on it balance, so that it has no tendency to change its state of motion.
For a particle this means the net force is zero; for a [[rigid body|EZ9P9ERKNE]] both the net force and the net [[torque|9636MFFSGC]] must vanish.
For a conservative system described by generalized coordinates, the equilibrium configurations are the critical points of the potential energy.
Equilibria are the starting point of statics and of the study of stability.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A book lying on a table is in equilibrium: gravity pulls it down and the table pushes it up equally hard.
A seesaw with a $30$ kg child $2$ metres from the pivot and a $20$ kg child $3$ metres on the other side is balanced.
Indeed $30 \cdot 2 = 20 \cdot 3$, so the turning effects cancel.
An equilibrium can be stable, like a ball at the bottom of a bowl, or unstable, like a pencil standing on its tip.
