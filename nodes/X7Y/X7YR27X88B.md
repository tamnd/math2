## Statement

Consider the enzyme reaction $E + S \rightleftharpoons C \to E + P$ with [[mass-action rate equations|TTH05RV6SW]], rate constants $k_1, k_{-1}, k_2 > 0$, initial substrate $s_0 > 0$, total enzyme $e_0 > 0$ and no initial complex.
In the dimensionless variables $u = s / s_0$, $v = c / e_0$, $\tau = k_1 e_0 t$, with $K = (k_{-1} + k_2)/(k_1 s_0)$, $\lambda = k_2 / (k_1 s_0)$ and $\varepsilon = e_0 / s_0$, the scaled substrate concentration $u$ and complex concentration $v$ satisfy
$$\frac{du}{d\tau} = -u + (u + K - \lambda)\, v, \qquad \varepsilon \frac{dv}{d\tau} = u - (u + K)\, v, \qquad u(0) = 1,\ v(0) = 0.$$
Let $U$ solve the reduced problem
$$\frac{dU}{d\tau} = -\frac{\lambda\, U}{U + K}, \qquad U(0) = 1.$$
Then for every $T > 0$ and every $\tau_0 \in (0, T)$, as $\varepsilon \to 0$ with $K$ and $\lambda$ fixed,
$$\sup_{0 \le \tau \le T} \abs{u(\tau) - U(\tau)} \to 0, \qquad \sup_{\tau_0 \le \tau \le T} \abs{v(\tau) - \frac{U(\tau)}{U(\tau) + K}} \to 0.$$

## Remarks

The system is [[singularly perturbed|G6J21TGRMM]], with the slow variable $u$ and the fast variable $v$.
For fixed $u \ge 0$ the fast equation has the unique equilibrium $v = u/(u + K)$, and it is attracting since the derivative of the right side in $v$ is $-(u + K) < 0$.
So the conclusion follows from [[Tikhonov's theorem|8X8Y61MGHW]]; substituting $v = u/(u + K)$ in the slow equation gives the reduced problem.
The approximation $v \approx u/(u+K)$ fails in the initial layer of length $O(\varepsilon)$ in $\tau$, where the complex builds up from $v = 0$.
In the original units the reduced problem is $ds/dt = -V_{\max}\, s/(K_M + s)$ with $V_{\max} = k_2 e_0$ and $K_M = (k_{-1} + k_2)/k_1$.
Segel and Slemrod showed that the smaller parameter $e_0 / (s_0 + K_M)$ is enough for the approximation to be good.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an enzyme reaction the enzyme is usually much scarcer than the substrate.
The enzyme–substrate complex then forms and breaks up quickly compared with the slow use of the substrate.
After a short start-up, the complex level simply follows the current substrate level, as if it were in balance.
This lets one replace two equations with one simpler equation for the substrate.

### L2 human

The approximation is the standard example of model reduction by time-scale separation in biochemistry.
Setting the derivative of a fast variable to zero is the formal step; Tikhonov's theorem is the justification and also says where it breaks down.
The same reasoning gives the Goldbeter–Koshland rate law for covalent modification cycles, and it reduces gene regulation models in which messenger RNA is fast compared with protein.
