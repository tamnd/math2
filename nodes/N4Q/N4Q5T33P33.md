## Summary

A self-concordant function is a convex function whose third derivative is controlled by its second derivative.
Like Newton's method itself, the bound does not change under affine changes of coordinates.
It gives complexity bounds for Newton's method that do not depend on unknown constants.
Yurii Nesterov and Arkadi Nemirovski introduced the notion as the basis of the theory of [[interior-point methods|M7HYT44ZJA]].
The logarithmic barrier $-\sum_i \log(b_i - a_i^{T} x)$ of a polyhedron is the main example.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Newton's method replaces a function by its best quadratic approximation at each step.
This works well when the curvature does not change too fast.
A self-concordant function is one where the change of curvature is small compared with the curvature itself, in a precise and scale-free way.
For example, $-\log x$ on $x > 0$ becomes steep near $0$, but its curvature $1/x^2$ changes at a rate that matches the bound exactly.
