## Summary

Arbitrage pricing theory (APT) explains expected asset returns by their exposures to a few common risk factors.
If returns follow a linear factor model, then the absence of [[arbitrage|Q626SC54P7]] forces expected returns to be linear in the factor loadings.
It was proposed by Stephen Ross in 1976 as an alternative to the [[capital asset pricing model|3T10Q8A736]].
Unlike CAPM, it needs no assumption about investor preferences or the market portfolio.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose all stock prices are pushed around by a few common forces, such as interest rates and oil prices.
Two portfolios with the same exposure to these forces carry the same risk.
If one of them earned more on average, you could buy it, sell the other, and make money with no risk.
APT says that such free money cannot last, so expected returns must line up with exposures to the forces.
