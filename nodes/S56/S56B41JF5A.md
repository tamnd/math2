## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

1. Characteristic polynomial. $\operatorname{tr} A = 5$, $\det A = 3$, and the sum of the principal $2 \times 2$ minors is $3 + 4 + 0 = 7$. So $\chi_A = x^3 - 5x^2 + 7x - 3 = (x - 1)^2 (x - 3)$.
2. The eigenvalue $1$ has multiplicity $2$, and $3$ has multiplicity $1$.
3. For $\lambda = 1$: $A - I = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $2$, since its first two rows are opposite and the third is not a multiple of them. So $d_1 = 1$.
4. $(A - I)^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -4 & -4 \\ 0 & 4 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $1$, so $d_2 = 2 = m_1$.
5. Block counts for $1$: jumps $b_1, b_2 = 1, 1$. So one block, of size $2$: $J_2(1)$.
6. For $\lambda = 3$: the multiplicity is $1$, so there is one block $J_1(3)$. Indeed $\operatorname{rank}(A - 3I) = 2$.
7. So $J = J_2(1) \oplus J_1(3)$.
8. Chain for $1$. $\ker (A - I) = \operatorname{span}\{(1, -1, 1)\}$ and $\ker (A - I)^2 = \operatorname{span}\{(1, 0, 0), (0, -1, 1)\}$. Choose $u = (1, 0, 0) \notin \ker(A - I)$. Then $(A - I) u = (1, -1, 1)$.
9. Eigenvector for $3$. $A - 3I = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & -1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ kills $(1, -1, 0)$.
10. Transition matrix. $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, with columns $(A - I) u$, $u$, and the eigenvector for $3$.
11. Check. $AP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ -1 & -1 & -3 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and $PJ$ has columns $p_1$, $p_1 + p_2$, $3 p_3$, which are the same. So $AP = PJ$.
12. $\det P = -1$, expanding along the third row: $1 \cdot \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -1$. So $A = P J P^{-1}$.

## Remarks

$A$ is not diagonalisable, because the eigenvalue $1$ has algebraic multiplicity $2$ but geometric multiplicity $1$.
The minimal polynomial is $(x - 1)^2 (x - 3)$, equal to $\chi_A$, so $A$ is cyclic.
If the entry $a_{33}$ were changed, the eigenvalues would change and the block structure could change with them.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This matrix has two eigenvalues, $1$ twice and $3$ once.
The eigenvalue $1$ has only one line of eigenvectors, so it needs a block of size $2$.
The eigenvalue $3$ gets a block of size $1$.

### L2 human

The ranks $2, 1$ of $(A - I)$ and $(A - I)^2$ give kernel dimensions $1, 2$, hence one block of size $2$.
The top vector $u = e_1$ lies in $\ker (A - I)^2$ but not in $\ker (A - I)$, and $(A - I) e_1$ is the first column of $A - I$.
The check $AP = PJ$ compares three columns.
