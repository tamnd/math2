## Example

We run the [[method for computing the Jordan form|7M1W0ZA49Q]] on the matrix
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
over $\mathbb{Q}$, and we find both the form and a transition matrix.

1. For a $3 \times 3$ matrix, $\chi_A = x^3 - (\operatorname{tr} A)\, x^2 + c_2\, x - \det A$, where $c_2$ is the sum of the principal $2 \times 2$ minors. Here $\operatorname{tr} A = 5$, $c_2 = 3 + 4 + 0 = 7$ and $\det A = 3$, so the [[characteristic polynomial|0VSSPAFM2C]] is
$$\chi_A = x^3 - 5x^2 + 7x - 3 = (x - 1)^2 (x - 3).$$
2. So the eigenvalue $1$ has multiplicity $2$, and the eigenvalue $3$ has multiplicity $1$.
3. For $\lambda = 1$ the matrix
$$A - I = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
has rank $2$, since its first two rows are opposite and the third row is not a multiple of the first, so $d_1 = 1$ by the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]].
4. The square is
$$(A - I)^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -4 & -4 \\ 0 & 4 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
which has rank $1$, so $d_2 = 2$, which equals the multiplicity of $1$.
5. The jumps are $d_1 - d_0 = 1$ and $d_2 - d_1 = 1$, so the eigenvalue $1$ has one block, of size $2$.
6. The eigenvalue $3$ is a simple root, so it has one block $J_1(3)$. So $J = J_2(1) \oplus J_1(3)$.
7. For the chain of $1$, the kernel of $(A - I)^2$ is spanned by $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, -1, 1)$, and the kernel of $A - I$ is spanned by $(1, -1, 1)$. The vector $u = e_1$ lies in the first kernel and not in the second, and $(A - I) e_1 = (1, -1, 1)$.
8. For the eigenvalue $3$, the matrix $A - 3I = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & -1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ kills $(1, -1, 0)$.
9. Put the chain and the eigenvector into the columns of $P$, in the order $(A - I) u$, $u$, $(1, -1, 0)$:
$$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$
10. Check. The product is
$$AP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ -1 & -1 & -3 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
and the columns of $PJ$ are $p_1$, $p_1 + p_2$ and $3 p_3$, which are the same three columns.
11. Expanding along the third row, the [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]] of $P$ is $1 \cdot \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -1 \ne 0$. So $P$ is invertible and $A = P J P^{-1}$.

## Remarks

The matrix is not diagonalizable: the eigenvalue $1$ has algebraic multiplicity $2$ but only a line of eigenvectors.
Its minimal polynomial is $(x - 1)^2 (x - 3)$, the same as $\chi_A$, because the largest block for each eigenvalue has the full multiplicity.
So $A$ is cyclic, and its [[rational canonical form|042JWAR3Q0]] is a single companion matrix of $x^3 - 5x^2 + 7x - 3$.

The choice of the top vector is free within limits.
Any $u$ in $\ker (A - I)^2$ outside $\ker (A - I)$ works, for example $u = (0, -1, 1)$, which gives $(A - I) u = (-1, 1, -1)$, another eigenvector for $1$.
The choice $u = (1, -1, 1)$ does not work, since it is an eigenvector and $(A - I) u = 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This matrix has the eigenvalue $1$ twice and the eigenvalue $3$ once.
The eigenvalue $1$ has only one line of eigenvectors, so the two copies of $1$ must share one Jordan block of size $2$.
The eigenvalue $3$ gets its own block of size $1$, and the form is a $2 \times 2$ block next to the number $3$.

### L2 human

The ranks $2$ and $1$ of $A - I$ and $(A - I)^2$ give the kernel dimensions $1$ and $2$, so the kernel grows by one at each step, which means one block of size $2$.
The top vector $u = e_1$ lies in $\ker (A - I)^2$ but not in $\ker (A - I)$, and $(A - I) e_1$ is simply the first column of $A - I$.
The check $AP = PJ$ compares three columns, and it avoids computing $P^{-1}$.
The trap is the order of the columns: with $u$ first and $(A - I) u$ second, the check fails, and you get the lower block $J_2(1)^{\mathsf T}$ instead.
