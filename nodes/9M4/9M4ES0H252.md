## Definition

Let $\Omega \subset \R^n$ be a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary $\Gamma$ and outward unit normal $\nu$.
Let $L$ be a linear elliptic differential operator with constant coefficients and a known [[fundamental solution|K2NK9A8M5F]] $G(x, y)$.
Consider $Lu = 0$ in $\Omega$ with boundary conditions on $\Gamma$.
Write $q = \partial u / \partial \nu$ (or the conormal derivative of $L$, such as the traction in elasticity).
The *boundary element method* consists of the following steps.

1. Boundary integral equation: Green's representation formula, taken as $x \in \Omega$ tends to the boundary, gives for $x \in \Gamma$
$$c(x)\, u(x) + \int_\Gamma \frac{\partial G}{\partial \nu_y}(x, y)\, u(y)\, dS_y = \int_\Gamma G(x, y)\, q(y)\, dS_y,$$
where $c(x) = \tfrac12$ at smooth points of $\Gamma$.
   This holds for the Laplacian with $G$ normalised so that $-\Delta_y G(x, \cdot) = \delta_x$.
2. Boundary mesh: $\Gamma$ is divided into finitely many boundary elements, and $u$ and $q$ are approximated on each element by polynomials with unknown nodal values.
3. Discretisation: the integral equation is imposed at the nodes (collocation) or against test functions (Galerkin).
   This gives a linear system $H \mathbf{u} = G \mathbf{q}$ with dense matrices $H$ and $G$.
4. Solution: the known boundary data fill half of the unknowns, and the system is solved for the rest.
   Then $u$ inside $\Omega$ is computed from the representation formula.

## Remarks

Example: for the Laplace equation in $\R^2$, $G(x, y) = -\tfrac{1}{2\pi} \log \abs{x - y}$ is the [[fundamental solution of the Laplacian|BSJ8X2XRN9]], and the method needs only a mesh of the boundary curve.
For a Dirichlet problem, $u$ is given on $\Gamma$ and the system is solved for the nodal values of $q$.

Non-example: for an equation with variable coefficients and no known fundamental solution, step 1 is not available, and the method cannot be applied without extra domain integrals.

The method described here is the direct method, whose unknowns are the boundary values of $u$ and $q$.
Indirect methods instead write $u$ as a layer potential with an unknown density, as in the [[boundary integral method for the Dirichlet problem|QQ62R5ZAXJ]].
With $N$ boundary unknowns its matrices have $N^2$ entries, so fast multipole or hierarchical matrix techniques are used for large problems.
