## Statement

Let $X$ be a finite [[CW complex|5W95QFHXPR]].
Let $\widetilde{KO}(X)$ be reduced [[real K-theory|P7MN6MQV7P]].
Let $J(X)$ be its quotient by the subgroup generated by the classes $[E] - [F]$ of the following kind.
For some $m, n$ with $\operatorname{rank} E + m = \operatorname{rank} F + n$, the [[sphere bundles|3VQ2V5HH3R]] of $E \oplus \R^m$ and $F \oplus \R^n$ are fibre homotopy equivalent.
Let $\psi^k$ be the $k$-th [[Adams operation|KQP64TH307]] on $\widetilde{KO}(X)$.
Then for every integer $k$ and every $x \in \widetilde{KO}(X)$ there is an integer $e \ge 0$ such that
$$k^e (\psi^k - 1)(x) = 0 \quad \text{in } J(X).$$

## Remarks

Adams stated this conjecture in his series of papers on the groups $J(X)$, and proved it in special cases.
The full statement was proved by Daniel Quillen and by Dennis Sullivan, and later also by Becker and Gottlieb with the transfer.
The proofs of Quillen and of Sullivan use ideas from algebraic geometry over fields of positive characteristic and from étale homotopy theory.

The theorem gives an upper bound for $J(X)$, and Adams had found a matching lower bound using the e-invariant.
Together they determine $J(X)$ for many $X$, in particular for spheres, which gives the image of the stable [[J-homomorphism|YYJPRD1HYW]] in the [[stable homotopy groups of spheres|HGZAYPQBXW]].
For $n = 4m - 1$ with $m \ge 1$, this image is cyclic of order equal to the denominator of $B_{2m}/4m$, where $B_{2m}$ is a Bernoulli number.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two different vector bundles can have sphere bundles that are the same from the point of view of homotopy theory.
The group $J(X)$ measures vector bundles up to this coarser equivalence.
The Adams conjecture says that applying an Adams operation to a bundle changes it only by something invisible in $J(X)$, up to multiplication by a power of $k$.

### L2 human

This is the key step in the computation of the image of the J-homomorphism, which is the most regular and best understood part of the stable homotopy groups of spheres.
The denominators of Bernoulli numbers that give its order connect homotopy theory with number theory.
The proofs led to new methods, such as the use of étale homotopy and of transfers for fibrations.
