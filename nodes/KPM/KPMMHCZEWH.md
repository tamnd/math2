## Definition

Expand the internal part of the [[Earth's magnetic field|Z13HPJFV1F]] in spherical harmonics with Gauss coefficients $g_n^m, h_n^m$.
The coefficient $g_1^0$ is the axial dipole coefficient, the strength of the [[dipole|2KPTTBM584]] aligned with the rotation axis.
The *polarity* of the field at a time is *normal* if the average of $g_1^0$ over a few thousand years is negative.
It is *reversed* if this average is positive.
A *geomagnetic reversal* is a transition from one polarity to the other.
After it, the new polarity persists for a time long compared with the transition.

## Remarks

Example: the Brunhes–Matuyama reversal, about 780,000 years ago, took the field from reversed polarity to the present normal polarity, and normal polarity has persisted since then.
Non-example: a *geomagnetic excursion* is a large swing of the field direction that returns to the original polarity within a short time; it fails the persistence clause.
With $g_1^0 < 0$, the dipole points to the geographic south, so field lines leave the southern hemisphere and a compass needle points north.
The sequence of polarity intervals through time is the geomagnetic polarity time scale.
