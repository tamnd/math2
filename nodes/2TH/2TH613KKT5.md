## Definition

Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over a field $k$.
For a [[subalgebra|WKSSTQ69AK]] $\mathfrak h$ of $\mathfrak g$, its [[normalizer|KYTR9VBDHV]] is $N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h) = \set{x \in \mathfrak g : [x, \mathfrak h] \subseteq \mathfrak h}$.
A *Cartan subalgebra* of $\mathfrak g$ is a subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ such that:

1. $\mathfrak h$ is [[nilpotent|MEJ9PGMJ9H]];
2. $N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h) = \mathfrak h$.

## Remarks

Example: in the [[special linear Lie algebra|1ZAT1Z967A]] $\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)$ with $\operatorname{char} k \ne 2$, basis $e, h, f$ and $[h, e] = 2e$, $[h, f] = -2f$, $[e, f] = h$, let $\mathfrak h = kh$.
It is abelian, hence nilpotent.
For $x = ae + bh + cf$ one has $[x, h] = -2a e + 2c f$, which lies in $kh$ only if $a = c = 0$, so $N(\mathfrak h) = \mathfrak h$.

Non-example: $ke$ is abelian, hence nilpotent, but it fails clause 2, since $[h, e] = 2e \in ke$ and so $h \in N(ke)$.

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic $0$ and let $\mathfrak g$ be semisimple.
Then the Cartan subalgebras are exactly the maximal toral subalgebras, the maximal subalgebras made of semisimple elements, and they are abelian.
Over an infinite field every finite-dimensional Lie algebra has a Cartan subalgebra, and over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ any two are conjugate by an automorphism of $\mathfrak g$.
