## Summary

The Richards equation describes the movement of water in an unsaturated soil, where the pores hold both water and air.
It combines conservation of water with [[Darcy's law|DX8RV2XG6H]], in which the hydraulic conductivity depends on how wet the soil is.
It is a nonlinear, possibly degenerate, parabolic equation.
It is the basic model of infiltration and drainage in hydrology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When rain falls on dry soil, water soaks downward.
Gravity pulls it down, and dry soil also sucks water in through capillary forces, like a sponge.
Wet soil lets water pass much more easily than dry soil.
The Richards equation puts these three facts into one equation for the water content of the soil at each depth and time.
