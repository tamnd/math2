## Summary

The method of characteristics in gas dynamics solves the equations of smooth compressible flow by following the curves along which sound waves travel.
In one-dimensional isentropic flow these curves move with speeds $u + c$ and $u - c$, where $u$ is the flow velocity and $c$ the [[speed of sound|SN4J6KP03C]].
Along each family a [[Riemann invariant|GB7R2ERCTA]] stays constant, so the partial differential equations reduce to bookkeeping along curves.
It is the classical tool for nozzle design, simple waves and the formation of [[shock waves|FRVV0NHBXT]].
It is a special case of the general [[method of characteristics|7XAPBCJK3B]].
See the [[definition|QEN19KBT4T]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A small pressure disturbance in a moving gas is carried by the flow and also travels at the speed of sound relative to the gas.
So it moves at $u + c$ forwards and at $u - c$ backwards.
Each of these moving signals carries one number that does not change along its path.
If one knows these two numbers at a point, one knows the velocity and the sound speed there.
For a gas at rest with sound speed $c_0$, the signal paths are the straight lines $x = x_0 \pm c_0 t$.
When signals of one family catch up with each other, the smooth flow breaks down and a shock forms.
