## Summary

The indirect utility function gives the highest utility that a consumer can reach at given prices and wealth.
It is the optimal value of the [[utility maximization problem|A56BG7XYM6]], so it measures welfare in terms of prices and income instead of goods.
It is nonincreasing in prices, nondecreasing in wealth, homogeneous of degree zero and quasiconvex in prices and wealth.
Roy's identity recovers the [[Marshallian demand|EQPEPJ7QQZ]] from its derivatives.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Instead of asking which basket a consumer buys, ask how happy they end up.
The indirect utility answers this as a function of the prices and of the money they have.
Higher prices make them worse off, and more money makes them better off.
For example, with the utility $\sqrt{x_1 x_2}$ and $w$ euros, the best basket gives the utility $w / (2\sqrt{p_1 p_2})$.
