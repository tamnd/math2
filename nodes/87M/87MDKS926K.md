## Example

Let $P$ be a unary predicate symbol and $y$ a variable.
We derive $\forall x\, P(x) \Rightarrow \exists x\, P(x)$ in LK without cut, and we check that the derivation has the [[subformula property|Z2NXKDK06G]].
The whole derivation is three lines.
$$\dfrac{\dfrac{P(y) \Rightarrow P(y)}{\forall x\, P(x) \Rightarrow P(y)}\,{\forall}\mathrm{L}}{\forall x\, P(x) \Rightarrow \exists x\, P(x)}\,{\exists}\mathrm{R}$$

1. The top line $P(y) \Rightarrow P(y)$ is an axiom.
2. The rule ${\forall}$L with the term $t = y$ gives $\forall x\, P(x) \Rightarrow P(y)$, because the [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]] $P(x)[y/x]$ is $P(y)$.
3. The rule ${\exists}$R with the same term gives $\forall x\, P(x) \Rightarrow \exists x\, P(x)$.
4. The formulas of the derivation are $P(y)$, $\forall x\, P(x)$ and $\exists x\, P(x)$.
5. The formula $P(y)$ is the instance $P(x)[y/x]$, so it is a subformula of $\forall x\, P(x)$ in Gentzen's extended sense.
6. The other two formulas occur in the end sequent, so every formula of the derivation is a subformula of the end sequent.

## Remarks

The example shows why first-order subformulas must include the instances $B[t/x]$.
Without them, $P(y)$ would not count, and no cut-free derivation of this sequent would have the property.

Neither ${\forall}$L nor ${\exists}$R has an eigenvariable condition, so any term would do in place of $y$, for example a constant $c$.
The derivation needs some term, and this matches the semantics: first-order structures have nonempty domains, so "everything has $P$" implies "something has $P$".

The converse sequent $\exists x\, P(x) \Rightarrow \forall x\, P(x)$ is not derivable.
It is false in a structure with two elements, where $P$ holds of the first and not of the second.
A cut-free attempt fails at the eigenvariable condition: ${\exists}$L and ${\forall}$R both need a fresh variable, and the axiom at the top would need the two fresh variables to be equal.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The claim is: if everything has property $P$, then something has property $P$.
The proof picks one object $y$ and notes that $y$ has $P$.
The statement "$y$ has $P$" is a piece of "everything has $P$", made by naming one object.
So even this proof only uses pieces of what it proves.

### L2 human

Work from the bottom up.
The goal has a quantifier on each side, so first remove $\exists x\, P(x)$ on the right with ${\exists}$R and a chosen term $y$, then remove $\forall x\, P(x)$ on the left with ${\forall}$L and the same term.
What is left is $P(y) \Rightarrow P(y)$, an axiom.
Watch out: ${\forall}$R and ${\exists}$L have eigenvariable conditions and do not fit here, while ${\forall}$L and ${\exists}$R allow any term.
