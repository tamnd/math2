## Summary

The Jacobi integral is the conserved quantity of the [[circular restricted three-body problem|Y2R2QRPD1B]].
It is minus twice the energy per unit mass of the small body, measured in the frame that rotates with the two large bodies.
It is a [[first integral|0262JP0NV9]], and by a result of Poincaré the problem has no other independent analytic one.
It divides space into regions that the small body can reach and regions it can never enter.
It was found by Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi in 1836.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the frame that turns with the Sun and Jupiter, an asteroid does not keep its ordinary energy, because the frame rotates.
A modified energy, which includes the effect of the rotation, is kept exactly; this is the Jacobi integral.
Since speed squared cannot be negative, the value of the Jacobi integral forbids some regions of space.
For example, an asteroid with a large Jacobi constant that starts close to the Sun can never get close to Jupiter.
