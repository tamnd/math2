## Summary

The Airy and Pratt models are the two classical hydrostatic models of [[isostasy|7CJTGB6M0V]].
In the Airy model the crust has constant density and high topography is balanced by a thick root.
In the Pratt model all columns reach down to the same depth of compensation, and high topography is balanced by lower density.
They are used to predict the gravity effect of compensated topography and to interpret [[Bouguer anomalies|NMH22NTFJT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the Airy picture, mountains are like icebergs: the higher they stand, the deeper their root reaches into the mantle.
In the Pratt picture, all columns reach down to the same depth, and the high ones are made of lighter material, like corks next to wooden blocks.
Both pictures make every column weigh the same at depth.
Real mountain belts are often closer to the Airy picture, while the Pratt picture fits some oceanic ridges, where hot rock is lighter.
