## Definition

Let a thin isotropic plate have mid-plane $\Omega \subset \R^2$, thickness $h$, Young's modulus $E$, Poisson's ratio $\nu$ and bending stiffness $D = Eh^3/(12(1 - \nu^2))$.
Let $q$ be a transverse load per unit area, and let each part of $\partial\Omega$ be clamped, simply supported or free.
The *plate bending problem* in [[Kirchhoff–Love theory|9E6E0SCEYA]] is to find the deflection $w$ such that:

1. $D \Delta^2 w = q$ in $\Omega$, the [[biharmonic plate equation|X1SGK619VJ]].
2. On a clamped edge, $w = 0$ and $\partial_n w = 0$.
3. On a simply supported edge, $w = 0$ and the normal bending moment $M_{nn}$ vanishes.
4. On a free edge, $M_{nn} = 0$ and the Kirchhoff effective shear force $Q_n + \partial_t M_{nt}$ vanishes, where $t$ is the tangent direction.

The bending stresses then are $\sigma_{\alpha\beta} = 12 z M_{\alpha\beta}/h^3$ for $\abs{z} \le h/2$, with $M_{\alpha\beta}$ the bending moments of the plate.

## Remarks

Example: a circular plate of radius $a$, clamped at its edge and under a uniform load $q$, has $w(r) = q(a^2 - r^2)^2/(64D)$.
Check: in polar form $\Delta r^4 = 16 r^2$ and $\Delta r^2 = 4$, so $\Delta^2 w = q/D$, and $w(a) = w'(a) = 0$.
The centre deflection is $qa^4/(64D)$.

Non-example: $w = q(a^2 - r^2)^2/(64D)$ on a simply supported circular plate satisfies clause 1 and $w(a) = 0$, but not $M_{rr}(a) = 0$, since its curvature at the edge is not zero.
The simply supported solution is a different function.

The largest stress magnitude in the plate is $6 \abs{M}/h^2$ at a face, where $\abs{M}$ is the largest principal bending moment.
