## Summary

Cavitation is the formation of vapour cavities in a liquid where the local pressure drops below the vapour pressure.
The cavities collapse later when the pressure rises again.
It occurs near fast propellers, in pumps, in valves and in strong sound fields.
The collapse of the cavities is violent and can erode metal surfaces, make noise and reduce the efficiency of machines.
The dynamics of a single cavity is modelled by the [[Rayleigh–Plesset equation|7HAFBCXD5A]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Water boils when it is heated, but it can also boil without heating if its pressure is lowered enough.
Behind the blades of a fast ship propeller the pressure drops so low that small vapour bubbles appear.
When these bubbles are swept into a region of higher pressure they collapse in a tiny fraction of a second.
The collapse sends out shock waves and tiny jets, which can pit the metal of the propeller over time.
