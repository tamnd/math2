## Statement

[[Special relativity|8CJ62QP9SN]] rests on two postulates about inertial frames of reference.

1. The [[principle of relativity|5ABAVATERQ]]: the laws of physics take the same form in every inertial frame.
2. The invariance of the speed of light: in every inertial frame, light in vacuum has the same speed $c$, whatever the motion of its source.

## Remarks

Add the homogeneity of space and time and the isotropy of space.
Then the two postulates force each change of coordinates between inertial frames to be a Lorentz transformation followed by a translation.
The idea is that the map must be affine and must send each light cone $\set{x : -c^2 t^2 + \abs{\mathbf{x}}^2 = 0}$ to a light cone.
Without the second postulate, the same symmetry arguments still leave the Galilean transformations as a possibility, which is the case of an infinite limiting speed.
Time dilation, length contraction and the relativity of simultaneity are consequences of the two postulates.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The first postulate says that no experiment done inside a closed lab can tell whether the lab is at rest or moving uniformly.
The second says that everybody measures the same speed for light, even when they move towards or away from the lamp.
These two statements look harmless, but together they force clocks and rulers to behave in new ways.

### L2 human

The postulates are the physical input; the mathematical output is Minkowski space with its Lorentz group of symmetries.
In a course, one first derives the Lorentz transformation from the postulates, then reads off time dilation, length contraction and the velocity-addition formula from it.
Maxwell's equations of electromagnetism satisfy the first postulate exactly when frames are related by Lorentz transformations, which was a main historical motivation.
