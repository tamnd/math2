## Statement

Let $\mu \in \R$, and let $f_T(\varepsilon) = 1/(e^{(\varepsilon - \mu)/k_B T} + 1)$ be the [[Fermi–Dirac distribution|FM6XWC4BST]] at temperature $T > 0$.
Let $H \colon \R \to \R$ be continuous, vanish for $\varepsilon < \varepsilon_0$ for some $\varepsilon_0 < \mu$, grow at most polynomially as $\varepsilon \to \infty$, and be infinitely differentiable near $\mu$.
Let $\zeta$ be the [[Riemann zeta function|1XWBY4N0VT]].
Then for every integer $M \ge 1$, as $T \to 0$,
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} H(\varepsilon) f_T(\varepsilon) \, d\varepsilon = \int_{-\infty}^{\mu} H(\varepsilon) \, d\varepsilon + \sum_{n=1}^{M} 2 \left(1 - 2^{1 - 2n}\right) \zeta(2n) \, (k_B T)^{2n} H^{(2n-1)}(\mu) + O\!\left((k_B T)^{2M+2}\right).$$
The first two correction terms are
$$\frac{\pi^2}{6} (k_B T)^2 H'(\mu) + \frac{7 \pi^4}{360} (k_B T)^4 H'''(\mu).$$

## Remarks

Integrate by parts with $K(\varepsilon) = \int_{-\infty}^{\varepsilon} H$ to get $\int K(\varepsilon) \, (-f_T'(\varepsilon)) \, d\varepsilon$.
The weight $-f_T'$ is an even bump around $\mu$ of width about $k_B T$ and total mass $1$, so Taylor expanding $K$ at $\mu$ leaves only even moments.
These moments are $\int_{\R} x^{2n} \frac{e^x}{(e^x + 1)^2} \, dx = 2 (1 - 2^{1-2n}) (2n)! \, \zeta(2n)$ after the scaling $x = (\varepsilon - \mu)/k_B T$; the parts of the integral far from $\mu$ are exponentially small.
The series is asymptotic and need not converge.

Example: for the free electron gas, take $H(\varepsilon) = g(\varepsilon)$, the density of states.
Keeping the first correction gives the chemical potential $\mu(T) \approx E_F \left(1 - \frac{\pi^2}{12} (k_B T / E_F)^2\right)$ and the electronic heat capacity $C \approx \frac{\pi^2}{2} N k_B \, \frac{k_B T}{E_F}$, linear in $T$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

At low temperature the Fermi–Dirac occupation is almost a sharp step at $\mu$, rounded over a width of order $k_B T$.
The expansion says how much an integral against this rounded step differs from the integral against the sharp step.
The answer is a series in $T^2$, whose terms only involve derivatives of $H$ at the step.
This is why many properties of metals change with temperature like $T^2$, and their heat capacity like $T$.

### L2 human

The expansion is the standard tool for the degenerate [[Fermi gas|663MJA8NAC]] at $k_B T \ll E_F$.
One applies it twice: once to the particle number, to find $\mu(T)$, and once to the energy, to find the heat capacity.
The coefficients $\pi^2/6$ and $7\pi^4/360$ are $2\eta(2)$ and $2\eta(4)$, where $\eta(s) = (1 - 2^{1-s}) \zeta(s)$ is the Dirichlet eta function.
The first correction is usually enough, unless $H$ varies strongly on the scale $k_B T$ around $\mu$.
