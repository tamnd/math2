## Summary

Order, lattices and universal algebra is the study of ordered sets, of lattices, and of the notions that all algebraic structures share.
A partially ordered set is a set with a relation that is reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive, and a lattice is a partially ordered set in which each pair of elements has a least upper bound and a greatest lower bound.
Orders and lattices occur in logic, topology, combinatorics and computer science, for example as Boolean algebras, as lattices of subgroups and as the domains of program semantics.
Universal algebra gives one theory of homomorphisms, congruences and identities for all algebras.
Boole and Dedekind started the algebra of logic and the theory of lattices in the nineteenth century, and Garrett Birkhoff made lattice theory and universal algebra into fields of their own in the 1930s.
This topic collects the order, lattices and universal algebra pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Group theory, mathematical logic, set theory and category theory have their own topics.
