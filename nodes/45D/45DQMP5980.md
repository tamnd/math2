## Problem

An input is a pair $(G, H)$ of finite [[graphs|00C0BR7D7H]], given by adjacency matrices.
The *graph isomorphism problem* asks whether $G$ and $H$ are [[isomorphic|0F7QKGJS6B]].
That is, it asks for a bijection $\pi$ from the vertices of $G$ to the vertices of $H$ with the following property.
For all vertices $u, v$ of $G$, they are adjacent in $G$ exactly when $\pi(u), \pi(v)$ are adjacent in $H$.

## Remarks

The problem lies in [[NP|QQRA9GBW51]], since an isomorphism $\pi$ is a certificate checkable in polynomial time.
It is not known to lie in [[P|GVT2NKN37K]], and it is not known to be [[NP-complete|JGZWHWA8P8]].
If it were NP-complete, the polynomial hierarchy would collapse to its second level, so most researchers expect that it is not.
It is thus a main candidate for a natural NP-intermediate problem, a kind of problem that exists by [[Ladner's theorem|3GT13ACR68]] if $\mathrm{P} \ne \mathrm{NP}$.
In 2015 László Babai announced an algorithm in quasi-polynomial time $\exp((\log n)^{O(1)})$ for graphs with $n$ vertices.
Polynomial-time algorithms are known for special classes, such as trees, planar graphs and graphs of bounded degree.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two drawings of networks are given, with the nodes labelled differently.
The question is whether they show the same network after renaming the nodes.
Trying all $n!$ renamings works but is hopeless for large $n$.
The problem is unusual: it is not known to be easy, yet there are strong reasons to think it is not among the hardest problems in NP.
