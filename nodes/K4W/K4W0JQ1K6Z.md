## Summary

Timoshenko–Ehrenfest beam theory is a [[beam theory|GDM0J9ZCBT]] that accounts for shear deformation and for the rotational inertia of the cross-sections.
Cross-sections stay plane but need not stay perpendicular to the bent axis, so the rotation of a section is a second unknown besides the deflection.
It is more accurate than the [[Euler–Bernoulli beam equation|13FGKM7NQ0]] for short or thick beams and for vibrations of high frequency.
When the shear stiffness tends to infinity and the rotational inertia is neglected, it reduces to the Euler–Bernoulli theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a beam as a stack of thin cards standing side by side along its length.
In the simplest theory the cards always stay at right angles to the bent centre line.
In a short, thick beam the cards also slide against each other a little, which adds extra sag.
Timoshenko theory lets each card tilt on its own, and it charges the beam an energy for the sliding.
For a long thin beam the extra sag is tiny, but for a stubby beam it can be a large part of the total.
