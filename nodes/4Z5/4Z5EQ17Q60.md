## Summary

An anharmonic oscillator is an oscillating system whose restoring force is not proportional to the displacement.
It is what a real oscillator looks like once the displacement is no longer small, beyond the [[harmonic oscillator|0YG0ZJPTWG]] approximation.
Its frequency depends on the amplitude, and under periodic forcing it shows nonlinear resonance and can behave chaotically.
The [[pendulum equation|0SHPXM2HWH]] and the [[Duffing equation|SGQQGP261M]] are the standard examples.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A perfect spring pulls back twice as hard when stretched twice as far.
Real springs, pendulums and molecular bonds only do this approximately, for small displacements.
For large swings the pull grows faster or slower than the stretch, and the oscillator is called anharmonic.
For example, a pendulum released from a large angle takes noticeably longer per swing than one released from a small angle.
