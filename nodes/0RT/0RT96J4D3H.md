## Definition

A linear form is a linear map whose values are scalars.
Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a vector space over $F$.
A *linear form* on $V$ is a [[linear map|0RJF668P1T]] $f \colon V \to F$, where $F$ is a vector space over itself.
Thus
$$f(u + v) = f(u) + f(v) \quad \text{and} \quad f(av) = a f(v)$$
for all $u, v \in V$ and all $a \in F$.

## Notation

A linear form is also called a *linear functional*, a *covector* or a *one-form*.
The value $f(v)$ is also written $\langle f, v \rangle$.

## Remarks

The definition has two conditions, and each one rules out a familiar map.
Additivity rules out $(x, y) \mapsto xy$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, which sends $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ to $0$ but sends their sum $(1, 1)$ to $1$.
Homogeneity rules out complex conjugation on $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{C}$, since $\overline{i \cdot 1} = -i$ but $i \cdot \bar 1 = i$.
The target must be the field $F$ itself, so a linear map $V \to F^2$ is not a linear form, although each of its two coordinates is one.

A small example on $\mathbb{R}^2$ is $f(x, y) = 2x - 3y$.
It sends $(3, 2)$ to $0$, and its kernel is the line $2x = 3y$.
On $F^n$ each linear form is $x \mapsto c_1 x_1 + \dots + c_n x_n$ for unique scalars $c_i = f(e_i)$.
The [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]] is a linear form on the space of $n \times n$ matrices, and the linear forms on $V$ together make up the [[dual space|07JBSZE0V4]].
The [[concept page|07YQ1C78G0]] gives more examples, the non-examples and the first properties.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A linear form takes a vector and returns one number, and it respects adding and scaling.
On the plane, $(x, y) \mapsto 2x - 3y$ is a linear form.
It sends $(1, 1)$ to $-1$ and $(2, 2)$ to $-2$, so doubling the input doubles the output.
The points it sends to $0$ form the line $2x = 3y$.
