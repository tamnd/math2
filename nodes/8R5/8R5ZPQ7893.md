## Definition

A *context-free grammar* is a [[formal grammar|DP1HXZGPCM]] $G = (N, \Sigma, P, S)$ in which every rule has the form $A \to \beta$ with $A \in N$ and $\beta \in (N \cup \Sigma)^*$.
The relation $\Rightarrow$, derivations and the [[language|5MFZ4VNY6D]] $L(G) = \set{w \in \Sigma^* : S \Rightarrow^* w}$ are those of the formal grammar.

## Remarks

Example: $N = \set{S}$, $\Sigma = \set{(, )}$, $P = \set{S \to SS, \ S \to (S), \ S \to \varepsilon}$.
Every left side is the single nonterminal $S$, so this grammar is context-free; it generates the balanced strings of brackets.
Non-example: a grammar with the rule $aA \to ab$ is not context-free, because the left side has two symbols.
A *leftmost derivation* rewrites the leftmost nonterminal at each step.
A derivation of a [[string|1P6EP01HZF]] $w$ corresponds to a *parse tree*, a [[rooted tree|0Y0JGC2K9T]] with root $S$.
Its inner nodes are labelled by nonterminals, the children of a node are read off a rule, and the leaves spell $w$ from left to right.
Parse trees of $w$ are in bijection with leftmost derivations of $w$.
