## Summary

A bound state of a quantum system is a normalisable eigenvector of its Hamiltonian.
It describes a particle that stays in a bounded region for all time, such as an electron held by a nucleus.
Bound states correspond to the point spectrum of the Hamiltonian, while scattering states correspond to the continuous spectrum.
For a Schrödinger operator with a potential that vanishes at infinity, the bound states with negative energy lie below the [[essential spectrum|FRZG0E15SK]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some quantum particles are trapped and some fly away.
A trapped particle is in a bound state: it has a definite energy, and its wave function dies off far away, so the total probability is finite.
A free particle has a wave that spreads over all of space and cannot be normalised.
The electron of a hydrogen atom in its lowest state, with wave function proportional to $e^{-r}$, is a typical bound state.
