## Summary

A detonation is a combustion wave that travels through a reactive gas faster than sound.
It is a [[shock wave|FRVV0NHBXT]] coupled to a chemical reaction.
The shock heats the fresh mixture, the mixture burns, and the energy released drives the shock.
It differs from a deflagration, an ordinary [[flame|5P38MRV478]], which moves slower than sound by conduction of heat.
The [[Chapman–Jouguet condition|ZZCNP3N36B]] and the [[ZND model|8KJJKCR26M]] are the classical theories of its speed and structure.
See the [[definition|GAS1A4ZYYF]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A candle flame creeps into fresh gas slowly, by heating its neighbour.
A detonation instead smashes into the fresh gas with a shock, which heats it so much that it burns at once.
The burning then pushes the shock forward, so the two travel together.
In gaseous mixtures such waves move at speeds of the order of kilometres per second.
