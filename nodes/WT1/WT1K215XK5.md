## Proof

Let $J$ be a two-sided ideal of $M_n(F)$ with $J \ne \{0\}$.
The plan is to cut one nonzero entry out of a matrix in $J$, turn it into every matrix unit, and add these up to the identity.

1. Choose $A \in J$ with $A \ne 0$, and choose indices $j, k$ with $a_{jk} \ne 0$.
2. Write $A = \sum_{p, q} a_{pq} E_{pq}$. By the [[product of matrix units|E66YRYVWQ3]], $E_{ij} E_{pq} E_{kl}$ equals $E_{il}$ when $p = j$ and $q = k$, and $0$ otherwise. So for any indices $i$ and $l$,
$$E_{ij} A E_{kl} = \sum_{p, q} a_{pq} E_{ij} E_{pq} E_{kl} = a_{jk} E_{il}.$$
3. $J$ is a two-sided ideal and $A \in J$, so $E_{ij} A E_{kl} \in J$. Thus $a_{jk} E_{il} \in J$.
4. Since $F$ is a field, $a_{jk}$ has an inverse, and the [[scalar matrix|0FRM62SG7D]] $a_{jk}^{-1} I$ lies in $M_n(F)$. Multiplying by it on the left keeps us in $J$, so $(a_{jk}^{-1} I)(a_{jk} E_{il}) = E_{il} \in J$.
5. Step 4 holds for every pair $(i, l)$, so every [[matrix unit|4H93XF4RVH]] lies in $J$.
6. A [[two-sided ideal|0CD87SFM2N]] is closed under addition, so $I_n = E_{11} + E_{22} + \dots + E_{nn} \in J$.
7. For every $B \in M_n(F)$, we have $B = B I_n \in J$. So $J = M_n(F)$.

## Idea

The products $E_{ij} A E_{kl}$ cut out the single entry $a_{jk}$ of $A$ and place it in position $(i, l)$.
Over a field, one nonzero entry can be scaled to $1$.
So one nonzero matrix in the ideal produces every matrix unit, then the identity, and then everything.

## Variants

Over a division ring $D$, the same proof works word for word, with the inverse $a_{jk}^{-1}$ multiplied on the left in step 4.

Over a general ring $R$ with identity, steps 1 to 3 still show that $a_{jk} E_{il} \in J$ for every entry $a_{jk}$ of every matrix in $J$.
The set $I$ of all entries of matrices in $J$ is then a two-sided ideal of $R$, and $J = M_n(I)$.
This gives the correspondence between ideals of $R$ and ideals of $M_n(R)$.
