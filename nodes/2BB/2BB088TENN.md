## Definition

*Rational thermodynamics* is the theory of thermodynamic processes in [[continuous media|X01DZTS224]] built on these principles.

1. Absolute [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]] $\theta > 0$ and specific [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]] $\eta$ are primitive fields, defined for all states and not only near equilibrium.
2. Every process satisfies the balance laws of mass, linear momentum, angular momentum and energy.
3. A material is given by [[constitutive equations|Q4C55V47A8]] for the stress, heat flux, specific internal energy and specific entropy. They are functions of the history of the deformation and the temperature.
4. The second law is the [[Clausius–Duhem inequality|MKXA9E1Z2T]], and the constitutive equations must be such that it holds in every process that satisfies the balance laws.

## Remarks

Example: a thermoelastic solid with heat conduction, whose constitutive equations depend on the deformation gradient, the temperature and the temperature gradient, is a material in this sense.
Clause 4 then implies that the free energy does not depend on the temperature gradient and that the stress and the entropy are derivatives of the free energy.
Non-example: classical non-equilibrium thermodynamics based on local equilibrium defines entropy only through the equilibrium Gibbs relation, so it does not take clause 1 as an axiom.
The approach is also called continuum thermodynamics; its critics question the use of entropy and temperature far from equilibrium.
