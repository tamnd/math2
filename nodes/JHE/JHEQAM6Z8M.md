## Summary

The Föppl–von Kármán equations describe thin elastic plates whose deflections are large compared with their thickness but still small compared with their width.
They couple the bending of the plate with the stretching of its mid-plane, which the linear [[biharmonic plate equation|X1SGK619VJ]] ignores.
The stretching is encoded by an [[Airy stress function|7ZGSJRYBGK]], and it is driven by the Gaussian curvature of the deflected surface.
They explain the [[buckling|4GAM4KXSM8]] of plates, wrinkling and crumpling of sheets, and the stiffening of a plate as it bends.

## Explanations

### L1 human

You can roll a sheet of paper into a tube without stretching it, but you cannot press it onto a ball without creasing or stretching it.
That is because a tube is curved in one direction only, while a ball is curved in two.
The Föppl–von Kármán equations say exactly this for a thin plate: bending into a doubly curved shape forces the plate to stretch in its own plane.
Stretching costs much more energy than bending, so this coupling makes a strongly bent plate stiffer and makes compressed plates buckle into wrinkles.
