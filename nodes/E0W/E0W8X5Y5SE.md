## Statement

Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]] and let $K_2(F)$ be [[K2 of the ring|W7T56A6YF8]] $F$.
Then $K_2(F)$ is the abelian group generated by the symbols $\set{a, b}$, for $a, b \in F^\times$, subject only to the relations
$$\set{aa', b} = \set{a, b}\set{a', b}, \qquad \set{a, bb'} = \set{a, b}\set{a, b'}, \qquad \set{a, 1 - a} = 1 \ (a \ne 0, 1).$$
Equivalently, the map $(a, b) \mapsto \set{a, b}$ is a universal [[Steinberg symbol|HRYG5D46EQ]].
That is, let $c$ be a Steinberg symbol on $F$ with values in an abelian group $A$.
Then $c(a, b) = h(\set{a, b})$ for a unique homomorphism $h \colon K_2(F) \to A$.

## Remarks

In the language of [[Milnor K-theory|2YXDQ7Q445]], the theorem says that the natural map $K_2^M(F) \to K_2(F)$ is an isomorphism.
The symbol $\set{a, b}$ is defined in the Steinberg group as a commutator of lifts of the diagonal matrices $\operatorname{diag}(a, a^{-1}, 1)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(b, 1, b^{-1})$.
The hard part of the proof is the absence of other relations.
From a Steinberg symbol one builds a central extension of $SL(F)$, and the universal property of the Steinberg group gives the homomorphism $h$.
Matsumoto proved a more general result for the universal central extensions of split simply connected groups over fields.

Example: by the theorem, the real Hilbert symbol induces $h \colon K_2(\R) \to \set{\pm 1}$ with $h(\set{-1, -1}) = -1$, so $\set{-1, -1} \ne 1$ in $K_2(\R)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

$K_2$ of a field is defined through matrices and a big group with many relations, which is hard to compute directly.
Matsumoto's theorem gives a short description: $K_2(F)$ is generated by symbols in two nonzero elements, with only two obvious kinds of rules.
So a homomorphism out of $K_2(F)$ is the same as a symbol obeying those rules.

### L2 human

The theorem turns classical symbols into homomorphisms out of $K_2$: the Hilbert symbol, the norm residue symbol and the tame symbol all factor through $K_2(F)$.
This is how one computes $K_2(\Q)$ with tame symbols and relates $K_2(F)$ to the Brauer group.
It is also the starting point of Milnor K-theory, which takes the same generators and relations in every degree.
