## Summary

The operator product expansion writes the product of two local fields at nearby points as a sum of single local fields at one point.
The coefficients are functions of the separation.
Kenneth Wilson proposed it in 1969 as a general feature of quantum field theory.
In a [[conformal field theory|837V89WJKS]] the coefficient functions are fixed by symmetry up to constants, and the expansion converges.
So the dimensions of the fields and these constants determine all correlation functions.
In two dimensions it becomes the algebraic structure of a [[vertex operator algebra|GHRPQAJY43]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two fields placed very close together look, from far away, like a single field placed at one point.
The operator product expansion makes this precise: it lists the single fields that the pair can look like, each with a weight that depends on the distance between the two.
Usually some weights blow up as the distance shrinks, which records how singular the product of two fields is.
It works like a multiplication table for the fields of the theory.
