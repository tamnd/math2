## Statement

Let $f \colon \R^n \to \R$ be a differentiable [[convex function|07GR3TNW4H]] whose [[gradient|0ABK9V9Y64]] $\nabla f$ is [[Lipschitz|0AW1E6YTJY]] with constant $L > 0$, that is, $\norm{\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)} \le L \norm{x - y}$ for all $x, y$.
Assume that $f$ has a minimiser $x^*$.
Let $x_0 \in \R^n$, put $y_1 = x_0$ and $t_1 = 1$, and for $k \ge 1$ define
$$x_k = y_k - \frac{1}{L} \nabla f(y_k), \qquad t_{k+1} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4 t_k^2}}{2}, \qquad y_{k+1} = x_k + \frac{t_k - 1}{t_{k+1}} (x_k - x_{k-1}).$$
Then for every $k \ge 1$,
$$f(x_k) - f(x^*) \le \frac{2 L \norm{x_0 - x^*}^2}{(k + 1)^2}.$$

## Remarks

The proof uses an estimate sequence, or equivalently a potential function of the form $t_k^2 \big(f(x_k) - f(x^*)\big) + \tfrac{L}{2} \norm{u_k - x^*}^2$ with suitable points $u_k$, which does not increase from one step to the next.
The choice of $t_k$ satisfies $t_{k+1}^2 - t_{k+1} = t_k^2$ and gives $t_k \ge (k + 1)/2$, which yields the factor $(k + 1)^2$.

Plain [[gradient descent|12317R8RTJ]] with step $1/L$ only guarantees a bound of order $L \norm{x_0 - x^*}^2 / k$.
For example, to reach accuracy $\varepsilon$, gradient descent may need of order $1/\varepsilon$ steps while the accelerated method needs of order $1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
The rate $1/k^2$ cannot be improved in general.
Nesterov showed this for every method whose steps lie in the span of earlier gradients.
For such a method there are functions as above on which the error after $k$ steps is at least a constant times $L \norm{x_0 - x^*}^2 / k^2$.
This lower bound holds as long as $k$ is small compared with $n$.

The Lipschitz condition on $\nabla f$ is needed: for convex functions that are only Lipschitz, not smooth, first-order methods cannot in general do better than order $1/\sqrt{k}$.
The method is not monotone, so $f(x_k)$ may increase at some steps.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gradient descent walks downhill one step at a time.
The accelerated method also remembers its last move and keeps some of that momentum, like a ball rolling down a valley.
With the right amount of momentum it reaches a given accuracy much faster: the error shrinks like $1/k^2$ instead of $1/k$ after $k$ steps.

### L2 human

The theorem gives the optimal worst-case rate for minimising smooth convex functions with gradient information.
The same idea combined with a proximal step gives FISTA, which handles a nonsmooth term such as an $\ell_1$ penalty.
For strongly convex functions a variant with constant momentum converges linearly, with a rate depending on the square root of the condition number rather than the condition number itself.
The form above, with the sequence $t_k$, is the one of Beck and Teboulle; Nesterov introduced the method in 1983.
