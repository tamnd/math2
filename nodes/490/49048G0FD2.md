## Example

We show that $p, \neg p \nvDash_{D_2} p \land \neg p$, and that the discussive conjunction $\land_d$ restores adjunction.

1. No world of an S5 model makes $p \land \neg p$ true. So $\Diamond(p \land \neg p)$ is false in every model.
2. Hence $p \land \neg p \vDash_{D_2} q$ for every $q$: a single contradictory premise explodes.
3. The [[two-world model|9X7ZPK15C5]] makes $\Diamond p$ and $\Diamond\neg p$ true. By step 1, it makes $\Diamond(p \land \neg p)$ false.
4. So $p, \neg p \nvDash_{D_2} p \land \neg p$. [[Conjunction|025YZN3VJW]] introduction, or adjunction, fails.
5. Now take $p \land_d \neg p := p \land \Diamond\neg p$. In the same model, $p \land \Diamond\neg p$ is true at $w_1$, since $p$ is true there and $\Diamond\neg p$ is true at every world.
6. In general, if $\Diamond A$ and $\Diamond B$ are true, then at a world $w$ where $A$ is true, $A \land \Diamond B$ is true. So $\Diamond(A \land \Diamond B)$ is true, and $A, B \vDash_{D_2} A \land_d B$.

## Remarks

Step 2 shows that $D_2$ is not "paraconsistent everywhere": it tolerates contradictions spread over premises, not inside one premise.
Steps 5 and 6 show that the discussive conjunction expresses "$A$ is held, and someone holds $B$".
It does not explode: $p \land_d \neg p$ holds in the model of step 3 while $q$ does not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ann says "$p$" and Bob says "not $p$".
Nobody says "$p$ and not $p$", so the discussion does not contain that sentence.
But the weaker claim "$p$, and someone says not $p$" is true of the discussion.
Discussive conjunction is this weaker claim.

### L2 human

Compare $\Diamond(p \land \neg p)$ with $\Diamond(p \land \Diamond\neg p)$ in the two-world model.
The first needs one world with $p$ and $\neg p$; there is none.
The second needs one world with $p$, while $\neg p$ may hold at another world; $w_1$ is such a world.
A trap is to read $\land_d$ as symmetric; $A \land_d B$ and $B \land_d A$ express different claims.
