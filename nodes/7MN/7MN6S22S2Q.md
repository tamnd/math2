## Proof

We build $u$ from an orthonormal basis, then prove uniqueness, and then the properties of $\Phi$.

1. Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional [[inner product space|01EK923SKP]] over $\mathbb{K}$, with [[inner product|0VGSYBWHV2]] linear in the first slot. Let $f \colon V \to \mathbb{K}$ be a [[linear form|0RT96J4D3H]].
2. Conjugate symmetry and linearity in the first slot give $\ip{w}{a u + b u'} = \overline{a}\ip{w}{u} + \overline{b}\ip{w}{u'}$ for all vectors and scalars.
3. By the [[existence theorem|0Y85GYEGSF]], $V$ has an [[orthonormal basis|0FWJ0VW561]] $e_1, \dots, e_n$. Set $u = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \overline{f(e_i)}\, e_i$.
4. Let $v \in V$. By the [[formula for coordinates|09DZ8CD1AK]], $v = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \ip{v}{e_i} e_i$.
5. Since $f$ is linear, step 4 gives $f(v) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \ip{v}{e_i} f(e_i)$.
6. By step 2, $\ip{v}{u} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \overline{\overline{f(e_i)}}\, \ip{v}{e_i} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(e_i) \ip{v}{e_i}$.
7. Steps 5 and 6 give $f(v) = \ip{v}{u}$ for all $v \in V$. This proves existence in part 1, and part 2.
8. Uniqueness. Let $u, u' \in V$ with $\ip{v}{u} = f(v) = \ip{v}{u'}$ for all $v \in V$.
9. By step 2, $\ip{v}{u - u'} = \ip{v}{u} - \ip{v}{u'} = 0$ for all $v \in V$.
10. Take $v = u - u'$. Then $\ip{u - u'}{u - u'} = 0$, and positive definiteness gives $u = u'$.
11. Part 3. Each $\Phi(u)$ is a linear form, by linearity in the first slot. So $\Phi$ maps $V$ to $V^*$.
12. Steps 3 to 7 show that each $f \in V^*$ equals $\Phi(u)$ for some $u$. So $\Phi$ is surjective.
13. If $\Phi(u) = \Phi(u')$, then steps 8 to 10 with $f = \Phi(u)$ give $u = u'$. So $\Phi$ is injective.
14. By step 2, $\Phi(u + u')(v) = \Phi(u)(v) + \Phi(u')(v)$ and $\Phi(cu)(v) = \overline{c}\, \Phi(u)(v)$ for every $v$.
15. So $\Phi$ is additive and $\Phi(cu) = \overline{c}\, \Phi(u)$. Over $\mathbb{R}$, $\overline{c} = c$, so $\Phi$ is linear.

## Idea

A linear form is fixed by its values on a basis.
For an orthonormal basis, the form $\ip{\cdot}{u}$ with $u = \sum_i c_i e_i$ takes the value $\overline{c_i}$ at $e_i$.
So we choose $c_i = \overline{f(e_i)}$.
Uniqueness holds because only $0$ is orthogonal to every vector.

## Variants

- Without an orthonormal basis: $\Phi$ is injective by steps 8 to 10, and $\dim V = \dim V^*$. An injective map between spaces of equal dimension is onto. Over $\mathbb{C}$ one applies this to $\Phi$ as a map of real vector spaces.
- With a kernel: for $f \neq 0$, pick a unit vector $w$ orthogonal to $\ker f$, and set $u = \overline{f(w)}\, w$. This is the proof that extends to Hilbert spaces.
