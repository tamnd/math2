## Proof

We build $u$ from an orthonormal basis, then prove that it is unique, and then read off the properties of $\Phi$.

1. Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional [[inner product space|01EK923SKP]] over $\mathbb{K}$, with an [[inner product|0VGSYBWHV2]] that is linear in the first slot. Let $f \colon V \to \mathbb{K}$ be a [[linear form|0RT96J4D3H]].
2. Conjugate symmetry and linearity in the first slot give conjugate-linearity in the second slot: $\ip{w}{a u + b u'} = \overline{a} \ip{w}{u} + \overline{b} \ip{w}{u'}$ for all vectors $w, u, u'$ and scalars $a, b$.
3. By the [[existence theorem|0Y85GYEGSF]], $V$ has an [[orthonormal basis|0FWJ0VW561]] $e_1, \dots, e_n$. Set $u = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \overline{f(e_i)}\, e_i$.
4. Let $v \in V$. By the [[formula for coordinates|09DZ8CD1AK]] in an orthonormal basis, $v = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \ip{v}{e_i} e_i$.
5. Since $f$ is linear, step 4 gives $f(v) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \ip{v}{e_i} f(e_i)$.
6. By step 2, $\ip{v}{u} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \overline{\overline{f(e_i)}}\, \ip{v}{e_i} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(e_i) \ip{v}{e_i}$.
7. Steps 5 and 6 give $f(v) = \ip{v}{u}$ for all $v \in V$. This proves the existence in part 1, and part 2.
8. Uniqueness. Let $u, u' \in V$ with $\ip{v}{u} = f(v) = \ip{v}{u'}$ for all $v \in V$.
9. By step 2, $\ip{v}{u - u'} = \ip{v}{u} - \ip{v}{u'} = 0$ for all $v \in V$.
10. Take $v = u - u'$. Then $\ip{u - u'}{u - u'} = 0$, and positive definiteness gives $u - u' = 0$.
11. Part 3. Each $\Phi(u) = \ip{\cdot}{u}$ is a linear form, by linearity in the first slot, so $\Phi$ maps $V$ to $V^*$.
12. Steps 3 to 7 show that each $f \in V^*$ equals $\Phi(u)$ for some $u$, so $\Phi$ is surjective.
13. If $\Phi(u) = \Phi(u')$, then steps 8 to 10 with $f = \Phi(u)$ give $u = u'$, so $\Phi$ is injective.
14. By step 2, $\Phi(u + u')(v) = \Phi(u)(v) + \Phi(u')(v)$ and $\Phi(c u)(v) = \overline{c}\, \Phi(u)(v)$ for every $v \in V$.
15. So $\Phi$ is additive and $\Phi(c u) = \overline{c}\, \Phi(u)$. Over $\mathbb{R}$ we have $\overline{c} = c$, so $\Phi$ is linear.

This proves parts 1 to 3.

## Idea

A linear form is fixed by its values on a basis, so we only need a vector $u$ whose form $\ip{\cdot}{u}$ has the right values on an orthonormal basis.
For $u = \sum_i c_i e_i$, orthonormality gives $\ip{e_j}{u} = \overline{c_j}$, so the choice $c_j = \overline{f(e_j)}$ does it.
The two conjugates cancel, and that is why the formula for $u$ has a bar.
Uniqueness holds because the only vector orthogonal to every vector is $0$.

## Variants

There is a proof without an orthonormal basis.
Steps 8 to 13 show that $\Phi$ is injective, and $V$ and $V^*$ have the same dimension; an injective map between spaces of the same finite dimension is onto.
Over $\mathbb{C}$ the map $\Phi$ is not complex linear, so we apply this to $\Phi$ as a real linear map between the real spaces $V$ and $V^*$, which both have real dimension $2n$.

A second proof uses the kernel of $f$, and it is the one that extends to Hilbert spaces.
For $f \ne 0$, the kernel has dimension $n - 1$, so its orthogonal complement is a line; pick a unit vector $w$ on that line, and set $u = \overline{f(w)}\, w$.
Every $v$ splits as $v = (v - \lambda w) + \lambda w$ with $\lambda = f(v) / f(w)$ and $v - \lambda w \in \ker f$, so $\ip{v}{u} = \lambda f(w) \ip{w}{w} = f(v)$.
