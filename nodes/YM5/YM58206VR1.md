## Summary

Bland's rule is a pivot rule for the [[simplex algorithm|MH272QHWPM]].
Among the candidates to enter or to leave the basis, it always chooses the one with the smallest index.
Robert Bland proved that with this rule the simplex algorithm never cycles.
So it ends after finitely many steps, even on [[degenerate|PJVT97Z7EN]] programs.
It is simple to state, but in practice it often needs many more steps than other rules.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The simplex method has to make choices at each step: which variable to bring in and which to take out.
A careless choice can make it go round in circles forever at a degenerate corner.
Bland's rule breaks every tie in the same plain way: take the variable with the lowest number.
Surprisingly, this simple rule is enough to guarantee that the method always stops.
For example, if $x_2$ and $x_5$ could both enter the basis, Bland's rule picks $x_2$.
