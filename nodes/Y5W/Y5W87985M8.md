## Definition

Consider a linear wave problem in a layered medium.
Its properties depend only on the depth $z$ and are constant in each of finitely many layers.
Assume that the medium is at rest before time $t = 0$ and that the source is impulsive.
Write $x$ for a horizontal coordinate and $u(x, z, t)$ for the field.
The *Cagniard–De Hoop method* computes $u$ in these steps.

1. Take the one-sided [[Laplace transform|0V49JEZW06]] in $t$ with a real parameter $s > 0$.
2. Take a [[Fourier transform|DPVM34ADZV]] in $x$ whose wavenumber is written $s p$, so that $p$ is a horizontal slowness.
3. Solve the resulting ordinary differential equations in $z$, layer by layer.
   The transformed field becomes an integral over $p$ of terms $F(p) \, e^{-s \, \tau(p)}$, with $\tau(p)$ linear in $x$ and $z$.
4. For each term, deform the integration path in the complex $p$-plane, using Cauchy's theorem, into the *Cagniard path* on which $\tau(p)$ is real and increasing.
5. Change variables from $p$ to $\tau$ along that path, so that the term takes the form $\int_0^\infty f(\tau) e^{-s \tau} \, d\tau$.
6. Read off $f$ as the time-domain contribution, by the uniqueness of the Laplace transform for real $s$.

## Remarks

Example: for the scalar wave equation $c^{-2} u_{tt} - \Delta u = \delta(x)\delta(z)\delta(t)$ in the plane, the Cagniard path starts at $\tau = r/c$ with $r = \sqrt{x^2 + z^2}$.
The method yields $u = H(t - r/c) / \big(2 \pi \sqrt{t^2 - r^2/c^2}\big)$, where $H$ is the [[Heaviside step function|09C4JP7Y0M]].
This is the known fundamental solution of the two-dimensional wave equation.

Non-example: a medium whose properties vary continuously with depth does not fit the setting, because step 3 then gives no exponential form $e^{-s\tau(p)}$ with $\tau$ linear in $x$ and $z$.

The method works with real $s$ only, which is enough because a Laplace transform is determined by its values on a half-line.
In elastic problems each term corresponds to one wave, such as a [[P-wave|EEPJJHC5JZ]], an [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]], a head wave or a [[Rayleigh wave|X32RRM7ATM]].
