## Summary

Operations research is the discipline that uses mathematical models and analysis to improve decisions in organizations and systems.
Its main tools are [[mathematical optimization|W3ZXKT3DS1]], probability and statistics, [[queueing theory|P64V0HTE78]] and simulation.
Typical problems are scheduling, routing, inventory control, facility location and the allocation of scarce resources.
Linear programming and the simplex method grew up within it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Operations research turns practical decisions into mathematical problems and solves them.
An airline must decide which crew flies which plane, a factory how much of each product to make, a hospital how many nurses to have on each shift.
In each case one builds a model with variables, rules and a goal, and then computes a good or best decision.
For example, a factory earns $3$ per chair and $5$ per table and has limited wood and labour.
A small linear program tells it how many of each to make.
