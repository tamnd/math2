## Summary

Matsuno's theory describes all the small-amplitude waves of a shallow fluid layer trapped near the equator.
It uses the linear [[shallow water equations|NT73Y95RY5]] on the equatorial [[beta plane|F2D7MXW8V1]].
The waves are confined within an equatorial deformation radius of the equator, and their latitudinal structure is given by [[Hermite functions|52VS07H6GK]].
They form a discrete family: inertia-gravity waves, equatorial [[Rossby waves|6N1MQMA9TM]], the mixed Rossby–gravity wave and the equatorial [[Kelvin wave|QERM9Y9QSJ]].
The theory, published by Taroh Matsuno in 1966, is the basis for understanding the observed waves of the tropical atmosphere and ocean.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The equator acts like a channel for waves, because the Earth's rotation pushes moving fluid back towards it from both sides.
Matsuno worked out every kind of wave that can live in this channel.
Each wave has a shape across the equator described by a simple bell-shaped function times a polynomial, with $0$, $1$, $2$, ... crossings of zero.
Some of these waves are fast and behave like gravity waves, others are slow and move westward like Rossby waves, and one, the Kelvin wave, always moves eastward.
Observers later found these waves in the winds and clouds of the tropics.
