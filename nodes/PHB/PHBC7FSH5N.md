## Definition

Let $A$ be a separable [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]].
Let $(H, \rho, F_0)$ and $(H, \rho, F_1)$ be [[Fredholm modules|Y30V3RBNGV]] over $A$ of the same parity.
They are *operator homotopic* if there is a norm-continuous path $(F_t)_{t \in [0, 1]}$ such that each $(H, \rho, F_t)$ is a Fredholm module of that parity.
*Equivalence* of Fredholm modules is the equivalence relation generated by unitary equivalence, operator homotopy, and addition of degenerate modules.
The *K-homology* group $K^0(A)$ is the set of equivalence classes of even Fredholm modules over $A$.
The group $K^1(A)$ is the set of equivalence classes of odd Fredholm modules over $A$.
In both, the addition is the direct sum of Fredholm modules.
For a compact metric space $X$, the K-homology groups of $X$ are $K_i(X) = K^i(C(X))$ for $i = 0, 1$.

## Remarks

With the direct sum, $K^0(A)$ and $K^1(A)$ are abelian groups, and degenerate modules represent $0$.
These groups are naturally isomorphic to the Kasparov groups $KK(A, \C)$ and $KK^1(A, \C)$ of [[KK-theory|1W9Q7W378J]].
Example: for $A = \C$, an even Fredholm module is essentially a Fredholm operator from the even to the odd part of $H$.
Its class is its index, so $K^0(\C) \cong \Z$; also $K^1(\C) = 0$.
Example: for $A = C(\mathbb{T})$, the odd module given by the Hardy space projection generates $K^1(C(\mathbb{T})) \cong \Z$.
Non-example: a triple $(H, \rho, F)$ with $[F, \rho(a)]$ not compact for some $a$ is not a Fredholm module and defines no class.
