## Statement

Let $n, m, p \ge 1$, and let $A$, $B$, $C$ be real or complex matrices of sizes $n \times n$, $n \times m$ and $p \times n$.
Let $I$ be the $n \times n$ identity matrix.

1. The system $x' = Ax + Bu$ is [[controllable|A9WFM77Z4P]] if and only if the $n \times (n + m)$ matrix $\begin{pmatrix} \lambda I - A & B \end{pmatrix}$ has [[rank|0ZC9YTXZRY]] $n$ for every $\lambda \in \C$.
2. The system $x' = Ax$, $y = Cx$ is [[observable|C4HN3EAQWM]] if and only if the $(n + p) \times n$ matrix $\begin{pmatrix} \lambda I - A \\ C \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $n$ for every $\lambda \in \C$.

In both parts it suffices to check the [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]] $\lambda$ of $A$, since for other $\lambda$ the block $\lambda I - A$ is already invertible.

## Remarks

Proof of part 1: if the rank drops at $\lambda$, there is a row vector $w \ne 0$ with $w A = \lambda w$ and $w B = 0$.
Then $w A^k B = \lambda^k w B = 0$ for all $k$, and the [[Kalman rank condition|9NSCZ2KBEB]] fails.
Conversely, if the Kalman matrix has rank less than $n$, the row vectors $w$ with $w A^k B = 0$ for $k = 0, \ldots, n - 1$ form a nonzero space.
By the [[Cayley–Hamilton theorem|0F765KFC2J]], each such $w$ satisfies $w A^k B = 0$ for all $k \ge 0$, so this space is invariant under right multiplication by $A$.
A nonzero invariant space contains a left eigenvector $w$ of $A$, and it satisfies $w B = 0$, so the rank drops at its eigenvalue.
Part 2 is part 1 for the transposed pair $(A^T, C^T)$.
Example: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ give, at $\lambda = 2$, the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ of rank $1$, so the system is not controllable: the second coordinate obeys $x_2' = 2 x_2$ whatever the input.
The test must use complex $\lambda$ even for real matrices, since the eigenvalues of $A$ may be complex.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A linear system splits into modes, one for each eigenvalue of its matrix.
The lemma says that the system is controllable exactly when the input reaches every mode, and observable exactly when the output sees every mode.
One checks this one eigenvalue at a time by a rank computation.
It also tells which modes are the bad ones, not only that something fails.

### L2 human

The lemma is also called the Popov–Belevitch–Hautus test, or PBH test.
It is often easier to use than the [[Kalman rank condition|9NSCZ2KBEB]], because it localizes the failure at an eigenvalue.
Restricting $\lambda$ to the closed right half-plane gives the standard tests for stabilizability and detectability.
It also shows quickly that state feedback does not change controllability, since $\begin{pmatrix} \lambda I - A - BK & B \end{pmatrix}$ is obtained from $\begin{pmatrix} \lambda I - A & B \end{pmatrix}$ by column operations.
