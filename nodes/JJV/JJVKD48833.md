## Statement

Let $n \ge 2$.
A [[Kakeya set|6PEN7AKC7M]] in $\R^n$ is a compact set $K \subseteq \R^n$ that contains a line segment of length $1$ in every direction.
Then every Kakeya set $K \subseteq \R^n$ has [[Hausdorff dimension|SSNFTHZ6YJ]] equal to $n$.

## Remarks

A Kakeya set can have [[Lebesgue measure|2Z2QVWZ262]] zero, as Besicovitch showed.
So the conjecture says that such a set, though it may be null, is still as large as possible in the sense of dimension.
A weaker form asks only that the lower [[Minkowski dimension|WYRKJ412FT]] of every Kakeya set in $\R^n$ is $n$.
Since the Hausdorff dimension never exceeds the lower Minkowski dimension, and both are at most $n$, the Hausdorff form implies the Minkowski form.

Status: the case $n = 2$ is a theorem of Roy Davies from 1971.
For general $n$, Thomas Wolff proved in 1995 that the Hausdorff dimension is at least $(n + 2)/2$.
In 2025 Hong Wang and Joshua Zahl posted a proof of the case $n = 3$.
The conjecture is open for $n \ge 4$.

The conjecture is closely tied to harmonic analysis.
The [[restriction conjecture|9CE11G48JY]] implies it.
Its analogue over finite fields was proved by Dvir, see [[Kakeya problem over finite fields|KSW18V47ZD]].
The question grew out of the [[Kakeya needle problem|WZ42B0SAVM]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a set in space that contains a needle of length one pointing in every possible direction.
Such a set can be so thin that its volume is zero.
The conjecture says that it can never be thin in a finer sense: it must still be fully $n$-dimensional.
In the plane this is known, in three-dimensional space a proof was announced in 2025, and in higher dimensions it is open.
