## Definition

Let $G$ be the [[transfer function|86JKV3TRFD]] of a single-input single-output [[linear time-invariant system|6J9MPVHSMF]].
Assume that $G$ is a proper [[rational function|0XDK9CGYKJ]] with real coefficients and no [[pole|1TE3QF8EYC]] on the imaginary axis.
The *frequency response* of the system is the function $\omega \mapsto G(i\omega)$ for $\omega \in \R$.
Its absolute value $\abs{G(i\omega)}$ is the *gain* at angular frequency $\omega$, and its argument $\arg G(i\omega)$ is the *phase* at $\omega$.

## Remarks

If all poles of $G$ have negative real part and the input is $u(t) = \sin(\omega t)$, then the output tends to $\abs{G(i\omega)} \sin(\omega t + \arg G(i\omega))$ as $t \to \infty$.
Since $G$ has real coefficients, $G(-i\omega) = \overline{G(i\omega)}$, so it is enough to know the frequency response for $\omega \ge 0$.

Example: for $G(s) = 1/(s + 1)$, $G(i\omega) = 1/(1 + i\omega)$, with gain $1/\sqrt{1 + \omega^2}$ and phase $-\arctan \omega$.

Non-example: $G(s) = 1/s$ has a pole at $0$, so $G(i\omega)$ is not defined at $\omega = 0$.
Its steady-state interpretation also fails: the input $\sin t$ gives the output $1 - \cos t$, which is not a sinusoid around $0$.
