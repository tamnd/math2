## Definition

Consider steady, frictionless, horizontal flow of a fluid of density $\rho > 0$ along curved streamlines that are parallel to the isobars.
Use natural coordinates: the speed is $V \ge 0$ and the unit normal $\mathbf{n}$ points to the left of the flow.
Let $R$ be the signed radius of curvature, positive when the centre of curvature lies to the left.
The speed $V$ is a *cyclostrophic wind* if
$$\frac{V^2}{R} = -\frac{1}{\rho} \frac{\partial p}{\partial n},$$
where $p$ is the pressure.

## Remarks

This is the [[gradient wind|C0HR875S0K]] balance without the Coriolis term, which is justified when the [[Rossby number|SCYK4FMY59]] $V / (\abs{f} \abs{R})$ is large.
The solution is $V = \sqrt{-(R / \rho) \, \partial p / \partial n}$.
It is real only if $R \, \partial p / \partial n < 0$, that is, when the pressure is lowest at the centre of curvature.
So a cyclostrophic vortex always has a low at its centre, and it can turn in either direction.

Example: for a tornado with $R = 100$ m and $V = 50$ m/s, the required pressure gradient is $\rho V^2 / R \approx 1.2 \cdot 2500 / 100 = 30$ Pa/m.
With $f = 10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$, the Rossby number is $50 / (10^{-4} \cdot 100) = 5000$, so neglecting the Coriolis term is justified.

Non-example: a high-pressure centre cannot be in cyclostrophic balance, since then $R \, \partial p / \partial n > 0$ and the equation has no real solution.
