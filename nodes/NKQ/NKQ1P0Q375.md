## Statement

Consider the zero-dimensional [[energy balance model|M182YC20JJ]]
$$C \frac{dT}{dt} = Q \big( 1 - \alpha(T) \big) - \varepsilon \sigma T^4, \qquad T > 0,$$
with constants $C, \varepsilon, \sigma > 0$ and solar input $Q > 0$.
The albedo $\alpha \colon (0, \infty) \to [0, a]$ is continuous, with a constant $a < 1$, and it models the ice–albedo feedback.
Let $G(T) = \varepsilon \sigma T^4 / \big( 1 - \alpha(T) \big)$.
Assume $G$ is continuously differentiable, and let $T_1 < T_2$ with $G(T_2) < G(T_1)$.
Assume $G$ is strictly increasing on $(0, T_1]$ and on $[T_2, \infty)$, and strictly decreasing on $[T_1, T_2]$.
Then:

1. for $Q < G(T_2)$ there is a single [[equilibrium|0N8NGKDZDZ]], it lies below $T_1$ (a cold, ice-covered state), and it is stable;
2. for $G(T_2) < Q < G(T_1)$ there are three equilibria: a stable one below $T_1$, an unstable one in $(T_1, T_2)$, and a stable one above $T_2$ (a warm state);
3. for $Q > G(T_1)$ there is a single equilibrium, above $T_2$, and it is stable;
4. at $Q = G(T_2)$ and $Q = G(T_1)$ a stable equilibrium and the unstable one merge and disappear, in a [[saddle-node bifurcation|MAP5J6HK0T]] when $G''$ does not vanish there.

In particular, if $Q$ decreases slowly below $G(T_2)$, the warm state is lost and the temperature falls to the cold state.
It returns to the warm state only when $Q$ rises above $G(T_1)$.

## Remarks

The right side equals $\big( 1 - \alpha(T) \big) \big( Q - G(T) \big)$, and $1 - \alpha > 0$.
So the equilibria are the solutions of $G(T) = Q$, and $T$ increases exactly when $G(T) < Q$.
An equilibrium where $G$ is increasing attracts nearby states, and one where $G$ is decreasing repels them.
Counting the solutions of $G(T) = Q$ on the three monotone pieces gives clauses 1 to 3.

Example: take a ramp albedo, equal to $\alpha_i$ for $T \le T_i$, to $\alpha_w < \alpha_i$ for $T \ge T_w$, and linear in between.
It gives such a $G$ when the jump $\alpha_i - \alpha_w$ is large compared with the ramp width $T_w - T_i$.
The [[ice–albedo feedback|GEQJK8XJ4C]] is what makes $G$ decrease on the ramp.
Without the feedback, with $\alpha$ constant, $G$ is strictly increasing, and there is exactly one equilibrium for each $Q$: no bistability and no snowball transition.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Earth could have two stable climates for the same sunshine: a warm one like today, and a frozen one where ice covers the whole planet.
A frozen Earth reflects so much sunlight that it stays frozen.
If the sunshine slowly weakens, the warm climate eventually stops existing, and the planet tips into the frozen state.
To thaw, the sunshine must rise well above the level at which the planet froze.
This lag is called hysteresis.

### L2 human

The statement is a [[Lyapunov stability|0F2SYCKDT2]] and bifurcation analysis of a scalar equation, read off from the graph of $G$.
The same fold appears in the one-dimensional [[Budyko–Sellers model|GEQJK8XJ4C]].
There the stable branch of states with a partial ice cap meets an unstable branch with larger ice caps in a saddle-node bifurcation.
Geological evidence suggests that the Earth went through near-global glaciations in the Neoproterozoic.
The model shows why the escape from such a state needs a large forcing, such as a strong rise of carbon dioxide from volcanoes.
