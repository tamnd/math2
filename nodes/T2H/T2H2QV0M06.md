## Statement

Row and column operations together bring every matrix to an identity block padded with zeros, and the size of that block, the rank, is the only thing that survives.
Let $F$ be a [[field|0QA8CHH8MG]] and let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over $F$ of [[rank|0EWQEYRVA8]] $r$.

1. (Rank normal form.) There are [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]] matrices $P$ of size $m \times m$ and $Q$ of size $n \times n$ such that
$$PAQ = N_r = \begin{pmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
where the zero blocks have sizes $r \times (n - r)$, $(m - r) \times r$ and $(m - r) \times (n - r)$.
Also, $P$ and $Q$ can be chosen as products of elementary matrices.
2. (Classification.) Two $m \times n$ matrices over $F$ are [[equivalent|0P21AVFFSP]] if and only if they have the same rank.

## Remarks

### The theorem at work

Take $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$, of rank $1$.
The row operations $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$, then the column operation $C_2 \to C_2 - 2C_1$, give
$$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ -3 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad PA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Check: $PAQ = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 + 2 \\ 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = N_1$.
The [[worked $2 \times 3$ example|RX693922PV]] also needs a column exchange.

### Why the hypotheses are needed

The matrices must have the same size, since equivalence is only defined for matrices of one size.
$F$ must be a field.
Over $\mathbb{Z}$ the matrices $(2)$ and $(1)$ both have rank $1$, but $(2) = p\,(1)\,q$ with units $p, q = \pm 1$ is impossible.
Over a principal ideal domain the classification needs the Smith normal form.

### What it gives you

Row rank equals column rank: $A^{\mathsf{T}} = Q^{\mathsf{T}} N_r^{\mathsf{T}} P^{\mathsf{T}}$ with invertible outer factors, and $N_r^{\mathsf{T}}$ has rank $r$, so $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ has rank $r$.
A matrix of rank $r$ is a sum of $r$ matrices of rank one, because $N_r = e_1 e_1^{\mathsf{T}} + \dots + e_r e_r^{\mathsf{T}}$ and $A = P^{-1} N_r Q^{-1}$.
Each matrix of rank $r$ also factors as $A = BC$ with $B$ of size $m \times r$ and $C$ of size $r \times n$, both of rank $r$, which is a full rank factorization.
A square matrix is invertible if and only if its normal form is $I_n$.

### Converse and generalizations

The normal form $N_r$ of $A$ is unique, because $r$ is determined by $A$, but $P$ and $Q$ are far from unique.
For a linear map $T \colon V \to W$ of rank $r$ between finite-dimensional spaces, there are bases of $V$ and $W$ in which the matrix of $T$ is $N_r$.
For similarity of square matrices the classification is much finer, and it is given by the rational canonical form, or by the Jordan normal form over an algebraically closed field.

## Explanations

### L1 human

With row operations and column operations together, every matrix can be brought to a very simple form.
It has $1$s on the diagonal in the first $r$ places and $0$s everywhere else.
The number $r$ is the rank, and it cannot change under these operations.

### L2 human

To find $P$ and $Q$, row reduce $A$ to reduced row echelon form, then move the pivot columns to the front and clear the other columns with column replacements.
Record the row operations in $P$ and the column operations in $Q$, in the order you use them.
A trap: the row operations multiply on the left, so $P = E_k \cdots E_1$ with the last operation first, while the column operations multiply on the right, so $Q = F_1 \cdots F_l$ with the first operation first.

### L3 human

The theorem says that $\mathrm{GL}_m(F) \times \mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ has exactly $\min(m, n) + 1$ orbits on the $m \times n$ matrices.
This is the simplest case of a classification of matrices up to a group action.
For similarity, congruence and unitary equivalence, the analogous problems lead to the canonical forms of later sub-areas.
