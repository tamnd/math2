## Definition

Let $R$ be a [[ring|0B1261B5ED]] with identity and let $I$ be a two-sided [[ideal|096Z9MP2ME]] of $R$.

1. The *double* of $R$ along $I$ is the ring $D(R, I) = \set{(a, b) \in R \times R : a - b \in I}$, and $p_1 \colon D(R, I) \to R$ is the first projection.
The *relative group* $K_0(R, I)$ is the kernel of the map $K_0(D(R, I)) \to K_0(R)$ induced by $p_1$, where $K_0$ is [[K0 of a ring|Q14JS2MA1C]].
2. Let $GL(R)$ be the stable [[general linear group|0FCHE902D7]] and let $GL(I)$ be the kernel of the reduction map $GL(R) \to GL(R/I)$ to the [[quotient ring|0076HWHGHQ]].
Let $E(R, I)$ be the smallest normal subgroup of the [[elementary subgroup|E5JXB0E324]] $E(R)$ that contains every elementary matrix $e_{ij}(x)$ with $i \ne j$ and $x \in I$.
The *relative group* $K_1(R, I)$ is the quotient $GL(I)/E(R, I)$.

## Remarks

The relative Whitehead lemma says that $E(R, I)$ is a normal subgroup of $GL(R)$ and contains the commutator subgroup of $GL(I)$, so $K_1(R, I)$ is an abelian group.
There is an exact sequence
$$K_1(R, I) \to K_1(R) \to K_1(R/I) \to K_0(R, I) \to K_0(R) \to K_0(R/I),$$
where $K_1$ is [[K1 of a ring|SXVNJY4XFY]].
With Milnor's relative $K_2$ or Quillen's higher groups, the sequence continues to the left.

Example: take $I = R$.
Then $R/I = 0$, so $GL(I) = GL(R)$ and $E(R, I) = E(R)$, which gives $K_1(R, R) = K_1(R)$.
Also $D(R, R) = R \times R$, and $K_0(R \times R) = K_0(R) \oplus K_0(R)$, so $K_0(R, R)$ is the second copy of $K_0(R)$.

Non-example: the subgroup generated by the matrices $e_{ij}(x)$ with $x \in I$ alone, without taking the normal closure in $E(R)$, can be strictly smaller than $E(R, I)$.
So it cannot replace $E(R, I)$ in the definition of $K_1(R, I)$.

The group $K_0(R, I)$ depends only on $I$ viewed as a ring without identity; this is excision for $K_0$.
For $K_1$ the analogous statement fails in general.
