## Summary

A heat engine is a system that runs through a [[thermodynamic cycle|AGRGCDWQF8]] and turns part of the [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] it takes in into [[work|S8H21RWQ86]].
It takes heat from a hot source and gives some heat to a cold sink.
The [[second law of thermodynamics|T11MGWEKJ3]] forbids an engine that turns all the heat it absorbs into work.
How good an engine is is measured by its [[thermal efficiency|Z686C32X8S]], which [[Carnot's theorem|4Y41F2C3P9]] bounds by $1 - T_C/T_H$.
Steam engines, petrol and diesel engines and gas turbines are modelled as heat engines.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A heat engine turns heat into motion.
In a steam engine, burning coal boils water, the steam pushes a piston, and the used steam is cooled and condensed.
Some heat always has to be thrown away to the cold side; no engine can avoid this.
The bigger the gap between the hot and the cold temperatures, the larger the share of the heat that can become work.
