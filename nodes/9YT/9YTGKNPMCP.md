## Summary

A polytrope is a ball of gas in [[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]] whose pressure is a fixed power of its density, $P = K \rho^{1 + 1/n}$.
The number $n$ is the polytropic index.
For a polytrope the equations of stellar structure reduce to one equation, the [[Lane–Emden equation|ANRZZMH2Q0]].
So the whole star is described by a single function of one variable.
Polytropes are the simplest models of stars, white dwarfs and gaseous planets, and they give the first estimate of the [[Chandrasekhar limit|J8C4PN2R4H]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Real stars need complicated physics to say how pressure depends on density and temperature.
A polytrope cuts this short by assuming a simple power law between pressure and density.
Then gravity and pressure can be balanced with paper and pencil, or with one short computation.
For example, $n = 3/2$ describes a fully convective star or a cool white dwarf, and $n = 3$ is a fair first model of the Sun.
The higher the index, the more the mass is concentrated towards the centre.
