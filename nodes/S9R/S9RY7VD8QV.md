## Summary

The Henyey track is a part of the path of a young star in the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram.
The star follows it after it develops a radiative core and leaves its [[Hayashi track|GRQ41F2G55]].
Along it the star keeps contracting, its surface temperature rises and its luminosity changes little.
So the track runs almost horizontally to the left, towards the main sequence.
Stars of very low mass stay fully convective and do not follow a Henyey track.
It is named after Louis Henyey, whose group computed such contracting radiative models in the 1950s.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A young star first slides down the chart of brightness against temperature while it is stirred by convection all through.
Once its centre becomes calm and carries heat by radiation, its path bends.
It keeps shrinking, but now its surface gets hotter while its brightness stays about the same, so it moves sideways on the chart.
The path ends when hydrogen starts burning in the core and the star joins the main sequence.
