## Summary

Hasselmann's kinetic equation describes how the [[spectrum of ocean waves|GYQCDP4VN8]] changes in time through weak nonlinear interactions among quartets of waves.
Klaus Hasselmann derived it in 1962 from the equations of surface gravity waves, under the assumption that the phases of the waves are random.
It has the form of a [[Boltzmann equation|MH6CSWD7F0]] in which waves play the role of particles and resonant four-wave interactions play the role of collisions.
Together with terms for wind input and wave breaking, it is the core of modern wave forecasting models such as WAM.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ocean waves of slightly different lengths and directions push on each other very weakly.
Over hundreds of wave periods these small pushes move energy from one part of the spectrum to another.
Hasselmann's equation keeps track of this slow exchange, in the same way that the Boltzmann equation keeps track of collisions in a gas.
One result is that energy moves toward longer waves, which is why the peak of a growing sea shifts to longer periods.
