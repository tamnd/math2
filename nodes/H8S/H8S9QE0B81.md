## Summary

Inverse Compton scattering is the scattering of low-energy photons by high-energy electrons, in which the photons gain energy and the electrons lose it.
For relativistic electrons with [[Lorentz factor|2E9AJVZS6E]] $\gamma$, a photon's energy is raised on average by a factor of order $\gamma^2$.
It turns radio or microwave photons into X-rays and gamma rays in many astrophysical sources.
It is also the main energy loss of relativistic electrons in strong radiation fields, alongside [[synchrotron radiation|TXJMA0HPG7]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In ordinary Compton scattering an energetic photon hits an electron at rest and loses energy.
In the inverse process the electron is the fast one, and it kicks the photon to higher energy, like a fast bat hitting a slow ball.
An electron with $\gamma = 1000$ can turn a photon of the cosmic microwave background into an X-ray photon.
The same effect, with hot but not relativistic electrons, changes the microwave background seen through clusters of galaxies.
