## Statement

Consider the Lur'e system $x' = Ax + bu$, $y = cx$, $u = -\psi(t, y)$ of the [[absolute stability problem|Z67CH2W4W6]], with $\psi$ in the sector $[\alpha, \beta]$, $\alpha < \beta$.
Assume that $(A, b)$ is [[controllable|A9WFM77Z4P]], $(A, c)$ is [[observable|C4HN3EAQWM]], and $A$ has no eigenvalue on the imaginary axis.
Let $G(s) = c(sI - A)^{-1} b$ be the [[transfer function|86JKV3TRFD]], and let $m$ be the number of eigenvalues of $A$ with positive real part.
When $\alpha \ne 0$, let $D(\alpha, \beta)$ be the closed disc in $\C$ whose diameter is the segment between $-1/\alpha$ and $-1/\beta$.
The system is absolutely stable for the sector $[\alpha, \beta]$ if one of the following holds.

1. $0 < \alpha < \beta$, the [[Nyquist plot|Z7SPHAWMDX]] $\omega \mapsto G(i\omega)$ does not meet $D(\alpha, \beta)$, and it winds around $D(\alpha, \beta)$ exactly $m$ times counterclockwise.
2. $0 = \alpha < \beta$, $A$ is [[Hurwitz-stable|9A3TBBHRFA]], and $\operatorname{Re} G(i\omega) > -1/\beta$ for all $\omega \in \R$.
3. $\alpha < 0 < \beta$, $A$ is Hurwitz-stable, and $G(i\omega)$ lies in the interior of $D(\alpha, \beta)$ for all $\omega \in \R$.

## Remarks

The proof applies a loop transformation that moves the sector to $[0, \infty)$; the frequency condition then says that a transformed transfer function is strictly positive real.
The [[Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma|4NJC7AGT5N]] then gives a quadratic Lyapunov function $x^T P x$ that works for every nonlinearity in the sector, even time-varying ones.
The criterion generalizes the [[Nyquist stability criterion|EZ1HMS875F]]: as $\beta$ decreases to $\alpha = k$, the disc shrinks to the point $-1/k$, which is the critical point for the linear gain $k$.

Example: let $G(s) = 1/(s + 1)$, so $A = -1$ is Hurwitz.
Since $\operatorname{Re} G(i\omega) = 1/(1 + \omega^2) > 0$, case 2 holds for every $\beta > 0$, so the system is absolutely stable for every sector $[0, \beta]$.

The conditions are sufficient but not necessary: a system can be absolutely stable for a sector even though the Nyquist plot meets the disc.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Consider a linear system in a loop with a nonlinearity whose graph stays between two lines of slopes $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
The loop is stable if the frequency response of the linear part stays away from a certain disc.
The disc is built from $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and the test can be checked on a plot.
It guarantees stability for every nonlinearity in the range, not only for one.

### L2 human

For a single gain $k$, the Nyquist criterion asks the plot to avoid the point $-1/k$ in the right way.
For an uncertain gain between $\alpha$ and $\beta$ that may even change with time, the circle criterion asks it to avoid the whole disc spanned by $-1/\alpha$ and $-1/\beta$.
For a symmetric sector $[-\beta, \beta]$, case 3 says $\abs{G(i\omega)} < 1/\beta$ for all $\omega$, which is the condition of the [[small-gain theorem|1CF728E75M]].
When the nonlinearity does not depend on time, the Popov criterion gives a less restrictive test for sectors of the form $[0, \beta]$.
