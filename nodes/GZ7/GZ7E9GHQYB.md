## Summary

An S-wave is a shear elastic wave in a solid, in which the particles move across the direction in which the wave travels.
In a homogeneous isotropic solid it travels at the speed $c_S = \sqrt{\mu/\rho}$, which is smaller than the speed of [[P-waves|EEPJJHC5JZ]].
In seismology S stands for secondary, because these waves arrive after the P-waves.
Fluids have no shear stiffness, so S-waves cannot cross them; this is how seismologists learned that the outer core of the Earth is liquid.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Shake one end of a rope up and down and a wave runs along it while each bit of rope moves only up and down.
An S-wave in rock is like that: the rock is sheared sideways while the wave moves forward.
It needs a material that resists being sheared, so it travels in solids but not in water or air.
In typical rock it moves at roughly half to two thirds of the speed of a P-wave.
