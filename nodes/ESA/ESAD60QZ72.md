## Summary

Extended irreversible thermodynamics is a theory of [[non-equilibrium thermodynamics|WC6CK8PXPX]] in which the fluxes, such as the heat flux, are independent state variables.
The entropy then depends on the fluxes as well as on the classical variables, and the fluxes obey their own evolution equations with relaxation times.
This leads to hyperbolic transport equations, such as the [[Cattaneo–Vernotte equation|FFD8ZJBMMD]] for heat, with finite speeds of propagation.
It is used for fast processes and high frequencies, where classical theory based on local equilibrium fails.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The classical heat equation says that a sudden heat pulse is felt, a tiny bit, everywhere at once.
This is unphysical, and it matters for very fast or very small processes.
Extended irreversible thermodynamics lets the heat flux take a short time to respond to a temperature gradient.
Heat then travels as a damped wave with a finite speed, as observed in [[second sound|SYW4JJJTW3]] in some materials at low temperature.
