## Summary

The grand canonical ensemble describes a system that exchanges both energy and particles with a large reservoir.
The temperature $T$ and the chemical potential $\mu$ are fixed, while the energy and the particle number fluctuate.
A microstate with energy $E$ and $N$ particles has weight proportional to $e^{-(E - \mu N) / (k_B T)}$.
It is the natural ensemble for open systems, for quantum gases of bosons and fermions, and for the [[cluster expansion|XH2XEVGNSK]] of gases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a small imaginary box inside a large container of gas.
Molecules fly in and out of the box, so both its energy and its number of molecules change all the time.
The chemical potential $\mu$ measures how much the reservoir pushes particles into the box.
For a single site that is either empty or holds one particle of energy $\varepsilon$, the site is occupied with probability $1 / (e^{(\varepsilon - \mu) / (k_B T)} + 1)$.
This is the Fermi–Dirac occupation law.
