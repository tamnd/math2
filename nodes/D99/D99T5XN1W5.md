## Definition

Let $N$ be a finite set of $n$ voters, and let $v$ be a *simple game* on $N$.
That is, $v$ is a [[cooperative game|39HGM44CFZ]] with $v(S) \in \set{0, 1}$ for every $S \subseteq N$, $v(\emptyset) = 0$ and $v(N) = 1$.
It is also monotone: $v(S) \le v(T)$ whenever $S \subseteq T$.
A coalition $S$ is *winning* if $v(S) = 1$.
A voter $i$ is a *swing* in a winning coalition $S$ if $i \in S$ and $S \setminus \set{i}$ is not winning.
Let $\eta_i$ be the number of winning coalitions in which $i$ is a swing.
The *Banzhaf power index* of $i$ is
$$\beta_i = \frac{\eta_i}{\sum_{j \in N} \eta_j}.$$

## Remarks

Example: the weighted voting game $[3; 2, 1, 1]$, where voters $1, 2, 3$ have weights $2, 1, 1$ and a coalition wins when its total weight is at least $3$.
The winning coalitions are $\set{1, 2}$, $\set{1, 3}$ and $\set{1, 2, 3}$.
Voter $1$ is a swing in all three, voter $2$ only in $\set{1, 2}$, and voter $3$ only in $\set{1, 3}$.
So $\eta = (3, 1, 1)$ and $\beta = (3/5, 1/5, 1/5)$.
Non-example: in the coalition $\set{1, 2, 3}$, voter $2$ is not a swing, since $\set{1, 3}$ still has weight $3$ and wins.
Some authors use the unnormalised index $\eta_i / 2^{n-1}$, the probability that $i$ is decisive when every other voter votes yes or no with probability $1/2$ independently.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The index counts how often each voter can turn a win into a loss by walking away.
Dividing by the total count turns these numbers into shares of power that add up to $1$.
