## Summary

The Seidel aberrations are the five primary monochromatic aberrations of a rotationally symmetric optical system: spherical aberration, coma, astigmatism, field curvature and distortion.
They are the terms of lowest order in the expansion of the [[wave aberration|VG0GPBKP84]] that are not mere refocusing or tilt.
They describe the first errors beyond the [[paraxial approximation|N111BHFQ2H]] and are the starting point of classical lens design.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Paraxial optics assumes that every ray stays close to the axis.
When rays go through the edge of a lens or come from points far from the axis, the first errors appear, and they come in exactly five types.
Spherical aberration blurs even points on the axis; coma makes off-axis points look like small comets; astigmatism focuses lines in two directions at different depths.
Field curvature makes the sharp image of a flat object lie on a curved surface, and distortion bends straight lines into curves without blurring.
