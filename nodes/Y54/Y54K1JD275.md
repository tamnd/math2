## Definition

Let $R$ be a [[commutative ring|0310REJJY8]], and let $M$ and $N$ be [[modules|0NZ8JA25WK]] over $R$.
Let $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle \colon M \times N \to R$ be a perfect pairing, that is, an $R$-bilinear map with two properties.
The map $x \mapsto \langle x, \cdot \rangle$ is an isomorphism from $M$ onto $\Hom_R(N, R)$, and $y \mapsto \langle \cdot, y \rangle$ is an isomorphism from $N$ onto $\Hom_R(M, R)$.
Let $I$ be a set.
A *root pairing* indexed by $I$ is a pair of injective maps $i \mapsto \alpha_i$ from $I$ to $M$ and $i \mapsto \alpha_i^\vee$ from $I$ to $N$ such that:

1. $\langle \alpha_i, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle = 2$ for each $i \in I$;
2. for all $i, j \in I$ there is $k \in I$ with $s_i(\alpha_j) = \alpha_k$ and $s_i^\vee(\alpha_j^\vee) = \alpha_k^\vee$.

Here $s_i(x) = x - \langle x, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle \alpha_i$ for $x \in M$ and $s_i^\vee(y) = y - \langle \alpha_i, y \rangle \alpha_i^\vee$ for $y \in N$.
The $\alpha_i$ are the *roots* and the $\alpha_i^\vee$ are the *coroots*.

## Remarks

Example: a [[root system|AVVV93CASN]] $\Phi$ in a Euclidean space $E$ gives a root pairing with $R = \R$, $M = E$, $N = E^*$ and $I = \Phi$.
The coroot of $\alpha$ is the linear form $x \mapsto 2(x, \alpha)/(\alpha, \alpha)$, the [[coroot|XV6Q01FVAS]] of $\alpha$ seen in the [[dual space|07JBSZE0V4]].
Clause 1 holds since $2(\alpha, \alpha)/(\alpha, \alpha) = 2$, and clause 2 holds since $s_\alpha$ is an isometry that permutes $\Phi$.
Non-example: on $M = N = \Z$ with $\langle x, y \rangle = xy$, the root $1$ with coroot $1$ fails clause 1, since $\langle 1, 1 \rangle = 1$.
The map $s_i$ is a reflection: it fixes the kernel of $\langle \cdot, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle$ and sends $\alpha_i$ to $-\alpha_i$.
When $R = \Z$, the modules $M$ and $N$ are free of finite rank and $I$ is finite, a root pairing is the same as a root datum $(M, \Phi, N, \Phi^\vee)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A root pairing lists roots on one side and coroots on the other side of a duality.
Each root and its coroot pair to $2$, and together they define a reflection.
The rule is that all these reflections permute the roots, and in the same way the coroots.
