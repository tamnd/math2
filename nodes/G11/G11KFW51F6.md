## Summary

The quantum mutual information of a bipartite quantum state measures the total correlation between its two parts.
It is built from the [[von Neumann entropy|H53TSRFWQ4]] of the state and of its two marginals.
The classical mutual information is built from Shannon entropies in the same way.
It counts classical correlations and [[entanglement|CHNXT8CCK7]] together.
It appears in channel capacities, in the Holevo bound and in the study of correlations in many-body systems.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two systems $A$ and $B$ may share a joint quantum state.
The mutual information asks how much the uncertainty about the whole is less than the sum of the uncertainties about the parts.
If the parts are independent, nothing is gained and the mutual information is $0$.
For a maximally entangled pair of qubits, each qubit alone looks completely random, but the pair is in a pure state.
Then the mutual information is $2 \log 2$, twice the largest value a classical pair of bits can reach.
