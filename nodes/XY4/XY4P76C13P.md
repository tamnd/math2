## Definition

Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the set of [[equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]] [[states|TD4C13V3J6]] of a thermodynamic system $\Sigma$.
A process of $\Sigma$ is *quasistatic* if $\Sigma$ is in an equilibrium state at every time of the process.
Such a process is described by a continuous curve $\gamma \colon [0, 1] \to \mathcal{S}$.

## Remarks

Expanding a gas in a cylinder very slowly against a piston, at each moment balancing the gas pressure, is quasistatic.
The free expansion of a gas into a vacuum, after a wall is removed, is not quasistatic.
During it, the gas is not in an equilibrium state, so it has no well-defined pressure.
A quasistatic process need not be reversible: slow expansion against a piston with friction is quasistatic but dissipates energy.
