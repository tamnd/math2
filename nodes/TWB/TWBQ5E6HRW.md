## Summary

The Atkinson index is a family of measures of income inequality indexed by a parameter $\varepsilon \ge 0$ of inequality aversion.
It compares the mean income with the equally distributed equivalent income.
This is the income which, given to everyone, yields the same social welfare as the actual distribution.
The index is the fraction of total income that society would give up to reach equality.
Larger $\varepsilon$ gives more weight to the poorest incomes.
It was introduced by Anthony Atkinson in 1970.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose society cares more about a dollar going to a poor person than to a rich one.
Then an unequal distribution is worth less to society than the same total shared equally.
The Atkinson index asks how much of the total we could throw away and still be as well off, if the rest were shared equally.
For two people with incomes $1$ and $4$ and $\varepsilon = 1$, an equal income of $2$ each is just as good, so $20\%$ of the mean $2.5$ is "wasted" by inequality.
