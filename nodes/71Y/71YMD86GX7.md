## Definition

Consider a static, spherically symmetric, non-rotating star of mass $M$ and radius $R$.
Let $r \in [0, R]$ be the distance from the centre.
Let $m(r)$ be the mass inside radius $r$, $\rho(r)$ the density, $P(r)$ the pressure and $T(r)$ the temperature.
Let $L(r)$ be the energy per unit time that flows outward through the sphere of radius $r$.
Let the material functions $P(\rho, T)$ (equation of state), $\kappa(\rho, T)$ (opacity) and $\varepsilon(\rho, T)$ (energy generation per unit mass) be given for a fixed chemical composition.
Let $G$ be the gravitational constant, $c$ the speed of light and $a$ the radiation constant.
A *stellar structure model* is a solution of the system

1. $\dfrac{dm}{dr} = 4 \pi r^2 \rho$ (mass conservation);
2. $\dfrac{dP}{dr} = -\dfrac{G m \rho}{r^2}$ ([[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]]);
3. $\dfrac{dL}{dr} = 4 \pi r^2 \rho \varepsilon$ (energy conservation);
4. $\dfrac{dT}{dr} = -\dfrac{3 \kappa \rho L}{16 \pi a c r^2 T^3}$ where energy is carried by radiation, and $\dfrac{dT}{dr} = \left(1 - \dfrac{1}{\gamma}\right) \dfrac{T}{P} \dfrac{dP}{dr}$ where it is carried by convection, with $\gamma$ the adiabatic index;

with the boundary conditions $m(0) = 0$, $L(0) = 0$, $m(R) = M$, and $P(R) = 0$, $T(R) = 0$ in the simplest form.

## Remarks

Example: the uniform-density model with $\rho = \rho_0$ satisfies clauses 1 and 2 with $m(r) = \frac{4}{3} \pi \rho_0 r^3$ and $P(r) = \frac{2}{3} \pi G \rho_0^2 (R^2 - r^2)$.
Indeed, $dP/dr = -\frac{4}{3} \pi G \rho_0^2 r = -G m \rho_0 / r^2$, and $P(R) = 0$.
Non-example: a ball with uniform density and uniform pressure $P_0 > 0$ fails clause 2, since $dP/dr = 0$ while $G m \rho / r^2 > 0$ for $r > 0$.
Which form of clause 4 applies at a given radius is decided by the [[Schwarzschild criterion for convection|85GWQPTJ3W]].
More realistic models replace $P(R) = T(R) = 0$ by conditions from a stellar atmosphere, and add time derivatives to follow the evolution of the composition.
Clause 4 for radiation is often written with $\sigma = ac/4$, the Stefan–Boltzmann constant, as $dT/dr = -3 \kappa \rho L / (64 \pi \sigma r^2 T^3)$.
