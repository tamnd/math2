## Summary

Dual control theory studies the control of systems whose state or parameters are uncertain, where each control action has two roles at once.
It steers the system toward the goal, and it also produces information that reduces the uncertainty for later decisions.
The theory was introduced by A. A. Feldbaum.
It explains why the optimal [[stochastic control|B24T6DQCDR]] of an uncertain system usually differs from acting on the current best estimate.
It is a theoretical basis of [[adaptive control|F2RD2ZTZ8V]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine driving an unfamiliar car on ice.
You want to stay on the road, but you also want to learn how the car reacts, and a small test movement of the wheel teaches you a lot.
Steering only by your current best guess of the car's behaviour ignores the value of learning; steering only to learn wastes effort.
Dual control looks for the best balance between these two goals, caution and probing.
For example, a controller for a system $x_{k+1} = x_k + b u_k$ with unknown gain $b$ may deliberately use a nonzero input at first, because a zero input would never reveal $b$.
