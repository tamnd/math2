## Summary

The cosmological principle is the assumption that, on large enough scales, the universe looks the same at every place and in every direction.
In general relativity it says that [[spacetime|63ZKC7AFX2]] can be sliced into spatial sections that are homogeneous and isotropic.
This symmetry assumption reduces the Einstein equations for the whole universe to a few ordinary differential equations.
It is the starting point of the [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric|ECJMS4VDTF]] and of standard cosmology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a huge foam of galaxies.
Up close it is lumpy, but if you average over very large boxes, every box looks about the same as every other.
The cosmological principle says that this is exactly true at a fixed cosmic time: no place and no direction is special.
For example, the faint microwave radiation from the early universe has almost the same temperature in every direction of the sky, which supports isotropy.
