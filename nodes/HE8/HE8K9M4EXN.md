## Summary

A subgame perfect equilibrium is a strategy profile of an extensive-form game that is a Nash equilibrium in every subgame.
It rules out equilibria that rest on threats which a player would not carry out when the time comes.
Reinhard Selten introduced it in 1965.
In finite games of perfect information it is found by backward induction.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Nash equilibrium can rely on a bluff: "If you enter my market, I will start a ruinous price war."
If the price war would also hurt the threatening firm, the threat is not believable.
Subgame perfection asks that plans be sensible at every point of the game, even at points that are never reached.
So the bluff is ruled out, and the entrant enters.
