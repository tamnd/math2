## Summary

The Weil representation is a unitary representation of the [[metaplectic group|8B0JM2YDE2]], a two-fold cover of the [[symplectic group|X12FS2JAP0]] over a local field.
Over the real numbers it is the [[oscillator representation|CCR7BAQ5N5]].
It comes from the [[Stone–von Neumann theorem|Z877XDBMQ3]].
Twisting the Schrödinger representation of the [[Heisenberg group|ZZYCV5EX9J]] by a symplectic map gives an equivalent representation.
The intertwining operators then form the Weil representation.
It is the source of theta functions and of the theta correspondence in number theory.
In physics it describes the symmetries of the quantum harmonic oscillator.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In quantum mechanics, position and momentum act on wave functions in $L^2(\R^n)$.
Consider a linear change of coordinates in phase space that keeps the symplectic form, such as a rotation mixing position and momentum.
It can be carried out by a unitary operator on $L^2(\R^n)$.
The Fourier transform is the operator for the quarter turn that sends position to momentum.
These operators can be normalized only up to a sign, so they form a representation of a double cover of the symplectic group rather than of the group itself.
That representation is the Weil representation.
