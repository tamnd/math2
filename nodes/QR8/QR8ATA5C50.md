## Summary

Teleparallelism describes gravity by a connection that has zero curvature but nonzero [[torsion|JZNQAN00ZJ]], built from a field of orthonormal frames.
Because the curvature vanishes, vectors at distant points can be compared without ambiguity, which gives the name, "parallelism at a distance".
Einstein studied it from 1928 in an attempt at a unified field theory.
Its main modern form, the teleparallel equivalent of general relativity, has the same field equations as general relativity.
Modified versions such as $f(T)$ gravity are [[alternatives to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In general relativity gravity shows up as curvature: carrying an arrow around a loop turns it.
Teleparallel gravity uses a different rule for carrying arrows, under which they never turn, so there is no curvature.
Instead the gravitational field shows up as torsion, which measures how small parallelograms fail to close.
The two descriptions can give exactly the same predictions, so they are two languages for the same physics.
