## Summary

The kappa mechanism is the main process that drives [[stellar pulsation|QW9QPV0HHG]] in classical pulsating stars such as Cepheids and RR Lyrae stars.
The letter $\kappa$ stands for the opacity of the stellar gas.
In a layer where the opacity rises when the gas is compressed, radiation is trapped during compression and released during expansion.
The layer then works like a heat engine and feeds energy into the oscillation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a lid on a pot of boiling water that traps steam, pops up, lets the steam out and falls back.
In most of a star, compressed gas becomes more transparent, which damps any oscillation.
In a layer where helium is partly ionised, compressed gas becomes more opaque instead.
That layer holds back heat when squeezed and pushes outward harder on the next expansion, so the oscillation grows.
This explains why stars in a narrow strip of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram, the instability strip, pulsate.
