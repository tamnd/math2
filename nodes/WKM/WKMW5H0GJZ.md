## Statement

Consider a relativistic quantum theory in four spacetime dimensions with massive particles, satisfying the hypotheses of the [[Coleman–Mandula theorem|YKPXT94HWP]].
Allow the symmetries of the [[S-matrix|YDC9XBJDE1]] to be generated by a [[Lie superalgebra|7CZ4Q5H78N]] $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_0 \oplus \mathfrak{g}_1$ instead of a Lie algebra.
Then the following hold.

1. The even part $\mathfrak{g}_0$ is the direct sum of the Lie algebra of the [[Poincaré group|8ZQ9TA4Q2Q]] and an internal symmetry algebra.
2. The odd part $\mathfrak{g}_1$ is spanned by $N$ spinor charges $Q^A_\alpha$ and their adjoints $\bar Q_{\dot\alpha A}$, with $A = 1, \dots, N$.
   The $Q^A_\alpha$ transform as $(\tfrac12, 0)$ and the $\bar Q_{\dot\alpha A}$ as $(0, \tfrac12)$ under the Lorentz group.
3. With a standard normalization, $\{Q^A_\alpha, \bar Q_{\dot\beta B}\} = 2 \delta^A_B \sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta} P_\mu$, where $P_\mu$ is the momentum and $\sigma^\mu = (1, \sigma^1, \sigma^2, \sigma^3)$.
4. $\{Q^A_\alpha, Q^B_\beta\} = \epsilon_{\alpha\beta} Z^{AB}$, where the central charges $Z^{AB} = -Z^{BA}$ commute with all of $\mathfrak{g}$.
5. The internal symmetries that act nontrivially on the supercharges form a subalgebra of $\mathfrak{u}(N)$, the R-symmetry.

## Remarks

The proof applies the Coleman–Mandula theorem to the even part.
Then it classifies the representations of the Lorentz group in which odd generators can transform: higher spinor representations would give charges whose anticommutators violate the Coleman–Mandula restrictions.
The anticommutator of $Q$ and $\bar Q$ is a positive operator, which is why the energy is nonnegative in a supersymmetric theory.

Example: for $N = 1$ there are no central charges, and the superalgebra is the $N = 1$ super-Poincaré algebra.
If all particles are massless, the symmetry superalgebra can be larger, a superconformal algebra.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Coleman–Mandula theorem forbids mixing spacetime symmetry with internal symmetry when symmetries are generated by ordinary commutators.
If some generators are allowed to anticommute, there is exactly one way out: supersymmetry.
This theorem lists all the possible supersymmetry algebras.

### L2 human

The theorem is why four-dimensional supersymmetry is classified by the number $N$ of supercharges.
Theories with $N = 1$, $N = 2$ and $N = 4$ supersymmetry are studied a lot, and central charges appear in the BPS bounds of $N = 2$ theories such as [[Seiberg–Witten theory|BKKX6C8HR9]].
The theorem relies on the S-matrix, so it says nothing directly about theories without massive particles and nontrivial scattering.
