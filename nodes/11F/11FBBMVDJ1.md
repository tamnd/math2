## Definition

Let two phases $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of a pure substance coexist at temperature $T$ and pressure $p$, in a first-order [[phase transition|6WJ0NADBT4]].
Let $h_\alpha, h_\beta$ be their molar [[enthalpies|722RZK72P5]] and $s_\alpha, s_\beta$ their molar [[entropies|SPY89W0RJ7]] at $(T, p)$.
Let one mole change from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ reversibly at constant $T$ and $p$.
The molar *latent heat* $L$ of the transition is the [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] absorbed in this change.
It is given by
$$L = h_\beta - h_\alpha = T (s_\beta - s_\alpha).$$
Per unit mass instead of per mole, it is the specific latent heat.

## Remarks

The first equality holds because the heat absorbed in an [[isobaric process|WYAY33ZVYQ]] is the change of enthalpy.
The second holds because the [[chemical potentials|WVH4PVW64P]] $\mu = h - Ts$ of the two phases are equal at coexistence.
For water boiling at $101.325\,\mathrm{kPa}$, the specific latent heat of vaporization is about $2.26\,\mathrm{MJ/kg}$.
A non-example: the heat needed to warm liquid water from $20$ to $30$ degrees Celsius is not a latent heat.
The temperature changes and no change of phase occurs; such heat is called sensible heat.
The latent heat from $\beta$ to $\alpha$ is $-L$: heat is released when steam condenses or water freezes.
