## Summary

The particle horizon of an observer bounds the part of the universe from which light could have reached the observer since the beginning.
It is a kind of [[cosmological horizon|CZ5PH864FG]], and it is the largest distance from which the observer can have received any signal so far.
It grows with time.
It exists exactly when the integral of $dt/a$ from the beginning converges, as in models with a Big Bang and decelerating early expansion.
The size of the particle horizon at early times is at the heart of the horizon problem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Since the universe has a finite age and light has a finite speed, we can only have seen objects close enough for their light to have arrived by now.
The particle horizon is the edge of that observable region.
Because space expanded while the light travelled, its present distance is larger than the age of the universe times the speed of light.
In a universe filled with matter only, it is three times the age times the speed of light.
