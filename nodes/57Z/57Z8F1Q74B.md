## Example

The simplest *tight-binding model* describes an electron hopping between the sites of the chain $\Z$.
Let $\varepsilon_0 \in \R$ and $t > 0$, and define the operator $H$ on $\ell^2(\Z)$ by
$$(H\psi)(n) = \varepsilon_0 \psi(n) - t\big(\psi(n+1) + \psi(n-1)\big).$$
It is a bounded [[self-adjoint operator|0NEGN5CRSC]].
The Fourier series map $\psi \mapsto \hat\psi(k) = \sum_n \psi(n) e^{-ikn}$ from $\ell^2(\Z)$ onto $L^2([-\pi, \pi])$ turns $H$ into multiplication by
$$E(k) = \varepsilon_0 - 2t\cos k, \qquad k \in [-\pi, \pi].$$
Check: the shift $\psi(n) \mapsto \psi(n+1)$ becomes multiplication by $e^{ik}$, and the shift $\psi(n) \mapsto \psi(n-1)$ becomes multiplication by $e^{-ik}$, whose sum is $2\cos k$.
So the band structure is the single band $E(k)$ over the [[Brillouin zone|EKTERAYFJ2]] $[-\pi, \pi]$, and the spectrum of $H$ is the interval $[\varepsilon_0 - 2t, \varepsilon_0 + 2t]$.

On the square lattice $\Z^2$ with hopping $t$ to the four neighbours, the same computation gives $E(k) = \varepsilon_0 - 2t(\cos k_1 + \cos k_2)$ on $[-\pi, \pi]^2$.
Adding a quasi-periodic potential to the chain gives the [[almost Mathieu operator|D08SKTH2T2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the tight-binding picture each electron sits on an atom, and occasionally jumps to a neighbouring atom.
The jump amplitude $t$ is small when atoms are far apart.
The jumps spread a single atomic energy level $\varepsilon_0$ into a band of width $4t$ in one dimension.
A wave with wave number $k$ along the chain has energy $\varepsilon_0 - 2t\cos k$: lowest when all sites are in phase and highest when neighbours have opposite signs.
