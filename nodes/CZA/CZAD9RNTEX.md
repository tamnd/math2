## Summary

The theory of island biogeography, due to Robert MacArthur and Edward O. Wilson, explains the number of species on an island as a balance.
New species arrive by immigration from a mainland, and species already present go extinct.
The number of species settles at the value where the two rates are equal.
The theory predicts that large islands close to the mainland hold more species than small remote islands, and it is used in conservation planning.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of an island near a continent full of species.
When the island is empty, almost every arriving species is new, so the count of species grows quickly.
As the island fills up, fewer arrivals are new, and more of the resident species die out.
The count stops changing when as many species arrive as disappear.
For example, if $10$ new species arrive per century and $10$ resident species vanish per century, the island stays at its current number of species.
