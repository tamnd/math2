## Summary

Mechanism design studies how to build the rules of a game so that self-interested players, acting on private information, produce an outcome the designer wants.
It reverses the usual direction of [[game theory|J8A9GBFSTY]]: the desired outcome is fixed first, and the game is chosen to achieve it.
Auctions, voting rules, matching markets and taxation schemes are all mechanisms.
Central results include the revelation principle and the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves mechanism, as well as impossibility theorems that limit what any mechanism can do.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A seller wants to sell an item to the buyer who values it most, but only the buyers know their values.
She cannot simply ask, since buyers may lie to pay less.
Mechanism design looks for rules, such as "the highest bidder wins and pays the second highest bid", under which telling the truth is in each buyer's interest.
More generally it asks which goals can be reached when people act strategically, and how.
