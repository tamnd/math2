## Proof

Let $T$ be complete with infinite models in a countable signature.

### Saturated implies small

1. Let $M$ be countable and saturated. We show by induction on $n$ that every $p \in S_n(T)$ is realized in $M$.
2. For $n = 0$ there is nothing to prove. Let $p(\bar x, y) \in S_{n+1}(T)$, and let $\bar a$ in $M$ realize its restriction to $\bar x$.
3. Let $q(y) = \set{\varphi(\bar a, y) : \varphi(\bar x, y) \in p}$. For $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k$ in $p$, the formula $\exists y \, \bigwedge_i \varphi_i$ is in $p$, so it holds of $\bar a$. So $q$ is a type over $\bar a$ that is finitely satisfiable in $M$.
4. By saturation some $b$ realizes $q$, and $(\bar a, b)$ realizes $p$.
5. $M$ has countably many $n$-tuples, so $S_n(T)$ is countable.

### Small implies a saturated model

1. If $T$ is small, then for a finite tuple $\bar a$ in a model, the $1$-types over $\bar a$ are countable, since each one gives a type of $T$ in one more variable.
2. Let $N$ be a countable model. It has countably many finite subsets, so countably many $1$-types over them.
3. Add a constant for each element of $N$ and a new constant $c_q$ for each such type $q$, and take the elementary diagram of $N$ with $q(c_q)$. Each finite part is satisfiable in $N$, so by [[compactness|1NR3HAWG9D]] and the [[Löwenheim-Skolem theorem|484M0TZ1S7]] there is a countable $N' \succeq N$ realizing every $q$.
4. Starting from a countable model $N_0$, repeat step 3 to get $N_0 \preceq N_1 \preceq N_2 \preceq \cdots$. By the [[elementary chain theorem|NHCRZ74JHH]], $M = \bigcup_k N_k$ is a countable elementary extension of each $N_k$.
5. A finite $A \subseteq M$ lies in some $N_k$, and a type over $A$ is realized in $N_{k+1} \preceq M$. So $M$ is saturated.

### Universality and uniqueness

1. Let $N$ be countable and $M$ countable saturated, with $N = \set{n_0, n_1, \dots}$. The empty map is [[partial elementary|XTNYY0WRQ5]], because $T$ is complete.
2. Given a partial elementary $f$ on $\set{n_0, \dots, n_{k-1}}$, the type of $n_k$ over these elements, moved by $f$, is a type over a finite subset of $M$. A realization $m$ gives a partial elementary extension with $n_k \mapsto m$.
3. The union of these maps is an elementary embedding $N \to M$.
4. If $N$ is also saturated, the same step works in the other direction, and the [[back-and-forth method|6F2DB4HTJR]] gives an isomorphism.

## Idea

Small means that the needed types can be listed in a countable sequence, so a countable chain of models can realize them all.
Conversely, a countable model that realizes everything shows that there was only a countable amount to realize.
Universality and uniqueness use saturation to take one more step in a forth or back-and-forth construction.

## Variants

The chain construction works in any regular uncountable $\kappa$ with $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$, by a chain of length $\kappa$.
For countable saturated models one may instead build the model directly by a Henkin construction that lists all types over finite sets of constants.
