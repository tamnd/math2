## Statement

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]] with [[Cartan subalgebra|BB098JPWGA]] $\mathfrak{h}$ and positive roots $\Phi^+$, and let $\rho = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \alpha$.
Let $(\cdot, \cdot)$ be the inner product on the real span of the roots induced by the Killing form.
Let $\lambda$ be a dominant integral weight, and let $L(\lambda)$ be the finite-dimensional irreducible module of [[highest weight|C5W8BE1DRY]] $\lambda$.
Then
$$\dim L(\lambda) = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \frac{(\lambda + \rho, \alpha)}{(\rho, \alpha)}.$$

## Remarks

The formula follows from the [[Weyl character formula|G2SA2YNA33]] by evaluating both sides at $t\rho$ and letting $t \to 0$; the denominator formula turns both alternating sums into products.
The quotient does not change if the inner product is replaced by any $W$-invariant inner product, since each factor is a ratio for one root.
Example: for $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\C)$ there is one positive root, and $\dim L(n) = (n + 1)/1 = n + 1$.
For $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\C)$ and $\lambda = a\omega_1 + b\omega_2$, the three positive roots give $\dim L(\lambda) = \frac{(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)}{2}$.
For $a = b = 1$ this is $8$, the dimension of the adjoint representation.
For a nondominant $\lambda$ the right side can be zero or negative, so dominance is needed.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The formula gives the dimension of each irreducible representation as a product of simple fractions, one for each positive root.
It needs only the highest weight and the roots.
For $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ it gives the dimensions $1, 3, 3, 6, 8, 10, \dots$ that appear in the quark model of physics.

### L2 human

It is the most used corollary of the Weyl character formula.
It shows that $\dim L(\lambda)$ is a polynomial in the coordinates of $\lambda$, of degree equal to the number of positive roots.
For compact Lie groups it gives the dimensions of the irreducible representations of the group.
