## Summary

The Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re} = U L / \nu$ is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] that compares inertial forces with viscous forces in a flow.
At small Reynolds number viscosity dominates and flows are smooth and orderly.
At large Reynolds number inertia dominates and flows tend to become unstable and turbulent.
It is the main parameter of the incompressible [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] after they are made dimensionless.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Honey poured from a spoon flows smoothly; water from a fast tap churns and splashes.
The Reynolds number explains the difference: it is large when the flow is fast, large and not very sticky, and small when it is slow, small or very sticky.
A bacterium swimming in water has a Reynolds number far below $1$, so for it water feels like thick syrup.
A ship has a Reynolds number in the hundreds of millions or more, so the water around it is turbulent.
