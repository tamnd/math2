## Definition

Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]], let $F^\times$ be its group of nonzero elements, and let $\Gamma$ be an [[abelian group|082GHNPJ53]] written additively.
A *commutation factor* on $\Gamma$ is a map $\varepsilon \colon \Gamma \times \Gamma \to F^\times$ such that for all $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \Gamma$:

1. $\varepsilon(\alpha, \beta)\, \varepsilon(\beta, \alpha) = 1$;
2. $\varepsilon(\alpha + \beta, \gamma) = \varepsilon(\alpha, \gamma)\, \varepsilon(\beta, \gamma)$;
3. $\varepsilon(\alpha, \beta + \gamma) = \varepsilon(\alpha, \beta)\, \varepsilon(\alpha, \gamma)$.

A *color Lie algebra* (or $\varepsilon$-Lie algebra) over $F$ is a $\Gamma$-graded [[vector space|000AYPA9AJ]] $L = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Gamma} L_\alpha$ with a bilinear map $[\cdot, \cdot] \colon L \times L \to L$.
The conditions are, for all $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \Gamma$ and $x \in L_\alpha$, $y \in L_\beta$, $z \in L_\gamma$:

4. $[L_\alpha, L_\beta] \subseteq L_{\alpha + \beta}$;
5. $[x, y] = -\varepsilon(\alpha, \beta)\, [y, x]$;
6. $\varepsilon(\gamma, \alpha)\, [x, [y, z]\,] + \varepsilon(\alpha, \beta)\, [y, [z, x]\,] + \varepsilon(\beta, \gamma)\, [z, [x, y]\,] = 0$.

## Remarks

Example: $\Gamma = \Z/2\Z$ and $\varepsilon(\alpha, \beta) = (-1)^{\alpha\beta}$.
Clauses 1 to 3 hold because $(-1)^{\alpha\beta}$ is symmetric with values $\pm 1$ and additive in each exponent.
Clauses 4 to 6 are then the axioms of a [[Lie superalgebra|7CZ4Q5H78N]].
With $\Gamma = 0$ and $\varepsilon = 1$, clauses 4 to 6 are the axioms of a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]].

Example: if $A = \bigoplus_\alpha A_\alpha$ is a $\Gamma$-graded associative algebra, then $[x, y] = xy - \varepsilon(\alpha, \beta)\, yx$ for $x \in A_\alpha$, $y \in A_\beta$ makes $A$ a color Lie algebra.

Non-example: on $\Gamma = \Z$ with $F = \R$, the map $\varepsilon(\alpha, \beta) = 2^{\alpha\beta}$ satisfies clauses 2 and 3 but fails clause 1, since $\varepsilon(1, 1)^2 = 4 \ne 1$.

Clause 1 with $\alpha = \beta$ gives $\varepsilon(\alpha, \alpha) = \pm 1$, which splits $\Gamma$ into an even and an odd part.
