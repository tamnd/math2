## Summary

Let $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family indexed by an arbitrary set $I$.
It is summable when its finite partial sums approach a limit as the finite set of indices grows.
This notion of sum uses no order on $I$, so it works for index sets such as $\N \times \N$, $\Z$ or uncountable sets.
For real or complex terms indexed by $\N$, summability is the same as [[absolute convergence|0YBDSJARWN]].
The exact definition is in [[summable family|Z4H7272S9S]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An ordinary series adds its terms in a fixed order.
A summable family has a sum that does not depend on any order.
Once a large enough finite batch of terms is added, adding further terms keeps you close to the sum.
For example, the numbers $1/(2^m 3^n)$ for all pairs $(m, n)$ of natural numbers form a summable family with sum $3$.
