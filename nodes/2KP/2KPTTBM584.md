## Summary

The dipole model approximates [[Earth's magnetic field|Z13HPJFV1F]] by the field of a single [[magnetic dipole|SSAWJ3JF5Y]] at the centre of the Earth.
It consists of the degree-one terms of the spherical harmonic expansion, which carry most of the field at the surface.
The dipole axis is tilted by about ten degrees from the rotation axis, and the points where it meets the surface are the geomagnetic poles.
The model gives simple formulas for intensity and inclination as functions of magnetic latitude, which are used in paleomagnetism and for geomagnetic coordinates.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The simplest picture of the Earth's field is a short bar magnet at the centre of the Earth, slightly tilted from the spin axis.
Its field lines leave the southern hemisphere, loop around through space, and enter the northern hemisphere.
In this model the field is twice as strong at the magnetic poles as at the magnetic equator.
At magnetic latitude $45$ degrees the field dips about $63$ degrees below the horizontal.
