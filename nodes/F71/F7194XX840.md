## Example

Let $m > 0$ be a mass, $\omega > 0$ an angular frequency and $\hbar > 0$ the reduced Planck constant.
The *quantum harmonic oscillator* is the [[Schrödinger operator|89AA6XJY5K]]
$$H = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{d^2}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2$$
on $L^2(\R)$.
Its spectrum is purely [[discrete|PJWY3JJQQ7]], and its eigenvalues are
$$E_n = \hbar \omega \left( n + \tfrac12 \right), \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots,$$
each of multiplicity one.
With $a = m\omega/\hbar$, an eigenfunction for $E_n$ is $\psi_n(x) = H_n(\sqrt{a}\, x)\, e^{-a x^2/2}$, where $H_n$ is the $n$-th [[Hermite polynomial|MGACY98E9H]].
Up to normalisation these are the [[Hermite functions|52VS07H6GK]], and they form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\R)$ after normalisation.

Check for $n = 0$: with $\psi_0(x) = e^{-a x^2/2}$ one has $\psi_0'' = (a^2 x^2 - a)\psi_0$.
So $H\psi_0 = \left( -\frac{\hbar^2 a^2}{2m} x^2 + \frac{\hbar^2 a}{2m} + \frac{m\omega^2}{2} x^2 \right) \psi_0$.
Since $\hbar^2 a^2 / (2m) = m\omega^2/2$, the terms in $x^2$ cancel, and $H\psi_0 = \frac{\hbar \omega}{2} \psi_0$.

## Remarks

The [[annihilation operator|7N0J4DF8N5]] $A = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}} \left( x + \frac{\hbar}{m\omega} \frac{d}{dx} \right)$ and its adjoint $A^\dagger$ satisfy $[A, A^\dagger] = 1$ and $H = \hbar\omega \left( A^\dagger A + \frac12 \right)$.
From these relations alone, $A^\dagger$ raises the energy by $\hbar\omega$ and $A$ lowers it, which gives the whole spectrum.
The levels are equally spaced, unlike those of most other potentials.
The ground state energy $\hbar\omega/2$ is positive, in contrast with the classical [[harmonic oscillator|0YG0ZJPTWG]], whose minimum energy is $0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A quantum particle attached to a spring can only have certain energies.
These energies form a ladder with equal steps of size $\hbar\omega$.
The lowest rung is not zero but $\hbar\omega/2$: the particle can never be completely at rest.
The lowest state is a bell curve centred at the bottom of the well.
The model describes small vibrations of molecules and the modes of light, so it appears all over physics.
