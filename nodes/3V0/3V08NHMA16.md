## Definition

Let an [[exchange economy|HGMHZSM0XQ]] have $L$ goods and $I$ consumers, where consumer $i$ has consumption set $\R^L_+$, a [[preference relation|JGP0Q56FRY]] $\succsim_i$ and an endowment $\omega_i \in \R^L_+$.
For a price vector $p \in \R^L_+$, $p \ne 0$, the budget set of consumer $i$ is $B_i(p) = \set{x \in \R^L_+ : p \cdot x \le p \cdot \omega_i}$.
A *competitive equilibrium* is a pair $(p, x)$ of a price vector $p \in \R^L_+ \setminus \set{0}$ and an allocation $x = (x_1, \dots, x_I)$ with $x_i \in \R^L_+$, such that:

1. for each $i$, $x_i \in B_i(p)$ and $x_i \succsim_i y$ for every $y \in B_i(p)$;
2. markets clear: $\sum_{i=1}^{I} x_i = \sum_{i=1}^{I} \omega_i$.

The allocation $x$ is then called a competitive (or Walrasian) equilibrium allocation.

## Remarks

Example: two consumers with utility $\sqrt{x y}$ and endowments $\omega_1 = (1, 0)$ and $\omega_2 = (0, 1)$.
At $p = (1, 1)$ each has wealth $1$ and the best bundle is $(1/2, 1/2)$, since a consumer with this utility spends half of the wealth on each good.
The bundles add up to $(1, 1) = \omega_1 + \omega_2$, so $(p, ((1/2, 1/2), (1/2, 1/2)))$ is a competitive equilibrium.

Non-example: at $p = (1, 2)$ the best bundles are $(1/2, 1/4)$ and $(1, 1/2)$.
Each is optimal in its budget set, but the total demand for good $1$ is $3/2 > 1$, so clause 2 fails.

If $(p, x)$ is a competitive equilibrium, so is $(t p, x)$ for every $t > 0$.
In an economy with production, clause 1 is joined by profit maximization of each firm at the prices $p$, and profits are paid to the owners.
This is the equilibrium of the [[Arrow–Debreu model|SVGN7R3RDY]].
Some texts allow free disposal and replace clause 2 by $\sum_i x_i \le \sum_i \omega_i$ with $p_\ell = 0$ for each good in excess supply.
