## Summary

Special relativity is the physical theory of space and time in the absence of gravity.
Its model of spacetime is [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]], and observers in uniform motion are related by [[Lorentz transformations|WEVGN4C7PQ]] instead of Galilean transformations.
It explains why the speed of light is the same for every inertial observer.
It is the starting point of general relativity and of relativistic quantum theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In everyday life, speeds simply add and clocks agree for everyone.
Special relativity says this is only approximately true: moving clocks run slow, moving rods shrink, and two events that are simultaneous for one observer need not be simultaneous for another.
All of this follows from two ideas: the laws of physics are the same for all observers in uniform motion, and light always moves at the same speed $c$.
For example, a clock moving at $60\%$ of the speed of light ticks only $8$ times while a clock at rest ticks $10$ times.
At low speeds the differences are tiny, which is why Newtonian mechanics works so well in daily life.
