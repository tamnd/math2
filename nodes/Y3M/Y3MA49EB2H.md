## Summary

Gravitational microlensing is lensing by a star, planet or other compact object whose images are too close together to be separated by a telescope.
The lens shows up only as a temporary brightening of the source as lens and source move across the sky relative to each other.
It is used to find planets around distant stars and to search for dark compact objects in the halo of the Milky Way.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A star passing almost exactly in front of a more distant star bends its light and makes it look brighter for a while.
The two images it makes are only a few thousandths of an arcsecond apart, so they blur into one point.
What we see is a star that brightens smoothly and fades again over days or weeks, with the same shape of light curve in every colour.
If the lensing star has a planet, the planet can add a short extra spike to this curve.
