## Statement

Consider the [[Ising model|R0KGM32QN6]] on the square lattice $\Z^2$ with nearest-neighbour coupling $J > 0$ and no external field, at inverse temperature $\beta > 0$, and let $K = \beta J$.
For $n \ge 1$ let $Z_n$ be its partition function on the $n \times n$ box.
Then the free energy per site exists in the [[thermodynamic limit|FPYQWE8X12]] and equals
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n^2} \ln Z_n = \ln 2 + \frac{1}{8 \pi^2} \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^{2\pi} \ln\!\Big[ \cosh^2(2K) - \sinh(2K) \big( \cos\theta_1 + \cos\theta_2 \big) \Big] \, d\theta_1 \, d\theta_2.$$
This function of $K$ is real analytic except at the single point $K_c$ with $\sinh(2K_c) = 1$, that is $K_c = \frac12 \ln(1 + \sqrt 2)$.

## Remarks

Lars Onsager obtained the formula in 1944 by computing the largest eigenvalue of the transfer matrix of the model, using the algebra generated by its two factors.
Later proofs write the partition function as a Pfaffian or determinant, through free fermions, dimer coverings or the Kac–Ward formula.
Check at $K = 0$: the integrand is $\ln 1 = 0$, so the limit is $\ln 2$, the entropy of independent spins.
For large $K$ the right-hand side is about $2K$, which matches the ground state energy $-2J$ per site, since each site has two bonds.
The argument of the logarithm equals $(\sinh 2K - 1)^2$ at $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 0$, which explains the singularity at $K_c$: the specific heat diverges logarithmically there.
The spontaneous magnetization for $K > K_c$ is $\left(1 - \sinh(2K)^{-4}\right)^{1/8}$; Onsager announced this formula, and C. N. Yang published a proof in 1952.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The two-dimensional Ising model is a grid of up-or-down spins that like to agree with their neighbours.
Onsager found an exact formula for its free energy, from which all thermodynamic quantities follow.
The formula shows a sharp phase transition at a definite temperature, which settled that statistical mechanics can produce phase transitions at all.
It remains one of the few interacting models in two or more dimensions with an exact solution.

### L2 human

The critical point agrees with the self-dual point of [[Kramers–Wannier duality|GBM9TF1V7K]], found three years earlier.
Near $K_c$ the solution gives the critical exponent $0$ for the specific heat (a logarithmic divergence) and $1/8$ for the magnetization, which differ from mean-field values.
These exact exponents became the main test case for scaling theory and the renormalization group.
The method of transfer matrices used here also underlies the solutions of many later exactly solvable lattice models.
