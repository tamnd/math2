## Definition

Let $S \ge 0$ be the concentration of a growth-limiting substrate, let $\mu_{\max} > 0$ be the maximal specific growth rate, and let $K_s > 0$ be the half-saturation constant.
The *Monod equation* gives the specific growth rate
$$\mu(S) = \frac{\mu_{\max}\, S}{K_s + S}.$$
For a population of biomass concentration $X$, the growth is then $dX/dt = \mu(S)\, X$.
The substrate is used at rate $\mu(S) X / Y$, where $Y > 0$ is the yield coefficient.

## Remarks

The function $\mu$ is [[increasing|0A7KV2WB7A]] with $\mu(0) = 0$, $\mu(K_s) = \mu_{\max}/2$ and $\mu(S) \to \mu_{\max}$ as $S \to \infty$.
Example: $\mu_{\max} = 0.8\ \mathrm{h}^{-1}$ and $K_s = 4$ mg/L give $\mu(4) = 0.8 \cdot 4/8 = 0.4\ \mathrm{h}^{-1}$.
Non-example: the Haldane law $\mu(S) = \mu_{\max} S/(K_s + S + S^2/K_i)$ for an inhibiting substrate is not a Monod equation; it decreases for large $S$.
The formula has the form of the [[Michaelis–Menten rate law|6KMKXSX99V]], with $K_s$ in place of $K_M$.
