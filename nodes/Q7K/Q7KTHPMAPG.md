## Proof

1. Let $G$ be a [[group|G5S1M9]] with identity $e$, and let $g \in G$. By definition, $\langle g \rangle = \set{g^k : k \in \Z}$ is the [[cyclic subgroup generated by $g$|0YT586TKXF]].
2. Let $i < j$ be integers with $g^i = g^j$. By the [[laws of exponents|00DRK90Z6K]], $g^{j-i} = g^j g^{-i} = g^i g^{-i} = g^0 = e$, and $j - i > 0$.
3. First suppose $g$ has finite [[order|0JPYSD5E0B]] $n$. Let $k \in \Z$. By the [[division algorithm|0CHQKZFK5S]], $k = qn + r$ with $q, r \in \Z$ and $0 \leq r \leq n-1$.
4. Since $n$ divides $k - r = qn$, [[the divisibility criterion for powers|0F5QC101Q7]] gives $g^{k-r} = e$.
5. By the laws of exponents, $g^k = g^{k-r}g^r = g^r$. So each element of $\langle g \rangle$ lies in $\set{e, g, g^2, \ldots, g^{n-1}}$.
6. Conversely each $g^r$ with $0 \leq r \leq n-1$ lies in $\langle g \rangle$, and $g^0 = e$. So $\langle g \rangle = \set{e, g, g^2, \ldots, g^{n-1}}$.
7. Suppose $g^i = g^j$ with $0 \leq i < j \leq n-1$. By step 2, $g^{j-i} = e$ with $0 < j - i < n$. This contradicts the minimality of $n$.
8. So the $n$ elements $e, g, \ldots, g^{n-1}$ are distinct, and $\langle g \rangle$ has [[order|0SEX9F9J5Z]] $n$.
9. Now suppose $g$ has infinite order. If $g^i = g^j$ with $i < j$, step 2 gives a positive integer $j - i$ with $g^{j-i} = e$. This is impossible.
10. So $g^i \neq g^j$ whenever $i \neq j$. The map $k \mapsto g^k$ from $\Z$ to $\langle g \rangle$ is injective, so $\langle g \rangle$ is infinite.

## Idea

Two powers $g^i$ and $g^j$ are equal exactly when $g^{j-i} = e$. If $g$ has order $n$, the exponents can be reduced modulo $n$, and no two of $0, \ldots, n-1$ give the same power. If $g$ has infinite order, no two exponents give the same power.
