## Proof

We write out the Leibniz formula for the whole matrix, discard the terms that meet the zero block, and factor what remains into the Leibniz formulas for $A$ and $D$.

1. Let $N = m + n$, and let $M = (M_{ij})$ be the $N \times N$ [[block matrix|01AXG9KZQF]] $\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ 0 & D \end{pmatrix}$.
   By the definition of the blocks, $M_{ij} = a_{ij}$ for $i, j \le m$, and $M_{m+k,\, m+l} = d_{kl}$ for $k, l \le n$.
   Also $M_{ij} = 0$ whenever $i > m$ and $j \le m$.
2. By the [[Leibniz formula|016PE4VZ8Q]],
$$\det M = \sum_{\sigma \in S_N} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) \prod_{i=1}^{N} M_{i, \sigma(i)}.$$
3. Let $T$ be the set of $\sigma \in S_N$ with $\sigma(i) > m$ for every $i > m$.
   If $\sigma \notin T$, then $\sigma(i) \le m$ for some $i > m$.
   So the factor $M_{i, \sigma(i)}$ lies in the zero block, and the term of $\sigma$ is $0$.
4. Let $\sigma \in T$.
   Then $\sigma$ maps the finite set $\{m+1, \dots, N\}$ injectively into itself, so it maps this set onto itself.
   Since $\sigma$ is a bijection of $\{1, \dots, N\}$, it also maps $\{1, \dots, m\}$ onto itself.
5. So each $\sigma \in T$ defines $\alpha \in S_m$ and $\beta \in S_n$ by $\alpha(i) = \sigma(i)$ for $i \le m$ and $\beta(k) = \sigma(m + k) - m$ for $k \le n$.
   Conversely, every pair $(\alpha, \beta) \in S_m \times S_n$ comes from exactly one $\sigma \in T$, given by the same two formulas.
   So $\sigma \mapsto (\alpha, \beta)$ is a bijection from $T$ onto $S_m \times S_n$.
6. Let $\alpha'$ act as $\alpha$ on $\{1, \dots, m\}$ and fix the other points.
   Let $\beta'$ fix $\{1, \dots, m\}$ and send $m + k$ to $m + \beta(k)$.
   Then $\sigma = \alpha' \beta'$, since the two factors move disjoint sets of points.
7. If $\alpha$ is a product of transpositions in $S_m$, the same transpositions, read in $S_N$, give $\alpha'$.
   So $\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha') = \operatorname{sgn}(\alpha)$ by the [[definition of the sign|0SK90238BH]].
   Shifting each transposition by $m$ gives $\operatorname{sgn}(\beta') = \operatorname{sgn}(\beta)$ in the same way.
8. The sign is a [[homomorphism|0JEJJCB0EX]] on $S_N$, so
$$\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = \operatorname{sgn}(\alpha') \operatorname{sgn}(\beta') = \operatorname{sgn}(\alpha) \operatorname{sgn}(\beta).$$
9. By step 1, the product in the term of $\sigma \in T$ splits as
$$\prod_{i=1}^{N} M_{i, \sigma(i)} = \prod_{i=1}^{m} a_{i, \alpha(i)} \cdot \prod_{k=1}^{n} d_{k, \beta(k)}.$$
10. Steps 3, 5, 8 and 9 turn the Leibniz sum into a double sum over $S_m \times S_n$, and this double sum factors:
$$\det M = \Bigl(\sum_{\alpha \in S_m} \operatorname{sgn}(\alpha) \prod_{i=1}^{m} a_{i, \alpha(i)}\Bigr) \Bigl(\sum_{\beta \in S_n} \operatorname{sgn}(\beta) \prod_{k=1}^{n} d_{k, \beta(k)}\Bigr).$$
11. By the Leibniz formula for $A$ and for $D$, the two factors are $\det A$ and $\det D$.
   So $\det M = \det A \cdot \det D$.

## Idea

Each term of the Leibniz formula picks one entry in every row and every column.
If a term picks an entry from the zero block, it is zero.
So the only terms that count pick their entries in the first $m$ rows from the first $m$ columns, and their entries in the last $n$ rows from the last $n$ columns.
Such a choice is a choice for $A$ and an independent choice for $D$, and the signs multiply.
That is why the sum factors into $\det A \cdot \det D$.

For $m = n = 1$ the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & d \end{pmatrix}$, the only surviving permutation is the identity, and the formula is $ad$.

## Variants

A second proof factors
$$\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ 0 & D \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} I_m & 0 \\ 0 & D \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ 0 & I_n \end{pmatrix}$$
and uses [[multiplicativity|04CPJ6AF00]].
Laplace expansion along the first $m$ rows of the first factor, one row at a time, gives $\det D$.
Laplace expansion along the last $n$ rows of the second factor gives $\det A$.

Over a field there is also a proof by row reduction.
Row operations inside the first $m$ rows bring $A$ to triangular form, and row operations inside the last $n$ rows do the same for $D$.
Each operation changes $\det M$ by the same factor as it changes $\det A$ or $\det D$.
Neither kind disturbs the zero block, so the result is triangular, and its diagonal is made of the two diagonals.
