## Example

Let $v = (1, 2, 3) \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
The map $w \mapsto v \times w$ is linear, and its matrix is
$$[v]_\times = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -3 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

1. The diagonal is $0$, and $a_{12} = -3 = -a_{21}$, $a_{13} = 2 = -a_{31}$, $a_{23} = -1 = -a_{32}$. So $[v]_\times$ is [[skew-symmetric|03RNVFDEAN]].
2. Take $w = (1, 0, 0)$. Then $v \times w = (2 \cdot 0 - 3 \cdot 0,\ 3 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 0,\ 1 \cdot 0 - 2 \cdot 1) = (0, 3, -2)$.
3. The [[matrix-vector product|0CCQV3884B]] $[v]_\times w$ is the first column $(0, 3, -2)$, which agrees.
4. Take $w = (0, 1, 1)$. Then $v \times w = (2 - 3, 0 - 1, 1 - 0) = (-1, -1, 1)$, and $[v]_\times w = (-3 + 2, 0 - 1, 1 + 0) = (-1, -1, 1)$.
5. $[v]_\times v = (0 - 6 + 6, 3 + 0 - 3, -2 + 2 + 0) = 0$, as $v \times v = 0$.

## Remarks

For $v = (v_1, v_2, v_3)$ the general form is $[v]_\times = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -v_3 & v_2 \\ v_3 & 0 & -v_1 \\ -v_2 & v_1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
The map $v \mapsto [v]_\times$ is a linear bijection from $\mathbb{R}^3$ onto the $3 \times 3$ real skew-symmetric matrices.
Each $w$ satisfies $w \cdot (v \times w) = 0$, which is $w^{\mathsf{T}} [v]_\times w = 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The cross product with a fixed vector can be written as multiplication by a matrix.
That matrix has zeros on the diagonal and opposite numbers in mirror positions.

### L2 human

The vector $v \times w$ is perpendicular to $w$, which is the skew-symmetry $w^{\mathsf{T}} [v]_\times w = 0$.
In mechanics, $[\omega]_\times$ gives the velocity $\omega \times r$ of a point $r$ on a body that turns with angular velocity $\omega$.
