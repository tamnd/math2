## Summary

Let $L$ be a [[signature|WDVVEYD6AT]].
A *universal sentence* is a [[sentence|DKFGQTRYZE]] $\forall x_1 \cdots \forall x_n \, \theta$ with $\theta$ quantifier-free and $n \ge 0$.
A class $\mathcal{K}$ of $L$-structures is a *universal class* if there is a set $\Sigma$ of universal sentences such that $\mathcal{K}$ is exactly the class of models of $\Sigma$.
Every universal class is an [[elementary class|MEC3ZMC4F1]], and it is closed under isomorphisms, [[substructures|676H7EGTKJ]] and [[ultraproducts|AX9Q86GB8M]]; [[this theorem|~f72a8819]] shows that these closure properties characterize it.
Universal classes are the classes in which membership is decided by finitely generated pieces, and they include the varieties and quasivarieties of algebra.
The formal definition is [[this definition|NX9X1A5EKY]].

## Motivation

Many classes in algebra and combinatorics are defined by forbidding a finite pattern.
A graph is triangle-free if no three vertices are pairwise adjacent, and a group is torsion-free if no $x \ne e$ has $x^n = e$.
Such conditions are checked on finitely many elements at a time, so they hold in every part of a structure that satisfies them.
Universal sentences are the first-order form of "no bad pattern occurs".
The [[Łoś-Tarski theorem|9RN0KYXNT1]] shows that this form is forced: an elementary class closed under substructures is universal.

## Notation

- $\mathrm{Th}_\forall(\mathcal{K})$ is the set of universal sentences true in every member of $\mathcal{K}$, the *universal theory* of $\mathcal{K}$.
- $\mathbf{I}$, $\mathbf{S}$ and $\mathbf{P}_U$ are the operators that close a class under isomorphic copies, substructures and ultraproducts. Universal sentences are also called $\forall_1$ or $\Pi_1$ sentences.
- All structures have nonempty domains, as usual in model theory.

## Remarks

### Examples

- Graphs in the signature $\set{E}$, with $\forall x \, \neg E(x, x)$ and $\forall x \forall y \, (E(x, y) \rightarrow E(y, x))$.
- [[Triangle-free graphs|~75e6a923]], with one more universal axiom.
- [[$k$-colorable graphs|~5861fb96]], for each $k \ge 1$; the axioms come from compactness.
- Groups in the signature $\set{\cdot, {}^{-1}, e}$, and more generally every variety of algebras.
- Linear orders, partial orders and integral domains in their usual signatures.
- In a finite relational signature, every class defined by forbidding a set of finite structures as substructures.

### Non-examples

- [[Fields|~c71294bd]]: the subring $\mathbb{Z}$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ is not a field, so the class is not closed under substructures.
- Groups in the signature $\set{\cdot}$: the positive integers are a substructure of $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ and not a group.
- Dense linear orders: $(\mathbb{Z}, <)$ is a substructure of $(\mathbb{Q}, <)$ and is not dense.
- Finite graphs: the class is closed under substructures but not under ultraproducts, so it is not elementary.

### Basic properties

- A structure belongs to a universal class $\mathcal{K}$ if and only if each of its finitely generated substructures belongs to $\mathcal{K}$; see [[this theorem|~f72a8819]].
- The universal class generated by $\mathcal{K}$, the class of models of $\mathrm{Th}_\forall(\mathcal{K})$, is $\mathbf{I}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{P}_U(\mathcal{K})$.
- Intersections of universal classes are universal. A union of two universal classes is universal, since $\forall \bar x \, \theta \vee \forall \bar y \, \eta$ is equivalent to $\forall \bar x \, \forall \bar y \, (\theta \vee \eta)$ when $\bar x$ and $\bar y$ are disjoint.
- Universal classes are closed under unions of chains, by the [[Chang-Łoś-Suszko theorem|~99a892b2]] in its easy direction.
- They need not be closed under products or homomorphic images: linear orders fail both. Closure under products is the extra property of [[universal Horn classes|9A4CYWXPEA]].

### History

The Łoś-Tarski theorem of the 1950s identified the universal classes among the elementary classes.
The characterization by substructures and ultraproducts rests on [[Łoś's theorem|AGYBSTD7BG]] of 1955.

### Common errors

- To check closure under subsets instead of substructures. In a signature with functions or constants, a substructure must contain the constants and be closed under the functions.
- To think that a universal class is closed under products. The product of two 2-element chains is not a chain.
- To drop the ultraproduct condition. The finite graphs are closed under substructures and are not universal.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A universal class is a family of structures defined by rules that forbid patterns.
For example, "no three people all know each other" forbids a triangle in a friendship network.
If a network has no triangle, then every smaller part of it has no triangle either.
So to test such a rule, it is enough to look at three people at a time.

### L2 human

We show that partial orders in the signature $\set{\le}$ form a universal class.

1. Reflexivity is $\forall x \, (x \le x)$, a universal sentence.
2. Antisymmetry is $\forall x \forall y \, (x \le y \wedge y \le x \rightarrow x = y)$, a universal sentence.
3. Transitivity is $\forall x \forall y \forall z \, (x \le y \wedge y \le z \rightarrow x \le z)$, a universal sentence.
4. The partial orders are exactly the models of these three sentences, so they form a universal class.
5. A check: the divisors of $12$ under divisibility form a partial order, and the subset $\set{1, 2, 3}$ is again a partial order.

The typical use is to see at once that substructures stay in the class.
A trap is to add totality, $\forall x \forall y \, (x \le y \vee y \le x)$, and to expect closure under products: the linear orders are still a universal class, but the product of two chains is not a chain.

### L3 human

In a finite relational signature, a universal class is the same as the class of structures whose age avoids a set of forbidden finite structures.
This links universal classes with hereditary graph classes in combinatorics and with the ages of Fraïssé theory.
In categorical terms, a universal class is closed under embeddings into its members and under ultraproducts, which are filtered colimits of products.
The algebraic refinements are the [[universal Horn classes|9A4CYWXPEA]], closed also under products, and the varieties of [[Birkhoff's HSP theorem|5083DHSK82]], closed also under homomorphic images.
