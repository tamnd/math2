## Summary

Mathematical biology is the branch of applied mathematics that builds and studies mathematical models of living systems.
Its tools include [[differential equations|003R0V5M7V]], [[dynamical systems|BBZP1R872N]], [[stochastic processes|0QH5ENKZM1]], graphs and statistics.
It matters because a model turns a biological hypothesis into precise predictions that can be checked against data.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A biologist sees rabbits multiply, a disease spread, or a gene become common.
Mathematical biology writes such a story as equations and then asks what the equations force to happen.
For example, if each rabbit pair has a fixed number of offspring per year with no limits, the number of rabbits is multiplied by the same factor every year.
The equations show at once that this growth cannot go on forever, which points to the role of food and space.
Similar models describe heartbeats, nerve signals, the spread of epidemics and the evolution of species.
