## Statement

Let $k$ be a field of [[characteristic|1ZB13N78XW]] $0$, and let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over $k$.
Then $\mathfrak g$ has a faithful finite-dimensional [[representation|TZAZ163QGK]].
Equivalently, $\mathfrak g$ is isomorphic to a Lie subalgebra of the [[general linear Lie algebra|G8FEGYDSNE]] $\mathfrak{gl}_n(k)$ for some $n$.

## Remarks

The representation can be chosen so that every element of the [[nilradical|ET8Y1AR5YX]] of $\mathfrak g$ acts by a [[nilpotent endomorphism|063D5Z2C70]].

The kernel of the [[adjoint representation|54XSPNAM1J]] is the [[center|JJ9WQWCP9G]] of $\mathfrak g$, so the theorem is easy when the center is $0$.
In general one builds a faithful finite-dimensional module of the nilradical as a quotient of its universal enveloping algebra.
Then one extends the action step by step to all of $\mathfrak g$.

Example: the [[Heisenberg algebra|HBVZGRKCWQ]] with basis $x, y, z$, $[x, y] = z$ and $z$ central has nonzero center, so its adjoint representation is not faithful.
It is nevertheless isomorphic to the algebra of strictly upper triangular $3 \times 3$ matrices, with $x = E_{12}$, $y = E_{23}$ and $z = E_{13}$, since $[E_{12}, E_{23}] = E_{13}$.

Finite dimension of $\mathfrak g$ is needed, since an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra cannot embed in a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{gl}_n(k)$.
The same conclusion holds in positive characteristic, by Iwasawa's theorem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ado's theorem says that every finite-dimensional Lie algebra can be realized by matrices, with the bracket being the commutator $AB - BA$.
So the abstract definition of a Lie algebra does not produce anything beyond what matrices already give, as long as the dimension is finite.

### L2 human

The theorem lets one prove results about abstract Lie algebras by working with matrices.
Over $\R$, it gives a quick proof of Lie's third theorem.
Every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of a Lie group, namely of a connected subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\R)$.
The group version fails: there are Lie groups, such as the universal cover of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\R)$, that have no faithful finite-dimensional representation.
