## Statement

Let $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ be the vacuum permittivity.
Consider a linear isotropic dielectric made of identical molecules with number density $N$ per unit volume.
Assume that each molecule acquires the [[dipole moment|BANHMXD2TD]] $p = \alpha E_{\mathrm{loc}}$, where $\alpha > 0$ is the molecular polarizability.
Assume that the local field acting on a molecule is $E_{\mathrm{loc}} = E + P / (3 \varepsilon_0)$, where $E$ is the macroscopic [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] and $P = N p$ is the [[polarization density|ASQAH710WN]].
Assume $N \alpha < 3 \varepsilon_0$.
Then the relative [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]] $\varepsilon_r$ of the material satisfies
$$\frac{\varepsilon_r - 1}{\varepsilon_r + 2} = \frac{N \alpha}{3 \varepsilon_0}.$$

## Remarks

Put $s = N \alpha / (3 \varepsilon_0)$.
From $P = N \alpha (E + P / (3 \varepsilon_0))$ one gets $P = 3 \varepsilon_0 s E / (1 - s)$, so $\chi_e = 3 s / (1 - s)$.
Then $\varepsilon_r = 1 + \chi_e = (1 + 2s)/(1 - s)$, and solving for $s$ gives the relation.
The local field formula is the Lorentz field: the field at the centre of a small spherical cavity in a uniformly polarized medium, where the nearby dipoles cancel.
That cancellation holds for molecules on a cubic lattice or in random positions, so the relation is a model result for such materials, mostly gases and simple liquids.
Example: for a dilute gas, $s$ is small and the relation gives $\varepsilon_r \approx 1 + N \alpha / \varepsilon_0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Clausius–Mossotti relation links a property of single molecules, how easily they polarize, to a bulk property of the material, its permittivity.
The twist is that each molecule feels not only the applied field but also the field of all its polarized neighbours.
Taking this into account gives the factor $\varepsilon_r + 2$ in the denominator.

### L2 human

The relation follows from the definitions of [[polarization density|ASQAH710WN]] and [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]] together with the Lorentz local field.
It predicts that $(\varepsilon_r - 1)/(\varepsilon_r + 2)$ is proportional to the density, which agrees well with measurements on gases.
Its optical form, with $\varepsilon_r$ replaced by the square of the refractive index, is the Lorentz–Lorenz equation.
The assumption $N \alpha < 3 \varepsilon_0$ is needed; as $N \alpha$ approaches $3 \varepsilon_0$ the model predicts $\varepsilon_r \to \infty$, the so-called polarization catastrophe.
