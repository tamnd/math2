## Summary

Thermal conduction is the transfer of [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] inside a body, or between bodies in contact, without any bulk motion of matter.
It is driven by differences of [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]], and heat flows from hot to cold.
In most materials it obeys [[Fourier's law|9YJZ1PP2Y8]], and together with conservation of energy this leads to the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]].
It is one of the three modes of heat transfer, with [[convection|RK24AYNG4Z]] and radiation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hold one end of a metal spoon in hot tea, and soon the other end gets warm, although nothing in the spoon moves.
The fast-moving particles at the hot end pass energy to their slower neighbours, and these pass it on again.
Heat thus creeps from the hot end to the cold end.
Metals conduct heat well, while wood, air and foam conduct it poorly, which is why pot handles are made of wood or plastic.
