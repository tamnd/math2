## Summary

Sidereal time measures the rotation of the Earth relative to the stars rather than relative to the Sun.
The local sidereal time is the [[hour angle|20B8CQHH1S]] of the March equinox, so it equals the right ascension of the stars on the meridian.
A sidereal day is about $23$ hours $56$ minutes $4$ seconds of mean solar time.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary time follows the Sun, and sidereal time follows the stars.
While the Earth turns once, it also moves along its orbit, so the Sun needs a few extra minutes to come back to the same place in the sky.
The stars do not, so a star day is about four minutes shorter than a Sun day.
An astronomer who knows the sidereal time knows at once which stars are on the meridian: those whose right ascension equals it.
