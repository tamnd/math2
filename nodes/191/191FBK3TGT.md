## Definition

Let $k$ be an [[algebraically closed field|0PF6TM8TWT]] of [[characteristic|1ZB13N78XW]] $0$, and let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over $k$.
A *parabolic subalgebra* of $\mathfrak g$ is a [[subalgebra|WKSSTQ69AK]] $\mathfrak p$ of $\mathfrak g$ that contains a [[Borel subalgebra|AP4MMD8FPR]] of $\mathfrak g$.

## Remarks

Example: in the [[general linear Lie algebra|G8FEGYDSNE]] $\mathfrak{gl}_3(k)$, let $\mathfrak p$ be the set of matrices whose $(3, 1)$ and $(3, 2)$ entries vanish.
It is closed under the commutator, since it is the set of matrices that map the plane $k e_1 + k e_2$ into itself.
It contains the upper triangular matrices, which form a Borel subalgebra, so $\mathfrak p$ is parabolic.

Non-example: in the [[special linear Lie algebra|1ZAT1Z967A]] $\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)$, the line $kh$ spanned by $h = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1)$ is a subalgebra, but it is not parabolic.
Every Borel subalgebra of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)$ has dimension $2$, so none fits inside the one-dimensional $kh$.

Every Borel subalgebra and the whole algebra $\mathfrak g$ are parabolic.
For a semisimple $\mathfrak g$ with a fixed Borel subalgebra $\mathfrak b$ and base $\Delta$ of simple roots, the parabolic subalgebras containing $\mathfrak b$ correspond bijectively to the subsets of $\Delta$.
