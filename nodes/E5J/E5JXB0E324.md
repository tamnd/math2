## Summary

The elementary subgroup $E_n(R)$ is a subgroup of the general linear group $GL_n(R)$ over a ring $R$.
It is generated by the elementary matrices that add a multiple of one row to another.
The union $E(R)$ inside the [[stable general linear group|G8R9561PXT]] is, by Whitehead's lemma, the commutator subgroup of $GL(R)$.
So [[K1 of a ring|SXVNJY4XFY]] is the quotient $GL(R)/E(R)$, which measures how far invertible matrices are from products of row operations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The simplest invertible matrices are those of the row operation "add $r$ times row $j$ to row $i$".
The elementary subgroup consists of all products of such operations.
Over a field, Gaussian elimination shows that these products are exactly the matrices of determinant $1$.
Over other rings this can fail, and the difference is what algebraic K-theory studies.
For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\Z$ is elementary, while $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is not, since its determinant is $-1$.
