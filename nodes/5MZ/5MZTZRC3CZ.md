## Summary

A normal-form game, or strategic-form game, lists the players, the strategies of each player, and the payoff of each player for each combination of strategies.
The players are thought of as choosing their strategies at the same time and without seeing the others' choices.
It is the basic model of noncooperative game theory, and the setting in which Nash equilibrium is defined.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For two players a normal-form game is a table.
The rows are the choices of player 1 and the columns are the choices of player 2.
Each cell holds a pair of numbers: what player 1 gets and what player 2 gets.
In matching pennies each player shows heads or tails; player 1 wins $1$ from player 2 if the coins match and loses $1$ otherwise.
So the cell (heads, heads) holds $(1, -1)$ and the cell (heads, tails) holds $(-1, 1)$.
