## Definition

Consider a viscous fluid in a uniform gravitational field $g = -g e_z$, with $g > 0$ and $e_z$ the upward unit vector.
Assume its density depends on temperature $T$ as $\rho = \rho_0 \big(1 - \beta (T - T_0)\big)$, with $\abs{\beta (T - T_0)} \ll 1$.
Here $\rho_0 > 0$ is a reference density, $T_0$ a reference temperature and $\beta$ the thermal expansion coefficient.
The *Boussinesq approximation* replaces the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] and the heat equation of the fluid by the following system.
$$\nabla \cdot u = 0.$$
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + (u \cdot \nabla) u = -\frac{1}{\rho_0} \nabla p + \nu \Delta u + g \beta (T - T_0) e_z.$$
$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} + u \cdot \nabla T = \kappa \Delta T.$$
Here $u$ is the velocity, $p$ the pressure minus the hydrostatic pressure $-\rho_0 g z$, $\nu$ the kinematic [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] and $\kappa$ the thermal diffusivity.

## Remarks

The density is replaced by $\rho_0$ in the mass equation and in the inertia term; it keeps its variation only in the gravity force $\rho g$.
There the constant part $\rho_0 g$ is absorbed in the pressure, and the rest, divided by $\rho_0$, is the buoyancy term $g \beta (T - T_0) e_z$.
Often one writes $b = g \beta (T - T_0)$, the buoyancy, so that the term is $b e_z$.

Example: water near $20$ °C has $\beta \approx 2 \times 10^{-4}$ K$^{-1}$, so a temperature difference of $10$ K gives $\abs{\beta \Delta T} \approx 2 \times 10^{-3}$, and the approximation applies.

Non-example: a gas heated from $300$ K to $600$ K changes its density by a factor of about two, so $\abs{\beta (T - T_0)}$ is not small and the approximation fails.

The same model with salinity in place of, or in addition to, temperature is used for the ocean.
