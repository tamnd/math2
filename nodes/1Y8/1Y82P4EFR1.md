## Example

Let the subject term $S$ be the [[prime numbers|01826KMG2H]] and the predicate term $P$ be the odd positive integers.
The four corners of the [[square of opposition|VYNBSVZ2TH]] are A "all primes are odd", E "no prime is odd", I "some prime is odd" and O "some prime is not odd".
We find the truth value of each corner and check each relation of the square.

1. A is false, because $2$ is a prime and $2$ is even.
2. E is false, because $3$ is a prime and $3$ is odd.
3. I is true, with witness $3$.
4. O is true, with witness $2$.
5. A and O are contradictories, and exactly one of them, O, is true.
6. E and I are contradictories, and exactly one of them, I, is true.
7. A and E are contraries, and both are false.
   Contraries only exclude that both are true, so this is allowed.
8. I and O are subcontraries, and both are true.
   Subcontraries only exclude that both are false, so this is allowed.
9. Subalternation from A to I and from E to O makes no claim here, because A and E are false.
10. The subject term is not empty, because $2$ is a prime.
    So the traditional and the modern square give the same answers for these terms.

## Remarks

Both contraries are false and both subcontraries are true exactly when the subject class meets the predicate class and also meets its complement.
Here the witnesses are $3$ in $P$ and $2$ outside $P$.

Keep the subject and change the predicate, and the other two patterns of a non-empty subject appear.
With $P$ the integers greater than $1$, every prime is in $P$, so A and I are true and E and O are false.
With $P$ the multiples of $4$, no prime is in $P$, so E and O are true and A and I are false.
So for a non-empty subject there are exactly three patterns: $S \subseteq P$, $S \cap P = \varnothing$, and the mixed case of this example.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the four sentences "all primes are odd", "no prime is odd", "some prime is odd" and "some prime is not odd".
The number $2$ is a prime that is even, and the number $3$ is a prime that is odd.
So the "all" sentence and the "no" sentence are both false, and the two "some" sentences are both true.
Each "all" or "no" sentence is false exactly when the "some" sentence across the diagonal is true.

### L2 human

We fill in the square for a second predicate, the primes that leave remainder $1$ when divided by $4$.

1. A, "all primes leave remainder $1$ mod $4$", is false: $3$ leaves remainder $3$.
2. E, "no prime leaves remainder $1$ mod $4$", is false: $5$ leaves remainder $1$.
3. I is true with witness $5$, and O is true with witness $3$ (or $2$).
4. So this predicate gives the mixed pattern again, with the same shape as odd numbers.

To place a pair of terms on the square, look for one witness inside $P$ and one outside $P$.
The trap is to stop after one witness: $5$ alone makes I true, and only $3$ settles that A is false.
