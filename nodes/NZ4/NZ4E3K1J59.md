## Summary

The drag coefficient is a dimensionless number that measures the drag force on a body moving through a fluid.
It compares the drag with the dynamic pressure of the flow times a reference area of the body.
By [[dynamic similarity|ZX6ZEWPPSB]] it depends only on the shape of the body and on dimensionless numbers such as the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]] and the Mach number.
It lets measurements on a model in a wind tunnel predict the drag on a full-size car, plane or ship.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The drag on a car grows with the square of its speed, with the density of the air and with the size of the car.
Divide out these effects and what is left is a single number that describes how streamlined the shape is.
A flat plate facing the wind has a drag coefficient of about $1$ or more, a streamlined shape has a much smaller one.
