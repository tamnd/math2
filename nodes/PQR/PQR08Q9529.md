## Example

Over any field $F$, let
$$N_1 = J_3(0) \oplus J_3(0) \oplus J_1(0), \qquad N_2 = J_3(0) \oplus J_2(0) \oplus J_2(0),$$
two $7 \times 7$ sums of nilpotent [[Jordan blocks|0PBFS92AJP]].
We show that they agree in characteristic polynomial, minimal polynomial and rank, and that they are still not similar.

1. Both matrices are upper triangular with zero diagonal, so $\chi_{N_1} = \chi_{N_2} = x^7$.
2. A block $J_k(0)$ satisfies $J_k(0)^k = 0$ and $J_k(0)^{k-1} \ne 0$. The largest block of both matrices is $J_3(0)$, so $N_1^3 = N_2^3 = 0$ while $N_1^2 \ne 0$ and $N_2^2 \ne 0$. Hence both [[minimal polynomials|03NDVCSHSB]] are $x^3$.
3. The rank of $J_k(0)$ is $k - 1$, and ranks add over blocks, so
$$\operatorname{rank} N_1 = 2 + 2 + 0 = 4, \qquad \operatorname{rank} N_2 = 2 + 1 + 1 = 4.$$
4. So both kernels have dimension $3$, and both matrices have $3$ Jordan blocks.
5. The rank of $J_k(0)^2$ is $\max(k - 2, 0)$, so
$$\operatorname{rank} N_1^2 = 1 + 1 + 0 = 2, \qquad \operatorname{rank} N_2^2 = 1 + 0 + 0 = 1.$$
6. If $N_2 = P^{-1} N_1 P$, then $N_2^2 = P^{-1} N_1^2 P$, and the two squares would have equal rank. They do not, so $N_1$ and $N_2$ are not [[similar|027KDW2K72]].

## Remarks

For nilpotent matrices the three invariants read off three numbers of the Jordan partition.
The characteristic polynomial gives the size $n$, the minimal polynomial gives the largest part, and the rank gives $n$ minus the number of parts.
The partitions $(3, 3, 1)$ and $(3, 2, 2)$ have the same size $7$, the same largest part $3$ and the same number of parts $3$, so the three invariants cannot tell them apart.

The size $7$ is the smallest possible for this kind of example.
A check of all partitions of $n \le 6$ shows that the size, the largest part and the number of parts always determine the partition there.
In size $8$ there are already two such pairs, $(4, 3, 1)$ with $(4, 2, 2)$, and $(3, 3, 1, 1)$ with $(3, 2, 2, 1)$.

What is enough?
The ranks of $(A - \lambda I)^k$ for all eigenvalues $\lambda$ and all $k$ decide similarity of matrices with split characteristic polynomial, by the uniqueness part of the [[Jordan normal form|085HQ5G5TE]].
Here the ranks of the squares already separate the two matrices.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two matrices can agree in several numbers and still not be similar.
Both of these matrices are built from three ladders with seven rungs in all, and the tallest ladder has three rungs in each.
But one is built as $3 + 3 + 1$ and the other as $3 + 2 + 2$.
After two steps down, the first has two rungs left and the second has only one, and that difference shows that they are not similar.

### L2 human

The test that works is to compare the ranks of the powers $N, N^2, N^3$, not only of $N$.
For $N_1$ these ranks are $4, 2, 0$, and for $N_2$ they are $4, 1, 0$.
The number of blocks of size at least $2$ is $\operatorname{rank} N - \operatorname{rank} N^2$, which is $2$ for $N_1$ and $3$ for $N_2$.
This matches the partitions: $(3, 3, 1)$ has two parts of size at least $2$, and $(3, 2, 2)$ has three.
The trap is to stop at the first rank, since the rank only counts the blocks.
