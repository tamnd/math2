## Summary

The thermal conductivity $k$ of a material measures how well it conducts heat.
It is the constant in [[Fourier's law|9YJZ1PP2Y8]] that links the [[heat flux|1Y36434YTE]] to the temperature gradient.
Its unit is the watt per metre per kelvin.
Good conductors such as copper have large $k$, and insulators such as foam have small $k$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two walls of the same thickness separate a warm room from the cold outside, one of brick and one of foam.
Much more heat leaks through the brick wall, because brick has a larger thermal conductivity.
The conductivity tells how many watts flow through a slab of area one square metre and thickness one metre when its faces differ by one kelvin.
Copper has a value of about $400$ in these units, glass about $1$, and still air about $0.025$.
