## Summary

The Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution is the distribution of molecular velocities in a dilute gas in thermal equilibrium.
Each velocity component, measured relative to the bulk velocity, is [[normally distributed|05AQE0QR0D]] with variance $kT/m$, where $T$ is the temperature and $m$ the molecular mass.
It is the equilibrium of the [[Boltzmann equation|MH6CSWD7F0]], the state in which collisions no longer change the distribution.
Maxwell found it in 1860, and Boltzmann later derived it from his kinetic equation.
See the [[definition|3QM3WDNAT7]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a gas at rest, molecules move in all directions with many different speeds.
Most have speeds near a typical value, and very few are very slow or very fast.
The Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution gives the exact proportions: a bell curve for each velocity component.
For nitrogen at room temperature the typical speed is a few hundred metres per second.
Hotter gas has a wider bell curve, so its molecules move faster on average.
