## Summary

A thermodynamic cycle is a sequence of processes that brings a system back to its initial state.
Since every [[state function|T8J5XRYBZZ]] returns to its starting value, the net [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] taken in over the cycle equals the net [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] done.
Cycles are the working principle of every [[heat engine|S7XFW04T3V]], refrigerator and heat pump.
Standard ideal cycles include the [[Carnot cycle|2DF8JBNKNG]], the [[Otto cycle|N8C4JXD90C]], the [[Diesel cycle|42WPJMQK0C]] and the [[Brayton cycle|9GZ4K1GA16]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a gas in a cylinder with a piston.
You heat it, it expands and pushes the piston; then you cool it and push the piston back to where it started.
The gas is now exactly as it was at the beginning, so you can repeat the process again and again.
Because the gas pushed harder while it was hot than you needed to push while it was cold, you gained some work each round.
On a pressure–volume diagram the cycle is a closed loop, and the work gained is the area inside the loop.
