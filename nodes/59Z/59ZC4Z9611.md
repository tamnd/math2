## Summary

A barrier function of a set is a continuous function on its interior that grows to $+\infty$ as one approaches the boundary.
Adding a small multiple of it to an objective replaces a constrained problem by a family of problems that are unconstrained on the interior.
The logarithmic barrier is the main example, and it drives the [[interior-point methods|M7HYT44ZJA]] of [[convex optimization|1W1BXJN9ER]].
It differs from a [[penalty method|AY9Q1VNDJB]], which works outside the feasible set.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a fence around the feasible region that is electrified more and more strongly as you get close to it.
A point that minimises "objective plus barrier" stays strictly inside, because the barrier is huge near the fence.
As the weight of the barrier shrinks, the minimiser can move closer to the fence, toward the true constrained optimum.
For the constraint $x \ge 0$ on the real line, the function $-\ln x$ is a barrier: it is finite for $x > 0$ and tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to 0^+$.
