## Summary

Critical phenomena are the behaviours of a physical system near a critical point.
Such a point is a point of a continuous phase transition, often the end point of a line of first-order transitions.
There the correlation length diverges, thermodynamic quantities follow power laws with [[critical exponents|SQ41AZGZ2P]], and the system looks the same at all scales.
These exponents depend only on a few features, such as dimension and symmetry, which is called universality.
The [[renormalization group|5XTSCZCF7Z]] explains these facts.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heat water in a closed container under high pressure, and at one special temperature and pressure the liquid and the vapour become the same.
Near this critical point the fluid turns milky, because droplets and bubbles of every size appear.
A magnet near its Curie temperature behaves in the same way, with patches of aligned spins of every size.
Many quantities then grow or shrink like powers of the distance to the critical temperature, such as $\abs{T - T_c}^{-1.24}$.
The surprise is that very different systems share the same powers.
