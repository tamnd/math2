## Example

We prove: for every [[integer|0C4JS9RF5M]] $n$, if $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd.
The definition we use is that an integer $x$ is odd when $x = 2m + 1$ for some integer $m$.
The proof is a [[direct proof|0NTF6DMVAP]]: it assumes the hypothesis and computes forward to the conclusion.

1. Let $n$ be an integer, and assume that $n$ is odd.
   This is the hypothesis.
2. By the definition of odd, there is an integer $k$ with $n = 2k + 1$.
3. Square both sides:
   $$n^2 = (2k + 1)^2 = 4k^2 + 4k + 1.$$
4. Take the factor $2$ out of the first two terms:
   $$n^2 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1.$$
5. Put $m = 2k^2 + 2k$.
   Then $m$ is an integer, because sums and products of integers are integers.
6. So $n^2 = 2m + 1$, and $n^2$ is odd by the definition of odd.

Check: $n = 5$ gives $k = 2$ and $m = 12$, and $2 \cdot 12 + 1 = 25 = 5^2$.

## Remarks

The proof shows the three moves of most direct proofs.
Step 2 unfolds the definition in the hypothesis, steps 3 and 4 compute, and steps 5 and 6 fold the result back into the definition of the conclusion.
At no point does the proof assume anything about $n^2$.

A change of the exponent keeps the method.
For the cube we get
$$(2k + 1)^3 = 8k^3 + 12k^2 + 6k + 1 = 2(4k^3 + 6k^2 + 3k) + 1,$$
so the cube of an odd integer is odd as well.

A change of direction changes the method.
The converse, "if $n^2$ is odd, then $n$ is odd", is also true, but a direct proof stalls, because $n^2 = 2m + 1$ gives no formula for $n$.
Its [[contrapositive|0BB5TPGQ1N]] is easy: if $n$ is even, then $n = 2k$, and $n^2 = 2(2k^2)$ is even.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the odd number $5$.
It is $2 \cdot 2 + 1$, one more than an even number.
Its square is $25 = 24 + 1$, again one more than an even number.
The proof does this calculation for a general odd number $2k + 1$.
The square is $4k^2 + 4k + 1$, and the first two terms together are even.
So the square is one more than an even number, which means it is odd.

### L2 human

Does the proof also cover negative integers?
Try $n = -7$, which is odd because $-7 = 2 \cdot (-4) + 1$, so $k = -4$.
The formula of step 5 gives
$$m = 2k^2 + 2k = 2 \cdot 16 - 8 = 24,$$
and $2m + 1 = 49 = (-7)^2$, as it should.
Nothing in the proof used that $k$ is positive, so every odd integer is covered.
The fact is often used through its contrapositive, "if $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even", which is a step in the proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.
Watch out: writing "$n = 2n + 1$" reuses the letter $n$ and gives the false equation $n = -1$, so the integer of step 2 must get a new letter.
