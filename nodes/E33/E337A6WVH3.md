## Summary

A residuated lattice is a [[lattice|WH4SFAACAS]] with a [[monoid|0N6R902FG1]] multiplication that has two division operations, the residuals; see [[the definition|S9Z6PKTXAS]].
The residuals are characterized by $x \cdot y \le z \iff y \le x \backslash z \iff x \le z / y$.
[[Heyting algebras|NG943XNQWH]], [[Boolean algebras|0XADJKM699]], lattice-ordered groups and the lattices of ideals of a ring are examples.
Residuated lattices are the algebraic semantics of substructural logics, where the multiplication is a conjunction and the residuals are implications.

## Motivation

In a substructural logic, a conjunction may fail to be idempotent or commutative, so it is not the lattice meet.
One still wants an implication that internalizes the conjunction: "$x$ and $y$ give $z$" iff "$y$ gives ($x$ implies $z$)".
The residuals are exactly such implications, on each side.
The lattice of ideals of a commutative ring was one of the original motivations.

## Notation

The multiplication is written $x \cdot y$, $x \otimes y$ or $x ; y$, and its unit $e$, $1$ or $1'$.
The two residuals are $x \backslash z$ and $z / y$; texts differ on which one they call left and which right.
In the commutative case both residuals agree, and one writes $x \to z$.
An *FL-algebra* is a residuated lattice with one more constant $0$, used to define the negations $x \backslash 0$ and $0 / x$; see Galatos, Jipsen, Kowalski and Ono.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. Every [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]], with $x \cdot y = x \land y$ and both residuals equal to $\to$.
2. [[The ideals of the integers|~7fd8cf5a]], with product of ideals and the ideal quotient.
3. [[The integers as an ordered group|~86ee724c]], with $\min$, $\max$, $+$ and subtraction.
4. [[MV-algebras|8T4M7RN9AC]] and [[BL-algebras|~d07e9350]], with strong conjunction and its residuum.
5. [[The formal languages over an alphabet|~92bb6b9d]], with concatenation; and [[the full relation algebra on a set|~1daf4bfe]], with composition.

### Non-examples

1. [[The natural numbers with multiplication|~7a14b73f]]: the residual $0 \backslash 0$ does not exist.
2. $[0, 1]$ with the drastic t-norm, $x \ast y = \min(x, y)$ if $\max(x, y) = 1$ and $0$ otherwise.
   For $x = 0.5$, $y = 0.2$, the set of $z$ with $z \ast 0.5 \le 0.2$ is $[0, 1)$, with no largest element.

### Basic properties

1. Each map $y \mapsto x \cdot y$ is the lower adjoint of a [[Galois connection|Q5JHEPRYQ6]], with upper adjoint $z \mapsto x \backslash z$.
2. So multiplication is monotone and distributes over every existing join.
3. Residuation can be written as equations, so residuated lattices form a variety.
4. Varieties of FL-algebras correspond, with inclusions reversed, to the axiomatic extensions of the full Lambek calculus; see Galatos, Jipsen, Kowalski and Ono.
5. A residuated lattice is *integral* if $e$ is the top, and *commutative* if $\cdot$ is; MV-algebras and BL-algebras are bounded, integral and commutative.

### History

Ward and Dilworth introduced residuated lattices in a 1939 paper titled "Residuated lattices", in the Transactions of the AMS, with the ideal lattices of rings in view.
The book of Galatos, Jipsen, Kowalski and Ono presents the subject as an algebraic view of substructural logics.

### Common errors

1. Assuming $x \cdot y = x \land y$. That holds in Heyting algebras, but not in ℓ-groups or MV-algebras.
2. Assuming $e$ is the top. In $\mathbb{Z}$ the unit is $0$, and there is no top.
3. Misreading "Kleene algebra" in action algebras, which are residuated semilattices that are also Kleene algebras. There the term means the [[algebra of regular expressions|BK4DDS24XC]], not the De Morgan notion.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the positive real numbers, $xy \le z$ holds exactly when $y \le z/x$.
So division gives the best answer to the question "how large can $y$ be so that $x$ times $y$ stays below $z$?".
A residuated lattice asks for such a best answer in an abstract ordered setting, from the left and from the right.
In logic, "$x$ and $y$ imply $z$" holds exactly when "$y$ implies ($x$ implies $z$)", and this is the same rule.

### L2 human

1. In [[the ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$|~7fd8cf5a]], take $x = 6\mathbb{Z}$ and $z = 4\mathbb{Z}$.
2. The residual $x \backslash z$ is the set of $r$ with $6r \in 4\mathbb{Z}$, which is $2\mathbb{Z}$.
3. Check: $6\mathbb{Z} \cdot 2\mathbb{Z} = 12\mathbb{Z} \subseteq 4\mathbb{Z}$, and every larger ideal $\mathbb{Z}$ gives $6\mathbb{Z} \not\subseteq 4\mathbb{Z}$.

The typical use: one proves a law for a substructural logic by an equation in a variety of residuated lattices.
The trap: the order in the ideal lattice is inclusion, so a larger number gives a smaller ideal.

### L3 human

A residuated lattice is a lattice-ordered monoid whose left and right translations have upper adjoints.
Complete residuated lattices are the [[quantales|DA619M38C3]] with a unit, and they model linear logic.
The variety of residuated lattices is congruence distributive, and congruences correspond to convex normal subalgebras; see Galatos, Jipsen, Kowalski and Ono.
Through this, the [[algebraizable logic|~58360ece]] machinery applies to all substructural logics at once.
