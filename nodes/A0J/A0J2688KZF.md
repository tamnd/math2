## Summary

Yang–Mills theory is the [[gauge theory|2GKJQNQMVN]] whose action is the [[Yang–Mills functional|CKGDR9P9PA]], the integral of the squared norm of the curvature of a connection.
For the circle group it is Maxwell's theory of light.
For a non-abelian group such as $SU(2)$ or $SU(3)$ the gauge fields interact with each other.
Chen-Ning Yang and Robert Mills introduced the non-abelian theory in 1954.
Quantum Yang–Mills theories describe the strong and weak forces, and giving a rigorous construction of them is a [[Millennium Prize Problem|VXC1Y32FD6]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Maxwell's theory of electricity and magnetism has a symmetry given by turning a phase, a single angle at each point.
Yang and Mills replaced this angle by a rotation in a group where the order of rotations matters, such as the group of $2 \times 2$ unitary matrices.
The answer is a theory with several kinds of field quanta, which carry the very charge they respond to, so they attract or repel one another.
This self-interaction makes the theory nonlinear and much richer than electromagnetism.
The same equations also turned out to be powerful tools in pure geometry.
