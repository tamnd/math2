## Definition

Let $F$ be a field of characteristic not $2$, and let $W(F)$ be its [[Witt ring|KFVY0RPGF2]].
The map $e_0 \colon W(F) \to \Z/2\Z$ sends the class of a nondegenerate [[quadratic form|0RBQS1JJCR]] $q$ to $\dim q$ modulo $2$.
It is a well-defined surjective [[ring homomorphism|0VTH8KPCG2]].
The *fundamental ideal* of $W(F)$ is its kernel
$$I(F) = \ker(e_0) = \set{[q] \in W(F) : \dim q \text{ is even}}.$$
For $n \ge 1$, $I^n(F)$ denotes the $n$-th power of this [[ideal|096Z9MP2ME]], and $I^0(F) = W(F)$.

## Remarks

The map $e_0$ is well defined because the hyperbolic plane has dimension $2$, and it respects products because $\dim(q \otimes q') = \dim q \cdot \dim q'$.
For $a \in F^\times$ write $\langle\langle a \rangle\rangle = \langle 1, -a \rangle$, the form $x^2 - a y^2$.
The ideal $I(F)$ is generated as a group by the classes $\langle\langle a \rangle\rangle$.
Its power $I^n(F)$ is generated by the products $\langle\langle a_1 \rangle\rangle \cdots \langle\langle a_n \rangle\rangle$, the $n$-fold Pfister forms.
The signed discriminant gives an isomorphism $I(F)/I^2(F) \cong F^\times / (F^\times)^2$.
Example: $W(\R) \cong \Z$ by the signature, and $I(\R) = 2\Z$, since a form has even dimension exactly when its signature is even.
Non-example: the form $\langle 1 \rangle$, that is $x^2$, has odd dimension, so its class is not in $I(F)$ for any $F$.
