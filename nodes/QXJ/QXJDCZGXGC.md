## Summary

The modes of an optical fiber are the field patterns that travel along the fiber without changing shape.
In a step-index fiber, light is confined in a core of higher refractive index by total internal reflection at the cladding.
A guided mode has a propagation constant between the wavenumbers of the cladding and of the core, and decays exponentially in the cladding.
The number of guided modes is governed by the normalized frequency $V$.
For $V < 2.405$ only one mode is guided, which is the basis of single-mode fibers for long-distance communication.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An optical fiber is a thin glass thread with a core that slows light a little more than the outer layer.
Light bouncing inside the core at shallow angles is trapped there by total internal reflection.
Because of wave interference, only certain patterns of light, the modes, can travel steadily down the fiber.
If the core is thin enough, only one pattern fits, and all light travels at the same speed, so pulses stay sharp over hundreds of kilometres.
