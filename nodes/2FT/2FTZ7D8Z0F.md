## Summary

Angular velocity measures how fast and about which axis something turns.
In three dimensions it is a vector along the axis of rotation, oriented by the right-hand rule.
Its length is the rate of turning in radians per unit time.
For a turning body or frame, the [[velocity|K160D2Z0CS]] of each point at position $r$ from a point of the axis is the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]] $\omega \times r$.
It is the time derivative of a rotation, expressed through the Lie algebra of the [[rotation group|0CGYNDCWRR]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A record turning $33$ times a minute has an angular speed of $33 \cdot 2\pi$ radians per minute about its spindle.
Every point of the record turns at this same rate, but points far from the centre move faster, because they travel along bigger circles.
The angular velocity vector points along the spindle, up if the record turns counterclockwise seen from above.
The Earth has an angular velocity of one turn per day about the axis through its poles.
