## Example

Let $\alpha > 0$, $\rho_{-2} > 0$ and $r_{-2} > 0$.
The *Einasto profile* is the spherical density
$$\rho(r) = \rho_{-2} \exp\left( -\frac{2}{\alpha} \left[ \left( \frac{r}{r_{-2}} \right)^{\alpha} - 1 \right] \right).$$
Its logarithmic slope is
$$\frac{d \ln \rho}{d \ln r} = -2 \left( \frac{r}{r_{-2}} \right)^{\alpha},$$
so the slope varies continuously with radius and equals $-2$ exactly at $r = r_{-2}$, where $\rho = \rho_{-2}$.

The mass inside radius $r$, with $x = r/r_{-2}$, is
$$M(r) = \frac{4 \pi \rho_{-2} r_{-2}^3 e^{2/\alpha}}{\alpha} \left( \frac{\alpha}{2} \right)^{3/\alpha} \gamma\left( \frac{3}{\alpha}, \frac{2}{\alpha} x^{\alpha} \right),$$
where $\gamma$ is the lower [[incomplete gamma function|YB7ZV0HG7K]].
Check: substituting $u = \frac{2}{\alpha} s^\alpha$ in $\int_0^x e^{-2 s^\alpha/\alpha} s^2 \, ds$ gives $s^2 \, ds = \frac{1}{\alpha} \left( \frac{\alpha}{2} \right)^{3/\alpha} u^{3/\alpha - 1} \, du$.

## Remarks

As $r \to \infty$, $\gamma$ tends to the [[gamma function|0H5SYBSED9]] value $\Gamma(3/\alpha)$, so unlike the Navarro–Frenk–White profile the total mass is finite.
The central density is finite as well, equal to $\rho_{-2} e^{2/\alpha}$.
The profile has the same form as the Sérsic law for surface brightness, applied to a three-dimensional density.
Simulated dark matter halos are fitted by the Einasto profile with small values of $\alpha$, and the fit is often better than with the Navarro–Frenk–White profile.
The circular speed $\sqrt{G M(r)/r}$, with $G$ the gravitational constant, gives the [[rotation curve|V4FYBDSGMT]] of such a halo.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Einasto profile describes how the density of a galaxy or a dark matter halo falls off with distance from its centre.
Its special feature is that the steepness of the fall-off changes smoothly with radius, controlled by one shape number $\alpha$.
Near the centre it is almost flat, and far out it drops faster than any power of the radius.
