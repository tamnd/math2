## Summary

A rate equation, or rate law, expresses the speed of a chemical reaction as a function of the concentrations of the species involved.
In the common power-law form, the rate is a rate constant times a product of powers of concentrations.
The exponents are the orders of the reaction in each species, and they are found by experiment.
Rate equations turn chemistry into [[ordinary differential equations|0NT64JV96A]] for the concentrations, which is the starting point of chemical kinetics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A reaction goes faster when its ingredients are more crowded, because molecules meet more often.
The rate equation says exactly how the speed depends on the amounts present.
For a decay $A \to B$ with rate $k[A]$, the concentration obeys $d[A]/dt = -k[A]$.
Its solution $[A](t) = [A]_0 e^{-kt}$ halves every $\ln 2 / k$ units of time.
So a first-order reaction behaves like radioactive decay.
