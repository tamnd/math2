## Definition

Let $F$ be a field and let $(V_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of [[vector spaces|05EGHEQZAW]] over $F$.
The *direct product* $\prod_{i \in I} V_i$ is the set of all families $(v_i)_{i \in I}$ with $v_i \in V_i$ for each $i \in I$, with the operations
$$(v_i)_i + (w_i)_i = (v_i + w_i)_i, \qquad a (v_i)_i = (a v_i)_i$$
for $a \in F$.
It is a vector space over $F$ whose zero vector is the family $(0)_i$.
For $j \in I$, the *projection* $\pi_j \colon \prod_{i \in I} V_i \to V_j$ is $(v_i)_i \mapsto v_j$.

In particular, for two spaces $V$ and $W$, the product $V \times W$ is the set of pairs $(v, w)$ with
$$(v, w) + (v', w') = (v + v', w + w'), \qquad a(v, w) = (av, aw).$$

## Notation

We write $V_1 \times \cdots \times V_k$ for a finite family and $V^I$ for a product of copies of $V$.

## Remarks

Each vector space axiom holds in the product because it holds in each factor, entry by entry.
The projections $\pi_j$ are linear maps.
For a finite family, the product is also the [[external direct sum|0S7W6EG32V]], written $V \oplus W$.
The [[dimension of a direct product|06HN47VPTH]] is $\dim V + \dim W$, and for example $F^m \times F^n$ is isomorphic to $F^{m+n}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The product of vector spaces has vectors that are lists: one vector from each space.
We add lists entry by entry and scale every entry.
The plane $\R^2$ is the product of two copies of the line $\R$.

### L2 human

The space $\R^{\N}$ of all real sequences is the product of countably many copies of $\R$.
The sequence $(1, 1, 1, \dots)$ is a vector of it, and so is $(1, 2, 3, \dots)$.
Their sum is $(2, 3, 4, \dots)$, computed entry by entry.
