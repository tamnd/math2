## Definition

Let $x_1, \dots, x_n > 0$ be incomes with mean $\mu = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i$, and let $\varepsilon \ge 0$ be the inequality aversion parameter.
For $\varepsilon \ne 1$, the *Atkinson index* is
$$A_\varepsilon = 1 - \frac{1}{\mu} \left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^{1 - \varepsilon} \right)^{1/(1 - \varepsilon)}.$$
For $\varepsilon = 1$ it is
$$A_1 = 1 - \frac{1}{\mu} \left( \prod_{i=1}^n x_i \right)^{1/n}.$$
The subtracted term is $y_\varepsilon/\mu$, where $y_\varepsilon$ is the power mean of order $1 - \varepsilon$, called the equally distributed equivalent income.
For $\varepsilon = 1$ this power mean is the geometric mean, which is its limit as $\varepsilon \to 1$.

## Remarks

Example: incomes $1$ and $4$ with $\varepsilon = 1$.
The mean is $5/2$ and the geometric mean is $\sqrt{4} = 2$, so $A_1 = 1 - 2/(5/2) = 1/5$.

Non-example: the parameter $\varepsilon = -1$ is not allowed.
For incomes $1$ and $4$ it would give $1 - \sqrt{17/2}/(5/2) \approx -0.17$, a negative value, because the power mean of order $2$ exceeds the mean.

For $\varepsilon \ge 0$, the [[power mean inequality|XZ1EXQPJ6S]] gives $y_\varepsilon \le \mu$, so $0 \le A_\varepsilon < 1$.
The case $\varepsilon = 1$ is the [[inequality of arithmetic and geometric means|0DRF0F3XC9]].
Equality $A_\varepsilon = 0$ holds for $\varepsilon > 0$ exactly when all incomes are equal, and $A_0 = 0$ always.
The index does not change when all incomes are multiplied by the same positive number.
The case $\varepsilon = 1$ uses the [[natural logarithm|04DXAFN2ES]] implicitly: $\ln y_1$ is the average of the $\ln x_i$.
