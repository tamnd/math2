## Summary

The configuration space of a mechanical system is the set of all positions that the system can take.
A point of it records the position of every part of the system at one instant.
For systems with independent [[holonomic constraints|AXA8HN8Y45]] it is a [[smooth manifold|F45VQ47GPV]], and a motion of the system is a curve in it.
Its dimension is the number of degrees of freedom, and its geometry carries much of the mechanics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A single particle moving freely in space has configuration space $\R^3$: a position is a point.
A planar pendulum on a rigid rod can only sit on a circle, so its configuration space is a circle.
A double pendulum in the plane needs two angles, so its configuration space is a torus, the surface of a doughnut.
Watching the system move is the same as watching one point run along a curve in this space.
