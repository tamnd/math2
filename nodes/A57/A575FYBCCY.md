## Summary

The Dennis–Stein symbol $\langle a, b \rangle$ is an element of $K_2(R)$ attached to two elements $a, b$ of a commutative ring $R$ such that $1 + ab$ is a unit.
Unlike the Steinberg symbol, it does not require $a$ or $b$ to be units.
It is the convenient kind of symbol for rings with many non-units, such as local rings and rings with nilpotent elements.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Steinberg symbol needs two invertible elements.
In a ring like $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, the interesting elements, such as $\varepsilon$, are not invertible.
The Dennis–Stein symbol needs only that $1 + ab$ is invertible, which is true for $a = \varepsilon$ and any $b$.
It is built from a short product of generators of the Steinberg group whose image is a diagonal matrix, corrected by the standard lift of that diagonal matrix.
