## Definition

For $a \in \R$ and an integer $n \ge 0$, the *Macaulay bracket* is
$$\langle x - a \rangle^n = \begin{cases} (x - a)^n & \text{if } x \ge a, \\ 0 & \text{if } x < a, \end{cases}$$
so that $\langle x - a \rangle^0$ is the [[Heaviside step function|09C4JP7Y0M]] $H(x - a)$.
It satisfies $\int \langle x - a \rangle^n \, dx = \langle x - a \rangle^{n+1}/(n+1) + C$ for $n \ge 0$.
Let a beam with constant bending stiffness $EI$ occupy $0 \le x \le L$.
Let it carry point forces, point couples and piecewise polynomial distributed loads, and have deflection $w$ in the direction of the loads.
*Macaulay's method* is the following procedure.

1. Write the [[bending moment|Q6XAPXMGH0]] $M(x)$ on all of $[0, L]$ as one sum of terms $c \, \langle x - a \rangle^n$, a group for each load and reaction.
2. Integrate $EI w'' = -M$ twice term by term with the rule above, which introduces two constants $C_1$ and $C_2$.
3. Determine $C_1$ and $C_2$ from the support conditions.

## Remarks

Example: a simply supported beam with a load $P$ at $x = a$, and $b = L - a$, has $M(x) = (Pb/L) x - P\langle x - a \rangle$.
Integrating gives $EI w = -\frac{Pb}{6L} x^3 + \frac{P}{6} \langle x - a \rangle^3 + C_1 x + C_2$.
The condition $w(0) = 0$ gives $C_2 = 0$, and $w(L) = 0$ gives $C_1 = Pb(L^2 - b^2)/(6L)$.
For $a = b = L/2$ this gives the mid-span deflection $w(L/2) = PL^3/(48EI)$.

Non-example: the bracket $\langle x - a \rangle^n$ is not the ordinary power $(x - a)^n$.
Using ordinary parentheses would switch the load on along the whole beam and give a wrong moment to the left of $a$.

The method relies on the continuity of $w$ and $w'$ at the load points, which the brackets with $n \ge 1$ build in automatically.
