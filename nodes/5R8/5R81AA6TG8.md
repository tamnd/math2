## Definition

A *fitness landscape* is a triple $(X, G, f)$, where:

1. $X$ is a set of genotypes;
2. $G$ is a [[graph|0JD5C82QMB]] with vertex set $X$, in which $x$ and $y$ are adjacent when one mutation turns $x$ into $y$;
3. $f \colon X \to \R$ is the fitness function.

In the usual case $X = A^L$ is the set of sequences of length $L$ over a finite alphabet $A$.
Then $x, y$ are adjacent when their [[Hamming distance|K5287V64FH]] is $1$.
A genotype $x$ is a *local peak* if $f(x) > f(y)$ for every neighbour $y$ of $x$ in $G$.
So a local peak is a strict [[local maximum|00999TYPXB]] of $f$ on the graph.

## Remarks

Example: $X = \set{0, 1}^2$ with $f(00) = 1$, $f(01) = f(10) = 0.8$, $f(11) = 1.2$; both $00$ and $11$ are local peaks, since each is fitter than its two neighbours $01$ and $10$.
Non-example: a map $f \colon X \to \R$ without the graph $G$ is not a fitness landscape in this sense, since clause 2 is missing and local peaks are not defined.
A landscape is *additive* if $f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^L f_i(x_i)$; generic additive landscapes on $\set{0,1}^L$ have exactly one local peak.
