## Summary

Spontaneous magnetization is the [[magnetization|A2WZA4QA3V]] that a ferromagnet keeps when the external field is switched off.
It is zero above the critical temperature and positive below it, so it is the [[order parameter|XSA203109Z]] of the ferromagnetic transition.
In the [[Ising model|R0KGM32QN6]] it is defined as a limit of the magnetization as the field tends to zero after the thermodynamic limit.
Its behaviour near the critical temperature defines the critical exponent $\beta$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A piece of iron placed in a strong magnetic field becomes a magnet.
Remove the field, and below a certain temperature the iron stays a magnet on its own.
This leftover magnetization is the spontaneous magnetization.
It comes from the atoms keeping each other aligned, without help from outside.
Heat the iron above about $770$ degrees Celsius, the Curie temperature, and the spontaneous magnetization disappears.
