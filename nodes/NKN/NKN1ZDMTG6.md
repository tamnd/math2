## Summary

The normal modes, or free oscillations, of the Earth are the standing elastic-gravitational vibrations of the whole planet.
After a great earthquake the Earth rings like a bell for days, at a discrete set of frequencies.
The modes come in two families, spheroidal and toroidal, labelled by an overtone number and a [[spherical harmonic|3F32Y21HP4]] degree.
Their frequencies constrain the density and elastic structure of the whole Earth, including the inner core.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bell struck with a hammer keeps sounding at its own notes, whatever the hammer did.
The Earth does the same after a very large earthquake, but its notes are very low: the deepest one has a period of about $54$ minutes.
Some notes squeeze and stretch the planet, like a ball being squashed into an egg and back; these are spheroidal.
Others twist the shells of the planet against each other; these are toroidal.
These vibrations were first recorded beyond doubt after the great Chilean earthquake of 1960.
