## Summary

The Wang–Landau algorithm is a [[Monte Carlo method|DR0E829VX6]] that estimates the density of states $g(E)$, the number of configurations with energy $E$.
It performs a random walk in configuration space that is biased by the current estimate of $1/g(E)$.
It updates this estimate as it goes, until all energies are visited about equally often.
From $g(E)$ one obtains the partition function and thermal averages of energy functions at every temperature from a single run.
It is useful for systems with rough energy landscapes and for first-order transitions, where ordinary sampling at fixed temperature gets stuck.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Most configurations of a system have typical energies, and very few have very low or very high ones.
A walk that favours rare energies can visit all of them.
The Wang–Landau algorithm learns how rare each energy is while walking: every time it visits an energy it marks that energy as more common and so less attractive.
When the walk visits all energies equally often, the marks it has collected are an estimate of how many configurations each energy has.
Knowing these counts, one can compute the thermodynamics at any temperature by a simple sum.
