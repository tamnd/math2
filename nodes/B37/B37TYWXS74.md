## Summary

In mathematical logic (a subtopic within the field of formal logic), two formulae are **equisatisfiable** if the first formula is satisfiable whenever the second is and vice versa; in other words, either both formulae are satisfiable or neither is. The truth values of two equisatisfiable formulae may nevertheless disagree for a particular assignment of variables. As a result, equisatisfiability differs from [[logical equivalence|06GYGS41JK]], since two equivalent formulae always have the same models, whereas equisatisfiable ones need only share satisfiability status.
