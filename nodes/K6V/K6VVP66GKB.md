## Summary

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra with a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form.
Its Casimir element is a quadratic element of the centre of the [[universal enveloping algebra|SCZ8PRVY00]] $U(\mathfrak{g})$.
It acts by a scalar on each irreducible finite-dimensional module of a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]].
For the Killing form, this scalar is nonzero on every nontrivial irreducible module.
This makes the Casimir element a key tool in the proof of [[Weyl's theorem on complete reducibility|92EXQ237Q6]].
In physics, the Casimir of the rotation algebra is the total angular momentum squared $J^2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a Lie algebra the elements usually do not commute with each other.
The Casimir element is a special sum of squares that commutes with everything.
Because of that, it acts on an irreducible representation as multiplication by one number.
For the rotations of space, with the normalisation used in physics, this number is $j(j+1)$ on the representation of spin $j$, the familiar eigenvalue of $J^2$ in quantum mechanics.
