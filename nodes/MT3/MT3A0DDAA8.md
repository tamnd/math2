## Summary

The Rossby radius of deformation is a horizontal length scale of rotating fluids.
At this scale the effects of the Earth's rotation become as important as those of buoyancy or of the free surface.
It equals a gravity wave speed divided by the Coriolis parameter.
Motions larger than this scale are close to [[geostrophic balance|CZ1S1P465Y]].
It sets the typical size of weather systems in the atmosphere and of eddies in the ocean.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A disturbance in a fluid spreads out as gravity waves, while the rotation of the planet tries to turn the flow sideways.
The deformation radius is the distance a gravity wave travels in the time the rotation needs to take control.
In the atmosphere it is about $1000$ km, the size of a typical storm system in the mid-latitudes.
In the ocean the internal version is only tens of kilometres, which is why ocean eddies are much smaller than weather systems.
