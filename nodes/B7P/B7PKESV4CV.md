## Summary

The Strouhal number $\mathrm{St} = f L / U$ is a [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] for unsteady flows.
It compares the frequency of the flow with the rate at which the flow carries fluid past an object.
It describes oscillating flows, above all the periodic shedding of vortices behind a bluff body.
For a circular cylinder, vortex shedding happens at a Strouhal number close to $0.2$ over a wide range of flow speeds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A flag flutters in the wind and a wire hums in a breeze, because the air leaves the object in a regular pattern of swirls.
The Strouhal number links how often this happens to the wind speed and the size of the object.
If the Strouhal number is about $0.2$, a wire $1\ \mathrm{cm}$ thick in a wind of $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$ sheds about $200$ swirls per second, which you can hear.
