## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring in which $2$ is invertible, for example a field of characteristic not $2$.
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $R$.
Then there are a unique [[symmetric matrix|0N28QN4CYA]] $S$ and a unique [[skew-symmetric matrix|03RNVFDEAN]] $K$ with $A = S + K$.
They are
$$S = \tfrac12 \left(A + A^{\mathsf{T}}\right), \qquad K = \tfrac12 \left(A - A^{\mathsf{T}}\right).$$

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The number $2$ must be invertible. Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ we have $-1 = 1$, so every skew-symmetric matrix is symmetric.
- Then $S + K$ is always symmetric over $\mathbb{F}_2$. So $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, which is not symmetric, has no decomposition.
- Uniqueness also fails over $\mathbb{F}_2$: $I = I + 0 = 0 + I$, and $I$ is both symmetric and skew-symmetric there.
- Over $\mathbb{Z}$, $2$ is not invertible, and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ again has no decomposition, because $S$ would need $s_{12} = s_{21} = \tfrac12$.

### Consequences

- As vector spaces, $M_n(F) = \operatorname{Sym}_n(F) \oplus \operatorname{Skew}_n(F)$ when $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$.
- Counting dimensions gives $n^2 = \tfrac{n(n+1)}{2} + \tfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$.
- The maps $A \mapsto S$ and $A \mapsto K$ are [[idempotent|0KCE8CG86P]] linear maps on $M_n(F)$, the projections onto the two parts.
- For real $A$ and every column vector $x$, $x^{\mathsf{T}} A x = x^{\mathsf{T}} S x$. So a quadratic form only sees the symmetric part.

### Generalizations

- The same proof splits each element of a module with an involution into a $+1$ part and a $-1$ part, when $2$ is invertible.
- For complex matrices, the [[Cartesian decomposition|E2MNC3W2DM]] $A = H + iK$ is the Hermitian analogue.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every square matrix is half a symmetric matrix plus half a skew-symmetric matrix.
The symmetric part averages each entry with its mirror entry.
The skew part takes half their difference.

### L2 human

The transpose is a linear map $T$ on $M_n(F)$ with $T^2 = \mathrm{id}$.
The symmetric matrices are the vectors with $T A = A$ and the skew-symmetric ones those with $T A = -A$.
The formulas for $S$ and $K$ are the usual splitting into the two eigenspaces of an involution.
