## Summary

Geophysical fluid dynamics is the part of [[fluid dynamics|F2JRWJNYN4]] that studies large-scale flows of the atmosphere, the oceans and other planetary fluids.
Two effects dominate these flows: the rotation of the planet, through the Coriolis force, and stable density stratification, through buoyancy.
Typical results are [[geostrophic balance|CZ1S1P465Y]], [[Rossby waves|6N1MQMA9TM]], [[internal waves|GPKN5XHJ1F]] and [[Ekman layers|H5GNFWEW80]].
It supplies the theory behind weather prediction and climate models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Winds and ocean currents are flows on a spinning planet.
Over a few metres the spin of the Earth does not matter, but over hundreds of kilometres and several days it changes everything.
For example, air does not flow straight from high to low pressure; it circles around the pressure centres, which is why weather maps show swirling storms.
Geophysical fluid dynamics studies such effects of rotation and of layering by density.
