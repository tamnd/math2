## Definition

Let $g(x) \in \F_2[x]$ be a polynomial of degree $r \ge 1$ over the [[finite field|0ZXZ82FTX2]] $\F_2$, the generator polynomial.
Identify a message $m = (m_{k-1}, \dots, m_1, m_0)$ of $k$ bits with the polynomial $m(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} m_i x^i$ in the [[polynomial ring|0SDC934R52]] $\F_2[x]$.

1. The *CRC* of $m$ is the remainder $\rho(x)$ of the [[polynomial division|D6D0SR1FK3]] of $x^r m(x)$ by $g(x)$, written as a word of $r$ bits.
2. The sent word is the polynomial $s(x) = x^r m(x) + \rho(x)$, that is, the message followed by its CRC.
3. A received word $y(x)$ passes the check if $g(x)$ divides $y(x)$, and an error is reported otherwise.

## Remarks

Since $-\rho = \rho$ in $\F_2[x]$, the sent word $s(x)$ is divisible by $g(x)$, so it passes the check.
An error pattern $e(x)$ goes undetected exactly when $g(x)$ divides $e(x)$.
If $g$ has a nonzero constant term, every burst of errors of length at most $r$ is detected.
Such an error is $x^i b(x)$ with $b(0) = 1$ and $\deg b < r$, and $g$ cannot divide it.
Example: let $g(x) = x^3 + x + 1$ and $m = 1101$, so $m(x) = x^3 + x^2 + 1$.
Dividing $x^3 m(x) = x^6 + x^5 + x^3$ by $g(x)$ leaves the remainder $1$, so the CRC is $001$ and the sent word is $1101001$.
Non-example of a valid sent word: flipping the third bit gives $1111001$, which is not divisible by $g(x)$, since its remainder is $x^2 + x$; the check reports the error.
Suppose $g$ divides $x^n - 1$ and $k + r \le n$.
Then each sent word, padded with zeros to length $n$, is a codeword of the [[cyclic code|E04GGZ1Q91]] of length $n$ generated by $g$.
So a CRC is a shortened cyclic code.
