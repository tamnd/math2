## Summary

Kingman's coalescent is the random tree in which, going back in time, each pair of lineages merges at rate $1$.
It is the limit of the genealogies of samples in many neutral population models, such as the Wright–Fisher and Moran models.
In this limit time is scaled by the population size.
It is the central model of [[coalescent theory|2W8Y2Y1C8J]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start with $n$ lineages and run time backward.
While there are $k$ lineages, wait a random time, short when $k$ is large, and then merge two of them chosen at random.
Repeat until one lineage is left.
With $n = 2$, the waiting time is exponential with mean $1$.
With many lineages the first mergers come fast.
The last merger, of two lineages, takes on average at least half of the expected depth of the tree.
