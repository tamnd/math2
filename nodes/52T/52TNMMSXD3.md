## Summary

The theory of computation is the study of models of computation and of the resources, such as time, space and randomness, that computational problems need.
A complexity class is the set of problems that a model of computation solves within a given bound on a resource, and the class P, for example, holds the decision problems that a deterministic Turing machine solves in polynomial time.
The area tells which problems have efficient algorithms, it gives the basis for the security of modern cryptography, and its automata and grammars are in use in compilers and in program verification.
Finite automata and the Chomsky hierarchy date from the 1950s, Hartmanis and Stearns defined time complexity classes in 1965, and Cook in 1971 and Levin in 1973 proved that satisfiability is NP-complete.
This topic collects the theory of computation pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Computability theory, mathematical cryptography, coding theory and the mathematics of machine learning have their own topics.
