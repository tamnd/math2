## Example

Let $M = \set{0, a, b, 1}$ with $0 < a, b < 1$, $a$ and $b$ incomparable, $\sim 0 = 1$, $\sim a = a$ and $\sim b = b$.
It is a [[De Morgan algebra|5Q53XWV0QN]].

1. The Kleene condition of [[the definition|GN34CR1AH1]] asks $x \land \sim x \le y \lor \sim y$ for all $x, y$.
2. Take $x = a$ and $y = b$. Then $a \land \sim a = a$ and $b \lor \sim b = b$.
3. But $a \not\le b$.

So no operation $\to$ makes $M$ a [[Nelson algebra|XRGDT262HQ]].

## Remarks

1. $M$ is the algebra of Belnap's four values, used for [[first-degree entailment|KXTP4P3GHT]].
2. The twist structures over Heyting algebras without the condition $a \land b = 0$ include $M$: over $H = \set{0, 1}$, all four pairs give $M$, with $a = (1, 1)$ and $b = (0, 0)$. These are the algebras of the paraconsistent logic $\mathbf{N4}$.
3. Every Kleene algebra satisfies the condition, so a De Morgan algebra fails it iff it is not a Kleene algebra.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Belnap's four values are true, false, both and neither.
A Nelson algebra forbids the value "both".
The Kleene condition expresses this: it says that "$x$ and not $x$" is never above "$y$ or not $y$".

### L2 human

1. In $M$, $a \land \sim a = a$ and $b \lor \sim b = b$.
2. The values $a$ and $b$ are incomparable.
3. So $a \le b$ fails, and the Kleene condition fails.

The typical use: the example marks the line between Nelson's logic and its paraconsistent variant.
The trap: $M$ fails the condition even though it is a distributive lattice with an involution.
