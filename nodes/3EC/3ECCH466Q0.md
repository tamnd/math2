## Definition

Consider spin-$1/2$ electrons with creation and annihilation operators $c^\dagger_{k\sigma}, c_{k\sigma}$ for momentum $k$ and spin $\sigma \in \set{\uparrow, \downarrow}$, single-particle energies $\varepsilon_k$, chemical potential $\mu$ and $\xi_k = \varepsilon_k - \mu$.
The *reduced BCS Hamiltonian* is
$$H = \sum_{k,\sigma} \xi_k \, c^\dagger_{k\sigma} c_{k\sigma} - \sum_{k, k'} V_{kk'} \, c^\dagger_{k\uparrow} c^\dagger_{-k\downarrow} c_{-k'\downarrow} c_{k'\uparrow},$$
where, in the BCS model, $V_{kk'} = V > 0$ if $\abs{\xi_k} \le \hbar\omega_D$ and $\abs{\xi_{k'}} \le \hbar\omega_D$, and $V_{kk'} = 0$ otherwise, for a cutoff energy $\hbar\omega_D$.
The *BCS state* is
$$\lvert \Psi \rangle = \prod_k \big(u_k + v_k \, c^\dagger_{k\uparrow} c^\dagger_{-k\downarrow}\big) \lvert 0 \rangle, \qquad \abs{u_k}^2 + \abs{v_k}^2 = 1.$$
Minimising the energy over such states at zero temperature gives the *gap equation*
$$\Delta_k = \sum_{k'} V_{kk'} \frac{\Delta_{k'}}{2E_{k'}}, \qquad E_k = \sqrt{\xi_k^2 + \abs{\Delta_k}^2},$$
where $E_k$ is the energy of a quasiparticle excitation.

## Remarks

In the BCS model $\Delta_k = \Delta$ is constant in the shell $\abs{\xi_k} \le \hbar\omega_D$.
Replacing the sum by an integral with density of states $N(0)$ per spin at the [[Fermi surface|GK3SVTXXKX]], a nonzero solution satisfies $1 = N(0)V \int_0^{\hbar\omega_D} d\xi / \sqrt{\xi^2 + \Delta^2}$.
So $\Delta = \hbar\omega_D / \sinh\big(1/(N(0)V)\big)$, which is close to $2\hbar\omega_D e^{-1/(N(0)V)}$ for weak coupling.
At temperature $T > 0$ the factor $1/(2E_k)$ becomes $\tanh(E_k / 2k_B T)/(2E_k)$, and $\Delta$ vanishes at $T_c$ with $k_B T_c \approx 1.13\, \hbar\omega_D e^{-1/(N(0)V)}$.

Example: $\Delta = 0$ always solves the gap equation; it is the normal state, a filled Fermi sea with $v_k = 1$ for $\xi_k < 0$ and $v_k = 0$ for $\xi_k > 0$.
For $V > 0$ the nonzero solution has lower energy, however small $V$ is.

Non-example: for a repulsive interaction, $V < 0$, the right side of $1 = N(0)V \int d\xi / \sqrt{\xi^2 + \Delta^2}$ is negative, so the only solution is $\Delta = 0$ and there is no superconductivity.
