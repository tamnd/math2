## Summary

The Spence signaling model is a model of a labour market in which workers know their own productivity and employers do not.
Workers can acquire education, which does not raise productivity but is cheaper for the more productive workers.
Because of this cost difference, education can act as a credible signal of productivity.
It is the founding example of a [[signaling game|DNVNS5HNYR]] and a basic model of the economics of information.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine two kinds of job seekers, good ones and average ones, who look the same to an employer.
A diploma does not make anyone better at the job, but it costs the good workers less effort to get.
If the diploma requirement is set just right, only good workers find it worth getting, so the diploma tells the employer who is who.
For example, let a good worker produce $2$ and an average worker produce $1$.
Let education level $e$ cost $e$ to an average worker but $e/2$ to a good one.
Then a requirement $e = 3/2$ separates the two kinds.
An average worker would get $2 - 3/2 = 1/2$ with the diploma and $1$ without it, so it skips the diploma.
A good worker gets $2 - 3/4 = 5/4$ with it and $1$ without it, so it takes the diploma.
