## Summary

The Bonnor–Ebert mass is the largest mass of an isothermal gas sphere in stable [[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]] under a given external pressure.
A more massive sphere at the same temperature and external pressure must collapse.
It is the analogue of the Jeans mass for a cloud of finite size.
It is used to judge whether dense cores in molecular clouds will form stars.
The equilibrium spheres themselves are called Bonnor–Ebert spheres.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a ball of cold gas held together by its own gravity and squeezed by warmer gas around it.
If the ball is light, its internal pressure balances gravity and the outside squeeze.
Adding more mass makes gravity stronger, and at some point no balanced state is stable any more.
That critical mass is the Bonnor–Ebert mass; it is larger for warmer gas and smaller for higher outside pressure.
