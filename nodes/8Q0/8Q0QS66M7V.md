## Summary

The Hartree–Fock method approximates the ground state of a system of many electrons by a single Slater determinant, an antisymmetrised product of one-electron orbitals.
It chooses the orbitals that minimise the energy, by the [[variational method|EYARCVGB0V]], so its energy is an upper bound for the true ground state energy.
The minimising orbitals solve a nonlinear eigenvalue problem, the Hartree–Fock equations, in which each electron feels the mean field of the others.
It is the starting point of most methods of quantum chemistry, and the difference from the exact energy is called the correlation energy.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The motion of many interacting electrons is far too complicated to solve exactly.
Hartree–Fock pretends that each electron moves in the average field created by all the others, while still respecting the Pauli principle.
One guesses orbitals, computes the average field, finds new orbitals in that field, and repeats until nothing changes: a self-consistent field.
For the helium atom this already gives the ground state energy to within about one and a half percent.
