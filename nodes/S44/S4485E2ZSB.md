## Definition

Let $G$ be a finite [[cyclic group|0KVGH0J5B3]] of order $q$, written multiplicatively, with generator $g$.
*ElGamal encryption* over $(G, g)$ is the [[public-key|01E9MNJYEX]] scheme with the following three algorithms.

1. Key generation: choose $x$ uniformly in $\set{0, 1, \dots, q - 1}$; the private key is $x$ and the public key is $h = g^x$.
2. Encryption of a message $m \in G$: choose $y$ uniformly in $\set{0, 1, \dots, q - 1}$, afresh for each message, and output the ciphertext $(c_1, c_2) = (g^y, m h^y)$.
3. Decryption of $(c_1, c_2)$: output $c_2 \, (c_1^x)^{-1}$.

## Remarks

Decryption is correct, since $c_1^x = g^{xy} = h^y$, so $c_2 (c_1^x)^{-1} = m h^y h^{-y} = m$.

Example: $G = (\Z/11\Z)^\times$, of order $q = 10$, with generator $g = 2$.
With $x = 3$ the public key is $h = 8$.
To encrypt $m = 5$ with $y = 2$: $c_1 = 4$ and $c_2 = 5 \cdot 64 \equiv 5 \cdot 9 = 45 \equiv 1 \pmod{11}$.
To decrypt: $c_1^x = 64 \equiv 9$, whose inverse modulo $11$ is $5$, and $1 \cdot 5 = 5 = m$.

Non-example: if the same $y$ is used for two messages $m$ and $m'$, clause 2 fails.
Then $c_2 / c_2' = m / m'$, so knowing one message reveals the other.

Recovering $x$ from $h$ is the [[discrete logarithm|0HJHYMGN3C]] problem in $G$.
If the decisional [[Diffie–Hellman|01TKKESHSB]] assumption holds in $G$, the scheme is semantically secure.
For this one takes $G$ of prime order, for example a subgroup of prime order of $(\Z/p\Z)^\times$.
