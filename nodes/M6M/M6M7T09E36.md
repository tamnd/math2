## Proof

We assume a truth formula, build the liar sentence for it with the diagonal lemma, and derive a contradiction, first in the model $\mathcal{N}$ and then in a theory $T$.

### The semantic form

1. Assume, for a contradiction, that a formula $\mathrm{True}(x)$ satisfies $\mathcal{N} \models \mathrm{True}(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \iff \mathcal{N} \models \varphi$ for every sentence $\varphi$.
2. Apply the [[diagonal lemma|3PRW76471J]] to the formula $\neg \mathrm{True}(x)$.
   It gives a sentence $\lambda$ with $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg \mathrm{True}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
3. $\mathcal{N}$ is a model of [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]], so by [[soundness|4A84VHDTHV]] the biconditional of step 2 is true in $\mathcal{N}$.
   That is, $\mathcal{N} \models \lambda$ exactly when $\mathcal{N} \not\models \mathrm{True}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
4. Step 1 with $\varphi := \lambda$ gives: $\mathcal{N} \models \mathrm{True}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$ exactly when $\mathcal{N} \models \lambda$.
5. Steps 3 and 4 give: $\mathcal{N} \models \lambda$ exactly when $\mathcal{N} \not\models \lambda$.
   This is impossible, so no formula $\mathrm{True}(x)$ as in step 1 exists.

### The syntactic form

6. Let $T \supseteq \mathsf{Q}$ be consistent, and assume that $T \vdash \mathrm{Tr}(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \varphi$ for every sentence $\varphi$.
7. The diagonal lemma applied to $\neg \mathrm{Tr}(x)$ gives $\lambda$ with $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg \mathrm{Tr}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
   Since $T$ contains $\mathsf{Q}$, $T$ proves the same biconditional.
8. Step 6 with $\varphi := \lambda$ gives $T \vdash \mathrm{Tr}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \lambda$.
9. Steps 7 and 8 give $T \vdash \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg \lambda$.
10. In propositional logic, $\lambda \leftrightarrow \neg \lambda$ implies both $\lambda$ and $\neg \lambda$.
    So $T$ proves a sentence and its negation, which contradicts the consistency of $T$ in step 6.

This proves both forms of [[Tarski's undefinability theorem|YRV6SYK2BY]].

## Idea

A truth formula would let arithmetic state the liar sentence "this sentence is not true".
The diagonal lemma builds this sentence, and the truth formula makes it equivalent to its own negation.
The two forms differ only in where the contradiction appears: in the model $\mathcal{N}$, or in the theory $T$.

## Variants

The proof uses the diagonal lemma and nothing about provability, so it is shorter than the proof of the first incompleteness theorem.
Step 3 uses only that $\mathcal{N}$ is a model of $\mathsf{Q}$.
So the same steps work in every model $M$ of $\mathsf{Q}$: no formula defines in $M$ the set of codes of sentences true in $M$.
With a [[partial truth predicate|Y2MF1EZAM7]] for $\Sigma_n$ in place of $\mathrm{True}$, the same steps show that the diagonal sentence is not a $\Sigma_n$ sentence, which is why such predicates exist without contradiction.
