## Proof

Semantic form.

1. Assume, for a contradiction, that $\mathrm{True}(x)$ satisfies $\mathcal{N} \models \mathrm{True}(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \iff \mathcal{N} \models \varphi$ for every sentence $\varphi$.
2. Apply the [[diagonal lemma|3PRW76471J]] to the formula $\neg \mathrm{True}(x)$. It gives a sentence $\lambda$ with $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg \mathrm{True}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
3. $\mathcal{N}$ is a model of $\mathsf{Q}$, so by [[soundness|4A84VHDTHV]], $\mathcal{N} \models \lambda \iff \mathcal{N} \models \neg \mathrm{True}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
4. By step 1 with $\varphi := \lambda$, $\mathcal{N} \models \mathrm{True}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \iff \mathcal{N} \models \lambda$.
5. Steps 3 and 4 give $\mathcal{N} \models \lambda \iff \mathcal{N} \not\models \lambda$, a contradiction. So no such formula exists.

Syntactic form.

6. Let $T \supseteq \mathsf{Q}$ be consistent, and assume that $T \vdash \mathrm{Tr}(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \varphi$ for every sentence $\varphi$.
7. The diagonal lemma gives $\lambda$ with $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg \mathrm{Tr}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$, so $T$ proves the same.
8. By step 6 with $\varphi := \lambda$, $T \vdash \mathrm{Tr}(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \lambda$.
9. Steps 7 and 8 give $T \vdash \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg \lambda$.
10. In propositional logic, $\lambda \leftrightarrow \neg \lambda$ implies $\lambda$ and $\neg \lambda$. So $T$ is inconsistent, against step 6.

## Idea

A truth predicate would let arithmetic form the liar sentence "this sentence is not true".
The diagonal lemma builds this sentence, and the truth predicate makes it equivalent to its own negation.
The two forms differ only in where the contradiction appears: in the model $\mathcal{N}$, or in the theory $T$.

## Variants

The proof uses only the diagonal lemma and no provability predicate, so it is shorter than the proof of the first incompleteness theorem.
The semantic proof works in every model $M$ of $\mathsf{Q}$ in place of $\mathcal{N}$: no formula defines in $M$ the set of codes of sentences true in $M$.
With a [[partial truth predicate|Y2MF1EZAM7]] in place of $\mathrm{True}$, the same diagonal sentence shows why such predicates cannot cover all sentences.
