## Proof

The plan: compare the two sides clause by clause, first on terms and then on quantifier-free formulas; only the variable cases need work.

Fix variables $x \neq y$ and terms $s, r$ with $x \notin \operatorname{Var}(r)$, and write $s' = s[r/y]$.
All substitutions are applied by the clauses of the [[definition of substitution|XN8TQE6SRK]].

1. Auxiliary claim: if $x \notin \operatorname{Var}(u)$, then $u[w/x] = u$ for every term $w$.
   We use [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on the [[term|6D3WA7N2W4]] $u$.
   A variable $z$ in $u$ satisfies $z \neq x$, so $z[w/x] = z$ by clause 1.
   A constant symbol is unchanged by clause 2.
   For $u = f(u_1, \ldots, u_n)$ we have $x \notin \operatorname{Var}(u_i)$ for each $i$, so clause 3 and the induction hypothesis give $u[w/x] = f(u_1, \ldots, u_n) = u$.
2. We prove $t[s/x][r/y] = t[r/y][s'/x]$ by structural induction on the term $t$.
   Steps 3 to 7 are the cases.
3. Case $t = x$.
   The left side is $x[s/x][r/y] = s[r/y] = s'$, by clause 1.
   On the right, $x[r/y] = x$ because $x \neq y$, and then $x[s'/x] = s'$.
   Both sides equal $s'$.
4. Case $t = y$.
   On the left, $y[s/x] = y$ because $y \neq x$, and then $y[r/y] = r$.
   On the right, $y[r/y] = r$, and then $r[s'/x] = r$ by step 1, since $x \notin \operatorname{Var}(r)$.
   Both sides equal $r$.
5. Case $t = z$, a variable different from $x$ and $y$.
   Each substitution leaves $z$ unchanged by clause 1, so both sides equal $z$.
6. Case $t = c$, a constant symbol.
   Both sides equal $c$ by clause 2.
7. Case $t = f(t_1, \ldots, t_n)$, where the claim holds for $t_1, \ldots, t_n$.
   By clause 3 applied twice, the left side is $f(t_1[s/x][r/y], \ldots, t_n[s/x][r/y])$ and the right side is $f(t_1[r/y][s'/x], \ldots, t_n[r/y][s'/x])$.
   The arguments agree by the induction hypothesis, so the two terms are equal.
8. By steps 3 to 7 and structural induction, part 1 of the statement holds for every term $t$.
9. For part 2 we use induction on quantifier-free formulas, which are built from atomic formulas by $\neg$ and the binary connectives, by the [[definition of formulas|2CHENV4DCC]].
10. Atomic case.
    For $\varphi = (t_1 = t_2)$, substitution acts on both terms, so the left side is $t_1[s/x][r/y] = t_2[s/x][r/y]$ and the right side is $t_1[r/y][s'/x] = t_2[r/y][s'/x]$.
    These agree by part 1.
    The case $R(t_1, \ldots, t_n)$ is the same, with $n$ terms.
11. Case $\neg \varphi$.
    Substitution commutes with $\neg$, so the left side is $\neg (\varphi[s/x][r/y])$ and the right side is $\neg (\varphi[r/y][s'/x])$.
    They agree by the induction hypothesis for $\varphi$.
12. Case $(\varphi \ast \psi)$ for a binary connective $\ast$.
    Substitution acts on both parts, and the parts agree by the induction hypothesis for $\varphi$ and $\psi$.
13. By steps 10 to 12 and induction on quantifier-free formulas, part 2 holds for every quantifier-free formula $\varphi$.
    This proves the lemma.

## Idea

Substitution is defined clause by clause, so both sides can be compared clause by clause.
Only the variables need work.
The variable $x$ turns into $s'$ on both sides, and the variable $y$ turns into $r$ on both sides.
The hypothesis $x \notin \operatorname{Var}(r)$ is what keeps $r$ unchanged on the right, in step 4.
The connectives and function symbols pass the equation through to their arguments.

## Variants

A second proof goes through composition of substitutions.
Both sides equal $t\theta$, where $\theta(x) = s'$, $\theta(y) = r$, and $\theta(z) = z$ for every other variable $z$.
This follows from the law $(t\theta_1)\theta_2 = t(\theta_1\theta_2)$, itself proved by induction on $t$, and from a computation of the two composite substitutions on the variables, as in steps 3 to 5.

The induction also extends to formulas with quantifiers, under the extra hypothesis that no variable of $s$ or $r$ is bound in $\varphi$.
For $\forall z \, \varphi$ there are three cases: $z = x$, $z = y$, or $z$ different from both.
If $z = x$, both sides become $\forall x \, (\varphi[r/y])$, since the substitution for $x$ stops at the quantifier.
If $z = y$, both sides become $\forall y \, (\varphi[s/x])$, since $y \notin \operatorname{Var}(s)$ gives $s' = s$.
If $z$ differs from both, $z$ occurs in neither $s$ nor $r$, so both substitutions pass the quantifier without capture and the induction hypothesis applies.
