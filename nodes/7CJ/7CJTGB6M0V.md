## Summary

Isostasy is the state of gravitational equilibrium in which the Earth's crust and lithosphere float on the denser, slowly flowing mantle.
In this state, at a certain depth called the depth of compensation, the weight of the overlying column per unit area is the same everywhere.
Isostasy explains why high mountains have light roots and why regional [[Bouguer anomalies|NMH22NTFJT]] are negative over mountains.
It also explains why land rises after an ice sheet melts.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of blocks of wood floating in water.
A thick block floats higher above the water, but it also sinks deeper below it.
The crust behaves in the same way over the mantle on long time scales.
A mountain range is like a thick block: its weight is balanced by a deep root of light crust that pushes into the mantle.
When the load changes, for example when an ice sheet melts, the crust slowly rises or sinks until the balance is restored.
