## Definition

Let $X$ be a [[state function|T8J5XRYBZZ]] defined for homogeneous [[systems|GYNNJBMTCP]] in [[equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]].
For a real number $\lambda > 0$, let $\lambda \Sigma$ be the system of the same material in the same condition as $\Sigma$.
In $\lambda \Sigma$ all amounts of matter are multiplied by $\lambda$.

1. $X$ is *extensive* if $X(\lambda \Sigma) = \lambda X(\Sigma)$ for all $\Sigma$ and all $\lambda > 0$.
2. $X$ is *intensive* if $X(\lambda \Sigma) = X(\Sigma)$ for all $\Sigma$ and all $\lambda > 0$.

## Remarks

Volume is extensive: doubling the amount of a gas at fixed temperature and pressure doubles its volume.
Temperature is intensive: the doubled system has the same temperature.
The square of the volume is neither: under scaling by $\lambda$ it is multiplied by $\lambda^2$, so it fails both clauses.
The ratio of two extensive quantities is intensive, for example the density $m/V$ or the molar volume $V/N$.
For a simple fluid, $U(\lambda S, \lambda V, \lambda N) = \lambda U(S, V, N)$, so the internal energy is a homogeneous function of degree $1$ of its extensive variables.
