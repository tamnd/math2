## Definition

Let $X$ be a [[compact|0TVJZYB6SH]] [[Hausdorff space|0S1FJ8VEX9]], and let $\mathbb{K}$ be $\R$ or $\C$.
Let $\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{K}}(X)$ be the set of isomorphism classes of finite-rank $\mathbb{K}$-[[vector bundles|P4DZM975BV]] on $X$.
It is a commutative [[monoid|0N6R902FG1]] under the direct sum $[E] + [F] = [E \oplus F]$, with the zero bundle as identity.
The *Grothendieck group of vector bundles* on $X$ is the [[Grothendieck group|5FDMY7PDYY]] of this monoid.
It is written $K(X)$ or $K^0(X)$ for $\mathbb{K} = \C$, and $KO(X)$ for $\mathbb{K} = \R$.

## Remarks

Every element of $K(X)$ is a difference $[E] - [F]$.
Two such differences $[E] - [F]$ and $[E'] - [F']$ are equal if and only if $E \oplus F' \oplus \underline{\mathbb{K}}^n \cong E' \oplus F \oplus \underline{\mathbb{K}}^n$ for some $n$, where $\underline{\mathbb{K}}^n$ is the trivial bundle of rank $n$.
The tensor product of bundles makes $K(X)$ a commutative ring with unit the trivial line bundle.
Example: for $X$ a point, a bundle is a vector space, so $K(\text{point}) \cong \Z$ through the dimension.
Non-example: the monoid $\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{K}}(X)$ itself is not a group, since a nonzero bundle has no additive inverse in it; inverses exist only after passing to $K(X)$.
The reduced group $\tilde K(X)$ is the kernel of the rank map $K(X) \to K(\set{x_0}) \cong \Z$ at a base point $x_0$.
