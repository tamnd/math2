## Definition

Let $\Omega \subset \R^3$ be a bounded domain, the reference configuration of a crystal in the austenite phase.
Let $\varphi(F, \theta)$ be a free energy density, defined for $3 \times 3$ matrices $F$ with $\det F > 0$ and temperatures $\theta$.
Let $SO(3)$ be the [[special orthogonal group|0CGYNDCWRR]] and let $\mathcal{P} \subset SO(3)$ be the point group of the austenite lattice.
The Ball–James theory of a [[martensitic transformation|EZRAMFGRZT]] assumes the following.

1. Frame indifference: $\varphi(RF, \theta) = \varphi(F, \theta)$ for all $R \in SO(3)$.
2. Material symmetry: $\varphi(FQ, \theta) = \varphi(F, \theta)$ for all $Q \in \mathcal{P}$.
3. Energy wells: below the transformation temperature, the minimum of $\varphi(\cdot, \theta)$ is attained exactly on $K = SO(3)U_1 \cup \cdots \cup SO(3)U_N$.
   Here $U_1$ is a symmetric positive definite matrix, and $U_1, \ldots, U_N$ are the distinct matrices $Q U_1 Q^T$ with $Q \in \mathcal{P}$.
   These matrices are the *variants* of martensite.

Let $y_k$ be a minimising sequence of the energy $\int_\Omega \varphi(\nabla y, \theta)\, dx$ under given boundary conditions.
A *microstructure* is described by the gradient [[Young measure|6TQSVJQDEH]] $(\nu_x)_{x \in \Omega}$ generated by $\nabla y_k$, which is supported in $K$.

Two matrices $A$ and $B$ are *compatible* if $A - B = a \otimes n$ for some vectors $a$ and $n \neq 0$, that is, if $A - B$ has rank at most one.
Two variants $U_i$ and $U_j$ form a *twin* if $R U_j - U_i = a \otimes n$ for some $R \in SO(3)$, $a \in \R^3$ and unit vector $n$.

## Remarks

Example: a simple laminate.
Let $R U_j - U_i = a \otimes n$ and $0 < \lambda < 1$.
Take deformations whose gradients alternate between $U_i$ and $R U_j$ in layers normal to $n$, with volume fractions $1 - \lambda$ and $\lambda$.
They are continuous, because the gradients jump only across planes normal to $n$.
As the layers get finer, these deformations approach the affine map with gradient $(1 - \lambda) U_i + \lambda R U_j$, while their energy stays at the minimum.

Non-example: two distinct matrices in the same well, $A = U_1$ and $B = R U_1$ with $R \in SO(3)$, $R \neq I$, are never rank-one connected, so no laminate can be made from one variant alone.

The compatibility condition $A - B = a \otimes n$ is the Hadamard jump condition for a continuous deformation whose gradient jumps across a plane with normal $n$.
It is linked to [[rank-one convexity|GK4P8G0PB2]] of the energy.
