## Summary

The constitutive relations of electromagnetism are the equations that describe how a material responds to electric and magnetic fields.
They express the [[electric displacement field|NJVXQZ780B]] $D$, the magnetizing field $H$ and often the current density in terms of the fields $E$ and $B$.
[[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] in matter do not form a closed system without them.
Simple materials have linear relations with constant [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]], [[permeability|5MJQ5J5H8Z]] and conductivity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Maxwell's equations hold in every material, but they do not know what the material is made of.
Glass, copper and iron react very differently to the same fields: glass gets slightly polarized, copper lets current flow, and iron becomes strongly magnetized.
The constitutive relations are the extra rules that describe this reaction for one particular material.
For many materials they are simple proportions, such as "the current is proportional to the electric field", which is Ohm's law.
