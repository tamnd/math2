## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 5 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$.
By the [[triangular rule|0K1KKMFHYH]], $\det A = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot (-1) \cdot 4 = -24$.

1. $C_{11} = (+1) \det \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = 3 \cdot (-1) \cdot 4 = -12$.
2. $C_{22} = (+1) \det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 5 & 3 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = -8$, $C_{33} = (+1) \det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = 24$, and $C_{44} = (+1) \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot (-1) = -6$.
3. For $i < j$, deleting row $i$ and column $j$ leaves an upper triangular matrix with a zero on its diagonal. So $C_{ij} = 0$ for $i < j$.
4. Expansion along row $2$ by the [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]]: $0 \cdot C_{21} + 3 \cdot (-8) + 1 \cdot 0 + 4 \cdot 0 = -24$.
5. Expansion along row $4$: $4 \cdot C_{44} = 4 \cdot (-6) = -24$. Expansion along column $1$: $2 \cdot C_{11} = -24$.

## Remarks

The full cofactor matrix is lower triangular: $\begin{pmatrix} -12 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 4 & -8 & 0 & 0 \\ -56 & -8 & 24 & 0 \\ 33 & 12 & -12 & -6 \end{pmatrix}$.
Its transpose, the adjugate, is upper triangular, as the inverse of an upper triangular matrix must be.
The sign of $C_{21}$ is $(-1)^{3} = -1$: here $M_{21} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 5 & 3 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = -4$, so $C_{21} = 4$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a triangular table, many cofactors are zero.
Each row then gives the determinant with almost no work.

### L2 human

The expansions along rows $2$ and $4$ and along column $1$ all give $-24$, which agrees with the product of the diagonal.
A trap: the checkerboard sign of position $(2, 1)$ is minus.
