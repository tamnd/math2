## Summary

The cyclostrophic wind is a steady circular wind in which the pressure gradient force balances the centrifugal force alone, with the Coriolis force neglected.
It is the limit of the [[gradient wind|C0HR875S0K]] when the [[Rossby number|SCYK4FMY59]] is large.
It describes small, intense vortices such as tornadoes, dust devils and the inner core of tropical cyclones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a small and fast whirl, the rotation of the Earth has no time to act.
What keeps the air going round is only the low pressure at the centre, which pulls the air inwards as it spins.
In a tornado of radius $100$ m with winds of $50$ m/s, the inward pull needed is about $25$ m/s$^2$, far more than the Coriolis effect of about $0.005$ m/s$^2$.
Because rotation plays no role, such a vortex can spin either clockwise or anticlockwise.
