## Summary

A Cremona diagram is a graphical way to find the forces in the bars of a plane statically determinate [[truss|E5ZHQSHB97]].
It joins the force polygons of all the joints into one drawing.
In it each bar force appears exactly once, as a segment parallel to the bar.
The bar forces are then read off as lengths, without solving equations.
It is named after Luigi Cremona and is related to the reciprocal figures of James Clerk Maxwell.

## Explanations

### L1 human

At a joint of a truss in balance, the forces acting on it, drawn head to tail, close up into a polygon.
Doing this for one joint at a time works, but each bar would be drawn twice.
The Cremona diagram arranges all these polygons so that neighbouring joints share the side for their common bar.
For a simple triangular roof truss, the diagram is a small figure of a few lines whose lengths give the push or pull in every bar.
