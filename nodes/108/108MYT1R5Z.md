## Summary

Quantum mechanics is the physical theory of matter and radiation at the scale of atoms and below.
In its standard mathematical form, a system is described by a complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]].
[[Quantum states|4NQN47A92F]] are unit vectors up to a phase, or more generally [[density operators|V1JTB52E8C]], and [[observables|DF5RN297GE]] are self-adjoint operators.
Measurement results are random, with probabilities given by the [[Born rule|MF4DYZ4YAX]], and time evolution is the unitary group generated by the [[Hamiltonian|VXDEQB46GV]].
The [[Dirac–von Neumann axioms|J1YDW3BE56]] fix this framework, and its study drove much of the spectral theory of unbounded operators.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In everyday physics a ball has a definite position and speed at each moment.
In quantum mechanics a particle is described instead by a wave of complex numbers spread over space.
The wave does not say where the particle is; it says how likely each result of a measurement is.
Two such waves can be added, and the sum describes another possible state, which is why interference appears.
For example, an electron sent through two slits builds up a striped pattern on a screen, one dot at a time, as the two parts of its wave interfere.
