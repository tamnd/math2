## Summary

An alternative algebra is a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] in which $x(xy) = (xx)y$ and $(yx)x = y(xx)$ for all $x, y$.
Equivalently, the [[associator|RT4YV20367]] is alternating: it vanishes when two of its arguments are equal, and so it changes sign when two arguments are swapped.
The [[octonions|1HRMT3DSX2]] are the main example that is not associative.
By [[Artin's theorem|PBRCDXKQFR]], any two elements generate an associative subalgebra.
The [[Moufang identities|CZ2BZQN2EV]] also hold in every alternative algebra.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Associative algebras let you move brackets freely.
Alternative algebras let you do so when a factor is repeated, as in $x(xy) = (xx)y$.
This weak rule is enough to make computations with two elements look exactly like computations in an associative algebra.
The octonions obey it, which is why they are much easier to handle than a general nonassociative algebra.
