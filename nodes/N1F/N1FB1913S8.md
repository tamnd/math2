## Definition

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be $\R$ or $\C$.
Let $A$ be a [[Hurwitz-stable|9A3TBBHRFA]] $n \times n$ matrix, $B$ an $n \times m$ matrix and $C$ a $p \times n$ matrix, all with entries in $\mathbb{K}$.
Let $\norm{\cdot}$ be the [[operator norm|06EKW1AKXF]] induced by the Euclidean norms.
A matrix $M$ is *unstable* if it has an [[eigenvalue|0BQAA2BK3Q]] $\lambda$ with $\operatorname{Re} \lambda \ge 0$.
The *stability radius* of $A$ with respect to the structure $(B, C)$ and perturbations over $\mathbb{K}$ is
$$r_{\mathbb{K}}(A; B, C) = \inf \set{\norm{\Delta} : \Delta \in \mathbb{K}^{m \times p},\ A + B \Delta C \text{ is unstable}},$$
with $\inf \emptyset = \infty$.
The *unstructured* stability radius is the case $B = C = I$.

## Remarks

For complex perturbations there is an explicit formula:
$$r_{\C}(A; B, C) = \Bigl(\sup_{\omega \in \R} \norm{C (i\omega I - A)^{-1} B}\Bigr)^{-1},$$
with $1/0 = \infty$.
In the unstructured case this is $\min_{\omega \in \R} \sigma_{\min}(i\omega I - A)$, the least [[singular value|09VMDSRXWJ]] of $i\omega I - A$ over $\omega$.
For a real matrix $A$ one has $r_{\R} \ge r_{\C}$, and the real radius is harder to compute.

Example: $A = -a$ with $a > 0$, $n = 1$, $B = C = 1$.
Then $\abs{i\omega + a} \ge a$ with equality at $\omega = 0$, so $r_{\C}(A) = a$.
Directly: $-a + \delta$ has real part $\ge 0$ only if $\abs{\delta} \ge a$, and $\delta = a$ gives $0$.

Non-example of what the radius measures: the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & t \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ has both eigenvalues at $-1$ for every $t$, but its unstructured stability radius tends to $0$ as $t \to \infty$.
Indeed, perturbing the lower left entry by $\varepsilon$ gives eigenvalues $-1 \pm \sqrt{t \varepsilon}$, unstable as soon as $t\varepsilon \ge 1$.
So the distance $1$ of the eigenvalues to the imaginary axis is not the stability radius.
