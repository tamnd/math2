## Definition

Let $K$ be a commutative ring, let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $m \times n$ [[matrix|0N1MY1T0W9]] over $K$, and let $1 \le k \le \min(m, n)$.
Choose row indices $i_1 < i_2 < \dots < i_k$ and column indices $j_1 < j_2 < \dots < j_k$, and put $I = \set{i_1, \dots, i_k}$ and $J = \set{j_1, \dots, j_k}$.
The $k \times k$ matrix $A_{I,J}$ has entry $a_{i_r j_s}$ in row $r$ and column $s$.
The *minor of order $k$* of $A$ with rows $I$ and columns $J$ is the [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]] $\det A_{I,J}$.

For a square matrix with $n \ge 2$, the *first minor* $M_{ij}$ is the minor with rows $\set{1, \dots, n} \setminus \set{i}$ and columns $\set{1, \dots, n} \setminus \set{j}$.
A minor is *principal* when $I = J$, and *leading principal* when $I = J = \set{1, \dots, k}$.

## Remarks

The rows and the columns are kept in increasing order; another order changes the determinant by a sign.
The minors of order $1$ are the entries of $A$.
Over a field, the [[rank|0ZC9YTXZRY]] of $A$ is the largest $k$ such that $A$ has a nonzero minor of order $k$; see the [[rank by minors|HVHQ50TJB1]].
The signed first minors are the [[cofactors|0XPKR18APA]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A minor is the determinant of a smaller square piece of a matrix.
For a $3 \times 3$ matrix, we delete one row and one column and take the $2 \times 2$ determinant of what is left.
Minors appear in the expansion of determinants and in the formula for the inverse.
