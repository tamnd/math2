## Example

We are given either a proof of $p$ or a proof of $q$, and we do not know which.
One term must still name a proof of $p \vee q$ in both cases, and the sum operation of [[justification logic|5HKWCXEFG1]] provides it.

The formula $\square p \vee \square q \to \square(p \vee q)$ is a theorem of $\mathsf{S4}$.
We give a normal realization in the logic of proofs, as the [[realization theorem|BP022FTWCP]] promises.
With $a{:}(p \to (p \vee q))$ and $b{:}(q \to (p \vee q))$ from axiom necessitation, the realization is the following.
$$x{:}p \vee y{:}q \to (a \cdot x + b \cdot y){:}(p \vee q).$$

1. The boxes of $\square p$ and $\square q$ are negative, so they get the distinct variables $x$ and $y$.
   The box of $\square(p \vee q)$ is positive.
2. Axiom necessitation gives $a{:}(p \to (p \vee q))$, and the application axiom gives $x{:}p \to (a \cdot x){:}(p \vee q)$.
3. In the same way, $y{:}q \to (b \cdot y){:}(p \vee q)$.
4. The sum axiom $s{:}\varphi \to (s + t){:}\varphi$ gives $(a \cdot x){:}(p \vee q) \to (a \cdot x + b \cdot y){:}(p \vee q)$.
5. The sum axiom $t{:}\varphi \to (s + t){:}\varphi$ gives $(b \cdot y){:}(p \vee q) \to (a \cdot x + b \cdot y){:}(p \vee q)$.
6. Steps 2 to 5 and proof by cases give the claim.

## Remarks

The positive box is introduced twice in a cut-free $\mathsf{S4}$ derivation, once from $\square p$ and once from $\square q$.
Each introduction gives its own term, $a \cdot x$ and $b \cdot y$, and the sum joins them into one term that works in both cases.
Without $+$, neither $a \cdot x$ nor $b \cdot y$ works alone.
In a model where only $y{:}q$ holds, nothing makes $(a \cdot x){:}(p \vee q)$ true.
For the theorem $\square p \to \square(p \vee q)$ with one disjunct, no sum is needed, and $x{:}p \to (a \cdot x){:}(p \vee q)$ is a realization.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We know that one of two documents exists: a proof of $p$ or a proof of $q$.
We do not know which.
To name a proof of "$p$ or $q$", we pack both possible proofs into one bundle.
Whichever document exists, the bundle contains a correct proof.
The sum $+$ is this bundle.

### L2 human

First mark the polarity of each box, and here the two boxes left of the main arrow are negative.
Give each negative box a new variable.
For the positive box, build one term for each way of proving it, and add the terms.

The typical use of sums is in realizations of theorems with disjunctions in the antecedent.
Watch out: $+$ is not a disjunction of formulas, since $(s + t){:}\varphi$ says that one term justifies one formula.
