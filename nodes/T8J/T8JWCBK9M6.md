## Summary

Magnetohydrodynamics, or MHD, is the study of electrically conducting fluids in magnetic fields.
It couples the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] with a low-frequency form of [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]].
The magnetic field pushes the fluid through the Lorentz force, and the moving fluid bends and stretches the field through the induction equation.
It models liquid metals, the Earth's core, the Sun, and plasmas in fusion devices.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Salt water, liquid iron and hot ionised gas all carry electric currents.
When such a fluid moves across a magnetic field, currents appear, and these currents feel a force from the field.
So the field acts on the flow, and the flow drags and twists the field.
In a very good conductor, field lines behave like elastic strings glued to the fluid.
This picture explains sunspots, solar flares and the magnetic field of the Earth.
