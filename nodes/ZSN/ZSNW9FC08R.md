## Summary

The Debye length is the distance over which mobile charges screen an electric field in a plasma or an electrolyte.
Beyond a few Debye lengths, the electric potential of a charge is exponentially small, because opposite charges gather around it.
It grows with temperature and decreases with the density of charge carriers.
It sets the scale of electric double layers at surfaces and of collective effects in plasmas.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put a charge into a soup of free positive and negative charges.
Opposite charges crowd around it and like charges move away, so from a distance the charge looks neutral.
The Debye length is the radius of this screening cloud.
Heat makes the cloud fuzzier and larger; more charge carriers make it tighter and smaller.
In sea water it is below a nanometre, while in the thin plasma between planets it is metres long.
