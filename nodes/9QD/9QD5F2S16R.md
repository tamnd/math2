## Definition

Let $u \colon \R^n_{\ge 0} \to \R$ be a [[utility function|GZZS4MTDG9]].
Assume that, for every price vector $p \in \R^n$ with all entries positive and every wealth $w > 0$, the [[utility maximization problem|A56BG7XYM6]]
$$\max \set{u(x) : x \in \R^n_{\ge 0},\ p \cdot x \le w}$$
has exactly one solution.
The *Marshallian demand function* of $u$ is the map $(p, w) \mapsto x(p, w)$ that sends $(p, w)$ to this solution.

## Remarks

For $u(x_1, x_2) = x_1^{a} x_2^{1 - a}$ with $0 < a < 1$, the demand is $x(p, w) = (a w / p_1, (1 - a) w / p_2)$.
Check: this bundle costs exactly $w$, and on the budget line $x_2 = (w - p_1 x_1)/p_2$ the derivative of $a \ln x_1 + (1 - a) \ln x_2$ vanishes only at $x_1 = a w / p_1$, where it changes sign from positive to negative.
For the linear utility $u(x_1, x_2) = x_1 + x_2$ and $p_1 = p_2$, every bundle on the budget line is optimal.
So the solution is not unique, and the demand is a correspondence, not a function.
A continuous, strictly quasiconcave utility always has a unique solution, so it has a Marshallian demand function.
The demand is homogeneous of degree zero: $x(t p, t w) = x(p, w)$ for $t > 0$, since the budget set does not change.
If $u$ is locally nonsatiated, Walras's law holds: $p \cdot x(p, w) = w$.
