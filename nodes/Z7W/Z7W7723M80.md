## Summary

The Morris–Lecar model is a two-variable conductance-based [[neuron model|RMWR3MDW0T]].
It has a calcium current that responds instantly to voltage and a potassium current controlled by one slow gating variable.
It was introduced by Morris and Lecar for the voltage oscillations of the barnacle giant muscle fibre.
Being planar, it is widely used to study by [[phase plane|05VDGH129F]] methods how a neuron starts firing, through different bifurcations depending on the parameters.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The model keeps only two quantities: the voltage, and the fraction of open potassium channels.
Calcium channels open at once when the voltage rises and push it up further; potassium channels open more slowly and pull it back down.
The race between the fast push and the slow pull produces spikes and oscillations.
Unlike the FitzHugh–Nagumo model, each term has a direct physiological meaning as a current through a type of channel.
