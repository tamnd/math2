## Summary

Viscoelasticity is the behaviour of materials that respond to load partly like an elastic solid and partly like a viscous fluid.
Their stress depends not only on the present strain but on the whole past history of strain, so they are materials with memory.
They show [[creep|YFTQJ7VY31]] under constant stress, stress relaxation under constant strain, and energy loss in cyclic loading.
Polymers, biological tissue, asphalt and many soils are viscoelastic, and in the linear theory the response is fixed by one function, the [[relaxation modulus|JSSYGY0V87]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Press a memory-foam pillow and let go: it comes back, but slowly.
An elastic spring would jump back at once, and honey would not come back at all.
Viscoelastic materials sit between these two extremes.
A simple model joins a spring and a dashpot (a piston moving in oil), and different ways of joining them give the [[Maxwell|PQ8Z5HS2JR]] and [[Kelvin–Voigt|2SP692ZRAH]] materials.
