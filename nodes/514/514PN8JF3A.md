## Summary

The Gouy phase is the extra phase shift that a focused beam picks up as it passes through its focus.
It is measured against a plane wave of the same frequency.
For a [[Gaussian beam|Y0QE726BE3]] it equals $\arctan(z / z_R)$, so it changes by $\pi$ in total from far before the focus to far after it.
Higher-order laser modes pick up a multiple of this shift, which is why modes of different order resonate at different frequencies in a laser cavity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine comparing the crests of a focused laser beam with the crests of a flat wave travelling beside it.
Far before the focus they keep a fixed offset.
While the beam squeezes through its focus, its crests slip ahead by half a wavelength in total.
After the focus the offset is fixed again, but shifted.
This slip is the Gouy phase; it happens to every wave that passes through a focus.
