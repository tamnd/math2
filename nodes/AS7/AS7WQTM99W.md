## Summary

Plane stress is a state of [[stress|KG2XWEMJ30]] in which all stress acts in one plane.
The components that involve the normal direction to that plane vanish.
It is the standard model for thin plates and sheets loaded only in their own plane.
It reduces a three-dimensional elasticity problem to a two-dimensional one, often solved with an [[Airy stress function|7ZGSJRYBGK]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a thin metal sheet pulled at its edges, all forces lying in the plane of the sheet.
Nothing pushes on its top or bottom face, and the sheet is so thin that the stress across its thickness stays almost zero inside too.
So only three stress numbers remain: two pulls in the plane and one shear in the plane.
The sheet can still get slightly thinner, so the strain across the thickness is not zero.
