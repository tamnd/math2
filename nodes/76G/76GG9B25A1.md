## Statement

Row reduction decides whether the columns of a matrix are independent: look for a pivot in every column.
Let $F$ be a field, and let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over $F$ with columns $v_1, \dots, v_n \in F^m$.

1. The following four conditions are equivalent.
   1. The columns $v_1, \dots, v_n$ are [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]].
   2. No column $v_j$ is a [[linear combination|0V8F3E48E6]] of the other columns.
   3. The [[homogeneous system|0KP0QNXPRT]] $Ax = 0$ has only the solution $x = 0$.
   4. Every column of a row echelon form of $A$ contains a [[pivot|0BCMGDHZGR]].
2. If $n > m$, then the columns of $A$ are linearly dependent.

## Remarks

### A check with numbers

Take the $2 \times 3$ matrix with columns $(1, 2)$, $(3, 4)$ and $(5, 6)$.
Part 2 already says that the columns are dependent, since $3 > 2$.
Row reduction shows where the dependence is: subtract twice row 1 from row 2.
$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 & 5 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \end{pmatrix} \to \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 & 5 \\ 0 & -2 & -4 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Column 3 has no pivot, so $x_3$ is free.
Set $x_3 = 1$; then $-2x_2 - 4 = 0$ gives $x_2 = -2$, and $x_1 + 3(-2) + 5 = 0$ gives $x_1 = 1$.
Check: $(1, 2) - 2(3, 4) + (5, 6) = (1 - 6 + 5, \ 2 - 8 + 6) = (0, 0)$.

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The scalars must form a field.
Over $\mathbb{Z}$, the $1 \times 2$ matrix $(2 \ \ 3)$ has the nonzero solution $x = (3, -2)$ of $Ax = 0$, so its columns are dependent.
But neither column is an integer multiple of the other, so conditions 1 and 2 are no longer equivalent.

The pivots must be read from an echelon form.
The matrix with columns $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 0)$ has a zero in its top left corner, but after one row swap both columns have pivots, and the columns are independent.

### What it gives you

Reading the conditions in the negative gives the test for dependence: the columns are dependent exactly when some column has no pivot, and each free variable gives a nonzero solution of $Ax = 0$.
That solution $x$ is the dependence relation $x_1 v_1 + \dots + x_n v_n = 0$, so the test does more than say yes or no.

For a square matrix, $n = m$, condition 4 says that the echelon form has $n$ pivots, so $A$ is row equivalent to the identity.
So $n$ vectors in $F^n$ are independent exactly when their matrix is invertible.

Watch out for a common phrase.
Some books say that in the dependent case "$Ax = 0$ has infinitely many solutions", but that is true only over an infinite field.
Over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$, the solution set has exactly $q^k$ elements, where $k \ge 1$ is the number of columns without a pivot.

### The converse

Part 1 is already a list of equivalences.
The converse of part 2 is false: with $n \le m$ the columns can still be dependent, as $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 4)$ show.

### Generalizations

For vectors in an abstract space of dimension $m$, write each vector in coordinates of a basis and apply the test to the matrix of coordinates.
Part 2 then says that more than $m$ vectors in a space of dimension $m$ are dependent, which is a form of the [[Steinitz exchange lemma|08ZDRN6H41]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put the vectors side by side as the columns of a table and simplify the table by row reduction.
If each column gets a leading entry, the vectors are independent.
If some column has no leading entry, its unknown can be chosen freely, and that choice gives a recipe that mixes the vectors into zero.
With more columns than rows, some column must miss out, so the vectors are dependent.

### L2 human

Here is a typical use: are $(1, 1, 0)$, $(1, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 1, 1)$ independent in $\mathbb{R}^3$?
Put them as columns.
Subtract row 1 from row 2, then add the new row 2 to row 3:
$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \to \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \to \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Every column has a pivot, so the vectors are independent over $\mathbb{R}$.
Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ the last pivot $2$ becomes $0$, and the vectors are dependent there, as in [[the example over F_2|KFJ7M9FRXV]].
So the test depends on the field through the arithmetic of the pivots.

### L3 human

Condition 3 says that the linear map $x \mapsto Ax$ from $F^n$ to $F^m$ is injective, and condition 4 says that its rank is $n$.
Part 2 is then the inequality $\operatorname{rank} A \le m$, since the image lies in $F^m$.
The companion result for spanning asks for a pivot in every row, and for a square matrix the two conditions coincide.
