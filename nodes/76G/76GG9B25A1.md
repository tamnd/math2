## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, and let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over $F$ with columns $v_1, \dots, v_n \in F^m$.

1. These four conditions are equivalent:
   1. The columns $v_1, \dots, v_n$ are [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]].
   2. No column $v_j$ is a [[linear combination|0V8F3E48E6]] of the other columns.
   3. The [[homogeneous system|0KP0QNXPRT]] $Ax = 0$ has only the solution $x = 0$.
   4. Every column of $A$ has a [[pivot|0BCMGDHZGR]] position.
2. If $n > m$, then the columns are linearly dependent.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The statement needs a field: over $\Z$, the $1 \times 2$ matrix $(2 \ 3)$ has the nonzero solution $x = (3, -2)$.
But neither column is an integer multiple of the other, so conditions 1 and 2 differ.
Some books state the dependent case as "$Ax = 0$ has infinitely many solutions", which holds only over an infinite field.
Over $\mathbb{F}_q$, a dependent system has exactly $q^k$ solutions, where $k \ge 1$ is the number of columns without a pivot.

### Consequences

The negations give a test for dependence: the columns are dependent exactly when $Ax = 0$ has a nonzero solution, exactly when some column has no pivot.
A nonzero solution $x$ gives the dependence relation $x_1 v_1 + \cdots + x_n v_n = 0$.
For $n = m$, condition 4 says that $A$ is row equivalent to $I$; so $n$ vectors in $F^n$ are independent exactly when their matrix is invertible.

### Converse

Part 1 is a list of equivalences.
The converse of part 2 fails: for $n \le m$, the columns can be dependent, as $(1, 2), (2, 4)$ show.

### Generalizations

For vectors in an abstract space of dimension $m$, take coordinates in a basis; the test applies to the coordinate matrix.
Part 2 becomes: in a space of dimension $m$, more than $m$ vectors are dependent; this is the [[Steinitz exchange lemma|08ZDRN6H41]] in another form.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put the vectors side by side as columns and row reduce.
If each column gets a pivot, the vectors are independent.
If some column has no pivot, its variable is free, and a free variable gives a nonzero solution of $Ax = 0$, which is a dependence relation.

### L2 human

Example: the columns $(1, 2), (3, 4), (5, 6)$ of a $2 \times 3$ matrix are dependent, because $3 > 2$.
Row reduction gives the rows $(1, 3, 5)$ and $(0, -2, -4)$, so column 3 has no pivot.
Set the free variable $x_3 = 1$; then $-2x_2 - 4 = 0$ gives $x_2 = -2$, and $x_1 + 3(-2) + 5 = 0$ gives $x_1 = 1$.
Check: $(1, 2) - 2(3, 4) + (5, 6) = (0, 0)$.
