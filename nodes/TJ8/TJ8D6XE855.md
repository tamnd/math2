## Definition

Let a homogeneous isotropic linear elastic solid fill the half-space $\set{x \in \R^3 : x_3 < 0}$, with density $\rho$, [[Lamé parameters|80737NDZ4V]] $\lambda, \mu$, and [[P-wave|EEPJJHC5JZ]] and [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]] speeds $c_P > c_S > 0$.
A *Rayleigh wave* is a solution of the [[elastodynamic|T1PYEBKBAJ]] equations without body force of the form
$$u(x, t) = \operatorname{Re} \left[ \left( U_1(x_3), \, 0, \, U_3(x_3) \right) e^{i k (x_1 - c t)} \right], \qquad k > 0,$$
such that:

1. $U_1(x_3) \to 0$ and $U_3(x_3) \to 0$ as $x_3 \to -\infty$,
2. the surface $x_3 = 0$ is free of traction, $\sigma_{13} = \sigma_{33} = 0$ there, and
3. $(U_1, U_3)$ is not identically zero.

Such solutions exist exactly when $c = c_R$, the unique root in $(0, c_S)$ of the Rayleigh equation
$$\left( 2 - \frac{c^2}{c_S^2} \right)^2 = 4 \sqrt{1 - \frac{c^2}{c_P^2}} \sqrt{1 - \frac{c^2}{c_S^2}}.$$

## Remarks

Example: for a Poisson solid, $\lambda = \mu$, one has $c_S^2 / c_P^2 = 1/3$, and the Rayleigh equation has the root $c_R^2 = (2 - 2/\sqrt{3}) \, c_S^2$, so $c_R \approx 0.919 \, c_S$.
Check: with $\xi = c_R^2 / c_S^2 \approx 0.845$, both sides equal $4/3$.

Non-example: a plane [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]] travelling along the surface in direction $x_1$ with vertical polarization does not decay with depth, so clause 1 fails.

The speed $c_R$ does not depend on $k$, so on a homogeneous half-space Rayleigh waves are not dispersive.
