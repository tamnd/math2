## Summary

Inductance is the ratio of the magnetic flux linked by an electric circuit to the current that produces it.
Self-inductance relates a circuit to its own current, and mutual inductance relates one circuit to the current in another.
By [[Faraday's law of induction|Y8Q6C2W8J6]], it also gives the voltage induced in a circuit when a current changes.
It depends only on the shape and position of the circuits and on the surrounding material, not on the current.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A current in a loop of wire makes a [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]], and some of that field passes through the loop itself and through nearby loops.
Double the current and the field doubles, so the amount of field through each loop doubles too.
Inductance is the fixed number that turns current into this amount of field.
A coil with many turns has a large inductance, because the field of each turn passes through all the other turns.
When the current changes, the changing field pushes back against the change, so a large inductance makes a circuit slow to start or stop its current.
