## Summary

An affine Lie algebra is a [[Kac–Moody algebra|HD13X0CNBD]] whose [[generalized Cartan matrix|38Z0HSXTCA]] is of affine type.
The untwisted ones are built from a finite-dimensional [[simple Lie algebra|XDAXWHBNSZ]] $\mathfrak{g}$.
One takes the [[loop algebra|YJFGP89JYD]] $\mathfrak{g} \otimes \C[t, t^{-1}]$, adds a one-dimensional center and adds a derivation.
They are the infinite-dimensional Kac–Moody algebras that are best understood.
Their representation theory is tied to modular forms, to identities such as the [[Macdonald identities|DV21A4N88B]], and to two-dimensional conformal field theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a simple Lie algebra such as $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and let its elements depend on a point of a circle.
The resulting loops can be added and bracketed pointwise, and they form an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra.
An untwisted affine Lie algebra is this loop algebra with one extra central element and one extra grading element.
The central element records a twist in the bracket that comes from integrating around the circle.
For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ the generalized Cartan matrix has rows $(2, -2)$ and $(-2, 2)$, the simplest affine matrix.
