## Proof

Formulas are built from variables with $\neg$, $\to$, $\Box$; $\land$, $\lor$, $\leftrightarrow$, $\top$ are the usual abbreviations.
A valuation in a [[modal algebra|JCP47TNBSD]] $A$ is a homomorphism $v$ from the formula algebra to $A$, with $v(\varphi \to \psi) = \neg v(\varphi) \lor v(\psi)$.
"Tautological reasoning" below means: a [[normal modal logic|JS8W7PA6JK]] contains all tautology instances and is closed under MP.

**Part 1, soundness.** If $\varphi \in \Lambda$ and $A \in \mathsf{V}(\Lambda)$, then $A \models \varphi$ by the definition of $\mathsf{V}(\Lambda)$.

**Part 1, completeness, by contraposition.** Let $\varphi \notin \Lambda$.

1. Put $\alpha \sim \beta$ iff $\alpha \leftrightarrow \beta \in \Lambda$.
2. By tautological reasoning, $\sim$ is an equivalence relation that respects $\neg$ and $\to$, as in [[the classical case|JVZ8APEV7A]].
3. Let $\alpha \sim \beta$. Then $\alpha \to \beta \in \Lambda$, and necessitation gives $\Box(\alpha \to \beta) \in \Lambda$.
4. The axiom K and MP give $\Box \alpha \to \Box \beta \in \Lambda$; in the same way $\Box \beta \to \Box \alpha \in \Lambda$.
5. So $\Box \alpha \sim \Box \beta$, and $\sim$ is a congruence for $\neg$, $\to$, $\Box$.
6. Let $A_\Lambda$ be the quotient; its Boolean reduct is a Boolean algebra, by the proof for the classical case.
7. $[\alpha] = 1$ iff $\alpha \in \Lambda$, since $\alpha \leftrightarrow \top \in \Lambda$ iff $\alpha \in \Lambda$, by tautological reasoning.
8. $\top \in \Lambda$, so $\Box \top \in \Lambda$ by necessitation, and $\Box [\top] = 1$ by step 7.
9. The tautology $\alpha \land \beta \to \alpha$, necessitation, K and MP give $\Box(\alpha \land \beta) \to \Box \alpha \in \Lambda$; the same for $\beta$.
10. The tautology $\alpha \to (\beta \to \alpha \land \beta)$, necessitation and K give $\Box \alpha \to \Box(\beta \to \alpha \land \beta) \in \Lambda$.
11. K gives $\Box(\beta \to \alpha \land \beta) \to (\Box \beta \to \Box(\alpha \land \beta)) \in \Lambda$.
12. By steps 9 to 11 and tautological reasoning, $\Box(\alpha \land \beta) \leftrightarrow \Box \alpha \land \Box \beta \in \Lambda$.
13. So $\Box$ preserves $1$ and $\land$ on $A_\Lambda$, and $A_\Lambda$ is a modal algebra.
14. Let $\psi \in \Lambda$ and let $v$ be a valuation in $A_\Lambda$. Choose $\sigma(p)$ with $v(p) = [\sigma(p)]$ for each variable $p$.
15. By induction on formulas, $v(\chi) = [\chi^\sigma]$ for every $\chi$, where $\chi^\sigma$ is the result of the substitution $\sigma$.
16. $\psi^\sigma \in \Lambda$ by uniform substitution, so $v(\psi) = 1$ by step 7. Hence $A_\Lambda \in \mathsf{V}(\Lambda)$.
17. The valuation $v_0(p) = [p]$ gives $v_0(\varphi) = [\varphi] \ne 1$, by step 7 and $\varphi \notin \Lambda$.
18. So $A_\Lambda \not\models \varphi$, which proves part 1.

**Part 2.**

19. $\mathsf{V}(\Lambda)$ is defined by the identities of modal algebras and the equations $\psi \approx 1$ for $\psi \in \Lambda$, so it is a variety.
20. Let $\mathsf{V}$ be a variety. $\mathrm{Log}(\mathsf{V})$ contains all tautology instances, since each $A \in \mathsf{V}$ has a Boolean reduct.
21. Axiom K: for $a, b \in A$, $\Box(\neg a \lor b) \land \Box a = \Box((\neg a \lor b) \land a) = \Box(a \land b) \le \Box b$.
22. So $\Box(\neg a \lor b) \le \neg \Box a \lor \Box b$, and K has the value $1$.
23. MP keeps the value $1$, and $v(\varphi) = 1$ gives $v(\Box \varphi) = \Box 1 = 1$, so necessitation keeps it.
24. If $v$ is a valuation and $\sigma$ a substitution, then $\chi \mapsto v(\chi^\sigma)$ is a valuation; so validity is closed under substitution.
25. Hence $\mathrm{Log}(\mathsf{V})$ is a normal modal logic.
26. $\mathsf{V} \subseteq \mathsf{V}(\mathrm{Log}(\mathsf{V}))$ by the definitions.
27. By [[Birkhoff's HSP theorem|5083DHSK82]], $\mathsf{V}$ is defined by a set $E$ of equations.
28. In a modal algebra, $s = t$ holds under a valuation iff $s \leftrightarrow t$ has the value $1$.
29. So for $(s \approx t) \in E$, $s \leftrightarrow t \in \mathrm{Log}(\mathsf{V})$.
30. Each $A \in \mathsf{V}(\mathrm{Log}(\mathsf{V}))$ validates these formulas, so $A$ satisfies $E$, and $A \in \mathsf{V}$.

**Part 3.**

31. By parts 1 and 2, $\mathrm{Log} \circ \mathsf{V}$ and $\mathsf{V} \circ \mathrm{Log}$ are identities on normal logics and on varieties.
32. Both maps reverse inclusion, by their definitions. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

The Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of $\Lambda$ is a modal algebra because necessitation and K make $\Box$ respect provable equivalence and conjunction.
It validates $\Lambda$ because $\Lambda$ is closed under substitution, and its canonical valuation refutes every non-theorem.
Part 2 is the observation that validity of formulas and truth of equations translate into each other.

## Variants

1. Through Kripke semantics: the canonical frame of $\Lambda$ refutes each non-theorem, but it need not validate $\Lambda$; the algebraic proof avoids this problem.
2. Through [[algebraizability|CESWST3NWA]]: the global consequence of $\Lambda$ is algebraizable, and the theorem is a case of the isomorphism between extensions and subvarieties.
