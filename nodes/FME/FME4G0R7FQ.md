## Summary

Let $g(r)$ be the radial distribution function of a homogeneous and isotropic fluid.
It compares the density of particles at distance $r$ from a given particle with the average density.
It is the basic quantity that describes the local structure of liquids and gases.
It can be measured by X-ray or neutron scattering, through its Fourier transform, the structure factor.
For pair potentials, the energy, the pressure and the compressibility of the fluid can all be written as integrals of $g$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Sit on one particle of a liquid and count its neighbours in thin shells at each distance $r$.
Divide by what you would expect if the other particles were spread out uniformly: that ratio is $g(r)$.
It is $0$ at very short distance, where particles cannot overlap, has a peak at the distance of the first layer of neighbours, and settles to $1$ far away.
For an ideal gas, where particles ignore each other, $g(r) = 1$ at all distances.
