## Summary

Wall's finiteness obstruction is an element $\sigma(X)$ attached to a finitely dominated CW complex $X$.
It lies in the [[reduced K0|GBJ1S3M5QV]] of the integral [[group ring|4NMTSX0XDS]] of $\pi_1(X)$.
It vanishes exactly when $X$ is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex.
It is a basic example of how algebraic K-theory measures obstructions in topology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some spaces behave like finite complexes up to homotopy, being retracts of finite ones up to homotopy, without being equivalent to a finite complex.
To compare them, one looks at the cellular chains of the universal cover as modules over the group ring of the fundamental group.
These chains can be replaced by finitely many projective modules, and the alternating sum of their classes is the obstruction.
If all those modules could be taken free, the class is zero and the space is equivalent to a finite complex.
A simply connected finitely dominated space always has obstruction zero, since every projective module over $\Z$ is stably free.
