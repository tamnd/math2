## Summary

Latent heat is the [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] that a substance absorbs or releases during a first-order [[phase transition|6WJ0NADBT4]] at constant temperature and pressure.
Examples are the heat of fusion for melting and the heat of vaporization for boiling.
It equals the jump in [[enthalpy|722RZK72P5]], and also the temperature times the jump in [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]], between the two phases.
It sets the slope of coexistence curves through the [[Clausius–Clapeyron relation|4XEQ5FA5P9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

If you heat a pot of water on a stove, the temperature rises until the water starts to boil.
From then on the temperature stays at the boiling point even though the stove keeps adding heat.
That heat goes into turning liquid into steam rather than into warming it: it is the latent, or hidden, heat.
For water it takes about $2.26$ million joules to boil away one kilogram, more than five times the heat needed to warm it from freezing to boiling.
The same heat comes back out when steam condenses, which is why steam burns are so severe.
