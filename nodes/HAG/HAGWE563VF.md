## Summary

The Dugdale strip-yield model describes the plastic zone ahead of a crack in a thin sheet of an elastic–perfectly plastic material.
The plastic zone is modelled as a thin strip along the crack line.
In the strip, the faces of a fictitious longer crack are held together by a constant stress equal to the yield stress.
The length of the strip is chosen so that the stress at the end of the strip is finite.
The model is a [[cohesive zone model|7P2EGHZM7Q]] with constant cohesive traction.
It gives the plastic zone size and the [[crack tip opening displacement|8HFZYWW7SP]] in closed form.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a thin metal sheet, the material just ahead of a crack yields and stretches in a narrow band.
Dugdale pictured this band as an extension of the crack whose faces are still held together by the yield stress of the metal.
The band is just long enough that the stress at its end is no longer infinite.
For small loads the band is short, and its length grows quickly as the load approaches the yield stress.
