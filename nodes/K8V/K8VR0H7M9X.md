## Summary

Thin-film interference is the [[interference|KX1EXRW47E]] of light reflected from the top and from the bottom surface of a thin layer.
The two reflected waves differ by twice the optical thickness of the layer and by possible phase jumps at the surfaces.
So some wavelengths are reflected strongly and others weakly, depending on the thickness.
It explains the colours of soap bubbles and oil films, and it is the principle of antireflection coatings and dielectric mirrors.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A soap bubble shows swirling colours although the soap itself has no colour.
Light bounces off the outer and the inner surface of the very thin soap wall.
The two reflected beams meet again, and for some colours they are in step and add, while for others they cancel.
Which colours win depends on the thickness of the wall, which changes from place to place.
Camera lenses carry a thin coating chosen so that reflected light cancels, and that is why they look slightly purple.
