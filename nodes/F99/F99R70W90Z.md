## Summary

Physical oceanography is the study of the physical state and motions of the ocean.
It covers currents driven by wind and by differences in density, waves, tides and mixing.
It also covers the exchange of heat and fresh water with the atmosphere.
Its mathematics is the fluid dynamics of a thin layer of stratified fluid on a rotating sphere.
It is built on the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] with the [[Coriolis force|6HZRX3WSQD]].
It is a central part of climate science, since the ocean stores and moves most of the heat of the climate system.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Physical oceanography asks how and why the sea moves.
The wind pushes the surface water, the Earth's rotation turns the moving water sideways, and cold or salty water sinks because it is heavy.
Together these effects make large circulating currents, such as the Gulf Stream along the east coast of North America.
Physical oceanographers measure the ocean with ships, floats and satellites, and they explain the measurements with equations of fluid motion.
