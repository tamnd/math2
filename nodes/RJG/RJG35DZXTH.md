## Summary

A decision maker is risk averse when they prefer receiving the [[expected value|0WQNRCNY3M]] of a gamble for sure to taking the gamble itself.
Under the [[expected utility hypothesis|8C2N37XPJ8]], this holds exactly when the utility of money is a [[concave function|0GCM7B6PBK]].
Risk aversion explains why people buy insurance and why riskier assets must offer higher expected returns.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Offer someone a choice between a sure $50$ euros and a coin flip for $0$ or $100$ euros.
Both have the same average, but a risk-averse person takes the sure $50$.
The reason is that each extra euro matters a little less when one already has more, so losing $50$ hurts more than winning $50$ pleases.
A utility such as $u(x) = \sqrt{x}$ captures this, because its graph bends downward.
