## Summary

A potential game is a game with a single function on strategy profiles, the potential, that tracks every player's incentive to change her strategy.
When one player switches, her gain equals the change in the potential.
Every finite potential game has a pure Nash equilibrium, and best-response dynamics reach one.
The notion was formalised by Dov Monderer and Lloyd Shapley in 1996.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of the potential as a landscape that all players climb together.
Each time any player improves her own payoff by switching, the landscape goes up by the same amount.
In a finite game the landscape has a highest point, and there nobody can improve.
Suppose each driver's payoff is the number of other drivers who keep to the same side of the road.
This is a potential game, and the potential is the number of pairs of drivers who agree.
