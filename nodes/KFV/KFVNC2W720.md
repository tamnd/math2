## Summary

A weight of a [[representation|TZAZ163QGK]] of a Lie algebra is a joint eigenvalue of an abelian subalgebra, recorded as a linear form.
For a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]], one uses a [[Cartan subalgebra|BB098JPWGA]], and every finite-dimensional module is the direct sum of its weight spaces.
The weights of the adjoint representation are $0$ and the roots.
Weights and their multiplicities organise the whole finite-dimensional representation theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Commuting matrices can be diagonalised at the same time, when each of them is diagonalisable.
On a common eigenvector, each matrix acts by a number, and these numbers depend linearly on the matrix.
That linear rule is a weight.
For example, the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(a, -a)$ acts on the two coordinate vectors of $\C^2$ by $a$ and by $-a$, so the weights are the linear rules $\operatorname{diag}(a, -a) \mapsto a$ and $\operatorname{diag}(a, -a) \mapsto -a$.
