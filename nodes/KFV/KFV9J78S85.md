## Definition

Let the environment be a large reservoir at fixed [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]] $T_0 > 0$ and pressure $p_0$.
Let a closed system have [[internal energy|F6JVQ12X43]] $U$, volume $V$ and [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]] $S$.
The *dead state* is the state of [[thermodynamic equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]] with the environment at $T_0$ and $p_0$.
Let $U_0$, $V_0$, $S_0$ be the values of these quantities in the dead state.
The *exergy* of the system is
$$X = (U - U_0) + p_0 (V - V_0) - T_0 (S - S_0).$$
It equals the maximum useful [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] that the system can deliver while it exchanges heat only with the environment and ends in the dead state.
Useful work means the work beyond the work done against the environment pressure.

## Remarks

Kinetic and potential energy of the system as a whole, when present, are added to $X$ in full.
For a steady flow, the specific flow exergy is $(h - h_0) - T_0 (s - s_0)$ plus kinetic and potential terms, where $h$ is the specific enthalpy and $s$ the specific entropy.

Example: a body of constant heat capacity $C$ at temperature $T$, with negligible volume change, has $U - U_0 = C(T - T_0)$ and $S - S_0 = C \ln(T / T_0)$.
So $X = C\big(T - T_0 - T_0 \ln(T / T_0)\big) = C T_0 (y - 1 - \ln y)$ with $y = T / T_0$.
Since $\ln y < y - 1$ for $y \ne 1$, the exergy is positive whenever $T \ne T_0$, both for a hot and for a cold body.

Non-example: the internal energy $U$ alone is not the exergy.
A system already in the dead state has $X = 0$ although $U_0$ may be large, because no work can be extracted from it with that environment.

By the second law, $X \ge 0$, and $X = 0$ in the dead state.
