## Summary

The Zoeppritz equations give the amplitudes of the reflected and transmitted waves when a plane elastic wave hits a flat interface between two solids.
An incident P-wave in general produces four waves: a reflected P-wave, a reflected S-wave, a transmitted P-wave and a transmitted S-wave.
The equations are a $4 \times 4$ linear system expressing that displacement and traction are continuous across the interface.
They are the exact basis of amplitude versus offset analysis in exploration seismology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a wave reaches the boundary between two rocks, part of it bounces back and part goes through.
In a solid, the wave can also change type: a push wave can create shear waves, and the other way round.
How much energy goes into each of the four outgoing waves depends on the angle of arrival and on the density and speeds of the two rocks.
At vertical incidence the answer is simple: the reflection depends only on the contrast in density times speed.
