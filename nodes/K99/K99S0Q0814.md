## Summary

A probabilistic Turing machine is a [[Turing machine|758EYCN27B]] that may flip fair coins and choose its next move by the outcome.
Its output on an input is then a random variable, and one asks for the probability of acceptance.
It is the standard model of randomised algorithms.
Classes such as BPP, the problems decided in polynomial time with error probability at most $1/3$, are defined with it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some algorithms work better when they are allowed to guess at random.
A probabilistic machine is a Turing machine with a coin: at each step it flips the coin and follows one of two rules.
On the same input it may give different answers on different runs.
One asks that it answers correctly with high probability, say at least $2/3$.
Running it many times and taking the majority answer then makes errors extremely rare.
