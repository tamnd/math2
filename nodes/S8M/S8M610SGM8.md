## Example

The simplest law of logic, "if $p$ then $p$", is not an axiom of the [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]] L, so we must build it.
Let $p$ be a propositional variable.
We use the axiom schemas
$$\text{(A1, or K)} \quad \varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi), \qquad \text{(A2, or S)} \quad (\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)) \to ((\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi)),$$
and the rule [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] (MP).
The following five formulas form a derivation of $p \to p$ from no hypotheses:
$$\begin{array}{rl} 1. & p \to ((p \to p) \to p) \\ 2. & (p \to ((p \to p) \to p)) \to ((p \to (p \to p)) \to (p \to p)) \\ 3. & (p \to (p \to p)) \to (p \to p) \\ 4. & p \to (p \to p) \\ 5. & p \to p \end{array}$$

We check each line against the definition of a derivation.

1. Line 1 is the instance of A1 with $\varphi := p$ and $\psi := p \to p$.
2. Line 2 is the instance of A2 with $\varphi := p$, $\psi := p \to p$ and $\chi := p$.
   Its antecedent $\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)$ becomes $p \to ((p \to p) \to p)$, which is line 1.
3. Line 3 follows by MP from lines 1 and 2, since line 2 is the formula "line 1 $\to$ line 3".
4. Line 4 is the instance of A1 with $\varphi := p$ and $\psi := p$.
5. Line 5 follows by MP from lines 4 and 3, since line 3 is the formula "line 4 $\to$ line 5".

Each line is an axiom or follows by MP from earlier lines, and the last line is $p \to p$.
So $\vdash p \to p$.

## Remarks

### Where the idea is

The choice $\psi := p \to p$ in line 2 is the one step that needs an idea.
It makes the antecedent of line 2 an instance of A1, so that one step of MP removes it.
The new antecedent, $p \to (p \to p)$, is again an instance of A1, and a second step of MP finishes.
With $\psi := p$ in line 2 instead, the instance is $(p \to (p \to p)) \to ((p \to p) \to (p \to p))$.
After one step of MP we would need $p \to p$ itself as the next antecedent, which is the formula we want, so the attempt goes in a circle.

### What changes with the formula

If $p$ is replaced by any formula $\varphi$ at every place, each line stays an instance of the same axiom or a step of MP.
So the same five lines prove $\vdash \varphi \to \varphi$, the theorem T1 of the [[basic theorems of L|Y060MF2NZ7]].
The derivation never uses A3, so it works in every Hilbert system with A1, A2 and MP, such as the [[implicational propositional calculus|ZMVBAY7ZZD]].
Under the Curry-Howard correspondence, the derivation is the combinator term $S K K$, which is the identity function.

### Watch out

A tempting wrong step applies MP to lines 2 and 4.
Line 2 has the antecedent $p \to ((p \to p) \to p)$, not $p \to (p \to p)$, so the step does not match the rule.
MP needs the exact formula $\alpha$ and the exact formula $\alpha \to \beta$, symbol for symbol.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"If $p$ then $p$" is true in every case, but L does not list it among its axioms.
So we build it from the axiom patterns, with modus ponens as the only tool.
Two lines fill in the patterns, one more line fills in the first pattern again, and two steps of modus ponens finish.
Each line can be checked by matching it against a pattern, with no thought about meaning.

### L2 human

We use the same method to derive $q \to q$ for another variable $q$.

1. $q \to ((q \to q) \to q)$, by A1 with $\varphi := q$ and $\psi := q \to q$.
2. $(q \to ((q \to q) \to q)) \to ((q \to (q \to q)) \to (q \to q))$, by A2 with $\varphi := q$, $\psi := q \to q$ and $\chi := q$.
3. $(q \to (q \to q)) \to (q \to q)$, by MP from lines 1 and 2.
4. $q \to (q \to q)$, by A1 with $\varphi := q$ and $\psi := q$.
5. $q \to q$, by MP from lines 4 and 3.

The typical use of this derivation is inside the proof of the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]], in the case where a line of a derivation is the hypothesis itself.
