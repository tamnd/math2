## Definition

*Special relativity* is the physical theory with the following structure.

1. The set of events is [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]] $M$, a real affine space of dimension $4$ whose translations carry the Minkowski form $\eta$.
2. An inertial frame is an affine coordinate system $(x^0, x^1, x^2, x^3)$ on $M$ in which $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$; here $x^0 = ct$, with time $t$ and speed of light $c > 0$.
3. Two inertial frames are related by an element of the [[Poincaré group|8ZQ9TA4Q2Q]], that is, by a [[Lorentz transformation|WEVGN4C7PQ]] followed by a translation.
4. The laws of physics keep their form under every change of inertial frame; this is the [[principle of relativity|5ABAVATERQ]] for the Poincaré group.

## Remarks

Example: the law "a free particle has a straight world line" is a law of special relativity.
Indeed, a Poincaré transformation is affine and sends straight lines to straight lines.
Non-example: Newton's law of gravitation uses the instantaneous distance between two bodies, so it is not a law of special relativity.
The reason is that simultaneity, and so the instantaneous distance, changes under a Lorentz boost.
Many texts use the opposite signature $(+, -, -, -)$; the physics is the same.
When the speed $v$ of all bodies is small compared with $c$, Lorentz transformations reduce to Galilean transformations and special relativity reduces to Newtonian mechanics.
