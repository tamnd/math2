## Summary

Consider a representation of a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]].
Its highest weight is the [[weight|KFVNC2W720]] that lies above all other weights, in the order given by the positive roots.
Every finite-dimensional irreducible representation has a highest weight, and by the [[theorem of the highest weight|J1EFY61K87]] this weight determines it.
A vector of highest weight is killed by every positive root vector.
Highest weights give the standard labels of irreducible representations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Weights of a representation can be drawn as points in a space, and the positive roots give a direction "up".
The highest weight is the top point of the picture, above all the others.
For the representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ of dimension $n + 1$, the weights are $n, n - 2, \dots, -n$, and the highest weight is $n$.
Knowing only this top point, one can rebuild the whole irreducible representation.
