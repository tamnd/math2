## Definition

Let an imaging system work with light of vacuum wavelength $\lambda$.
Let its object side be a medium of [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] $n$.
Let $\alpha$ be the half-angle of the cone of rays that the system accepts from an object point.
The *numerical aperture* is $\mathrm{NA} = n \sin \alpha$.
The system is *diffraction-limited* when its [[point spread function|HHWESQD7QB]] is that of an aberration-free system with the same aperture.
The *diffraction limit* of the system, in the sense of Abbe, is the length
$$d = \frac{\lambda}{2 \, \mathrm{NA}}.$$

## Remarks

In incoherent imaging, the [[optical transfer function|Z7S3Z4KGRS]] of a diffraction-limited system vanishes at all spatial frequencies above the cutoff $2\,\mathrm{NA}/\lambda$.
So a periodic object with period below $d$ is imaged as a uniform grey: $d$ is the smallest period that the system can pass.

Example: an oil immersion objective with $\mathrm{NA} = 1.4$ at $\lambda = 560$ nm has $d = 560 / 2.8 = 200$ nm.

Non-example: a lens with strong spherical aberration has a larger blur spot than the aberration-free system with the same aperture.
So it is not diffraction-limited, and its real resolution is worse than $d$.

Super-resolution microscopy goes beyond $d$ by using extra information, such as switching of fluorescent molecules or structured illumination.
This does not contradict the definition, which concerns a fixed linear imaging system.
