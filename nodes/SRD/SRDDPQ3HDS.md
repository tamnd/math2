## Definition

Let $\kappa = 8\pi G/c^4$, where $G$ is the gravitational constant and $c$ the speed of light, and let $f \colon \R \to \R$ be a smooth function.
Let $g$ be a [[Lorentzian metric|BY4QYYS788]] on a four-dimensional manifold and $\psi$ the matter fields.
*$f(R)$ gravity* (in the metric formalism) is the [[alternative to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]] with action
$$S = \frac{1}{2\kappa} \int f(R) \sqrt{-g}\, d^4x + S_m[g, \psi],$$
where $R$ is the [[scalar curvature|ZHX2VYQDZ3]] of $g$.

## Remarks

Varying $g$ gives
$$f'(R) R_{ab} - \frac12 f(R)\, g_{ab} + \left(g_{ab}\square - \nabla_a\nabla_b\right) f'(R) = \kappa T_{ab}.$$
Here $R_{ab}$ is the [[Ricci tensor|4GTNM0WHDH]], $\nabla$ the [[covariant derivative|5FZDQQHQKM]], $\square = \nabla^a\nabla_a$ and $T_{ab}$ the [[stress–energy tensor|REDZ92MKXX]].
These equations are of fourth order in $g$ when $f'' \ne 0$.
Example: $f(R) = R - 2\Lambda$ gives $f' = 1$, and the equations become Einstein's equations with cosmological constant $\Lambda$, from the [[Einstein–Hilbert action|JPE3A1SN7V]].
Non-example: a Lagrangian $R_{ab}R^{ab}$ depends on the Ricci tensor and not only on $R$, so it does not define an $f(R)$ theory.
When $f'' \ne 0$, setting $\phi = f'(R)$ rewrites the theory as a [[scalar–tensor theory|MWX9XPYEYY]]: [[Brans–Dicke theory|9WAYAKST60]] with $\omega = 0$ and the potential $V(\phi) = R\phi - f(R)$, with $R$ expressed through $\phi$.
In the Palatini formalism, where the metric and the connection are varied independently, one obtains a different theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

$f(R)$ gravity keeps the idea that gravity is curvature, but changes the rule that says how much curvature costs.
The extra freedom behaves like an additional scalar field.
