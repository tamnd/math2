## Summary

The cobweb model describes a market in which producers decide today's output from last period's price.
The price then adjusts so that demand absorbs this output.
The result is a first-order [[recurrence relation|0FYX44GP92]] for the price, whose path oscillates around the equilibrium price.
Whether the oscillations die out depends on the relative slopes of supply and demand.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of farmers who plant this year based on last year's price.
If last year's price was high, they plant a lot, the harvest floods the market and the price falls.
Then next year they plant little, and the price rises again.
On a supply and demand diagram the path of prices and quantities looks like a spider's web.
For example, with demand $10 - 2p$ and supply $1 + p$, the price $p_t = (9 - p_{t-1})/2$ moves towards $3$, swinging above and below it.
