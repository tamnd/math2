## Summary

The bending of plates is the deformation of a thin flat elastic body under loads perpendicular to its surface.
In the linear theory of thin plates the deflection solves the [[biharmonic plate equation|X1SGK619VJ]] with boundary conditions that depend on how the edges are supported.
From the deflection one gets the bending moments and hence the stresses, which are largest at the two faces of the plate.
Classical closed-form solutions exist for circular and rectangular plates, and finite elements treat general shapes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A glass table top, a floor or a drum skin made of steel all bend a little under weight.
How much they bend depends on the load, on the size and thickness of the plate and on how the edges are held.
A plate clamped all around its rim bends much less than one that merely rests on its rim.
For a round plate of radius $a$ clamped at the edge, the sag at the centre is $qa^4/(64D)$.
If it only rests on its rim, the sag is about four times larger for steel-like materials.
