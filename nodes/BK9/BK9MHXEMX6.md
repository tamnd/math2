## Summary

The winner's curse is an effect in [[auctions|86ZWXGHNGB]] where the object has a common value that no bidder knows exactly.
Each bidder sees only a noisy estimate, and the winner tends to be the bidder whose estimate was too high.
A bidder who bids her unconditional estimate therefore overpays on average when she wins.
Rational bidding corrects for this by conditioning on the event of winning.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine ten people bidding for a jar of coins whose total nobody knows.
Their guesses scatter around the true amount, some too low and some too high.
The person who wins is the one who guessed highest, so the winning guess is usually above the truth.
If each person bids the amount they guessed, the winner usually pays more than the jar holds.
Winning is bad news about your estimate, and smart bidders shade their bids down to account for it.
