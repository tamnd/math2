## Summary

Mathematical modelling of infectious diseases describes how an infection spreads through a population by equations or random processes.
The models split hosts into states such as susceptible, infected and recovered, and give rules for moving between states.
They predict whether an outbreak grows, how large it becomes, and how vaccination or other controls change it.
Key quantities are the [[basic reproduction number|VY4H52692D]] and the final size of an epidemic.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A model of an epidemic is a simplified story of who infects whom, written in mathematics.
Each person is in one box: not yet infected, infected, or recovered.
People move from box to box at rates fixed by the model, and the equations track how many people are in each box over time.
For example, if each infected person infects two others before recovering, the number of cases first doubles in every generation of infection.
