## Summary

An inviscid flow is a flow of a fluid with zero [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]], so that the only internal force is pressure.
Its motion is governed by the [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]].
Real fluids are viscous.
But at high [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]] the flow away from walls and wakes is close to inviscid.
This makes the idealisation the basis of classical aerodynamics and wave theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Friction inside a fluid makes neighbouring layers drag on each other.
In an inviscid flow this friction is ignored: layers can slide past each other freely, and only pressure pushes the fluid around.
Air flowing quickly over a wing is nearly like this, except in a thin layer next to the wing.
For example, a fluid turning as a rigid body, $u = (-y, x, 0)$, is an inviscid flow when the pressure rises outward as $(x^2 + y^2)/2$.
This pressure supplies the force that keeps the parcels on circles.
