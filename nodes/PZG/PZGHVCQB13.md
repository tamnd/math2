## Summary

A globally hyperbolic spacetime is a causal, time-oriented Lorentzian manifold.
In it, the set of events lying between any two events is compact.
It is the strongest of the usual [[causality conditions|EKA74X1SYS]].
Globally hyperbolic spacetimes are exactly those with a Cauchy surface, a moment of time from which the whole past and future are determined.
They are the natural setting for the initial value problem of general relativity and for wave equations on curved spacetimes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a globally hyperbolic spacetime, physics is predictable: data given at one instant fix everything before and after.
The definition asks that between any two events there is only a bounded, well-behaved region of events that lie causally between them.
Flat Minkowski spacetime is globally hyperbolic: between two events the in-between region is a closed double cone.
Removing a single point from it already breaks the property, since signals can then disappear into the hole.
