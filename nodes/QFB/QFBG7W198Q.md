## Summary

Forced convection is [[convective heat transfer|RK24AYNG4Z]] in which an outside agent moves the fluid.
The agent may be a pump, a fan, a moving body or the wind.
Because the flow is imposed, it is usually much stronger than buoyant flow, and it gives much higher heat transfer rates.
Its heat transfer is described by the [[Nusselt number|THH3V9AHDN]] as a function of the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]] and the [[Prandtl number|C4KSBMR413]].
Car radiators, heat exchangers and cooling fans all rely on it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Blowing on hot soup cools it faster than letting it stand.
The moving air carries the warm air away from the surface and brings fresh cool air in.
The flow does not come from the heat: you make it.
A fan cooling a computer chip and a pump pushing water through a car radiator work the same way.
