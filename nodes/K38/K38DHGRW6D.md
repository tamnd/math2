## Summary

The proper motion of a star is the rate at which its direction on the celestial sphere changes.
The effects of the observer's motion are removed first.
It is the angular part of the star's real motion through space, seen from the Sun.
It is measured in arcseconds or milliarcseconds per year, and with the distance it gives the star's velocity across the line of sight.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The constellations look fixed, but every star drifts very slowly across the sky.
This drift is the proper motion, the visible part of the star's own travel through space.
A typical bright star moves less than a tenth of an arcsecond per year, far too little to see by eye in a lifetime.
The fastest one, Barnard's Star, moves about $10$ arcseconds per year, the width of the full Moon in under two centuries.
