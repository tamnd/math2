## Example

Let $V = \R^2$ and $W = \R^3$, and form the [[direct product|0MM085RX00]] $V \times W$.
We check the [[dimension formula|06HN47VPTH]] $\dim(V \times W) = 2 + 3 = 5$ by finding a basis.

1. The vectors of $V \times W$ are the pairs $((a, b), (c, d, e))$ with real entries, and the operations act on each part.
2. Put the standard basis of $\R^2$ in the first slot and the standard basis of $\R^3$ in the second slot:
$$f_1 = ((1, 0), 0), \quad f_2 = ((0, 1), 0), \quad f_3 = (0, (1, 0, 0)), \quad f_4 = (0, (0, 1, 0)), \quad f_5 = (0, (0, 0, 1)).$$
3. The five vectors span $V \times W$, because every pair is a combination of them:
$$((a, b), (c, d, e)) = a f_1 + b f_2 + c f_3 + d f_4 + e f_5.$$
4. The five vectors are independent.
   If $a f_1 + b f_2 + c f_3 + d f_4 + e f_5 = 0$, then by step 3 the pair $((a, b), (c, d, e))$ is the zero pair $((0, 0), (0, 0, 0))$, so all five scalars are $0$.
5. So $f_1, \dots, f_5$ is a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]], and the [[dimension|05G2G0P224]] of $V \times W$ is $5$.

The map $((a, b), (c, d, e)) \mapsto (a, b, c, d, e)$ is linear and sends the basis $f_1, \dots, f_5$ to the standard basis of $\R^5$, so it is an isomorphism $\R^2 \times \R^3 \cong \R^5$.

## Remarks

The example shows how the proof of the dimension formula works: each basis of a factor is placed in its own slot, with zero in the other slot.
The first two vectors are a basis of the subspace $V \times 0$, and the last three are a basis of $0 \times W$.
These two subspaces meet only in the zero pair and together span $V \times W$, so $V \times W = (V \times 0) \oplus (0 \times W)$ as an internal direct sum.

If we change $W$ to $\R^k$, the same construction gives a basis of $2 + k$ vectors.
If we add a third factor, say $\R^2 \times \R^3 \times \R$, we get a sixth basis vector $(0, 0, 1)$ and dimension $6$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A vector of $\R^2 \times \R^3$ is a list of two numbers followed by a list of three numbers.
That is five numbers in all, so the product behaves just like $\R^5$.
For example, the pair $((1, 2), (3, 4, 5))$ corresponds to $(1, 2, 3, 4, 5)$.

### L2 human

We write the pair $u = ((2, -1), (0, 3, 1))$ in the basis $f_1, \dots, f_5$.
By step 3 the coordinates are the entries themselves:
$$u = 2 f_1 - f_2 + 0 f_3 + 3 f_4 + f_5.$$
Check: $2 f_1 - f_2 = ((2, -1), 0)$ and $3 f_4 + f_5 = (0, (0, 3, 1))$, and their sum is $u$.
Watch out: the dimension of a product is the sum of the dimensions, not the product, so $\dim(\R^2 \times \R^3)$ is $5$ and not $6$.
