## Example

We show that the logic $\mathsf{GL}$ of [[provability logic|WJK0YA4GP2]] proves
$$\neg \square \bot \to \neg \square \neg \square \bot.$$
Under a realization, $\neg \square \bot$ becomes the [[consistency statement|4NBAX3VRQ7]] $\mathrm{Con}(T)$, since $\bot^* = (0 = S0)$.
So the formula says: if $T$ is consistent, then $T$ does not prove $\mathrm{Con}(T)$.
This is the [[second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]], formalized inside $T$.

1. [[Löb's axiom|QQ6PZFGGB3]] with $\bot$ substituted for $p$ is $\square(\square \bot \to \bot) \to \square \bot$.
2. By the definition $\neg \varphi := \varphi \to \bot$, the formula $\square \bot \to \bot$ is $\neg \square \bot$. So step 1 is $\square \neg \square \bot \to \square \bot$.
3. The contrapositive of step 2 is $\neg \square \bot \to \neg \square \neg \square \bot$, by propositional logic.
4. Check on a frame. Take the frame with worlds $1$ and $2$ and $1 R 2$ only. At $2$, $\square \bot$ is true, since $2$ has no successors, so $\neg \square \bot$ is false at $2$.
5. At $1$, $\square \bot$ is false, since $1$ sees $2$. So $\neg \square \bot$ is true at $1$.
6. At $1$, $\square \neg \square \bot$ is false, since $1$ sees $2$, where $\neg \square \bot$ is false. So the conclusion $\neg \square \neg \square \bot$ is true at $1$, as step 3 requires.

## Remarks

The example shows that the modal form of the second incompleteness theorem is a one-line consequence of Löb's axiom.
The converse direction $\square \bot \to \square \neg \square \bot$ also holds in $\mathsf{GL}$: apply necessitation and the axiom $\mathrm{K}$ to the tautology $\bot \to \neg \square \bot$.
So $\mathsf{GL} \vdash \square \neg \square \bot \leftrightarrow \square \bot$: a theory proves its own consistency exactly when it is inconsistent.
The premise cannot be dropped: $\mathsf{GL} \nvdash \neg \square \neg \square \bot$, because at a world without successors $\square \neg \square \bot$ is true.
Step 2 uses that $\square \bot \to \bot$ and $\neg \square \bot$ are the same formula, so the argument is special to $\bot$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A consistent system of axioms cannot prove "I am consistent".
Provability logic writes this law with boxes: "not provable falsehood" implies "not provable that falsehood is not provable".
The law follows in one step from Löb's axiom.
On a picture with two points $1 \to 2$, the law holds at both points.

### L2 human

1. Write $c$ for $\neg \square \bot$, the consistency statement.
2. Löb's axiom for $\bot$ gives $\square c \to \square \bot$.
3. By contraposition, $c \to \neg \square c$.
4. Realized in $\mathsf{PA}$, this is $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA}) \to \neg \mathrm{Prov}(\ulcorner \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA}) \urcorner)$.

The typical use is to derive consequences of the second incompleteness theorem inside $\mathsf{GL}$ instead of inside arithmetic.
A trap: the formula does not say that $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is true; $\mathsf{GL}$ does not prove $\neg \square \bot$.
