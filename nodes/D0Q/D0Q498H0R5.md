## Proof

We assume that $\mathsf{PA}$ proves $\mathrm{PH}$, find in a non-standard model a least interval on which $\mathrm{PH}$ succeeds, and use an initial segment that is a model of $\mathsf{PA}$ to find a shorter one.
This proves part 1 of the [[Paris-Harrington theorem|509ZWA0R26]] from one cited lemma, and we cite parts 2 and 3.
Write $\mathrm{PH}$ for the [[strengthened finite Ramsey theorem|4P2BAT6YC3]] and $\mathsf{PA}$ for [[Peano arithmetic|MPSCREYC3M]].
A finite set $Z$ of positive integers is *large* if $\lvert Z \rvert \ge \min Z$.

### The indicator

For $1 \le a \le b$, let $Y(a, b)$ be the greatest $e \le b$ with the following property.
Every $e$-coloring of $[\{ a, \dots, b \}]^e$ has a large homogeneous set $Z \subseteq \{ a, \dots, b \}$ with $\lvert Z \rvert \ge e$.
The property is decided by a search over finitely many colorings, and the number of colorings is bounded by iterated exponentials of $b$.
So the relation $Y(a, b) \ge c$ is defined by a formula whose quantifiers are bounded by terms built from $+$, $\cdot$ and $x \mapsto 2^x$.

### Lemma 1: PH gives large values of the indicator

$\mathsf{PA}$ proves: if $\mathrm{PH}$ holds, then for all $a, e \ge 1$ there is $b$ with $Y(a, b) \ge e$.

1. We apply $\mathrm{PH}$ with exponent $e$, with $e + 1$ colors and with $m = a + e$.
   Let $N$ be the number that it gives, and let $b = \max(N, a)$.
2. Let $c$ be an $e$-coloring of $[\{ a, \dots, b \}]^e$.
   We extend it to $[\{ 1, \dots, b \}]^e$ by giving the color $e + 1$ to each set whose least element is below $a$.
3. We apply step 1 to the restriction to $[\{ 1, \dots, N \}]^e$.
   This gives a large set $Z$, homogeneous for the extended coloring, with $\lvert Z \rvert \ge a + e$.
4. Suppose that $Z$ has the color $e + 1$.
   Then $Z$ has at most $e - 1$ elements $\ge a$, because $e$ of them would form a set with a color from $c$, at most $e$.
5. Then $\lvert Z \rvert \le (a - 1) + (e - 1) < a + e$, which contradicts step 3.
   So the color of $Z$ is at most $e$.
6. If $Z$ had an element $z < a$, an $e$-subset of $Z$ with least element $z$ would have the color $e + 1$, because $\lvert Z \rvert \ge e$.
   So $Z \subseteq \{ a, \dots, b \}$.
7. So $Z$ is a large homogeneous set for $c$ with $\lvert Z \rvert \ge e$, and also $e \le \lvert Z \rvert \le b$.
8. So $e$ has the property in the definition of $Y(a, b)$, and $Y(a, b) \ge e$.
9. Each step is a finite combinatorial argument about the given numbers, so $\mathsf{PA}$ proves the lemma.

### Lemma 2 (cited) and Lemma 3

**Lemma 2** (indicator lemma, Paris and Harrington).
Let $M$ be a countable [[non-standard model|1QWN5CV4CG]] of $\mathsf{PA}$, and let $a < b$ be in $M$.
If $Y(a, b)$ is non-standard in $M$, then there is an initial segment $I$ of $M$ with $a \in I$, $b \notin I$ and $I \models \mathsf{PA}$.
We cite this lemma without proof.
Its proof builds $I$ from a sequence of nested homogeneous sets that behave like indiscernibles, and it uses the condition $\lvert Z \rvert \ge \min Z$ at every stage.
In the terms of [[indicators|JWTZ8NVVGK]], it says that $Y$ is an indicator for the initial segments that are models of $\mathsf{PA}$.

**Lemma 3** (absoluteness).
Let $I$ be an initial segment of $M$ with $I \models \mathsf{PA}$, and let $a, b', c \in I$.
Then $I \models Y(a, b') \ge c$ if and only if $M \models Y(a, b') \ge c$.

10. $I$ satisfies "$2^x$ exists" for each $x \in I$.
    The graph of $x \mapsto 2^x$ has a definition with bounded quantifiers, so $2^x$ is the same element in $I$ and in $M$.
11. So $I$ is closed under the terms that bound the quantifiers in the definition of $Y(a, b') \ge c$.
12. A formula with bounded quantifiers has the same truth value in an initial segment and in the whole model when its parameters and bounds lie in the segment.
    This holds by induction on the formula, because each bounded quantifier ranges over elements below a bound in $I$, and these are the same in $I$ and in $M$.

### Proof of part 1

13. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\mathsf{PA}$ proves $\mathrm{PH}$.
    Then $\mathsf{PA}$ proves $\forall a \, \exists b \; Y(a, b) \ge a$, by Lemma 1 with $e = a$.
14. By the [[compactness theorem|1NR3HAWG9D]] and the downward Löwenheim-Skolem theorem, there is a countable non-standard model $M$ of $\mathsf{PA}$.
    Let $a \in M$ be non-standard.
15. By step 13, $M \models \exists b \; Y(a, b) \ge a$.
    By the least number principle in $M$, there is a least such $b$ in $M$.
16. Then $Y(a, b) \ge a$ is non-standard, so Lemma 2 gives an initial segment $I$ with $a \in I$, $b \notin I$ and $I \models \mathsf{PA}$.
17. By step 13 applied in the model $I$ of $\mathsf{PA}$, $I \models \exists b' \; Y(a, b') \ge a$.
    Take such a $b' \in I$.
18. By Lemma 3, $M \models Y(a, b') \ge a$.
19. Also $b' < b$, because $b' \in I$, $b \notin I$ and $I$ is an initial segment.
20. This contradicts the choice of $b$ as the least such element, so $\mathsf{PA}$ does not prove $\mathrm{PH}$.

### Parts 2 and 3: cited results

21. **Lemma 4** (cited, Paris and Harrington).
    $\mathsf{PA}$ proves that $\mathrm{PH}$ is equivalent to $1\text{-}\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, the statement that every [[$\Sigma_1$|CV2E6JZHVP]] sentence provable in $\mathsf{PA}$ is true.
    This is part 2, and it gives that $\mathsf{PA} + 1\text{-}\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ proves $\mathrm{PH}$.
22. The [[compactness proof|Q0RKBRZXBD]] of $\mathrm{PH}$ uses the infinite Ramsey theorem and an infinite tree argument.
    Both are provable in second-order arithmetic and in $\mathsf{ZFC}$, which gives the rest of part 3.
23. **Lemma 5** (cited, Ketonen and Solovay 1981).
    The least witness $\sigma(x, x, x)$ of $\mathrm{PH}$ eventually dominates every function that $\mathsf{PA}$ proves total.
    This is the growth form of part 1.

## Idea

If $\mathsf{PA}$ proved $\mathrm{PH}$, every non-standard model would contain an interval $[a, b]$ with a non-standard indicator value, and a least such $b$ for a given infinite $a$.
The indicator lemma turns this interval into an initial segment that is itself a model of $\mathsf{PA}$ and that ends below $b$.
Inside that segment $\mathrm{PH}$ holds again, so it finds a shorter interval, and the shorter interval still works in the big model.
That contradicts the choice of $b$.

The trick with the extra color $e + 1$ in Lemma 1 moves $\mathrm{PH}$ from intervals that start at $1$ to intervals that start at $a$.
Largeness is what makes the indicator lemma work: a homogeneous set that is long compared to its least element can be thinned out many times and stay long.

## Variants

Lemma 4 gives a second proof of part 1.
If $\mathsf{PA}$ proved $\mathrm{PH}$, it would prove $1\text{-}\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ and so $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, against [[Gödel's second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]].

Ketonen and Solovay gave a direct combinatorial proof through growth rates, with no non-standard models.
The same scheme with another indicator proves the [[Kanamori-McAloon theorem|NM5WYBK6BG]].
