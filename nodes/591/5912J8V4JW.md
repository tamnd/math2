## Definition

Let a body consist of a rigid–perfectly plastic or elastic–perfectly plastic material with a convex [[yield surface|CWWCEXZ8BH]] and the [[associated flow rule|80NPDZSKBT]].
Let it carry loads $\lambda P$, where $P$ is a fixed load pattern and $\lambda \ge 0$ is the load factor.
The *collapse load factor* $\lambda_c$ is the value of $\lambda$ at which the body can undergo unrestricted plastic flow under constant loads.
*Limit analysis* is the determination of $\lambda_c$, or of bounds on it, through the following two notions.

1. A *statically admissible* stress field is a [[stress|KG2XWEMJ30]] field that is in equilibrium with $\lambda P$ and lies inside or on the yield surface everywhere. The lower bound theorem says that then $\lambda \le \lambda_c$.
2. A *kinematically admissible* mechanism is a velocity field that satisfies the kinematic boundary conditions and on which $P$ does positive work. Equating the rate of work of $\lambda P$ with the rate of plastic dissipation gives a value $\lambda$. The upper bound theorem says that then $\lambda \ge \lambda_c$.

## Remarks

Example: a simply supported beam of span $L$, with plastic moment $M_p$, carries a central point load $\lambda P$.
The bending moment $\lambda P L / 4$ at midspan reaches $M_p$ when $\lambda P = 4 M_p / L$, which gives a lower bound.
The mechanism with a hinge at midspan gives the same value as an upper bound, so the collapse load is $4M_p/L$.
Non-example: a body of linear elastic material has no collapse load, because its displacement stays proportional to the load for every load, so it never flows without bound.
The two theorems rely on the convexity of the yield surface and on the normality of the flow rule; without normality, as for many soils, the bounds can fail.
