## Example

One matrix can have several row echelon forms, but they all lead to the same reduced form.
Let $M = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{R}$.

1. The step $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1$ gives $U_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$.
2. $U_1$ has leading entries in columns $1$ and $2$ and no zero rows, so it is in [[row echelon form|0F27PBRWD7]].
3. Start again from $M$. The swap $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$ gives $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.
4. The step $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - \tfrac13 R_1$ makes row 2 equal to $(0, 2 - \tfrac43) = (0, \tfrac23)$, so $U_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 0 & \tfrac23 \end{bmatrix}$.
5. $U_2$ is also in row echelon form, and $U_2 \ne U_1$.
6. Reduce $U_1$ further. The step $R_2 \leftarrow -\tfrac12 R_2$ gives $(0, 1)$, and then $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 2R_2$ gives $(1, 0)$. The result is $I_2$.
7. Reduce $U_2$ further. The step $R_1 \leftarrow \tfrac13 R_1$ gives $(1, \tfrac43)$, the step $R_2 \leftarrow \tfrac32 R_2$ gives $(0, 1)$, and then $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - \tfrac43 R_2$ gives $(1, 0)$. The result is again $I_2$.

## Remarks

The two echelon forms share their pivot positions $(1, 1)$ and $(2, 2)$, and their [[reduced row echelon forms|0NNR68BFRY]] agree, as the uniqueness theorem requires.

Even the product of the pivots changes, from $1 \cdot (-2) = -2$ to $3 \cdot \tfrac23 = 2$.
The reason is the swap in step 3, which changes the sign of the determinant, and $\det M = 4 - 6 = -2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two students can simplify the same table in different ways and get different staircases.
Both can be right.
If both go on to the fully reduced form, they get the same answer.

### L2 human

1. When you compare your echelon form with a book, compare the pivot positions, not the numbers.
2. To compare the numbers, reduce both to the RREF.

Watch out: do not conclude that you made a mistake only because your echelon form differs from the one in the answers.
