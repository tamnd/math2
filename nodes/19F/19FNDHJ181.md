## Example

Let $M = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ over $\R$.

1. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1$ gives $U_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$.
2. $U_1$ has leading entries in columns $1$ and $2$, with no zero rows, so it is in [[row echelon form|0F27PBRWD7]].
3. Starting again, $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$ gives $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.
4. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - \tfrac13 R_1$ gives $(0, 2 - \tfrac43) = (0, \tfrac23)$, so $U_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 0 & \tfrac23 \end{bmatrix}$.
5. $U_2$ is also in row echelon form, and $U_2 \ne U_1$.
6. Reduce $U_1$: $R_2 \leftarrow -\tfrac12 R_2$ gives $(0, 1)$, then $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 2R_2$ gives $(1, 0)$. The result is $I_2$.
7. Reduce $U_2$: $R_1 \leftarrow \tfrac13 R_1$ gives $(1, \tfrac43)$, $R_2 \leftarrow \tfrac32 R_2$ gives $(0, 1)$, then $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - \tfrac43 R_2$ gives $(1, 0)$. The result is $I_2$.

## Remarks

Row echelon forms are not unique, but both have pivots in positions $(1, 1)$ and $(2, 2)$.
Their [[reduced row echelon forms|0NNR68BFRY]] agree, as the uniqueness theorem requires.
Even the product of the pivots changes: $1 \cdot (-2) = -2$ but $3 \cdot \tfrac23 = 2$, because the swap changes the sign of the determinant.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two students can simplify the same table in different ways and get different staircases.
Both can be right.
If both go on to the fully reduced form, they get the same answer.

### L2 human

1. When you compare your echelon form with a book, compare the pivot positions, not the numbers.
2. To compare the numbers, reduce both to the RREF.

A trap: do not conclude that you made a mistake only because your echelon form differs from the one in the answers.
