## Summary

A Stoneley wave is an elastic wave that travels along the plane interface between two solids.
It dies out with distance from the interface on both sides.
It is the analogue at an internal boundary of the [[Rayleigh wave|X32RRM7ATM]] at a free surface.
Unlike the Rayleigh wave it does not exist for every pair of materials, only when their densities and elastic constants lie in a restricted range.
Robert Stoneley studied it in 1924; the related wave at a fluid–solid interface, often called a Scholte wave, is used in borehole acoustics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Glue two different blocks of rock together along a flat face.
A Stoneley wave is a ripple that runs along the glued face and stays close to it, fading into both blocks.
It is a kind of wave that lives on the boundary, not in the volume.
In an oil well filled with mud, a similar wave runs along the wall of the borehole and helps to locate fractures.
