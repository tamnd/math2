## Example

Let $\Lambda$ be a finite graph with edge set $E$, and give each vertex $j$ a spin $S$.
Its spin operators $S^x_j, S^y_j, S^z_j$ act on the factor $\C^{2S+1}$ of the [[tensor product|EY0X2NKA3S]] $\mathcal{H} = \bigotimes_{j \in \Lambda} \C^{2S+1}$.
The *quantum Heisenberg model* is the [[lattice model|PAEME5EQRM]] with the [[self-adjoint|0NEGN5CRSC]] Hamiltonian
$$H = J \sum_{\set{i, j} \in E} \mathbf{S}_i \cdot \mathbf{S}_j, \qquad \mathbf{S}_i \cdot \mathbf{S}_j = S^x_i S^x_j + S^y_i S^y_j + S^z_i S^z_j,$$
with $\hbar = 1$.
For $J > 0$ it is antiferromagnetic and for $J < 0$ ferromagnetic.
Check on two spins $\tfrac12$: with $\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{S}_1 + \mathbf{S}_2$, one has $\mathbf{S}_1 \cdot \mathbf{S}_2 = \tfrac12\big(\mathbf{S}^2 - \tfrac32\big)$.
The singlet ($\mathbf{S}^2 = 0$) has energy $-\tfrac34 J$ and the three triplet states ($\mathbf{S}^2 = 2$) have energy $\tfrac14 J$.

## Remarks

The Hamiltonian commutes with the total spin $\sum_j \mathbf{S}_j$, so the model has full $SU(2)$ symmetry.
More general versions use different couplings $J_x, J_y, J_z$ in the three directions and add a magnetic field term $-h \sum_j S^z_j$.
For the spin-$\tfrac12$ chain, Bethe (1931) found the eigenstates by the [[Bethe ansatz|S92G5M6X41]], and Hulthén (1938) computed the antiferromagnetic ground-state energy per site in the infinite chain, $J\big(\tfrac14 - \ln 2\big)$.
The ferromagnetic ground states are easy: the fully aligned states, with energy $\tfrac{J}{4}\abs{E}$ for spin $\tfrac12$ and $J < 0$, together with their $SU(2)$ rotations.
The model describes magnetic insulators in which electrons are localized and interact through exchange.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each site of a crystal carries a tiny quantum magnet, and neighbouring magnets prefer to point the same way or opposite ways.
Unlike a classical magnet, a quantum spin cannot have a definite direction along all three axes at once.
So when neighbours prefer to point opposite ways, the lowest-energy state is usually a complicated entangled state.
On two sites, the lowest state for antiparallel coupling is the singlet, in which neither spin has a definite direction.
This model is the basic starting point for the theory of quantum magnetism.
