## Summary

The thermodynamic limit lets the volume and the number of particles of a system grow to infinity while the density stays fixed.
Extensive quantities, such as the energy or the logarithm of the partition function, are divided by the volume before the limit is taken.
In this limit the different [[ensembles|M0JYTP1C8B]] give the same thermodynamics, fluctuations of densities vanish, and sharp phase transitions can appear.
Proving that the limit exists is one of the first results of rigorous statistical mechanics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A real sample holds about $10^{23}$ atoms, which is so many that it behaves like an infinite system.
The thermodynamic limit makes this precise: let the box grow, keep the number of atoms per unit volume fixed, and look at quantities per unit volume.
Boundary effects then fade, because the surface of a large box is small compared with its volume.
For example, a cube of side $L$ in a lattice has about $6 L^2$ boundary sites among $L^3$ sites, a fraction that tends to $0$.
