## Proof

We prove the two directions separately, and in each direction we treat existence and uniqueness of the expression apart.
All families of scalars below have only finitely many nonzero terms, so every sum is a finite sum.

1. Assume that $(b_i)_{i \in I}$ is a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]], and let $v \in V$. The family [[spans|0BJKVF90Z6]] $V$, so $v = \sum_i a_i b_i$ for some scalars $a_i$. This gives existence.
2. For uniqueness, suppose that $v = \sum_i a_i b_i$ and $v = \sum_i a'_i b_i$. Subtracting the two expressions, with the axioms of a [[vector space|05EGHEQZAW]], gives
$$\sum_i (a_i - a'_i) b_i = 0.$$
3. This is a relation among the $b_i$ with only finitely many nonzero coefficients. The family is [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]], so $a_i - a'_i = 0$ for every $i$. Hence $a_i = a'_i$, and the expression is unique.
4. Conversely, assume that each $v \in V$ has exactly one expression $v = \sum_i a_i b_i$. In particular each $v$ has at least one, so the family spans $V$.
5. Let $\sum_i c_i b_i = 0$ be a relation. The trivial combination $\sum_i 0 \cdot b_i$ is also equal to $0$, so we have two expressions for the vector $0$.
6. By the uniqueness in the hypothesis, the two expressions have the same coefficients, so $c_i = 0$ for every $i$. Hence the family is linearly independent.
7. By steps 4 and 6, the family spans $V$ and is independent, so it is a basis of $V$.

## Idea

Two expressions for the same vector differ by an expression for the zero vector.
Independence says that the only expression for zero is the trivial one, so the two expressions must agree.
Read backwards, the same remark says that unique expressions for zero are exactly what independence asks for.

## Variants

The whole statement can be packed into one map.
Let $\Phi \colon F^{(I)} \to V$ send $(a_i)$ to $\sum_i a_i b_i$; it is linear.
Spanning says that $\Phi$ is surjective, and independence says that its kernel is $\{0\}$, which for a linear map means that it is injective.
So the family is a basis exactly when $\Phi$ is bijective, which is the statement.
