## Summary

Kirchhoff–Love plate theory is the classical [[plate theory|782XMC21FH]] for thin plates.
It assumes that straight lines normal to the mid-plane stay straight, stay normal to the deformed mid-plane and keep their length.
Then the whole deformation is determined by the in-plane displacements and the deflection $w$ of the mid-plane.
For a linear isotropic plate the deflection solves the [[biharmonic plate equation|X1SGK619VJ]] $D \Delta^2 w = q$.
It is the two-dimensional analogue of the [[Euler–Bernoulli beam equation|13FGKM7NQ0]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw short lines across the thickness of a thin rubber sheet, at right angles to its surface.
Bend the sheet gently: the lines tilt, but they remain straight and at right angles to the bent surface.
Kirchhoff–Love theory takes this as a rule, which means that the bent shape of the middle surface tells everything.
The stiffness against bending is $D = Eh^3/(12(1 - \nu^2))$ for a plate of thickness $h$.
