## Summary

The Saffman–Taylor instability, or viscous fingering, is an instability of the interface between two fluids.
It occurs when a less viscous fluid pushes a more viscous one in a [[Hele-Shaw cell|CM1R6CSHAA]] or a porous medium.
A small bulge of the interface forward meets less resistance and so advances faster, and the interface breaks up into fingers.
Surface tension damps short wavelengths and sets the scale of the fingers.
The instability limits oil recovery by water injection and is a model problem for pattern formation; Saffman and Taylor analysed it in 1958.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Blow air into honey squeezed between two glass plates, and the air does not advance as a straight front.
It forms branching fingers that push into the honey.
Wherever the front gets slightly ahead, there is less honey left to push in front of it, so the push meets less resistance there, and that part runs further ahead.
If instead the honey pushed the air, the front would stay flat.
