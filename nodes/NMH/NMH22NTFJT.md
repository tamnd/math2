## Summary

The Bouguer anomaly is the [[free-air anomaly|WDMHD6BW8Y]] with the attraction of the rock between the station and sea level removed.
The rock is modelled as an infinite horizontal slab of constant density.
Removing the visible topography makes the anomaly show density contrasts below sea level.
It is the standard anomaly in exploration geophysics and in studies of [[isostasy|7CJTGB6M0V]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A station on a high plateau stands on a thick layer of rock, and this rock pulls the instrument down.
The Bouguer correction subtracts that pull, treating the layer as a flat slab as wide as the world.
What remains shows what lies deeper.
Large mountain ranges usually have strongly negative Bouguer anomalies.
This means light crustal roots lie below them, as isostasy predicts.
