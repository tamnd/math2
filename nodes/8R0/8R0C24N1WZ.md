## Definition

Let $F \colon \R^n \to \R$ be a differentiable function with [[gradient|0ABK9V9Y64]] $\nabla F$.
Let $x_0 \in \R^n$ and let $(\eta_k)_{k \ge 0}$ be positive step sizes.
*Stochastic gradient descent* produces [[random variables|0PNRKMRC55]] $x_k$ in $\R^n$ by
$$x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta_k G_k \qquad (k \ge 0),$$
where each $G_k$ is a random vector with conditional [[expected value|0WQNRCNY3M]] $\mathbb{E}[G_k \mid x_0, \dots, x_k] = \nabla F(x_k)$.

## Remarks

In the finite-sum case $F(x) = \frac1N \sum_{i=1}^N f_i(x)$, one draws $i_k$ uniformly from $\set{1, \dots, N}$, independently of the past, and takes $G_k = \nabla f_{i_k}(x_k)$; then $\mathbb{E}[G_k \mid x_0, \dots, x_k] = \frac1N \sum_i \nabla f_i(x_k) = \nabla F(x_k)$, so the definition applies.
Averaging over a random batch of indices also works.

Example: $F(x) = \frac12 \cdot \frac12 \big((x - 1)^2 + (x + 1)^2\big) = \frac12 (x^2 + 1)$ on $\R$, with $f_1(x) = \tfrac12 (x - 1)^2$ and $f_2(x) = \tfrac12 (x + 1)^2$.
Then $G_k$ is $x_k - 1$ or $x_k + 1$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each, and its mean is $x_k = F'(x_k)$.

Non-example: taking $G_k = \nabla f_{i_k}(x_k)$ with $i_k$ always equal to $1$ is not stochastic gradient descent for $F$, since then $\mathbb{E}[G_k] = \nabla f_1(x_k) \ne \nabla F(x_k)$ in general.

Take step sizes with $\sum_k \eta_k = \infty$ and $\sum_k \eta_k^2 < \infty$, as in the [[Robbins–Monro algorithm|CHHD44WDHJ]].
If $F$ is strongly convex and the variance of $G_k$ is suitably bounded, the iterates then converge to the minimiser with probability $1$.
If $G_k = \nabla F(x_k)$ exactly, the method is [[gradient descent|12317R8RTJ]].
