## Statement

Let $\lambda > 0$ be an arrival rate, $\mu > 0$ a service rate, $c \ge 1$ a number of servers, and $E = \lambda / \mu$ the offered load.
Customers arrive as a Poisson process of rate $\lambda$, and service times are independent [[exponential|0E99F51GVB]] random variables of rate $\mu$.

1. (Erlang B) In the loss system $M/M/c/c$, arrivals that find all $c$ servers busy are lost.
The stationary probability that all servers are busy, which is also the fraction of lost arrivals, is
$$B(E, c) = \frac{E^c / c!}{\sum_{k=0}^{c} E^k / k!}.$$
2. (Erlang C) In the delay system $M/M/c$ with unlimited waiting room and $E < c$, the stationary probability that an arriving customer must wait is
$$C(E, c) = \frac{\dfrac{E^c}{c!} \dfrac{c}{c - E}}{\sum_{k=0}^{c-1} \dfrac{E^k}{k!} + \dfrac{E^c}{c!} \dfrac{c}{c - E}}.$$

## Remarks

In both systems the number of customers is a [[birth–death process|E8DAD7052E]] with birth rate $\lambda$ and death rate $\min(n, c)\mu$.
The balance equations give the [[stationary distribution|0KRH5157WP]]: $\pi_n \propto E^n / n!$ for $n \le c$.
It stops at $c$ in the loss system and continues geometrically with ratio $E/c$ in the delay system.
Since Poisson arrivals see time averages, the time-stationary probabilities are also the probabilities seen by an arriving customer.
The two formulas are related by $C(E, c) = \dfrac{c\, B(E, c)}{c - E\,(1 - B(E, c))}$.
Example: for $c = 2$ and $E = 1$, $B = (1/2)/(5/2) = 1/5$ and $C = 1/(1 + 1 + 1) = 1/3$.
The Erlang B formula is insensitive: it stays valid for $M/G/c/c$ with any service law of mean $1/\mu$; the Erlang C formula needs exponential service.
The condition $E < c$ in part 2 is necessary: for $E \ge c$ the number of customers in the delay system has no stationary distribution.

## Explanations

### L1 human

These formulas, found by the Danish engineer A. K. Erlang for telephone exchanges, tell how many lines or agents are needed.
The B formula gives the share of calls that get a busy signal when there is no waiting.
The C formula gives the share of callers who must wait on hold when waiting is allowed.

### L2 human

The offered load $E$ is measured in erlangs: one erlang is one server kept busy all the time.
Call centres use the C formula, together with the mean wait $C(E, c) / (c\mu - \lambda)$, to choose the number of agents for a target service level.
The B formula is computed stably by the recursion $B(E, 0) = 1$, $B(E, c) = E\, B(E, c-1) / (c + E\, B(E, c-1))$.
Both are special cases of the stationary laws of the [[M/M/c queue|B22GZ9R487]] and its loss variant.
