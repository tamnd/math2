## Description

The *split-octonions* form the $8$-dimensional real algebra $\mathrm{CD}(\mathbb{H}, 1)$, the [[Cayley–Dickson double|T9XW7RBEHY]] of the [[quaternions|0GE4DVQKR8]] $\mathbb{H}$ with parameter $\mu = 1$.
A split-octonion is a pair $(a, b)$ of quaternions, with the product
$$(a, b)(c, d) = (ac + \bar{d} b,\; da + b\bar{c}).$$
The unit is $(1, 0)$, the conjugate of $(a, b)$ is $(\bar{a}, -b)$, and the norm is $N(a, b) = \abs{a}^2 - \abs{b}^2$.

## Remarks

The norm is multiplicative and has signature $(4, 4)$, so the split-octonions are a [[composition algebra|1Q9G6AST10]] and an [[octonion algebra|KJ145M3CVT]] over $\R$.
They are alternative and not associative.
They have zero divisors: with $\ell = (0, 1)$, one has $\ell^2 = (1, 0) = 1$, so $(1 + \ell)(1 - \ell) = 1 - \ell^2 = 0$.
Up to isomorphism, the octonion algebras over $\R$ are the split-octonions and the [[octonions|1HRMT3DSX2]] $\mathbb{O}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The split-octonions are built like the octonions, by doubling the quaternions, with one sign changed.
They keep the multiplicative norm, but the norm can now be zero or negative.
Because of that, some nonzero elements multiply to zero, and division fails.
