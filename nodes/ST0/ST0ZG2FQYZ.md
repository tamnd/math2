## Example

The *Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction* is a family of chemical reactions in which an organic acid, typically malonic acid, is oxidized by bromate in acid solution.
A metal catalyst, such as cerium ions or ferroin, takes part.
In a stirred vessel, the concentrations of bromide and of the oxidized catalyst oscillate in time for a long while.
The colour of the solution changes periodically.
The oscillation does not contradict thermodynamics: the system is far from [[chemical equilibrium|2KMN67HYNC]] and the oscillations die out as the reactants are used up.
Its mechanism is modelled by the [[Oregonator|8QAKE5E9DJ]], whose mass-action equations have a stable [[limit cycle|0AAQ73WRFB]] for suitable parameters.
In a thin unstirred layer, the reaction shows travelling waves, target patterns and spiral waves.
These are modelled by a [[reaction–diffusion system|TAAHWE917M]] built on the Oregonator.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Most chemical reactions run in one direction and then stop.
In the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction the mixture switches back and forth between two colours many times before it stops.
In a flat dish, rings and spirals of colour spread across the liquid.
It is the classic real example of a chemical clock and of pattern formation in chemistry.
