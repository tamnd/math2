## Summary

Homotopy K-theory, written $KH$, is a version of [[algebraic K-theory|5GVFZWMWC8]] introduced by Weibel that is homotopy invariant by construction.
It is obtained by applying nonconnective K-theory to the rings of polynomial functions on algebraic simplices and taking the realization.
For regular Noetherian rings it agrees with ordinary K-theory, and in general it combines the ideas of Karoubi–Villamayor K-theory and of Bass's negative K-groups.
Weibel showed that $KH$ satisfies excision, which ordinary K-theory does not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary K-theory notices nilpotent elements: the ring $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ has more K-theory after adding a polynomial variable.
Homotopy K-theory removes this effect by gluing together the K-theory of $R$, $R[t]$, and the higher polynomial rings $R[\Delta^p]$ into one object.
The result does not change when $R$ is replaced by $R[t]$.
On regular Noetherian rings nothing is lost, and $KH$ is just $K$.
