## Summary

Wick rotation replaces the real time variable $t$ by an imaginary time $t = -i\tau$.
It turns the Minkowski quadratic form $t^2 - \abs{\mathbf{x}}^2$ into $-(\tau^2 + \abs{\mathbf{x}}^2)$, so problems in Lorentzian spacetime become problems in Euclidean space.
In quantum mechanics it turns the oscillating evolution $e^{-itH/\hbar}$ into the decaying semigroup $e^{-\tau H/\hbar}$, and oscillatory [[path integrals|1NYTFT5NPE]] into integrals against genuine measures.
The [[Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction theorem|YS3ER7BW8W]] gives conditions under which a Euclidean theory can be rotated back to a relativistic one.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Many formulas of quantum physics contain a factor $e^{-itE}$ that oscillates and is hard to control.
If one pretends that time is an imaginary number, the same factor becomes $e^{-\tau E}$, which decays for positive energies $E$.
Calculations are much easier in this imaginary time, and the answer is then continued back to real time.
The name refers to the quarter turn in the complex time plane, from the real axis to the imaginary axis.
The same trick relates quantum mechanics to the heat equation and to Brownian motion.
