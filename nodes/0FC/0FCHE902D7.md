## Description

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $n \ge 1$ be an integer.
The *general linear group* $\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$ is the set of [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]] $n \times n$ matrices over $R$, with [[matrix multiplication|032Q9FB8MT]] as operation.
It is a [[group|G5S1M9]]:

1. The product of two invertible matrices is invertible, by the [[inverse of a product|0S4WTRE9GG]].
2. Matrix multiplication is [[associative|073E4XC016]].
3. The identity element is $I_n$.
4. The inverse of $A$ in the group is the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$, which is invertible by the [[rules for inverses|~b26cc133]].

Equivalently, $\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$ is the [[group of units|07C6ECNJET]] of the [[matrix ring|0FHVPRAE60]] $M_n(R)$.
For a field $F$ and a vector space $V$ over $F$, the group $\mathrm{GL}(V)$ is the group of [[linear isomorphisms|0B8T10CE7N]] of $V$ to itself.
The group $\mathrm{GL}(F^n)$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$.

## Remarks

- For $n = 1$, the group $\mathrm{GL}_1(R)$ is the group $R^{\times}$ of units of $R$. So $\mathrm{GL}_1(\mathbb{R})$ is the group of nonzero real numbers under multiplication.
- For $n \ge 2$, the group $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ is not abelian, as the [[$2 \times 2$ example|~397e159d]] shows.
- Over a field, $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ is generated by the [[elementary matrices|01REXW3WWE]], by [[invertibility and row reduction|1ZK55FS2BF]].
- The [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]] is a group homomorphism $\det \colon \mathrm{GL}_n(R) \to R^{\times}$, as the [[determinant homomorphism example|0YZ3T5Q7XN]] explains. Its kernel is the [[special linear group|0V9PMN0RBH]] $\mathrm{SL}_n(R)$.
- The centre of $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ is the group of nonzero scalar matrices $cI_n$.
- Over a commutative ring, $A$ is in $\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$ exactly when $\det A$ is a unit, by the [[determinant criterion over rings|03Q7RE2HXR]]. So $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$ is the group of integer matrices with determinant $\pm 1$.
- For a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ with $q$ elements, the [[order of the general linear group|0JQ1KNQW2K]] is $(q^n - 1)(q^n - q) \cdots (q^n - q^{n-1})$; this belongs to group theory. The smallest nonabelian case is [[$\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/2)$, of order $6$|M170X47PG4]].
- Over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathrm{GL}_n$ is an open subset of the space of matrices and a [[matrix Lie group|36KSZ7GA5S]]; this belongs to Lie theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The general linear group collects all the invertible matrices of one size.
We can multiply two of them, and we can undo each of them, so they form a group.
Geometrically, its elements are all the ways to transform space linearly without flattening it.

### L2 human

Worked example: in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$, let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. $\det A = 1 \ne 0$, so $A \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$, and in fact $A \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$.
2. The group inverse is $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ is not in the group, because its determinant is $0$.

A trap: $\mathrm{GL}_n$ is not closed under addition, since $I_n + (-I_n) = 0$ is singular.

### L3 human

$\mathrm{GL}_n$ is a functor from commutative rings to groups: a ring homomorphism $R \to S$ applied to each entry gives a group homomorphism $\mathrm{GL}_n(R) \to \mathrm{GL}_n(S)$.
In algebraic geometry, $\mathrm{GL}_n$ is the affine group scheme with coordinate ring $\mathbb{Z}[x_{ij}, 1/\det]$.
The study of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$ as a finite group, and of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R})$ as a Lie group, belongs to group theory and to Lie theory.
