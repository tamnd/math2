## Statement

Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]] of characteristic different from $2$, and let $A$ be an [[associative algebra|3CTW1P4HBP]] over $F$.
For $x, y \in A$ put $x \circ y = \frac{1}{2}(xy + yx)$.
Then for all $x, y \in A$ the Jordan identity holds:
$$(x \circ y) \circ (x \circ x) = x \circ \big(y \circ (x \circ x)\big).$$
Hence $A$ with the product $\circ$ is a [[Jordan algebra|6C2MCVB8C7]], written $A^+$.

## Remarks

Proof idea: $x \circ x = x^2$, so both sides are symmetrized products with $x^2$.
Expanding gives $(x \circ y) \circ x^2 = \frac{1}{4}(xyx^2 + yx^3 + x^3 y + x^2 y x)$.
Expanding the right side gives $\frac{1}{4}(xyx^2 + x^3 y + yx^3 + x^2 y x)$, the same four terms.
The product $\circ$ is commutative, so $A^+$ satisfies both axioms of a Jordan algebra.

Example: for real $2 \times 2$ matrices, $x = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has $x^2 = 0$, so both sides vanish for every $y$.

Equivalent forms: write $L_a$ for the map $b \mapsto a \circ b$.
In a commutative algebra the Jordan identity says that $L_x$ and $L_{x \circ x}$ commute for every $x$.
The product $\circ$ is not associative in general.
For the $2 \times 2$ matrix units $e_{ij}$, one has $(e_{12} \circ e_{21}) \circ e_{11} = \frac{1}{2} e_{11}$ but $e_{12} \circ (e_{21} \circ e_{11}) = \frac{1}{4}(e_{11} + e_{22})$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Matrices can be multiplied, but the product depends on the order.
Averaging the two orders gives a product that does not depend on the order.
This averaged product loses associativity, but it keeps one special case of it, the Jordan identity.
That identity is the defining rule of Jordan algebras, so every associative algebra gives a Jordan algebra.

### L2 human

The algebras $A^+$ and their subalgebras are the special Jordan algebras, and they are the main source of examples.
The Jordan identity implies that a Jordan algebra is power-associative, so powers $x^n$ are well defined.
Linearizing the identity, that is, replacing $x$ by $x + t z$ and comparing coefficients of $t$, gives multilinear identities that hold in every Jordan algebra over a field of characteristic $0$.
Not every Jordan algebra is of the form $A^+$ or a subalgebra of one: the Albert algebra of $3 \times 3$ Hermitian octonion matrices is exceptional.
