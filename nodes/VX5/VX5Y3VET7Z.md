## Definition

Let $\mathfrak{h}$ and $\mathcal{K}$ be complex [[Hilbert spaces|HM2HHE3J51]], with inner products linear in the second argument.
For bounded operators $A, B$ on $\mathcal{K}$ write $\{A, B\} = AB + BA$.
A *representation of the canonical anticommutation relations* over $\mathfrak{h}$ on $\mathcal{K}$ is an antilinear map $f \mapsto a(f)$ into the bounded operators on $\mathcal{K}$.
It must satisfy, for all $f, g \in \mathfrak{h}$,
$$\{a(f), a(g)\} = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \{a(f), a(g)^*\} = \ip{f}{g}\, I,$$
where $a(g)^*$ is the [[adjoint|6HSJBJ8TTQ]] of $a(g)$, also written $a^*(g)$.
The *CAR algebra* over $\mathfrak{h}$ is the [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]] generated by the operators $a(f)$ of such a representation.

## Remarks

The relations imply $\{a^*(f), a^*(g)\} = 0$ by taking adjoints, and $a^*(f)^2 = 0$ by taking $g = f$.
They also give $\norm{a(f)} = \norm{f}$.
The CAR algebra does not depend on the representation: any two are isomorphic by a $*$-isomorphism matching the generators $a(f)$.

Example: let $\mathfrak{h} = \C$ and $\mathcal{K} = \C^2$ with standard basis $e_1, e_2$.
Let $a(z) = \bar z\, A$, where $A$ is the matrix with $A e_2 = e_1$ and $A e_1 = 0$.
Then $A^2 = 0$, so $\{a(z), a(w)\} = 0$, and $A A^* + A^* A$ is the sum of the projections onto $e_1$ and $e_2$, which is $I$; hence $\{a(z), a(w)^*\} = \bar z w\, I = \ip{z}{w} I$.
Here the CAR algebra is the full matrix algebra $M_2(\C)$.

Non-example: the bosonic annihilation operator on $\ell^2(\N)$, with $a e_n = \sqrt{n}\, e_{n-1}$, satisfies $a a^* - a^* a = I$ instead.
It is unbounded and $a^2 \ne 0$, so it fails both relations.

The fermionic creation and annihilation operators on the antisymmetric Fock space give the Fock representation of the CAR.
