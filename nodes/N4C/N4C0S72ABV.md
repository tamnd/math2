## Definition

Let $A = (a_{ij})_{1 \le i, j \le n}$ be a [[generalized Cartan matrix|38Z0HSXTCA]] of rank $\ell$.
A *realization* of $A$ is a triple $(\mathfrak{h}, \Pi, \Pi^\vee)$ where $\mathfrak{h}$ is a complex vector space of dimension $2n - \ell$.
Here $\Pi = \set{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n} \subset \mathfrak{h}^*$ and $\Pi^\vee = \set{\alpha_1^\vee, \ldots, \alpha_n^\vee} \subset \mathfrak{h}$ are linearly independent sets with $\alpha_j(\alpha_i^\vee) = a_{ij}$ for all $i, j$.
The *Kac–Moody algebra* $\mathfrak{g}(A)$ is the complex [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] generated by $\mathfrak{h}$ and elements $e_1, \ldots, e_n, f_1, \ldots, f_n$, subject to the relations:

1. $[h, h'] = 0$ for all $h, h' \in \mathfrak{h}$;
2. $[h, e_i] = \alpha_i(h)\, e_i$ and $[h, f_i] = -\alpha_i(h)\, f_i$ for all $h \in \mathfrak{h}$ and all $i$;
3. $[e_i, f_j] = \delta_{ij}\, \alpha_i^\vee$ for all $i, j$, where $\delta_{ij}$ is the Kronecker delta;
4. $(\operatorname{ad} e_i)^{1 - a_{ij}}(e_j) = 0$ and $(\operatorname{ad} f_i)^{1 - a_{ij}}(f_j) = 0$ for all $i \neq j$, where $\operatorname{ad} x$ is the map $y \mapsto [x, y]$.

## Remarks

Example: for $n = 1$ and $A = (2)$, the rank is $\ell = 1$, so $\mathfrak{h} = \C \alpha_1^\vee$ and $\alpha_1(\alpha_1^\vee) = 2$.
With $h = \alpha_1^\vee$, the relations read $[h, e_1] = 2e_1$, $[h, f_1] = -2f_1$ and $[e_1, f_1] = h$, which are the relations of the standard basis of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\C)$, and $\mathfrak{g}(A) \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\C)$.

Non-example: the matrix $(1)$ is not a generalized Cartan matrix, since its diagonal entry is not $2$, so it does not define a Kac–Moody algebra.

The realization is unique up to isomorphism, so $\mathfrak{g}(A)$ depends only on $A$.
Kac's book defines $\mathfrak{g}(A)$ instead as the quotient of the algebra with relations 1 to 3 by its largest ideal $\mathfrak{r}$ with $\mathfrak{r} \cap \mathfrak{h} = 0$.
The two definitions agree when $A$ is symmetrizable, by the Gabber–Kac theorem.
If $A$ is the Cartan matrix of a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra, then $\ell = n$ and $\mathfrak{g}(A)$ is that Lie algebra, by [[Serre's theorem|9YTSFBHZKG]].
