## Statement

Let $A$ be an [[alternative algebra|N8K9SSVMRN]] over a commutative ring $R$.
Let $x, y \in A$.
Then the [[subalgebra|7CHG6SD365]] of $A$ generated by $x$ and $y$ is associative.

## Remarks

The idea of the proof: the subalgebra is spanned by the products of copies of $x$ and $y$ with all bracketings.
One shows that the [[associator|RT4YV20367]] of any three such products is $0$, by induction on their total length, using that the associator is alternating and the Moufang identities.
The converse also holds: if every subalgebra generated by two elements is associative, then $(x, x, y) = (y, x, x) = 0$, so the algebra is alternative.
Example: in the [[octonions|1HRMT3DSX2]], any two elements lie in an associative subalgebra, which is isomorphic to $\R$, $\C$ or the quaternions.
The statement fails for three elements: the octonion units $i$, $j$ and $\ell$ satisfy $(ij)\ell \neq i(j\ell)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an alternative algebra, three elements may fail to associate.
Artin's theorem says that two elements never cause trouble.
Any expression built from only two elements can be computed as if the algebra were associative.

### L2 human

Taking $y = x$ shows that every alternative algebra is power-associative.
In the octonions a nonzero $x$ has the inverse $x^{-1} = \bar{x}/(x\bar{x})$, which is a combination of $1$ and $x$.
So the theorem, applied to $x$ and $y$, gives $x^{-1}(xy) = y = (yx)x^{-1}$.
This is the reason why the nonzero octonions behave like a group in many computations, although they only form a Moufang loop.
The theorem is a key step in the study of alternative division algebras and composition algebras.
