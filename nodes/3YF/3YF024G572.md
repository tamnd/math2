## Summary

The innermost stable circular orbit, or ISCO, of a compact mass is the smallest circular orbit on which a test particle can move stably.
For the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]] of mass $M$ it lies at $r = 6GM/c^2$, three times the [[Schwarzschild radius|G48AGJAZNG]].
In Newtonian gravity every radius allows a stable circular orbit, so the ISCO is a purely relativistic effect.
It marks the inner edge of thin accretion discs and the end of the slow inspiral of a small body into a [[black hole|YHJ0PDNY8T]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A planet can circle the Sun at any distance and stay on its orbit after a small nudge.
Near a black hole this is no longer true.
Below a certain radius, a small nudge makes the orbiting body spiral in and fall into the hole.
That radius is the innermost stable circular orbit; for a non-rotating black hole it is $6M$ in geometric units.
Gas in a disc around a black hole flows inwards slowly down to about this radius, and then plunges.
