## Statement

Let $W$ be a one-dimensional [[Wiener process|22735FGJV6]].
Consider the scalar Itô [[stochastic differential equation|B219G9SHJA]]
$$dX_t = a(X_t)\, dt + b(X_t)\, dW_t, \qquad X_0 = x_0 \in \R, \qquad 0 \le t \le T.$$
Assume, as for the [[Milstein method|C5WJT0NWGV]], that $a$ and $b$ are twice continuously differentiable with bounded first and second derivatives.
Assume also that $b b'$ is globally Lipschitz continuous.
Let $h = T/N$, $t_n = n h$ and $\Delta W_n = W_{t_{n+1}} - W_{t_n}$.
The derivative-free scheme of strong order $1$ sets $Y_0 = x_0$,
$$\Upsilon_n = Y_n + a(Y_n)\, h + b(Y_n) \sqrt{h},$$
$$Y_{n+1} = Y_n + a(Y_n)\, h + b(Y_n)\, \Delta W_n + \frac{b(\Upsilon_n) - b(Y_n)}{2 \sqrt{h}} \left( (\Delta W_n)^2 - h \right).$$
Then there is a constant $C$, independent of $N$, such that
$$\max_{0 \le n \le N} \left( \mathbb{E} \abs{X_{t_n} - Y_n}^2 \right)^{1/2} \le C h.$$

## Remarks

By Taylor's theorem, $\frac{b(\Upsilon_n) - b(Y_n)}{\sqrt{h}} = b'(Y_n)\, b(Y_n) + O(\sqrt{h})$.
So the scheme differs from the Milstein scheme by a term of size $O(\sqrt{h}) \cdot O(h)$ with mean zero, and it keeps the strong order $1$.
As in deterministic [[Runge–Kutta methods|7DBPJKYC6C]], extra evaluations of a coefficient replace its derivative.
A naive transfer of a deterministic Runge–Kutta method to the stochastic case does not in general give a convergent or high-order scheme, because Itô calculus changes the order conditions.
Example: for $dX = \sigma X\, dW$, one has $\Upsilon_n = Y_n (1 + \sigma \sqrt{h})$, and the correction term equals $\frac{\sigma^2}{2} Y_n ((\Delta W_n)^2 - h)$, exactly the Milstein term.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Milstein method for random differential equations needs the derivative of the noise coefficient.
This scheme avoids the derivative: it evaluates the coefficient at a second, slightly shifted point and uses the difference.
It keeps the accuracy of Milstein's method, so halving the step roughly halves the pathwise error.

### L2 human

Stochastic Runge–Kutta methods are built to match the Itô–Taylor expansion up to a given order, the way deterministic ones match the Taylor expansion.
Their order conditions are described with rooted trees, as for deterministic methods, but with several kinds of nodes for the drift and the noise.
For several noise sources, strong order $1$ again needs iterated integrals or a commutativity condition on the noise coefficients.
