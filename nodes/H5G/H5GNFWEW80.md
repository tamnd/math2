## Summary

An Ekman layer is a thin [[boundary layer|YCFPD0GE5G]] in a rotating fluid in which friction is balanced by the Coriolis force.
It forms at the bottom of the ocean and the atmosphere, and below the sea surface where the wind drags the water.
Inside it the velocity turns with depth along a spiral, and the net transport of fluid is at right angles to the surface stress.
The convergence and divergence of this transport drive upwelling, downwelling and the spin-down of rotating flows.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When wind blows over the sea, it drags the top layer of water along.
On the rotating Earth the Coriolis force turns the moving water to the right in the northern hemisphere.
Each deeper layer is dragged by the one above it and turned a little further, so the current forms a spiral that weakens with depth.
Added up over the whole layer, the water moves at a right angle to the wind.
Along some coasts this pushes surface water offshore, and cold, nutrient-rich water rises from below.
