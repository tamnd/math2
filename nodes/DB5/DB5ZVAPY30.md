## Summary

De Donder–Weyl theory is the covariant Hamiltonian form of a first-order Lagrangian field theory, named after Théophile De Donder and Hermann Weyl.
It assigns to each field component one polymomentum for every spacetime direction.
It replaces the Euler–Lagrange equations by first-order equations for the fields and the polymomenta.
It is the basic example of [[covariant Hamiltonian field theory|90XD2E09FD]].
It applies when the Lagrangian density is regular, that is, when the polymomenta determine the field derivatives.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the [[Lagrangian density|SANYJGZ311]] of a field theory, which depends on the fields and on their slopes in every direction of spacetime.
For each field and each direction, the derivative of the density with respect to that slope is a new variable, a polymomentum.
One builds a single function of the fields and polymomenta, the De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian, by a [[Legendre transformation|ZQYMWVT1TT]].
Then the field equations become simple first-order equations, just as in [[Hamiltonian mechanics|MPM0RHWCN6]], but with all spacetime directions treated alike.
For one particle, where spacetime is just the time line, the construction gives back the usual Hamilton equations.
