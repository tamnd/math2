## Summary

The near field and the far field are the regions close to and far from a radiating source, where the electromagnetic field behaves differently.
In the far field, the field is a spherical wave: it falls off like $1/r$, is transverse, and carries energy outward.
In the near field, terms falling off like $1/r^2$ and $1/r^3$ dominate.
There much of the energy is stored and exchanged with the source rather than radiated.
The distinction organizes antenna design, measurement and wireless power transfer.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stand very close to a radio antenna and the field there is like the static field of charges and currents, strong but not travelling far.
Walk away many wavelengths and what remains is a clean wave, like ripples far from a stone dropped in a pond.
The ripples far away have a fixed shape in each direction, the radiation pattern, and only their strength changes as $1/r$.
Near-field effects power contactless card readers and phone chargers; far-field effects carry radio and television.
