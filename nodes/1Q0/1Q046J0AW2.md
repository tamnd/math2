## Summary

Proof complexity asks how long the shortest proof of a true propositional statement must be.
A SAT solver can show that a formula is satisfiable by printing one assignment, but to show that a formula is unsatisfiable it must print something much harder to shorten: a proof.
Proof complexity is the part of [[proof theory|N4EDJNMTEF]] and of complexity theory that measures such proofs.
Its objects are [[propositional proof systems|3V24CYWDT6]]: polynomial-time functions whose values are exactly the [[tautologies|05006AJ7DZ]].
For a proof system $f$ and a tautology $\tau$, the central quantity is the size of the shortest $f$-proof:
$$s_f(\tau) = \min \{ \lvert w \rvert : f(w) = \tau \}.$$
Cook and Reckhow showed in 1979 that some proof system has $s_f(\tau)$ bounded by a polynomial in $\lvert \tau \rvert$ exactly when [[NP|SBDQH0TJ5X]] equals [[coNP|NBSD5FG7NY]].

## Motivation

You know that $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$ is open, and that no one can prove that SAT needs exponential time.
Proof complexity attacks a nearby question one proof system at a time.
The [[Cook-Reckhow theorem|YS1T29B00B]] says that $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$ holds if and only if every proof system has a family of tautologies with no proofs of polynomial size.
So a lower bound for each stronger system is one more step toward that separation, and this plan is called Cook's program.

The second reason is practical.
A DPLL or CDCL solver that answers "unsatisfiable" has, in effect, built a [[resolution|6JSH907W5R]] refutation, and its running time is at least proportional to the size of that refutation.
So a lower bound for resolution is a lower bound for these solvers on unsatisfiable inputs; see [[SAT and SMT solving|3BW08M1EX7]].
The third reason comes from logic: proof systems match weak theories of [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]], and a lower bound for a system gives an independence result for its theory.

## Notation

Each result in proof complexity names its measure, and the measures are not interchangeable.
The *size* $s_f(\tau)$ counts symbols, or for resolution often clauses.
The *length*, or *number of lines*, counts the formulas of a line-based proof.
The *width* $w(F \vdash \bot)$ is the least possible largest clause in a resolution refutation of a CNF $F$.
The *degree* is the least possible largest degree of a polynomial in an algebraic refutation.
The *space* is the number of lines kept in memory at once while a proof is checked.
Many books write $S(F \vdash \bot)$ for size in clauses and $\bot$ or $\square$ for the empty clause.

## Remarks

### A first refutation and its measures

Take the CNF $F$ with four clauses $\{p, q\}$, $\{p, \neg q\}$, $\{\neg p, r\}$ and $\{\neg p, \neg r\}$.
The resolution rule takes two clauses with a complementary pair, $C \cup \{x\}$ and $D \cup \{\neg x\}$, and derives $C \cup D$.
Here is a refutation, as in [[the measures of a small resolution refutation|M5M70S9855]]:
$$\{p, q\}, \ \{p, \neg q\} \vdash \{p\}, \qquad \{\neg p, r\}, \ \{\neg p, \neg r\} \vdash \{\neg p\}, \qquad \{p\}, \ \{\neg p\} \vdash \square.$$
The proof has $4 + 3 = 7$ clauses and $2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 0 = 10$ literals, and its widest clause has $2$ literals.
So in one small proof, size in clauses is $7$, size in literals is $10$, and width is $2$.
Check: no refutation of $F$ is shorter, because each three of the clauses can be satisfied, so all four axioms are needed, and joining four axioms into one proof takes at least three steps.

### The trivial upper bound

Every tautology has a proof of size about $2^n$, where $n$ is the number of variables: write out the truth table.
The [[truth-table proof system|67MYPBSHCJ]] makes this exact, with a proof of $\tau$ that is $\tau$ followed by $2^n$ padding letters.
So the question is never whether proofs exist.
It is whether some system beats $2^n$ on every family, and by how much.

### The hierarchy of systems

The main systems form a partial order under p-simulation, and each system has its own lower bound story.
At the bottom is [[resolution|6JSH907W5R]].
For resolution size, [[Haken's theorem|4PCEYEW3JG]] gives exponential lower bounds on the [[pigeonhole formulas|RJ571F7FF0]], [[Urquhart's theorem|HF1GHVRDSD]] does the same on [[Tseitin formulas|JXTCC6KXNG]], and the [[size-width relation|GWDGRPAQSM]] turns width bounds into size bounds.
Above it, [[cutting planes|KKCX2HQP2K]] refutes the pigeonhole formulas in polynomial size, and Pudlák proved exponential cutting-planes lower bounds by [[feasible interpolation|59GJCM8C66]].
The [[algebraic proof systems|ZERBQSTZB5]] are measured by degree.
[[Bounded-depth Frege|41QJ1WEMJ3]] needs superpolynomial size on the pigeonhole formulas by [[Ajtai's theorem|Q0ERK64J78]].
At the top sit [[Frege|J2BAR09TPF]] and [[extended Frege|5YSE04SHFH]], where no superpolynomial lower bound is known; see [[lower bounds for Frege systems|ANSV13M4FS]].

### Finding proofs, not only having them

A second question is whether short proofs can be found fast.
A system is [[automatizable|WBECDG163W]] if some algorithm finds a proof in time polynomial in the size of the shortest proof.
Atserias and Müller proved that resolution is not automatizable unless $\mathrm{P} = \mathrm{NP}$.
So even the weakest system of the hierarchy is hard to search.

### What it is not

[[Checking one satisfying assignment|72CPDNTXKT]] is short evidence that a formula is satisfiable.
That is an NP question, and an assignment says nothing about the other $2^n - 1$ assignments, so it is not evidence for a tautology.
The study of proofs in first-order theories without any bound on size, such as consistency proofs by ordinal analysis, belongs to [[structural proof theory|R10RMQCJQX]].
Proof complexity asks how large proofs are, and not only whether they exist.

### Watch out

The first trap is to carry a lower bound to a stronger system.
The pigeonhole formulas need exponential size in resolution, but they have polynomial-size proofs in cutting planes and in Frege.
So every lower bound must name its system.
The second trap is to mix measures.
A Frege proof with $10$ lines can have lines of length $1000$, so a bound on lines is not a bound on symbols.
The third trap is to overread a lower bound: a bound for one system does not give $\mathrm{P} \ne \mathrm{NP}$, and only a bound for all systems gives $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$.

### History

Tseitin proved lower bounds for regular resolution in 1968, before the field had a name.
Cook and Reckhow gave the general definition of a proof system and its link with $\mathrm{NP}$ and $\mathrm{coNP}$ in 1979.
Haken proved the first exponential lower bound for general resolution in 1985.
Ajtai proved the first superpolynomial lower bound for bounded-depth Frege in 1988.
Superpolynomial lower bounds for Frege systems remain open.

### Try it

First, $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has $n(n+1)$ variables, so how many rows has its truth table when $n = 3$?
There are $12$ variables, so the table has $2^{12} = 4096$ rows.

Second, does an exponential lower bound on resolution size for some family show that SAT is not in $\mathrm{P}$?
No, because a lower bound for one system leaves every other algorithm and every stronger system open.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A tautology is a formula that is true in every case, and a truth table checks this one row at a time.
With $n$ letters the table has $2^n$ rows, so $30$ letters already give about a billion rows.
A clever proof can be much shorter than the table.
For example, "$p$ or $q$" and "$p$ or not $q$" together give "$p$" in one step, whatever $q$ is.
Proof complexity asks whether such shortcuts always exist, and it answers system by system.

### L2 human

We place the pigeonhole formulas $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ in the hierarchy, measured by size.
In the truth-table system there are $n(n+1)$ variables, so the size is about $2^{n(n+1)}$.
In resolution the size is $2^{\Omega(n)}$, by Haken's theorem.
In bounded-depth Frege of any fixed depth the size grows faster than every polynomial, by Ajtai's theorem.
In cutting planes and in Frege the size is polynomial in $n$.
So one family of formulas separates the systems: it is hard low in the hierarchy and easy high up.
This is the typical use of the concept: for a family of formulas, give an upper and a lower bound in each system, and name the system and the measure each time.

### L3 human

Proof complexity studies the nondeterministic complexity of the coNP-complete language $\mathrm{TAUT}$, one proof system at a time.
Its lower bound methods borrow from combinatorics (restrictions, expansion of graphs), from circuit complexity (switching lemmas, monotone circuit bounds through interpolation) and from algebra (degree of polynomials).
On the logic side, each system is the nonuniform version of a theory of [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]], and its lower bounds are independence results for that theory.
The base of propositional logic is in [[the propositional calculus|JDK63589SA]], and the first-order theories are in [[formal arithmetic and its fragments|QHCRN4JVG0]].
