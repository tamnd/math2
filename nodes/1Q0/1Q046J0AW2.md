## Summary

Proof complexity is the part of [[proof theory|N4EDJNMTEF]] and of computational complexity that studies the size of formal proofs.
Its main objects are [[propositional proof systems|3V24CYWDT6]], and the question is how short a proof of a given [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]] can be in a given system.
Cook and Reckhow showed in 1979 that a proof system with short proofs of all tautologies exists if and only if [[NP|SBDQH0TJ5X]] equals [[coNP|NBSD5FG7NY]].
So lower bounds on proof size for ever stronger systems are a route toward separating complexity classes.

## Motivation

Proof complexity has three aims.

1. *Complexity theory.* The [[Cook-Reckhow theorem|YS1T29B00B]] reduces the question $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$ to lower bounds for all proof systems. Lower bounds for one system after another are Cook's program toward this question.
2. *SAT solving.* A run of a DPLL or CDCL solver on an unsatisfiable CNF gives a [[resolution|6JSH907W5R]] refutation. So lower bounds for resolution are lower bounds on the running time of these solvers, on unsatisfiable inputs; see [[SAT and SMT solving|3BW08M1EX7]].
3. *Logic.* Proof systems correspond to weak theories of [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]]. A lower bound for a proof system gives an independence result for the corresponding theory.

## Notation

Fix a proof system $f$ and a tautology $\tau$, or a refutation system and an unsatisfiable CNF $F$.
Each page of this topic names its measure, from the following list.

- *Size* $s_f(\tau)$: the least number of symbols of an $f$-proof of $\tau$.
- *Length* or number of lines: the least number of lines of a proof, in a system whose proofs are sequences of lines.
- *Width* $w(F \vdash \bot)$: the least, over all resolution refutations of $F$, of the largest number of literals in a clause.
- *Degree*: the least, over all algebraic refutations of $F$, of the largest degree of a polynomial in the refutation.
- *Space*: the least number of lines that must be kept in memory at once, when a refutation is checked line by line.

## Remarks

### Examples

- [[The measures of a small resolution refutation|M5M70S9855]]: one refutation of a small CNF has size $7$ clauses, $10$ literals in all, and width $2$. It shows that the measures differ.
- [[The truth-table proof system|67MYPBSHCJ]]: a proof of a tautology with $n$ variables is padding of length $2^n$. It is the trivial proof system, and every system of interest gives shorter proofs on some families.

### Non-examples

- [[Checking one satisfying assignment|72CPDNTXKT]] gives short evidence that a formula is satisfiable. This is a question about NP, and the evidence does not show that the formula is a tautology. So it is not a question of proof complexity.
- The study of proofs in first-order theories without any bound on the size, such as consistency proofs, belongs to [[structural proof theory|R10RMQCJQX]]. Proof complexity asks how large proofs are, and not only whether they exist.

### Basic properties

The main proof systems form a hierarchy, ordered by p-simulation.

1. [[Tree-like and dag-like resolution|6JSH907W5R]], at the bottom. Exponential lower bounds are known: [[Haken's theorem|4PCEYEW3JG]] for the [[pigeonhole formulas|RJ571F7FF0]], [[Urquhart's theorem|HF1GHVRDSD]] for the [[Tseitin formulas|JXTCC6KXNG]], and the [[size-width relation|GWDGRPAQSM]] of Ben-Sasson and Wigderson for random 3-CNFs.
2. [[Cutting planes|KKCX2HQP2K]] and the [[algebraic proof systems|ZERBQSTZB5]]. Cutting planes has polynomial-size refutations of the pigeonhole formulas, and Pudlák proved exponential lower bounds for it by [[feasible interpolation|59GJCM8C66]].
3. [[Bounded-depth Frege|41QJ1WEMJ3]]. [[Ajtai's theorem|Q0ERK64J78]] gives superpolynomial lower bounds for the pigeonhole formulas.
4. [[Frege systems|J2BAR09TPF]] and [[extended Frege|5YSE04SHFH]], at the top of this list. No superpolynomial lower bound is known; see [[lower bounds for Frege systems|ANSV13M4FS]]. This is an open problem.

Beside the size of proofs there is the question of finding them.
A system is [[automatizable|WBECDG163W]] if proofs can be found in time polynomial in the size of the shortest proof.
Atserias and Müller showed that resolution is not automatizable unless $\mathrm{P} = \mathrm{NP}$.

### History

Tseitin proved lower bounds for regular resolution in 1968, before the field had a name.
Cook and Reckhow gave the general definition of a proof system and its relation to $\mathrm{NP}$ and $\mathrm{coNP}$ in 1979.
Haken proved the first exponential lower bound for general resolution in 1985, and Ajtai the first superpolynomial bound for bounded-depth Frege in 1988.
Lower bounds for Frege systems remain open.

### Common errors

- A lower bound for one system says nothing about a stronger system. The pigeonhole formulas are hard for resolution and easy for cutting planes and Frege.
- Size, length, width, degree and space are different measures. A bound on the number of lines is not a bound on the number of symbols.
- A lower bound for all proof systems would give $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$, and so $\mathrm{P} \ne \mathrm{NP}$. Lower bounds for particular systems do not imply either.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A tautology is a formula that is true under every truth assignment.
One can check this with a truth table, but the table for $n$ variables has $2^n$ rows.
A proof can be much shorter, but it is not clear that a short proof always exists.
Proof complexity asks how long the shortest proof must be, in a given proof system.
For example, in the resolution system, every proof of the pigeonhole principle, "$n + 1$ pigeons do not fit in $n$ holes one per hole", has size exponential in $n$.

### L2 human

We compare the sizes of proofs of the pigeonhole formulas $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ in four systems.

1. Truth tables: the formulas have $n(n+1)$ variables, so the table has $2^{n(n+1)}$ rows.
2. Resolution: exponential in $n$, by Haken's theorem.
3. Bounded-depth Frege of a fixed depth: more than every polynomial in $n$, by Ajtai's theorem.
4. Cutting planes and Frege: polynomial in $n$.

The typical use of the concept is to place a family of tautologies in this table, with an upper and a lower bound for each system.
A trap: each entry in the table names its system and its measure.

### L3 human

Proof complexity studies the nondeterministic complexity of the coNP-complete language of tautologies, one proof system at a time.
Its lower bounds use combinatorics (restrictions, expansion), circuit complexity (switching lemmas, interpolation by monotone circuits) and algebra (degree of polynomials).
On the logic side, the systems are the nonuniform versions of theories of [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]], and their lower bounds are independence results for these theories.
The base of propositional logic is in [[the propositional calculus|JDK63589SA]], and the first-order theories are in [[formal arithmetic and its fragments|QHCRN4JVG0]].
