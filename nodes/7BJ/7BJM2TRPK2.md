## Summary

The geometric phase, or Berry phase, is a phase acquired by an eigenstate of a quantum Hamiltonian.
It appears when the parameters of the Hamiltonian are moved slowly around a closed loop, in addition to the usual dynamical phase.
It depends only on the loop in parameter space, not on the speed at which it is traversed.
Mathematically it is the [[holonomy|WHDZ3MK56B]] of a natural connection, the Berry connection, on the line bundle of eigenvectors.
It explains effects such as the Aharonov–Bohm effect and the quantisation of the Hall conductance, and its optical case is the [[Pancharatnam–Berry phase|VKEYXGFS6E]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Carry a quantum system slowly around a loop of settings and bring it back to where it started.
The state comes back the same, except for a phase factor.
Part of that phase counts the time spent, but another part only remembers the shape of the loop.
Take a spin one-half aligned with a magnetic field, and turn the field slowly around a cone.
The geometric phase is then half the solid angle of the cone, up to sign.
It is like walking a pointer around a loop on a sphere without turning it: at the end it has turned by the enclosed area.
