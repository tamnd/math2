## Summary

Classical mechanics is the study of the motion of particles, systems of particles and rigid bodies under forces, in the setting of Newton's laws.
A mechanical system is described by its configuration, and its equations of motion fix how the configuration changes in time from given initial positions and velocities.
Classical mechanics predicts the motion of planets, satellites, machines and structures, and it is the starting point for the other parts of physics.
Newton published his laws of motion in 1687, and Lagrange, Hamilton and Jacobi later recast mechanics in terms of energy and variational principles.
This topic collects the classical mechanics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Symplectic geometry, dynamical systems, the calculus of variations, continuum mechanics and relativity have their own topics.
