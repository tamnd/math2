## Summary

A reversible process is an idealised process that can be run backwards, so that both the system and its surroundings return to their initial states.
It is [[quasistatic|PVDWD7HGFG]] and has no dissipation, such as friction or heat flow across a finite temperature difference.
Reversible processes give the bounds of thermodynamics, such as the maximal efficiency of a heat engine.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Film a slow, frictionless piston moving in and out, and the film looks right when played backwards.
Film an egg breaking or a hot cup cooling, and the backward film looks impossible.
The first kind of change is reversible; the second kind is not.
Real processes are never exactly reversible, but very slow and smooth ones come close.
