## Summary

The Brunt–Väisälä frequency, or buoyancy frequency, is the frequency at which a small parcel of fluid oscillates vertically when displaced in a stably stratified fluid.
It measures the strength of the stratification.
It is the largest frequency of [[internal waves|GPKN5XHJ1F]], and it enters the [[Richardson number|6CXB96Y39R]] and the [[quasi-geostrophic equations|8Z4QBWTWVR]].
A negative square signals an unstable stratification, which leads to convection.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a lake in summer, warm light water lies on top of cold heavy water.
Push a small blob of water upward: it is now heavier than its new surroundings, so it sinks back, overshoots, and bobs up and down.
The rate of this bobbing is the Brunt–Väisälä frequency.
The more strongly the density changes with depth, the faster the blob bobs; in the ocean a typical period is several minutes to an hour.
