## Summary

Anisotropic heat conduction is [[conduction|SW8X60GYZT]] in a material that conducts heat better in some directions than in others.
Crystals, wood, fibre composites and layered rocks are examples.
The [[thermal conductivity|XBQBT9E7JT]] becomes a symmetric positive definite matrix $K$, and [[Fourier's law|9YJZ1PP2Y8]] becomes $\mathbf{q} = -K \nabla T$.
The heat flux then need not point straight down the temperature gradient.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Wood carries heat more easily along the grain than across it.
Heat a spot on a wooden board and the warm region spreads into an oval stretched along the grain, not a circle.
In such a material the heat flow can even go somewhat sideways to the direction in which the temperature drops fastest.
The symmetry of $K$ is linked to the [[Onsager reciprocal relations|9YACB2RQ80]], and its positivity to the second law of thermodynamics.
