## Summary

The enthalpy of a system is its internal energy plus the product of its pressure and volume.
It is the [[thermodynamic potential|MKZW03Q590]] whose natural variables are entropy and pressure.
In a process at constant pressure, the change of enthalpy equals the heat taken in.
So enthalpy is the natural measure of heat in chemistry and engineering, where most processes run at constant pressure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a gas is heated in an open container, part of the energy goes into pushing the air away as the gas expands.
Enthalpy counts this part as well: it is the energy of the gas plus the energy needed to make room for it.
For example, the heat given off when a gram of fuel burns in open air is a change of enthalpy.
Tables of "heats of reaction" in chemistry are tables of enthalpy changes.
