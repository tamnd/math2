## Definition

In the *equal-variance Gaussian model of signal detection theory*, each trial produces an internal evidence value $X$, a [[random variable|0PNRKMRC55]] with:

1. $X \sim N(0, 1)$ on noise trials and $X \sim N(d', 1)$ on signal trials, for a constant $d' \ge 0$ called the *sensitivity*;
2. the response "signal present" exactly when $X > c$, for a constant $c \in \R$ called the *criterion*.

Let $\Phi$ be the [[cumulative distribution function|0H8H7ZTHW2]] of the standard [[normal distribution|05AQE0QR0D]].
The *hit rate* is $H = P(X > c \mid \text{signal}) = 1 - \Phi(c - d')$ and the *false alarm rate* is $F = P(X > c \mid \text{noise}) = 1 - \Phi(c)$.

## Remarks

Solving for the parameters gives $d' = \Phi^{-1}(H) - \Phi^{-1}(F)$ and $c = -\Phi^{-1}(F)$.
Example: $H = 0.8413$ and $F = 0.1587$ give $\Phi^{-1}(H) \approx 1$ and $\Phi^{-1}(F) \approx -1$, so $d' \approx 2$ and $c \approx 1$.
Check: $1 - \Phi(1 - 2) = \Phi(1) \approx 0.8413$.
Non-example: a model where the observer answers "yes" at random with probability $1/2$, regardless of $X$, violates clause 2; it gives $H = F$, which the model fits only with $d' = 0$.
Varying $c$ with $d'$ fixed traces the receiver operating characteristic curve $c \mapsto (F, H)$.
