## Summary

A Cobb–Douglas production function gives output as a constant times a product of powers of the inputs.
An example is $A L^{\alpha} K^{\beta}$ for labour $L$ and capital $K$.
Each exponent is the elasticity of output with respect to its input, and the sum of the exponents measures the returns to scale.
It is the most used functional form for production in economics, and the same form serves as a utility function in consumer theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a factory that uses workers and machines.
With a Cobb–Douglas function, raising the number of workers by $1\%$ raises output by about $\alpha \%$, whatever the levels of the inputs are.
For $Q = L^{1/2} K^{1/2}$, doubling both workers and machines doubles output, while doubling only the workers multiplies output by $\sqrt{2}$.
Both inputs are needed: with no workers or no machines, nothing is produced.
