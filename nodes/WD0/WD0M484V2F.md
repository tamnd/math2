## Definition

*Non-equilibrium thermodynamics* is the macroscopic theory of systems that are not in [[thermodynamic equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]] and of the [[irreversible processes|D36GZB2766]] in them.
In its classical form it rests on these assumptions.

1. The system is in [[local thermodynamic equilibrium|J027RJ924H]]: temperature, pressure, chemical potentials and densities are fields in space and time that obey the equilibrium relations locally.
2. Each extensive quantity obeys a local balance equation with a flux and a source term.
3. The local [[entropy production|STR73R5MX4]] is a sum $\sigma = \sum_k J_k \cdot X_k \ge 0$ of products of fluxes $J_k$ and conjugate thermodynamic forces $X_k$.
4. Constitutive laws give the fluxes as functions of the forces; in the linear regime $J_i = \sum_k L_{ik} X_k$.

## Remarks

Example: heat conduction in a solid with temperature field $T(x,t)$ fits this frame.
The flux is the heat flux $J_q$, the force is $\nabla(1/T)$, and Fourier's law $J_q = -k \nabla T$ is a linear law with $L = kT^2$.
Non-example: a gas so dilute that a molecule crosses the whole container between collisions has no local temperature, so clause 1 fails; such systems need kinetic theory.
Theories that drop clause 1, such as extended irreversible thermodynamics, are also counted as non-equilibrium thermodynamics in a broader sense.
