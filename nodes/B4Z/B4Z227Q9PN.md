## Definition

Let $x_1, \dots, x_N$ be points in $\R^d$ with $d = 2$ or $3$, and let $q_1, \dots, q_N$ be real numbers.
Let $G$ be a kernel such as the [[fundamental solution of the Laplacian|9Y01DS79PR]].
The task is to compute the potentials $\phi_i = \sum_{j \ne i} q_j G(x_i, x_j)$ for $i = 1, \dots, N$.
The *fast multipole method* with expansion order $p$ computes approximations of all $\phi_i$ as follows.

1. Enclose the points in a box. Subdivide it recursively into $2^d$ children, giving a tree, until each leaf box holds a bounded number of points.
2. For each leaf, form the [[multipole expansion|KE84W5KNT8]] of its points truncated at order $p$. Going up the tree, translate the expansions of the children to the centre of the parent and add them.
3. For each box, convert the multipole expansions of the well-separated boxes in its interaction list into one local expansion about its centre. Add to it the local expansion inherited from its parent.
4. At each leaf, evaluate the local expansion at its points, and add the interactions with points in the neighbouring leaves directly.

## Remarks

Example: in the plane, identify $\R^2$ with $\C$ and take $G(z, w) = \log \abs{z - w}$.
For points $z_j$ in a disc of centre $c$ and radius $r$, and for $\abs{z - c} > r$,
$$\sum_j q_j \log(z - z_j) = Q \log(z - c) - \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{1}{k} \sum_j q_j \frac{(z_j - c)^k}{(z - c)^k}, \qquad Q = \sum_j q_j.$$
This follows from $\log(z - z_j) = \log(z - c) + \log\big(1 - \frac{z_j - c}{z - c}\big)$ and the series of $\log(1 - u)$.
The potential is the real part, and keeping $p$ terms gives an error of order $(r / \abs{z - c})^p$.

Non-example: the Barnes–Hut tree code also groups far points by boxes, but it has no step 3: each point is evaluated against far boxes one by one.
Its cost is $O(N \log N)$, not $O(N)$.

For a fixed order $p$ and points that are not too unevenly spread, the cost of the method is $O(N)$, and the error decreases geometrically as $p$ grows.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The method has two passes over a tree of boxes.
Going up, it summarises each box by a short list of numbers that describe its far field.
Going down, it turns the summaries of far boxes into a smooth local description of their pull near each box.
Only near neighbours are computed exactly.
