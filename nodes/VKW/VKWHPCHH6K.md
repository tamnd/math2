## Summary

The angular momentum operator is the quantum version of the classical [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] $L = x \times p$.
Its three components are self-adjoint operators that do not commute: $[L_x, L_y] = i\hbar L_z$ and cyclic permutations.
They generate the action of rotations on wave functions.
Triples of operators with these relations that integrate to an action of rotations come from unitary representations of [[SU(2)|E5P8AG6CK7]].
As a consequence, the squared angular momentum takes the values $\hbar^2 j(j+1)$, where $j$ is an integer or half-integer.
Each component then takes the values $\hbar m$ with $m = -j, \ldots, j$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In quantum mechanics, the amount of rotation of a particle is measured by operators instead of numbers.
The rotation about different axes cannot be known exactly at the same time, because the three operators do not commute.
What can be known together is the total amount $\hbar^2 j(j+1)$ and the amount about one chosen axis, $\hbar m$.
For example, the orbital angular momentum of an electron in a $p$ orbital has $j = 1$, so its value about the $z$-axis is $-\hbar$, $0$ or $\hbar$.
