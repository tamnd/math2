## Summary

A stochastic game is a dynamic game played in stages, in which the game played at each stage depends on a state.
The current state and the players' actions determine the stage payoffs and the probabilities of the next state.
Lloyd Shapley introduced stochastic games in 1953.
They contain [[repeated games|XHE1BSE7BF]] (one state) and Markov decision processes (one player) as special cases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a board game in which the board changes as you play, partly by your moves and partly by dice.
At each turn all players see the current board, choose their moves, collect points, and then the moves and the dice decide the next board.
The players care about the points collected over the whole game.
A simple example is a fishing contest where the stock of fish is the state: heavy fishing today earns more now but leaves fewer fish tomorrow.
