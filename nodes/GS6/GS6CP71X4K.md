## Summary

Total internal reflection is the complete reflection of a wave at the boundary with a medium of lower refractive index.
It happens when the angle of incidence exceeds a critical angle.
Above that angle Snell's law has no real solution for the refracted ray, and no power crosses the boundary.
A field still leaks a short distance into the second medium as an evanescent wave.
It is the guiding principle of optical fibers and of many prisms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light going from glass into air bends away from the normal.
If you tilt the ray more and more, the refracted ray lies flatter and flatter along the surface.
Past a certain angle, about $42^\circ$ for glass and air, there is no refracted ray at all, and every bit of the light bounces back.
You can see this when you look up at the surface of the water from below in a swimming pool: at shallow angles it shines like a mirror.
