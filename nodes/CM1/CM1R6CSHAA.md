## Summary

Hele-Shaw flow is the slow viscous flow between two parallel plates separated by a narrow gap.
After averaging across the gap, the velocity is proportional to the pressure gradient, and the pressure is a harmonic function in the plane.
So this viscous flow obeys the same equations as a two-dimensional [[potential flow|84K7TEWZ2Z]].
It also obeys the same equations as [[Darcy's law|DX8RV2XG6H]] for flow in a porous medium.
A Hele-Shaw cell is used to visualise streamlines, and its moving boundary problems are a classic topic linked with complex analysis.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Squeeze a thin layer of syrup between two glass plates and push it with air or water.
The fluid is so thin and slow that friction against the plates controls everything.
Each bit of fluid then simply moves downhill in pressure, at a speed proportional to the slope of the pressure.
Surprisingly, the streamlines look exactly like those of an ideal frictionless fluid flowing around obstacles.
