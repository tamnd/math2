## Summary

Solid mechanics is the branch of [[continuum mechanics|X01DZTS224]] that studies solid bodies: how they deform, carry loads, vibrate and break.
A solid, unlike a fluid, has a preferred shape, so its stress depends on how far it is deformed from a reference configuration.
Its main theories are elasticity, plasticity and viscoelasticity, and it underlies structural, mechanical and materials engineering.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Push on a rubber block and it changes shape; let go and it springs back.
Water has no such memory of a shape, which is the basic difference between a solid and a fluid.
Solid mechanics asks how much a body deforms under given forces and how large the internal forces become.
A simple case is a hanging wire: a load $W$ on a wire of cross-section area $A$ produces the stress $W/A$ inside it.
When the load is small, the wire stretches in proportion to it.
