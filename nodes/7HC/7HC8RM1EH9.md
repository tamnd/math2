## Summary

The post-Newtonian expansion is an approximation scheme for general relativity in systems where gravity is weak and matter moves slowly compared with light.
The metric and the equations of motion are expanded in powers of $1/c^2$, and the leading term is [[Newtonian gravity|29WRN5J730]].
Each further order, called first post-Newtonian (1PN), 2PN and so on, adds corrections of relative size $v^2/c^2 \sim GM/(rc^2)$.
It is the main tool for the solar system and for the inspiral phase of binary stars and black holes that emit gravitational waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Newton's theory of gravity is very accurate when bodies move slowly and gravity is weak.
The post-Newtonian expansion starts from Newton and then adds small corrections, one layer at a time, like the terms of a Taylor series.
The size of each layer is set by the ratio of the speed of the bodies to the speed of light, squared.
For the Earth around the Sun this ratio is about $10^{-8}$, so the first correction already gives excellent accuracy.
For two black holes about to merge the ratio is much larger, and many layers are needed.
