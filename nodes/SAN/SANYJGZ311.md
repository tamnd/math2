## Summary

A Lagrangian density is a function of a point of spacetime, the values of the fields there and their first derivatives.
Its integral over a region of spacetime is the action of the field configuration.
The field equations of a classical field theory are the [[Euler–Lagrange equations|PS0VV149ET]] of this action.
It plays for fields the role that the Lagrangian of [[Lagrangian mechanics|47JMCBM3VJ]] plays for systems with finitely many degrees of freedom.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In mechanics, a Lagrangian is one number for the whole system at each time, usually kinetic minus potential energy.
A field, such as the height of a vibrating membrane, has a value at every point, so it has infinitely many degrees of freedom.
The Lagrangian density gives an energy-like quantity per unit of volume and time, computed from the field and its slopes at a point.
For a string with small transverse displacement $\varphi(t, x)$, density $\rho$ and tension $T$, it is $\frac{1}{2} \rho \varphi_t^2 - \frac{1}{2} T \varphi_x^2$.
Integrating it over space and time gives the action, and requiring the action to be stationary gives the [[wave equation|5EHD2XBQDG]] $\rho \varphi_{tt} = T \varphi_{xx}$.
