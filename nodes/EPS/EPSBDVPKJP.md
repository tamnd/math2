## Statement

One binary operation, "take the larger value and step up", generates every operation on $m$ values.
Let $m \ge 2$ and $V = \{0, 1, \dots, m - 1\}$, and define Webb's function as follows.
$$w(x, y) = \max(x, y) + 1 \bmod m$$

**Theorem.** The operation $w$ is a [[Sheffer function|6KFS240R42]] on $V$: every operation $V^n \to V$ with $n \ge 1$ is a composition of copies of $w$ and projections.

The [[proof|P1FJ5TEFBX]] defines Post's connectives from $w$.

## Remarks

### Why each part is needed

The step up "$+1$" is essential.
Without it the operation is $\max$, and every term in $\max$ sends $(a, \dots, a)$ to $a$, so it is not a Sheffer function; see [[the non-example|3WGZ1AR5E0]].

The reduction "mod $m$" is needed for $w$ to be an operation on $V$ at all.
Without it, $w(m - 1, m - 1) = m$ lies outside $V$.

The condition $m \ge 2$ excludes the one-element set, where every operation is a projection and the notion says nothing.

### What it gives you

Every $m$-valued logic whose connectives are truth functions on $V$ can be rewritten with $w$ as its only connective.
The rewriting can make formulas much longer, so it serves theory, not practice.
For $m = 2$, $w(x, y) = 1 - \max(x, y)$ is NOR, so the theorem extends the classical fact that NOR alone is complete.
[[Webb's function on three values|AR2E4BF627]] checks the first steps of the proof with the table for $m = 3$.

### The converse

The converse fails: not every Sheffer function on $V$ has the form of $w$.
For $m = 2$, NAND is a Sheffer function and it is not NOR.

### Generalizations

Any operation from which Post's $\neg$ and $\max$ are terms is a Sheffer function, by [[Post's theorem|8P3Y6T0RZS]].
For arbitrary operations, Słupecki's criterion and Rosenberg's classification of maximal clones decide the question; see [[Sheffer function|1K829SJG3K]].

## History

Webb published the result in 1935, in a short paper in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.
His $m$-valued generalization of NOR is now called the Webb operation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the larger of two values and move it one step up, with the top value wrapping around to $0$.
This single operation can build every table on the $m$ values.
For two values it is the NOR gate.

### L2 human

The proof has three short moves.
First feed the same input twice: $w(x, x)$ is the shift $S x = x + 1 \bmod m$.
Then shift $m - 1$ more times to undo the step up inside $w$, which gives $\max$.
Finally shift $m - 1$ times on its own to get Post's negation $x - 1 \bmod m$.
For $m = 3$ at $(0, 1)$: $w(0, 1) = 2$, and two shifts give $0$ and then $1 = \max(0, 1)$.
The typical use is this reduction to a set that is already known to be complete.
Watch out: the number of extra shifts depends on $m$, so the formula for $\max$ changes with the number of values.

### L3 human

The clone generated by $w$ contains $S$ and $\max$, hence Post's connectives, hence all operations.
In this sense Webb's theorem is a corollary of Post's theorem plus one line of algebra.
Many other Sheffer functions exist on $m$ values, and Rosenberg's theorem characterizes them all as the operations that preserve none of the relations of the maximal clones.
