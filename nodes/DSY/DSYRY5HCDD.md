## Summary

An oblique shock is a [[shock wave|FRVV0NHBXT]] in a supersonic flow that is inclined to the incoming flow direction.
The flow crossing it is turned towards the shock by a deflection angle and slowed down, while the velocity component along the shock is unchanged.
Oblique shocks form when a supersonic flow meets a wedge, a concave corner or the inlet of an engine.
The relation between the Mach number, the shock angle and the deflection angle is the basic tool of their theory.
See the [[definition|X32JRW8WWT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a supersonic stream hits a wedge, the air must turn to follow the wedge surface.
It cannot turn gradually, because the information travels too slowly, so it turns abruptly at a slanted shock starting at the tip.
Behind this shock the air is compressed and flows parallel to the wedge.
For a stream at twice the speed of sound, a shock at $45$ degrees turns the air by about $15$ degrees.
