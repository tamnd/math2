## Summary

Gauge fixing is the choice of an extra condition on the potentials of a field theory.
The condition removes, fully or in part, the freedom to change the potentials without changing the physical fields.
In electromagnetism the [[four-potential|BQFCNWFV9Q]] is determined by the fields only up to the gradient of a function, and a gauge condition picks out preferred potentials.
Different conditions, such as the [[Lorenz gauge|YBHAVTWF82]] and the [[Coulomb gauge|JWGVKWD7TA]], simplify the equations in different ways.
Gauge fixing is also a necessary step in the quantization of gauge theories.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The electric and magnetic fields can be computed from potentials, but many different potentials give the same fields.
This is like measuring heights from different sea levels: the slopes do not change.
To compute anything one has to agree on one choice, and the agreement is a gauge condition.
A good choice makes the equations for the potentials simple, for example turning them into wave equations.
