## Summary

The no-slip condition is the boundary condition that a viscous fluid has the same velocity as a solid wall at the wall.
For a wall at rest it says that the fluid velocity is zero on the wall.
It is the standard boundary condition for the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]], and it is the reason why [[boundary layers|YCFPD0GE5G]] form near walls.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Fluid touching a solid surface sticks to it.
Water at the wall of a pipe does not move, while water in the middle flows fastest.
Dust on a fan blade stays on the blade even when the fan spins fast, because the air right at the blade moves with it.
Mathematically, the condition fixes the whole velocity vector on the boundary, so it is a [[Dirichlet boundary condition|EETVA6QYX4]] for the velocity.
