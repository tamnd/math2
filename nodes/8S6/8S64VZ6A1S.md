## Definition

Let $AP$ be a set of atomic propositions.
The formulas of *linear temporal logic* over $AP$ are given by the grammar
$$\varphi ::= p \mid \neg \varphi \mid \varphi \land \varphi \mid X \varphi \mid \varphi \, U \, \varphi, \qquad p \in AP.$$
A formula is interpreted on an infinite word $w = w_0 w_1 w_2 \cdots$ with each $w_i \subseteq AP$.
For $i \ge 0$, let $w^{i} = w_i w_{i+1} \cdots$ be the suffix of $w$ from position $i$.
The relation $w \models \varphi$ is defined by induction on $\varphi$:

1. $w \models p$ if and only if $p \in w_0$;
2. $w \models \neg\varphi$ if and only if $w \not\models \varphi$, and $w \models \varphi \land \psi$ if and only if $w \models \varphi$ and $w \models \psi$;
3. $w \models X\varphi$ if and only if $w^{1} \models \varphi$;
4. $w \models \varphi \, U \, \psi$ if and only if there is $j \ge 0$ with $w^{j} \models \psi$ and $w^{i} \models \varphi$ for all $0 \le i < j$.

Let $s$ be a state of a [[Kripke structure|RFQ8DJZNQT]] with a total transition relation and a labelling $L$ of states by subsets of $AP$.
Then $s$ satisfies $\varphi$ if the word $L(s_0) L(s_1) \cdots$ of every infinite path $s = s_0, s_1, \dots$ satisfies $\varphi$.

## Notation

1. $F\varphi = \top \, U \, \varphi$ ("eventually") and $G\varphi = \neg F \neg \varphi$ ("always"), where $\top = p \vee \neg p$ for a fixed $p$.
2. The other connectives of [[propositional logic|09K9MWD2QQ]] are defined as usual.
3. $\mathrm{Words}(\varphi)$ is the set of infinite words over $2^{AP}$ that satisfy $\varphi$.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. Let $AP = \set{p}$ and $w = \emptyset \, \emptyset \, \set{p} \, \set{p} \, \set{p} \cdots$. Then $w \models F G p$, because $w^{2} \models G p$: every suffix of $w^{2}$ starts with a letter containing $p$.
2. On the same word, $w \models \neg p \, U \, p$ with witness $j = 2$, and $w \models XXp$.

### Non-examples

1. The word $w' = \set{p} \, \emptyset \, \set{p} \, \emptyset \cdots$ does not satisfy $F G p$. Every suffix of $w'$ has a later position without $p$, so no suffix satisfies $G p$.
2. A finite word is not a model in this definition. Variants for finite traces exist, and there $X\varphi$ is false at the last position.

### Basic properties

1. The satisfaction relation at position $i$ depends only on the suffix $w^i$.
2. The until is reflexive: $\varphi \, U \, \psi$ holds when $\psi$ holds at position $0$, whatever $\varphi$ is.
3. If the transition relation is not total, some states have no infinite path, and they satisfy every formula. This is why totality is assumed.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture time as a line of moments $0, 1, 2, \dots$, and at each moment some basic facts are true.
An LTL formula is a statement about the whole line, read from moment $0$.
"$G\,\mathit{safe}$" says the system is safe at every moment.
"$G(\mathit{req} \to F\,\mathit{grant})$" says that whenever a request happens, a grant happens then or later.
A system satisfies a formula when every possible run of it does.

### L2 human

We evaluate $p \, U \, q$ on $w = \set{p}\,\set{p}\,\set{q}\,\emptyset\,\emptyset \cdots$.

1. The witness must be a $j$ with $q \in w_j$, so $j = 2$.
2. For $i = 0$ and $i = 1$ we have $p \in w_i$.
3. So $w \models p \, U \, q$.
4. At position $3$ the suffix $w^3$ has no $q$ at all, so $w^3 \not\models p \, U \, q$.

A trap is the clause for a state: it quantifies over all paths, so a state can satisfy neither $Fp$ nor $\neg Fp$.

### L3 human

The semantics is the [[standard translation|YW6YGBMVQY]] style reading over the frame $(\mathbb{N}, <)$, with $U$ as a binary modality.
Clause 4 is a first-order formula in one free variable over $(\mathbb{N}, <)$, so every LTL formula is first-order definable on words.
