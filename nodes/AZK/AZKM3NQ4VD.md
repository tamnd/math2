## Summary

The displacement current density is the time derivative of the [[electric displacement field|NJVXQZ780B]].
Maxwell added it to [[Ampère's circuital law|HK2E8BX09G]], so that a changing electric field produces a magnetic field just as an electric current does.
Without it Ampère's law would contradict conservation of charge for currents that are not steady.
With it [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] predict electromagnetic waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Charge a capacitor: current flows in the wires, but no charge crosses the gap between the plates.
Still, a magnetic field circles the gap just as it circles the wires.
The reason is that the electric field in the gap grows while the capacitor charges.
A growing electric field acts like a current for the purpose of making magnetic fields, and this is the displacement current.
