## Definition

Let $G$ be the proper real-rational [[transfer function|86JKV3TRFD]] matrix of a plant, of size $p \times m$.
Let $K$ be a controller of size $m \times p$ in negative feedback with it.
The *loop transfer function* is $L = G K$, and the closed loop has the [[sensitivity function|7J72HJX4T7]] $S = (I + L)^{-1}$ and the complementary sensitivity $T = L (I + L)^{-1}$.
Let $\omega_\ell < \omega_h$ be two frequencies and let $\gamma > 1$.
*Loop-shaping* is the design of a stabilizing controller $K$ such that the singular values of $L(i\omega)$ meet a prescribed shape, typically:

1. $\underline\sigma(L(i\omega)) \ge \gamma$ for $\omega \le \omega_\ell$, so that $\bar\sigma(S(i\omega)) \le 1/(\gamma - 1)$ there;
2. $\bar\sigma(L(i\omega)) \le 1/\gamma$ for $\omega \ge \omega_h$, so that $\bar\sigma(T(i\omega)) \le 1/(\gamma - 1)$ there;
3. the closed loop is internally stable with adequate [[gain and phase margins|J39ZSFW0YF]] near the crossover frequencies between $\omega_\ell$ and $\omega_h$.

Here $\bar\sigma$ and $\underline\sigma$ are the largest and smallest [[singular values|09VMDSRXWJ]].

## Remarks

The bounds in clauses 1 and 2 follow from $\bar\sigma((I + L)^{-1}) \le 1/(\underline\sigma(L) - 1)$ when $\underline\sigma(L) > 1$, and $\bar\sigma(L (I + L)^{-1}) \le \bar\sigma(L)/(1 - \bar\sigma(L))$ when $\bar\sigma(L) < 1$.
Example: for $G(s) = 1/s$ and $K = 1$, the loop $L(s) = 1/s$ has gain $1/\omega$.
The gain is at least $10$ for $\omega \le 0.1$ and at most $1/10$ for $\omega \ge 10$, and the phase at crossover is $-90^\circ$.
So the shape is met with $\gamma = 10$, $\omega_\ell = 0.1$ and $\omega_h = 10$.
Non-example: $G(s) = 1/s$ and $K(s) = 1/s$ give $L = 1/s^2$ with phase $-180^\circ$ at all frequencies; the closed loop $s^2 + 1$ has poles on the imaginary axis, so clause 3 fails.
In H-infinity loop-shaping one first shapes $G_s = W_2 G W_1$ with weights $W_1$, $W_2$, then computes a controller $K_s$ that robustly stabilizes $G_s$ against normalized coprime factor perturbations, and uses $K = W_1 K_s W_2$.
