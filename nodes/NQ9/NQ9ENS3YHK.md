## Summary

The Goldreich–Tremaine theory computes the gravitational torque between a thin rotating disk and a satellite that orbits inside or near it.
Peter Goldreich and Scott Tremaine developed it around 1979 and 1980, first for planetary rings and then for disks around young stars.
The torque is exchanged only at a discrete set of [[Lindblad resonances|FJR64ZRB4E]] and corotation resonances, where the satellite launches spiral density waves.
It is the basis of the theory of [[planetary migration|YMDCDFBTQ1]], of gap opening by moons in rings, and of shepherd satellites.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A moon going around inside a ring of particles pulls on the ring a little at every pass.
Most of these pulls cancel, but at special radii, where the ring particles move in step with the moon's pull, the effects add up.
There the moon stirs spiral waves in the ring, like a paddle stirring water, and the waves carry angular momentum away.
The ring outside the moon is pushed outward and the ring inside is pushed inward, so a moon can clear a gap or keep a narrow ring confined.
