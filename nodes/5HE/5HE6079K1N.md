## Definition

*Physical geodesy* is the branch of [[geodesy|WCXMP72NSC]] that determines the gravity field of the Earth and its relation to the figure of the Earth.
Its central unknown is the gravity potential, the [[geopotential|5Y2BWNY6MF]] $W$, as a function on and outside the Earth's surface.
Its methods are those of [[potential theory|9PM8Q2E30Q]], applied to measurements of gravity, of the direction of the plumb line and of satellite orbits.

## Remarks

Example: determining the geoid from gravity measurements by Stokes' formula is a problem of physical geodesy.
Non-example: computing the distance between two points of a given ellipsoid uses no gravity data; it belongs to geometric geodesy.
Physical geodesy also provides orthometric heights, which are measured from the geoid along the plumb line.
