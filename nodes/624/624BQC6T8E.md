## Statement

Let a column have length $L$, effective length factor $K$, cross-section area $A$ and second moment of area $I$.
Let $r = \sqrt{I/A}$ be its radius of gyration and $s = KL/r$ its slenderness ratio.
Let its material have [[Young's modulus|T8ENJX1905]] $E$ and compressive yield stress $\sigma_y$.
Set $s_t = \sqrt{2 \pi^2 E / \sigma_y}$.
Johnson's parabolic formula is the following empirical rule.
For $s < s_t$ the critical compressive stress of the column is
$$\sigma_{cr} = \sigma_y - \frac{1}{E} \left( \frac{\sigma_y \, s}{2 \pi} \right)^2,$$
while for $s \ge s_t$ the critical stress is given by [[Euler's critical load|E8JCKAE09C]], $\sigma_{cr} = \pi^2 E / s^2$.

## Remarks

The formula is not derived from a model; it is a parabola fitted to tests on columns of intermediate slenderness.
It is chosen so that the two curves join smoothly.
At $s = s_t$ both give $\sigma_{cr} = \sigma_y / 2$, and both have slope $-\sigma_y / s_t$ in $s$, so the parabola is tangent to the Euler curve there.
At $s = 0$ the parabola gives $\sigma_{cr} = \sigma_y$, the crushing stress of a very short block.

Example: for a steel with $E = 200$ GPa and $\sigma_y = 250$ MPa, $s_t = \sqrt{2 \pi^2 \cdot 800} \approx 126$.
A column with $s = 60$ then has $\sigma_{cr} \approx 250 - (250 \cdot 60 / 2\pi)^2 / 200000 \approx 221.5$ MPa, with stresses in MPa.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Euler's formula is right for long thin columns, but for stubby columns it predicts loads above what the material can carry.
Johnson's rule replaces it, for columns that are not slender, by a curve that starts at the yield stress and bends down until it meets Euler's curve.
Engineers use the pair to size columns of every length.

### L2 human

The formula sits between pure yielding and pure elastic buckling and reflects that intermediate columns fail by inelastic buckling.
Tangent-modulus and reduced-modulus theories give a mechanical account of this regime; Johnson's parabola is a simple design substitute.
Another approach to the same regime is the [[Perry–Robertson formula|A1FNYW0HJ9]], which instead models initial crookedness.
