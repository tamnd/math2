## Example

Let $T$ be a [[complete theory|655K8Y7VS8]] in a signature $L$.
We show that the class of models of $T$ has the [[joint embedding property|59FQ5Q2B8R]].

1. Let $M, N \models T$.
2. Since $T$ is complete, $M$ and $N$ are [[elementarily equivalent|P3B1V182N4]].
3. By the [[elementary amalgamation theorem|P2VMSM4H4Y]], there are a structure $K$ and elementary embeddings $M \to K$ and $N \to K$.
4. The structure $K$ is elementarily equivalent to $M$, so $K \models T$.
5. Elementary embeddings are embeddings, so $M$ and $N$ embed into a common model of $T$.

## Remarks

The argument gives more than JEP: the two embeddings are elementary.
The converse fails: the theory of linear orders has JEP, but it is not complete, since it does not decide whether there is a least element.
The example shows that JEP is a weak, universal form of completeness.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two models of a complete theory agree on every first-order sentence.
Because they agree so much, both fit inside a single bigger model.

### L2 human

We take $T$ the theory of $(\Q, <)$, dense linear orders without endpoints.

1. Let $M = (\Q, <)$ and $N = (\R, <)$, both models of $T$.
2. The inclusion $\Q \to \R$ is an elementary embedding, and the identity on $\R$ is one too.
3. So $K = \R$ witnesses JEP for this pair.

The typical use is to see that JEP is automatic for complete theories.
A trap is to expect a common elementary extension of models of different completions; then even plain JEP can fail.
