## Summary

The Born approximation is a way to compute a wave scattered by a weak scatterer.
The total field inside the scatterer is replaced by the incident field, so the scattered field becomes an explicit integral.
In the far field, the scattering amplitude is then a Fourier transform of the scatterer's contrast.
It is the basis of many imaging methods, from X-ray crystallography to diffraction tomography.
See the [[definition|8PPSNDPQ1P]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A wave meets an object and each small piece of the object sends out a small new wave.
In reality those small waves also hit the other pieces and are scattered again.
If the object barely disturbs the wave, these repeated echoes are tiny, and one can ignore them.
Then each piece just responds to the original wave, and the scattered wave is a simple sum over the pieces.
For a faint glass bead in a laser beam, this gives a good picture of the light pattern around it.
