## Summary

A metamaterial is an artificial composite with structure on a scale much smaller than the wavelength.
This structure gives it effective electromagnetic properties that its ingredients do not have.
Its effective [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]] and [[permeability|5MJQ5J5H8Z]] are set by the shape and arrangement of its building blocks, not only by their chemistry.
Metamaterials can have a negative refractive index or strongly anisotropic responses.
They are the hardware of transformation optics and of proposals for cloaking and super-resolution.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary materials get their optical properties from atoms, which are far smaller than the wavelength of light.
A metamaterial copies this idea one level up: it is built from tiny designed elements, such as small metal rings and wires, that are still smaller than the wavelength.
A wave passing through cannot see the individual elements and responds to the average.
By designing the elements, one can make this average behave in ways no natural material does, for example bending light the wrong way.
