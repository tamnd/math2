## Proof

We prove a reduction lemma that removes one cut of largest degree at the cost of adding heights, then an elimination lemma that removes all such cuts at the cost of one exponential, and we iterate.
This gives part 3 of the theorem, for the one-sided propositional calculus of the statement.
Sequents are finite sets of formulas in negation normal form, and $\Gamma, A$ means $\Gamma \cup \{ A \}$.

### Heights and ranks

We write $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma$, for natural numbers $\alpha$ and $\rho$, when $\Gamma$ is an axiom, or when $\Gamma$ follows by ${\wedge}$, ${\vee}$ or cut from premises $\Gamma, A_i$ with $\vdash^{\alpha_i}_\rho \Gamma, A_i$ and $\alpha_i < \alpha$ for each premise.
For a cut we also require $|C| < \rho$ for its cut formula $C$.
So $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma$ says that $\Gamma$ has a derivation of height at most $\alpha$ whose cut formulas have degree less than $\rho$.
This matches the cut rank of the statement, the largest $|C| + 1$, and $\vdash^\alpha_0 \Gamma$ means a derivation without cut.

1. Monotonicity: if $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma$ and $\alpha \leq \beta$, then $\vdash^\beta_\rho \Gamma$.
   The conditions $\alpha_i < \alpha$ imply $\alpha_i < \beta$.
2. Negation keeps the degree, $|\neg A| = |A|$.
   This holds by induction on $A$, because the de Morgan laws exchange $\wedge$ and $\vee$ and keep the number of connectives.
3. Weakening: if $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma$ and $\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'$, then $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma'$.
   We use induction on $\alpha$.
4. In step 3 an axiom stays an axiom.
   For a rule, the principal formula lies in $\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'$, and the induction hypothesis gives the premises $\Gamma', A_i$ with the same heights.

### The reduction lemma, first cases

The lemma says: let $|C| = \rho$, $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma, C$ and $\vdash^\beta_\rho \Delta, \neg C$; then $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta$.
We use induction on $\alpha + \beta$, for all sets $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ at once, and fix derivations of $\Gamma, C$ and $\Delta, \neg C$ with these heights.

5. Case A: the set $\Gamma, C$ is an axiom, with $p$ and $\neg p$ in it.
   If both lie in $\Gamma$, then $\Gamma, \Delta$ is an axiom.
6. Otherwise $C$ is one of $p$ and $\neg p$, and $\neg C$ is the other one, which lies in $\Gamma$.
   So $\Delta, \neg C \subseteq \Gamma, \Delta$, and weakening (step 3) and monotonicity give $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta$.
7. Case B: the last rule for $\Gamma, C$ is a cut, or a ${\wedge}$ or ${\vee}$ step whose principal formula is not $C$.
   Its premises are $\Gamma, C, A_i$ with heights $\alpha_i < \alpha$.
8. The induction hypothesis, applied to the set $\Gamma \cup \{ A_i \}$ and to $\Delta$, gives $\vdash^{\alpha_i + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, A_i, \Delta$.
   This is allowed even when $A_i$ is $C$.
9. The principal formula lies in $\Gamma, C$ and is not $C$, so it lies in $\Gamma, \Delta$.
   A cut keeps its cut formula, of degree less than $\rho$.
10. So the same rule gives $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta$, since $\alpha_i + \beta < \alpha + \beta$.

### The reduction lemma, the principal case

11. Case C: the last rule for $\Gamma, C$ is ${\wedge}$ or ${\vee}$ with principal formula $C$.
    Then $C$ is not a literal, and neither is $\neg C$.
12. We look at the last rule for $\Delta, \neg C$.
    If it is an axiom, its pair $p, \neg p$ lies in $\Delta$, because $\neg C$ is not a literal, and then $\Gamma, \Delta$ is an axiom.
13. If it is a cut, or a step whose principal formula is not $\neg C$, we argue as in steps 7 to 10 with the two sides exchanged.
    Its premises have heights $\beta_j < \beta$, and $\alpha + \beta_j < \alpha + \beta$.
14. It remains that the two last steps have principal formulas $C$ and $\neg C$.
    Let $C = A_0 \wedge A_1$, so that $\neg C = \neg A_0 \vee \neg A_1$.
15. The premises for $\Gamma, C$ are $\Gamma, C, A_0$ and $\Gamma, C, A_1$, with heights $\alpha_0, \alpha_1 < \alpha$.
16. The premise for $\Delta, \neg C$ is $\Delta, \neg C, \neg A_k$ for one $k$, with height $\beta_0 < \beta$.
17. The induction hypothesis for $\Gamma, C, A_k$ and $\Delta, \neg C$ gives $\vdash^{\alpha_k + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, A_k, \Delta$.
18. The induction hypothesis for $\Gamma, C$ and $\Delta, \neg C, \neg A_k$ gives $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta_0}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta, \neg A_k$.
19. We have $|A_k| < |C| = \rho$, so a cut on $A_k$ joins steps 17 and 18 and gives $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta$.
20. If $C = A_0 \vee A_1$, then $\neg C = \neg A_0 \wedge \neg A_1$.
    Steps 15 to 19 apply with the roles of the two derivations exchanged, and the cut is again on $A_k$.

### The elimination lemma and the theorem

The elimination lemma says: if $\vdash^\alpha_{\rho + 1} \Gamma$, then $\vdash^{2^\alpha}_\rho \Gamma$.
We prove it by induction on $\alpha$.

21. If $\Gamma$ is an axiom, then $\vdash^{2^\alpha}_\rho \Gamma$ holds at once.
22. If the last rule is ${\wedge}$, ${\vee}$ or a cut of degree less than $\rho$, the induction hypothesis gives the premises with heights $2^{\alpha_i} < 2^\alpha$, and the same rule gives $\vdash^{2^\alpha}_\rho \Gamma$.
23. If the last rule is a cut on $C$ with $|C| = \rho$, the induction hypothesis gives $\vdash^{2^{\alpha_0}}_\rho \Gamma, C$ and $\vdash^{2^{\alpha_1}}_\rho \Gamma, \neg C$.
24. By step 2, $|\neg C| = \rho$ as well, and the reduction lemma with $\Delta = \Gamma$ gives $\vdash^{2^{\alpha_0} + 2^{\alpha_1}}_\rho \Gamma$.
25. Since $\alpha_0, \alpha_1 \leq \alpha - 1$, we have $2^{\alpha_0} + 2^{\alpha_1} \leq 2 \cdot 2^{\alpha - 1} = 2^\alpha$, and monotonicity gives $\vdash^{2^\alpha}_\rho \Gamma$.

Now let $\vdash^h_r \Gamma$; we show $\vdash^{2_r(h)}_0 \Gamma$ by induction on $r$.

26. For $r = 0$ we have $2_0(h) = h$, and there is nothing to do.
27. For $r + 1$, the elimination lemma gives $\vdash^{2^h}_r \Gamma$, and the induction hypothesis gives $\vdash^{2_r(2^h)}_0 \Gamma$.
28. Both $2_r(2^h)$ and $2_{r + 1}(h)$ are towers of $r + 1$ twos with $h$ on top, so they are equal and $\vdash^{2_{r+1}(h)}_0 \Gamma$.

This proves part 3 of the theorem.

## Idea

The reduction lemma removes one cut of the largest degree from two derivations that have no cut of that degree.
It does not push the cut up one rule at a time; it recurses into both derivations until the cut formula is principal on both sides, and there it cuts on a smaller formula.
The heights add, so removing all cuts of the largest degree turns a height $\alpha$ into at most $2^\alpha$.
Each degree costs one exponential, and $r$ degrees cost a tower of height $r$.
Sets of formulas build in contraction and exchange, so the mix rule is not needed.

## Variants

[[Gentzen's proof with the mix rule|8HV015W88G]] works for two-sided sequents of lists, for LJ and for first-order logic.
The method of rank reduction extends to one-sided first-order calculi, where a substitution lemma handles the quantifier cases.
With infinitary rules, such as the $\omega$-rule of arithmetic, the heights become ordinals, and the same two lemmas are the basis of [[ordinal analysis|C5E5TG8PGP]].
The [[complexity of cut elimination|B5F5HB4YK7]] shows that the tower $2_r(h)$ cannot be replaced by an elementary bound.
