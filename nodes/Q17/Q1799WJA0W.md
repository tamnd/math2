## Proof

This proof gives part 3 of the theorem, for the one-sided propositional calculus of the statement.
Recall that sequents are finite sets of formulas in negation normal form, and that $\Gamma, A$ means $\Gamma \cup \{A\}$.

**Heights and ranks.**
We write $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma$, for natural numbers $\alpha$ and $\rho$, when one of the following holds.

- $\Gamma$ is an axiom.
- $\Gamma$ follows by ${\wedge}$, ${\vee}$ or cut from premises $\Gamma, A_i$ with $\vdash^{\alpha_i}_\rho \Gamma, A_i$ and $\alpha_i < \alpha$ for each premise. For a cut, the cut formula $C$ also satisfies $|C| < \rho$.

So $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma$ says that $\Gamma$ has a derivation of height at most $\alpha$ whose cut formulas have degree less than $\rho$.
This matches the statement, where the cut rank is the largest $|C| + 1$.
In particular $\vdash^\alpha_0 \Gamma$ means a derivation without cut.

1. Monotonicity: if $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma$ and $\alpha \leq \beta$, then $\vdash^\beta_\rho \Gamma$. The conditions $\alpha_i < \alpha$ imply $\alpha_i < \beta$.
2. Negation keeps the degree: $|\neg A| = |A|$. This holds by induction on $A$, because the de Morgan laws exchange $\wedge$ and $\vee$ and keep the number of connectives.
3. Weakening: if $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma$ and $\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'$, then $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma'$. We use induction on $\alpha$.
4. In step 3, an axiom stays an axiom. For a rule, the principal formula lies in $\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'$, and the induction hypothesis gives the premises $\Gamma', A_i$ with the same heights.

**The reduction lemma.**
Let $|C| = \rho$, and let $\vdash^\alpha_\rho \Gamma, C$ and $\vdash^\beta_\rho \Delta, \neg C$.
Then $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta$.
We prove this by induction on $\alpha + \beta$, for all sets $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ at once.
Fix derivations of $\Gamma, C$ and of $\Delta, \neg C$ with these heights.

5. Case A: the set $\Gamma, C$ is an axiom, with $p$ and $\neg p$ in it. If both lie in $\Gamma$, then $\Gamma, \Delta$ is an axiom.
6. Otherwise $C$ is one of $p$ and $\neg p$, and $\neg C$ is the other one, which lies in $\Gamma$. So $\Delta, \neg C \subseteq \Gamma, \Delta$, and weakening (step 3) and monotonicity give $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta$.
7. Case B: the last rule for $\Gamma, C$ is a cut, or a ${\wedge}$ or ${\vee}$ step whose principal formula is not $C$. Its premises are $\Gamma, C, A_i$ with heights $\alpha_i < \alpha$.
8. The induction hypothesis, applied to $\Gamma \cup \{A_i\}$ and $\Delta$, gives $\vdash^{\alpha_i + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, A_i, \Delta$. This is allowed even when $A_i$ is $C$.
9. The principal formula lies in $\Gamma, C$ and is not $C$, so it lies in $\Gamma, \Delta$. A cut keeps its cut formula, of degree less than $\rho$.
10. So the same rule gives $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta$, since $\alpha_i + \beta < \alpha + \beta$.
11. Case C: the last rule for $\Gamma, C$ is ${\wedge}$ or ${\vee}$ with principal formula $C$. Then $C$ is not a literal, and neither is $\neg C$.
12. We now look at the last rule for $\Delta, \neg C$. If it is an axiom, then its pair $p, \neg p$ lies in $\Delta$, because $\neg C$ is not a literal. Then $\Gamma, \Delta$ is an axiom.
13. If it is a cut, or a step whose principal formula is not $\neg C$, we argue as in steps 7 to 10 with the two sides exchanged. Its premises have heights $\beta_j < \beta$, and $\alpha + \beta_j < \alpha + \beta$.
14. It remains that both last steps have principal formulas $C$ and $\neg C$. Let $C = A_0 \wedge A_1$, so $\neg C = \neg A_0 \vee \neg A_1$.
15. The premises for $\Gamma, C$ are $\Gamma, C, A_0$ and $\Gamma, C, A_1$, with heights $\alpha_0, \alpha_1 < \alpha$.
16. The premise for $\Delta, \neg C$ is $\Delta, \neg C, \neg A_k$ for one $k$, with height $\beta_0 < \beta$.
17. The induction hypothesis for $\Gamma, C, A_k$ and $\Delta, \neg C$ gives $\vdash^{\alpha_k + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, A_k, \Delta$.
18. The induction hypothesis for $\Gamma, C$ and $\Delta, \neg C, \neg A_k$ gives $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta_0}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta, \neg A_k$.
19. We have $|A_k| < |C| = \rho$. So a cut on $A_k$ joins steps 17 and 18, and gives $\vdash^{\alpha + \beta}_\rho \Gamma, \Delta$.
20. If $C = A_0 \vee A_1$, then $\neg C = \neg A_0 \wedge \neg A_1$. Steps 15 to 19 apply with the roles of the two derivations exchanged, and the cut is again on $A_k$.

**The elimination lemma.**
If $\vdash^\alpha_{\rho + 1} \Gamma$, then $\vdash^{2^\alpha}_\rho \Gamma$.
We prove this by induction on $\alpha$.

21. If $\Gamma$ is an axiom, then $\vdash^{2^\alpha}_\rho \Gamma$ holds at once.
22. If the last rule is ${\wedge}$, ${\vee}$ or a cut of degree less than $\rho$, the induction hypothesis gives the premises with heights $2^{\alpha_i} < 2^\alpha$. The same rule gives $\vdash^{2^\alpha}_\rho \Gamma$.
23. If the last rule is a cut on $C$ with $|C| = \rho$, the induction hypothesis gives $\vdash^{2^{\alpha_0}}_\rho \Gamma, C$ and $\vdash^{2^{\alpha_1}}_\rho \Gamma, \neg C$.
24. By step 2, $|\neg C| = \rho$ too. The reduction lemma, with $\Delta = \Gamma$, gives $\vdash^{2^{\alpha_0} + 2^{\alpha_1}}_\rho \Gamma$.
25. Since $\alpha_0, \alpha_1 \leq \alpha - 1$, we have $2^{\alpha_0} + 2^{\alpha_1} \leq 2 \cdot 2^{\alpha - 1} = 2^\alpha$. Monotonicity gives $\vdash^{2^\alpha}_\rho \Gamma$.

**The theorem.**

26. Let $\vdash^h_r \Gamma$. We show $\vdash^{2_r(h)}_0 \Gamma$ by induction on $r$.
27. For $r = 0$, we have $2_0(h) = h$, and there is nothing to do.
28. For $r + 1$, the elimination lemma gives $\vdash^{2^h}_r \Gamma$. The induction hypothesis gives $\vdash^{2_r(2^h)}_0 \Gamma$.
29. By induction on $r$, $2_r(2^h) = 2_{r + 1}(h)$, since both are towers of $r + 1$ twos with $h$ on top. So $\vdash^{2_{r+1}(h)}_0 \Gamma$.

This proves part 3 of the theorem.

## Idea

The reduction lemma removes one cut of maximal degree from two derivations without cuts of that degree.
It does not push the cut up step by step: it recurses on both derivations until the cut formula is principal on both sides.
Removing all cuts of the largest degree turns a height $\alpha$ into at most $2^\alpha$, so each degree costs one exponential.
Sets of formulas build in contraction and exchange, so the mix rule is not needed.

## Variants

[[Gentzen's proof with the mix rule|8HV015W88G]] works for two-sided sequents of lists, for LJ and for first-order logic.
The method of this proof extends to one-sided first-order calculi, where a substitution lemma handles the quantifier cases.
With infinitary rules, such as the $\omega$-rule of arithmetic, the heights become ordinals, and the same lemmas are the basis of [[ordinal analysis|C5E5TG8PGP]].
The [[complexity of cut elimination|B5F5HB4YK7]] shows that the tower $2_r(h)$ cannot be replaced by an elementary bound.
