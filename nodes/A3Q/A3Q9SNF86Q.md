## Summary

Multiphase flow is the flow of two or more distinct phases or immiscible fluids at the same time.
Examples are gas bubbles in a liquid, oil and water, and solid particles in a gas.
The phases are separated by interfaces that move and deform with the flow.
Surface tension, phase change and exchange across the interfaces make it richer than single-phase [[fluid dynamics|F2JRWJNYN4]].
It is modelled either by tracking the interfaces, by diffuse interface models, or by averaged equations for interpenetrating continua.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Boiling water, a fizzy drink, rain falling through air and oil seeping with water through rock are all multiphase flows.
In each one, two different materials move together and touch along a boundary.
The boundary can stretch, break into drops or bubbles, and merge again.
Much of the difficulty is to describe what happens at these moving boundaries.
