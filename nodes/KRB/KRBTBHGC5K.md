## Summary

The functional response of a predator is its rate of food intake as a function of the density of its prey.
C. S. Holling described three standard types: linear (type I), saturating (type II), and sigmoid (type III).
The choice of functional response changes the dynamics of predator–prey [[population models|1YEMFQ1H32]], for example whether they have stable equilibria or oscillations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A hungry fox eats more rabbits when rabbits are common, but only up to a point.
Each rabbit takes time to catch and to eat, so even with endless rabbits the fox has a maximal rate.
This gives the type II curve: intake rises fast at first and then levels off.
In type III the predator hardly hunts a rare prey, for example because it has not learned to find it, so the curve starts flat, then rises, then levels off.
