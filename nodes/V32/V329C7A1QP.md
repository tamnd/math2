## Summary

Quantum electrodynamics, or QED, is the quantum field theory of electrons, positrons and light.
It is the [[gauge theory|2GKJQNQMVN]] with gauge group $U(1)$ in which a [[Dirac field|XNZX7VXXR2]] is coupled to the electromagnetic potential.
Its predictions are computed by perturbation theory with [[Feynman diagrams|BS6HVNMTWC]] and [[renormalization|1X0W3AEBEJ]], and some of them agree with experiment to about ten significant digits.
Feynman, Schwinger and Tomonaga received the 1965 Nobel Prize in Physics for its formulation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Quantum electrodynamics describes how charged particles, such as electrons, interact with light.
In this theory light comes in packets, the photons, and two electrons repel each other by exchanging photons.
Each possible history of exchanges is drawn as a small diagram, and adding up the diagrams gives the answer.
The magnetic strength of the electron, computed in this way, matches measurements to extraordinary precision.
