## Summary

Amplitude versus offset (AVO) is the change of the amplitude of a seismic reflection with the distance between source and receiver.
A larger offset means a larger angle of incidence, and the reflection coefficient depends on this angle through the [[Zoeppritz equations|QQQJM3SA2C]].
Measuring this dependence gives information on the contrasts of P-speed, S-speed and density at the reflector.
It is widely used in oil and gas exploration, since gas in a rock lowers its P-speed much more than its S-speed.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a seismic survey many receivers record the same reflector, each at a different distance from the source.
Close receivers see waves that hit the reflector almost straight on, and far receivers see waves that hit it at a slant.
The strength of the echo changes with the slant, and the way it changes depends on what the rocks are made of.
For example, a sand full of gas can give an echo that grows stronger with distance, a classic sign used to find gas.
