## Summary

Fresnel's wave surface, also called the ray surface, describes light sent out from a point inside an anisotropic crystal.
It is the surface that this light reaches in unit time.
It is a quartic surface with two sheets, one for each of the two polarizations that a [[birefringent|8CWCYQJ44T]] crystal allows in each direction.
In a uniaxial crystal it splits into a sphere and a spheroid.
In a biaxial crystal the two sheets meet at four singular points, and this geometry explains [[conical refraction|D7MTADETE9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light a tiny flash inside a crystal and look at the shape of the light after one second.
In glass the light forms a single growing sphere.
In a crystal like calcite it forms two shells: a sphere for one polarization and a flattened or stretched ball for the other.
In more complex crystals the two shells are joined into one strange surface with four dimples where they touch.
