## Summary

A Killing horizon is a null hypersurface of a [[Lorentzian manifold|BY4QYYS788]].
It is generated by the orbits of a [[Killing vector field|5AKNNW3W4Z]] that becomes null on it.
Each Killing horizon carries a number, the surface gravity, that measures how the Killing field fails to be affinely parametrised along the horizon.
The event horizons of the Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes are Killing horizons.
The surface gravity enters the laws of black hole mechanics and the temperature of Hawking radiation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Killing vector field describes a symmetry of spacetime, such as the flow of time in a static spacetime.
Usually this flow moves slower than light, but on some surfaces it becomes exactly light-like.
Such a surface is a Killing horizon: it is made of light rays that stay at the same place with respect to the symmetry.
In flat spacetime, an observer with constant acceleration sees such a horizon, the Rindler horizon, behind which signals can never reach them.
For the Schwarzschild black hole of mass $M$, the horizon is the sphere $r = 2M$.
