## Summary

The gradient wind is a steady horizontal wind that blows along curved isobars.
In it the pressure gradient force, the Coriolis force and the centrifugal force balance.
It refines the [[geostrophic wind|CZ1S1P465Y]] by including the curvature of the flow.
Around a low the gradient wind is weaker than the geostrophic wind, and around a high it is stronger.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Winds above the friction of the ground roughly follow the lines of equal pressure.
When those lines are curved, as around a storm, the air must also be pulled towards the centre of the curve.
The gradient wind is the wind speed for which the push of the pressure difference, the sideways pull of the Earth's rotation and this curving all match.
Around a low pressure centre, part of the pressure push is used to curve the air, so the wind is a bit slower than the straight-line estimate.
Around a high the opposite happens, and there is a limit to how strong the pressure gradient of a high can be.
