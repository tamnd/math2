## Summary

The Boussinesq eddy viscosity hypothesis models the [[Reynolds stress|FDQE4PF63G]] of a turbulent flow as proportional to the mean rate of strain.
It has the same form as the viscous stress of a Newtonian fluid.
The coefficient, the eddy viscosity, is a property of the flow, not of the fluid, and is usually much larger than the molecular [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]].
Joseph Boussinesq proposed it in 1877.
It is the basis of most engineering turbulence models, which differ in how they compute the eddy viscosity.
It is one approach to the [[closure problem|A2X0FXC6HK]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Turbulent eddies stir a fluid and so smooth out differences in its mean velocity, much as molecular friction does but far more strongly.
The hypothesis says: treat the eddies as an extra, much larger viscosity.
Then the averaged equations look like ordinary viscous flow equations with a bigger viscosity.
This is a crude picture, because eddies are large and can carry momentum the wrong way, but it works surprisingly often.
