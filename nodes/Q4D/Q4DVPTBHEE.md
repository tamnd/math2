## Summary

The complex potential of a plane, incompressible and irrotational flow is a [[holomorphic function|0NAHXR1FRH]] $w = \varphi + i \psi$.
Its real part is the [[velocity potential|470A1RJWBP]] and its imaginary part is the [[stream function|E66111XRRA]].
Its derivative gives the velocity directly, $w'(z) = u - i v$.
It lets one use complex analysis, in particular conformal maps, to solve problems of two-dimensional [[potential flow|84K7TEWZ2Z]], such as the flow around an airfoil.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a flat, swirl-free flow of an incompressible fluid, two families of curves fit together at right angles: the streamlines, along which fluid moves, and the equipotential lines.
Packing both into one complex function makes the whole flow a single holomorphic function.
For example, $w = U z$ is a uniform stream of speed $U$ to the right, and $w = z^2/2$ is the flow into a corner.
Adding complex potentials adds flows, so complicated flows can be built from simple pieces.
