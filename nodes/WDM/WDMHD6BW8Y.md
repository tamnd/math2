## Summary

The free-air anomaly is the [[gravity anomaly|J6V1B7SP0Q]] obtained by reducing observed gravity to sea level with the normal change of gravity with height only.
It treats the station as if it stood in free air.
The masses between the station and sea level are ignored.
It is the anomaly used in [[Stokes' formula|MNK0GW8ZM6]] for the geoid, and over the oceans it closely follows the sea-floor topography.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gravity gets weaker as you go up, by about $0.3$ mGal for each metre of height.
A measurement on a hilltop is therefore smaller than it would be at sea level, even if nothing is unusual below.
The free-air correction adds back this loss, as if the instrument hung in the air above sea level.
After the correction, one subtracts the normal gravity of the reference ellipsoid.
For a station at $1000$ m, the correction is about $+309$ mGal.
