## Summary

In number theory, a **practical number** or **panarithmic number** is a positive integer $n$ such that all smaller positive integers can be represented as sums of distinct [[divisors|0PG2H7WKGG]] of $n$. For example, 12 is a practical number because all the numbers from 1 to 11 can be expressed as sums of its divisors 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6: as well as these divisors themselves, we have 5 = 3 + 2, 7 = 6 + 1, 8 = 6 + 2, 9 = 6 + 3, 10 = 6 + 3 + 1, and 11 = 6 + 3 + 2.
