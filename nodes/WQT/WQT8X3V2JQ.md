## Summary

The instant centre of rotation of a rigid body moving in a plane is the point whose velocity is zero at a given instant.
At that instant every point of the body moves as if the body were turning about this point.
The velocity of each point of the body is perpendicular to the line joining it to the centre and proportional to its distance from it.
It exists whenever the [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]] is not zero, and it moves as time goes on.
It is a basic tool for finding velocities in planar mechanisms, together with [[Kennedy's theorem|FZMP3ND4BN]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a wheel rolling on the ground without slipping.
At each moment, the point of the wheel touching the ground is at rest, and the whole wheel is pivoting about it.
That contact point is the instant centre of rotation.
The top of the wheel is twice as far from it as the hub, so it moves twice as fast as the hub.
A body that slides without turning has no instant centre; one says the centre is at infinity.
