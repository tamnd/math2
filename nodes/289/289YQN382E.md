## Summary

An Arthur–Merlin protocol is an [[interactive proof system|7PFQZJF1DX]] in which the verifier, Arthur, shows all his random bits to the prover, Merlin.
The classes MA and AM are defined by protocols with one message from Merlin, sent before or after Arthur's random string.
László Babai introduced them in 1985.
Graph non-isomorphism is in AM, and $\mathrm{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{MA} \subseteq \mathrm{AM} \subseteq \Pi_2^p$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Merlin is a wizard who knows everything but may lie, and Arthur is a king who can only compute quickly and toss coins.
In an Arthur–Merlin game, Arthur's coin tosses are public: he simply announces them, and Merlin replies.
Surprisingly, keeping coins secret gives no extra power for protocols with a bounded number of rounds, by a result of Goldwasser and Sipser.
So these simple games capture interactive proofs with any constant number of rounds.
