## Summary

Rindler coordinates are coordinates on a wedge of [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]] adapted to a family of uniformly accelerated observers.
They replace the Cartesian time and space coordinates $(ct, x)$ by hyperbolic polar coordinates $(\rho, \eta)$ with $ct = \rho \sinh\eta$ and $x = \rho \cosh\eta$.
They are the simplest example of a non-inertial frame in special relativity and a model for the region near the horizon of a black hole.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary polar coordinates describe the plane with circles and angles.
Rindler coordinates do the same in the spacetime plane, but with hyperbolas and hyperbolic angles.
Each hyperbola $x^2 - c^2 t^2 = \rho^2$ is the path of an observer with constant acceleration $c^2/\rho$, and the hyperbolic angle plays the role of their time.
The light rays $x = \pm ct$ form a horizon: accelerated observers never receive signals from beyond $x = ct$.
