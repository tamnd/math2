## Summary

K-theory with coefficients $\Z/\ell$ is obtained by dividing the [[K-theory spectrum|2C1M0NWPB0]] by the integer $\ell$, in the homotopical sense of a cofibre.
Its groups $K_n(R; \Z/\ell)$ sit in short exact sequences with $K_n(R)/\ell$ and the $\ell$-torsion of $K_{n-1}(R)$.
Mod $\ell$ K-groups are often much easier to compute than integral ones.
The rigidity theorems of Suslin and Gabber, and the comparison with étale cohomology, are statements about them.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Working modulo $\ell$ forgets all information about divisibility by numbers prime to $\ell$, and keeps what concerns $\ell$.
For abelian groups this is like replacing a group $A$ by $A/\ell A$, but done in a way that does not lose exactness.
That is why the $\ell$-torsion of the group one degree lower also appears.
For example, the mod $\ell$ K-theory of the complex numbers is very simple, although their integral K-groups are huge.
