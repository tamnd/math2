## Summary

The Madelung equations rewrite the [[Schrödinger equation|EPZR6H2WJN]] as the equations of a compressible fluid.
The squared modulus of the wave function becomes a density, and the gradient of its phase becomes a velocity.
The result is a continuity equation and an Euler-type momentum equation with an extra force from the quantum potential.
They are the basis of quantum hydrodynamics and of the fluid description of [[superfluids|4PZBRJ78X4]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A quantum particle is described by a complex wave, which has a size and a phase at every point.
Madelung noticed that one can read the size squared as the density of a fluid and the slope of the phase as its velocity.
Then the Schrödinger equation says that this fluid conserves its mass and moves like an ordinary fluid, except for one extra quantum force.
This picture helps to see quantum effects, such as vortices in superfluids, in the language of flows.
