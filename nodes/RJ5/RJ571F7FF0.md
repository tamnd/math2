## Example

Three pigeons do not fit into two holes with at most one pigeon in each.
Write $x_{ij}$ for "pigeon $i$ sits in hole $j$", with $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ and $j \in \{1, 2\}$.
The claim that they do fit is the CNF $\mathrm{PHP}^3_2$ with these $9$ clauses.

- *Pigeon clauses*, one for each pigeon: $\{x_{11}, x_{12}\}$, $\{x_{21}, x_{22}\}$, $\{x_{31}, x_{32}\}$.
- *Hole clauses*, one for each hole and each pair of pigeons: $\{\neg x_{11}, \neg x_{21}\}$, $\{\neg x_{11}, \neg x_{31}\}$, $\{\neg x_{21}, \neg x_{31}\}$ for hole $1$, and the same three with $x_{i2}$ for hole $2$.

That is $3$ pigeon clauses and $2 \cdot \binom{3}{2} = 6$ hole clauses.
No assignment satisfies all $9$, and the L2 explanation refutes them by resolution in $10$ steps.

In general, let $m > n \ge 1$.
The [[pigeonhole principle|0HGVCWQGY3]] says that there is no injective map from $m$ pigeons into $n$ holes.
Its negation is the [[CNF|0B3D4C8YF2]] $\mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ in the variables $x_{ij}$, for pigeons $i \in \{1, \dots, m\}$ and holes $j \in \{1, \dots, n\}$.
It has the pigeon clauses $P_i = \{x_{i1}, \dots, x_{in}\}$ and the hole clauses $H_{i i' j} = \{\neg x_{ij}, \neg x_{i'j}\}$ for $i < i'$.
So it has $mn$ variables and $m + n \binom{m}{2}$ clauses.
The tautology $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ is the propositional form of the pigeonhole principle for this $m$ and $n$.

*Variants.*

- $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ is the usual case, with one extra pigeon.
- The *weak* pigeonhole formulas have many more pigeons, such as $m = 2n$ or $m = n^2$.
- The *functional* version adds the clauses $\{\neg x_{ij}, \neg x_{ij'}\}$ for $j \ne j'$: each pigeon sits in at most one hole.
- The *onto* version adds the clauses $\{x_{1j}, \dots, x_{mj}\}$: every hole is used.
- The *graph* version keeps only the variables $x_{ij}$ for the edges of a bipartite graph between pigeons and holes.

## Remarks

### Weak systems: resolution and bounded depth

[[Resolution refutations|6JSH907W5R]] of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ need size $2^{\Omega(n)}$ in clauses, by [[Haken's theorem|4PCEYEW3JG]] of 1985.
For the weak versions with any $m > n$, Raz proved a resolution size bound $2^{n^{\varepsilon}}$ in 2004.
In [[bounded-depth Frege|41QJ1WEMJ3]], Ajtai proved in 1988 that proofs of $\neg\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ of each fixed depth need superpolynomial size; see [[Ajtai's theorem|Q0ERK64J78]].
Krajíček, Pudlák and Woods, and independently Pitassi, Beame and Impagliazzo, improved this to size $2^{n^{\varepsilon_d}}$ for depth $d$.
For the weak principle, Paris, Wilkie and Woods gave quasi-polynomial-size bounded-depth Frege proofs in 1988.

### Strong systems: Frege, extended Frege, cutting planes

[[Frege|J2BAR09TPF]] proofs of $\neg\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ of polynomial size exist, by [[Buss's theorem|VRX23FZV21]] of 1987.
Cook and Reckhow gave polynomial-size proofs in [[extended Frege|5YSE04SHFH]] in 1979, and Cook gave polynomial-size extended resolution refutations in 1976.
$\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has polynomial-size [[cutting-planes refutations|40KJ0VKNG7]], because cutting planes can add up the inequalities $\sum_j x_{ij} \ge 1$ and $\sum_i x_{ij} \le 1$.

### Algebraic systems

Razborov proved in 1998 that [[polynomial calculus|ZERBQSTZB5]] refutations of $\mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ need degree greater than $n/2$, over every field and for every $m > n$.
So the measure that fails there is degree, and the failure does not depend on the number of pigeons.

### Watch out

The formulas are easy or hard depending on the system, never in an absolute sense.
"The pigeonhole principle has no short proofs" is false: it has polynomial-size Frege proofs.
The pigeon clauses have width $n$, so the [[size-width relation|GWDGRPAQSM]] does not apply directly.
The graph version on a bounded-degree expander has bounded width, and the width method is applied to it instead.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If $5$ letters go into $4$ mailboxes, some mailbox gets two letters.
Written as a list of clauses, the opposite claim says: every letter is in some box, and no box has two letters.
The list $\mathrm{PHP}^5_4$ has $20$ variables, $5$ clauses of the first kind and $4 \cdot \binom{5}{2} = 40$ clauses of the second kind.

### L2 human

We refute $\mathrm{PHP}^3_2$ by resolution.
First we show that pigeon $1$ is not in hole $1$.

1. From $\{\neg x_{11}, \neg x_{21}\}$ and $\{x_{21}, x_{22}\}$, resolve on $x_{21}$: $\{\neg x_{11}, x_{22}\}$.
2. From $\{\neg x_{11}, \neg x_{31}\}$ and $\{x_{31}, x_{32}\}$, resolve on $x_{31}$: $\{\neg x_{11}, x_{32}\}$.
3. From step 1 and $\{\neg x_{22}, \neg x_{32}\}$, resolve on $x_{22}$: $\{\neg x_{11}, \neg x_{32}\}$.
4. From steps 3 and 2, resolve on $x_{32}$: $\{\neg x_{11}\}$.
5. From $\{\neg x_{12}, \neg x_{22}\}$ and $\{x_{21}, x_{22}\}$, resolve on $x_{22}$: $\{\neg x_{12}, x_{21}\}$.
6. From $\{\neg x_{12}, \neg x_{32}\}$ and $\{x_{31}, x_{32}\}$, resolve on $x_{32}$: $\{\neg x_{12}, x_{31}\}$.
7. From step 5 and $\{\neg x_{21}, \neg x_{31}\}$, resolve on $x_{21}$: $\{\neg x_{12}, \neg x_{31}\}$.
8. From steps 7 and 6, resolve on $x_{31}$: $\{\neg x_{12}\}$.
9. From $\{x_{11}, x_{12}\}$ and $\{\neg x_{11}\}$, resolve on $x_{11}$: $\{x_{12}\}$.
10. From $\{x_{12}\}$ and $\{\neg x_{12}\}$, resolve on $x_{12}$: $\square$.

The refutation has the $9$ axioms and $10$ resolvents, so $19$ clauses, and width $2$.
It uses the clauses of pigeons $2$ and $3$ twice, so a tree-like copy needs them written twice, which gives $21$ clauses.

### L3 human

The pigeonhole principle is a basic counting statement, and the formulas measure how much counting a proof system can do.
Systems whose lines can express sums of many bits, such as Frege systems and cutting planes, prove it in polynomial size.
Systems whose lines are clauses or formulas of constant depth cannot, and their lower bound proofs use the same restriction methods as lower bounds for the corresponding circuits.
In [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]], the weak pigeonhole principle is a central axiom, and its strength depends on the number of pigeons.
