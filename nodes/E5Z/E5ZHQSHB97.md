## Summary

A truss is a structure made of straight bars joined at their ends by pins, loaded only at the joints.
Under these idealizations every bar is either pulled or pushed along its own axis and carries no bending.
The equilibrium of a truss is a finite linear system, which makes it the simplest structure to analyse.
It links structural mechanics with the [[rigidity of frameworks|V5YS9BZX0N]].
Bridges, roofs, cranes and towers are often built as trusses.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a railway bridge made of steel bars arranged in triangles.
A triangle of bars cannot change its shape without changing the length of a bar, so a net of triangles is stiff even if the joints are free hinges.
Each bar then only has to resist being stretched or squashed, which steel does very well.
That is why trusses can span long distances with little material.
