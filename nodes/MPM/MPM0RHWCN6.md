## Summary

Hamiltonian mechanics is the formulation of classical mechanics in terms of positions $q$, momenta $p$ and one function $H(q, p, t)$, the Hamiltonian, usually the total energy.
The motion is a curve in [[phase space|CPEC2NW2KF]] that obeys [[Hamilton's equations|CFYQ6R38YP]], a first-order system with a special symmetric structure.
It is equivalent to [[Lagrangian mechanics|47JMCBM3VJ]] for regular Lagrangians, via the Legendre transform.
Its geometry, the [[symplectic structure|CAN490EPRT]] and the [[Poisson bracket|2S0MXDT4YH]], underlies perturbation theory, integrable systems, statistical mechanics and quantum mechanics.
See the [[definition|GP0SM9D9X0]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Instead of position and velocity, Hamiltonian mechanics uses position and momentum as the basic variables.
The whole system is described by one function, the energy written in these variables.
The position changes at a rate equal to the slope of the energy in the momentum direction.
The momentum changes at a rate equal to minus the slope in the position direction.
For a mass $m$ on a spring of stiffness $k$, $H = p^2/(2m) + k q^2/2$, and the equations give $\dot q = p/m$ and $\dot p = -k q$.
The state moves along an ellipse in the $(q, p)$ plane, the level curve of the energy.
