## Definition

Let $V, V' \subseteq \R^{2n}$ be open, with coordinates $(q, p) = (q_1, \ldots, q_n, p_1, \ldots, p_n)$ on $V$ and $(Q, P)$ on $V'$.
A *canonical transformation* is a [[diffeomorphism|0KZZ70ZHXP]] $\varphi \colon V \to V'$, $(q, p) \mapsto (Q, P)$, such that
$$\sum_{i=1}^n dP_i \wedge dQ_i = \sum_{i=1}^n dp_i \wedge dq_i \quad \text{on } V.$$
Equivalently, the [[Jacobian matrix|0YF0VXKXGB]] $D\varphi$ satisfies $D\varphi^{T} \, \Omega \, D\varphi = \Omega$ at each point, where $\Omega = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & I_n \\ -I_n & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

## Remarks

The condition says that $(Q, P)$ are [[canonical coordinates|XKDVF07R1Q]] whenever $(q, p)$ are.
It is also equivalent to $\{Q_i, Q_j\} = 0$, $\{P_i, P_j\} = 0$ and $\{Q_i, P_j\} = \delta_{ij}$ for the [[Poisson bracket|2S0MXDT4YH]] in the variables $(q, p)$.
Let $\varphi$ be canonical and let $H$ be a Hamiltonian on $V$.
Then $\varphi$ maps the solutions of the [[Hamiltonian system|0EDKGA2FAK]] of $H$ to the solutions of the Hamiltonian system of $K = H \circ \varphi^{-1}$.
Many physics texts allow transformations that depend on time, or that multiply the symplectic form by a constant; the definition above is the restricted, time-independent case.

Example: for $n = 1$, $\varphi(q, p) = (p, -q)$.
Here $dP \wedge dQ = (-dq) \wedge dp = dp \wedge dq$, so $\varphi$ is canonical.

Non-example: $\varphi(q, p) = (2q, p)$ gives $dP \wedge dQ = 2 \, dp \wedge dq$, so it is not canonical.
For $H = \frac{1}{2} p^2$, the new function $K(Q, P) = \frac{1}{2} P^2$ has the equation $\dot Q = P$, while the true motion has $\dot Q = 2 \dot q = 2P$.
