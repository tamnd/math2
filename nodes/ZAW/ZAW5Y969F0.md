## Problem

Consider one consumer and goods $0, 1, \dots, n$.
Good $0$ is labour, which cannot be taxed, and the consumer owns only labour.
Producer prices $p = (p_1, \dots, p_n)$ are fixed.
A government chooses commodity taxes $t = (t_1, \dots, t_n)$, so that consumer prices are $q = p + t$.
Let $v(q)$ be the consumer's [[indirect utility|N9S29DKCY8]] and $x_i(q)$ the [[Marshallian demand|EQPEPJ7QQZ]] for good $i$.
Given a revenue requirement $R > 0$, find taxes $t$ that maximise $v(p + t)$ subject to $\sum_{i=1}^n t_i x_i(p + t) = R$.

## Remarks

Lump-sum taxes are excluded, so every tax distorts some choice, and the problem is to raise $R$ with the least loss of welfare.
With [[Lagrange multipliers|XADAEPN54D]], an interior solution satisfies the *Ramsey rule*:
$$\sum_{i=1}^n t_i \frac{\partial h_k}{\partial q_i} = -\theta \, h_k \quad \text{for all } k = 1, \dots, n,$$
for a constant $\theta > 0$, where $h_k$ is the [[Hicksian demand|ZFX4T69SST]] at the optimum.
To first order, taxes reduce the compensated demand of every good by the same proportion.
If cross-price effects vanish, the rule becomes the inverse elasticity rule $t_k/q_k = \theta/\varepsilon_k$, where $\varepsilon_k$ is the absolute value of the compensated own-price elasticity of good $k$.
So goods with less elastic demand are taxed at higher rates.
Frank Ramsey solved this problem in 1927, at the suggestion of Arthur Pigou.
The same mathematics gives Ramsey pricing for a regulated firm that must cover its costs.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A government needs a fixed amount of money and can only tax the things people buy.
Every tax makes people change what they buy, and this change is a loss.
Ramsey's answer is to tax most heavily the goods whose purchases change least when their price goes up.
