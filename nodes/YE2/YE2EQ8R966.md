## Statement

Let $A$ be a commutative [[Henselian local ring|RNXPV3KC5J]] with [[residue field|NJ3APQ8BHH]] $k$, and let $\ell \ge 2$ be an integer that is invertible in $A$.
Then for every $n \ge 0$ the quotient map $A \to k$ induces an isomorphism
$$K_n(A; \Z/\ell) \to K_n(k; \Z/\ell)$$
of [[K-groups with coefficients in $\Z/\ell$|ANJ5719F7F]].

## Remarks

Gabber proved the theorem in the more general setting of Henselian pairs $(A, I)$, comparing $A$ with $A/I$.
The proof develops the specialization argument of Suslin's rigidity theorem, with the Henselian property used to lift points from the residue field to the ring.

At work: the ring of [[$p$-adic integers|V6TJAFZ4MD]] $\Z_p$ is Henselian with residue field $\F_p$.
For $\ell$ prime to $p$, the theorem gives $K_n(\Z_p; \Z/\ell) \cong K_n(\F_p; \Z/\ell)$, known from Quillen's [[computation for finite fields|CJSGE7JSGH]].

The hypothesis that $\ell$ is invertible is necessary.
Let $p$ be odd and $\ell = p$.
Then $K_1(\Z_p; \Z/p) = \Z_p^\times/(\Z_p^\times)^p \cong \Z/p$, since $\Z_p^\times \cong \mu_{p-1} \times (1 + p\Z_p)$ and $1 + p\Z_p \cong \Z_p$.
But $K_1(\F_p; \Z/p) = \F_p^\times/(\F_p^\times)^p = 0$, because $\F_p^\times$ has order $p - 1$, prime to $p$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Henselian local ring, such as the $p$-adic integers, is very close to its residue field: simple roots of polynomial equations modulo the maximal ideal lift to the ring.
The theorem says that modulo $\ell$, with $\ell$ invertible, the K-theory of the ring is exactly the K-theory of the residue field.
So mod $\ell$ K-theory does not see the infinitesimal thickening.

### L2 human

The theorem computes the stalks of mod $\ell$ K-theory in the étale topology.
The local rings there are strictly Henselian, so their mod $\ell$ K-groups are those of separably closed fields.
This is the starting point of the comparison between algebraic K-theory and étale cohomology, in étale K-theory and the Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture.
It extends Suslin's rigidity theorem for extensions of algebraically closed fields.
