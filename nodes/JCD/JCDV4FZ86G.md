## Summary

Harish-Chandra's Schwartz space $\mathcal{C}(G)$ is a space of smooth, rapidly decreasing functions on a semisimple Lie group $G$.
It plays the role that the [[Schwartz space|CG9JEATW3Q]] plays on $\R^n$, but the decay is measured against Harish-Chandra's function $\Xi$ rather than against polynomials.
It contains the smooth compactly supported functions and lies in $L^2(G)$.
Harish-Chandra used it to define tempered distributions on $G$ and to prove the [[Harish-Chandra Plancherel theorem|N137YA7RGS]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a semisimple group the volume of a ball grows exponentially with its radius.
So functions that decay only like a negative power of the distance are not even square integrable.
Harish-Chandra's Schwartz space asks for decay like the special function $\Xi$, which already decays exponentially, times any negative power of the distance.
Smooth functions with compact support satisfy this, and so do the matrix coefficients of the discrete series.
