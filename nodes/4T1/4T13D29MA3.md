## Definition

Consider an [[exchange economy|HGMHZSM0XQ]] with two goods and two consumers, with endowments $\omega_1, \omega_2 \in \R^2_+$ and total endowment $\bar\omega = \omega_1 + \omega_2$ with $\bar\omega_1, \bar\omega_2 > 0$.
The *Edgeworth box* of the economy is the rectangle $E = [0, \bar\omega_1] \times [0, \bar\omega_2]$, where a point $a \in E$ stands for the allocation
$$x_1 = a, \qquad x_2 = \bar\omega - a.$$
Consumer $1$'s bundle is measured from the corner $(0, 0)$ and consumer $2$'s bundle from the corner $\bar\omega$.
The endowment point is $\omega_1 \in E$.
The *contract curve* is the set of points of $E$ whose allocations are [[Pareto efficient|JREVYHFY4T]].

## Remarks

Example: with $\omega_1 = (1, 0)$ and $\omega_2 = (0, 1)$, the box is the unit square and the endowment point is its lower right corner $(1, 0)$.
Let both consumers have utility $\sqrt{x y}$.
The point $(1/2, 1/2)$ stands for the allocation giving each consumer $(1/2, 1/2)$, which is the competitive equilibrium allocation.
The contract curve is the diagonal $\set{(t, t) : 0 \le t \le 1}$.

Non-example: the point $(1/2, 3/2)$ does not lie in this box, since it would give consumer $2$ the bundle $(1/2, -1/2)$ with a negative amount of good $2$.

A budget line through the endowment point with slope $-p_1 / p_2$ is the budget line of both consumers at the same time.
At a competitive equilibrium both consumers' best points on this line coincide.
The box only describes two goods and two consumers; for more of either the idea survives as the set of feasible allocations.
