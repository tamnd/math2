## Summary

A Cartan subalgebra of a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] $\mathfrak g$ is a [[nilpotent|MEJ9PGMJ9H]] subalgebra that is equal to its own normalizer.
For a complex semisimple Lie algebra, the Cartan subalgebras are the maximal abelian subalgebras made of semisimple elements, and all of them are conjugate.
A Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ acts diagonally on $\mathfrak g$, and the eigenvalues of this action, the roots, form a root system.
This is the starting point of the classification of semisimple Lie algebras and of highest weight theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the Lie algebra of $n \times n$ trace zero matrices, the diagonal matrices form a Cartan subalgebra.
They commute with each other, and no matrix outside them brackets every diagonal matrix back into the diagonal ones.
Every matrix of the algebra can be sorted by how the diagonal matrices act on it: the matrix units $E_{ij}$ with $i \ne j$ are eigenvectors.
This sorting, the root decomposition, is the main tool for understanding the whole algebra.
