## Proof

We split $V$ with polynomials in $T$ that add up to the identity, then we compute the dimensions from the characteristic polynomial, and last we build $D$ and $N$ from the projections.

*Polynomial facts.*

1. Write $p = p_T$, $q_i = (t - \lambda_i)^{m_i}$ and $f_i = \prod_{j \ne i} q_j$, so that $p = q_i f_i$ for each $i$.
   Let $W_i = \ker q_i(T)$, a [[kernel|02YFME3RGW]] in $V$.
2. The ring $F[t]$ is a [[Euclidean domain|0DQG39K2NH]], hence a [[principal ideal domain|0QKVE1GD9A]], hence a [[unique factorization domain|055HT85T3S]].
   Each $t - c$ has degree $1$, so it is an [[irreducible polynomial|00X2A9G9W3]], and two distinct monic polynomials $t - c$ are not associates.
3. By step 2 and the uniqueness of factorizations, each monic divisor of $\prod_j (t - \lambda_j)^{m_j}$ has the form $\prod_j (t - \lambda_j)^{e_j}$ with $0 \le e_j \le m_j$.
   In the same way, each monic divisor of $(t - c)^r$ has the form $(t - c)^e$ with $0 \le e \le r$.
4. The [[ideal generated|030TZ0DQWT]] by $f_1, \dots, f_k$ is principal and nonzero, so it is generated by a monic polynomial $d$.
   Then $d$ divides each $f_i$ and also $p$.
5. By step 3, $d = \prod_j (t - \lambda_j)^{e_j}$.
   The factor $t - \lambda_i$ does not occur in $f_i$, and $d$ divides $f_i$, so uniqueness gives $e_i = 0$.
   This holds for each $i$, so $d = 1$.

*The direct sum.*

6. By step 5 there are $a_1, \dots, a_k \in F[t]$ with $\sum_i a_i f_i = 1$.
   Let $\pi_i = a_i(T) f_i(T)$.
   Evaluation at $T$ respects sums and products, by the definition of the [[value at $T$|0BNEXSKNRV]], so $\sum_i \pi_i = \id_V$.
7. Polynomials in $T$ commute with each other, so each polynomial in $T$ maps $W_i$ into itself.
   In particular, $W_i$ is [[$T$-invariant|0EP59ESQ94]].
8. Let $v \in V$.
   Then $q_i(T) \pi_i(v) = a_i(T) p(T)(v) = 0$, by the [[Cayley-Hamilton theorem|0QEM68Y0DE]].
   So $\pi_i(v) \in W_i$, and $v = \sum_i \pi_i(v)$ lies in $W_1 + \cdots + W_k$.
9. Let $w \in W_j$ and $i \ne j$.
   Then $q_j$ divides $f_i$, so $\pi_i(w) = 0$, and step 6 gives $w = \pi_j(w)$.
10. Let $w_j \in W_j$ with $\sum_j w_j = 0$.
    Applying $\pi_l$ and using step 9 gives $w_l = \pi_l(0) = 0$ for each $l$.
    With step 8, $V = W_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus W_k$ as a [[direct sum|041JTZCK9Q]].

*The pieces are the generalized eigenspaces.*

11. The definition of the [[generalized eigenspace|07PNDWA7CE]], with the exponent $m_i$, gives $W_i \subseteq V_{\lambda_i}$.
12. Conversely, let $v \in V_{\lambda_i}$, with $(T - \lambda_i \id_V)^r (v) = 0$.
    Let $j \ne i$ and $u = \pi_j(v)$.
    By step 7, $(T - \lambda_i \id_V)^r (u) = \pi_j\big((T - \lambda_i \id_V)^r (v)\big) = 0$, and $q_j(T)(u) = 0$ by step 8.
13. The ideal generated by $(t - \lambda_i)^r$ and $q_j$ has a monic generator $d'$.
    By step 3, $d'$ divides $q_j$, so $d' = (t - \lambda_j)^e$.
    It also divides $(t - \lambda_i)^r$, so uniqueness gives $e = 0$ and $d' = 1$.
14. So $1 = b \, (t - \lambda_i)^r + c \, q_j$ for some $b, c \in F[t]$.
    Applying this identity at $T$ to $u$ gives $u = 0$, by step 12.
15. Hence $\pi_j(v) = 0$ for every $j \ne i$, and $v = \pi_i(v) \in W_i$ by steps 6 and 8.
    So $W_i = V_{\lambda_i}$, and $V = V_{\lambda_1} \oplus \cdots \oplus V_{\lambda_k}$, which is part 1.

*The dimensions.*

16. Let $d_i = \dim W_i$, and choose a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of each $W_i$.
    By step 10, their concatenation $B$ is a basis of $V$.
17. Let $T_i = T|_{W_i}$ be the [[restriction|0PFPWKBDTJ]], and let $A_i$ be its [[matrix|0YYMY95FWS]] in the chosen basis of $W_i$.
    By step 7, the matrix of $T$ in $B$ is the [[block diagonal matrix|0M0SNKZ482]] $\operatorname{diag}(A_1, \dots, A_k)$.
18. The matrix $t I - \operatorname{diag}(A_1, \dots, A_k)$ is block diagonal with blocks $t I - A_i$.
    Using the [[determinant of a block triangular matrix|0J9TVTNB4K]] $k - 1$ times gives $p_T = \prod_i p_{A_i}$, by the [[definition of $p_T$|0BZ81AJNWF]].
19. Let $w \in W_i$ be nonzero with $T(w) = \mu w$.
    Then $0 = q_i(T)(w) = (\mu - \lambda_i)^{m_i} w$, so $\mu = \lambda_i$, and $\lambda_i$ is the only possible eigenvalue of $T_i$.
20. The polynomial $p_{A_i}$ is monic of degree $d_i$, and it divides $p_T$ by step 18.
    By step 3, $p_{A_i} = \prod_j (t - \lambda_j)^{e_{ij}}$.
21. Suppose that $e_{ij} > 0$ for some $j \ne i$.
    Then $\lambda_j$ is a root of $p_{A_i}$, so it is an eigenvalue of $T_i$ by [[the roots of the characteristic polynomial|053QVHF6N7]], and this contradicts step 19.
22. So $p_{A_i} = (t - \lambda_i)^{d_i}$, by comparing degrees.
    Step 18 then gives $\prod_i (t - \lambda_i)^{d_i} = p_T = \prod_i (t - \lambda_i)^{m_i}$.
23. The $t - \lambda_i$ are pairwise non-associate irreducible elements, by step 2.
    Uniqueness of factorization in the [[unique factorization domain|06YMXJMXYS]] $F[t]$ gives $d_i = m_i$ for each $i$.
24. On $W_i$ we have $(T - \lambda_i \id)^{m_i} = q_i(T) = 0$, by the definition of $W_i$.
    So $T - \lambda_i \id$ is nilpotent on $V_{\lambda_i}$, and with steps 7, 15 and 23 this proves part 2.

*The projections and the two parts.*

25. By steps 9 and 6, $\pi_i$ is the identity on $W_i$ and is $0$ on $W_j$ for $j \ne i$.
    So $\pi_i$ is the projection of part 3, and $\pi_i = (a_i f_i)(T)$ is a polynomial in $T$, which proves part 3.
26. The map $D = \sum_i \lambda_i \pi_i$ is a polynomial in $T$, and so is $N = T - D$.
    Polynomials in $T$ commute, so $D N = N D$.
27. By step 25, $D$ acts on $W_i$ as $\lambda_i \id$.
    So the basis $B$ of step 16 consists of eigenvectors of $D$, and $D$ is diagonalizable.
28. On $W_i$ we have $N = T - \lambda_i \id$.
    Let $m = \max_i m_i$, so that $N^m$ is $0$ on each $W_i$ by step 24.
29. Every vector of $V$ is a sum of vectors of the $W_i$, by step 8.
    So $N^m = 0$, and $N$ is nilpotent, which proves part 4.

## Idea

The polynomials $f_i$ have no common factor, so some combination of them equals $1$.
Plugging in $T$ turns that combination into maps $\pi_i$ that add up to the identity.
Cayley-Hamilton shows that each $\pi_i$ lands in $\ker (T - \lambda_i)^{m_i}$, and coprimality shows that these kernels meet only in $0$.
For the dimensions, $T$ is block diagonal with one block for each eigenvalue, and unique factorization matches the exponents of $p_T$ with the sizes of the blocks.

## Variants

Steps 1 to 10 are the [[kernels lemma|ACBNK1VFVG]] for the coprime factors $(t - \lambda_i)^{m_i}$ of $p_T$, applied with $p_T(T) = 0$.
Steps 16 to 23 can be replaced by the [[dimension of the generalized eigenspace|PHZ684KGWS]], which gives $\dim V_{\lambda_i} = m_i$ directly.
Axler, Chapter 8, avoids the characteristic polynomial.
He shows that the generalized eigenspaces span $V$ by induction on $\dim V$, using the splitting $V = \ker (T - \lambda)^n \oplus \operatorname{im} (T - \lambda)^n$.
