## Definition

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite [[connected graph|0KWFFMDF8T]], let $w \colon E \to \R$ be a weight function, and let $r \in V$.
*Prim's algorithm* with start vertex $r$ is the following [[algorithm|9DMSG5HAQX]].

1. Set $S = \set{r}$ and $F = \emptyset$.
2. While $S \ne V$, choose an edge $uv \in E$ with $u \in S$ and $v \notin S$ of least weight among such edges.
3. Add $v$ to $S$ and $uv$ to $F$, then return to step 2.

At every stage $(S, F)$ is a [[tree|062D9KXJB1]].
At the end, $F$ is the edge set of a [[minimum spanning tree|0JW7KT6PQW]] of $G$.

## Remarks

Example: let $G$ be the triangle on $\set{a, b, c}$ with $w(ab) = 1$, $w(bc) = 2$, $w(ac) = 3$, and let $r = a$.
The first step picks $ab$ (weight $1$ beats $3$), and the second picks $bc$ (weight $2$ beats $3$).
The result $\set{ab, bc}$ is a [[spanning tree|0S4ZEZM7KE]] of least weight $3$.

Non-example: if $G$ is not connected, step 2 eventually finds no edge leaving $S$ while $S \ne V$.
The algorithm then only spans the component of $r$.

Correctness follows from the cut property applied to the partition $(S, V \setminus S)$.
With a binary heap the running time is $O(m \log n)$, and with a Fibonacci heap it is $O(m + n \log n)$, where $n = \abs{V}$ and $m = \abs{E}$.
