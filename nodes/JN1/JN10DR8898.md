## Definition

Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]], let $F^\times$ be its [[group of units|07C6ECNJET]], and let $A$ be an [[abelian group|082GHNPJ53]] written multiplicatively.
A *Steinberg symbol* on $F$ with values in $A$ is a map $c \colon F^\times \times F^\times \to A$ such that:

1. $c(aa', b) = c(a, b) \, c(a', b)$ for all $a, a', b \in F^\times$.
2. $c(a, bb') = c(a, b) \, c(a, b')$ for all $a, b, b' \in F^\times$.
3. $c(a, 1 - a) = 1$ for all $a \in F$ with $a \ne 0$ and $a \ne 1$.

## Remarks

Every Steinberg symbol also satisfies $c(a, -a) = 1$ and $c(a, b) \, c(b, a) = 1$ for all $a, b \in F^\times$.

Example: on $F = \R$, let $c(a, b) = -1$ if $a < 0$ and $b < 0$, and $c(a, b) = 1$ otherwise; this is the real [[Hilbert symbol|WFG4YMBYVQ]].
It is multiplicative in each variable, since for fixed $b < 0$ it is the sign of $a$, and for fixed $b > 0$ it is constant.
For $a \ne 0, 1$ the numbers $a$ and $1 - a$ are never both negative, so clause 3 holds.

Non-example: on $F = \Q$, let $v_2$ be the $2$-adic valuation and $c(a, b) = (-1)^{v_2(a) v_2(b)}$.
Clauses 1 and 2 hold because $v_2$ is additive.
Clause 3 fails: for $a = 1/2$ also $1 - a = 1/2$, and $c(1/2, 1/2) = (-1)^{(-1)(-1)} = -1$.

The symbols $\set{a, b}$ in $K_2(F)$ form the universal Steinberg symbol, by Matsumoto's theorem.
