## Summary

The polarization of light describes how its electric field oscillates in the plane transverse to the direction of travel.
For monochromatic light the tip of the field vector traces an ellipse, the polarization ellipse.
This ellipse may degenerate into a line segment, or it may be a circle.
Accordingly light is linearly, circularly or elliptically polarized; light whose polarization varies randomly is unpolarized or partially polarized.
Polarization is described by Jones vectors for fully polarized light and by [[Stokes parameters|1WA9ASC6NF]] in general.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light is a wave in which the electric field swings back and forth across the direction of travel.
If it always swings along one line, say up and down, the light is linearly polarized.
If the swinging direction turns around steadily like a clock hand, the light is circularly polarized.
Polarized sunglasses block light swinging in one direction, which removes much of the glare reflected from water and roads.
Sunlight itself is unpolarized: its swinging direction changes randomly and very quickly.
