## Definition

Let $\eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ be the [[Minkowski metric|G49G4R1K04]] on $\R^4$ in coordinates $x^\mu = (ct, x, y, z)$.
Consider metrics $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$, where $h_{\mu\nu}$ is a symmetric tensor field with $\abs{h_{\mu\nu}} \ll 1$; indices are raised and lowered with $\eta$.
Let $T_{\mu\nu}$ be the [[stress–energy tensor|REDZ92MKXX]], taken to be of first order.
*Linearized gravity* is the theory obtained by keeping only the terms of first order in $h$ in the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] $G_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}$.
In terms of the trace-reversed perturbation $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \frac12 \eta_{\mu\nu} h$, with $h = \eta^{\mu\nu} h_{\mu\nu}$, the linearized equations are
$$-\square \bar h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu \partial^\rho \bar h_{\rho\nu} + \partial_\nu \partial^\rho \bar h_{\rho\mu} - \eta_{\mu\nu} \partial^\rho \partial^\sigma \bar h_{\rho\sigma} = \frac{16\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu},$$
where $\square = \eta^{\mu\nu} \partial_\mu \partial_\nu$.

## Remarks

The theory has the gauge freedom $h_{\mu\nu} \mapsto h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu \xi_\nu + \partial_\nu \xi_\mu$ for any small vector field $\xi$, which comes from infinitesimal changes of coordinates.
Using it ([[gauge fixing|TXD7MTYQYS]]), one can impose the harmonic (Lorenz, de Donder) gauge $\partial^\mu \bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
The equations then become the [[wave equations|5EHD2XBQDG]]
$$\square \bar h_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{16\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}.$$
The linearized equations imply $\partial^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, the conservation law of flat space.

Example: a static point mass $M$ has $T_{00} = M c^2 \delta^3(x)$.
In the harmonic gauge $\nabla^2 \bar h_{00} = -16\pi G M \delta^3(x)/c^2$ gives $\bar h_{00} = 4GM/(rc^2)$, the other components vanish, and so $h_{00} = 2GM/(rc^2)$ and $h_{ij} = 2GM \delta_{ij}/(rc^2)$.
This matches the Schwarzschild metric in isotropic coordinates to first order in $GM/(rc^2)$.

Non-example: near the horizon of a black hole of mass $M$, at $r$ close to $2GM/c^2$, the perturbation is not small, and linearized gravity does not apply.
