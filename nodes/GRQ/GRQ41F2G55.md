## Summary

The Hayashi track is the path in the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram followed by a fully convective star of given mass.
An example is a young star that contracts towards the main sequence.
Along it, the surface temperature stays nearly constant while the luminosity drops.
So the track is almost vertical, and it lies at low surface temperatures, around $3000$ to $5000 \, \mathrm{K}$.
It also marks a boundary: no star in [[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]] can be cooler at its surface than the Hayashi track of its mass.
The region beyond the track is the Hayashi forbidden zone.
It was described by Chūshirō Hayashi in 1961.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A star that has just formed is large, cool on the surface, and stirred all the way through by convection.
As it slowly shrinks, its surface stays at about the same temperature, so its brightness falls with its area.
On the usual chart of stars, brightness against temperature, it slides almost straight down.
Red giants, which are again almost fully convective, sit close to the same line on their way up.
