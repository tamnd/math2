## Example

We show $\nvdash (\neg \neg p \to p) \to p \vee \neg p$ in $\mathsf{IPC}$, with the [[slash|YRRKEEHNCD]].

1. Let $\Gamma = \set{\neg \neg p \to p}$. This formula is an implication with atomic consequent $p$, so it is Harrop.
2. So $\Gamma$ is self-slashing, by basic property 4 of the [[slash|YRRKEEHNCD]].
3. Suppose $\Gamma \vdash p \vee \neg p$. By Part B of [[the slash proof|284EX5GB4A]], $\Gamma \mid p \vee \neg p$.
4. So $\Gamma \mid p$ or $\Gamma \mid \neg p$, and by Part A, $\Gamma \vdash p$ or $\Gamma \vdash \neg p$.
5. The formula $\neg \neg p \to p$ is a classical tautology, so both classical valuations satisfy $\Gamma$.
6. The valuation with $p$ false shows $\Gamma \nvdash p$, and the valuation with $p$ true shows $\Gamma \nvdash \neg p$.
7. This contradicts step 4. So $\Gamma \nvdash p \vee \neg p$, and by the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] the implication is not a theorem.

## Remarks

The result concerns a single atom $p$.
For the schemes, the situation differs: the instance $\neg \neg (\varphi \vee \neg \varphi) \to \varphi \vee \neg \varphi$ of double negation elimination proves [[excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]], since $\neg \neg (\varphi \vee \neg \varphi)$ is a theorem.
A Kripke countermodel also exists: a root $r$ below two maximal worlds $u$ and $v$, with $p$ true only at $u$.
The world $v$ forces $\neg p$, so $r$ and $v$ do not force $\neg \neg p$, and $\neg \neg p$ holds only at $u$.
So $\neg \neg p \to p$ holds at every world, while $r$ forces neither $p$ nor $\neg p$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"If $p$ is not refutable, then $p$" is a weak principle about one statement $p$.
It does not tell us whether $p$ holds or fails.
The slash turns this into a test: a proof of "$p$ or not $p$" from it would have to pick a side, and neither side follows.

### L2 human

1. A Harrop hypothesis contains no disjunction in a positive position.
2. So it cannot supply the choice that a proof of $p \vee \neg p$ must make.
3. The classical valuations show that neither choice is forced.

The typical use is to separate an instance of one classical principle from an instance of another.
A trap: the scheme versions are equivalent over $\mathsf{IPC}$, while the single instances are not.
