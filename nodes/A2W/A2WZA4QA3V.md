## Summary

The magnetization of a material is the [[vector field|0299VCW1TH]] that gives its [[magnetic dipole moment|SSAWJ3JF5Y]] per unit volume.
It describes how much the atomic magnetic moments of a material line up, and in which direction.
A magnetized body produces the same [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] as a distribution of bound currents computed from its magnetization.
It is the magnetic counterpart of the [[polarization density|ASQAH710WN]] of a dielectric.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a piece of iron as made of a huge number of tiny compass needles.
In an unmagnetized piece they point every which way, and their effects cancel.
In a magnet many of them point the same way, and their effects add up.
The magnetization at a point is the total strength of the tiny needles in a small piece of material around the point, divided by the volume of that piece.
A bar magnet has a magnetization that points from its south pole to its north pole inside the bar.
