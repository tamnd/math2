## Summary

The Fell topology is the natural topology on the [[unitary dual|95Q8WZMN0W]] of a locally compact group, the set of equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations.
A representation lies in the closure of a set of representations when it is weakly contained in that set.
This means that its matrix coefficients can be approximated by those of the set.
It agrees with the hull-kernel topology of the spectrum of the group C\*-algebra.
For abelian groups it is the usual topology of the Pontryagin dual, but in general it need not be Hausdorff.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The irreducible representations of a group are its basic harmonics, and one wants to say when two of them are close.
Fell's idea is to compare the functions that a representation produces on the group, its matrix coefficients.
A representation is near a family when its matrix coefficients are nearly sums of matrix coefficients from the family, on large compact parts of the group.
For the circle group the harmonics are the integers, and they are all far apart; for the real line they form the real line with its usual distance.
