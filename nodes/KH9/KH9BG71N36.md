## Summary

A frame field, also called a tetrad or vierbein, is a choice of four vector fields on a region of [[spacetime|63ZKC7AFX2]].
At each point they form an orthonormal basis, with one timelike and three spacelike vectors.
It models a family of observers, each carrying a clock and three orthogonal measuring rods.
Components of tensors with respect to a frame field are the values that these observers actually measure.
Frame fields are needed to describe spinor fields in curved spacetime and they underlie the Newman–Penrose and the Cartan formalisms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Coordinates in curved spacetime are just labels, and the components of a vector in coordinates are often hard to interpret.
A frame field instead gives each event its own small laboratory: a time direction and three perpendicular space directions, all of unit length.
Measurements made in such a laboratory look exactly as in special relativity.
For example, outside a star, a static observer's frame has its time direction along the static time and its first space direction pointing radially outward.
