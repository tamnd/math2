## Summary

The advection–diffusion equation, also called the convection–diffusion equation, describes a quantity carried by a flow that also spreads by diffusion.
Examples are a dye concentration and a temperature.
It combines the transport equation with the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]].
It is the basic model of the transport of heat and of dissolved substances in fluids.
The relative strength of the two effects is measured by the [[Péclet number|961839PHN6]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put a drop of ink into a slowly flowing river.
The current carries the drop downstream, and at the same time the drop spreads out into a wider and paler cloud.
The advection–diffusion equation describes both motions together.
In one dimension with constant speed $U$ and diffusivity $D$, a cloud that starts as a point moves at speed $U$, and its width grows like $\sqrt{2 D t}$.
