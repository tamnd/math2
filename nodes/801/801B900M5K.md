## Statement

Let $G$ be the gravitational constant and $c$ the speed of light.
Let a body of [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] $J$ create a weak, slowly rotating gravitational field, described by [[linearized gravity|AH62KSWA6Y]].
Let a test body move on a bound orbit close to a [[Kepler orbit|BXFSBB8Q46]] with semi-major axis $a$ and eccentricity $e$ around it.
Then, to first order in $G/c^2$, the line of nodes of the orbit [[precesses|CFE9SNEHMP]] in the sense of the rotation, with average angular velocity
$$\dot\Omega_{LT} = \frac{2 G J}{c^2 a^3 (1 - e^2)^{3/2}}.$$

## Remarks

In the weak field a rotating body adds off-diagonal terms $g_{0i}$ to the metric.
They form a gravitomagnetic potential shaped like a magnetic dipole field, in the language of [[gravitoelectromagnetism|8VNKG7HFSA]].
The resulting gravitomagnetic force acts on the orbiting body like a magnetic force on a moving charge, and averaging it over one Kepler orbit gives the precession above.
The same field makes a gyroscope at position $\mathbf{r}$ precess relative to the distant stars with angular velocity $\frac{G}{c^2 r^3}\left[3(\mathbf{J}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})\hat{\mathbf{r}} - \mathbf{J}\right]$, where $\hat{\mathbf r} = \mathbf r/r$.
Lense and Thirring derived the effect in 1918.
For Earth satellites it is a few tens of milliarcseconds per year, and it was measured with the LAGEOS satellites.
It is one form of [[frame-dragging|WFCMWBVAEB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A satellite orbiting a spinning planet has its orbital plane slowly turned around the planet's axis, in the direction of the spin.
The rotating planet drags spacetime around, and the orbit is carried with it.
For the Earth the turning is tiny, but careful laser tracking of satellites has detected it.

### L2 human

The Lense–Thirring precession is the gravitational analogue of the precession of a charged particle's orbit around a magnetic dipole.
It must be separated from much larger Newtonian precessions caused by the oblateness of the Earth, which is why satellite tests combine several orbits.
For a gyroscope, the analogous effect is the frame-dragging precession measured by Gravity Probe B, which sits on top of the larger [[geodetic effect|31ZYBXSC50]].
