## Summary

Supergravity is a field theory that combines [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]] with local [[supersymmetry|S847N922FY]].
Local means that the supersymmetry transformation may vary from point to point.
Local supersymmetry forces the presence of gravity, and the graviton is paired with a spin-$3/2$ partner called the gravitino.
The first supergravity theory in four dimensions was found in 1976 by Freedman, van Nieuwenhuizen and Ferrara, and independently by Deser and Zumino.
Supergravity theories are the low-energy limits of superstring theories and of [[M-theory|FEC8C1595Y]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Supersymmetry is a symmetry that exchanges particles of integer spin (bosons) with particles of half-integer spin (fermions).
If one allows this exchange to be done differently at different points of spacetime, one is forced to include gravity.
That theory is supergravity.
Its simplest version has two fields: the graviton, which carries gravity, and the gravitino, its fermionic partner.
Supergravity exists only up to eleven spacetime dimensions, and the eleven-dimensional theory is unique.
