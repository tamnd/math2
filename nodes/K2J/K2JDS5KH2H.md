## Summary

Galilean invariance is the principle that the laws of motion have the same form in every [[inertial frame|Q05G0NZDNB]].
Mathematically, the set of solutions of the equations of motion is preserved by each [[Galilean transformation|8CTX67V404]].
Newton's equations for isolated systems have this property when the forces depend only on relative positions and velocities.
The forces must also turn along with the system under rotations.
Through Noether's theorem, this symmetry is linked to conservation laws.
These are energy, momentum and angular momentum, and the uniform motion of the centre of mass.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Inside a ship sailing smoothly at constant speed, no experiment tells you whether the ship moves.
A dropped coin falls straight down, and a game of table tennis plays out just as on land.
This is Galileo's observation, and Galilean invariance is its precise form: the physics looks the same to all observers who move uniformly relative to each other.
