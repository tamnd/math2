## Definition

Consider a material in [[continuum mechanics|X01DZTS224]] with [[constitutive equations|Q4C55V47A8]] in terms of a list of state variables.
They give the specific free energy $\psi$, the specific entropy $\eta$, the stress and the heat flux.
The *Coleman–Noll procedure* derives restrictions on these functions in three steps.

1. Combine the energy balance with the [[Clausius–Duhem inequality|MKXA9E1Z2T]] to get a reduced dissipation inequality without the heat supply.
2. Require this inequality in every process. The body force and heat supply are chosen so that the balances of momentum and energy hold.
3. Expand the time derivative of $\psi$ by the chain rule. Where the inequality is affine in a rate that can take any value at an instant, set its coefficient to $0$.

## Remarks

Example: take a thermoelastic body with reference density $\rho_0$, deformation gradient $F$, temperature $\theta > 0$ and referential temperature gradient $g$.
Let its first Piola–Kirchhoff stress $P$ and referential heat flux $q_0$ be functions of $(F, \theta, g)$, like $\psi$ and $\eta$.
Step 1 gives
$$\rho_0 (\dot\psi + \eta \dot\theta) - P : \dot F + \frac{q_0 \cdot g}{\theta} \le 0.$$
Step 3 applies to the arbitrary rates $\dot F$, $\dot \theta$ and $\dot g$, and gives that $\psi$ does not depend on $g$, that $\eta = -\partial \psi / \partial \theta$, that $P = \rho_0\, \partial \psi / \partial F$, and that $q_0 \cdot g \le 0$.
Non-example: requiring the inequality only in equilibrium states, where all rates vanish, is not this procedure, and it gives none of these restrictions.
The method is named after Bernard Coleman and Walter Noll.
