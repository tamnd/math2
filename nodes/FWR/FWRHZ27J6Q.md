## Summary

The matter power spectrum $P(k)$ measures how strongly the density of matter in the universe fluctuates on the length scale $2\pi/k$.
It is the [[Fourier transform|DPVM34ADZV]] of the two-point correlation of the density contrast.
It is the main statistic that links theories of the early universe with the observed [[structure formation|VXMCTGXRW6]] of galaxies and clusters.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine the matter of the universe as a sea with waves of all sizes.
The power spectrum tells how tall the waves of each wavelength are, on average.
Large $P(k)$ at small $k$ means strong lumps on large scales; large $P(k)$ at large $k$ means strong lumps on small scales.
For example, a field with Gaussian correlation $\xi(r) = A e^{-r^2/(2R^2)}$ has $P(k) = A (2\pi R^2)^{3/2} e^{-k^2 R^2 / 2}$, so its power sits mostly at wavelengths longer than $R$.
