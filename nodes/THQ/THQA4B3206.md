## Definition

Consider an expanding universe with [[scale factor|J70F32P86W]] $a(t)$ governed by the [[Friedmann equations|PPHFS2P4G4]], with mean matter density $\bar\rho(t)$.
The *density contrast* at comoving position $x$ is
$$\delta(x, t) = \frac{\rho(x, t) - \bar\rho(t)}{\bar\rho(t)}.$$
*Structure formation* is the evolution of $\delta$ under gravity.
It leads from a nearly Gaussian random field with $\abs{\delta} \ll 1$ at early times to bound objects with $\delta \gg 1$, such as halos and clusters.
It has a linear regime, $\abs{\delta} \ll 1$, in which each Fourier mode grows independently, and a nonlinear regime.

## Remarks

The statistics of $\delta$ in the linear regime are given by the [[matter power spectrum|FWRHZ27J6Q]].
The growth is driven by the [[Jeans instability|S1PZ23YAXQ]], slowed by the expansion: in a universe of matter only, linear perturbations grow as $\delta \propto a$.

Example: a region with $\delta = 10^{-3}$ at $a = 10^{-3}$ in such a universe reaches $\delta \approx 1$ near $a = 1$ in linear theory, and then goes nonlinear and collapses.

Non-example: a perfectly homogeneous universe has $\delta \equiv 0$ at all times; there is no structure to grow.

The nonlinear regime is studied with N-body simulations and with approximate models such as spherical collapse.
