## Summary

The Wheeler–DeWitt equation is the quantum version of the [[Hamiltonian constraint|D87R3GJMH6]] of general relativity.
It is a functional differential equation for a wave function of the geometry of space, called the wave function of the universe.
Bryce DeWitt published it in 1967, after discussions with John Wheeler.
It contains no time derivative, which is the origin of the problem of time in canonical [[quantum gravity|M1VXRT5X56]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the [[ADM formalism|CFAFAPV7HB]], general relativity is a theory of how the shape of three-dimensional space changes.
Applying the rules of [[canonical quantization|7NW2C10YSR]] to this description gives a wave function that assigns an amplitude to each possible shape of space.
The Wheeler–DeWitt equation is the condition this wave function must satisfy.
It plays the role of the [[Schrödinger equation|EPZR6H2WJN]], but with zero on the side where the time derivative would be.
So the wave function does not change with time, and time must be found inside the geometry or the matter.
