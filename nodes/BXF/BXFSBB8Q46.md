## Summary

A Kepler orbit is the path of a body that moves under the inverse-square gravity of a single point mass and feels no other force.
It is a conic section with the attracting centre at a focus: an ellipse, a parabola or a hyperbola.
In the degenerate case it is a radial line.
It is the exact solution of the two-body problem and the starting point of all orbit calculations.
See the [[definition|N94189FERD]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Throw a stone very hard in empty space near a star, and it follows a Kepler orbit.
If it is too slow to escape, the path is an ellipse that closes on itself.
If it is fast, the path is a hyperbola that comes in, swings round the star and leaves.
The orbit of the Earth around the Sun is very nearly a Kepler orbit, an ellipse that is almost a circle.
