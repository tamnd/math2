## Statement

Let $L$ be a [[signature|WDVVEYD6AT]], and let $\abs{L}$ be the [[cardinality|0G0ASKNA6Z]] of its set of symbols.
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be an infinite $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]], and let $\kappa$ be an infinite [[cardinal|0EBACQFAZW]] with $\abs{L} \le \kappa$.

1. Downward: if $\kappa \le \abs{M}$ and $A \subseteq M$ has $\abs{A} \le \kappa$, then $\mathcal{M}$ has an [[elementary substructure|8WZF9SBP39]] $\mathcal{N}$ with $A \subseteq N$ and $\abs{N} = \kappa$.
2. Upward: if $\abs{M} \le \kappa$, then $\mathcal{M}$ has an elementary extension $\mathcal{N}$ with $\abs{N} = \kappa$.

So, for every infinite $\kappa \ge \abs{L}$, an infinite structure has an elementary substructure or an elementary extension of cardinality $\kappa$.
In particular, an $L$-theory with an infinite model has a model of each infinite cardinality $\kappa \ge \abs{L}$.
Proofs are in [[the proof by Skolem hulls|J640Y51K78]] and [[the proof by compactness|2P0R6QHMJF]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The structure must be infinite.
A finite $\mathcal{M}$ with $n$ elements satisfies the sentence "there are exactly $n$ elements", so every structure elementarily equivalent to it has $n$ elements.
The bound $\abs{L} \le \kappa$ is needed in the downward part.
Let $L$ have $\aleph_1$ constant symbols, interpreted as distinct real numbers in $\mathcal{M} = \R$.
Every substructure contains the values of all constants, so $\mathcal{M}$ has no countable elementary substructure.
The bound $\abs{A} \le \kappa$ is needed, because $A \subseteq N$ forces $\abs{N} \ge \abs{A}$.
In the upward part, $\abs{M} \le \kappa$ is needed, because $M \subseteq N$ forces $\abs{N} \ge \abs{M}$.

### Consequences

- A first-order theory with an infinite model is not categorical: it has non-isomorphic models of different infinite cardinalities. So model theory studies categoricity in one cardinality, as in [[Morley's categoricity theorem|V7N84P60SJ]].
- The [[Łoś-Vaught test|ZR7Y5C235P]]: a theory in a countable signature with no finite models that is categorical in some infinite cardinal is [[complete|655K8Y7VS8]].
- The real field has a countable elementary substructure; the field of [[real algebraic numbers|D5MSQ48C9Y]] is one.
- If set theory has a model, then it has a countable model. This is [[Skolem's paradox|68QZWDXAQ4]].
- Arithmetic has [[nonstandard models|1QWN5CV4CG]] of every infinite cardinality; see also [[elementary equivalence versus isomorphism|4WGKBXG068]].

### Converse

The theorem has no converse in the usual sense, since its conclusion holds for every infinite structure.
A converse at the level of logics is [[Lindström's theorem|FYZ3CJGZYV]]: first-order logic is the strongest logic that has the compactness property and the downward property for countable models.
Second-order logic fails the theorem: the second-order Peano axioms have $\N$ as their only model up to isomorphism.

### Generalizations

The downward part holds for countable fragments of the infinitary logic $L_{\omega_1 \omega}$, and gives countable models.
The upward part fails for $L_{\omega_1 \omega}$, which can say that a structure is countable.
Two-cardinal theorems ask for models in which two definable sets have prescribed cardinalities; see the sub-area on [[indiscernibles and two-cardinal theorems|W9CHA4PD9H]].

## History

Leopold Löwenheim proved in 1915 that a satisfiable sentence in a countable signature has a countable model.
Thoralf Skolem proved in 1920, with the axiom of choice, that a countable theory satisfied in a structure is satisfied in a countable substructure of it.
In 1922 Skolem proved without choice that such a theory has a countable model.
Anatoly Maltsev proved the general form in 1936.
He cited a note of Skolem, according to which Alfred Tarski had proved the theorem in a seminar in 1928, and the general form is sometimes called the Löwenheim-Skolem-Tarski theorem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

First-order logic cannot control the size of an infinite structure.
If some first-order axioms have an infinite model, then they have models of every infinite size, small and large, at least as large as the number of symbols.
For example, there is a countable list of numbers that satisfies exactly the same first-order statements, about addition, multiplication and order, as the real numbers.
The real algebraic numbers, such as $\sqrt{2}$, form one such list.

### L2 human

We use the theorem to show that the theory $\mathrm{DLO}$ of [[dense linear orders without endpoints|2W4S2H4VYA]] is complete.

1. The signature $\set{<}$ is finite, so $\abs{L} \le \aleph_0$, and $\mathrm{DLO}$ has no finite models.
2. Let $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ be models of $\mathrm{DLO}$. Both are infinite, so by the downward part, with $\kappa = \aleph_0$, each has a countable elementary substructure $\mathcal{A}_0$ and $\mathcal{B}_0$.
3. $\mathcal{A}_0$ and $\mathcal{B}_0$ are models of $\mathrm{DLO}$, since they are elementarily equivalent to $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$.
4. By [[Cantor's isomorphism theorem|ABN5PTXEHY]], $\mathcal{A}_0 \cong \mathcal{B}_0$.
5. So $\mathcal{A} \equiv \mathcal{A}_0 \equiv \mathcal{B}_0 \equiv \mathcal{B}$, and any two models of $\mathrm{DLO}$ satisfy the same sentences.

The typical use is step 2: a question about all models reduces to a question about models of one convenient size.
A trap is to forget the bound $\abs{L} \le \kappa$; in an uncountable signature there may be no countable model at all.

### L3 human

The downward part is a closure argument: close a set under witnesses, by the [[Tarski-Vaught test|60SA99BER7]] or by [[Skolem functions|NKQY9NB955]], and count.
The upward part is a compactness argument with the [[elementary diagram|EP5E317TNW]] and many new constants, followed by the downward part.
Together they show that a theory says nothing about the size of its infinite models beyond $\abs{L}$, and size enters model theory only through finer questions: categoricity in a cardinal, the spectrum function $I(T, \kappa)$, saturation, and two-cardinal phenomena.
In Mathlib the downward part is `FirstOrder.Language.exists_elementarySubstructure_card_eq`.
The same idea, with stronger closure conditions, gives countable elementary submodels of $V_\theta$ in set theory, which are a standard tool for forcing and for reflection arguments.
