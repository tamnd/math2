## Summary

An isothermal process is a process in which the [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]] of the system stays constant.
It usually happens when the system is in contact with a large heat reservoir and changes slowly.
For an [[ideal gas|N7R0H2VNDJ]], the internal energy does not change in such a process, so the heat absorbed equals the work done by the gas.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A gas in a metal cylinder standing in a big water bath keeps the temperature of the bath if it is squeezed slowly.
The heat produced by squeezing flows out into the water, so the gas does not warm up.
For such a gas, pressure times volume stays constant: halve the volume and the pressure doubles.
This is Boyle's law, and its graph in the pressure-volume plane is a hyperbola.
