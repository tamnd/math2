## Summary

An arbitrage is a trading strategy that costs nothing, can never lose money, and makes money with positive probability.
The assumption that a market has no arbitrage is the basic principle of mathematical finance.
It forces prices to be consistent with each other, and it underlies the pricing of derivatives by replication.
The [[fundamental theorem of asset pricing|TYVNND6JN0]] characterises markets without arbitrage.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An arbitrage is a free lunch: a way to make money with no risk and no money down.
If a stock sells for $100$ in one place and $101$ in another, buy in the first and sell in the second.
Traders hunt for such chances, and their trades remove them quickly.
So models of markets assume that there are none.
