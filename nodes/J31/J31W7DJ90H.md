## Statement

Let a compressible fluid have density $\rho$, velocity $u = (u_1, u_2, u_3)$, pressure $p$ and viscous stress tensor $\tau_{ij}$.
Assume that it satisfies the mass and momentum equations of the compressible [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] with no external force:
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u_j)}{\partial x_j} = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial (\rho u_i)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u_i u_j + p \delta_{ij} - \tau_{ij})}{\partial x_j} = 0,$$
with summation over repeated indices.
Let $\rho_0$, $p_0$ be constants, let $c_0 > 0$ be a constant, and set $\rho' = \rho - \rho_0$ and $p' = p - p_0$.
Define the *Lighthill stress tensor*
$$T_{ij} = \rho u_i u_j + (p' - c_0^2 \rho') \delta_{ij} - \tau_{ij}.$$
Then
$$\frac{\partial^2 \rho'}{\partial t^2} - c_0^2 \, \Delta \rho' = \frac{\partial^2 T_{ij}}{\partial x_i \, \partial x_j}.$$

## Remarks

The proof takes the time derivative of the mass equation and the divergence of the momentum equation, and subtracts the second from the first; this eliminates $\rho u_i$.
Then $c_0^2 \Delta \rho'$ is subtracted from both sides.
No approximation is made, so the equation is exact; it is a rearrangement of the flow equations.

The *analogy* reads the left side as the [[acoustic wave equation|85WZYYHZYS]] of a fluid at rest with [[speed of sound|SN4J6KP03C]] $c_0$.
It reads the right side as a given quadrupole source.
In a jet, $T_{ij}$ is small outside the turbulent region, so far from the jet $\rho'$ is the sound radiated by the turbulence.
A scaling argument on this source gives Lighthill's eighth power law: for a low Mach number jet of speed $U$ and diameter $D$, the radiated acoustic power scales like $\rho_0 U^8 D^2 / c_0^5$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A turbulent jet is noisy, but its noise is a small side effect of a violent flow.
Lighthill rewrote the exact flow equations so that they look like the equation of sound in still air, plus a source term.
All the complicated motion of the jet goes into the source term.
If one can estimate that source, one can estimate the noise.

### L2 human

The analogy turns a nonlinear flow problem into a linear wave problem with a known right side, which can be solved with the free-space Green's function of the wave equation.
The source $\partial_i \partial_j T_{ij}$ is a double divergence, so it radiates like a distribution of quadrupoles, which are inefficient radiators at low Mach number.
This explains the strong dependence $U^8$ of jet noise on the jet speed.
The [[Ffowcs Williams–Hawkings equation|W0ZTN95BMW]] extends the analogy to flows with moving solid surfaces, where monopole and dipole sources appear too.
