## Summary

The Kretschmann scalar is the full contraction $R_{abcd} R^{abcd}$ of the [[Riemann curvature tensor|JA29ETCM02]] with itself.
It is a scalar invariant, so it does not depend on the choice of coordinates.
In general relativity it is used to tell real curvature singularities apart from coordinate artefacts.
For the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]] it equals $48 G^2 M^2 / (c^4 r^6)$, which is finite at the horizon and infinite at $r = 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Curvature in four dimensions is described by many numbers, the components of the Riemann tensor, and these change from one coordinate system to another.
Squaring the tensor and adding everything up gives one number at each point that every observer agrees on.
If this number is infinite somewhere, the trouble there is real and cannot be removed by better coordinates.
Near a Schwarzschild black hole it stays finite at the horizon, which shows that the horizon is not a true singularity.
