## Summary

A shape-memory alloy is a metal alloy that can be deformed at low temperature and returns to its original shape when it is heated.
The effect comes from a reversible [[martensitic transformation|EZRAMFGRZT]] between a high-temperature phase, austenite, and a low-temperature phase, martensite.
Nickel–titanium alloys are the best-known examples.
In mathematics these alloys are a main motivation for the energy-minimisation theory of crystal microstructure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

At low temperature the crystal lattice of the alloy can sit in several slightly different shapes, called variants.
Bending the metal does not break atomic bonds; it only changes which variants are present and how they are arranged.
On heating, all variants turn back into the single high-temperature lattice, and that lattice has only one shape, so the piece recovers its original form.
For example, a bent nickel–titanium wire straightens itself when it is put into hot water.
Above the transformation temperature the same alloys show superelasticity: they take large strains under load and spring back when unloaded.
