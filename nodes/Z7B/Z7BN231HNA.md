## Summary

Thomson scattering is the elastic [[scattering|8BQYB8H5CT]] of electromagnetic radiation by a free charged particle, in the classical, low-energy limit.
The incident wave shakes the charge, and the accelerated charge radiates a wave of the same frequency.
Its cross-section does not depend on the frequency.
It governs the scattering of light by free electrons in plasmas and in stars, and it is the low-energy limit of Compton scattering.
See the [[definition|HFN7D2CZM0]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An electron sits in a light wave.
The electric field of the wave pushes the electron back and forth at the frequency of the light.
A charge that moves back and forth sends out its own light, so part of the incoming light is sent off in new directions.
The electron sends little light along its line of motion and the most light at right angles to it.
The effective target area of one electron is about $6.65 \times 10^{-29}$ square metres, tiny but important in the hot gas of stars.
