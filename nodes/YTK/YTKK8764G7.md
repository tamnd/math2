## Proof

We show that any two bases give the same value of $\det [T]_B$, by writing one matrix as a conjugate of the other and using the product rule.

1. Let $n = \dim V$.
   If $n = 0$, both sides of the [[statement|0KMN32XGPF]] equal $1$, by the convention for $V = 0$ and the value $1$ of the empty determinant.
2. So let $n \ge 1$, and let $B$ and $B'$ be two bases of $V$ with the [[matrices of $T$|0YYMY95FWS]] $[T]_B$ and $[T]_{B'}$.
3. Let $P$ be the [[change of basis matrix|0KWXYZPJKN]] from $B$ to $B'$, whose columns are the coordinates of the vectors of $B'$ in $B$.
4. By the [[change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]], $P$ is invertible and
$$[T]_{B'} = P^{-1} [T]_B P.$$
5. The [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]] is multiplicative by the [[product rule|04CPJ6AF00]], so
$$\det [T]_{B'} = \det(P^{-1}) \, \det [T]_B \, \det P.$$
6. These three factors are elements of the field $F$, which is commutative.
   So we may reorder them:
$$\det [T]_{B'} = \det(P^{-1}) \det P \cdot \det [T]_B.$$
7. By the product rule again and the definition of the [[inverse|0TWTS88TWN]],
$$\det(P^{-1}) \det P = \det(P^{-1} P) = \det I_n.$$
8. By the [[determinant of the identity|0Z4BWARC38]], $\det I_n = 1$.
   So steps 6 to 8 give $\det [T]_{B'} = \det [T]_B$ for any two bases $B$ and $B'$.
9. The [[determinant of $T$|03F5KP94K0]] is defined as $\det [T]_{B'}$ for some chosen basis $B'$.
   By step 8 this equals $\det [T]_B$ for the given basis $B$, which is the statement.

## Idea

A change of basis replaces the matrix $[T]_B$ by the similar matrix $P^{-1} [T]_B P$.
The determinant turns this product of three matrices into a product of three numbers.
Numbers commute, so $\det P^{-1}$ and $\det P$ meet and cancel to $1$, and only $\det [T]_B$ is left.

## Variants

A proof without matrices uses the top exterior power $\Lambda^n V$, a line on which $\Lambda^n T$ acts as multiplication by one scalar.
That scalar does not mention a basis, and computing it with the generator $b_1 \wedge \dots \wedge b_n$ gives $\det [T]_B$.
For an endomorphism of a free module of finite rank over a commutative ring, the matrix proof above applies word for word.
