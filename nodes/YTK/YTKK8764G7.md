## Proof

We show that every basis gives the same value of $\det [T]_B$.
Then the definition of $\det T$ gives this value for each basis.

1. Let $n = \dim V$. If $n = 0$, both sides equal $1$ by the conventions for $V = 0$ and the empty matrix.
2. So let $n \ge 1$. The [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]] is then defined for the $n \times n$ matrices below.
3. Let $B$ and $B'$ be two bases of $V$, with [[matrices of $T$|0YYMY95FWS]] $[T]_B$ and $[T]_{B'}$.
4. Let $P$ be the [[change of basis matrix|0KWXYZPJKN]] from $B$ to $B'$.
5. By the [[change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]], $P$ is invertible and $[T]_{B'} = P^{-1} [T]_B P$.
6. By the [[product rule|04CPJ6AF00]], $\det [T]_{B'} = \det(P^{-1}) \det [T]_B \det P$.
7. The field $F$ is commutative, so the right side equals $\det(P^{-1}) \det P \cdot \det [T]_B$.
8. By the product rule and the [[inverse|0TWTS88TWN]], $\det(P^{-1}) \det P = \det(P^{-1} P) = \det I_n$.
9. By the [[determinant of the identity|0Z4BWARC38]], $\det I_n = 1$.
10. By steps 6 to 9, $\det [T]_{B'} = \det [T]_B$ for any two bases $B$ and $B'$.
11. The [[determinant of $T$|03F5KP94K0]] is $\det [T]_{B'}$ for some chosen basis $B'$.
12. By step 10, $\det T = \det [T]_B$ for the given basis $B$.

## Idea

Changing the basis replaces the matrix $[T]_B$ by a similar matrix $P^{-1} [T]_B P$.
The determinant is multiplicative, so the factors $\det P^{-1}$ and $\det P$ cancel.

## Variants

A proof without matrices uses the exterior power $\Lambda^n V$, a line on which $\Lambda^n T$ acts as one scalar.
For an endomorphism of a free module of finite rank over a commutative ring, the same steps apply word for word.
