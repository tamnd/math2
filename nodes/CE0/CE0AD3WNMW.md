## Summary

The Harrison–Zeldovich spectrum is the scale-invariant spectrum of primordial density fluctuations.
In it the [[matter power spectrum|FWRHZ27J6Q]] of the initial fluctuations is proportional to $k$, so the spectral index is $n_s = 1$.
Each scale then has the same density contrast when it enters the cosmological horizon.
It was proposed by Edward Harrison and by Yakov Zeldovich, and independently by Jim Peebles and Jer Yu.
It is the reference case for measured spectral indices.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The early universe had tiny ripples in its density, and the spectrum tells how strong they were at each size.
The Harrison–Zeldovich choice says that no size is special: the ripples look the same whatever scale you look at, in a precise sense.
This is the simplest guess one can make without a theory of where the ripples came from.
Measurements of the cosmic microwave background give an index $n_s$ of about $0.965$, very close to the Harrison–Zeldovich value $1$ but slightly below it.
