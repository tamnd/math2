## Example

Claim: for all real numbers $a$ and $b$, $a^2 - b^2 \ge 0$.
A student writes this argument.

1. Without loss of generality, $|a| \ge |b|$.
2. Then $a^2 = |a|^2 \ge |b|^2 = b^2$.
3. So $a^2 - b^2 \ge 0$.

The claim is false, and line 1 is not a valid use of [[without loss of generality|Y7NE0E6PCV]].
We check the condition that fails.

1. A wlog step needs a transformation that keeps the statement and moves each skipped case into the chosen case.
2. The natural candidate is the exchange of $a$ and $b$.
   It does move the case $|a| < |b|$ into the case $|a| > |b|$.
3. But it does not keep the statement.
   It turns $a^2 - b^2 \ge 0$ into $b^2 - a^2 \ge 0$, which is a different claim.
4. So the case $|a| < |b|$ is not covered, and there the claim fails.
   For $a = 0$ and $b = 1$ we get $a^2 - b^2 = -1 < 0$, a [[counterexample|0ED72CTBNP]].

## Remarks

Lines 2 and 3 of the student's argument are correct.
They prove "if $|a| \ge |b|$, then $a^2 \ge b^2$", and the only error is the claim that this case is enough.

A statement is symmetric in $a$ and $b$ when exchanging the letters gives the same statement.
The claim $a^2 + b^2 \ge 0$ is symmetric.
The expression $a^2 - b^2$ is antisymmetric: the exchange turns it into its negative.
For an antisymmetric expression, wlog can still work for a statement about its absolute value, because $|b^2 - a^2| = |a^2 - b^2|$.
For example, we may assume $|a| \ge |b|$ when proving
$$|a^2 - b^2| = \big| |a| - |b| \big| \cdot \big( |a| + |b| \big).$$

## Explanations

### L1 human

The claim says that the square of the first number is always at least the square of the second.
The student said: let the first number be the bigger one.
But the claim treats the first and the second number differently, so we cannot rename them at will.
With first number $0$ and second number $1$, the squares are $0$ and $1$, and the claim fails.

### L2 human

Here is a test that tells you whether "wlog $a \le b$" is allowed.
Write the statement $S(a, b)$ with all its hypotheses, and write $S(b, a)$ by exchanging the letters everywhere.
If $S(b, a)$ says the same as $S(a, b)$, the step is allowed.
For "$\tfrac{a + b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab}$ for $a, b \ge 0$", the exchange gives the same statement, because $b + a = a + b$ and $ba = ab$, so the step is allowed.
For "$a - b \le |a| + |b|$", the exchange gives "$b - a \le |a| + |b|$", a different inequality, so the step is not allowed as it stands.
The typical use is to run this one-line test before you write "wlog", and to put the result in the proof.
Watch out: in the second case both inequalities are true, but a proof of one is not a proof of the other without a further argument.
