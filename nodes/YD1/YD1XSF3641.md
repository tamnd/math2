## Example

Claim: for all real numbers $a$ and $b$, $a^2 - b^2 \geq 0$.
A student writes this argument.

1. Without loss of generality, $|a| \geq |b|$.
2. Then $a^2 = |a|^2 \geq |b|^2 = b^2$.
3. So $a^2 - b^2 \geq 0$.

The claim is false, and step 1 is not a valid use of [[without loss of generality|Y7NE0E6PCV]].
We check the condition that fails.

1. A wlog step needs a symmetry that keeps the statement and maps each case into the chosen case.
2. The natural candidate is the exchange of $a$ and $b$. It maps the case $|a| < |b|$ into the case $|a| > |b|$.
3. But it does not keep the statement: it changes $a^2 - b^2 \geq 0$ into $b^2 - a^2 \geq 0$, a different claim.
4. So the case $|a| < |b|$ is not covered. There the claim fails: $a = 0$ and $b = 1$ give $a^2 - b^2 = -1$, a [[counterexample|0ED72CTBNP]].

## Remarks

Steps 2 and 3 of the student's argument are correct; they prove the statement "if $|a| \geq |b|$, then $a^2 \geq b^2$".
The error is only in the claim that this case is enough.

A statement is symmetric in $a$ and $b$ when exchanging the letters gives the same statement.
The claim $a^2 + b^2 \geq 0$ is symmetric, and $a^2 - b^2 \geq 0$ is antisymmetric: the exchange turns it into its own reverse.
For an antisymmetric expression, wlog can still be used for a statement about its absolute value, such as $|a^2 - b^2| = \big||a| - |b|\big| \cdot (|a| + |b|)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The claim says that the square of the first number is always at least the square of the second.
The student said: let the first number be the bigger one.
But the claim treats the first and second numbers differently, so we cannot rename them at will.
With first number $0$ and second number $1$, the squares are $0$ and $1$, and the claim fails.

### L2 human

We test whether a statement allows "wlog $a \leq b$".

1. Write the statement $S(a, b)$, with all hypotheses.
2. Write $S(b, a)$ by exchanging the letters everywhere.
3. If $S(b, a)$ is the same statement as $S(a, b)$, or is equivalent to it, the wlog step is allowed.
4. For $S(a, b)$: "$\frac{a + b}{2} \geq \sqrt{ab}$ for $a, b \geq 0$", step 2 gives the same statement, so wlog is allowed.
5. For $S(a, b)$: "$a - b \leq |a| + |b|$", step 2 gives "$b - a \leq |a| + |b|$", a different statement, so the step is not allowed as it stands.

The typical use: the test of steps 1 to 3 takes one line, and it belongs in the proof.

A trap: in step 5 both statements are true, but the proof of one does not prove the other without a further argument.
