## Summary

An elastic metamaterial is an engineered composite whose small built-in structures make it respond to waves in ways that its ingredient materials do not.
In certain frequency bands its effective mass density or its effective elastic modulus can be negative, so that waves cannot travel through it.
The usual cause is a local [[resonance|4F812GHFZK]] of the inner structures, at wavelengths much longer than the structures.
Such materials are used to block vibration and to steer elastic and acoustic waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a hollow ball with a heavy core hanging inside on a spring.
If you shake the shell slowly, the core moves along and the whole thing feels heavy.
If you shake it just above the natural frequency of the core, the core swings against you, and the ball pushes back as if its mass were negative.
A solid built from many such cells stops waves in that frequency band, even though the band can be far below what the size of the cells would suggest.
