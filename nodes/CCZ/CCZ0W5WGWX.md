## Summary

An anticommutative algebra is a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] in which the square of every element is zero.
It follows that $xy = -yx$ for all $x$ and $y$: swapping the factors changes the sign.
[[Lie algebras|5JJYBJ6A8K]] and $\R^3$ with the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]] are the basic examples.
Anticommutativity is far from associativity, so such algebras are studied with their own identities, such as the [[Jacobi identity|ZKFN197MBR]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an anticommutative algebra, multiplying in the other order flips the sign of the result.
Multiplying an element by itself always gives zero.
The cross product of vectors in space is the standard picture: $v \times w = -(w \times v)$ and $v \times v = 0$.
