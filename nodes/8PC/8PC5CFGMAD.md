## Summary

A zero-sum game is a game in which the payoffs of the players add up to zero for every outcome.
For two players, one player's gain is exactly the other's loss, so the interests are strictly opposed.
Two-player zero-sum games have a value, by von Neumann's minimax theorem, and optimal strategies can be found by linear programming.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Poker between two friends is zero-sum: whatever one wins, the other loses.
Trade is usually not zero-sum, since both sides can gain.
A two-player zero-sum game has a number called its value.
The first player can guarantee to win at least the value on average, and the second player can keep her loss to at most the value.
Matching pennies is zero-sum with value $0$.
