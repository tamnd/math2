## Summary

Classical mechanics is the theory of the motion of bodies under forces, in the form that Newton gave it.
In its simplest model, a system is a finite set of point particles with masses and positions in [[Euclidean space|0XPR1E85W0]].
Its motion solves a system of second-order [[ordinary differential equations|0NT64JV96A]].
It describes motion well at speeds small compared with the speed of light and at scales large compared with atoms.
Much of the theory of differential equations, the calculus of variations and symplectic geometry grew out of it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Classical mechanics answers one question: given where things are, how fast they move, and which forces act on them, where will they be later?
Think of a ball thrown in the air, a planet around the Sun, or a swinging pendulum.
Each body is modelled by a point with a mass, and the forces tell how its velocity changes.
For a ball thrown upward at $10$ m/s, gravity slows it by about $9.8$ m/s each second, so it stops rising after about one second.
The same rules, applied to many bodies at once, describe the whole Solar System.
