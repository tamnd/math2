## Example

Let $G = GL_2(\mathbb{F}_2)$, the [[general linear group|0FCHE902D7]] of invertible $2 \times 2$ matrices over the field with two elements.
It has $(4 - 1)(4 - 2) = 6$ elements.
For a $2 \times 2$ matrix, $\chi_A = x^2 + (\operatorname{tr} A)\, x + \det A$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$, since $-1 = 1$.

1. The six elements are $I$, the three matrices $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and the two matrices $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. Each of the three matrices in the second group has trace $0$ and determinant $1$, so its characteristic polynomial is $x^2 + 1 = (x + 1)^2$. None of them is scalar.
3. Each of the two matrices in the third group has trace $1$ and determinant $1$, so its characteristic polynomial is $x^2 + x + 1$. This polynomial has no root in $\mathbb{F}_2$, since its values at $0$ and $1$ are both $1$.
4. A nonscalar $2 \times 2$ matrix has the single invariant factor $\chi_A$. So the three matrices of step 2 are conjugate in $G$, and the two matrices of step 3 are conjugate to the [[companion matrix|0VH1TC1PWF]] $C(x^2 + x + 1) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
5. So $G$ has three [[conjugacy classes|0DEBXAJ5SA]]: $\{I\}$, a class of size $3$ and a class of size $2$. This agrees with $q^2 - 1 = 3$ for $q = 2$.
6. Centralizers. For $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, the units of $\mathbb{F}_2[A]$ are $I$ and $A$, so $|C_G(A)| = 2 = q(q - 1)$, and the [[class size|0VK5V9P8G3]] is $6 / 2 = 3$.
7. For $C = C(x^2 + x + 1)$, $\mathbb{F}_2[C]$ is a field with $4$ elements, so $|C_G(C)| = 3 = q^2 - 1$, and the class size is $6 / 3 = 2$.
8. The [[class equation|08BPSC6YTE]] reads $6 = 1 + 3 + 2$.
9. The third type of the theorem, $\operatorname{diag}(a, b)$ with $a \ne b$ both nonzero, does not occur, because $1$ is the only nonzero element of $\mathbb{F}_2$. This agrees with $(q - 1)(q - 2)/2 = 0$.

## Remarks

In the language of partitions, $I$ has $\lambda(x + 1) = (1, 1)$, the class of size $3$ has $\lambda(x + 1) = (2)$, and the class of size $2$ has $\lambda(x^2 + x + 1) = (1)$.
The group $G$ permutes the three nonzero vectors of $\mathbb{F}_2^2$, and this gives an isomorphism with the symmetric group $S_3$.
Under it, the class of size $3$ goes to the transpositions and the class of size $2$ to the $3$-cycles.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The six invertible $2 \times 2$ matrices over $\{0, 1\}$ fall into three groups of mutually conjugate matrices.
The groups have sizes $1$, $3$ and $2$.

### L2 human

Trace and determinant decide the class of a nonscalar $2 \times 2$ matrix.
Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ the determinant of an invertible matrix is always $1$, so only the trace separates the two nonscalar classes.
The trap is to forget the scalar class: $I$ also has trace $0$ and determinant $1$, but it is alone in its class.
