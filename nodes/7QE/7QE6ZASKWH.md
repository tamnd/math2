## Summary

Fluid–structure interaction is the coupled behaviour of a deformable solid and a fluid that flows around it or inside it.
The fluid pushes on the solid, the solid deforms, and the deformation changes the domain and the boundary velocity of the fluid.
The model couples the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] with the equations of [[elastodynamics|T1PYEBKBAJ]] through conditions on the moving interface.
It explains flutter of wings, vibration of bridges in wind and the motion of heart valves and blood vessels.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A flag in the wind is a good picture.
The air pushes the cloth, the cloth bends, and the bent cloth changes how the air flows, which changes the push again.
Neither the air nor the cloth can be computed alone, because each needs the other as input.
A tall chimney that sheds vortices and starts to sway is another example.
