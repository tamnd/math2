## Summary

Control and systems theory is the study of dynamical systems that have inputs and outputs, and of the ways to choose the inputs so that a system behaves as required.
A control system is a dynamical system with an input that we can choose, called the control, and an output that we can measure.
Feedback computes the input from the measured output, and it can make a system stable and less sensitive to disturbances and to errors in the model.
Maxwell studied the stability of governors in 1868, Nyquist and Bode built the frequency-domain theory of feedback amplifiers in the 1930s and 1940s, and Kalman introduced the state-space theory around 1960.
This topic collects the control and systems theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Optimal control and the calculus of variations, dynamical systems, ordinary differential equations and information theory have their own topics.
