## Proof

Let $E$ be a [[row echelon form|0VYCDGMJJJ]] that is row equivalent to $M = [A \mid b]$.

1. Scale each nonzero row of $E$ so that its leading entry becomes $1$.
2. A scaling by a nonzero number does not move the leading entry of a row.
3. Then, for each pivot from the bottom up, subtract multiples of its row from the rows above to clear its column.
4. The pivot row has zeros to the left of its leading column, which is to the right of the leading columns of the rows above.
5. So step 3 does not change the leading entry of any row, and every column of a leading entry is cleared.
6. The result $R$ is in [[reduced row echelon form|0WF5AQYTW6]], with the same leading columns as $E$.
7. $R$ is row equivalent to $E$, hence to $M$, by transitivity in [[the row equivalence theorem|E4GKK727ZM]].
8. By [[the uniqueness of the RREF|BF9SMZMJZE]], $R$ is the RREF of $M$, so the pivot columns of $E$ are those of $\operatorname{rref}(M)$.
9. This shows that the pivot columns, and so $k$, do not depend on the choice of $E$.
10. Write $R = [R_0 \mid c]$; by [[the row equivalence theorem|E4GKK727ZM]], $Ax = b$ and $R_0 x = c$ have the same solutions.
11. Part 1: by [[the lemma on systems in RREF|P3HMRQN5KT]], part 1, a leading entry in the last column of $R$ gives no solution.
12. If the last column of $R$ has no leading entry, part 3 of that lemma with $t = 0$ gives a solution.
13. By step 6, the last column of $R$ has a leading entry if and only if the last column of $E$ has one.
14. A leading entry in the last column means a row $(0, \dots, 0 \mid c)$ with $c \neq 0$; this proves part 1.
15. Parts 2 to 5: assume consistency; then part 4 of the lemma gives a bijection from $F^k$ onto the solution set.
16. If $k = 0$, then $F^0$ has one element, so the solution is unique.
17. If $k \ge 1$, then $F^k$ has at least $|F| \ge 2$ elements, so the solution is not unique; this proves part 3.
18. If $F$ is infinite and $k \ge 1$, then $F^k$ is infinite, which proves part 4.
19. If $|F| = q$, then $|F^k| = q^k$, which proves part 5.

## Idea

Turn any echelon form into the unique RREF without moving the pivots, and read everything from the RREF.

## Variants

If $E$ is already the RREF, steps 1 to 9 are not needed, and the theorem is a direct restatement of [[the lemma on systems in RREF|P3HMRQN5KT]].
Some books prove only the case $F = \R$, and then part 5 is absent.
