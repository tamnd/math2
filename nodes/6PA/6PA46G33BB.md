## Summary

The Heston model is a [[stochastic volatility|2KQF3JSAQ7]] model in which the variance of the asset price follows a mean-reverting square-root process.
It was introduced by Steven Heston in 1993.
Its main strength is that European option prices have a semi-closed form through the characteristic function of the log-price.
With a negative correlation between price and variance it produces the downward skew seen in equity option markets.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Heston model lets the variance of a stock move randomly but always pulls it back towards a long-run average.
The noise in the variance shrinks when the variance is small, which keeps the variance from becoming negative.
The stock price and the variance are shaken by two noises that can move together or against each other.
For example, with a long-run variance $\theta = 0.04$, the volatility tends to return to $\sqrt{0.04} = 20\%$.
