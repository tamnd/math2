## Statement

Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\Q$, let $m \ge 0$, and let $L(h^m(X), s)$ be the L-function of the $m$-th $\ell$-adic cohomology of $X$.
Let $n$ be an integer with $n > m/2 + 1$.
Let $K^{(n)}_\Q$ denote the weight $n$ eigenspace of the [[Adams operations|KQP64TH307]] on rational K-theory.
Let $H^{m+1}_{\mathcal{M}}(X, \Q(n))_\Z \subseteq K_{2n-m-1}(X)^{(n)}_\Q$ be the subspace of classes that come from a regular proper model of $X$ over $\Z$.
Then:

1. The [[Beilinson regulator|SXJWFF0WAJ]] induces an isomorphism of real vector spaces
$$H^{m+1}_{\mathcal{M}}(X, \Q(n))_\Z \otimes_\Q \R \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } H^{m+1}_{\mathcal{D}}(X_\R, \R(n)).$$
2. The value $L(h^m(X), n)$ equals, up to a nonzero rational factor, the determinant of this isomorphism.
The determinant is taken with respect to the rational structures given by the motivic group and by Betti and de Rham cohomology.

## Remarks

For $n > m/2 + 1$ the Euler product of $L(h^m(X), s)$ converges at $s = n$, by Deligne's proof of the Weil conjectures, so the value is defined and nonzero.
Beilinson also stated versions at the other integers, using the leading coefficient of the L-function and conjectural analytic continuation.

For $X = \operatorname{Spec} F$ with $F$ a number field and $m = 0$, the L-function is the Dedekind zeta function $\zeta_F(s)$, and the conjecture follows from Borel's theorem on the [[Borel regulator|D22EZ3CNAF]].
The conjectures are open in general.
For modular curves Beilinson proved a weak form.
He constructed elements of K-theory whose regulator determinant gives the L-value up to a nonzero rational factor, without showing that they span the integral subspace.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The L-function of a variety collects counts of its points modulo all primes into one analytic function.
Beilinson proposed that the values of this function at integers are explained by K-theory.
The K-groups should give a lattice in a real vector space through the regulator, and the volume of that lattice should be the L-value, up to a rational number.
This generalises the class number formula, where the units of a number field explain the residue of the zeta function at $s = 1$.
