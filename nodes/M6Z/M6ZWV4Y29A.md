## Summary

The principal stresses at a point are the eigenvalues of the symmetric [[Cauchy stress tensor|KG2XWEMJ30]] $\sigma$, and the principal directions are its eigenvectors.
On a plane perpendicular to a principal direction, the traction is purely normal, with no shear.
The largest and smallest principal stresses bound the normal stress on every plane, and many failure criteria are stated in terms of them.

## Explanations

### L1 human

At any point of a loaded body one can turn a small cube so that its faces feel only pushes or pulls, and no sideways forces.
The pushes or pulls on these three special faces are the principal stresses.
For a plate pulled with stress $s$ in one direction and nothing else, the principal stresses are $s$, $0$ and $0$.
For a pure shear the principal stresses are $\tau$, $0$ and $-\tau$, at $45$ degrees to the shear directions.
