## Summary

The primitive equations are the equations of motion used in most models of the global atmosphere and ocean.
They come from the Navier–Stokes equations on a rotating planet by replacing the vertical momentum equation with hydrostatic balance.
This is accurate because these fluid layers are very thin compared with their horizontal extent.
They keep the horizontal momentum equations, conservation of mass, a thermodynamic equation and an equation of state.
They are the starting point from which [[geostrophic balance|CZ1S1P465Y]] and the [[quasi-geostrophic equations|8Z4QBWTWVR]] are derived.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The atmosphere and the ocean are like thin sheets wrapped around the Earth: a few kilometres deep but thousands of kilometres wide.
In such a thin sheet the vertical motion is weak, and the pressure at each point is simply the weight of the fluid above it.
The primitive equations build this fact in.
Weather and climate models solve them on a grid covering the globe.
