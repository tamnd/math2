## Proof

We factor the block matrix as a lower block triangular matrix with identity diagonal blocks times an upper block triangular matrix, and we take determinants.

1. Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}$ be the [[block matrix|01AXG9KZQF]] of the statement, and let $S = D - C A^{-1} B$.
   Let
$$L = \begin{pmatrix} I_p & 0 \\ C A^{-1} & I_q \end{pmatrix}, \qquad U = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ 0 & S \end{pmatrix},$$
   where $I_p$ and $I_q$ are [[identity matrices|06DW9BAWB1]] and the $0$ are zero blocks of the right sizes.
2. Block matrices with the same splitting of the indices multiply block by block, like $2 \times 2$ matrices.
   The reason is the definition of the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]]: entry $(r, s)$ of $XY$ is $\sum_k X_{rk} Y_{ks}$, and we can split this sum into the indices $k \le p$ and $k > p$.
3. By step 2, the two upper blocks of $LU$ are $I_p A + 0 \cdot 0 = A$ and $I_p B + 0 \cdot S = B$.
4. The lower left block of $LU$ is
$$C A^{-1} A + I_q \cdot 0 = C (A^{-1} A) = C,$$
   by [[associativity|073E4XC016]] and the definition of the [[inverse|0TWTS88TWN]].
5. The lower right block of $LU$ is
$$C A^{-1} B + I_q S = C A^{-1} B + D - C A^{-1} B = D.$$
   So $LU = M$.
6. The matrix $U$ is upper block triangular, so the rule for [[block triangular matrices|0J9TVTNB4K]] gives $\det U = \det A \cdot \det S$.
7. The matrix $L$ is lower block triangular with diagonal blocks $I_p$ and $I_q$.
   The same rule, in its lower form, and $\det I_p = \det I_q = 1$ from the [[determinant of the identity|0Z4BWARC38]] give $\det L = 1$.
8. By the [[product rule|04CPJ6AF00]] and steps 5 to 7,
$$\det M = \det L \cdot \det U = \det A \cdot \det(D - C A^{-1} B).$$

## Idea

Think of block Gaussian elimination.
To clear the block $C$, subtract $C A^{-1}$ times the first block row from the second block row.
This replaces $D$ by $D - C A^{-1} B$ and leaves a block triangular matrix.
The operation is multiplication on the left by a block triangular matrix with identity blocks on its diagonal, so it does not change the determinant, just like adding a multiple of one row to another.
The determinant of what remains is $\det A$ times the determinant of the Schur complement.

For $1 \times 1$ blocks the factorization reads
$$\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ c/a & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & d - cb/a \end{pmatrix},$$
which is the first step of the LU factorization.

## Variants

If $D$ is invertible, the factorization
$$\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} I_p & B D^{-1} \\ 0 & I_q \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} A - B D^{-1} C & 0 \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}$$
gives $\det D \cdot \det(A - B D^{-1} C)$ in the same way.
A symmetric version multiplies by triangular matrices on both sides:
$$\begin{pmatrix} I_p & 0 \\ -C A^{-1} & I_q \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} I_p & -A^{-1} B \\ 0 & I_q \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} A & 0 \\ 0 & S \end{pmatrix}.$$
Both outer factors have determinant $1$, so this also gives the formula, and it shows that $M$ and the block diagonal matrix with blocks $A$ and $S$ are congruent when $M$ is symmetric.
