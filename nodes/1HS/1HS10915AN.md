## Definition

Let $S$ be the finite set of species of an ecological community.
The *food web* of the community is the [[directed graph|0KFYGSHHF5]] with vertex set $S$ defined as follows.
It has an arc from $i$ to $j$ exactly when species $j$ feeds on species $i$.
A species with no incoming arc is a *basal species*, and a species with no outgoing arc is a *top species*.
A directed path in the food web is a *food chain*.

## Remarks

Example: let $S = \set{\text{grass}, \text{rabbit}, \text{fox}}$, with arcs grass $\to$ rabbit and rabbit $\to$ fox.
The grass is basal, the fox is top, and grass $\to$ rabbit $\to$ fox is a food chain of length $2$.
Non-example: a graph with an arc from fox to grass is not the food web of this community.
The arc would say that grass feeds on foxes, which is false.
With $L$ arcs and $\abs{S}$ species, the number $L / \abs{S}^2$ is called the connectance of the web.
Some authors draw the arcs in the other direction, from the eater to its food; the two conventions give the same graph up to reversal of all arcs.
The trophic level of a species is often defined as $1$ for basal species and $1$ plus the mean trophic level of its food species otherwise.
