## Summary

Equilibrium thermodynamics has a natural formulation in [[contact geometry|HBQ8BZ7XB5]].
The thermodynamic phase space has coordinates for a potential, the extensive variables and their conjugate intensive variables, with the contact form $dU - T\,dS + p\,dV - \dots$.
The equilibrium states of a given system form a [[Legendrian submanifold|Q2DMSTWZYZ]], on which the form vanishes; this is the [[fundamental thermodynamic relation|41NHDR6MH6]].
Changes of thermodynamic potential by [[Legendre transformation|2B0YR3D0XA]] are then contact transformations that preserve this structure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a simple gas, an equilibrium state is described by five numbers: energy $U$, entropy $S$, volume $V$, temperature $T$ and pressure $p$.
They are not independent: once a material is fixed, only two of them can be chosen, and the others follow.
The rule $dU = T\,dS - p\,dV$ ties them together, and in geometry such a rule is a contact form on five-dimensional space.
The states of a real material then form a two-dimensional surface that is everywhere tangent to the planes picked out by the form.
Switching from energy to enthalpy or free energy is just a change of coordinates that keeps this picture intact.
