## Definition

Let $d \in \set{2, 3}$, let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite [[graph|00C0BR7D7H]], and let $p \colon V \to \R^d$ place the vertices so that $p(i) \ne p(j)$ for every edge $ij$.
A *truss* (or *pin-jointed framework*) is such a placement, read as follows.

1. Each vertex $i$ is a frictionless pin joint at $p(i)$, and each edge $ij$ is a straight bar from $p(i)$ to $p(j)$.
2. External forces $f_i \in \R^d$ act only at the joints.
3. Each bar $ij$ carries only an axial force $t_{ij} = t_{ji} \in \R$, positive in tension and negative in compression.

The truss is in equilibrium under $f$ when each free joint $i$ satisfies
$$\sum_{j : ij \in E} t_{ij} \, \frac{p(j) - p(i)}{\norm{p(j) - p(i)}} + f_i = 0.$$
Here a free joint is one that is not a support, and the denominator is the [[Euclidean distance|0NPPGHQNXQ]] of the two joints.

## Remarks

Example: three bars forming a triangle in the plane with joints $p(1) = (0, 0)$, $p(2) = (2, 0)$, $p(3) = (1, 1)$, pinned at joint $1$, on a roller at joint $2$, with a downward load $f_3 = (0, -F)$.
At joint $3$ the two bars point along $(-1, -1)/\sqrt{2}$ and $(1, -1)/\sqrt{2}$, and the horizontal balance gives $t_{13} = t_{23}$.
The vertical balance gives $-\sqrt{2}\, t_{13} - F = 0$, so both inclined bars are in compression with $t_{13} = t_{23} = -F/\sqrt{2}$.

Non-example: a portal frame whose columns and beam are welded rigidly at the corners.
Its members carry bending moments, so clause 3 fails, and it is a frame rather than a truss.
