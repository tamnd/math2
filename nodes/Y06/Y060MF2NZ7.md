## Statement

The system L derives eight basic laws of implication and negation, and it allows the chaining of implications.

Let L be the [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]] for formulas built from propositional variables with $\neg$ and $\to$.
Its axiom schemas are (A1) $\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)$, (A2) $(\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)) \to ((\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi))$ and (A3) $(\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi) \to ((\neg \psi \to \varphi) \to \psi)$.
Its only rule is [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] (MP).
For a set of formulas $\Gamma$ and a formula $\varphi$, $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ means that $\varphi$ has a derivation in L from $\Gamma$, and $\vdash \varphi$ means $\emptyset \vdash \varphi$.

For all formulas $\varphi$, $\psi$ and $\chi$, the following hold:
$$\begin{array}{ll} \text{(T1)} & \vdash \varphi \to \varphi, \\ \text{(T2)} & \vdash \neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi, \\ \text{(T3)} & \vdash \varphi \to \neg \neg \varphi, \\ \text{(T4)} & \vdash \neg \varphi \to (\varphi \to \psi), \\ \text{(T5)} & \vdash (\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi) \to (\varphi \to \psi), \\ \text{(T6)} & \vdash (\varphi \to \psi) \to (\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi), \\ \text{(T7)} & \vdash \varphi \to (\neg \psi \to \neg (\varphi \to \psi)), \\ \text{(T8)} & \vdash (\varphi \to \psi) \to ((\neg \varphi \to \psi) \to \psi). \end{array}$$
Also the derived rule (HS) holds: for each set $\Gamma$, if $\Gamma \vdash \varphi \to \psi$ and $\Gamma \vdash \psi \to \chi$, then $\Gamma \vdash \varphi \to \chi$.

## Remarks

### The labels

The labels T1 to T8 and HS are fixed, and other pages cite the laws by these labels.
The proof is in [[the proof of the basic theorems of L|XXK5Q5NHAR]].
T2 and T3 are the two directions of the [[double negation law|0NFAGWH84K]], and T5 and T6 are the two directions of the [[contrapositive|04T1CT7212]] law.
T4 says that a contradiction gives everything, and T8 is proof by cases.

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The lemma is about one fixed system, so its hypotheses are the three axiom schemas and the rule MP.
Without A3, the law T2 cannot be derived.
To see this, read $\to$ by its usual truth table, and read $\neg$ as the function that always takes the value $1$.
Then A1 and A2 always take the value $1$, and MP leads from formulas with the value $1$ to formulas with the value $1$.
So every formula derivable from A1 and A2 by MP always takes the value $1$.
But $\neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi$ takes the value $1 \to 0 = 0$ when $\varphi$ takes the value $0$.
This is the method of [[independence of axioms|729RCBPAM3]].
Without A1 and A2, the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] fails, and the proofs of T2 to T8 and HS use it.

### What it gives you

The lemma supplies the steps of both proofs of the [[completeness theorem for propositional logic|81ZNY1XKYJ]].
In Kalmár's proof, T1, A1, T3, T4 and T7 handle the cases of the induction on formulas, and T8 removes one variable at a time.
In the proof with maximal consistent sets, T4 and T7 give the truth lemma for $\to$.

### The converse

Each of T1 to T8 is a [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]], as the [[soundness theorem for propositional logic|XD629S54CS]] predicts.
Conversely, every tautology in $\neg$ and $\to$ is derivable in L.
That converse is the completeness theorem, and it needs much more work than the lemma.

## History

Frege's Begriffsschrift of 1879 already derived laws of this kind, among them both double negation laws, from his own axioms.
In Mendelson's textbook, the laws T2 to T8 appear as one lemma just after the deduction theorem, and they serve the proof of completeness by Kalmár's method.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The system L has only three axiom patterns and one rule, so even simple laws of logic need proofs in it.
This lemma proves eight such laws once, so that later proofs can use them as tools.
For example, T2 says that "not not $\varphi$" implies $\varphi$.
T8 says that if $\varphi$ gives $\psi$ and "not $\varphi$" also gives $\psi$, then $\psi$ holds in any case.
This is the formal form of a proof by cases.

### L2 human

We show how a later proof uses the lemma, with a derivation of $\neg \neg p \to \neg \neg (q \to p)$ for variables $p$ and $q$.

1. By T2, $\vdash \neg \neg p \to p$.
2. By A1, $\vdash p \to (q \to p)$.
3. By HS with steps 1 and 2, $\vdash \neg \neg p \to (q \to p)$.
4. By T3 with $\varphi := q \to p$, $\vdash (q \to p) \to \neg \neg (q \to p)$.
5. By HS with steps 3 and 4, $\vdash \neg \neg p \to \neg \neg (q \to p)$.

The typical use is as a library: a proof in L quotes T1 to T8 as lines, since the derivation of each one can be copied into the proof.
Watch out: T1 to T8 are schemas, and in step 4 the letter $\varphi$ stands for the compound formula $q \to p$.
Each instance must use the same substitution at every place.

### L3 human

Derived laws of this kind make a Hilbert system usable, and each textbook proves its own list of them for its own axioms.
In the [[Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra|QC9M3Q8B8Q]] of L, two formulas are identified when each one derives the other, and T2 and T3 say that $\varphi$ and $\neg \neg \varphi$ are identified.

In [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]], T1, T3, T4, T6 and T7 remain valid, but T2 and T5 do not.
T8 also fails there: with $\psi$ the formula $\varphi \lor \neg \varphi$, it would give the law of excluded middle.
The classical content of L comes from A3, and T2 is the first law whose proof uses A3.
