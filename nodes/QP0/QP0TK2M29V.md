## Summary

The electronic band structure of a crystal is the family of energy functions $k \mapsto E_m(k)$ of an electron in a periodic potential.
Here $m$ is a band number and $k$ is a wave vector in the [[Brillouin zone|EKTERAYFJ2]].
It comes from Bloch's theorem: eigenstates of a periodic Hamiltonian can be labelled by a crystal momentum $k$.
The allowed energies form intervals, the bands, which can be separated by forbidden intervals, the gaps.
Whether the Fermi energy falls in a band or in a gap decides whether the solid is a metal or an insulator.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A free electron can have any energy $E \ge 0$.
In a crystal the electron moves through a regular array of atoms, and waves scattered by the atoms interfere.
For most energies an electron wave still travels through the crystal, but for some energies the scattered waves cancel it, and no travelling state exists.
So the allowed energies come in bands with gaps between them.
Filling these bands with electrons, two per state, explains why copper conducts and diamond does not.
