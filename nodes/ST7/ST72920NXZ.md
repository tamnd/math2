## Definition

Let $G = (V, E)$ be an infinite, connected, vertex-transitive [[graph|00C0BR7D7H]] in which every vertex has finite degree, and let $o \in V$.
For $n \ge 1$, let $c_n$ be the number of [[self-avoiding walks|E2BVEB570S]] of length $n$ in $G$ that start at $o$.
The *connective constant* of $G$ is
$$\mu(G) = \lim_{n \to \infty} c_n^{1/n}.$$

## Remarks

The limit exists and does not depend on $o$.
By vertex-transitivity, $c_n$ is the same for every starting vertex.
A walk of length $m + n$ splits into a walk of length $m$ and a walk of length $n$ that starts at its endpoint, so $c_{m+n} \le c_m c_n$.
Thus $\log c_n$ is a [[subadditive|3X9DD9J3CV]] sequence, and [[Fekete's lemma|8A4GGX1MEY]] gives $\lim_n \frac{1}{n} \log c_n = \inf_n \frac1n \log c_n$.
In particular $c_n \ge \mu^n$ for all $n$.

Example: for the integer line $\Z$, a self-avoiding walk never changes direction, so $c_n = 2$ for all $n \ge 1$ and $\mu(\Z) = \lim 2^{1/n} = 1$.
For a $d$-regular graph, $c_n \le d (d - 1)^{n-1}$, so $\mu \le d - 1$; for $\Z^d$, walks that only move in positive coordinate directions give $\mu(\Z^d) \ge d$.

Non-example: on a finite graph, $c_n = 0$ for $n \ge \abs{V}$, so the growth rate is $0$ and carries no information; this is why $G$ is required to be infinite.

The value $\mu = \sqrt{2 + \sqrt 2}$ for the hexagonal lattice is the [[Duminil-Copin–Smirnov theorem|FRNXHYX2SH]].
