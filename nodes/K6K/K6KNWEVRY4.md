## Summary

Orbital elements are six numbers that fix a Kepler orbit and the position of the body on it.
Two give the size and shape of the conic and three give its orientation in space.
The last one gives the position along the orbit at a reference time.
They replace the position and velocity vectors by quantities that are constant for an unperturbed orbit.
This makes them the natural coordinates for describing slow changes caused by perturbations.
See the [[definition|QD69WK2YRG]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

To describe an orbit, give its size, its stretch, how it is tilted, and where the body is on it.
The semi-major axis says how big the ellipse is and the eccentricity how flat it is.
Three angles say how the plane of the ellipse is tilted and turned and where the ellipse points within its plane.
For example, a circular orbit over the equator has eccentricity $0$ and inclination $0$.
