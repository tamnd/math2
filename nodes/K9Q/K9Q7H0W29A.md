## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be an [[alphabet|RABRP2KG81]].
Let $\Sigma^+$ be the set of nonempty finite words over $\Sigma$, and $\Sigma^\omega$ the set of infinite words over $\Sigma$.
For $V \subseteq \Sigma^+$, let $V^\omega$ be the set of infinite words $v_1 v_2 v_3 \cdots$ with all $v_i \in V$.
For $U \subseteq \Sigma^*$ and $W \subseteq \Sigma^\omega$, let $UW = \set{u\alpha : u \in U,\ \alpha \in W}$.
A language $L \subseteq \Sigma^\omega$ is *omega-regular* when there are an integer $n \ge 0$ and [[regular languages|18WVBDCS38]] $U_1, \ldots, U_n \subseteq \Sigma^*$ and $V_1, \ldots, V_n \subseteq \Sigma^+$ such that
$$L = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} U_i V_i^\omega.$$

## Remarks

Example: over $\set{a, b}$, the set of infinite words with infinitely many $a$'s is omega-regular, as $(b^* a)^\omega$ with $n = 1$, $U_1 = \set{\varepsilon}$ and $V_1 = b^* a$.
Non-example: $L = \set{a^k b^k c^\omega : k \ge 0}$ over $\set{a, b, c}$ is not omega-regular.
For an omega-regular $L$, the set $\set{u \in \Sigma^* : u c^\omega \in L}$ is regular, and here it would be $\set{a^k b^k : k \ge 0} \cup \set{a^k b^k c^m : k \ge 0,\ m \ge 1}$, whose intersection with $a^* b^*$ is not regular.
By a theorem of Büchi, a language $L \subseteq \Sigma^\omega$ is omega-regular if and only if it is accepted by a nondeterministic [[Büchi automaton|92DM43HQZT]].
The condition $V_i \subseteq \Sigma^+$ excludes the empty word, for which an infinite repetition would not give an infinite word.
