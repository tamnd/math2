## Summary

Hydrology is the study of the movement, distribution and quality of water on and below the surface of the Earth.
It follows the water cycle of precipitation, infiltration, runoff, groundwater flow and evaporation.
Its mathematical side models river flow, flood waves, infiltration into soil, and flow through porous rock, the last based on [[Darcy's law|DX8RV2XG6H]].
It also uses statistics heavily, to estimate the probability of floods and droughts from limited records.
Hydrology serves water supply, flood protection, agriculture and the management of rivers and aquifers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Rain falls on the land; some soaks into the ground, some runs off into streams, and some evaporates back into the air.
Hydrology follows each of these paths and asks how much water goes where, and how fast.
A hydrologist might predict how high a river will rise after a storm, or how far the water table will drop around a pumping well.
For example, water in sand moves only metres per day underground, while a flood wave can travel down a river many kilometres in an hour.
