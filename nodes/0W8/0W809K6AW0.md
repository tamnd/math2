## Definition

We define the matrix whose rows are the columns of a given matrix.
Let $R$ be a ring and let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $m \times n$ [[matrix|0N1MY1T0W9]] over $R$.
The *transpose* of $A$ is the $n \times m$ matrix $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ with entries
$$(A^{\mathsf{T}})_{ij} = a_{ji}, \qquad 1 \le i \le n, \quad 1 \le j \le m.$$

## Notation

Other common notations are $A^T$, $A^t$, ${}^t\!A$, $A^{\top}$ and $A'$.
In Mathlib, `Matrix.transpose A` is written `Aᵀ`.

## Remarks

The definition exchanges the two indices, so the entry in position $(j, i)$ of $A$ moves to position $(i, j)$ of $A^{\mathsf{T}}$.
That is why the size changes from $m \times n$ to $n \times m$: the index $i$ of $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ runs over the columns of $A$, and $j$ runs over the rows.
The diagonal entries, with $i = j$, do not move.

For example, the transpose of $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$.
A tempting wrong answer is $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 6 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, which is a quarter turn of the array; it fails the rule, since its entry $(1, 1)$ is $3$ and not $a_{11} = 1$.

The rules $(A^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}} = A$, $(A + B)^{\mathsf{T}} = A^{\mathsf{T}} + B^{\mathsf{T}}$ and $(cA)^{\mathsf{T}} = c A^{\mathsf{T}}$ follow at once from the entry formula, over any ring.
The rule $(AB)^{\mathsf{T}} = B^{\mathsf{T}} A^{\mathsf{T}}$ needs a commutative ring; see the [[transpose of a product|09GK5PS824]].
The concept page on the [[transpose|09DXWYRD43]] gives the uses, and the [[matrix of the dual map is the transpose|0ZPB3180AY]] of the matrix of the map.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The transpose turns the rows of a matrix into its columns.
Row 1 becomes column 1, row 2 becomes column 2, and so on.
For a square matrix, this is a flip across the diagonal from the top left to the bottom right.

### L2 human

To write down $A^{\mathsf{T}}$, read $A$ column by column and write each column as a row.
For $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 5 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, the columns are $(1, 2, -1)$ and $(0, 5, 3)$, so $A^{\mathsf{T}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
Check one entry with the formula: $(A^{\mathsf{T}})_{23} = a_{32} = 3$.
