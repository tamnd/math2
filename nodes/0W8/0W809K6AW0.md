## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring and let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $m \times n$ [[matrix|0N1MY1T0W9]] over $R$.
The *transpose* of $A$ is the $n \times m$ matrix $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ with entries
$$(A^{\mathsf{T}})_{ij} = a_{ji}, \qquad 1 \le i \le n, \ 1 \le j \le m.$$

## Notation

- Other notations are $A^T$, $A^t$, ${}^t\!A$, $A'$ and $A^{\top}$.
- In Mathlib, `Matrix.transpose A` is written `Aᵀ`.

## Remarks

- The transpose of $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$.
- The rules $(A^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}} = A$, $(A + B)^{\mathsf{T}} = A^{\mathsf{T}} + B^{\mathsf{T}}$ and $(cA)^{\mathsf{T}} = c A^{\mathsf{T}}$ hold, the last one over a commutative ring.
- Over a commutative ring, $(AB)^{\mathsf{T}} = B^{\mathsf{T}} A^{\mathsf{T}}$; see the [[transpose of a product|09GK5PS824]].
- The [[matrix of the dual map is the transpose|0ZPB3180AY]] of the matrix of the map.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The transpose turns the rows of a matrix into its columns.
Row $i$ of $A$ becomes column $i$ of $A^{\mathsf{T}}$.

### L2 human

The definition exchanges the two indices, so the entry at $(i, j)$ moves to $(j, i)$.
The diagonal entries, with $i = j$, do not move.
