## Summary

A sorting network is a fixed sequence of compare-and-swap operations on $n$ positions that sorts every input of length $n$.
Unlike a general [[sorting algorithm|MGKDQAM7K3]], it makes comparisons that do not depend on the data.
So it can be built as hardware and run in parallel.
The main measures are the size, the number of comparators, and the depth, the number of parallel rounds.
The [[zero–one principle|FMR0TZ9Z88]] reduces the check that a network sorts to inputs of $0$'s and $1$'s.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture $n$ horizontal wires carrying numbers from left to right.
A comparator is a vertical bar joining two wires: it puts the smaller number on the upper wire and the larger on the lower wire.
A sorting network is an arrangement of bars such that the numbers always come out in order, whatever came in.
For three wires, comparing wires $1$ and $2$, then $2$ and $3$, then $1$ and $2$ again sorts every input.
