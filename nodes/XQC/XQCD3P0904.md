## Definition

Let $I \subseteq \R$ be an open interval, let $n \ge 1$, and let $A \colon I \to \R^{n \times n}$ be continuous.
For $t_0 \in I$, the *state-transition matrix* $\Phi(\cdot, t_0) \colon I \to \R^{n \times n}$ is the unique solution of the matrix [[differential equation|0NT64JV96A]]
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \Phi(t, t_0) = A(t) \, \Phi(t, t_0), \qquad \Phi(t_0, t_0) = I_n,$$
where $I_n$ is the identity matrix.
The [[Picard–Lindelöf theorem|0P6KRD5B7A]] gives a unique local solution, and because the equation is linear with continuous coefficients this solution extends to all of $I$.

## Remarks

The solution of $x' = A(t) x$ with $x(t_0) = x_0$ is $x(t) = \Phi(t, t_0) x_0$.
For a continuous $B \colon I \to \R^{n \times m}$ and a control $u$, the solution of $x' = A(t) x + B(t) u(t)$ with $x(t_0) = x_0$ is
$$x(t) = \Phi(t, t_0) x_0 + \int_{t_0}^{t} \Phi(t, s) B(s) u(s) \, ds.$$
It satisfies $\Phi(t, s) \Phi(s, r) = \Phi(t, r)$ and $\Phi(t, s)^{-1} = \Phi(s, t)$.
For any [[fundamental matrix|0H1GN6P9R5]] $X$ of the system, $\Phi(t, t_0) = X(t) X(t_0)^{-1}$.
Example: for a constant matrix $A$, $\Phi(t, t_0) = e^{A(t - t_0)}$, the [[matrix exponential|020M04FN1H]]; indeed its derivative in $t$ is $A e^{A(t - t_0)}$ and its value at $t_0$ is $I_n$.
Non-example: in general $\exp\left(\int_{t_0}^{t} A(s) \, ds\right)$ is not the state-transition matrix; it is when the matrices $A(t)$ and $A(s)$ commute for all $t, s$.
