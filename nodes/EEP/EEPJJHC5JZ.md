## Summary

A P-wave is a compressional elastic wave in a solid.
Its particles move back and forth along the direction in which the wave travels.
In a homogeneous isotropic solid it travels at the speed $c_P = \sqrt{(\lambda + 2\mu)/\rho}$, the fastest speed of any wave in [[elastodynamics|T1PYEBKBAJ]].
The name comes from seismology, where P stands for primary: these are the first waves of an earthquake to reach a seismometer.
P-waves also travel through fluids, where they are ordinary sound waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Push the end of a stretched slinky sharply and a bunch of tightly packed coils runs along it.
Each coil moves only a little forward and back, in the same direction as the bunch travels.
A P-wave in rock is the same kind of motion: the rock is squeezed and stretched in the direction of travel.
In granite it moves at around six kilometres per second.
