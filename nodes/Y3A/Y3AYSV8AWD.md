## Summary

A cavity resonator is a closed region with conducting walls in which electromagnetic fields can oscillate at certain discrete resonant frequencies.
Mathematically, its resonances are the eigenvalues of the Maxwell operator on a bounded domain with a perfect-conductor boundary condition.
Real cavities lose a little energy in the walls, which is measured by the quality factor $Q$.
Cavities are used as filters, frequency references, and in particle accelerators and microwave ovens.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A cavity resonator is a metal box that rings with electromagnetic waves, like a bell rings with sound.
Waves bounce between the walls, and only those that fit the box exactly reinforce themselves.
These fitting patterns are the modes, each with its own frequency.
A cube of side $10$ cm has its lowest resonance at about $2.1$ GHz, close to the frequency of a microwave oven.
