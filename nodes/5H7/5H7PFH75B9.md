## Proof

We show that every term names itself in $\mathcal{M}_H$, derive the truth conditions for each type of sentence, and then push truth up from the atoms of $H$ by induction on a rank.
Let $L$, $T_L$, $H$ and $\mathcal{M}_H$ be as in [[Hintikka's lemma|2A6QP78XN3]], write $\mathcal{M}$ for $\mathcal{M}_H$, and write $t^{\mathcal{M}}$ for the value of a closed term $t$.

Part A: the structure and its terms.

1. The language $L$ has a constant symbol $c$, and $c \in T_L$.
   So the domain $T_L$ is nonempty, and $\mathcal{M}$ is an $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]].
2. Each $n$-ary function symbol $f$ gets a map $T_L^n \to T_L$, because $f(t_1, \dots, t_n)$ is closed when $t_1, \dots, t_n$ are closed.
3. We claim $t^{\mathcal{M}} = t$ for every closed term $t$, by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $t$.
   For a constant $c$, $c^{\mathcal{M}} = c$ by the definition of $\mathcal{M}$.
   For $t = f(t_1, \dots, t_n)$ with $t_i^{\mathcal{M}} = t_i$, the definitions of term value and of $f^{\mathcal{M}}$ give
   $$t^{\mathcal{M}} = f^{\mathcal{M}}(t_1^{\mathcal{M}}, \dots, t_n^{\mathcal{M}}) = f^{\mathcal{M}}(t_1, \dots, t_n) = f(t_1, \dots, t_n) = t.$$

Part B: the truth conditions in $\mathcal{M}$.

4. Atomic sentences.
   By the definition of [[satisfaction|2RBQPV02AS]], $\mathcal{M} \models R(t_1, \dots, t_n)$ exactly when $(t_1^{\mathcal{M}}, \dots, t_n^{\mathcal{M}}) \in R^{\mathcal{M}}$.
   By step 3 this tuple is $(t_1, \dots, t_n)$, and by the definition of $R^{\mathcal{M}}$ it lies in $R^{\mathcal{M}}$ exactly when $R(t_1, \dots, t_n) \in H$.
   So an atomic sentence $A$ is true in $\mathcal{M}$ exactly when $A \in H$.
5. Instances.
   Let $\varphi$ have only $x$ free, and let $t \in T_L$.
   A closed term is free for $x$, so the substitution lemma for [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]] gives: $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[t/x]$ exactly when $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s]$ for an assignment $s$ with $s(x) = t^{\mathcal{M}} = t$.
6. Quantifiers.
   Every element of the domain is a closed term, so by step 5, $\mathcal{M} \models \forall x\, \varphi$ exactly when $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[t/x]$ for every $t \in T_L$.
   In the same way, $\mathcal{M} \models \exists x\, \varphi$ exactly when $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[t/x]$ for some $t \in T_L$.
7. The $\alpha$ and $\beta$ sentences.
   The truth tables of $\neg, \wedge, \vee, \to$ give, case by case in the table of [[semantic tableaux|WGWBBVE8GX]]: $\alpha$ is true exactly when $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ are true, and $\beta$ is true exactly when $\beta_1$ or $\beta_2$ is true.
   For example, $\varphi \to \psi$ is true exactly when $\neg\varphi$ is true or $\psi$ is true.
8. The $\gamma$ and $\delta$ sentences.
   By step 6, $\forall x\, \varphi$ is true exactly when every $\varphi[t/x]$ is true, and $\neg\exists x\, \varphi$ is true exactly when every $\neg\varphi[t/x]$ is true.
   So $\gamma$ is true exactly when $\gamma(t)$ is true for every $t \in T_L$.
   In the same way, $\delta$ is true exactly when $\delta(t)$ is true for some $t \in T_L$.

Part C: the rank.

9. The rank $r(\varphi)$ is the number of occurrences of $\neg, \wedge, \vee, \to, \forall, \exists$ in $\varphi$.
   A substitution of a term does not change it, so $r(\varphi[t/x]) = r(\varphi)$.
10. Every component and instance has smaller rank than its sentence.
    With $a = r(\varphi)$ and $b = r(\psi)$, the sentence $\neg(\varphi \vee \psi)$ has rank $a + b + 2$ and components of rank $a + 1$ and $b + 1$.
    The sentence $\varphi \to \psi$ has rank $a + b + 1$ and components $\neg\varphi$ and $\psi$ of rank $a + 1$ and $b$.
    The sentence $\neg\exists x\, \varphi$ has rank $a + 2$ and instances of rank $a + 1$, by step 9.
    The other rows of the table are checked in the same way, and in each one the rank drops by at least $1$.
11. Every sentence is exactly one of: atomic, a negated atom, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ or $\delta$, since its main symbol decides the type.

Part D: the induction.

12. We prove by strong [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $n$ that every $\varphi \in H$ with $r(\varphi) = n$ is true in $\mathcal{M}$.
    We assume the claim for all smaller ranks and argue by [[cases|0WCP0PVET6]] on the type of $\varphi$, using step 11.
13. If $\varphi$ is atomic, then $\varphi \in H$, and step 4 gives $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$.
14. If $\varphi = \neg A$ with $A$ atomic, condition 1 of a [[Hintikka set|RJNDEG07R1]] gives $A \notin H$.
    Step 4 gives $\mathcal{M} \not\models A$, so $\mathcal{M} \models \neg A$.
15. If $\varphi$ is an $\alpha$ sentence, condition 2 gives $\alpha_1, \alpha_2 \in H$.
    Both have smaller rank by step 10, so both are true by the induction hypothesis, and step 7 gives $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$.
16. If $\varphi$ is a $\beta$ sentence, condition 3 gives $\beta_i \in H$ for some $i$.
    It is true by step 10 and the induction hypothesis, and step 7 gives $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$.
17. If $\varphi$ is a $\gamma$ sentence, condition 4 gives $\gamma(t) \in H$ for every $t \in T_L$.
    Each is true by step 10 and the induction hypothesis, and step 8 gives $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$.
18. If $\varphi$ is a $\delta$ sentence, condition 5 gives $\delta(t) \in H$ for some $t \in T_L$.
    It is true by step 10 and the induction hypothesis, and step 8 gives $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$.

Every sentence of $H$ has some rank, so steps 12 to 18 give $\mathcal{M}_H \models \varphi$ for every $\varphi \in H$.
So $\mathcal{M}_H$ is a model of $H$, and $H$ is satisfiable.

## Idea

The model takes the closed terms as its elements, so every element has a name, and it makes an atom true exactly when the atom is in $H$.
Because every element has a name, a quantified sentence is true exactly when the right instances are true.
The conditions of a Hintikka set then push truth from the atoms of $H$ up to all of its members.
The induction runs on the rank and not on subformulas, because an instance $\varphi[t/x]$ is not a subformula of $\forall x\, \varphi$, though it has smaller rank.

## Variants

With the equality symbol, the same proof works on the quotient of $T_L$ by $s \sim t \iff (s = t) \in H$, under the extra conditions (E1) and (E2) in the remarks of the statement.
Step 4 then also covers the atomic sentences $s = t$.
For propositional logic, Parts A and B reduce to the truth tables, the model is the valuation that makes a letter true exactly when it is in $H$, and the induction is on the length of formulas.
Another proof first extends $H$ so that it contains each atomic sentence or its negation, and then reads off the model; that extension is not needed here.
