## Summary

Thermoelasticity is the theory of elastic bodies in which deformation and temperature interact.
A change of temperature makes a body expand or contract, and when this expansion is prevented thermal stresses arise; conversely, fast deformation changes the temperature.
Its constitutive law is the [[Duhamel–Neumann law|RT1JE5V0PW]], which adds a thermal term to [[Hooke's law|V56XAESDNE]].
The theory couples the equations of [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] with the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]].
It explains cracking of glass under sudden heating, the design of expansion joints, and thermoelastic damping.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heat a metal rod and it gets longer.
If the rod is clamped between two walls so that it cannot get longer, it pushes on the walls instead, and that push is a thermal stress.
Pour boiling water into a cold glass and the inside expands while the outside does not, which can crack the glass.
For a clamped steel bar, a rise of $50$ degrees already gives a stress of about $120$ MPa, using $E \approx 200$ GPa and $\alpha \approx 12 \times 10^{-6}$ per degree.
