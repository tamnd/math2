## Statement

Let $p \in \C^n$ and let $(X,p)$ be a [[germ of an analytic set|Q9B74BYZ8C]] at $p$ that has pure codimension $1$.

Then there is an open neighbourhood $U$ of $p$ and a representative $X \subset U$ of the germ $(X,p)$, which is an [[analytic subset|VMDTGTMN9P]] of $U$.

There are also analytic subsets $X_1, \ldots, X_k$ of $U$, each of [[pure dimension|C2R5Q2Q717]] equal to $n-1$, with the following two properties.

1. For every $\ell$, the set of [[regular points|JPP8N6MZGP]] of $X_\ell$ is connected.
2. $X = X_1 \cup \cdots \cup X_k$.

## Remarks

So near $p$, a hypervariety is a finite union of hypervarieties, and each one is connected away from its singular points.

Example: the germ of $\set{z_1 z_2 = 0}$ in $\C^2$ at the origin is the union of the two axes. Each axis has connected regular part.
