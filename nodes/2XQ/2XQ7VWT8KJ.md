## Summary

A Bayesian game is a game in which players have private information about payoffs, modelled by types drawn from a common prior distribution.
John Harsanyi introduced the model in 1967–1968, turning games of incomplete information into games with a move of chance.
Its main solution concept is Bayesian Nash equilibrium, in which each type of each player best responds given her beliefs about the other players' types.
Auctions, signalling and voting with private information are all modelled as Bayesian games.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In many real games you do not know exactly what the other players want or know.
A bidder in an auction does not know how much the other bidders value the object.
The trick of a Bayesian game is to imagine that chance first deals each player a private card, her type, which says what she values or knows.
Everyone knows the odds with which the cards are dealt, but each player sees only her own card.
Then each player plans what to do for each card she might hold.
