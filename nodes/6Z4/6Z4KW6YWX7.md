## Definition

The *Milankovitch cycles* are the quasi-periodic variations of three orbital parameters that control the insolation of the Earth.
They are caused by the gravitational pull of the other planets and of the Moon.
The three parameters are:

1. the [[eccentricity|0HVX863SAJ]] $e$ of the Earth's orbit, with main periods of about $100\,000$ and $405\,000$ years;
2. the obliquity $\varepsilon$, the angle between the rotation axis and the normal to the orbital plane. It varies between about $22.1^\circ$ and $24.5^\circ$, with a main period of about $41\,000$ years;
3. the climatic precession $e \sin \varpi$, where $\varpi$ is the longitude of perihelion measured from the moving vernal equinox. It combines the [[axial precession|G0HP8ED7NW]] with the motion of the perihelion, with main periods of about $19\,000$ to $23\,000$ years.

## Remarks

Example: by [[Kepler's second law|M1A7XV292M]], the mean over one year of $1/r^2$, with $r$ the distance from the Sun, equals $1/\big( a^2 \sqrt{1 - e^2} \big)$, where $a$ is the semi-major axis.
So the global annual mean insolation is $S_0 / \big( 4 \sqrt{1 - e^2} \big)$, with $S_0$ the solar flux at distance $a$.
For $e$ between $0$ and $0.06$ this changes by less than $0.2\%$; the cycles act mainly by redistributing sunlight between seasons and latitudes, as clauses 2 and 3 do.

Non-example: the 11-year cycle of solar activity changes the output of the Sun, not the orbit or the axis of the Earth, so it is not a Milankovitch cycle.

Milanković argued that the summer insolation at high northern latitudes, near $65^\circ$ N, controls the growth and decay of ice sheets.
Spectral analysis of oxygen isotope records from deep-sea sediments, in work by Hays, Imbrie and Shackleton published in 1976, found the periods of all three cycles.
