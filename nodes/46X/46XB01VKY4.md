## Summary

The coefficient of restitution of a collision is the ratio of the relative speed of separation to the relative speed of approach.
It is $1$ for an [[elastic collision|NSF6MKF91K]] and $0$ for a perfectly [[inelastic collision|1BFJYGPGB3]] in which the bodies stick.
Newton's experimental rule treats it as a constant of the pair of materials.
Together with conservation of momentum, it then determines the outcome of a head-on collision.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Drop a ball on a hard floor and watch how high it bounces.
A superball bounces almost back to your hand, a tennis ball less, and a lump of clay not at all.
The coefficient of restitution measures this: it is the ratio of the speed just after the bounce to the speed just before.
If a ball dropped from $1$ m bounces to $0.64$ m, its speeds are in the ratio $0.8$, so the coefficient is $0.8$.
