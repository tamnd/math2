## Summary

Vincenty's formulae are two iterative methods, published by Thaddeus Vincenty in 1975, for the [[geodesic problems on an ellipsoid|KA7Z56ZFPQ]].
The direct method finds the endpoint of a geodesic of given start, azimuth and length.
The inverse method finds the length and azimuths of the geodesic between two given points.
They are accurate to about $0.5$ mm on the Earth ellipsoid, but the inverse iteration may fail for nearly antipodal points.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Vincenty's formulae compute distances on a squashed Earth, not on a round one.
They map the ellipsoid onto an auxiliary sphere, where a geodesic becomes a great circle.
On that sphere, the longitude difference must be corrected for the flattening, and the correction is found by repeated guessing.
After a few rounds the guess stops changing, and series in the flattening give the distance.
For two points in Europe a few rounds suffice, but for two nearly opposite points the guessing may never settle.
