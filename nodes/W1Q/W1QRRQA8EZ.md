## Summary

Consider a binary star on a circular orbit.
The Roche lobe of a star is the region around it in which material is bound to that star rather than to its companion.
Its boundary is a critical surface of equal effective potential in the frame that rotates with the binary.
This surface passes through the inner Lagrange point.
A star that grows larger than its Roche lobe pours gas through the inner Lagrange point onto its companion.
This mass transfer drives the evolution of close binaries, cataclysmic variables and X-ray binaries.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two stars circle each other, and each pulls on the gas around it.
Picture the landscape of the combined pull, seen from a merry-go-round that turns with the stars.
Each star sits at the bottom of a valley, and the two valleys meet at a pass between them.
The Roche lobe of a star is everything in its valley up to the height of the pass, a teardrop shape with its tip at the pass.
For two stars of equal mass the two lobes are mirror images, and the pass sits halfway between the stars.
A star that swells beyond its lobe spills gas over the pass to the other star.
