## Statement

Let $A$ be a real $m \times n$ matrix, let $b \in \R^m$ and let $c \in \R^n$.
Consider the primal program "maximize $c^T x$ subject to $A x \le b$, $x \ge 0$" and its [[dual linear program|14VMJNXMDC]] "minimize $b^T y$ subject to $A^T y \ge c$, $y \ge 0$".
If $x \in \R^n$ is feasible for the primal and $y \in \R^m$ is feasible for the dual, then
$$c^T x \le b^T y.$$

## Remarks

Proof: since $x \ge 0$ and $A^T y \ge c$, one has $c^T x \le (A^T y)^T x = y^T A x$.
Since $y \ge 0$ and $A x \le b$, one has $y^T A x \le y^T b$.

Consequences: if the primal is unbounded, the dual is infeasible, and if the dual is unbounded, the primal is infeasible.
If feasible $x$ and $y$ satisfy $c^T x = b^T y$, then both are optimal.

Example: for "maximize $x_1 + x_2$ subject to $x_1 + 2 x_2 \le 4$, $3 x_1 + x_2 \le 6$, $x \ge 0$", the dual point $y = (1, 0)$ is feasible, since $1 \ge 1$ and $2 \ge 1$.
It shows that every primal feasible $x$ has $x_1 + x_2 \le 4$.

The sign condition $y \ge 0$ is needed, since it lets one multiply $A x \le b$ by $y$.
For "maximize $x_1$ subject to $x_1 \le 1$, $-x_1 \le 0$, $x_1 \ge 0$", the vector $y = (0, -1)$ satisfies $A^T y = 1 \ge 1$ but not $y \ge 0$.
Its value $b^T y = 0$ is smaller than $c^T x = 1$ at the feasible point $x_1 = 1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Any feasible point of the dual gives an upper bound on what the primal can achieve.
So the best primal value is always at most the best dual value.
This makes dual solutions useful as certificates: they prove that no primal point can be better than a given number.

### L2 human

Weak duality is a two-line computation, but it is the starting point of duality theory.
The strong duality theorem adds that the two optimal values are actually equal when either one is finite.
The same argument works for Lagrangian duality in nonlinear programming, where it gives the duality gap $\ge 0$.
