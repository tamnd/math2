## Summary

This topic is a part of [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].
It covers the logical form of definitions, the kinds of definitions, the need to show that a definition makes sense, and the choice between equivalent definitions.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Mathematical definition|~d1d36166]]: the logical form of a definition, and the claims that a definition can hide.
2. [[Kinds of definitions|~917f5106]]: explicit, recursive, by abstraction, axiomatic and by universal property, with the obligation of each kind.
3. [[Well-defined|~a33d83e5]]: a definition by representatives needs a proof that the result does not depend on the choice.
4. [[Equivalent definitions|~9724d86e]]: two conditions that single out the same objects, and how to prove it.
5. [[Definite descriptions in definitions|~5d2f5192]]: a definition with "the" needs existence and uniqueness.
6. [[Up to|~2311cd14]]: uniqueness relative to an equivalence relation.
7. [[Primitive notion|~d06afda5]]: the undefined terms at the base of a theory, fixed by axioms.
8. [[Choosing good definitions|~eef58dc7]]: why definitions take their current form, and how counterexamples change them.

### Prerequisites

1. [[The language of mathematical statements|WMY13MXY3W]]: quantifiers, connectives and the biconditional.
2. [[Mathematical proof|0B94TKFQJX]]: what a proof is, because each hidden claim of a definition needs one.
3. [[Proof methods|G9JFJGY4TQ]]: direct proof, contradiction and contrapositive, which the uniqueness and well-definedness proofs use.
