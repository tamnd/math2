## Example

Let $\mathfrak{M}$ have worlds $w, v, x, y$ with $w R v$, $v R x$ and $v R y$ only.
Let $\mathfrak{N}$ have worlds $w', v_1, v_2, x', y'$ with $w' R' v_1$, $w' R' v_2$, $v_1 R' x'$ and $v_2 R' y'$ only.
Let $p$ be true exactly at $x$ and $x'$, and let $q$ be true exactly at $y$ and $y'$.
We show that $w$ and $w'$ have the same paths of atoms but are not [[bisimilar|7T740SFHHV]].

1. The paths from $w$ are $w$, $wv$, $wvx$ and $wvy$. Their sequences of sets of true variables are $(\emptyset)$, $(\emptyset, \emptyset)$, $(\emptyset, \emptyset, \set{p})$ and $(\emptyset, \emptyset, \set{q})$.
2. The paths from $w'$ are $w'$, $w'v_1$, $w'v_2$, $w'v_1x'$ and $w'v_2y'$. They give the same four sequences.
3. Let $\varphi = \lozenge(\lozenge p \wedge \lozenge q)$. At $v$, both $\lozenge p$ and $\lozenge q$ are true, so $\varphi$ is true at $w$.
4. At $v_1$, the only successor is $x'$, where $q$ is false, so $\lozenge q$ is false at $v_1$.
5. At $v_2$, the only successor is $y'$, where $p$ is false, so $\lozenge p$ is false at $v_2$.
6. So $\lozenge p \wedge \lozenge q$ is false at both successors of $w'$, and $\varphi$ is false at $w'$.
7. By [[bisimulation invariance|TKFFTAX30E]], bisimilar worlds agree on $\varphi$. So $w$ and $w'$ are not bisimilar.

## Remarks

The example shows that bisimilarity is finer than equality of the sets of paths.
The failure is visible in the game: Spoiler moves $w' R' v_1$, Duplicator must answer $w R v$, and Spoiler then moves $v R y$, which $v_1$ cannot answer.
The order of the choices matters: $\mathfrak{M}$ postpones the choice between $p$ and $q$, and $\mathfrak{N}$ makes it at the first step.
If the variables $p$ and $q$ are both replaced by one variable $r$, the two models become bisimilar, by the relation that pairs $w$ with $w'$, $v$ with $v_1$ and $v_2$, and the four leaves with each other.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two vending machines both take a coin, and then give tea or coffee.
The first machine lets the customer choose the drink after the coin.
The second machine chooses the drink by itself at the moment it takes the coin.
Both can produce the same runs, but only the first one always lets the customer get both choices after paying.
Bisimulation sees this difference, and the list of possible runs does not.

### L2 human

1. Play the bisimulation game from $(w, w')$.
2. Spoiler moves in $\mathfrak{N}$ from $w'$ to $v_1$.
3. Duplicator must move in $\mathfrak{M}$ from $w$ to $v$.
4. Spoiler moves in $\mathfrak{M}$ from $v$ to $y$, where $q$ is true.
5. Duplicator must move from $v_1$, and its only successor $x'$ has $q$ false. So Duplicator loses.

The typical use is in process theory, where bisimilarity separates processes with the same runs but different branching.
A trap: two models can also satisfy the same formulas of the form $\lozenge \lozenge p$ and $\lozenge \lozenge q$ and still differ; only formulas that mix $\lozenge$ and $\wedge$ under a $\lozenge$ separate them.
