## Example

Two models can produce exactly the same runs and still differ in when a choice is made, and bisimulation sees that difference.
Let $\mathfrak{M}$ have worlds $w, v, x, y$ with $w R v$, $v R x$ and $v R y$ only.
Let $\mathfrak{N}$ have worlds $w', v_1, v_2, x', y'$ with $w' R' v_1$, $w' R' v_2$, $v_1 R' x'$ and $v_2 R' y'$ only.
Let $p$ be true exactly at $x$ and $x'$, and let $q$ be true exactly at $y$ and $y'$.
We show that $w$ and $w'$ have the same paths of atoms but are not [[bisimilar|7T740SFHHV]].

1. The paths from $w$ are $w$, $wv$, $wvx$ and $wvy$.
   Their sequences of true variables are $(\emptyset)$, $(\emptyset, \emptyset)$, $(\emptyset, \emptyset, \{p\})$ and $(\emptyset, \emptyset, \{q\})$.
2. The paths from $w'$ are $w'$, $w'v_1$, $w'v_2$, $w'v_1x'$ and $w'v_2y'$.
   They give the same four sequences, with $(\emptyset, \emptyset)$ produced twice.
3. Let $\varphi = \lozenge(\lozenge p \wedge \lozenge q)$.
   At $v$ both $\lozenge p$ and $\lozenge q$ are true, so $\varphi$ is true at $w$.
4. At $v_1$ the only successor is $x'$, where $q$ is false, so $\lozenge q$ is false at $v_1$.
5. At $v_2$ the only successor is $y'$, where $p$ is false, so $\lozenge p$ is false at $v_2$.
6. So $\lozenge p \wedge \lozenge q$ is false at both successors of $w'$, and $\varphi$ is false at $w'$.
7. Bisimilar worlds agree on every formula, by [[bisimulation invariance|TKFFTAX30E]], so $w$ and $w'$ are not bisimilar.

## Remarks

The example shows that bisimilarity is finer than equality of the sets of runs, which process theory calls trace equivalence.
The difference is the timing of a choice.
The model $\mathfrak{M}$ postpones the choice between $p$ and $q$ until after the first step, and $\mathfrak{N}$ makes it at the first step.

The failure is visible in the game too.
Spoiler moves $w \to v$, and Duplicator must pick $v_1$ or $v_2$.
If she picks $v_1$, Spoiler moves $v \to y$, and $v_1$ can only reach $x'$, where $q$ is false; the case $v_2$ is symmetric, with $x$ in place of $y$.

Change the atoms and the difference disappears.
If $p$ and $q$ are both replaced by one variable $r$, the models become bisimilar, by the relation that pairs $w$ with $w'$, $v$ with $v_1$ and with $v_2$, and each leaf of $\mathfrak{M}$ with each leaf of $\mathfrak{N}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two vending machines both take a coin and then give tea or coffee.
The first lets you choose the drink after you pay.
The second chooses the drink by itself at the moment it takes the coin.
Both can produce the same runs, "coin, tea" and "coin, coffee".
Only the first one always lets you have either drink after paying, and bisimulation sees this, while the list of runs does not.

### L2 human

Here is a second winning play for Spoiler, which starts on the other side.
Start at $(w, w')$, and let Spoiler move in $\mathfrak{N}$ from $w'$ to $v_1$.
Duplicator must move in $\mathfrak{M}$ from $w$ to $v$, her only choice.
Now Spoiler switches sides and moves in $\mathfrak{M}$ from $v$ to $y$, where $q$ is true.
Duplicator must move from $v_1$, and its only successor $x'$ has $q$ false, so she loses.
This play gives the separating formula $\lozenge \square \neg q$, which is true at $w'$ through $v_1$ and false at $w$, since $v$ sees $y$.
This is the typical use in process theory: bisimilarity separates processes with the same runs but different branching.
Watch out: formulas such as $\lozenge \lozenge p$ and $\lozenge \lozenge q$ are true at both roots, so you need a formula that puts $\wedge$ under a $\lozenge$ to see the difference.
