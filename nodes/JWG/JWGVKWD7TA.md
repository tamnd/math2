## Summary

The Coulomb gauge is the [[gauge fixing|TXD7MTYQYS]] condition that the [[magnetic vector potential|8PEMR2J7EF]] has zero divergence.
In this gauge the scalar potential is the instantaneous Coulomb potential of the charge distribution, found from [[Poisson's equation|DWK5N5SKTK]].
The vector potential is then purely transverse, which suits magnetostatics, radiation in the absence of charges and the quantization of the electromagnetic field.
Unlike the Lorenz gauge, it singles out a frame of reference.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Coulomb gauge is a rule that removes the part of the vector potential that spreads out from points or flows into them.
What remains swirls around, like the flow of an incompressible fluid.
With this rule the electric potential at each instant is just the one computed from the charges at that instant by Coulomb's law.
All the effects of time delay and of radiation are then carried by the vector potential.
