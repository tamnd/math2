## Definition

Let $K$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]].
Let $A = \bigoplus_{n \in \Z} A^n$ be a [[graded|8KB4SQGC0C]] [[associative algebra|3CTW1P4HBP]] over $K$ with unit $1$ that is graded commutative: $ab = (-1)^{\abs{a}\abs{b}} ba$ for homogeneous $a, b$ of degrees $\abs{a}, \abs{b}$.
For homogeneous $a$, let $L_a \colon A \to A$ be the map $x \mapsto ax$, of degree $\abs{a}$.
For linear maps $S, T$ of degrees $s, t$, let $[S, T] = ST - (-1)^{st} TS$.
A *Batalin–Vilkovisky algebra* is such an $A$ with a linear map $\Delta \colon A \to A$ such that:

1. $\Delta(A^n) \subseteq A^{n-1}$ for all $n$.
2. $\Delta \circ \Delta = 0$.
3. $\Delta$ is a differential operator of order at most $2$: $[[[\Delta, L_a], L_b], L_c] = 0$ for all homogeneous $a, b, c \in A$.

## Remarks

Many authors also require $\Delta(1) = 0$.
Then the bracket given, up to a sign convention, by $\Delta(ab) - \Delta(a)\, b - (-1)^{\abs{a}} a\, \Delta(b)$ makes $A$ a [[Gerstenhaber algebra|GJPPS5N213]], and $\Delta$ is a derivation of this bracket.

Example: $A = K[x_1, \ldots, x_n] \otimes \Lambda[\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n]$, with each $x_i$ of degree $0$ and each $\xi_i$ of degree $1$, and $\Delta = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x_i \partial \xi_i}$.
Here $\Delta$ lowers degree by $1$.
It is a sum of products of two derivations, hence of order at most $2$.
In $\Delta^2$ the terms for $i \ne j$ cancel in pairs because $\partial_{\xi_i}$ and $\partial_{\xi_j}$ anticommute, and the terms for $i = j$ vanish because $\partial_{\xi_i}^2 = 0$.
Reading $\xi_i$ as $\partial / \partial x_i$, this is the algebra of polynomial multivector fields on $K^n$, $\Delta$ is the divergence, and the bracket is the [[Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket|FJ1MDBZ680]].

Non-example: $A = K[x_1, x_2] \otimes \Lambda[\xi]$ with $\Delta = \frac{\partial^3}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2 \partial \xi}$.
Clauses 1 and 2 hold, since $\partial_\xi^2 = 0$.
Clause 3 fails: $[\Delta, L_{x_1}] = \partial_{x_2} \partial_\xi$, then $[\partial_{x_2} \partial_\xi, L_{x_2}] = \partial_\xi$, and $[\partial_\xi, L_\xi] = \id \ne 0$.
