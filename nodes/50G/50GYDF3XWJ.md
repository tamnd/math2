## Summary

The equivalent potential temperature of moist air is a [[potential temperature|SJTMRNXFZ3]] that counts the latent heat of its water vapour.
It is the potential temperature the air would have after all its vapour has condensed and the released heat has warmed the air.
Unlike the potential temperature, it stays nearly constant when a rising parcel becomes saturated and forms cloud.
It is used to follow moist air masses and to judge the stability of the atmosphere to moist convection.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Water vapour carries hidden heat: when it condenses into cloud droplets, it releases heat into the air.
The equivalent potential temperature counts this hidden heat as if it had already been released.
So a warm, humid parcel near the ground has a high equivalent potential temperature, even if its plain temperature is ordinary.
For example, take air with potential temperature $300$ K and $15$ grams of vapour per kilogram of dry air.
Its equivalent potential temperature is roughly $340$ K.
When the equivalent potential temperature decreases with height, the lower air is a source of thunderstorms.
