## Summary

Bogoliubov theory is an approximate treatment of a [[Bose gas|W733NG6P3M]] with weak repulsive interactions at low temperature.
It assumes that almost all particles occupy the zero-momentum state, as in [[Bose–Einstein condensation|YYBRA846TR]].
It keeps only the terms of the Hamiltonian that are quadratic in the other modes.
The quadratic Hamiltonian is diagonalized by a Bogoliubov transformation, which gives a gas of independent quasiparticles.
Their energy is linear in the momentum at small momentum, which explains the sound waves and the [[superfluidity|4PZBRJ78X4]] of such a gas.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a cold gas of bosons almost all particles sit in the lowest state, the condensate.
The weak repulsion knocks a few pairs of particles out of the condensate with opposite momenta.
Bogoliubov's idea is to treat the condensate as a large classical reservoir and to look only at these few excited particles.
The excitations that come out are not single particles but mixtures of a particle moving one way and a missing particle moving the other way.
At long wavelength they are sound waves, with a speed set by the density and the strength of the repulsion.
