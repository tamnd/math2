## Summary

The expectation value of an [[observable|DF5RN297GE]] $A$ in a [[quantum state|4NQN47A92F]] is the mean result of many measurements of $A$ in that state.
For a state vector $\psi$ it is $\ip{\psi}{A\psi}$, and for a [[density operator|V1JTB52E8C]] $\rho$ and a bounded $A$ it is $\operatorname{tr}(\rho A)$.
It is the [[expected value|0WQNRCNY3M]] of the probability distribution of the results given by the [[Born rule|MF4DYZ4YAX]].
Expectation values are the quantities that the [[Ehrenfest theorem|BA3H1Y16TC]] and the uncertainty relations are about.

## Explanations

### L1 human

One measurement of a quantum quantity gives a random result.
If one repeats the experiment many times with the same preparation, the average of the results settles down to a fixed number, the expectation value.
For a spin in the state $(1, 1)/\sqrt{2}$, the vertical spin gives $1$ or $-1$ with equal chances, so its expectation value is $0$.
The expectation value need not be a possible result: here $0$ never comes out of a single measurement.
