## Summary

Optical activity, or optical rotation, is the turning of the plane of linear [[polarization|PJ8ZSJ0QDW]] as light passes through certain materials.
It happens in chiral media, such as quartz or a sugar solution, where the two circular polarizations have different [[refractive indices|9VGK0SP6EQ]].
The angle of rotation grows linearly with the length of the path.
It is used to measure the concentration of chiral molecules and to tell mirror-image molecules apart.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Shine light polarized vertically through a tube of sugar water, and it comes out polarized at a tilt.
The longer the tube or the sweeter the water, the larger the tilt.
The reason is that the sugar molecules have a handedness, like a screw.
Linear polarization is a mix of left and right circular light, and the molecules slow one of them a little more than the other, which turns the mix.
