## Summary

The initial mass function describes how the masses of stars are distributed when a population of stars is born.
It is a density on the range of stellar masses, usually normalised to the number or to the total mass of stars.
Above about one solar mass it is close to a power law, with many more low-mass stars than high-mass stars.
It sets how much light, how many supernovae and how much chemical enrichment a stellar population produces.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a gas cloud forms a cluster of stars, the stars are born with very different masses.
The initial mass function tells how many stars appear in each range of mass.
It is like a histogram of birth masses, smoothed out.
For example, with the classical power law, for each star of ten solar masses about $220$ stars of one solar mass are born in equal mass intervals.
