## Summary

A Nichols plot shows the [[frequency response|BMY8QZSD9H]] $G(i\omega)$ of a linear system as one curve parametrized by the frequency.
The phase is on the horizontal axis and the gain in decibels on the vertical axis.
It carries the same data as a [[Bode plot|EMVW7N54FE]], but in one picture.
Multiplying the loop by a constant gain shifts the curve up or down.
This makes the plot convenient for tuning a feedback loop and reading off [[gain and phase margins|J39ZSFW0YF]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the two graphs of a Bode plot, gain and phase against frequency, and plot gain against phase instead, forgetting the frequency axis.
Each frequency gives one point, and as the frequency grows the points trace a curve.
For $G(s) = 1/(s + 1)$ the curve starts at phase $0$ and gain $0$ decibels, and goes down towards phase $-90$ degrees as the gain falls.
The point at gain $0$ decibels and phase $-180$ degrees is the critical point of a feedback loop.
