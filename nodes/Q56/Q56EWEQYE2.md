## Summary

A context-free language is a [[formal language|5MFZ4VNY6D]] generated by some [[context-free grammar|CTCY2R8Q77]].
The class contains every [[regular language|18WVBDCS38]] and also languages with nesting, such as $\set{a^n b^n : n \ge 0}$.
Context-free languages are closed under union, concatenation and Kleene star, but not under intersection or complement.
Their membership problem is decidable in polynomial time, which makes them the basis of parsing.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A language is context-free when a grammar with one-placeholder rules can produce exactly its words.
Such grammars can match an opening symbol with a closing symbol far away, as in brackets.
A finite automaton cannot do this, because it cannot count without bound.
For example, the words $ab$, $aabb$, $aaabbb$, and so on, form a context-free language that is not regular.
