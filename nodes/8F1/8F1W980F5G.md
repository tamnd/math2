## Summary

Arnold's geometric formulation views the motion of an ideal incompressible fluid as a [[geodesic|NSYMQMHPDN]] on the infinite-dimensional group of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms of the fluid domain.
The metric is given by the kinetic energy of the fluid, and the [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]] are the geodesic equations of this metric.
Vladimir Arnold proposed it in 1966, extending the description of a rigid body as a geodesic on the rotation group.
It brings the tools of Riemannian geometry and [[Lie group|7JBRGCVJRB]] theory, such as curvature and conservation laws from symmetries, to hydrodynamics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Follow every particle of a fluid in a closed container from time $0$ to time $t$.
The result is a rearrangement of the container that keeps volumes, since the fluid is incompressible.
As time passes, these rearrangements form a path in the space of all volume-preserving rearrangements.
Arnold saw that an ideal fluid moves along the "straightest" such path, with the kinetic energy as the measure of length.
So fluid motion is a free motion, like a ball rolling on a smooth surface, only on an infinite-dimensional space.
