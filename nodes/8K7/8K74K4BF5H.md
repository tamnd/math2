## Example

Let $F$ be a field with $1 + 1 \ne 0$, and let $V = F^2$.
For $x, y \in V$ put
$$\omega(x, y) = x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1 = \det \begin{pmatrix} x_1 & y_1 \\ x_2 & y_2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
We check that $\omega$ is an alternating, nondegenerate and not symmetric form.

1. The form $\omega$ is bilinear, because the [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]] is linear in each column when the other column is fixed.
2. The form $\omega$ is [[alternating|08ZQAKBKHF]], because the two products cancel when $y = x$:
$$\omega(x, x) = x_1 x_2 - x_2 x_1 = 0.$$
3. The [[matrix|0P24YW565F]] of $\omega$ in the standard basis is
$$J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
since $\omega(e_1, e_1) = \omega(e_2, e_2) = 0$, $\omega(e_1, e_2) = 1$ and $\omega(e_2, e_1) = -1$.
4. The determinant is $\det J = 0 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot (-1) = 1 \ne 0$, so $\omega$ is nondegenerate.
5. So $(F^2, \omega)$ is a [[symplectic vector space|PQ7JJY0VPH]], and $(e_1, e_2)$ is a symplectic basis.
6. The form is not symmetric: $\omega(e_1, e_2) = 1$ and $\omega(e_2, e_1) = -1$, and $1 \ne -1$ because $1 + 1 \ne 0$.
7. Over $\mathbb{R}$, $\lvert \omega(x, y) \rvert$ is the area of the parallelogram with sides $x$ and $y$. For $x = (2, 1)$ and $y = (1, 3)$:
$$\omega(x, y) = 2 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 1 = 5.$$

## Remarks

The determinant form is the smallest symplectic space, and by the [[classification of alternating forms|V8KKHKXXB3]] every finite-dimensional symplectic space is an orthogonal sum of copies of it.
Every line $Fv$ is isotropic, since $\omega(v, v) = 0$, and a line has half the dimension of the plane, so every line in $F^2$ is a Lagrangian subspace.

What changes when the hypotheses change?
If $1 + 1 = 0$, the form is still alternating and nondegenerate, but now $-1 = 1$, so $\omega(x, y) = x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1$ is also symmetric; the hypothesis of the example is needed only for step 6.
On $F^n$ with $n \ge 3$, the same formula $x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1$ is alternating but degenerate, since $e_3$ lies in its radical.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two arrows in the plane span a parallelogram.
The rule gives its area with a sign: positive when the turn from the first arrow to the second is counterclockwise, and negative otherwise.
For $(2, 1)$ and $(1, 3)$ the value is $5$, and in the other order it is $-5$.
An arrow with itself spans no area, so the rule gives $0$.

### L2 human

Here is a second basis on which $\omega$ keeps its shape.
Take $b_1 = (1, 1)$ and $b_2 = (1, 2)$, the columns of $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
Then
$$\omega(b_1, b_2) = 1 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 1 = 1, \qquad \omega(b_2, b_1) = -1, \qquad \omega(b_1, b_1) = \omega(b_2, b_2) = 0.$$
So the new matrix $P^{\mathsf{T}} J P$ is again $J$, and $(b_1, b_2)$ is a second symplectic basis.
This works because $\omega(Px, Py) = \det P \cdot \omega(x, y)$ and $\det P = 2 - 1 = 1$.
In dimension $2$ the linear maps that keep $\omega$ are exactly the maps of determinant $1$.
Watch out: if $\det P = 3$, the new matrix is $3J$, not $J$, so the basis is not symplectic.
