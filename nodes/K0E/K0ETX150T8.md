## Proof

We replace $A$ by its reduced form, which has the same null space, and we use that each solution is fixed by its free entries.

1. By the [[definition|0PQ2GGY1QV]], $\operatorname{Null}(A)$ is the kernel of the linear map $x \mapsto Ax$, and the kernel of a linear map is a subspace by the [[kernel lemma|REWYZ8N7EN]], which proves part 1.
2. By the [[theorem on row reduction|FCM29NQ6DN]], $A$ is row equivalent to its reduced form $R$, and by part 4 of the [[theorem on row equivalence|E4GKK727ZM]], $\operatorname{Null}(A) = \operatorname{Null}(R)$.
3. The augmented matrix $[R \mid 0]$ is in reduced row echelon form, and its last column has no leading entry, so part 2 of the [[theorem on systems in reduced form|P3HMRQN5KT]] applies with $c = 0$.
4. By parts 3 and 4 of that theorem, for each $t \in F^k$ there is exactly one $x \in \operatorname{Null}(R)$ with $x_{f_l} = t_l$ for all $l$, and its pivot entries are
$$x_{p_i} = -\sum_{l=1}^k R_{i f_l}\, t_l \qquad (i = 1, \dots, r).$$
5. Now compute $\sum_{l=1}^k t_l s_l$: at position $f_{l'}$ only $s_{l'}$ is nonzero, so the entry is $t_{l'}$, and at position $p_i$ the entry is $\sum_l t_l (-R_{i f_l})$.
6. These are the entries of the vector $x$ of step 4, so $x = \sum_{l=1}^k t_l s_l$.
7. Each $s_l$ lies in $\operatorname{Null}(A)$, because it is the vector $x$ of step 4 for $t = e_l$.
8. For spanning, let $x \in \operatorname{Null}(A)$ and put $t_l = x_{f_l}$; by the uniqueness in step 4 and by step 6, $x = \sum_l t_l s_l$.
9. For independence, let $\sum_l t_l s_l = 0$; by step 5 its entry at position $f_{l'}$ is $t_{l'}$, so $t_{l'} = 0$ for each $l'$.
10. By steps 7 to 9, $s_1, \dots, s_k$ form a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $\operatorname{Null}(A)$, which proves part 2.
11. By the [[pivot basis theorem|11CSKXQNG3]], $\operatorname{rank} A = r$, the number of pivots, so the definition of [[dimension|05G2G0P224]] gives $\dim \operatorname{Null}(A) = k = n - r = n - \operatorname{rank} A$, which proves part 3.

## Idea

Row reduction does not change the null space, so we may work with the reduced form.
There each solution is fixed by the values of the free variables, and these values can be anything.
So the solution set is a copy of $F^k$, and the solutions for the unit vectors of $F^k$ form a basis.
Independence is read at the free positions, where the basis vectors look like the unit vectors themselves.

## Variants

The [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]] for the map $x \mapsto Ax$ gives part 3 without the explicit basis, once we know that the rank is the number of pivots.
The proof above gives more, namely the basis itself.
The [[parametric form|KQT68YPHD1]] of the solution set writes the general solution as $\sum_l t_l s_l$, which is step 6 in the language of systems.
