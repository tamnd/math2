## Definition

Let $n \ge 1$ and $\hbar > 0$, and let $a \in \mathcal{S}(\R^{2n})$ be a function in the [[Schwartz space|CG9JEATW3Q]] of the variables $(x, \xi) \in \R^n \times \R^n$.
The *Weyl quantization* of $a$ is the operator $\mathrm{Op}^w(a)$ on $\mathcal{S}(\R^n)$ given by
$$(\mathrm{Op}^w(a)\, \psi)(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^n} \int_{\R^n} \int_{\R^n} a\!\left(\frac{x + y}{2}, \xi\right) e^{i (x - y) \cdot \xi / \hbar}\, \psi(y)\, dy\, d\xi.$$
For every $a \in \mathcal{S}'(\R^{2n})$ the same formula, read in the sense of distributions, defines a continuous linear map $\mathrm{Op}^w(a)$.
It maps $\mathcal{S}(\R^n)$ to the [[tempered distributions|AES2T5JPFP]] $\mathcal{S}'(\R^n)$.
The function $a$ is the *Weyl symbol* of $\mathrm{Op}^w(a)$.

## Remarks

Example: for $a(x, \xi) = \xi_j$ the $\xi$-integral is a [[Fourier transform|DPVM34ADZV]] pair, and $\mathrm{Op}^w(a) = -i\hbar\, \partial/\partial x_j$, the momentum operator $P_j$.
For $a(x, \xi) = x_j$ the $\xi$-integral gives $(2\pi\hbar)^n \delta(x - y)$, and $\mathrm{Op}^w(a)$ is multiplication by $\frac{x_j + x_j}{2} = x_j$, the position operator $X_j$.
For $a = x_1 \xi_1$ one gets $\mathrm{Op}^w(a) = \frac12(X_1 P_1 + P_1 X_1)$.

If $a$ is real-valued, then $\mathrm{Op}^w(a)$ is symmetric: $\ip{\varphi}{\mathrm{Op}^w(a)\psi} = \ip{\mathrm{Op}^w(a)\varphi}{\psi}$ for $\varphi, \psi \in \mathcal{S}(\R^n)$.

Non-example: the standard (Kohn–Nirenberg) quantization, with $a(x, \xi)$ in place of $a\!\left(\frac{x+y}{2}, \xi\right)$, sends $x_1 \xi_1$ to $X_1 P_1$, which is not symmetric; so it is a different rule.

For polynomials $a$ of degree at most $2$ in $(x, \xi)$, Weyl quantization turns Poisson brackets into commutators exactly: $[\mathrm{Op}^w(a), \mathrm{Op}^w(b)] = i\hbar\, \mathrm{Op}^w(\{a, b\})$.
In [[canonical quantization|7NW2C10YSR]] terms, $\mathrm{Op}^w(a)$ is the operator $a(X, P)$ with the symmetric ordering.
