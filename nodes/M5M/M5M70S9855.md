## Example

Let $F$ be the CNF with the four clauses $\set{p, q}$, $\set{p, \neg q}$, $\set{\neg p, r}$, $\set{\neg p, \neg r}$.
It is unsatisfiable: the first two clauses force $p = 1$, and the last two force $p = 0$.
Here is a [[resolution refutation|6JSH907W5R]] $\pi$ of $F$.

1. $\set{p, q}$, axiom.
2. $\set{p, \neg q}$, axiom.
3. $\set{p}$, resolvent of 1 and 2 on $q$.
4. $\set{\neg p, r}$, axiom.
5. $\set{\neg p, \neg r}$, axiom.
6. $\set{\neg p}$, resolvent of 4 and 5 on $r$.
7. $\square$, resolvent of 3 and 6 on $p$.

We compute the measures.

- *Size.* $\pi$ has $7$ clauses. Counted in literals, its size is $2 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 0 = 10$.
- *Width.* The widest clauses have $2$ literals, so $w(\pi) = 2$. Also $w(F) = 2$.
- *Tree-like.* Each derived clause $\set{p}$, $\set{\neg p}$ is a premise of one step only. So $\pi$ is tree-like.
- *Regular.* The path from $\square$ to $\set{p, q}$ has the pivots $p$ and then $q$, and the path to $\set{\neg p, r}$ has the pivots $p$ and then $r$. No pivot repeats, so $\pi$ is regular.

No refutation of $F$ is smaller.
A refutation must use all four clauses, because each set of three of them is satisfiable.
Start with the four axioms as four separate parts.
Each resolution step joins the parts of its two premises, so it lowers the number of parts by at most one.
At the end one part remains, so a refutation has at least three steps and at least $4 + 3 = 7$ clauses.
So $S(F \vdash \square) = 7$ and $w(F \vdash \square) = 2$.

## Remarks

The example shows the measures of [[proof complexity|1Q046J0AW2]] on one proof: size in clauses, size in literals, and width.
For larger formulas these measures can differ a lot.
The [[size-width relation|GWDGRPAQSM]] says that a large width forces a large size.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The four clauses say: "$p$ or $q$", "$p$ or not $q$", "not $p$ or $r$", "not $p$ or not $r$".
The first two together say "$p$", and the last two together say "not $p$".
The proof writes $7$ clauses, and none of them has more than $2$ letters.

### L2 human

We check that every three clauses of $F$ are satisfiable.

1. Without $\set{p, q}$: take $p = 0$, $q = 0$. The other three clauses hold.
2. Without $\set{p, \neg q}$: take $p = 0$, $q = 1$.
3. Without $\set{\neg p, r}$: take $p = 1$, $r = 0$.
4. Without $\set{\neg p, \neg r}$: take $p = 1$, $r = 1$.

So a refutation must use all four axioms.
The typical use of such a count is a minimality argument for small formulas.
A trap: "size" may mean clauses or literals, so a page must say which.
