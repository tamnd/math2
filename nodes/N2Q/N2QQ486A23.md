## Definition

Let $\theta > 0$ and let $G$ be a proper real rational [[transfer function|86JKV3TRFD]].
Consider a plant with transfer function $P(s) = G(s)\, e^{-\theta s}$, that is, the system $G$ followed by a delay of $\theta$.
Let $C$ be a controller designed for the delay-free plant $G$.
The *Smith predictor* built from $C$ and the model $G e^{-\theta s}$ is the controller
$$C_{\mathrm{SP}}(s) = \frac{C(s)}{1 + C(s)\, G(s) \big(1 - e^{-\theta s}\big)},$$
used in unity negative [[feedback|7NYES2D0Z4]] with the plant $P$.

## Remarks

Equivalently, $C$ acts on the error between the reference and the signal $y + \hat{y}_0 - \hat{y}$, where $\hat{y}_0$ and $\hat{y}$ are the outputs of the models $G$ and $G e^{-\theta s}$ driven by $u$.
With $L = C_{\mathrm{SP}} P$, one has $1 + L = (1 + CG)/\big(1 + CG(1 - e^{-\theta s})\big)$, so the [[closed-loop transfer function|RYTSV821V8]] is
$$T(s) = \frac{L(s)}{1 + L(s)} = \frac{C(s) G(s)}{1 + C(s) G(s)}\, e^{-\theta s}.$$
This is the delay-free closed loop followed by the delay; the characteristic equation $1 + CG = 0$ no longer contains $e^{-\theta s}$.

Example: $G(s) = 1/(s + 1)$ and $C = 1$ give $T(s) = e^{-\theta s}/(s + 2)$, a stable first-order response delayed by $\theta$.

Non-example: for an unstable $G$, such as $G(s) = 1/(s - 1)$, the same construction does not give an internally stable loop, even when $T$ is stable.
The reason is that the internal model contains the unstable mode in open loop.
So the definition is meant for stable $G$.

With a mismatch between the model and the plant, the formula for $T$ no longer holds, and robustness must be checked separately.
