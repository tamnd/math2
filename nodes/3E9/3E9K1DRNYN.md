## Summary

A strain energy density function gives the elastic energy stored per unit volume of a body as a function of the local deformation.
Its derivative with respect to the [[deformation gradient|HTYRR2WDQ6]] is the stress.
Choosing this one scalar function fixes the whole elastic response of a material, and it makes variational methods available in elasticity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When you stretch a spring you store energy in it, and you get the energy back when you let it go.
An elastic solid does the same, and the strain energy density says how much energy each small piece stores for a given deformation.
The force the material exerts is the rate at which this energy changes with deformation.
For a linear spring the energy is $\tfrac12 k x^2$ and its derivative $kx$ is the force; the strain energy density is the analogue for solids.
