## Summary

The action of a mechanical system is a number attached to each path of the system, usually the integral of the Lagrangian over time.
By the [[stationary-action principle|Q104C67P26]], the actual motions are the paths at which the action is stationary.
It has the dimension of energy times time, the same as Planck's constant.
The action is the central object of the variational formulation of mechanics and of field theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine all the ways a ball could travel from one place to another in one second.
For each such path, compute the kinetic energy minus the potential energy at each instant, and add it up over the second.
This total is the action of the path.
The path the ball really takes is one for which small changes of the path do not change the action, to first order.
