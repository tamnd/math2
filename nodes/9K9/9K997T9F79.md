## Definition

Let $\Phi(R, z)$ be an axisymmetric gravitational potential, written in cylindrical coordinates $(R, \varphi, z)$, with symmetry plane $z = 0$ and $\partial \Phi / \partial R (R, 0) \ge 0$ for $R > 0$.
The *circular speed* at radius $R > 0$ is
$$v_c(R) = \sqrt{R \, \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial R}(R, 0)},$$
the speed of a circular orbit of radius $R$ in the plane $z = 0$.
The *rotation curve* is the function $R \mapsto v_c(R)$, or its graph.
The [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]] of the circular orbit is $\Omega(R) = v_c(R)/R$.

## Remarks

Check of the formula: on a circular orbit the centripetal acceleration $v_c^2 / R$ equals the inward radial force per unit mass $\partial \Phi / \partial R$.
The potential is linked to the mass density by [[Poisson's equation|DWK5N5SKTK]] $\nabla^2 \Phi = 4 \pi G \rho$.

Example: for a spherical mass distribution with mass $M(r)$ inside radius $r$, [[Newton's law of gravitation|29WRN5J730]] gives $\partial \Phi / \partial r = G M(r)/r^2$, so $v_c(r)^2 = G M(r)/r$.
For a point mass $M$ this gives the Keplerian curve $v_c = \sqrt{GM/r}$.
For a density $\rho(r) = v_0^2 / (4 \pi G r^2)$ one gets $M(r) = v_0^2 r / G$ and the flat curve $v_c = v_0$.

Non-example: in a potential with $\partial \Phi / \partial R < 0$ at some radius the net force there points outward, so no circular orbit exists and $v_c$ is not defined at that radius.

In practice the rotation curve is measured from Doppler shifts of the gas, often the 21 cm line of neutral hydrogen.
