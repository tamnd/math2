## Example

Let $K$ be an [[algebraically closed field|0PF6TM8TWT]], let $A \subseteq K$, and let $k$ be the subfield of $K$ generated by $A$.
We show that the model-theoretic $\operatorname{acl}(A)$ is the set $k^{\mathrm{alg}}$ of elements of $K$ that are [[algebraic|0H628RSGWX]] over $k$.

1. Let $b$ be algebraic over $k$, with $f(b) = 0$ for a nonzero $f \in k[x]$.
2. Each coefficient of $f$ is a quotient of two polynomial expressions in finitely many elements of $A$. Multiplying by the denominators gives a nonzero $g(x)$ whose coefficients are terms in $A$.
3. The formula $g(x) = 0$ is an $L_A$-formula, holds at $b$, and has at most $\deg g$ solutions. So $b \in \operatorname{acl}(A)$.
4. Let $b$ be [[transcendental|0NQMZ89X4A]] over $k$, and let $\varphi(x)$ be an $L_A$-formula that holds at $b$.
5. By quantifier elimination for [[algebraically closed fields|MT8TDVPBXB]], $\varphi$ is a Boolean combination of equations $g_i(x) = 0$ with nonzero or zero polynomials $g_i$ over $k$.
6. The element $b$ is a root of no nonzero $g_i$, and $\varphi(b)$ holds, so $\varphi$ holds at every element that is a root of none of the nonzero $g_i$.
7. These are all elements of $K$ except finitely many, and $K$ is infinite. So $\varphi(K)$ is infinite, and $b \notin \operatorname{acl}(A)$.

So $\operatorname{acl}(A) = k^{\mathrm{alg}}$, which is algebraically closed, and it is the field-theoretic [[algebraic closure|0X6AB5P3G1]] of $k$ inside $K$.

## Remarks

The definable closure is smaller.
In characteristic $0$, $\operatorname{dcl}(A) = k$: an element algebraic over $k$ and not in $k$ has a conjugate over $k$, and some automorphism of $K$ over $k$ maps it to that conjugate.
In characteristic $p > 0$, $\operatorname{dcl}(A)$ is the [[perfect closure|PWNZQVQ175]] of $k$, since $x^p = a$ has exactly one solution.
The closure $\operatorname{acl}$ in $K$ has the exchange property, by the theory of transcendence bases, and its dimension is the transcendence degree.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the complex numbers, the elements that the field operations tie to a set are the roots of polynomial equations with coefficients built from the set.
For the empty set these are the algebraic numbers, such as $\sqrt{2}$ and $i$.
A number like $\pi$ solves no such equation, and every property of $\pi$ holds for infinitely many numbers.

### L2 human

We find $\operatorname{acl}(\set{\pi})$ in $\C$.

1. The subfield generated by $\set{\pi}$ is $\Q(\pi)$.
2. By the example, $\operatorname{acl}(\set{\pi})$ is the set of complex numbers algebraic over $\Q(\pi)$, such as $\sqrt{\pi}$ and $\pi + i$.
3. The number $e$ is in it only if $e$ is algebraic over $\Q(\pi)$, which is an open problem.

The typical use is to translate model-theoretic closure into field theory.
The trap is to use the same answer for real closed fields, where the closure is the real closure and need not be algebraically closed.
