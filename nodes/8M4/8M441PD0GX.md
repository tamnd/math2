## Summary

A phase-field model describes a material with several phases by a smooth order parameter that varies continuously across thin interfaces.
The evolution of the order parameter decreases a free energy that combines a bulk energy with several minima and a gradient energy.
The interfaces are not tracked explicitly; they are the thin regions where the order parameter changes quickly.
The [[Allen–Cahn equation|7S813PEV69]] and the [[Cahn–Hilliard equation|DKN15NVKSB]] are the basic phase-field equations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of ice in water, and give each point a number: $1$ in ice, $-1$ in water.
A phase-field model lets this number change smoothly from $1$ to $-1$ over a thin layer instead of jumping.
The energy prefers the values $1$ and $-1$ but also penalises fast changes, and this balance fixes the width of the layer.
As the system lowers its energy, the layer moves, and so the shape of the ice changes.
Suppose the model does not conserve the amount of each phase.
Then a small round drop described this way shrinks over time, as a real interface driven by surface tension does.
