## Summary

A rotating reference frame is a frame of reference whose axes turn relative to an [[inertial frame|Q05G0NZDNB]].
Coordinates fixed to the Earth, to a merry-go-round or to a spinning satellite are examples.
Velocities measured in such a frame differ from the inertial ones by $\omega \times r$, where $\omega$ is the [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]] of the frame.
Newton's second law holds in a rotating frame only after fictitious forces, such as the Coriolis and centrifugal forces, are added.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Sit on a merry-go-round and look at a tree outside.
In your own frame, the tree seems to move around you in a circle, although it stands still.
Your frame turns, so motions you see in it are mixed with the turning of the frame.
Weather maps use a frame that turns with the Earth, and this is why winds seem to bend.
