## Summary

Love numbers are dimensionless numbers that measure how much an elastic planet deforms under a tidal or loading potential.
For each degree $n$ of the potential, $h_n$ measures the radial displacement of the surface and $l_n$ the horizontal displacement.
The number $k_n$ measures the extra potential produced by the redistributed mass.
They were introduced by Augustus Love; the number $l$ is also called the Shida number.
They encode the elastic structure of the interior and enter the [[Earth tide|JFX02GA88H]], the ocean tide and the period of the [[Chandler wobble|9RESF83PY2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Moon does not only raise tides in the sea; it also stretches the solid Earth by a few tens of centimetres.
How much the Earth stretches depends on how stiff it is inside.
A perfectly rigid Earth would not move at all, and a fluid Earth would move as much as the water.
Love numbers are the scale factors that say where the real Earth lies between these extremes.
For the Earth, the degree two values are roughly $h_2 \approx 0.6$, $k_2 \approx 0.3$ and $l_2 \approx 0.08$.
