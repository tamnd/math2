## Summary

Lagrangian mechanics is a formulation of classical mechanics in which a system is described by one function, the Lagrangian, of its coordinates and velocities.
The motions are the solutions of the [[Euler–Lagrange equations|PS0VV149ET]] of this function, or equivalently the stationary points of the action.
It works in any coordinates and it removes the forces of ideal constraints from the equations.
It is the bridge from Newton's laws to Hamiltonian mechanics, to symmetries and conservation laws, and to field theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In Newton's mechanics one adds up forces, which is awkward for a bead on a wire or a pendulum, where the constraint forces are unknown.
In Lagrangian mechanics one instead chooses convenient coordinates, such as the angle of a pendulum.
One writes the kinetic energy minus the potential energy in these coordinates, and a fixed recipe turns it into the equations of motion.
For a pendulum of length $\ell$, with angle $\theta$, the recipe gives $\ddot\theta = -(g/\ell)\sin\theta$ without ever computing the tension in the rod.
