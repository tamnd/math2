## Summary

The yield surface of a material is the surface in the space of stresses that separates elastic states from states at which plastic flow begins.
Stresses inside it produce only elastic deformation; stresses on it can produce plastic deformation; stresses outside it cannot be sustained in rate-independent plasticity.
For isotropic materials it is drawn in the space of [[principal stresses|M6ZWV4Y29A]].
There the von Mises surface is a circular cylinder and the Tresca surface a hexagonal prism.
The Mohr–Coulomb surface is a hexagonal pyramid and the Drucker–Prager surface a circular cone.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of all possible ways to load a small cube of metal: pull, push or shear in any combination.
Each combination is a point in a six-dimensional stress space.
Gentle loads form a region around the origin where the metal only deforms elastically.
The edge of that region is the yield surface: once the load reaches it, the metal starts to deform permanently.
