## Summary

A normed division algebra is a real algebra with a unit and a norm.
The norm of a product must be the product of the norms.
The multiplicative norm forbids zero divisors, so in finite dimension such an algebra is a [[division algebra|8H3JKHAAHE]].
By [[Hurwitz's theorem|WZ81H3S4KK]] there are exactly four of finite dimension: $\R$, $\C$, the [[quaternions|0GE4DVQKR8]] and the [[octonions|1HRMT3DSX2]].
They are the positive definite cases of [[composition algebras|1Q9G6AST10]] over $\R$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For complex numbers, $\abs{zw} = \abs{z}\abs{w}$: the length of a product is the product of the lengths.
A normed division algebra is a number system with this rule.
The rule implies that a product of nonzero elements is nonzero, so one can divide.
Only four such systems exist, of dimensions $1$, $2$, $4$ and $8$.
