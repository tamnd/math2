## Summary

Coherence measures how well a wave field at one point and time is correlated with the same field at another point or a later time.
It is described by the mutual coherence function, the correlation of the field values.
Its normalised form, the complex degree of coherence, has modulus at most $1$.
Coherence decides whether two parts of a wave can show stable [[interference|KX1EXRW47E]] fringes: the fringe visibility is proportional to the degree of coherence.
Temporal coherence concerns delays at one point and is linked to the spectral width.
Spatial coherence concerns two points at the same time and is linked to the size of the source.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Real light is not a perfect endless wave; its phase drifts in a random way.
If the light at two places drifts in step, the two places are coherent, and their light can make clear interference fringes.
If the drifts are unrelated, the fringes wash out.
Laser light stays in step over long delays, while sunlight stays in step only over a few wavelengths.
Light from a small faraway star is coherent over a fairly wide patch, which astronomers use to measure the size of stars.
