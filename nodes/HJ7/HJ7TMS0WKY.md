## Summary

A KMS state is a [[state|F7MB2WREBJ]] on a [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]] with a time evolution that satisfies the Kubo–Martin–Schwinger condition at an inverse temperature $\beta$.
It is the definition of a thermal equilibrium state for infinite quantum systems, where the Gibbs density $e^{-\beta H} / \tr e^{-\beta H}$ makes no sense.
For a finite quantum system the KMS state is exactly the Gibbs state.
Several KMS states at the same $\beta$ signal a phase transition.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a finite quantum system, thermal equilibrium is described by the operator $e^{-\beta H}$ divided by its trace.
For an infinite system, such as an infinite crystal, the trace is infinite and this recipe breaks down.
The KMS condition keeps a property of the Gibbs state that still makes sense.
It is a twisted symmetry between the correlations $\omega(a \, \tau_t(b))$ and $\omega(\tau_t(b) \, a)$, where time is shifted by the imaginary amount $i \beta$.
On a matrix algebra, the only state with this property is the Gibbs state.
