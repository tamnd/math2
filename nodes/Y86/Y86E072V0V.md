## Summary

The Shannon entropy of a [[discrete random variable|0X2J0VY6DN]] measures the average uncertainty of its value, or the average information gained when its value is revealed.
For a random variable with [[probability mass function|0D6J4B671D]] $p$ it is $H(X) = -\sum_x p(x) \log p(x)$.
It is the basic quantity of [[information theory|38JECZ46C2]].
By [[Shannon's source coding theorem|PC8ASRWARE]], it is the least average number of bits per symbol needed to encode the values of $X$.
Joint entropy, conditional entropy and mutual information are built from it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Entropy measures how hard a random outcome is to guess.
A fair coin has entropy $1$ bit; a coin that always shows heads has entropy $0$, because there is nothing to learn.
A fair die has entropy $\log_2 6 \approx 2.58$ bits, more than a coin, because there are more equally likely outcomes.
Among all random variables with $n$ possible values, the uniform one has the largest entropy, $\log_2 n$ bits.
One can think of entropy as the average number of yes-or-no questions needed to find the outcome with a good strategy.
