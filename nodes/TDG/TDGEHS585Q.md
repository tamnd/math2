## Summary

The Hartle–Hawking state, or no-boundary wave function, is a proposal of James Hartle and Stephen Hawking (1983) for the quantum state of the universe.
It is defined by a [[Euclidean path integral|MGPT8N01FW]] over compact four-geometries whose only boundary is the present three-dimensional space.
It gives a solution of the [[Wheeler–DeWitt equation|TNKMDJB5FP]] without imposing any initial condition, since the geometries have no initial boundary.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A wave function of the universe needs a starting condition, but the universe at its beginning has no "before" to supply one.
Hartle and Hawking proposed that, going back in imaginary time, the universe closes off smoothly like the South Pole of a globe.
There is then no edge where an initial condition is needed.
In the simplest model, the early universe is half of a four-dimensional sphere glued to an expanding de Sitter universe.
The proposal is used in quantum cosmology to predict which universes are likely.
