## Summary

The Ramsey–Cass–Koopmans model is a model of economic growth in which saving is chosen optimally rather than fixed.
A representative household chooses a consumption path to maximise discounted lifetime utility, subject to the capital accumulation equation of the [[Solow–Swan model|223GCENAVJ]].
The optimal path is described by an Euler equation for consumption together with the capital equation.
It converges to a steady state along a saddle path.
It is the standard example of an infinite-horizon [[optimal control|EZ8NBZ8D0E]] problem in economics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the Solow–Swan model people save a fixed share of their income.
Here a household decides how much to save by weighing consumption today against consumption tomorrow.
Saving is worth it when capital earns more than the household's impatience costs.
In the long run capital settles where its net return equals the rate of impatience.
For example, with output $k^{1/2}$, no depreciation and impatience rate $1/4$, capital settles where $\frac{1}{2} k^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{4}$, that is at $k = 4$.
