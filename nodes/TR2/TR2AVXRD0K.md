## Summary

The cutoff frequency of a waveguide mode is the lowest frequency at which that mode can propagate.
Above it, the mode travels along the guide; below it, the mode decays exponentially along the guide and carries no power over long distances.
It is set by the size and shape of the cross-section, through an eigenvalue of the transverse Laplacian.
In electronics the same term names the frequency at which a filter's response drops to half power.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A wave fits into a metal pipe only if its wavelength is short enough compared with the width of the pipe.
Roughly, half a wavelength must fit across the pipe for the simplest pattern.
Lower frequencies have longer wavelengths, and below the cutoff they cannot squeeze through: the field dies away within a short distance.
This is why the metal mesh in a microwave oven door, with holes much smaller than the $12$ cm wavelength, keeps the microwaves in while letting visible light out.
