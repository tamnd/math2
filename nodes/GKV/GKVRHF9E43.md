## Summary

Rayleigh–Bénard convection is the motion of a horizontal layer of fluid heated from below and cooled from above.
When the temperature difference is small, heat crosses the layer by conduction and the fluid stays at rest.
When the [[Rayleigh number|CEKBV27MP0]] exceeds a critical value, the rest state becomes unstable and the fluid organises into convection rolls or cells.
It is the standard model problem for buoyancy-driven [[hydrodynamic stability|H028A17Z55]], pattern formation and thermal turbulence.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heat a thin layer of oil in a pan from below.
The warm oil at the bottom is lighter and wants to rise, the cool oil at the top is heavier and wants to sink.
Viscosity and heat diffusion resist this, so nothing happens at first.
Once the heating is strong enough, the oil starts to turn over in regular rolls or hexagonal cells, carrying heat upwards much faster.
Bénard saw such hexagonal cells in experiments in 1900, and Rayleigh explained the onset in 1916.
