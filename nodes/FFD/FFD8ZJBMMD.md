## Summary

The Cattaneo–Vernotte equation is a law of heat conduction that adds a relaxation time to [[Fourier's law|9YJZ1PP2Y8]].
The [[heat flux|1Y36434YTE]] does not react to a temperature gradient at once, but relaxes towards the Fourier value.
Combined with conservation of energy, it gives a damped wave equation for the temperature.
So heat travels with a finite speed instead of the infinite speed of the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The ordinary heat equation has a strange feature: heat a point and, in theory, the temperature rises everywhere at once, if only a tiny bit.
Cattaneo and Vernotte fixed this by letting the heat flux respond with a short delay.
The temperature then obeys an equation of the same type as the [[telegrapher's equations|1HR9NZM8B2]].
Disturbances spread as fronts that move at a finite speed and die out over time.
In most everyday situations the delay is tiny and Fourier's law is excellent.
The correction matters for very fast or very small processes, and it is linked to [[second sound|SYW4JJJTW3]].
