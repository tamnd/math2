## Summary

A Lie coalgebra is the dual notion of a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]].
It is a vector space with a cobracket that sends each element to an antisymmetric tensor of order two.
The cobracket satisfies a dual form of the Jacobi identity.
The [[dual space|07JBSZE0V4]] of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra is a Lie coalgebra, and every finite-dimensional Lie coalgebra arises in this way.
Lie coalgebras appear in rational homotopy theory and as one half of a [[Lie bialgebra|M4WGFHBKP5]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Lie bracket takes two elements and produces one.
A cobracket goes the other way: it takes one element and produces a combination of pairs, with the pairs counted antisymmetrically.
If you turn all the arrows of the axioms of a Lie algebra around, you get the axioms of a Lie coalgebra.
For example, a linear form $\xi$ on a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ gives the two-variable form $(x, y) \mapsto \xi([x, y])$, and this rule is the cobracket of the dual space $\mathfrak{g}^*$.
