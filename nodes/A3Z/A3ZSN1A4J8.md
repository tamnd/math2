## Summary

The cosmic distance ladder is the chain of methods by which astronomers measure distances, from the solar system to the most distant galaxies.
Each method works over a limited range and is calibrated with the methods of shorter range.
Geometric methods such as [[stellar parallax|93CFD71XZ5]] form the lowest rungs, and standard candles and the Hubble law the higher ones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

No single method measures every distance in the universe.
Close by, one can use geometry, as a surveyor does.
Farther out, one uses objects whose true brightness is known, called standard candles, and compares it with how bright they look.
To know the true brightness of a standard candle, one first finds some of them close enough to measure by geometry.
So each rung of the ladder rests on the one below it.
