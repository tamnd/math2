## Example

Let $T = \mathrm{ACF}_0$, the theory of [[algebraically closed fields|0PF6TM8TWT]] of characteristic $0$ in the signature $\set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$.
Let $\overline{\Q}$ be the field of algebraic numbers, the complex numbers that are roots of nonzero polynomials with rational coefficients.
We show that $\overline{\Q}$ is an [[atomic model|HSJ4F9J8SM]] and a [[prime model|S8W40PYNHX]] of $T$.

1. $\overline{\Q}$ is a [[field|0QFTETCHKB]], it is algebraically closed, and it has characteristic $0$. It is countable, since there are countably many polynomials over $\Q$ and each has finitely many roots.
2. $T$ has [[quantifier elimination|7FDDXV3JK7]]; see [[this page|MT8TDVPBXB]]. A quantifier-free formula in $\bar x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ is a Boolean combination of equations $f(\bar x) = 0$ with $f \in \Z[\bar x]$. So the type of a tuple $\bar a$ in a model of $T$ is fixed by its ideal $I(\bar a) = \set{f \in \Q[\bar x] : f(\bar a) = 0}$.
3. Let $\bar a \in \overline{\Q}^n$. The map $f \mapsto f(\bar a)$ sends $\Q[\bar x]$ onto $\Q[\bar a]$, which is a field, since each $a_i$ is algebraic. So $I(\bar a)$ is a [[maximal ideal|0PV74TR5FP]].
4. By [[Hilbert's basis theorem|0YKA9J3XEE]], $I(\bar a)$ is generated by finitely many polynomials $g_1, \dots, g_m$, which we may take with integer coefficients. Let $\varphi(\bar x)$ be $g_1(\bar x) = 0 \wedge \dots \wedge g_m(\bar x) = 0$.
5. Let $\bar b$ satisfy $\varphi$ in a model $K$ of $T$. Then $I(\bar b) \supseteq I(\bar a)$, and $I(\bar b)$ is a proper ideal, since $1 \notin I(\bar b)$. As $I(\bar a)$ is maximal, $I(\bar b) = I(\bar a)$.
6. By steps 2 and 5, $\operatorname{tp}^K(\bar b) = \operatorname{tp}^{\overline{\Q}}(\bar a)$. So $\varphi$ isolates the type of $\bar a$, and $\overline{\Q}$ is atomic.
7. $\overline{\Q}$ is countable and atomic, so it is prime by [[this theorem|EXC8913GGX]].

## Remarks

Step 7 can also be seen directly.
A model $K$ of $T$ contains $\Q$, and the elements of $K$ algebraic over $\Q$ form an algebraic closure of $\Q$, which is isomorphic to $\overline{\Q}$.
The inclusion of this subfield into $K$ is elementary, by quantifier elimination.
The same argument shows that the algebraic closure of the field with $p$ elements is the prime model of $\mathrm{ACF}_p$.
$\overline{\Q}$ is the only atomic model of $T$, up to isomorphism: a field of positive transcendence degree is not atomic, by [[this non-example|GPMKBKJ4A7]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ contains a copy of the algebraic numbers.
These numbers are forced to exist, since each one is a root of a polynomial that must have roots.
Each algebraic number is described, as far as the field language can tell, by its polynomial.
So the algebraic numbers are the smallest model, and the one that sits inside all others.

### L2 human

We run steps 3 to 6 for $a = \sqrt{2}$ with $n = 1$.

1. The ideal $I(\sqrt{2})$ is generated by $x^2 - 2$, which is irreducible over $\Q$.
2. Let $\varphi(x)$ be $x \cdot x = 1 + 1$.
3. In any model $K$, a root $b$ of $x^2 - 2$ has $I(b) = (x^2 - 2)$, so $b$ has the same type as $\sqrt{2}$.
4. In particular $\sqrt{2}$ and $-\sqrt{2}$ have the same type in $\overline{\Q}$.

The typical use is to identify the prime model of a theory of fields.
A trap is to forget that types are over the empty set: over the parameter $\sqrt{2}$, the elements $\sqrt{2}$ and $-\sqrt{2}$ have different types.
