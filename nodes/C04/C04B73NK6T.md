## Definition

The *nameless terms* are defined by these rules.

1. Every integer $n \ge 1$ is a nameless term, called an *index*.
2. If $M$ is a nameless term, then $\lambda\, M$ is a nameless term.
3. If $M$ and $N$ are nameless terms, then $(M\, N)$ is a nameless term.

Let $M$ be a closed term of the [[lambda calculus|BD756KR8FM]].
Its *de Bruijn form* is obtained by deleting the variable after each $\lambda$ and replacing each variable occurrence by an index.
The index of an occurrence of $x$ is $n$ when its binder $\lambda x$ is the $n$-th abstraction enclosing the occurrence, counting outward from the occurrence.

## Remarks

Example: $\lambda x.\, \lambda y.\, \lambda z.\, x\, z\, (y\, z)$ becomes $\lambda\, \lambda\, \lambda\, 3\, 1\, (2\, 1)$.
The occurrence of $x$ has the three abstractions $\lambda z$, $\lambda y$, $\lambda x$ around it, counting outward, and its binder is the third.

Non-example: $\lambda\, \lambda\, 1$ is not the de Bruijn form of $\lambda x.\, \lambda y.\, x$.
Counting outward from $x$, the first enclosing abstraction is $\lambda y$, so the index of $x$ is $2$, and the form is $\lambda\, \lambda\, 2$.
Counting from the outside instead gives *de Bruijn levels*, a different convention.

Two closed terms are $\alpha$-equivalent exactly when they have the same de Bruijn form.
For terms with free variables, one fixes a list of the free variables and continues the count past the outermost abstraction.
Beta reduction becomes $(\lambda\, M)\, N \to M[1 := N]$, where the substitution must shift the indices of free variables in $N$ and decrement those of $M$.
