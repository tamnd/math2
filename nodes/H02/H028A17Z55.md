## Summary

Hydrodynamic stability asks whether a steady flow persists when it is slightly disturbed.
The alternative is that small disturbances grow and change it into a different flow.
It is [[Lyapunov stability|0F2SYCKDT2]] applied to solutions of the equations of fluid motion.
The theory explains the onset of patterns such as convection cells and Taylor vortices, and the first steps of the [[transition to turbulence|8B94DQ86SN]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A smooth flow may be a correct solution of the equations and still never be seen, because the tiniest disturbance grows and destroys it.
Smoke rising from a candle is smooth at first and then breaks into swirls: the smooth rising flow has become unstable.
Hydrodynamic stability studies which flows survive small disturbances and which do not.
The usual tool is to linearise the equations around the flow and check whether some disturbance grows exponentially.
