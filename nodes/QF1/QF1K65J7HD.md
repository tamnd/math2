## Summary

The deceleration parameter $q$ is a dimensionless measure of whether the expansion of the universe is slowing down or speeding up.
It is built from the [[scale factor|J70F32P86W]] and its first two derivatives.
A positive $q$ means decelerating expansion, and a negative $q$ means accelerating expansion.
Observations of distant supernovae indicate that $q$ is negative today.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Hubble parameter says how fast the universe grows.
The deceleration parameter says whether that growth is slowing or speeding up, in a way that does not depend on the units of time.
For a universe filled only with ordinary matter, gravity slows the expansion and $q = 1/2$.
For a universe dominated by a cosmological constant, the expansion speeds up and $q = -1$.
