## Summary

Gödel showed that no finitistic argument can justify all of mathematics, but a large part of analysis can still be justified by finite means.
[[Hilbert's program|P1RW6JWX73]] asked for a finitistic consistency proof for all of mathematics, and by the [[second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]] this is impossible in full.
A *partial realization* of the program is a formal system $T$ that proves a significant part of ordinary mathematics, together with a finitistic proof that $T$ is conservative over a finitistic system for a class of finitistically meaningful sentences.
The main example is [[WKL0|7W9EG02T79]].
It is conservative over [[primitive recursive arithmetic|WCXH72T8D8]] for $\Pi^0_2$ sentences, and [[reverse mathematics|7SQS69T17R]] shows how much mathematics it proves.
Stephen Simpson called this approach finitistic reductionism.

## Motivation

Hilbert divided mathematics into a finitistic part, with concrete statements about finite objects, and an ideal part, with infinite sets.
The ideal part was to be justified by a finitistic proof that it proves no false finitistic statement.
Gödel's theorems show that no finitistic proof can do this for all of mathematics.
Reverse mathematics measures which ideal axioms each theorem needs.
So one can ask which theorems can still be reduced to finitism.
The answer covers a great many of them: the concrete consequences of every theorem provable in $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.

## Notation

$\mathsf{PRA}$ is primitive recursive arithmetic, which is commonly taken, following Tait, as the formal counterpart of finitistic reasoning.
A sentence is $\Pi^0_1$ if it has the form $\forall n\, \theta(n)$, and $\Pi^0_2$ if it has the form $\forall n\, \exists m\, \theta(n, m)$, with $\theta$ having only bounded quantifiers.
$T$ is *$\Pi^0_2$-conservative* over $\mathsf{PRA}$ if every $\Pi^0_2$ sentence that $T$ proves is also provable in $\mathsf{PRA}$.
$\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$ is the $\Pi^0_1$ sentence "no number codes a proof of $0 = 1$ in $\mathsf{PRA}$".

## Remarks

### WKL0 as the main example

The [[Π⁰₂-conservativity of WKL0 over PRA|V609D9SZ05]] has a proof that can be formalized in $\mathsf{PRA}$.
So $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, which proves the [[Heine-Borel theorem for the unit interval|KRS3VTWCHJ]], is a partial realization.
Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ is also equivalent to the Brouwer fixed point theorem, the Hahn-Banach theorem for separable Banach spaces, and Gödel's completeness theorem for countable languages.
So the $\Pi^0_2$ consequences of all these theorems have finitistic proofs.

### What the reduction gives

A [[Π⁰₂ theorem of WKL0 has a primitive recursive witness|3F0Y21T6DK]]: the ideal proof yields a finitistic computation.
For example, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ proves that there are infinitely many primes, and the conservation theorem returns a primitive recursive function that finds a prime above $n$.
A $\Pi^0_1$ theorem of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, such as a consistency statement, is already a theorem of $\mathsf{PRA}$.

### Systems that are not reducible

The [[Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem is not finitistically reducible|JEN1781V0E]]: with it, $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$.
$\mathsf{ACA}_0$ and every stronger system of the Big Five prove $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$, a $\Pi^0_1$ sentence that $\mathsf{PRA}$ does not prove.
So they are not partial realizations over $\mathsf{PRA}$.

### Predicative reductionism

There is a similar program one level higher.
[[ATR0|MKB74TX3Z8]] proves the same $\Pi^1_1$ sentences as Feferman's predicative system $\mathsf{IR}$, whose proof-theoretic ordinal is $\Gamma_0$.
So theorems of $\mathsf{ATR}_0$, such as the comparability of countable well-orderings, are reducible to [[predicative mathematics|43BSXNWQBG]].
Simpson calls this predicative reductionism.

### Watch out

A partial realization does not prove the consistency of all of mathematics.
It reduces only the theorems of one system, and only for one class of sentences.
The conservation result must itself have a finitistic proof.
A model-theoretic proof in a strong set theory does not by itself give a finitistic reduction.

### History

Hilbert set out the program in the 1920s, and Gödel's incompleteness theorems of 1931 showed that it cannot succeed in full.
Friedman proved the $\Pi^0_2$-conservativity of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ over $\mathsf{PRA}$.
Simpson presented finitistic reductionism in "Partial realizations of Hilbert's program" (Journal of Symbolic Logic, 1988).

### Try it

Is $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ a partial realization of Hilbert's program?
Answer: yes, in the same sense, since $\mathsf{RCA}_0 \subseteq \mathsf{WKL}_0$, so every $\Pi^0_2$ theorem of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is a $\Pi^0_2$ theorem of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.
It proves less ordinary mathematics, so $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ is the more useful example.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hilbert hoped to justify the use of infinite sets by finite methods.
That is not possible for all of mathematics.
But for a large part of analysis, the part provable in $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, the hope comes true.
Every concrete consequence of that part has a proof that uses only finite objects.
For example, a proof that uses infinite sets to show that there are infinitely many primes can be turned into a program that finds the primes.

### L2 human

To check that a theorem is finitistically reducible in this sense, show that $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves it from weak König's lemma.
One way to show that it is not is to show that it implies arithmetical comprehension over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, because then it proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$.
The typical use is the classification of a theorem of analysis: the Heine-Borel theorem is reducible, and the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem is not.
The trap is to forget the class of sentences: reducibility concerns $\Pi^0_2$ consequences only, and $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ proves statements about sets, such as weak König's lemma itself, that have no finitistic content.

### L3 human

The approach changes the question from "is mathematics consistent?" to "which part of mathematics is conservative over finitism?", and reverse mathematics answers the second question theorem by theorem.
Two features make $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ work.
It is $\Pi^1_1$-conservative over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, and the first-order part of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is $\Sigma^0_1$ induction, whose provably total functions are the primitive recursive ones.
The proof-theoretic ordinal $\omega^\omega$ of both $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ and $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ reflects this.
Whether $\mathsf{PRA}$ is the right formalization of finitism is a philosophical question, and the results hold for any finitistic system that contains $\mathsf{PRA}$.
