## Summary

A Luttinger liquid, or Tomonaga–Luttinger liquid, is the generic low-energy state of interacting gapless particles in one dimension.
Its excitations are not quasiparticles, as in a [[Fermi liquid|FW27X1KB37]], but collective density waves described by a free boson field.
Its correlation functions decay as power laws whose exponents depend on one interaction parameter $K$.
The name was introduced by Duncan Haldane in 1981, after the models of Sin-Itiro Tomonaga (1950) and Joaquin Luttinger (1963).

## Explanations

### L1 human

In one dimension particles cannot pass each other without colliding.
So a single electron cannot move alone: pushing one pushes its neighbours, and every excitation is a collective wave.
These waves behave like sound in a tube, and they are free to a very good approximation.
One number $K$ records how strongly the particles interact, with $K = 1$ for free fermions.
Quantities such as the momentum distribution then show power laws with exponents set by $K$, instead of the sharp jump of a three-dimensional metal.
