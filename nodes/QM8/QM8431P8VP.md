## Definition

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $(\C^2)^{\otimes n}$ be the state space of $n$ [[qubits|E9QTT3XBXS]], with computational basis $\lvert x \rangle$ for $x \in \set{0,1}^n$.
A *quantum computation* on $n$ qubits in the circuit model consists of three steps.

1. Prepare the initial state $\lvert 0 \cdots 0 \rangle$.
2. Apply a [[quantum circuit|S82TXJB05T]] $C$; let $U_C$ be the unitary operator that it implements.
3. Measure every qubit in the computational basis.

By the [[Born rule|MF4DYZ4YAX]], the output is $x \in \set{0,1}^n$ with probability $\abs{\langle x \rvert U_C \lvert 0 \cdots 0 \rangle}^2$.
*Quantum computing* is the use of such computations, for families of circuits produced by a classical algorithm, to solve computational problems.

## Remarks

Example: on one qubit, let $C$ be the Hadamard gate $H$.
Then $U_C \lvert 0 \rangle = (\lvert 0 \rangle + \lvert 1 \rangle)/\sqrt2$, and the output is $0$ or $1$ with probability $1/2$ each, a fair coin.

Non-example: a classical randomized algorithm that flips coins is not a quantum computation in this sense, because it has no complex amplitudes and no unitary step.
Classical reversible computation is the special case in which every gate permutes the computational basis.

The circuit model is equivalent in power to the [[quantum Turing machine|E1P61VYRRD]] up to polynomial overhead.
Other equivalent models include measurement-based and adiabatic quantum computation.
