## Example

In a triangle $ABC$ whose angles at $B$ and $C$ are acute, we construct a square $PQRS$ with $P$ and $Q$ on the side $BC$, $R$ on the side $CA$ and $S$ on the side $AB$.
The four conditions are hard to meet at once, so we [[vary the problem|KT5SASGTQ5]]: we drop the condition that $R$ lies on $CA$ and keep the other three.

1. Take any point $S_0$ on $AB$, let $P_0$ be its foot on $BC$, and complete the square $P_0 Q_0 R_0 S_0$ on the side of $P_0$ toward $C$.
2. This square meets three of the four conditions.
   Every square that meets these three conditions is the image of $P_0 Q_0 R_0 S_0$ under a [[homothety|0ZG1PNEP35]] with center $B$, as the L2 explanation proves.
3. So the free vertex $R$ of these squares runs along the ray from $B$ through $R_0$.
4. Restore the dropped condition: $R$ must lie on $CA$, so $R$ is the point where the ray $BR_0$ meets $CA$.
5. From $R$ drop the perpendicular to $BC$ to get $Q$, and complete the square $PQRS$.

Now a case with numbers: $B = (0, 0)$, $C = (6, 0)$ and $A = (2, 4)$, so that $AB$ is the line $y = 2x$ and $CA$ is the line $y = 6 - x$.

1. Take $S_0 = (1, 2)$ on $AB$, so $P_0 = (1, 0)$, $Q_0 = (3, 0)$ and $R_0 = (3, 2)$.
2. The ray $BR_0$ is $y = \frac{2}{3}x$, and it meets $y = 6 - x$ where $\frac{5}{3}x = 6$, so $R = (3.6, 2.4)$.
3. The square has side $2.4$, with $Q = (3.6, 0)$, $P = (1.2, 0)$ and $S = (1.2, 2.4)$.
4. Check: $S$ lies on $y = 2x$, since $2 \cdot 1.2 = 2.4$, and $R$ lies on $y = 6 - x$, since $6 - 3.6 = 2.4$.

The ratio of the homothety is $\frac{2.4}{2} = 1.2$.

## Remarks

Dropping a condition replaced one unknown square by a whole family of squares.
The family varies in a simple way, so the free vertex moves along a line, and the dropped condition picks one member.
Pólya used this problem in *How to Solve It* to show how keeping only a part of the condition leads to a locus.

There is also a formula for the side: it is $\frac{ah}{a + h}$, where $a = BC$ and $h$ is the height from $A$.
Here $a = 6$ and $h = 4$, so the side is $\frac{24}{10} = 2.4$, in agreement.
The acute angles at $B$ and $C$ make $P$ and $Q$ fall on the segment $BC$ and not on its extension.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We want a square inside a triangle, with one side of the square on the base and the two top corners on the two other sides.
That is hard, so first forget one demand: the top right corner may be anywhere.
Then it is easy to draw many such squares, small and large, all leaning on the left side of the triangle.
Their top right corners lie on one straight line from the bottom left corner of the triangle, and where this line meets the right side is the corner we need.

### L2 human

We prove the claim of step 2, that the free vertex runs along a ray.
Let $PQRS$ be any square with $P$ and $Q$ on the line $BC$, $S$ on the line $AB$, and $Q$ on the side of $P$ toward $C$.
Let $k = BS / BS_0$, and let $H$ be the homothety with center $B$ and ratio $k$.
Then $H$ maps $S_0$ to $S$, because both lie on the same ray from $B$.
A homothety with center $B$ maps the line $BC$ to itself, maps perpendicular lines to perpendicular lines, and multiplies lengths by $k$.
So it maps $P_0$, the foot of $S_0$, to $P$, the foot of $S$.
It maps the square on $P_0 S_0$ to the square on $PS$ on the same side, so $H(Q_0) = Q$ and $H(R_0) = R$.
Hence $R = H(R_0)$ lies on the ray from $B$ through $R_0$.

The locus had to be proved, not read off a figure, and the homothety gives that proof.
