## Summary

An internal wave is a gravity wave that travels inside a stably stratified fluid, rather than along its surface.
The restoring force is buoyancy, and its frequency cannot exceed the [[Brunt–Väisälä frequency|XQ82TE42FH]].
In a continuously stratified fluid the waves propagate obliquely, and their energy moves at right angles to their phase velocity.
Internal waves carry energy through the ocean and the atmosphere, and their breaking is a main cause of mixing in the deep ocean.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Surface waves ride on the boundary between water and air.
Inside the ocean there are also boundaries, or gradual changes, between lighter and heavier water, and waves can ride on these too.
Because the density difference is small, the restoring force is weak, so internal waves are slow and can be tens of metres high.
They are invisible from above, except for slicks and bands they sometimes leave on the surface.
