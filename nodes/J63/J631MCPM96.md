## Statement

Let $U \subseteq \R^n$ be open and consider a mechanical system with [[generalized coordinates|BCF1EX9W2M]] $q \in U$ and Lagrangian $L(q, \dot q) = T(q, \dot q) - V(q)$, where:

1. the [[kinetic energy|C9YCMAPKMT]] is $T(q, \dot q) = \tfrac12 \dot q^T M(q) \dot q$, with $M(q)$ a symmetric [[positive definite matrix|0KX66CZMBB]] depending continuously differentiably on $q$;
2. the [[potential energy|DF7DKHTQ7A]] $V \colon U \to \R$ is twice continuously differentiable;
3. the motions are the solutions of [[Lagrange's equations|BEM454AYTT]] for $L$.

If $q_0 \in U$ is a strict [[local minimum|09WRNGPTMV]] of $V$, then $(q_0, 0)$ is an [[equilibrium point|0N8NGKDZDZ]] of the equations of motion, and it is [[Lyapunov stable|0F2SYCKDT2]].

## Remarks

Proof idea: the total energy $E(q, \dot q) = T + V$ is conserved along motions.
Since $T$ is positive definite in $\dot q$ and $q_0$ is a strict local minimum of $V$, the function $E - V(q_0)$ is positive near $(q_0, 0)$ except at that point, where it vanishes.
So it is a Lyapunov function with zero derivative along motions, and a motion that starts with small energy above $E(q_0, 0)$ stays in a small neighbourhood of $(q_0, 0)$.

Example: the pendulum $L = \tfrac12 m l^2 \dot\theta^2 + m g l \cos\theta$ has $V(\theta) = -m g l \cos\theta$, with a strict minimum at $\theta = 0$, so the hanging position is stable.

The strictness of the minimum cannot be dropped: for a free particle on a line, $V = 0$ has a non-strict minimum at $0$.
The motion $q(t) = \varepsilon t$ starts arbitrarily close to $(0, 0)$ but leaves every bounded neighbourhood.
The theorem gives stability, not asymptotic stability: without friction, nearby motions oscillate forever and do not approach the equilibrium.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A ball resting at the bottom of a bowl stays near the bottom if it is nudged a little.
The theorem makes this precise for any frictionless mechanical system: a configuration where the potential energy has a strict minimum is a stable resting state.
The reason is energy conservation: a small push gives only a little energy, which is not enough to climb out of the valley.

### L2 human

The theorem is the classical prototype of Lyapunov's direct method, with the total energy as Lyapunov function.
It applies to any conservative holonomic system with time-independent constraints, such as pendulums, spring systems and rigid bodies in a potential.
Near a nondegenerate minimum, linearisation gives small oscillations with frequencies from the Hessian of $V$ relative to $M(q_0)$.
Whether the converse holds, that is, whether an equilibrium that is not a minimum of $V$ must be unstable, is a subtler question with only partial answers.
