## Summary

A nilpotent Lie algebra is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] whose [[lower central series|3Z22XPB6H5]] reaches $0$.
Equivalently, all brackets of sufficiently many elements, nested in the form $[x_1, [x_2, [\cdots, x_n]]]$, vanish.
Every nilpotent Lie algebra is [[solvable|W7A2R0GR3T]], but not conversely.
By Engel's theorem, a finite-dimensional Lie algebra is nilpotent exactly when each operator $\operatorname{ad} x$ is nilpotent.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a nilpotent Lie algebra, bracketing again and again with arbitrary elements always ends at zero after a fixed number of steps.
The strictly upper triangular matrices are the model: each bracket pushes the nonzero entries one diagonal further from the main diagonal.
The Heisenberg algebra, spanned by $x, y, z$ with $[x, y] = z$ and $z$ central, is the smallest nilpotent Lie algebra that is not commutative.
