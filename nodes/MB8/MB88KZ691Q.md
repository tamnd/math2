## Definition

Fix base dimensions, such as mass $\mathsf{M}$, length $\mathsf{L}$, time $\mathsf{T}$ and temperature $\Theta$.
Each positive physical quantity $q$ of a flow has a dimension $[q] = \mathsf{M}^{a} \mathsf{L}^{b} \mathsf{T}^{c} \Theta^{d}$ with rational exponents $a, b, c, d$.
Let $q_1, \dots, q_n$ be quantities of the flow and let $k_1, \dots, k_n$ be rational numbers.
The product $\Pi = q_1^{k_1} \cdots q_n^{k_n}$ is a *dimensionless number* if all the exponents of $[\Pi] = [q_1]^{k_1} \cdots [q_n]^{k_n}$ are zero.

## Remarks

The value of a dimensionless number does not change when the units of the base dimensions are changed.
Example: with density $\rho$, speed $U$, length $L$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu$, $[\rho U L / \mu] = (\mathsf{M} \mathsf{L}^{-3})(\mathsf{L} \mathsf{T}^{-1})(\mathsf{L}) / (\mathsf{M} \mathsf{L}^{-1} \mathsf{T}^{-1}) = 1$, so $\rho U L / \mu$ is dimensionless; it is the Reynolds number.
Non-example: $\rho U L$ has dimension $\mathsf{M} \mathsf{L}^{-1} \mathsf{T}^{-1}$, so it is not dimensionless.
Most dimensionless numbers of fluid mechanics are ratios of two forces, two time scales or two transport rates.
