## Summary

The van der Waals equation is an [[equation of state|YPT3SJSS5T]] for real fluids that corrects the ideal gas law by two constants.
One accounts for the attraction between molecules and one for their finite size.
It was proposed by Johannes Diderik van der Waals in 1873.
It is the simplest equation of state that predicts the condensation of a gas into a liquid and a critical point.
With the Maxwell construction it gives a qualitative theory of the liquid-gas transition.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Real molecules are not points, and they pull on each other a little.
Because they take up room, the space they can move in is smaller than the volume of the container.
Because they attract, they hit the walls less hard, so the pressure is lower than for an ideal gas.
The van der Waals equation puts both effects into the ideal gas law with two numbers fitted to each gas.
Below a critical temperature its pressure-volume curves have a wiggle, which signals that the gas can turn into a liquid.
