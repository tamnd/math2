## Example

Two point masses $m_1 > 0$ and $m_2 > 0$ hang on the two ends of an inextensible string of negligible mass.
The string runs over a pulley without mass and without friction.
Gravity acts downward with constant acceleration $g > 0$.

Let $y$ be the height of $m_1$ above a fixed level, and let $T$ be the tension of the string.
The string has constant length, so the height of $m_2$ is $c - y$ for a constant $c$; this is a [[holonomic constraint|AXA8HN8Y45]].
[[Newton's second law|KNBP19FG6K]] for each mass gives
$$m_1 \ddot y = T - m_1 g, \qquad -m_2 \ddot y = T - m_2 g.$$
Subtracting the second equation from the first gives $\ddot y$, and putting it back into the first equation gives $T$:
$$\ddot y = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\, g, \qquad T = \frac{2 m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\, g.$$
Check: if $m_1 = m_2$, then $\ddot y = 0$ and $T = m_1 g$, so the masses stay in equilibrium or move at constant speed.
If $m_2 \to 0$, then $\ddot y \to -g$ and $T \to 0$, so $m_1$ falls freely.

## Remarks

The machine has one degree of freedom, the height $y$.
Since $\abs{\ddot y} < g$, the motion is a slowed-down free fall, which made it a classical instrument to measure $g$ and to test the second law.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hang two weights on a string over a wheel.
The heavier weight goes down and pulls the lighter one up, but more slowly than a free fall.
The closer the two weights are, the slower the motion, and with equal weights nothing accelerates at all.
