## Definition

Let $F$ be a [[local field|J85FZEQG38]] of characteristic different from $2$, and let $\psi$ be a nontrivial unitary character of the additive group of $F$.
Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional [[symplectic vector space|PQ7JJY0VPH]] over $F$ with symplectic form $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$, and let $\mathrm{Sp}(W)$ be its [[symplectic group|X12FS2JAP0]].
Let $H(W) = W \times F$ be the [[Heisenberg group|ZZYCV5EX9J]] with product $(w, t)(w', t') = (w + w', t + t' + \tfrac12 \langle w, w' \rangle)$.
By the [[Stone–von Neumann theorem|Z877XDBMQ3]] there is, up to equivalence, a unique irreducible [[unitary representation|74BPYJNZTM]] $\rho_\psi$ of $H(W)$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ with $\rho_\psi(0, t) = \psi(t) \, \mathrm{id}$.
Each $g \in \mathrm{Sp}(W)$ acts on $H(W)$ by the automorphism $(w, t) \mapsto (g w, t)$.

Let $\mathrm{Mp}(W) \to \mathrm{Sp}(W)$ be the [[metaplectic group|8B0JM2YDE2]], a two-fold covering group, and write $\bar x$ for the image of $x \in \mathrm{Mp}(W)$ in $\mathrm{Sp}(W)$.
A *Weil representation* attached to $\psi$ is a unitary representation $\omega_\psi$ of $\mathrm{Mp}(W)$ on $\mathcal{H}$ such that
$$\omega_\psi(x) \, \rho_\psi(w, t) \, \omega_\psi(x)^{-1} = \rho_\psi(\bar x w, t)$$
for all $x \in \mathrm{Mp}(W)$, $w \in W$ and $t \in F$.

## Remarks

Since $\rho_\psi$ and $(w, t) \mapsto \rho_\psi(g w, t)$ have the same central character, the Stone–von Neumann theorem gives for each $g \in \mathrm{Sp}(W)$ a unitary operator $\omega(g)$ with $\omega(g) \rho_\psi(w, t) \omega(g)^{-1} = \rho_\psi(g w, t)$.
By Schur's lemma $\omega(g)$ is unique up to a scalar of modulus $1$, so $g \mapsto \omega(g)$ is a projective representation of $\mathrm{Sp}(W)$.
Weil showed that the scalars can be chosen so that this becomes a genuine representation of $\mathrm{Mp}(W)$.
Two Weil representations attached to the same $\psi$ differ by a unitary character of $\mathrm{Mp}(W)$, again by Schur's lemma.

For $F = \R$ the Weil representation is the [[oscillator representation|CCR7BAQ5N5]].

Example: let $F = \R$, $W = \R^2$ with $\langle (x, \xi), (x', \xi') \rangle = x \xi' - \xi x'$, and realize $\rho_\psi$ on $L^2(\R)$ (the Schrödinger model).
Up to scalars, the element $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \R) = \mathrm{Sp}(W)$ acts by a Fourier transform, and the diagonal element $\mathrm{diag}(a, a^{-1})$ acts by $f(x) \mapsto \abs{a}^{-1/2} f(a^{-1} x)$.
In this model $\omega_\psi$ preserves the even and the odd functions, and each of these two subspaces is irreducible.

Non-example: for $F = \R$ the projective representation $\omega$ cannot be made into a representation of $\mathrm{Sp}(W)$ itself, so the defining relation has no solution on $\mathrm{Sp}(W)$; the cover $\mathrm{Mp}(W)$ is needed.
