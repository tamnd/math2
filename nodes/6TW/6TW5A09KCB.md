## Summary

The canonical anticommutation relations (CAR) are the algebraic rules satisfied by fermionic creation and annihilation operators.
For each vector $f$ of a one-particle Hilbert space there is an operator $a(f)$, and these operators satisfy $\{a(f), a(g)\} = 0$ and $\{a(f), a^*(g)\} = \ip{f}{g}$, where $\{A, B\} = AB + BA$.
They express the [[Pauli exclusion principle|ZQGC326NP7]] algebraically, since they imply $a^*(f)^2 = 0$.
The [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]] generated by such operators, the CAR algebra, is unique up to isomorphism and is a basic object of quantum statistical mechanics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A fermion mode is either empty or occupied, like a switch that is off or on.
The annihilation operator turns "on" into "off" and the creation operator turns "off" into "on".
Trying to switch on a mode that is already on gives zero, which is the exclusion principle.
For one mode the operators are the $2 \times 2$ matrices with a single entry $1$ off the diagonal, and their anticommutator is the identity matrix.
The bosonic counterpart uses commutators instead and leads to the [[CCR algebra|4G9CNQZ7Y5]].
