## Summary

The refractive index of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to the phase velocity of light in the medium.
It tells how much light slows down and how much it bends when it enters the medium.
It enters Snell's law, the optical path length and the eikonal equation, so it is the one material constant of geometrical optics.
It usually depends on the frequency, which causes dispersion.
See the [[definition|M0590S1THC]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light travels at about $300\,000$ km per second in vacuum and more slowly in glass or water.
The refractive index says how many times slower: water has index about $1.33$ and common glass about $1.5$.
A beam that enters a medium of higher index at a slant bends toward the perpendicular.
Because the index changes a little with colour, a prism spreads white light into a rainbow.
