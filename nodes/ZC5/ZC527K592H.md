## Definition

Fix an Earth model, a source, and a seismic phase, that is, a type of wave together with its path, such as the direct P-wave.
The *travel-time curve* of that phase is a set of pairs $(X, T)$.
A pair belongs to it when a [[seismic ray|AZFGJKMKTP]] of that phase reaches the surface at distance $X$ from the source after the time $T$.
In a flat model $X$ is a horizontal distance; in a spherical model it is the angular distance $\Delta$.

## Remarks

Where the curve is a smooth function $T(X)$ near a ray, its slope is the [[ray parameter|JAFNCJNF6E]] of the ray: $dT/dX = p$.
Writing $T = pX + \tau(p)$ defines the intercept time $\tau(p)$, and $d\tau/dp = -X$.
Example: in a layer of thickness $h$ and speed $v_1$ over a half-space of speed $v_2 > v_1$, with the source at the surface, the direct wave has $T = X/v_1$.
The head wave refracted along the interface has $T = X / v_2 + 2h\sqrt{1/v_1^2 - 1/v_2^2}$, a line of slope $1/v_2$.
Its slope $1/v_2 = \sin i_c / v_1$ is the ray parameter of the ray at the critical angle $i_c$, as expected.
The two lines cross at $X = 2h \sqrt{(v_2 + v_1)/(v_2 - v_1)}$, beyond which the head wave arrives first.
Non-example: a single seismogram, ground motion against time at one station, is not a travel-time curve; it gives only one value of $X$.
The curve can be multivalued, with triplications, when the speed increases fast with depth.
