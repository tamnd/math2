## Summary

Every infinite partial order has an infinite chain or an infinite antichain, and this simple fact of combinatorics is strictly weaker than Ramsey's theorem for pairs, yet not provable in the base system.
The *reverse mathematics zoo* is the collection of such principles: theorems that are provable in a standard system such as $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ or $\mathsf{ACA}_0^+$, but that are not equivalent over [[RCA0|XQENB067R3]] to any of the five main subsystems.
For sentences $\mathsf{P}$ and $\mathsf{Q}$ of [[second-order arithmetic|DC60FYCSPM]], we say that $\mathsf{P}$ *implies* $\mathsf{Q}$ *over* $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ when $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathsf{P} \vdash \mathsf{Q}$.
The zoo records the known implications and non-implications between its members in this sense.
Its typical members are $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$, $\mathrm{SRT}^2_2$, $\mathrm{COH}$, $\mathrm{CAC}$, $\mathrm{ADS}$, $\mathrm{EM}$, $\mathrm{DNR}$, $\mathrm{WWKL}$ and Hindman's theorem $\mathrm{HT}$.
The name comes from the Reverse Mathematics Zoo website of Damir Dzhafarov, a database and diagram of these results.

## Motivation

[[Reverse mathematics|7SQS69T17R]] found that most theorems of ordinary mathematics are provable in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ or equivalent over it to one of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$.
[[Ramsey's theorem|00TQV3F5S4]] for pairs is the best-known theorem that breaks this pattern, as [[its reverse mathematics|ZT5KV6KJ3E]] shows.
To understand it, logicians split it into weaker principles and compared these with each other.
Each new principle met along the way joined the list, and the list became the zoo.
The zoo matters because it shows that five levels do not describe the strength of every theorem, and because its separations have driven new methods in computability theory.

## Notation

We write $\mathsf{P} \to \mathsf{Q}$ for "$\mathsf{P}$ implies $\mathsf{Q}$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$", and $[X]^2$ for the set of pairs $\{x, y\}$ with $x < y$ in $X$.
Papers also use $\mathsf{P} \le_\omega \mathsf{Q}$ for implication in all $\omega$-models, $\le_c$ for computable reduction and $\le_W$ for Weihrauch reduction.
"Infinite" always means "not bounded in $\mathbb{N}$".
Watch the direction of the symbols: $\mathsf{P} \le_W \mathsf{Q}$ says that $\mathsf{P}$ is the weaker problem, so it matches $\mathsf{Q} \to \mathsf{P}$.

## Remarks

### The principles below Ramsey's theorem

Start from $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$: every coloring $c \colon [\mathbb{N}]^2 \to \{0, 1\}$ has an infinite set $H$ on whose pairs $c$ is constant.
A coloring is *stable* if for each $x$ the color $c(x, y)$ is the same for all large $y$, and $\mathrm{SRT}^2_2$ is $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ for stable colorings.
$\mathrm{COH}$ says that for every sequence of sets $R_0, R_1, \ldots$ there is an infinite set $C$ such that, for each $i$, either $C \cap R_i$ or $C \setminus R_i$ is finite.
$\mathrm{CAC}$ says that every infinite [[partial order|0QV2JS9RJ3]] has an infinite chain or an infinite [[antichain|03HF498P1J]].
$\mathrm{ADS}$ says that every infinite [[linear order|0FRXBVVR03]] has an infinite ascending or an infinite descending sequence.
$\mathrm{EM}$, the Erdős-Moser principle, says that every infinite [[tournament|M2F9TN9MW5]] has an infinite transitive subtournament.
Each of these is $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ for a special kind of coloring, so each follows from it.

### Principles from computability and measure

$\mathrm{DNR}$ says that for every set $X$ there is a function $f$ with $f(e) \ne \Phi^X_e(e)$ whenever $\Phi^X_e(e)$ is defined.
$\mathrm{WWKL}$, [[weak weak König's lemma|BJQ77Y2QFX]], says that every binary tree of positive measure has a path.
[[Hindman's theorem|ZTWTZ8CSGV]] $\mathrm{HT}$ says that for every coloring of $\mathbb{N}$ with finitely many colors there is an infinite set whose finite sums of distinct elements all have one color.
The system $\mathsf{ACA}_0^+$ is $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ plus the axiom that the $\omega$-th [[Turing jump|60P2XWY5QX]] $X^{(\omega)}$ exists for every $X$.

### Two short implications

The arrow $\mathrm{RT}^2_2 \to \mathrm{CAC}$ colors a pair of points $0$ when they are comparable and $1$ when they are not.
A homogeneous set of color $0$ is a chain, and one of color $1$ is an antichain; the [[worked proof|C1051RBVB1]] checks that every set exists in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
The arrow $\mathrm{CAC} \to \mathrm{ADS}$ intersects the given linear order with the order of the numbers, so that chains ascend and antichains descend; see the [[second worked proof|NSEE4QP6JV]].
On the side of measure, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ proves $\mathrm{WWKL}$ at once: a tree of positive measure has strings of every length, so it is infinite, and [[weak König's lemma|7W9EG02T79]] gives a path.

### What is not in the zoo

Ramsey's theorem for triples is not in the zoo, because it is equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, as the [[case of triples|5DBP11VZTF]] shows.
The [[Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem|0KDJD249DT]] is also equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, so it fits the Big Five pattern.
The [[intermediate value theorem|00KAFBR32E]] is provable in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, and a principle that the base system proves adds nothing.

### The known map: Ramsey-type principles

Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman stated in 2001 that $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{SRT}^2_2 + \mathrm{COH}$.
The direction $\mathrm{RT}^2_2 \to \mathrm{COH}$ needed a repair of the induction used, given by Mileti and, independently, by Jockusch and Lempp.
Chong, Slaman and Yang showed in 2014 that $\mathrm{SRT}^2_2$ does not imply $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$, with a model whose numbers are nonstandard, and Monin and Patey later found an $\omega$-model.
Hirschfeldt and Shore proved $\mathrm{ADS} \to \mathrm{COH}$ and showed in 2007 that $\mathrm{CAC}$ and $\mathrm{ADS}$ are strictly weaker than $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$.
The chain is therefore
$$\mathrm{RT}^2_2 \to \mathrm{CAC} \to \mathrm{ADS} \to \mathrm{COH}.$$
Lerman, Solomon and Towsner showed that $\mathrm{ADS}$ does not imply $\mathrm{CAC}$, and that $\mathrm{EM}$ does not imply $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$.
In the other direction, $\mathrm{EM}$ and $\mathrm{ADS}$ together give $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$, an observation of Bovykin and Weiermann.
The idea is that a coloring is a tournament, a transitive subtournament is a linear order, and a monotone sequence in that order is homogeneous.

### The known map: measure and Hindman's theorem

Hirschfeldt, Jockusch, Kjos-Hanssen, Lempp and Slaman showed that $\mathrm{SRT}^2_2 \to \mathrm{DNR}$.
Hirschfeldt and Shore showed that $\mathrm{CAC}$ does not imply $\mathrm{DNR}$, so $\mathrm{CAC}$ does not imply $\mathrm{SRT}^2_2$ either.
On the measure side the chain is
$$\mathsf{WKL}_0 \to \mathrm{WWKL} \to \mathrm{DNR},$$
and both arrows are strict: Yu and Simpson proved the first in 1990, and Ambos-Spies, Kjos-Hanssen, Lempp and Slaman the second in 2004.
Liu showed in 2015 that $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ does not imply $\mathrm{WWKL}$, which strengthens his 2012 result for $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.
Blass, Hirst and Simpson showed in 1987 that $\mathsf{ACA}_0^+$ proves $\mathrm{HT}$ and that $\mathrm{HT}$ implies $\mathsf{ACA}_0$.
Whether $\mathrm{HT}$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, to $\mathsf{ACA}_0^+$ or to neither is open.

### How separations are proved

A non-implication $\mathsf{P} \not\to \mathsf{Q}$ is usually proved with an $\omega$-model of $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathsf{P}$ in which $\mathsf{Q}$ fails.
The $\omega$-models of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ are the Turing ideals, so this becomes a question about [[Turing degrees|P5N2ND3VCG]].
One builds solutions to instances of $\mathsf{P}$ that never compute a solution to one fixed instance of $\mathsf{Q}$, as in the proof of Seetapun's theorem.
The finer reductions, above all Weihrauch reducibility, are the subject of [[Weihrauch reducibility and the effective content of theorems|4G2JQ13XZ0]].

### Watch out

Do not read "it is not known that $\mathsf{P} \to \mathsf{Q}$" as "$\mathsf{P}$ does not imply $\mathsf{Q}$"; the diagram marks many arrows as open.
Do not treat an implication that holds in all $\omega$-models as a proof over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
Every $\omega$-model satisfies full induction, and so $\mathrm{B}\Sigma^0_2$, but $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ has $\Sigma^0_1$ induction only and does not prove $\mathrm{B}\Sigma^0_2$.
The first proof of $\mathrm{RT}^2_2 \to \mathrm{COH}$ failed at exactly this point, and the separation of $\mathrm{SRT}^2_2$ from $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ first needed a model with nonstandard numbers.

### History

Specker and Jockusch studied the computability of homogeneous sets around 1970.
Hirst's thesis of 1987 studied Ramsey's theorem in subsystems of second-order arithmetic.
Seetapun's theorem of 1995 and the paper of Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman in 2001 started the systematic study of principles below $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$.
Hirschfeldt and Shore added $\mathrm{ADS}$ and $\mathrm{CAC}$ in 2007.

### Try it

Show that $\mathrm{RT}^2_2 \to \mathrm{SRT}^2_2$.
Answer: a stable coloring is a coloring, so $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ already gives it an infinite homogeneous set.

Find a solution of $\mathrm{ADS}$ for the order $0 \prec 2 \prec 4 \prec \cdots \prec 5 \prec 3 \prec 1$ on $\mathbb{N}$.
Answer: the even numbers form a computable ascending sequence, and the odd numbers a computable descending one.
So this instance is solved in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$; the strength of $\mathrm{ADS}$ comes from orders whose monotone sequences are all hard to compute.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Logicians measure a theorem by the axioms that it needs.
For most theorems the answer is one of five standard systems of axioms, but some theorems of combinatorics fit none of them.
Here is one: in any endless list of different numbers, you can pick an endless sub-list that only goes up or only goes down.
For the list $3, 1, 4, 5, 9, 2, 6, \dots$ a rising sub-list starts $3, 4, 5, 9$.
The principle $\mathrm{ADS}$ says the same for every infinite ordering, and it is equivalent to none of the five systems.
The zoo is the collection of such in-between statements, with a map of which implies which.

### L2 human

We prove $\mathrm{RT}^2_2 \to \mathrm{EM}$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, renumbering the vertices so that the tournament lives on $\mathbb{N}$.
For $x < y$, color $\{x, y\}$ with $1$ if the edge goes $x \to y$ and with $0$ if it goes $y \to x$.
The coloring has no unbounded quantifier, so it exists by $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension, and $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ gives an infinite homogeneous set $H$.
If the color is $1$, every edge inside $H$ goes from the smaller to the larger number.
So $x \to y$ and $y \to z$ give $x < y < z$ and hence $x \to z$, and the subtournament on $H$ is transitive.
Color $0$ is the same argument with every edge reversed.
The typical work in the zoo pairs such a short reduction with a long construction of an $\omega$-model for the converse.
The trap is to use a set in the reduction whose existence needs more than $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension.

### L3 human

The zoo is not a linear order: $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ and $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ are incomparable, by Jockusch and by Liu.
Modern work measures principles by several reductions at once: implication over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, implication in $\omega$-models, computable reduction and the uniform Weihrauch reduction of computable analysis.
A second line asks for the first-order part of each principle, the arithmetic sentences that it proves; for $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ this is the work of Chong, Slaman and Yang.
The members $\mathrm{WWKL}$ and $\mathrm{DNR}$ connect the zoo to [[algorithmic randomness|KW3V44GPPC]], and the five main systems around it are surveyed in [[reverse mathematics and subsystems of second-order arithmetic|BY8AAFN7C5]].
