## Summary

The Heisenberg picture is the way of describing quantum dynamics in which [[observables|DF5RN297GE]] change in time and state vectors stay fixed.
An observable $A$ becomes $A(t) = U(t)^* A\, U(t)$, where $U(t) = e^{-itH/\hbar}$ is generated by the [[Hamiltonian|VXDEQB46GV]].
It gives the same expectation values as the [[Schrödinger picture|GEHZ2CCH2J]].
Its equation of motion $\frac{d}{dt}A(t) = \frac{i}{\hbar}[H, A(t)]$ has the same form as Hamilton's equations written with the [[Poisson bracket|2S0MXDT4YH]], which makes the link with classical mechanics clear.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the Heisenberg picture the state is a fixed snapshot of how the system was prepared, and the quantities one measures move in time.
For a free particle, the position operator at time $t$ is $X + tP/m$: the initial position plus the velocity times the time, as in classical mechanics.
Expectation values are the same in both pictures; only the bookkeeping differs.
