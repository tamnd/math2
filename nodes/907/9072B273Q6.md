## Proof

We show that induction up to $a$ for a jumped formula $\varphi^+$ gives induction up to $\omega^a$ for $\varphi$, climb the tower $1, \omega, \omega^\omega, \ldots$ by repeated jumps, and get part 2 from Gentzen's consistency proof.
The notation and the formulas $\operatorname{Prog}$ and $\operatorname{TI}$ are those of the statement.
We write $0$ for the least notation, $u + 1$ for the successor, $y + \omega^x$ for the sum, and $y + \omega^u \cdot n$ for $y$ followed by $n$ copies of $\omega^u$.
These are [[primitive recursive|5NVA2JRHPV]] operations on codes.
The tower of notations is $\tau_0 = \omega^0 = 1$ and $\tau_{n+1} = \omega^{\tau_n}$.

**Facts on notations.**
Each fact below is true for the ordinals named, by the properties of [[ordinal arithmetic|KAEDCQ8RM6]] given in brackets.
Each fact is a statement about finite terms.
PA proves it by induction on the length of the terms, with the [[comparison of Cantor normal forms|DXKWBFWCE0]].

- (N1) $\prec$ is a strict linear order, and no notation is below $0$. [Ordinals are well-ordered.]
- (N2) Each notation $x$ is $0$, or $x = u + 1$ for a notation $u$, or $x$ is a limit, which means $x \ne 0$ and $x$ is not a successor. [The last term of a normal form is $\omega^0$ or not.]
- (N3) $z \prec y + 1$ if and only if $z \prec y$ or $z = y$. [The successor of $y$ is the least ordinal above $y$.]
- (N4) $z \prec y + \omega^{u + 1}$ if and only if $z \prec y + \omega^u \cdot n$ for some $n$. [$\omega^{u+1} = \sup_n \omega^u n$, and $\gamma \mapsto y + \gamma$ is continuous.]
- (N5) If $x$ is a limit, then $z \prec y + \omega^x$ if and only if $z \prec y + \omega^u$ for some $u \prec x$. [$\omega^x = \sup_{u < x} \omega^u$, and $\gamma \mapsto y + \gamma$ is continuous.]
- (N6) $y + \omega^u \cdot (n + 1) = (y + \omega^u \cdot n) + \omega^u$, $0 + \omega^x = \omega^x$ and $y + \omega^0 = y + 1$. [Addition is associative, and $0$ is neutral.]
- (N7) For each notation $a$ there is an $n$ with $a \prec \tau_n$. [The tower is cofinal in $\varepsilon_0$.]

**Lemma A (one more step).**
For every notation $a$ and formula $\psi$, $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \operatorname{TI}(a, \psi) \to \operatorname{TI}(a + 1, \psi)$.

1. Argue in PA, and assume $\operatorname{TI}(a, \psi)$ and $\operatorname{Prog}(\psi)$.
   Then $\forall x \prec a \; \psi(x)$.
2. $\operatorname{Prog}(\psi)$ at $x = a$, with step 1, gives $\psi(a)$.
3. By (N3), $x \prec a + 1$ gives $x \prec a$ or $x = a$.
   Steps 1 and 2 give $\psi(x)$ in both cases.

**The jump.**
For a formula $\varphi(x)$, define the formula $\varphi^+(x)$ as follows.

$$\varphi^+(x) :\equiv \forall y \, \bigl( \forall z \prec y \; \varphi(z) \to \forall z \prec y + \omega^x \; \varphi(z) \bigr)$$

In words, $\varphi^+(x)$ says that if $\varphi$ holds below $y$, then $\varphi$ holds for a further $\omega^x$ steps.

**Lemma B.**
For every formula $\varphi$, $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \operatorname{Prog}(\varphi) \to \operatorname{Prog}(\varphi^+)$.

4. Argue in PA, and assume $\operatorname{Prog}(\varphi)$.
   Fix $x$ and assume (H), which is $\forall u \prec x \; \varphi^+(u)$.
   We must show $\varphi^+(x)$.
5. Fix $y$ and assume (A), which is $\forall z \prec y \; \varphi(z)$.
   We must show $\varphi(z)$ for all $z \prec y + \omega^x$.
   By (N2), there are three [[cases|0WCP0PVET6]].
6. Case $x = 0$.
   By (N6) and (N3), $z \prec y + \omega^0$ means $z \prec y$ or $z = y$.
   If $z \prec y$, (A) gives $\varphi(z)$, and if $z = y$, $\operatorname{Prog}(\varphi)$ and (A) give $\varphi(y)$.
7. Case $x = u + 1$.
   Then $u \prec x$, so (H) gives $\varphi^+(u)$.
   Let $\theta(n)$ be the formula $\forall z \prec y + \omega^u \cdot n \; \varphi(z)$.
8. $\theta(0)$ is (A), since $y + \omega^u \cdot 0 = y$.
9. Assume $\theta(n)$, and apply $\varphi^+(u)$ with $y + \omega^u \cdot n$ in place of $y$.
   This gives $\forall z \prec (y + \omega^u \cdot n) + \omega^u \; \varphi(z)$, which is $\theta(n + 1)$ by (N6).
10. The [[induction schema|1QD0KDDGYJ]] of PA for the formula $\theta$ gives $\forall n \, \theta(n)$.
11. Let $z \prec y + \omega^{u+1}$.
    By (N4), $z \prec y + \omega^u \cdot n$ for some $n$, so step 10 gives $\varphi(z)$.
12. Case $x$ a limit.
    Let $z \prec y + \omega^x$, so $z \prec y + \omega^u$ for some $u \prec x$, by (N5).
    (H) gives $\varphi^+(u)$, and with (A) it gives $\varphi(z)$.
13. Steps 6, 11 and 12 give $\varphi^+(x)$.
    Since $x$ was arbitrary, $\operatorname{Prog}(\varphi^+)$ holds.

**Lemma C (the jump).**
For every notation $a$ and formula $\varphi$, $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \operatorname{TI}(a + 1, \varphi^+) \to \operatorname{TI}(\omega^a, \varphi)$.

14. Argue in PA, and assume $\operatorname{TI}(a + 1, \varphi^+)$ and $\operatorname{Prog}(\varphi)$.
    Lemma B gives $\operatorname{Prog}(\varphi^+)$.
15. So $\forall x \prec a + 1 \; \varphi^+(x)$.
    Since $a \prec a + 1$ by (N3), $\varphi^+(a)$ holds.
16. Apply $\varphi^+(a)$ with $y = 0$.
    Its hypothesis $\forall z \prec 0 \; \varphi(z)$ holds because nothing is below $0$, by (N1).
17. So $\forall z \prec 0 + \omega^a \; \varphi(z)$, and $0 + \omega^a = \omega^a$ by (N6).
    This is $\operatorname{TI}(\omega^a, \varphi)$.

**Part 1.**

18. Claim: for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and every formula $\varphi$, $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \operatorname{TI}(\tau_n, \varphi)$.
    We prove this by [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $n$, outside PA, for all formulas at once.
19. Case $n = 0$.
    $\operatorname{TI}(0, \varphi)$ holds in PA, since nothing is below $0$ by (N1).
    Lemma A gives $\operatorname{TI}(0 + 1, \varphi)$, which is $\operatorname{TI}(\tau_0, \varphi)$.
20. Step from $n$ to $n + 1$.
    Let $\varphi$ be a formula.
    The induction hypothesis for the formula $\varphi^+$ gives $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \operatorname{TI}(\tau_n, \varphi^+)$.
21. Lemma A gives $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \operatorname{TI}(\tau_n + 1, \varphi^+)$.
    Lemma C gives $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \operatorname{TI}(\omega^{\tau_n}, \varphi)$, which is $\operatorname{TI}(\tau_{n+1}, \varphi)$.
22. Let $a$ be a notation.
    By (N7) there is an $n$ with $a \prec \tau_n$.
    This is a true primitive recursive statement about two numerals, so PA proves it.
23. By transitivity (N1), PA proves $x \prec a \to x \prec \tau_n$.
    So $\operatorname{TI}(\tau_n, \varphi)$ gives $\operatorname{TI}(a, \varphi)$ in PA, and this proves part 1.

**Part 2.**

24. We cite [[Gentzen's consistency proof|QPV0HNP22S]].
    Primitive recursive arithmetic (PRA) with transfinite induction along $\prec$ for primitive recursive predicates proves $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$.
25. Every theorem of PRA is a theorem of PA, after each primitive recursive function symbol is replaced by its definition in PA.
26. [[Assume, for a contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]], that PA proves $\operatorname{TI}(\varepsilon_0, \varphi)$ for each formula $\varphi$ that expresses a primitive recursive predicate.
27. Then PA can replace each use of transfinite induction in step 24 by modus ponens with $\operatorname{TI}(\varepsilon_0, \varphi)$.
    With step 25, PA proves $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$.
28. By [[Gödel's second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]], a consistent PA does not prove $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$.
    This contradicts step 27.
29. So, if PA is consistent, some formula $\varphi$ that expresses a primitive recursive predicate has $\mathrm{PA} \nvdash \operatorname{TI}(\varepsilon_0, \varphi)$.
    This proves part 2. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Induction along $a$ for the more complex formula $\varphi^+$ gives induction along $\omega^a$ for $\varphi$.
Ordinary induction on $n$ fills each block $y + \omega^u \cdot n$, and the limit cases need no work.
Each jump climbs one level of the tower $1, \omega, \omega^\omega, \ldots$, at the cost of one level of formula complexity.
Finitely many jumps reach any notation below $\varepsilon_0$, but no single proof reaches all of them, and this is why $\varepsilon_0$ is the limit.

## Variants

The same argument can use the accessible part instead of the jump.
In second-order arithmetic, let $\mathrm{Acc}$ be the set of notations $x$ that belong to every progressive set.
Then $\mathrm{Acc}$ is progressive, and the argument of Lemmas B and C shows that $x \in \mathrm{Acc}$ implies $\omega^x \in \mathrm{Acc}$, so every $\tau_n$ lies in $\mathrm{Acc}$.
PA has no set variables, and for one fixed formula $\varphi$ the jump $\varphi^+$ plays the role of the accessible part.

In $\mathrm{ACA}_0$ one proves that $\omega^X$ is a well-order whenever $X$ is one.
Applied to the standard notations, this gives the well-foundedness of each $\tau_n$ by a meta-induction on $n$.

Part 2 also follows from a direct analysis of PA, without the consistency statement.
One embeds PA into an infinitary system with the [[ω-rule|XAMKAB1BZM]] and eliminates cuts.
The bounds of this method show that if PA proves $\operatorname{TI}(a, \varphi)$ for a new predicate symbol $\varphi$, then $a$ names an ordinal below $\varepsilon_0$.
