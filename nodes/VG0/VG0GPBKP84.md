## Summary

An optical aberration is a departure of the rays of a real optical system from the ideal image predicted by the [[paraxial approximation|N111BHFQ2H]].
With aberrations, the rays from one object point do not meet again in one image point, and the image is blurred or deformed.
Aberrations are measured either by the displacement of rays in the image plane or by the deviation of the wavefront from a sphere.
Their analysis and correction are the core of lens design in [[geometrical optics|90F3NWRHVD]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An ideal lens would send every ray from one point of an object to one point of the image.
Real lenses with spherical surfaces do not quite do this: rays through the edge of the lens focus at a slightly different place than rays near the centre.
The result is a blurred spot instead of a sharp point.
Each such defect is called an aberration.
Colour adds another one: glass bends blue light more than red light, so the colours focus at different distances.
