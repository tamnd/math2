## Problem

Let $\Gamma$ be a [[circle|060W5CBZP6]] with centre $O$ in the plane.
Let $A$ and $B$ be two points of the plane that do not lie on $\Gamma$.
Find the points $P$ on $\Gamma$ at which a ray from $A$ is reflected to $B$ by the [[law of reflection|JSTZ7M5VEB]].
These are the points $P$ such that the line $OP$ bisects the angle $APB$.

## Remarks

The circle is the cross-section of a spherical mirror by the plane through $A$, $B$ and the centre of the sphere.
So the problem asks where to look in a spherical mirror to see a given point.
Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen) solved it geometrically with the help of conic sections.
In coordinates, the condition on $P$ leads to a polynomial equation of degree $4$.
Peter M. Neumann proved in 1997 that the points $P$ cannot in general be constructed with ruler and compass, so the problem is not a problem of [[constructible numbers|0QNFRBJF4Q]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

You stand in front of a round mirror, such as a shiny ball, and look at the reflection of a lamp.
At which point of the mirror do you see it?
At that point the mirror must bounce the light from the lamp straight to your eye, with equal angles on both sides of the mirror's normal.
Finding that point is surprisingly hard: it needs the solution of a fourth-degree equation.
