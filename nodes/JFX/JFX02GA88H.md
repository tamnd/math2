## Summary

The Earth tide, or body tide, is the periodic deformation of the solid Earth caused by the [[tidal force|48JY6FBEHB]] of the Moon and the Sun.
The ground rises and falls by a few tens of centimetres twice a day, and gravity and the direction of the vertical change slightly.
Its size is measured by the [[Love numbers|C8HCN8NEQP]], which tell how elastic the Earth is as a whole.
Earth tides must be removed from precise geodetic and gravity measurements, and they are a probe of the Earth's interior.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Moon pulls the near side of the Earth a little more than the centre, and the centre a little more than the far side.
The oceans respond to this stretching, but so does the rock: the whole Earth bulges slightly along the line to the Moon.
As the Earth turns, a point on the ground passes through two bulges a day, so it moves up and down twice a day.
Nobody feels this motion, because everything nearby moves with it.
A sensitive gravimeter does see it: gravity changes by up to a few parts in ten million over the day.
