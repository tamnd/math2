## Summary

To put $\neg (p \land q)$ into clause form without the distributive law, give each part a name.
Let $a$ name $p \land q$ and $b$ name the whole formula.
The clauses $\{ \neg a, p \}$, $\{ \neg a, q \}$, $\{ a, \neg p, \neg q \}$ say $a \leftrightarrow p \land q$, the clauses $\{ \neg b, \neg a \}$, $\{ b, a \}$ say $b \leftrightarrow \neg a$, and the unit clause $\{ b \}$ asks for the whole formula to be true.
These six clauses are satisfiable exactly when $\neg (p \land q)$ is.

In general, let $\varphi$ be a [[propositional formula|TT7WT5HPN9]] built with $\neg$, $\land$ and $\lor$.
For each subformula $\psi$ that is not a variable, take a new variable $x_\psi$, and for a variable $p$ put $x_p = p$.
For $\psi = \alpha \land \beta$, add the [[clauses|T3SXVH3ZZC]] $\{ \neg x_\psi, x_\alpha \}$, $\{ \neg x_\psi, x_\beta \}$, $\{ x_\psi, \neg x_\alpha, \neg x_\beta \}$, which say $x_\psi \leftrightarrow x_\alpha \land x_\beta$.
For $\psi = \alpha \lor \beta$, add $\{ x_\psi, \neg x_\alpha \}$, $\{ x_\psi, \neg x_\beta \}$, $\{ \neg x_\psi, x_\alpha, x_\beta \}$.
For $\psi = \neg \alpha$, add $\{ \neg x_\psi, \neg x_\alpha \}$ and $\{ x_\psi, x_\alpha \}$.
The *Tseitin transformation* $T(\varphi)$ is the set of all these defining clauses together with the unit clause $\{ x_\varphi \}$.
It is in [[conjunctive normal form|0B3D4C8YF2]], its size is linear in $\varphi$, and it is [[equisatisfiable|B37TYWXS74]] with $\varphi$, though not equivalent to it.
It is the standard way to give an arbitrary formula or circuit to a SAT solver or to [[resolution|ZDW6N1PGJ7]].

## Motivation

Every formula has an equivalent conjunctive normal form, but the distributive law can make it exponentially longer.
The formula $(x_1 \land y_1) \lor \cdots \lor (x_n \land y_n)$ has $2n$ literals, and every equivalent conjunctive normal form has at least $2^n$ clauses, as [[the size comparison|JZEBE7HZWQ]] shows.

A satisfiability test does not need an equivalent formula.
It needs only a formula that is satisfiable exactly when the original one is.
Names for subformulas give such a formula: each new variable stands for the value of one subformula, and short clauses force it to take that value.

## Notation

We write $x_\psi$ for the new variable of the subformula $\psi$, and $T(\varphi)$ for the result.
Many texts present the input as a circuit; then the new variables are the outputs of the gates, and $T(\varphi)$ joins one small set of clauses for each gate.
Other connectives get their own defining clauses, and for example $x_\psi \leftrightarrow (x_\alpha \leftrightarrow x_\beta)$ needs four clauses with three literals each.
The name is also written Tseytin.

## Remarks

### Small cases

For $\varphi = p \land q$ we get the clauses $\{ \neg x_\varphi, p \}$, $\{ \neg x_\varphi, q \}$, $\{ x_\varphi, \neg p, \neg q \}$ and $\{ x_\varphi \}$.
The only satisfying assignment is $p = q = x_\varphi = 1$, which matches the only model $p = q = 1$ of $\varphi$.

For $\varphi = p \land \neg p$ the defining clauses force $x_\varphi = 0$.
If $p = 0$, the clause $\{ \neg x_\varphi, p \}$ does it, and if $p = 1$, then $x_{\neg p} = 0$, and the clause $\{ \neg x_\varphi, x_{\neg p} \}$ does it.
So $T(\varphi)$ is unsatisfiable, as $\varphi$ is.

For $(p \land q) \lor (r \land s)$ the transformation gives $3$ new variables and $10$ clauses, worked out in [[a disjunction of conjunctions|9SSASE41M5]].
For $(x_1 \land y_1) \lor \cdots \lor (x_n \land y_n)$ it gives $6n - 2$ clauses, against $2^n$ for the distributive form.

### What is not the Tseitin transformation

Replacing a subformula by a new variable without the defining clauses is not the transformation.
It can turn the unsatisfiable $p \land \neg p$ into the satisfiable $p \land x$, as [[renaming without definitions|YZDFF693BC]] shows.

The distributive conversion is not it either.
It adds no variables and gives an equivalent formula, but it can be exponentially longer.

The defining clauses without the unit clause $\{ x_\varphi \}$ are always satisfiable, by the values of the subformulas under any assignment.
So they are not equisatisfiable with an unsatisfiable $\varphi$.

### Size and correctness

Let $\varphi$ have $m$ occurrences of connectives, and let $P$ be the set of its variables.
Then $T(\varphi)$ has at most $m$ new variables and at most $3m + 1$ clauses, each with at most $3$ literals, so it can be computed in linear time.

Every assignment $v$ to $P$ extends in exactly one way to an assignment that satisfies the defining clauses, namely $x_\psi \mapsto \bar v(\psi)$, where $\bar v$ is the [[truth valuation|H31CS3HK5P]] of $v$.
The proof is a [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]].

1. Put $w = v$ on $P$ and $w(x_\psi) = \bar v(\psi)$ for each subformula $\psi$.
2. For $\psi = \alpha \land \beta$ the defining clauses say $w(x_\psi) = \min(w(x_\alpha), w(x_\beta))$, and this holds because $\bar v(\alpha \land \beta) = \min(\bar v(\alpha), \bar v(\beta))$.
3. The cases $\lor$ and $\neg$ are the same, with $\max$ and with $1 - w(x_\alpha)$.
4. Conversely, if $w$ satisfies the defining clauses and $v$ is its restriction to $P$, induction on $\psi$ shows that the clauses force $w(x_\psi) = \bar v(\psi)$.

So $w$ satisfies $\{ x_\varphi \}$ exactly when $\bar v(\varphi) = 1$.
The models of $\varphi$ correspond one to one to the models of $T(\varphi)$, and the two are equisatisfiable.

### Size and correctness, continued

$T(\varphi)$ is not [[logically equivalent|0JMR2HM2N2]] to $\varphi$, because it mentions new variables.
But $T(\varphi) \models \varphi$, and every model of $\varphi$ extends to a model of $T(\varphi)$.

A formula $\varphi$ is a tautology exactly when $T(\neg \varphi)$ is unsatisfiable.
So a [[resolution refutation|DH1P95R390]] of $T(\neg \varphi)$ proves $\varphi$.

### Watch out

Do not forget the unit clause $\{ x_\varphi \}$.
Without it the clauses are always satisfiable.

$T(\varphi)$ is not equivalent to $\varphi$.
Only satisfiability is kept, so $T(\varphi)$ cannot replace $\varphi$ inside a larger formula.

Do not reuse one new variable for two different subformulas.
Equal subformulas may share a variable, but different ones may not.

Only the values on $P$ of a model of $T(\varphi)$ form a model of $\varphi$; the new variables are helpers.

### History

Grigori Tseitin introduced the transformation in the paper "On the complexity of derivation in propositional calculus".
He presented it at the Leningrad Seminar on Mathematical Logic in 1966, and the English translation appeared in 1970.
The same idea of naming subformulas gives the reduction of satisfiability of formulas and circuits to 3-CNF satisfiability in the theory of [[NP-completeness|REKDJ8V504]].

### Try it

How many clauses does $T(p \lor \neg q)$ have?
There are two connectives: $\neg q$ gives $2$ clauses, the $\lor$ gives $3$, and the unit clause gives $1$, so $6$ clauses.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a long rule is built from many small parts.
Writing it out in the form that a computer checker wants can make it huge.
Instead we give each part a short name, like "A" for "$p$ and $q$".
Then we add a few small rules that say what each name means.
The checker now works with many small rules instead of one huge one, and the new rules can all be met exactly when the old rule can.

### L2 human

We check the six clauses of $\neg (p \land q)$ from the Summary.

1. One model: $p = 1$, $q = 0$ makes $\varphi$ true.
   Then $a = 0$ and $b = 1$, and each of the six clauses has a true literal.
2. The converse: $\{ b \}$ and $\{ \neg b, \neg a \}$ give $a = 0$.
   Then $\{ a, \neg p, \neg q \}$ gives $p = 0$ or $q = 0$, so $\varphi$ is true.

To prove that $\varphi$ is a tautology, a SAT solver or a resolution prover tests $T(\neg \varphi)$ for unsatisfiability.

### L3 human

The transformation is a linear-time reduction from circuit satisfiability to 3-CNF satisfiability.
It gives one route to the [[Cook-Levin theorem|CTK8AH98Z1]]: the computation of a machine is a [[Boolean circuit|SQXNFGVADX]], and the circuit becomes a conjunctive normal form.
When a subformula occurs only positively, one direction of its definition is enough.
This refinement keeps equisatisfiability, but it loses the one to one correspondence of models.
Tseitin introduced the idea to compare the lengths of proofs in propositional systems.
His extended resolution allows such definitions inside a refutation, and it is one of the strong systems of [[propositional proof complexity|CBAF7J051K]].
