## Summary

The hour angle of a celestial object is an angle measured westward along the celestial equator.
It runs from the observer's meridian to the hour circle of the object.
It tells how long ago the object crossed the meridian, in units of time.
It links the fixed [[equatorial coordinates|QBEJJPMWV1]] of an object to what a local observer sees at a given time.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a star is due south and highest in the sky (for a northern observer), its hour angle is $0$.
As the Earth turns, the star moves west and its hour angle grows by about one hour every hour.
An hour angle of $+2^{\mathrm h}$ means the star crossed the meridian about two hours ago.
For the Sun, the hour angle is basically the time shown by a sundial, shifted by $12$ hours.
