## Definition

Let a material melt or freeze at temperature $T_m$, with [[latent heat|FN6Z9QKAH5]] $L > 0$ per unit mass.
Let $c_p > 0$ be the specific [[heat capacity|SYYGZ18FF5]] of the relevant phase.
Let $\Delta T > 0$ be a characteristic temperature difference, such as $\abs{T_b - T_m}$ for a boundary temperature $T_b$.
The *Stefan number* is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]]
$$\mathrm{Ste} = \frac{c_p \, \Delta T}{L}.$$

## Remarks

Example: for liquid water, $c_p \approx 4.2$ kilojoules per kilogram per kelvin and $L \approx 334$ kilojoules per kilogram.
A boundary held $20$ kelvin above $0$ degrees Celsius gives $\mathrm{Ste} \approx 4.2 \cdot 20 / 334 \approx 0.25$.

Non-example: the ratio $L / (c_p \, \Delta T)$ is the reciprocal of the Stefan number, not the Stefan number; some authors use it, so the convention should be checked.

With the latent heat per unit volume $\rho L$, the same number reads $\rho c_p \Delta T / (\rho L)$.
