## Summary

Plate theory models a flat elastic body whose thickness is small compared with its other dimensions.
Examples are a floor slab, a ship deck and a sheet of metal.
It replaces the three-dimensional equations of [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] by equations on the two-dimensional mid-plane.
An assumption on how lines across the thickness deform reduces the unknowns to the deflection of the mid-plane and a few other fields.
The main theories are [[Kirchhoff–Love plate theory|9E6E0SCEYA]] for thin plates and [[Mindlin–Reissner plate theory|M8F0SGSGQW]] for thicker ones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A plate is the two-dimensional cousin of a beam.
Push on the middle of a thin metal sheet held at its edges and it bows out in a smooth dome.
Plate theory tracks only the shape of the middle surface of the sheet and how stiff the sheet is against bending.
Because the bending stiffness grows like the cube of the thickness, a plate twice as thick bends eight times less under the same load.
