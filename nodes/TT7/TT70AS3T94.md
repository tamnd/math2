## Summary

A color Lie algebra is an algebra graded by an abelian group $\Gamma$.
Its bracket satisfies antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity up to scalar factors, given by a commutation factor on $\Gamma$.
For the trivial group it is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]], and for $\Gamma = \Z/2\Z$ with the usual signs it is a [[Lie superalgebra|7CZ4Q5H78N]].
Color Lie algebras, also called $\varepsilon$-Lie algebras, give a common frame for these two theories and allow more general gradings.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a Lie superalgebra, elements are even or odd, and swapping two odd elements costs a minus sign.
A color Lie algebra allows more "colors" than even and odd: the colors are the elements of an abelian group.
Swapping two elements of colors $\alpha$ and $\beta$ costs a scalar factor $\varepsilon(\alpha, \beta)$ instead of a sign.
The rules on $\varepsilon$ make sure that swapping back undoes the factor, and that the factors combine well with the grading.
An associative algebra graded by $\Gamma$ gives a color Lie algebra through a twisted commutator, just as an associative algebra gives a Lie algebra.
