## Statement

Let $\Omega \subseteq \R^3$ be an open set, and let a homogeneous isotropic material in [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] have [[Poisson's ratio|NWYZ9QBA84]] $\nu$, with $-1 < \nu < 1/2$.
Let $b \colon \Omega \to \R^3$ be a body force of class $C^1$.
Let $\sigma$ be a [[stress|KG2XWEMJ30]] field of class $C^2$ on $\Omega$ that is produced by a displacement field.
Assume that $\sigma$ satisfies the equilibrium equations $\partial_j \sigma_{ij} + b_i = 0$.
Then, for all $i, j$,
$$\Delta \sigma_{ij} + \frac{1}{1 + \nu} \, \partial_i \partial_j (\tr \sigma) = -\frac{\nu}{1 - \nu} \, \delta_{ij} \, \partial_k b_k - \partial_i b_j - \partial_j b_i,$$
where $\Delta$ is the [[Laplacian|0E0B6RA8K0]], $\delta_{ij}$ is the Kronecker delta, and repeated indices are summed.

## Remarks

The proof writes the strain in terms of the stress by the inverted Hooke's law $\varepsilon = \frac{1 + \nu}{E} \sigma - \frac{\nu}{E} (\tr \sigma) I$ and substitutes it into the [[Saint-Venant compatibility condition|F7WK9V3GBP]].
Contracting the result and using the equilibrium equations to replace divergences of $\sigma$ by derivatives of $b$ gives first $\Delta (\tr \sigma) = -\frac{1 + \nu}{1 - \nu} \partial_k b_k$, and then the six equations above.
Conversely, if $\Omega$ is simply connected, a symmetric stress field that satisfies the equilibrium equations and these six equations comes from a displacement field.
With zero body force the equations reduce to Beltrami's form $\Delta \sigma_{ij} + \frac{1}{1 + \nu} \partial_i \partial_j (\tr \sigma) = 0$; then $\tr \sigma$ is harmonic and each $\sigma_{ij}$ is biharmonic.
Example: a constant stress field with $b = 0$ satisfies them, since every term is a derivative of a constant; it is the stress of an affine displacement.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In elasticity one can try to find the stresses directly, without first finding how the body moves.
The balance of forces alone does not determine them: there are six stress components and only three balance equations.
The missing information is that the stresses must come from an actual deformation that keeps the body in one piece.
The Beltrami–Michell equations express this requirement in terms of the stresses alone.

### L2 human

The stress formulation of [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] solves for $\sigma$ from the equilibrium equations and the Beltrami–Michell equations, together with traction conditions on the boundary.
It is convenient when all boundary data are tractions.
In two dimensions the same idea, combined with an Airy stress function, leads to a single biharmonic equation.
The equations depend only on Poisson's ratio, so in this setting the stress field does not depend on the shear modulus.
