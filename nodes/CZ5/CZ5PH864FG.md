## Summary

A cosmological horizon is a limit on what an observer in an expanding universe can see or influence.
It comes from the finite speed of light and the expansion history.
The two main kinds are the particle horizon and the event horizon.
The particle horizon bounds what can have been seen up to now, and the event horizon bounds what can ever be seen.
Whether each one exists depends on the behaviour of the [[scale factor|J70F32P86W]] at early and at late times.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light needs time to travel, and the universe has a finite age, so some regions are too far for their light to have reached us yet.
If the expansion speeds up enough, some regions are so far that their light will never reach us, however long we wait.
These two boundaries are horizons in the sky of every observer.
In a universe dominated by a cosmological constant, the event horizon sits at a fixed proper distance $1/H$, in units with $c = 1$.
