## Definition

Let $\Phi$ be a [[root system|AVVV93CASN]] in a Euclidean space $E$.
For $\alpha \in \Phi$, let $s_\alpha$ be the [[reflection|06BRDF3F0E]] of $E$ in the hyperplane orthogonal to $\alpha$.
The *Weyl group* $W(\Phi)$ is the subgroup of the [[orthogonal group|0TKYAEJ8N7]] $O(E)$ generated by the reflections $s_\alpha$ for $\alpha \in \Phi$.

## Remarks

The [[group|G5S1M9]] $W(\Phi)$ is finite: each element permutes the finite set $\Phi$, and an element that fixes every root is the identity, since $\Phi$ spans $E$.
Example: for $A_{n-1}$, the roots $\varepsilon_i - \varepsilon_j$ in the hyperplane $x_1 + \cdots + x_n = 0$ of $\R^n$ give reflections that swap two coordinates, so $W(A_{n-1})$ is the symmetric group $S_n$ acting by permuting coordinates.
Non-example: the rotation of the hexagon $A_2$ by $60^\circ$ maps $\Phi$ onto $\Phi$, but it is not in $W(A_2) \cong S_3$.
It is $-\id$ composed with a rotation by $240^\circ$, and $-\id$ is not a permutation of the coordinates of the plane $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0$.
For a base $\Delta$ of $\Phi$, the simple reflections $s_\alpha$ with $\alpha \in \Delta$ already generate $W(\Phi)$, and they make it a [[Coxeter group|G6FND7X69J]].
For a compact connected Lie group, the [[Weyl group of a compact Lie group|EZGTEK3HQN]] is the Weyl group of its root system.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Weyl group collects all the motions obtained by reflecting again and again in the mirrors orthogonal to the roots.
It is a finite group of symmetries of the root system, but in general not all of them.
