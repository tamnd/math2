## Summary

The Windkessel model describes the arterial system as an elastic chamber that fills with each heartbeat and drains through the resistance of the small vessels.
It was formulated by the physiologist Otto Frank at the end of the nineteenth century.
In its simplest form it is a linear [[ordinary differential equation|0NT64JV96A]] for the arterial pressure.
This is the same equation as for a resistor and a capacitor in parallel.
It explains why blood pressure decays roughly exponentially between beats and is used to estimate arterial compliance.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The large arteries stretch when the heart pushes blood in, a bit like a balloon.
Between beats the stretched walls squeeze back and keep blood flowing to the body.
So the pressure does not drop to zero when the heart rests, but falls off smoothly.
The name comes from the air chamber of old fire engines, which smoothed the jerky flow of a hand pump in the same way.
