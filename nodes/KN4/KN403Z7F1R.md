## Summary

De Broglie–Bohm theory, also called pilot-wave theory or Bohmian mechanics, is a deterministic [[hidden-variable theory|QRW6NE1VY7]] of nonrelativistic quantum mechanics.
Particles have definite positions at all times, and they move along trajectories guided by the [[wave function|E9WK6KJ4SM]], which itself evolves by the [[Schrödinger equation|EPZR6H2WJN]].
If the initial positions are distributed according to $\abs{\psi}^2$, the theory reproduces the statistical predictions of standard quantum mechanics.
It is nonlocal: the velocity of one particle can depend on the positions of distant particles.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In this picture an electron is a real particle with a definite position, carried along by a wave like a boat on a current.
The wave is the usual quantum wave function, and it tells the particle how fast and in which direction to move.
We cannot know the exact starting position, only its probability distribution, and this is where quantum randomness comes from.
In the double-slit experiment, each particle goes through one slit, but the wave goes through both and steers the particles into the interference pattern.
