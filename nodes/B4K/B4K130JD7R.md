## Example

[[Minimal logic|V7MTF4P01E]] $\mathsf{ML}$ is contained in $\mathsf{CPC}$ and closed under modus ponens and substitution.
We show that it is not an [[intermediate logic|H5NSKEP3P2]], because it does not contain $\mathsf{IPC}$.

1. The formula $\bot \to p$ is an axiom of $\mathsf{IPC}$.
2. In $\mathsf{ML}$, no axiom mentions $\bot$, so $\bot$ behaves as a propositional variable.
3. Consider the classical valuation that makes $\bot$ true, as if it were a variable, and $p$ false.
4. Every axiom of $\mathsf{ML}$ is true under this valuation, and modus ponens preserves truth.
5. The formula $\bot \to p$ is false under it. So $\mathsf{ML} \nvdash \bot \to p$.
6. Hence $\mathsf{IPC} \not\subseteq \mathsf{ML}$, and $\mathsf{ML}$ is not intermediate.

## Remarks

$\mathsf{ML}$ is a subintuitionistic logic: it lies below $\mathsf{IPC}$.
Adding $\bot \to p$ to $\mathsf{ML}$ gives $\mathsf{IPC}$, so $\mathsf{ML}$ fails the definition only in its lower bound.
Its negation fragment is still strong: $\mathsf{ML}$ proves $\neg \neg (p \vee \neg p)$; see [[minimal logic|3MBG95617E]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Minimal logic drops the principle "from a contradiction, anything follows".
So it is weaker than intuitionistic logic, not stronger.
Intermediate logics must be at least as strong as intuitionistic logic.

### L2 human

1. Read $\bot$ as an ordinary letter in $\mathsf{ML}$.
2. A truth table where this letter is true and $p$ is false refutes $\bot \to p$.
3. So $\mathsf{ML}$ is a proper sublogic of $\mathsf{IPC}$.

The typical use is to separate the two directions of comparison with $\mathsf{IPC}$.
A trap: $\mathsf{ML}$ is contained in $\mathsf{CPC}$, so the upper bound alone does not make a logic intermediate.
