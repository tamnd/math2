## Statement

Let $F$ be an [[algebraic number field|MWBDFS2KJ5]] with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_F$, with $r_1$ real embeddings and $r_2$ pairs of complex conjugate non-real embeddings.
Let $\mu(F)$ be the finite group of roots of unity in $F$.
Then the determinant induces an isomorphism $K_1(\mathcal{O}_F) \cong \mathcal{O}_F^\times$, and
$$K_1(\mathcal{O}_F) \cong \mu(F) \times \Z^{r_1 + r_2 - 1}.$$
In particular $K_1(\mathcal{O}_F)$ is a finitely generated abelian group of rank $r_1 + r_2 - 1$.

## Remarks

For any commutative ring $A$ the determinant gives a split surjection $K_1(A) \to A^\times$ with kernel $SK_1(A)$, see [[K1 of a ring|SXVNJY4XFY]] and [[SK1|AAB05HS00Y]].
For $A = \mathcal{O}_F$, Bass, Milnor and Serre proved $SK_1(\mathcal{O}_F) = 0$, so $K_1(\mathcal{O}_F) = \mathcal{O}_F^\times$.
The second isomorphism is then [[Dirichlet's unit theorem|1D99CSKX6N]].

Example: for $F = \Q(\sqrt{2})$, $K_1(\Z[\sqrt{2}]) \cong \{\pm 1\} \times \Z$, generated by $-1$ and the unit $1 + \sqrt{2}$.
For $F = \Q$, $K_1(\Z) = \{\pm 1\}$, the determinants of invertible integer matrices.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The group $K_1$ of a ring records invertible matrices up to elementary row and column operations.
For a ring of integers of a number field, only the determinant survives, so $K_1$ is just the group of units.
Dirichlet's theorem then says how big that group is: a finite part of roots of unity times a free part of rank $r_1 + r_2 - 1$.

### L2 human

This is the first case of the pattern found by Borel: the rank of $K_n(\mathcal{O}_F)$ depends only on $n$, $r_1$ and $r_2$.
For $n = 1$ the logarithmic embedding of units plays the role that Borel's regulator plays for odd $n \ge 3$.
Its covolume is the classical [[regulator|RWK0HNF1NA]] that appears in the class number formula.
