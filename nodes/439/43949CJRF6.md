## Summary

In [[continuum mechanics|X01DZTS224]] a field can be written as a function of the material point and time: this is the Lagrangian or material description.
It can also be written as a function of the place in space and time: this is the Eulerian or spatial description.
The two descriptions are linked by the motion of the body.
Solid mechanics often prefers the Lagrangian form, fluid mechanics the Eulerian form, and the change between them produces the material derivative.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of measuring the temperature of a river.
You can drift with a floating thermometer and record what it reads: that is the Lagrangian view, following one piece of water.
Or you can stand on a bridge with a thermometer fixed in the water: that is the Eulerian view, watching one place while different water passes by.
Both describe the same river; the motion of the water converts one record into the other.
