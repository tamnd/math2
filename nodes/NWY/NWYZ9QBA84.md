## Summary

Poisson's ratio $\nu$ measures how much a material contracts sideways when it is stretched along one axis.
It is minus the ratio of the transverse strain to the axial strain in uniaxial tension.
For isotropic linear elastic materials it lies between $-1$ and $1/2$, and together with [[Young's modulus|T8ENJX1905]] it determines the material.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stretch a rubber band and it gets thinner.
Poisson's ratio compares the thinning with the lengthening.
If a bar gets $1\%$ longer and $0.3\%$ narrower, its Poisson's ratio is $0.3$, a typical value for metals.
Rubber is close to $0.5$, which means it keeps its volume, and cork is close to $0$, which is why it works well as a bottle stopper.
