## Summary

A stellar system has many weak gravitational encounters between pairs of stars.
Its two-body relaxation time is the time after which these encounters have changed the velocity of a typical star by about its own size.
For a system of $N$ equal stars it is about $N/(8 \ln N)$ crossing times.
On shorter times a star moves in the smooth mean field of the system, as in collisionless [[stellar dynamics|4V8BT1ZD00]].
On longer times encounters redistribute energy between stars and drive the system toward mass segregation and core collapse.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each time a star passes another star, it is deflected a little.
One distant passage does almost nothing, but there are very many of them, and their effects add up like the steps of a random walk.
The relaxation time is how long it takes until these small kicks have completely changed the star's motion.
For a galaxy with $10^{11}$ stars this takes far longer than the age of the universe, so galaxies are effectively collisionless.
For a globular cluster with $10^5$ stars it is much shorter than its age, so encounters shape the cluster.
