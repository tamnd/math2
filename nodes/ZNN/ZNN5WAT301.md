## Summary

The moment tensor of a seismic source is a symmetric $3 \times 3$ tensor that describes the source as a set of force couples at one point.
The seismic waves far from a small source depend on the source only through its moment tensor and its time history.
It covers faulting, explosions, and more complex sources such as collapses and volcanic events in one formalism.
Moment tensors are routinely estimated from long-period seismograms for every large earthquake.

## Explanations

### L1 human

From far away, an earthquake looks like a point that pushes and pulls the surrounding rock in certain directions.
Each push–pull pattern is a pair of opposite forces a small distance apart, called a force couple.
The moment tensor lists the strength of the couples in all combinations of the three directions, nine numbers of which six are independent.
A fault slip gives a pattern of two balanced couples; an explosion gives equal pushes in all directions.
