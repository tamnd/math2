## Summary

Ruin theory studies the surplus of an insurance company that collects premiums and pays random claims.
It asks how likely the surplus is ever to become negative.
The central quantity is the ruin probability as a function of the initial capital.
The classical setting is the [[Cramér–Lundberg model|F2NTQY7J54]], in which claims arrive as a Poisson process.
Results such as the [[Lundberg inequality|TSN5GZV241]] tell an insurer how much capital and how high a premium keep the ruin probability small.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An insurer starts with some money, receives premiums at a steady rate and pays claims that come at random times with random sizes.
Its balance goes up smoothly between claims and drops at each claim.
Ruin means the balance falls below zero at some moment.
Ruin theory computes the chance of this, and shows how it shrinks as the starting capital grows.
It is a continuous-time cousin of the [[gambler's ruin|0WFMKK7DFZ]] problem.
