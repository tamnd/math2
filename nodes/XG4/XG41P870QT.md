## Definition

Let $S$ be a [[semigroup|0XMEM406JM]], and let $\ell^\infty(S)$ be the space of bounded functions $S \to \R$.
A *mean* on $S$ is a linear map $m \colon \ell^\infty(S) \to \R$ with $m(f) \ge 0$ whenever $f \ge 0$, and $m(\mathbf{1}) = 1$.
For $s \in S$ and $f \in \ell^\infty(S)$, let $(\ell_s f)(t) = f(st)$ and $(r_s f)(t) = f(ts)$.

1. $S$ is *left amenable* if there is a mean $m$ with $m(\ell_s f) = m(f)$ for all $s \in S$ and $f \in \ell^\infty(S)$.
2. $S$ is *right amenable* if there is a mean $m$ with $m(r_s f) = m(f)$ for all $s$ and $f$.
3. $S$ is *amenable* if there is a mean that is both left and right invariant.

## Remarks

For a group, left amenability, right amenability and amenability all agree with the usual notion of [[amenable group|CEAT9BESKK]] and [[invariant mean|ZYNY2VFECQ]].
A semigroup that is both left and right amenable is amenable.

Example: $S = (\N, +)$ is amenable.
Let $m_n(f) = \frac1n \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} f(k)$; then $\abs{m_n(\ell_s f) - m_n(f)} \le 2s\norm{f}_\infty / n$.
A weak-$*$ cluster point of $(m_n)$ is a mean invariant under all $\ell_s$, and since $S$ is commutative, $r_s = \ell_s$.

Non-example: the free semigroup $S$ on two letters $a, b$ is not left amenable.
Let $f = \mathbf{1}_{aS}$, the indicator of the words that begin with $a$.
Then $\ell_a f = \mathbf{1}$, so an invariant mean would give $m(\mathbf{1}_{aS}) = m(\mathbf{1}) = 1$, and likewise $m(\mathbf{1}_{bS}) = 1$.
Since $aS$ and $bS$ are disjoint, $m(\mathbf{1}_{aS \cup bS}) = 2$, which is impossible for a mean.
