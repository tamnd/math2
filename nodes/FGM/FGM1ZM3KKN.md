## Summary

A Leibniz algebra is a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] in which multiplication by a fixed element is a derivation of the product.
Every [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] is a Leibniz algebra, and a Leibniz algebra in which every square $[x, x]$ vanishes is a Lie algebra.
So Leibniz algebras are "Lie algebras without antisymmetry".
They were studied by Loday, who introduced a Leibniz homology theory for them.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a Lie algebra, the Jacobi identity can be read as a product rule: bracketing with $a$ distributes over a bracket $[b, c]$ like a derivative over a product.
A Leibniz algebra keeps only this product rule and drops the rule $[x, y] = -[y, x]$.
So the square $[x, x]$ of an element may be nonzero.
For example, on the plane with basis $x, y$, the product with $[x, x] = y$ and all other products of basis vectors zero is a Leibniz algebra that is not a Lie algebra.
