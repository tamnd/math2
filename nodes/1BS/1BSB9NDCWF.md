## Summary

Thermal runaway is a self-accelerating rise of temperature in a system that produces heat.
It happens when warming the system raises its rate of heat production faster than its rate of heat loss.
Then no balance between production and loss is reached, and the temperature keeps rising.
It is the mechanism of thermal explosions of reacting mixtures and of failures of batteries and electronic components.
The classical models are [[Semenov's theory|7ZES3H0JJG]] and [[Frank-Kamenetskii theory|S8BJBSH8KW]].
See the [[definition|9MS4X2KC1H]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a pile of oily rags that slowly oxidises and warms up.
A warmer pile reacts faster and so produces even more heat.
If the pile can shed this heat to the air quickly enough, it settles at a slightly raised temperature.
If it cannot, the temperature climbs faster and faster until the pile catches fire.
This loop, in which heat makes more heat, is thermal runaway.
