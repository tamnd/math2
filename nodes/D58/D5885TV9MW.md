## Example

To find a power or the inverse of a block diagonal matrix, we find the power or the inverse of each block.
Let $A_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A_2 = (3)$, and let
$$M = \operatorname{diag}(A_1, A_2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

1. By the [[block multiplication rule|WR3RCF5PSQ]], the off-diagonal blocks of $M^2$ are $A_1 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot A_2 = 0$, so $M^2 = \operatorname{diag}(A_1^2, A_2^2)$.
2. The blocks are $A_1^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A_2^2 = (9)$, so
$$M^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 \end{pmatrix}.$$
3. If $A_1^m = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & m \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, then
$$A_1^{m+1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & m \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & m + 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},$$
so by induction this formula holds for every $m \ge 1$.
4. Applying step 1 repeatedly gives $M^m = \operatorname{diag}(A_1^m, A_2^m)$, that is,
$$M^m = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & m & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3^m \end{pmatrix}.$$
5. The blocks have inverses $A_1^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A_2^{-1} = (1/3)$, and we put $N = \operatorname{diag}(A_1^{-1}, A_2^{-1})$.
6. The block rule gives $MN = \operatorname{diag}(A_1 A_1^{-1}, A_2 A_2^{-1}) = \operatorname{diag}(I_2, 1) = I_3$, and in the same way $NM = I_3$.
7. So $M$ is [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]] and
$$M^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1/3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

## Remarks

### What the blocks buy you

The matrix $M$ is not diagonal, and the block $A_1$ cannot be diagonalized, since its only eigenvalue is $1$ and $A_1 \neq I_2$.
Even so, the block structure turns each computation into a $2 \times 2$ computation and a $1 \times 1$ computation.
The formula of step 4 with $m = -1$ gives the inverse of step 7, and the same induction applied to $M^{-1}$ shows that the formula holds for every integer $m$.

### Watch out

Only the blocks are inverted, not the entries.
Inverting each entry of $A_1$ would need $1/0$, and the inverse of $M$ has the entry $-1$ in a place where $M$ has $1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To square a block diagonal matrix, square each block.
To invert it, invert each block.
Here the top block counts up, $A_1^m$ has $m$ in its corner, while the bottom block multiplies by $3$ each time.

### L2 human

The two blocks act on the span of $e_1, e_2$ and on the span of $e_3$, and they do not mix these two subspaces.
So each power or inverse of $M$ also keeps the two parts apart, and the [[definition|0M0SNKZ482]] of a block diagonal matrix is preserved.
The same holds for any polynomial: $p(M) = \operatorname{diag}(p(A_1), p(A_2))$, so for $p(x) = (x - 1)^2 (x - 3)$ you get $p(A_1) = (A_1 - I)^2 (A_1 - 3I) = 0$, since $(A_1 - I)^2 = 0$, and $p(3) = 0$, hence $p(M) = 0$.
