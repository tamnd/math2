## Definition

Let $L$ be a signature and let $\mathcal{K}$ be a class of $L$-structures.
For $L$-structures $M \subseteq N$, the structure $M$ is *existentially closed in* $N$, written $M \preceq_1 N$, if every existential [[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] with parameters in $M$ that is true in $N$ is true in $M$.
A structure $M \in \mathcal{K}$ is *existentially closed in* $\mathcal{K}$ if $M \preceq_1 N$ for every $N \in \mathcal{K}$ that has $M$ as a [[substructure|676H7EGTKJ]].

For an $L$-[[theory|92SWXST1ZM]] $T$, an *existentially closed model of* $T$ is a [[model|MVCH6N9SNW]] of $T$ that is existentially closed in the class of models of $T$.

## Notation

The abbreviation e.c. is common.
The class of existentially closed models of $T$ is often written $\mathcal{E}_T$ or $T^{\mathrm{ec}}$.
In group theory and ring theory one also says *algebraically closed* group, field or division ring.

## Remarks

An existential formula has the form $\exists \bar y \, \chi(\bar x, \bar y)$ with $\chi$ quantifier-free.
Every quantifier-free formula is a disjunction of conjunctions of literals, and $\exists$ distributes over $\vee$.
So it suffices to check formulas $\exists \bar y \, \chi$ where $\chi$ is a conjunction of atomic and negated atomic formulas: a finite system of equations and inequations.

Example: in the class of fields, a field is existentially closed exactly when it is algebraically closed; see [[this example|2STE7K6ERM]].

Non-example: the field $\Q$ is not existentially closed among fields, since $y \cdot y = 1 + 1$ has a solution in $\Q(\sqrt 2)$ but not in $\Q$; see [[this non-example|DP933VYXJV]].

Some authors define e.c. models of $T$ as structures that are e.c. in the class of models of the universal part $T_\forall$.
For an [[inductive theory|G8TRE2KSRV]] the two definitions give the same structures.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A model is existentially closed when it already contains every solution that it could get from a larger model.
If a system of equations with numbers from the model has a solution in some larger model, it must have one in the model itself.
For example, among fields, the complex numbers are existentially closed, and the rational numbers are not: $x^2 = 2$ has no rational solution.

### L2 human

We check that $\R$ is not existentially closed among fields.

1. The field $\C$ contains $\R$ as a subfield.
2. The existential sentence $\exists y \, (y \cdot y + 1 = 0)$ is true in $\C$, with $y = i$.
3. In $\R$, every square is at least $0$, so $y \cdot y + 1 \ge 1$, and the sentence is false.
4. So $\R \not\preceq_1 \C$, and $\R$ is not existentially closed.

The typical use of the definition is in a chain construction, which builds an existentially closed model above a given model.
A trap is to forget the class $\mathcal{K}$: $\R$ is existentially closed among ordered fields, but not among fields.
