## Summary

This part of [[linear algebra|GN8GYSDZFX]] asks when a square matrix can be undone, and what elimination says about it when it can.
It starts with the inverse of a matrix, and it shows that a matrix has an inverse exactly when row reduction reaches the identity.
Each row operation is multiplication by an elementary matrix, so an invertible matrix is a product of elementary matrices, and recording the operations computes the inverse.
The invertible matrix theorem then collects the many equivalent ways of saying that a matrix is invertible.
The invertible matrices form the general linear group, and the matrices of determinant $1$ form the special linear group, which is generated by the transvections alone.
Elimination without row exchanges, written as a product, is the LU decomposition, and its block form leads to the Schur complement, the block inverse formula and the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury update of an inverse.
The sub-area ends with matrices that are not square or not invertible: with row and column operations together, only the rank is left.
