## Summary

Denotational semantics gives meaning to a programming language by mapping each program to a mathematical object, its denotation.
A typical denotation is a function between sets or between domains.
The map is compositional: the meaning of a compound program is computed from the meanings of its parts.
Christopher Strachey and Dana Scott founded the approach, and Scott built [[domains|1SAAZG46DG]] so that recursive programs could be given meanings as [[least fixed points|DBD64TH52D]].
It lets one prove two programs equivalent by showing that they have the same denotation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A program can be seen as a recipe that runs step by step, or as the function it computes.
Denotational semantics takes the second view: a program that doubles numbers means the mathematical function $n \mapsto 2n$.
To find the meaning of a long program, one combines the meanings of its pieces, just as one evaluates $2 \cdot (3 + 4)$ from $3$, $4$ and the operations.
A program that may loop forever means a partial function, undefined where it does not stop.
Loops are handled by approximating them step by step and taking the limit of the approximations.
