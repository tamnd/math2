## Summary

Einstein–Cartan theory is a theory of gravity in which the connection of spacetime is compatible with the metric but may have [[torsion|JZNQAN00ZJ]].
Curvature is coupled to the energy and momentum of matter, as in general relativity, and torsion is coupled to the intrinsic spin of matter.
Élie Cartan proposed torsion as a part of spacetime geometry in 1922, and Sciama and Kibble later derived the theory from gauging the Poincaré group.
In vacuum and for spinless matter it gives the same predictions as general relativity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

General relativity says that spacetime can bend.
Einstein–Cartan theory says that it can also twist, and that the twisting is caused by the spin of particles such as electrons.
The twisting is confined to where spinning matter actually is; it does not spread out like curvature does.
So the differences from general relativity are tiny except at enormous densities, such as in the very early universe.
