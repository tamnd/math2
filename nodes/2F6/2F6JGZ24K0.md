## Definition

Let $\mu > 0$ be the gravitational parameter of a central body, and let a body move with position $r(t) \in \R^3 \setminus \set{0}$ relative to it.
Its *perturbing acceleration* is
$$f = r'' + \frac{\mu\, r}{\norm{r}^3},$$
the part of its acceleration not explained by the central inverse-square attraction.
The motion is *perturbed* when $f$ is not identically zero.
The *orbital perturbation* is the resulting deviation of the motion from a [[Kepler orbit|BXFSBB8Q46]].
It is measured by the change in time of the [[osculating orbit|KAJTW4B41Q]].

## Remarks

Perturbations are called secular when the osculating elements drift steadily, and periodic when they oscillate.

Example: the flattening of the Earth adds to the potential a term with coefficient $J_2 \approx 1.08 \times 10^{-3}$.
To first order, its averaged effect makes the ascending node of a satellite turn at the rate
$$\frac{d\Omega}{dt} = -\frac{3}{2}\, n J_2 \left( \frac{R}{p} \right)^2 \cos i.$$
Here $n$ is the mean motion, $R$ the radius of the Earth, $p = a(1 - e^2)$ and $i$ the inclination.
Sun-synchronous satellites choose $i$ so that this rate matches the yearly motion of the Sun.

Non-example: in the exact two-body problem, $f = 0$, and the osculating orbit never changes.
