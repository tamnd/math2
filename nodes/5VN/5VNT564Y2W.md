## Summary

Call a number safe when all numbers below it are safe.
Then $0$ is safe because nothing is below it, then $1$, then $2$, and in the end every natural number is safe.
The safe numbers form the least set closed under this rule, and a theory of inductive definitions adds a name for such a least set to arithmetic.

The theory $\mathrm{ID}_1$ extends [[Peano arithmetic|0QFT7Z76N5]] as follows.
For each arithmetical formula $A(X, x)$ in which the set variable $X$ occurs only positively, it adds a predicate $I_A$ with two axioms: $I_A$ is closed under $A$, and $I_A$ is contained in every class that a formula defines and that is closed under $A$.
So $I_A$ is the [[least fixed point|DBD64TH52D]] of the operator, as far as the language can say it.
In $\mathrm{ID}_\nu$ the definitions are iterated along a primitive recursive well-ordering of type $\nu$, and the operator at stage $\mu$ may use the sets of earlier stages in any way, positively or negatively.
The exact axioms are in the [[definition|45GQ3KQVT3]].

## Motivation

Many sets in logic are defined from below, as the least set closed under some rules.
Examples are the [[well-founded part|0DQPNJNPYK]] of a relation, the set of well-founded computable trees, and [[Kleene's O|WHAP0K600D]].
Such a set need not be arithmetical: the well-founded part of a computable relation can be $\Pi^1_1$-complete.
The theory $\mathrm{ID}_1$ takes such sets as primitive objects and asserts only their closure and minimality.

Iteration comes just as naturally.
The trees that branch over all well-founded trees of the first kind form a set of the second level, and so on.
Since these sets are generated by rules, they looked like a constructive foundation for parts of analysis, and the ID theories were used to reduce impredicative subsystems of analysis to them.
Their ordinals are standard landmarks of [[ordinal analysis|C5E5TG8PGP]], measured by [[Buchholz's functions|E7WEFRMQTB]].

## Notation

We write $\mathrm{ID}_{<\nu}$ for the union of the theories $\mathrm{ID}_\mu$ with $\mu < \nu$, so $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ is the union of $\mathrm{ID}_1, \mathrm{ID}_2, \dots$.
The intuitionistic version over Heyting arithmetic is $\mathrm{ID}^i_\nu$, and $\mathrm{ID}^i_\nu(\mathcal{O})$ allows only the operators of the tree classes $\mathcal{O}_\mu$, the accessibility inductive definitions.
Much of the literature writes $\widehat{\mathrm{ID}}_\nu$ for the fixed-point theories, which assert $A(I_A) = I_A$ but drop the minimality scheme.
The new predicates appear as $I_A$, $P_A$ or $J_A$ in different sources.
We measure each theory by its [[proof-theoretic ordinal|C72RBH0CDR]], the supremum of the order types of the primitive recursive well-orderings it proves well-founded, and we write ordinals in Buchholz's notation $\psi_\nu$.

## Remarks

### The accessible part, worked in the theory

The opening rule is the operator $A(X, x) = \forall y \, (y < x \to X(y))$, and $X$ occurs positively in it.
Its least fixed point is the [[accessible part|CRP7Z3M9XD]] of $<$.
To prove $\forall x \, I_A(x)$ in $\mathrm{ID}_1$, use complete induction: if $I_A(y)$ for all $y < x$, then $A(I_A, x)$ holds, and closure gives $I_A(x)$.
For an arbitrary relation $\prec$ the same operator gives the well-founded part, and minimality gives transfinite induction along $\prec$ on $I_A$ for every formula.
That last fact is what makes $\mathrm{ID}_1$ strong.

### Why iterate

The [[tree classes O₁ and O₂|PDVPG1SN9X]] show the need for stages.
The class $\mathcal{O}_2$ contains trees that branch over all of $\mathcal{O}_1$, so its operator contains $\forall a \, (a \in \mathcal{O}_1 \to \dots)$.
There $\mathcal{O}_1$ stands in the antecedent of an implication, which is a negative place.
So $\mathcal{O}_1$ must be fixed at an earlier stage, and $\mathcal{O}_2$ is defined in $\mathrm{ID}_2$.

### The ordinals

With Buchholz's notation, $\mathrm{ID}_0$ is Peano arithmetic with ordinal $\varepsilon_0 = \psi_0(\Omega_1)$.
The ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_1$ is the [[Bachmann-Howard ordinal|QH3ENXFRSV]]
$$\lvert \mathrm{ID}_1 \rvert = \psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega + 1}) = \psi_0(\Omega_2).$$
In general $\lvert \mathrm{ID}_\nu \rvert = \psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\nu + 1})$ and $\lvert \mathrm{ID}_{<\nu} \rvert = \psi_0(\Omega_\nu)$.
So $\mathrm{ID}_\omega$ has the [[Takeuti-Feferman-Buchholz ordinal|NZWRK062WQ]] $\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1})$, and $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ has the smaller $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$, the same ordinal as $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$.

### The rules you can use

Positivity gives monotonicity: if $\forall x \, (F(x) \to G(x))$, then $\forall x \, (A(F, x) \to A(G, x))$, by induction on the build of $A$.
Monotonicity gives the fixed point $\forall x \, (I_A(x) \leftrightarrow A(I_A, x))$.
Right to left is closure, and for left to right take $F(x) = A(I_A, x)$: closure and monotonicity give $A(F, x) \to F(x)$, and minimality gives $I_A(x) \to F(x)$.
In the standard model $I_A$ is the union of the stages $I^\alpha_A = A(\bigcup_{\beta < \alpha} I^\beta_A)$, and the [[stages of an accessible part|TA0AAMRY00]] show that infinitely many stages can be needed.

### Comparison with analysis

Each $\mathrm{ID}_n$ with $n$ finite is interpreted in $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$.
Read $I_A$ as the intersection of all sets closed under $A$, which is a set by $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension.
Then every formula of $\mathrm{ID}_n$ becomes an arithmetical formula in these set parameters, so it defines a set, and induction and minimality hold.
This is the easy half of the equality of ordinals between $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$.

### What it is not

[[The operator ¬X|VF1N6N8ECC]] is not positive, and its closure and minimality axioms hold for $I = \mathbb{N}$, which is not a fixed point.
The fixed-point theory $\widehat{\mathrm{ID}}_1$ is not $\mathrm{ID}_1$: it lacks minimality, so the transfinite induction argument above fails in it.
[[Π¹₁-comprehension|2MGJJTSRFD]] is a second-order theory, not a theory of inductive definitions, though $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ has the ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$.

### Watch out

Do not confuse $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ with $\mathrm{ID}_\omega$: their ordinals $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$ and $\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1})$ differ by a lot.
Do not restrict minimality to sets.
The scheme holds for every formula $F$ of the extended language, including formulas that mention $I_A$, and that is the source of the strength.

### History

Georg Kreisel proposed formal theories of generalized inductive definitions in the early 1960s.
Solomon Feferman introduced the iterated theories $\mathrm{ID}_\nu$ in 1970 and used them to study subsystems of analysis.
Buchholz, with his $\Omega_{\mu + 1}$-rules, and Pohlers, with local predicativity, completed the ordinal analysis of $\mathrm{ID}_\nu$.
The book "Iterated inductive definitions and subsystems of analysis" (Lecture Notes in Mathematics 897, 1981) by Buchholz, Feferman, Pohlers and Sieg collects these results.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some sets are built step by step by rules, like "$0$ is in the set, and if $n$ is in the set then $n + 2$ is in the set".
The result is the smallest set that obeys the rules, here the even numbers.
A theory of inductive definitions adds a name for each such smallest set, with two axioms: the set obeys the rules, and it is inside every other set that obeys them.
For some rules the building needs infinitely many rounds, and these sets are hard to describe in ordinary arithmetic.
Iterating means that the rules of a later set may refer to sets built earlier.

### L2 human

We prove in $\mathrm{ID}_1$ that the accessible part $I_A$ of a relation $\prec$ supports transfinite induction.
Let $F(x)$ be any formula, and assume that $F$ is progressive:
$$\forall x \, \big( \forall y \, (y \prec x \to F(y)) \to F(x) \big).$$
The operator is $A(X, x) = \forall y \, (y \prec x \to X(y))$, so progressiveness says $\forall x \, (A(F, x) \to F(x))$.
The minimality scheme then gives $\forall x \, (I_A(x) \to F(x))$.

This is the lower bound of an ordinal analysis: each notation that provably lies in the accessible part of the notation order gives transfinite induction up to it.
Watch out: $\mathrm{ID}_1$ does not prove $I_A(x)$ for every given $x$, since that needs a proof that all predecessors are accessible, and for large notations this is exactly what the theory cannot prove.

### L3 human

The theory $\mathrm{ID}_1$ has the same proof-theoretic ordinal as Kripke-Platek set theory.
Its least fixed points are $\Pi^1_1$ sets, and $\Pi^1_1$ sets are the sets that are $\Sigma_1$-definable over the least admissible set $L_{\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}}$.
Positive arithmetical operators reach their fixed points by stage $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$.
In the iterated theories each stage matches a further admissible ordinal, which is why Buchholz's notation uses one cardinal $\Omega_\nu$ for each level.
The methods built for these theories, the $\Omega$-rule and [[local predicativity|5C4YBXQV15]], were then carried over to Π¹₁-comprehension and to set theories with reflection.
In type theory, inductive types such as W-types play the role of the operators of $\mathrm{ID}_\nu$.
