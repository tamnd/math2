## Summary

The optical path length of a path is the integral of the [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] along it.
It equals the distance that light would travel in vacuum in the time it takes to follow the path in the medium.
[[Fermat's principle|RKX9XJ0RVW]] says that light rays are paths of stationary optical path length.
Differences of optical path length give the phase differences that produce interference.
See the [[definition|G1R653GA1D]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light is slower in glass than in air, so a centimetre of glass costs more time than a centimetre of air.
The optical path length converts each piece of a path into the vacuum distance with the same travel time.
A glass plate $1$ cm thick with index $1.5$ has optical path length $1.5$ cm.
Two beams that meet after equal optical path lengths arrive in step, which is what interferometers measure.
