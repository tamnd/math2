## Summary

Murray–von Neumann equivalence is the basic way to compare projections in a C*-algebra or a von Neumann algebra.
Two projections are equivalent when a [[partial isometry|GGDSF85JPD]] of the algebra maps the range of one onto the range of the other.
In von Neumann algebras it is the tool behind the [[Murray–von Neumann classification of factors|QAWDC7SWKC]].
In C*-algebras it is the relation used to define the group [[K0|BP86XQRZD2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A projection on a Hilbert space picks out a closed subspace, its range.
Two subspaces of the same dimension can be matched by an isometric map from one onto the other.
Murray–von Neumann equivalence asks for such a matching map inside a given algebra of operators.
In the algebra of all bounded operators, two projections are equivalent exactly when their ranges have the same dimension.
So the projection onto the even basis vectors of $\ell^2(\N)$ is equivalent to the identity, which shows how infinite dimensions behave.
