## Summary

The Riemann–Silberstein vector is the complex vector field $F = E + i c B$ built from the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] and the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]].
In terms of it, the four source-free [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] become two equations for one complex field.
The [[duality rotations|MTMHQQ47N3]] become multiplication of $F$ by a phase, and the energy density becomes $\frac{\varepsilon_0}{2} \abs{F}^2$.
It is used to write Maxwell's equations in a form close to the Schrödinger equation, as a wave equation for the photon.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The electric field and the magnetic field are two real vectors at each point.
One can pack them into one complex vector: the electric field is the real part and $c$ times the magnetic field is the imaginary part.
With this packing, Maxwell's equations in empty space become shorter and their symmetries become easier to see.
For a circularly polarized light wave, the vector has constant length and only its phase turns as the wave passes.
