## Example

Let $L = \set{<}$ and $\mathcal{M} = (\Q, <)$, and let $D = \set{k / 2^m : k \in \Z, \ m \in \N}$ be the set of dyadic rationals.
We choose one [[Skolem function|NKQY9NB955]] $f_\varphi$ for each $L$-formula $\varphi(\bar x, y)$, and check that the closure of $\emptyset$ under these functions is $D$.

1. Witnesses in $D$. Let $\bar a$ be a tuple in $D$ and $b \in \Q$ with $\Q \models \varphi(\bar a, b)$. The construction of [[this example|AY1QQFQZ41]], with $D$ in place of $\Q$ and $\Q$ in place of $\R$, gives $d \in D$ with $\Q \models \varphi(\bar a, d)$.
2. The construction applies because $D$ is dense in $\Q$ and has no endpoints, and because affine maps with rational coefficients map $\Q$ onto $\Q$. So its map $\sigma$ is an [[automorphism|XY6CJMDY5M]] of $(\Q, <)$ that fixes $\bar a$.
3. Choice of $f_\varphi$. For the sentence $\exists y \, (y = y)$ we choose the element $0$. For $x < y$ we choose $x + 1$, and for $y < x$ we choose $x - 1$.
4. For $x_1 < y \land y < x_2$ we choose $(x_1 + x_2)/2$ when $x_1 < x_2$, and $x_1$ otherwise.
5. For every other $\varphi$ and every $\bar a$ in $D$ we choose a witness in $D$, by step 1, when a witness exists, and $0$ otherwise. On tuples outside $D$ we choose any witness.
6. Upper bound. The values in steps 3 to 5 on tuples in $D$ lie in $D$, because $D$ is closed under $x \pm 1$ and under midpoints. So the closure $H$ of $\emptyset$ is contained in $D$.
7. Integers. $0 \in H$ by step 3, and $H$ is closed under $x \mapsto x \pm 1$, so $\Z \subseteq H$.
8. Dyadics. By [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $m$, every $k / 2^m$ lies in $H$. For $k$ odd, $k / 2^{m+1}$ is the midpoint of $(k-1)/2^{m+1}$ and $(k+1)/2^{m+1}$, which have the form $j / 2^m$.
9. So $H = D$. By the [[Tarski-Vaught test|60SA99BER7]], $(D, <) \preceq (\Q, <)$, because $H$ contains $f_\varphi(\bar a)$ for every $\varphi$ and every $\bar a$ in $H$.

## Remarks

The example shows that a Skolem hull depends on the chosen Skolem functions.
With $x + 3$ and $x - 3$ in step 3, the same midpoints, and the witnesses of step 5 chosen in $3D = \set{3d : d \in D}$, the hull of $\emptyset$ is $3D$, which does not contain $1$.
With the choice $x_1 + (x_2 - x_1)/3$ for $x_1 < y < x_2$, the functions $x \pm 1$, and the witnesses of step 5 chosen among the rationals with a power of $3$ as denominator, the hull of $\emptyset$ is that set.
In every case the hull is a countable dense subset of $\Q$, and it is an [[elementary substructure|8WZF9SBP39]], as the general theory predicts.
Every elementary substructure of $(\Q, <)$ is a dense order without endpoints, so the hull of $\emptyset$ is always infinite.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start with no numbers at all and three rules: "pick $0$", "add one or subtract one", and "take the point halfway between two numbers".
Applying the rules again and again gives $0$, then $1$ and $-1$, then $\tfrac{1}{2}$, then $\tfrac{1}{4}$ and $\tfrac{3}{4}$, and so on.
These are the fractions whose denominator is a power of $2$.
They form a smaller copy of the rational numbers, with the same order properties: between any two there is another one, and there is no first or last one.

### L2 human

We check step 8 for the number $\tfrac{5}{8}$.

1. $\tfrac{5}{8} = 5 / 2^3$, and $5$ is odd.
2. It is the midpoint of $\tfrac{4}{8} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and $\tfrac{6}{8} = \tfrac{3}{4}$.
3. $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is the midpoint of $0$ and $1$, and $\tfrac{3}{4}$ is the midpoint of $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and $1$.
4. $0$ and $1$ come from step 3 and step 7. So $\tfrac{5}{8}$ is obtained in four rounds of closure.

The typical use is to see that "the" Skolem hull is a construction that depends on choices, and still gives an elementary substructure each time.
A trap is to think that the functions of steps 3 and 4 are enough by themselves.
Their closure is $D$, but the proof that $D$ is elementary needs step 1, which gives witnesses in $D$ for all other formulas.
