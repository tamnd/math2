## Summary

The Reynolds stress is the tensor $-\rho \overline{u_i' u_j'}$ formed from correlations of the velocity fluctuations of a [[turbulent|VY307F8Z0S]] flow.
It appears as an extra stress when the momentum equations are averaged with the [[Reynolds decomposition|V47TX9AFKF]].
It describes the transport of mean momentum by turbulent eddies, which is usually much larger than transport by molecular viscosity.
The equations of the mean flow do not determine it, which is the origin of the turbulence closure problem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a turbulent river, eddies carry fast water down towards the bed and slow water up towards the surface.
This mixing drags the layers of the mean flow on each other, just like a friction force.
The Reynolds stress measures this effective friction.
It comes from the eddies themselves, not from the stickiness of water, and in most turbulent flows it is far stronger.
