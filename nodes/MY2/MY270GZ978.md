## Definition

Consider two flows, a model and a prototype, each described by equations and boundary conditions that depend on dimensional parameters.
The two flows are *dynamically similar* if:

1. they are geometrically similar: for some scale factor $\lambda > 0$, the map $x \mapsto \lambda x$ sends the model boundaries onto the prototype boundaries, up to a rigid motion;
2. every [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] that appears in the dimensionless form of the governing equations and boundary conditions has the same value for both flows.

## Remarks

When the dimensionless problem has a unique solution, dynamically similar flows have the same dimensionless velocity and pressure fields, so measurements on the model can be scaled to the prototype.
Example: for steady incompressible flow past a body, the only parameter is the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]].
A model at scale $1/10$ in the same fluid at ten times the speed has the same $UL/\nu$, so the two flows are dynamically similar.
Non-example: take a ship model at scale $1/10$ in water.
Equal [[Froude number|R2QQEFPTP8]] needs the speed scaled by $\sqrt{1/10}$, while equal Reynolds number needs it scaled by $10$.
Both cannot hold, so the model and the ship are not fully dynamically similar.
In practice one matches the dominant number and corrects for the others.
