## Statement

Let $G$ be a [[locally compact abelian group|ZR09S9R3AW]] with [[Pontryagin dual|FMW57WNPDB]] $\widehat{G}$.
The *coset ring* of $\widehat{G}$ is the smallest [[ring of sets|KWE1DBMN9B]] of subsets of $\widehat{G}$ that contains every coset of every subgroup of $\widehat{G}$.
Here the subgroups are not required to be closed.
Let $\mu$ be an [[idempotent|0N5NJZKKN3]] of the [[measure algebra|8E2CDA2WSV]] $M(G)$, that is, $\mu * \mu = \mu$.
Then its [[Fourier–Stieltjes transform|FZQ7MVMPTN]] is $\widehat{\mu} = \mathbf{1}_E$ for a set $E$ in the coset ring of $\widehat{G}$.

## Remarks

The first step is easy: $\widehat{\mu * \mu} = \widehat{\mu}^2$, so $\widehat{\mu}^2 = \widehat{\mu}$, and $\widehat{\mu}$ takes only the values $0$ and $1$.
The hard part is the structure of the set $E = \set{\chi : \widehat{\mu}(\chi) = 1}$.
Cohen's proof bounds the norm $\norm{\mu}$ of an idempotent measure from below in terms of the complexity of $E$.
It uses this bound to show that $E$ is built from finitely many cosets.

Example: for $G = \mathbb{T}$ and an integer $m \ge 1$, let $\mu = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1} \delta_{e^{2\pi i k/m}}$, the normalised Haar measure of the subgroup of $m$-th roots of unity.
Then $\mu * \mu = \mu$, and $\widehat{\mu}(n) = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{k} e^{-2\pi i kn/m}$ is $1$ when $m$ divides $n$ and $0$ otherwise.
So $E = m\Z$, a subgroup of $\widehat{\mathbb{T}} = \Z$.

A partial converse: if $E$ is in the ring of sets generated by the cosets of open subgroups of $\widehat{G}$, then $\mathbf{1}_E = \widehat{\mu}$ for an idempotent $\mu$.
For an open subgroup $\Lambda$, the annihilator of $\Lambda$ is a compact subgroup of $G$ whose normalised Haar measure has transform $\mathbf{1}_\Lambda$.
Translates, products and complements are then handled with the algebra structure of $M(G)$.
When $G$ is compact, $\widehat{G}$ is discrete, every subgroup is open, and the theorem becomes a complete description of the idempotent measures.
For $G = \mathbb{T}$ the result was first proved by Helson.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An idempotent measure is one that does not change when it is convolved with itself, like the uniform measure on a finite subgroup.
Its Fourier transform can only take the values $0$ and $1$.
Cohen's theorem says that the set where it equals $1$ is not arbitrary: it is put together from finitely many cosets of subgroups by unions, intersections and complements.

### L2 human

The theorem classifies the idempotents of the measure algebra $M(G)$ in Fourier terms.
For $G = \mathbb{T}$ the coset ring of $\Z$ consists of the sets that differ by finitely many integers from a finite union of arithmetic progressions.
So the idempotent measures on the circle are very rigid.
The same work of Cohen gave the first lower bound in the Littlewood conjecture on the $L^1$ norms of exponential sums, with a related method.
The theorem is a basic tool in the study of homomorphisms between group algebras.
