## Description

Let $n \ge 1$, let $U(2^n)$ be the [[unitary group|0R9R0Y1HDK]] on $(\C^2)^{\otimes n}$, and let $\mathcal{P}_n$ be the [[Pauli group|M03133GAB6]] on $n$ qubits.
The *Clifford group* on $n$ qubits is the normalizer of $\mathcal{P}_n$ in $U(2^n)$:
$$\mathcal{C}_n = \set{ U \in U(2^n) : U P U^* \in \mathcal{P}_n \text{ for all } P \in \mathcal{P}_n }.$$
It is a [[group|G5S1M9]] under matrix multiplication.

## Remarks

The Clifford group contains all scalar matrices $e^{i\theta} I$, so it is infinite; one often works modulo these phases.
Up to phases, it is generated by the Hadamard gate, the phase gate and the controlled-NOT gate:
$$H = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad S = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & i \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathrm{CNOT} = |0\rangle\langle 0| \otimes I + |1\rangle\langle 1| \otimes X.$$
Here $H$ and $S$ act on any one qubit, and CNOT acts on any ordered pair of qubits.
For example, $H$ is a Clifford element: $HXH = Z$, $HZH = X$ and $HYH = -Y$, where $X, Y, Z$ are the [[Pauli matrices|XAQCW3D4QQ]].
Similarly $SXS^* = Y$ and $SZS^* = Z$.

The $T$ gate $\mathrm{diag}(1, e^{i\pi/4})$ is not a Clifford element, since $TXT^* = (X + Y)/\sqrt2$ is not in $\mathcal{P}_1$.

Conjugation by elements of $\mathcal{C}_n$ induces symplectic maps on the quotient $\mathcal{P}_n / \set{\pm I, \pm iI} \cong \F_2^{2n}$, which carries the symplectic form of the Pauli group.
Let $N$ be the subgroup of $\mathcal{C}_n$ generated by $\mathcal{P}_n$ and the phases.
This gives an isomorphism of the [[quotient|0KYX2N69J0]] $\mathcal{C}_n / N$ onto the [[symplectic group|X12FS2JAP0]] $Sp(2n, \F_2)$.
Here $\F_2$ is the [[field with two elements|0ZXZ82FTX2]].

Circuits built from Clifford gates acting on computational basis states can be simulated efficiently on a classical computer.
Adding any non-Clifford gate, such as $T$, gives a universal gate set.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Clifford group consists of the quantum operations on qubits that turn Pauli errors into Pauli errors.
It is generated by three simple gates: Hadamard, phase and controlled-NOT.
These gates are enough to create entanglement and to run error-correcting codes.
They are not enough for full quantum computing, since circuits made only of them can be simulated classically.
