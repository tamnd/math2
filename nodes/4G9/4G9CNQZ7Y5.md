## Summary

The CCR algebra of a symplectic vector space $(V, \sigma)$ is a [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]] generated by unitary elements $W(v)$, $v \in V$.
These satisfy the Weyl form of the canonical commutation relations.
It is the algebra of observables of a bosonic system with phase space $V$, such as a free quantum field.
The Weyl form uses bounded unitaries instead of the unbounded position and momentum operators, so that C*-algebra methods apply.
For finite-dimensional $V$ the [[Stone–von Neumann theorem|Z877XDBMQ3]] says that its regular irreducible representations are all equivalent, while for infinite-dimensional $V$ there are many inequivalent ones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In quantum mechanics position $Q$ and momentum $P$ satisfy $QP - PQ = i$, with $\hbar = 1$.
No bounded operators can satisfy this rule, so one works instead with the unitary operators $e^{i(bQ - aP)}$, which shift a wave function in position by $a$ and in momentum by $b$.
Two such shifts commute only up to a phase factor, and the phase is fixed by the symplectic area spanned by the two shifts.
The CCR algebra is the abstract algebra generated by these shifts with exactly this rule.
