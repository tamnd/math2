## Summary

A Følner sequence in a countable group is a sequence of finite sets that become more and more invariant under translation, relative to their size.
A countable group has a Følner sequence exactly when it satisfies the [[Følner condition|82R5XGCART]], and this happens exactly when it is [[amenable|CEAT9BESKK]].
Følner sequences are the sets over which one averages in ergodic theorems for actions of amenable groups.
In a locally compact group one uses compact sets of positive Haar measure instead of finite sets.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the integers, the intervals $\set{0, 1, \ldots, n-1}$ are almost unchanged when shifted by a fixed amount: only a few points at the ends move in or out.
As $n$ grows, these few points become a tiny fraction of the interval.
A sequence of finite sets with this property for every shift is a Følner sequence.
In $\Z^2$, growing squares do the same job, since the boundary of a square is small compared with its area.
