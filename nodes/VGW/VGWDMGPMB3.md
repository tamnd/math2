## Proof

We prove soundness from the properties of interior, and completeness by pulling finite countermodels back to $\mathbb{R}$ along continuous open maps.

### Cited results

Two results are cited without proof.
The first, (C1), is the finite model property of $\mathsf{S4}$: if $\mathsf{S4} \nvdash \varphi$, then $\varphi$ fails at the root of some finite rooted preorder under some valuation.
It follows from completeness for preorders, filtration, and passing to the subframe generated by a world.
The second, (C2), says that every finite rooted preorder $(W, \leq)$, with the topology of up-sets, is the image of $\mathbb{R}$ under a [[continuous|0VGFHTM75Y]] and [[open|0YCG648KDD]] map.

### Part A: soundness

This part proves item 1, and with it the inclusion $\mathsf{S4} \subseteq \mathrm{Log}(\mathbb{R})$.

1. Let $X$ be a space and $A, B \subseteq X$.
   The [[interior|0RZH6W98HK]] satisfies $\mathrm{Int}\, A \subseteq A$, and $\mathrm{Int}\, A$ is open, so $\mathrm{Int}\, \mathrm{Int}\, A = \mathrm{Int}\, A$.
   So $\square p \to p$ and $\square p \to \square \square p$ are valid.
2. Finite intersections of open sets are open, so $\mathrm{Int}(A \cap B) = \mathrm{Int}\, A \cap \mathrm{Int}\, B$, and the axiom $\mathrm{K}$ is valid by this computation:
$$\mathrm{Int}((X \setminus A) \cup B) \cap \mathrm{Int}\, A = \mathrm{Int}(A \cap B) \subseteq \mathrm{Int}\, B.$$
3. Tautologies are valid, since the connectives act by Boolean operations on sets, and modus ponens preserves validity.
4. Necessitation preserves validity, since $\mathrm{Int}\, X = X$.
5. So every theorem of $\mathsf{S4}$ is valid in $X$, which is item 1.

### Part B: open maps

This part shows that continuous open maps carry refutations backward.

6. Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be continuous and open.
   We claim that $f^{-1}(\mathrm{Int}\, B) = \mathrm{Int}\, f^{-1}(B)$ for every $B \subseteq Y$.
7. The set $f^{-1}(\mathrm{Int}\, B)$ is open by continuity, and it lies inside $f^{-1}(B)$, so it lies inside $\mathrm{Int}\, f^{-1}(B)$.
8. Conversely, let $x \in U \subseteq f^{-1}(B)$ with $U$ open.
   Then $f(U)$ is open because $f$ is open, it contains $f(x)$, and it lies inside $B$, so $f(x) \in \mathrm{Int}\, B$.
9. Let $V$ be a valuation on $Y$, and put $V'(p) = f^{-1}(V(p))$ on $X$.
   By induction on $\varphi$, $V'(\varphi) = f^{-1}(V(\varphi))$: preimages commute with Boolean operations, and step 6 gives the case of $\square$.
10. If $f$ is onto and $V(\varphi) \neq Y$, then $V'(\varphi) \neq X$, so a formula refuted in $Y$ is refuted in $X$.

### Part C: completeness

This part proves completeness for $\mathbb{R}$, item 2, and then item 3.

11. Let $\mathsf{S4} \nvdash \varphi$.
    By (C1), $\varphi$ fails at the root of a finite rooted preorder $(W, \leq)$ under some valuation.
12. By [[Alexandrov spaces give Kripke semantics|CWZ1K5AN1N]], $\varphi$ is refuted in the space of up-sets of $W$, under the same valuation.
13. By (C2) there is a continuous open map from $\mathbb{R}$ onto this space, so step 10 refutes $\varphi$ in $\mathbb{R}$.
14. So $\mathrm{Log}(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq \mathsf{S4}$, and with step 5 we get $\mathrm{Log}(\mathbb{R}) = \mathsf{S4}$, which is item 2.
15. The logic of all spaces contains $\mathsf{S4}$ by step 5, and it is contained in $\mathrm{Log}(\mathbb{R}) = \mathsf{S4}$, which gives item 3.

## Idea

Soundness is a reading of Kuratowski's axioms for the interior operator.
For completeness, a non-theorem of $\mathsf{S4}$ already fails on a finite preorder, and a finite preorder is a finite topological space.
Continuous open maps pull refutations back along preimages, so it is enough to map $\mathbb{R}$ onto every finite rooted preorder in this way.
The cited lemma (C2) cuts $\mathbb{R}$ into pieces, one for each point $w$ of the preorder.
Each point in the piece of $w$ has a neighborhood inside the pieces of the points above $w$, and each of its neighborhoods meets all those pieces.
For the two-point preorder $0 \leq 1$, the map that sends the integers to $0$ and every other real to $1$ does the job.
The preimage of the open set $\{1\}$ is open, and every open interval that meets an integer also contains non-integers, so its image is the whole space.

## Variants

McKinsey and Tarski gave an algebraic proof.
They worked with closure algebras and embedded the finite algebras that arise from countermodels into the closure algebra of a dense-in-itself separable metric space.
This embedding is the algebraic dual of the onto map of (C2).
Other proofs pass through trees.
A finite rooted preorder is the image of a finite tree of clusters under a bounded morphism, and $\mathbb{R}$ or the Cantor space is mapped onto such trees.
Kremer's proof of strong completeness extends the method from single formulas to infinite sets of formulas.
