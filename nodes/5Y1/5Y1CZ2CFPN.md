## Summary

The Drude model, proposed by Paul Drude in 1900, describes electrical conduction in metals.
In it, free electrons are accelerated by the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] and lose their momentum in random collisions.
It gives Ohm's law with an explicit conductivity, and a frequency-dependent conductivity and [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]].
It explains why metals reflect light below the plasma frequency and become transparent above it.
In optics it is the limit of the [[Lorentz oscillator model|QSR3C1A1P9]] without a restoring force.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture the electrons in a metal as a gas of tiny balls that the electric field pushes along.
Every so often a ball hits something and forgets its direction, so on average it drifts at a steady speed proportional to the field.
This steady drift is the electric current, and the model predicts that the current is proportional to the voltage, which is Ohm's law.
For light, which shakes the electrons very fast, the same picture explains the shine of metals.
