## Summary

Limb darkening is the decrease of the brightness of the disk of a star from its centre to its edge, the limb.
It happens because the temperature of a stellar atmosphere increases with depth, and a line of sight near the limb only reaches higher, cooler layers.
A limb-darkening law gives the intensity as a function of the angle between the line of sight and the surface normal.
It must be modelled to interpret eclipses of binary stars and transits of exoplanets.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pictures of the Sun show a disk that is brighter in the middle and darker near the edge.
At the centre we look straight down into the Sun, and we see down to a certain depth where the gas is hot.
Near the edge we look through the atmosphere at a slant, so the light comes from a higher layer that is cooler and dimmer.
In a simple model, the edge of the disk has $40\%$ of the brightness of the centre.
