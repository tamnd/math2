## Summary

The Ehrenfest classification sorts [[phase transitions|6WJ0NADBT4]] by the lowest derivative of the free energy that is discontinuous at the transition.
In a first-order transition the entropy or the volume jumps, so there is a [[latent heat|FN6Z9QKAH5]].
In a second-order transition these are continuous, but second derivatives such as the heat capacity jump.
The scheme is the origin of the words "first-order" and "second-order", though modern usage distinguishes only first-order and continuous transitions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When water boils, it suddenly takes much more room and swallows heat without warming up: a first derivative of the free energy jumps.
In some other transitions, such as the onset of superconductivity without a magnetic field, nothing jumps in volume or heat, but the heat capacity jumps.
Ehrenfest called the first kind first order and the second kind second order.
The number counts how many derivatives you must take before you see a jump.
