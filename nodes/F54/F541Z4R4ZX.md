## Statement

Let an isolated source of mass density $\rho(t, x)$ move slowly, with internal speeds $v \ll c$, and let its gravitational field be weak.
Define its reduced quadrupole moment
$$Q_{ij}(t) = \int \rho(t, x) \left(x_i x_j - \tfrac{1}{3}\delta_{ij} \abs{x}^2\right) d^3x, \qquad i, j \in \set{1, 2, 3}.$$
Let $r$ be the distance to the source, much larger than the wavelength.
Then, to leading order in $v/c$, the emitted [[gravitational wave|SJ0A4TZTFR]] in transverse-traceless gauge is
$$h^{TT}_{ij}(t, x) = \frac{2G}{c^4 r} \left(\ddot Q_{ij}\right)^{TT}\!\left(t - \frac{r}{c}\right).$$
The radiated power is
$$P = \frac{G}{5c^5} \left\langle \dddot Q_{ij}\, \dddot Q_{ij} \right\rangle.$$
Here dots denote time derivatives, repeated indices are summed, and $\langle \cdot \rangle$ is an average over several periods.

## Remarks

The formula comes from [[linearized gravity|AH62KSWA6Y]]: in Lorenz gauge the perturbation solves a wave equation with the stress-energy tensor as source, and the retarded solution is expanded in $v/c$.
Conservation of the stress-energy tensor turns the leading spatial stresses into the second time derivative of the quadrupole moment.
The monopole and dipole parts do not radiate, because mass and momentum are conserved.
Einstein derived the formula in 1918, with a corrected factor relative to his 1916 work.
The formula also holds for self-gravitating sources such as binary stars, as shown by post-Newtonian matching.

Example: two point masses $m_1, m_2$ on a Newtonian circular orbit of separation $a$ radiate the power
$$P = \frac{32}{5} \frac{G^4 m_1^2 m_2^2 (m_1 + m_2)}{c^5 a^5}.$$
The observed slow decay of the orbital period of the Hulse–Taylor binary pulsar agrees with this prediction.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The quadrupole formula tells how strongly a moving system shines in gravitational waves.
What matters is not the total mass nor how the centre of mass moves, but how the shape of the mass distribution changes in time.
A spinning dumbbell or two stars in orbit radiate; a perfectly round pulsating star does not.

### L2 human

It plays the role for gravity that the dipole formula plays for electromagnetism; there is no gravitational dipole radiation because momentum is conserved.
With energy balance it gives the inspiral of binaries through [[gravitational radiation reaction|EB51RMJ5Z0]].
Higher post-Newtonian corrections to the formula are needed to model the signals of compact binaries seen by detectors.
