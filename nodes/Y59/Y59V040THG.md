## Summary

The four-velocity of a massive particle is the derivative of its position in spacetime with respect to its [[proper time|YG74WHC3S9]].
It is a [[four-vector|S8RKKKMSTG]] tangent to the particle's [[world line|Z788G6MJ9E]], and its Minkowski squared length is always $-c^2$.
It is the relativistic replacement of ordinary velocity, and multiplied by the mass it gives the four-momentum.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary velocity tells how fast position changes per second of lab time.
Four-velocity tells how fast the event of the particle moves through spacetime per second of the particle's own clock.
Its time component says how much lab time passes per tick of the particle's clock, and its space components give the motion.
For a particle at rest it is $(c, 0, 0, 0)$; for a particle moving at $0.6 c$ along $x$ it is $(1.25 c, 0.75 c, 0, 0)$.
