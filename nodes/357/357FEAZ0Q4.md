## Statement

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, let $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be an open interval, and let $A \colon I \to \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be a [[differentiable|0K57YG3BB8]] map, with $n \ge 1$.
Then $t \mapsto \det A(t)$ is differentiable, and for all $t \in I$
$$\frac{d}{dt} \det A(t) = \operatorname{tr}\big(\operatorname{adj}(A(t))\, A'(t)\big),$$
where $\det$ is the [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]], $\operatorname{adj}$ is the [[adjugate|07W9H8QF38]] and $\operatorname{tr}$ is the [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]].
If $A(t)$ is invertible, this equals $\det A(t) \cdot \operatorname{tr}\big(A(t)^{-1} A'(t)\big)$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Differentiability of $A$ means that every entry $a_{ij}(t)$ is differentiable; the formula uses $A'(t)$.
- The form with $A(t)^{-1}$ needs $A(t)$ invertible; the form with the adjugate holds for every $A(t)$.
- For example, $A(t) = \begin{pmatrix} t & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is singular for all $t$, and both $\det A(t) = 0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A) A') = \operatorname{tr} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & t \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = 0$ agree.

### Consequences

- The derivative of $\det$ at the identity is the trace: $\frac{d}{dt} \det(I + tX) \big|_{t=0} = \operatorname{tr} X$.
- For $A(t) = e^{tX}$, $\frac{d}{dt} \det e^{tX} = \operatorname{tr}(X) \det e^{tX}$, so $\det e^{tX} = e^{t \operatorname{tr} X}$; see the [[determinant of a matrix exponential|0A1QQN333A]].
- For a fundamental matrix $\Phi$ of $x' = M(t) x$, $\frac{d}{dt} \det \Phi = \operatorname{tr}(M) \det \Phi$; this is Liouville's formula for the Wronskian.
- The gradient of $\det$ at $A$ is the cofactor matrix: $\partial \det A / \partial a_{ij} = C_{ij}$.

### Converse

The formula is an identity for every differentiable $A$, so a converse in the usual sense does not apply.

### Generalizations

- The formula holds over any commutative ring for formal derivatives, for example for matrices of polynomials or of power series.
- For a differentiable map $A$ of several variables, the differential is $d(\det A) = \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A)\, dA)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Jacobi's formula tells how fast the determinant of a matrix changes when the matrix changes.
Near the identity matrix, the rate of change of the determinant is the trace of the change.
For example, $\det\begin{pmatrix} 1 + t & 0 \\ 0 & 1 + 2t \end{pmatrix} = 1 + 3t + 2t^2$ has derivative $3$ at $t = 0$.
This is the trace of $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.

### L2 human

Let $A(t) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t \\ t & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. Directly, $\det A(t) = 1 - t^2$, with derivative $-2t$.
2. Here $\operatorname{adj} A(t) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -t \\ -t & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A'(t) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. So $\operatorname{adj}(A) A' = \begin{pmatrix} -t & 1 \\ 1 & -t \end{pmatrix}$, with trace $-2t$, which agrees.

The typical use is to differentiate $\log \lvert \det A \rvert$, which gives $\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1} A')$, in statistics and in physics.
A trap: the derivative of $\det A$ is not $\det A'$; here $\det A' = -1$ for all $t$.

### L3 human

The formula says that the differential of $\det$ at $A$ is the linear map $X \mapsto \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A) X)$.
In Lie theory, the derivative of $\det \colon \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{K}) \to \mathbb{K}^\times$ at the identity is $\operatorname{tr} \colon \mathfrak{gl}_n \to \mathbb{K}$.
So $\det \circ \exp = \exp \circ \operatorname{tr}$, and $\mathfrak{sl}_n$, the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{SL}_n$, consists of the matrices of trace $0$.
