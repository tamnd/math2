## Summary

Petzval field curvature is the aberration by which a lens images a flat object onto a curved surface instead of a plane.
In a system without astigmatism this surface is the Petzval surface.
Its curvature depends only on the powers and refractive indices of the elements, through the Petzval sum.
It is one of the [[Seidel aberrations|VHH260SY86]], and a flat image field requires the Petzval sum to vanish.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hold a magnifying glass over a flat page and project it on a screen.
If the centre is sharp, the edges are slightly blurred, and the other way round.
The sharp image of the flat page lies on a bowl-shaped surface, not on the flat screen.
For a single thin lens this bowl has a radius of about the refractive index times the focal length, so about $1.5$ times the focal length for ordinary glass.
