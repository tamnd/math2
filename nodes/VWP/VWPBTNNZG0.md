## Summary

The Kozai mechanism, or Lidov–Kozai mechanism, is a slow periodic exchange between the eccentricity and the inclination of an orbit.
It is caused by a distant third body.
When the inclination is large enough, a nearly circular orbit becomes very eccentric and then circular again, over and over.
It shapes the orbits of irregular moons, asteroids, satellites and triple stars.
See the [[definition|Y3MX5VTFVT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a small moon circling a planet while the distant Sun tugs on it from a tilted direction.
Averaged over many orbits, the tug cannot change the size of the orbit, but it can trade tilt for stretch.
So the orbit slowly becomes flatter and more eccentric while it becomes less tilted, and then the process reverses.
For an artificial satellite this can bring the low point of the orbit into the atmosphere.
