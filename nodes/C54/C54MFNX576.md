## Summary

Geophysical inversion is the estimation of properties of the Earth's interior from measurements made mostly at the surface.
The properties are, for example, density, seismic velocity or electrical conductivity.
It is an [[inverse problem|PCMW7KN5P4]]: a physical forward model predicts data from a model of the Earth, and inversion runs this map backwards.
The data are finite and noisy and the forward map often smooths, so the problem is usually ill-posed.
The [[non-uniqueness of potential field inversion|FE0W9KSV6S]] is a standard example.
Practical inversions therefore add prior information, most often by [[Tikhonov regularization|M68V0M5A3R]], and report how well each feature of the model is resolved.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We cannot dig to the centre of the Earth, but we can measure gravity, earthquake waves and electric fields at the surface.
Geophysical inversion asks which inside structure would produce exactly these measurements.
Usually many different structures fit the data, so one chooses the simplest or smoothest one that fits within the measurement errors.
For example, measuring the time that earthquake waves need to cross a region tells us roughly where the rock is fast and where it is slow.
