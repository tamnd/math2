## Summary

A random vibration is the motion of a mechanical system driven by a load that is a [[stochastic process|0QH5ENKZM1]].
Examples of such loads are wind, road roughness, ocean waves and earthquakes.
Such a motion cannot be predicted in detail, so it is described by statistics: mean values, correlation functions and spectral densities.
Consider a linear system driven by a [[stationary process|DT0QXJE705]].
The spectral density of its response is the spectral density of the load times the squared modulus of the frequency response.
The theory is used to design structures and vehicles for fatigue and for extreme loads.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A car driving on a bumpy road shakes in a way that never repeats exactly.
Engineers cannot write down the exact shape of every bump, but they can describe how often bumps of each size and spacing occur.
From that statistical description they compute how strongly the car shakes on average, and how often it shakes dangerously.
A simple model is a [[damped spring|0RNXVVTJTJ]] pushed by random noise.
