## Summary

Direct simulation Monte Carlo, or DSMC, is a [[Monte Carlo method|HWP11G3HTN]] for rarefied gas flows.
It follows a large sample of simulated molecules, each representing many real ones.
They move freely and collide at random with the statistics of the [[Boltzmann equation|MH6CSWD7F0]].
It is the main tool when the [[Knudsen number|22V2W7KB1D]] is too large for continuum equations.
Examples are the upper atmosphere, vacuum devices and microflows.
The method was introduced by Graeme Bird in the 1960s.
See the [[definition|ECVNFXD1A2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Instead of solving an equation for the distribution of molecules, DSMC imitates the molecules themselves.
The computer moves a few million model molecules in straight lines for a short time.
Then, in each small cell of space, it picks random pairs of molecules and lets some of them collide, with the right probabilities.
Averaging over the molecules in a cell gives the density, velocity and temperature of the gas there.
