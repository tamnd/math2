## Summary

Mohr–Coulomb theory is a model of failure for soils, rocks and concrete.
In these materials the shear strength grows with the compressive stress across the failure plane.
A plane fails when its shear stress reaches a cohesion plus a friction term proportional to the normal pressure on it.
In terms of [[Mohr's circle|DED41X44XM]], failure occurs when the largest circle touches a straight line, the failure envelope.
It is the basic strength criterion of geotechnical engineering.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pushing a book across a table is harder when someone presses down on the book.
Soil and rock behave in the same way: grains slide on each other more easily when they are not squeezed together.
Mohr–Coulomb theory says that a plane inside the material slips when the shear on it exceeds a fixed amount, the cohesion, plus a friction coefficient times the pressure across it.
This is why a sand pile has a fixed steepest slope, and why rock deep underground is stronger.
