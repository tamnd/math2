## Summary

This topic is a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]].
It covers soundness, the completeness theorem and its proofs, the compactness theorem and its first uses, Herbrand's theorem and the interpolation theorems for first-order logic.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Soundness theorem for first-order logic|~331f6430]]: every derivable sentence is a logical consequence.
2. [[Lindenbaum's lemma|~8ecc8dc7]]: every consistent set extends to a maximal consistent set.
3. [[Henkin construction|~95e9f5bb]]: witness constants and the term model of a maximal consistent set.
4. [[Model existence theorem|G0DTR159RN]]: every consistent set of sentences has a model.
5. [[Gödel's completeness theorem|WQ1ZE255SF]]: every logical consequence is derivable.
6. [[Compactness theorem|1NR3HAWG9D]]: a set of sentences has a model if each finite subset has one.
7. [[Applications of first-order compactness|~23a8e9a5]]: nonstandard models, finiteness and transfer between characteristics.
8. [[Recursively axiomatizable theory|3ANXZC3BEG]]: theories with a computable set of axioms.
9. [[Craig's theorem on axiomatizability|~3da5e788]]: a computably enumerable theory has a computable set of axioms.
10. [[Herbrand's theorem|VZ21F10WRS]]: first-order validity reduced to propositional validity of instances.
11. [[Craig interpolation theorem|E2CBFNAVZY]]: an implication explained in the common symbols.
12. [[Lyndon interpolation theorem|BRDT1BT7WW]]: interpolation that respects positive and negative occurrences.
13. [[Robinson's joint consistency theorem|~6d43779a]]: two consistent theories that agree on the common language have a common model.
14. [[Completeness versus incompleteness|~66a43ddf]]: the two meanings of "complete" and how the theorems of 1929 and 1931 fit together.
