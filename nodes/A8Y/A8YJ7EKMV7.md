## Summary

The spherical collapse model follows a uniform spherical overdensity in an expanding universe as it slows down, turns around and collapses.
The sphere behaves like a small closed universe, so its radius has an exact cycloid solution.
It gives the critical linear overdensity $\delta_c \approx 1.686$ for collapse and the virial overdensity $18\pi^2 \approx 178$ of a halo.
These two numbers are the input of the [[Press–Schechter formalism|SZ3ZJ5MJQN]] and of many halo definitions in [[structure formation|VXMCTGXRW6]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Throw a ball upwards: if it is slower than the escape speed, it rises, stops and falls back.
A region slightly denser than its surroundings does the same in the expanding universe: it expands at first, more slowly than the rest, then stops and falls in on itself.
The model treats the region as a perfect sphere of uniform density, which makes the motion solvable by hand.
After the collapse, the matter settles into a ball whose radius is half of its largest radius, with a density about $178$ times the mean density at that time.
