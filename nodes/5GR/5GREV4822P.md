## Definition

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real or complex vector space of observables.
Let $L \colon V \to V$ be a linear map, the Liouville operator.
An observable $A \in V$ evolves as $A(t) = e^{tL} A$, by the [[matrix exponential|020M04FN1H]].
Let $P \colon V \to V$ be a [[projection|033DA1WE8Y]] onto a subspace of relevant observables, and let $Q = I - P$.
The *Mori–Zwanzig formalism* is the decomposition, valid for every $A \in V$ and $t \ge 0$,
$$\frac{d}{dt} A(t) = e^{tL} P L A + \int_0^t e^{(t - s)L} P L F(s)\, ds + F(t),$$
where $F(t) = e^{tQL} Q L A$.
The three terms are called:

1. $e^{tL} P L A$, the Markovian (streaming) term;
2. $\int_0^t e^{(t - s)L} P L F(s)\, ds$, the memory term;
3. $F(t)$, the fluctuating force (noise term).

## Remarks

The decomposition follows from the Dyson identity $e^{tL} = e^{tQL} + \int_0^t e^{(t - s)L} P L e^{sQL}\, ds$ applied to $QLA$, after splitting $\tfrac{d}{dt}A(t) = e^{tL}PLA + e^{tL}QLA$.
The noise satisfies $P F(t) = 0$ for all $t$: $QLA$ lies in the range of $Q$, and $QL$ maps $V$ into that range, so $e^{tQL}$ preserves it.
Example: let $V = \R^2$ with basis $e_1, e_2$, let $L = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, let $P$ be the projection onto $\R e_1$ along $e_2$, and let $A = e_1$.
Then $LA = -e_2$, so $PLA = 0$ and the Markovian term vanishes.
Next $QLe_2 = 0$, so $F(t) = -e_2$ for all $t$, and $PLF(s) = -e_1$, so the memory term is $-\int_0^t A(u)\, du$.
The formalism gives $\tfrac{d}{dt}A(t) = -\int_0^t A(u)\, du - e_2$, and indeed $A(t) = (\cos t, -\sin t)$ satisfies it.
Non-example: take the non-idempotent map $P = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ in the same example.
The same formula can be written, but $Q = I - P$ is not a projection.
Here $PF(t) = 2t e_1 + O(t^2) \ne 0$ for small $t > 0$, so the noise is no longer orthogonal to the relevant variables.
In the usual applications $V$ is a Hilbert space of phase-space functions and $L$ is the Liouville operator of a Hamiltonian system.
Then $P$ is the orthogonal projection onto the chosen variables in the equilibrium inner product (Mori), or a conditional expectation (Zwanzig).
