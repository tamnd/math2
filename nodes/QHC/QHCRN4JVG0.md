## Summary

This topic is a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]].
It covers first-order theories of the natural numbers as formal systems, such as Robinson's Q, Peano arithmetic, its fragments by the complexity of induction and primitive recursive arithmetic, and the coding that the incompleteness theorems need.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Language of arithmetic|D8SD54M5PN]]: the symbols $0$, $S$, $+$, $\cdot$ and $<$, numerals and bounded quantifiers.
2. [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]]: eight axioms without induction, the base theory $\mathsf{Q}$.
3. [[Weak theories of arithmetic|Q5MT8WY7JM]]: $\mathsf{Q}$, $\mathsf{R}$ and the theory of concatenation, all essentially undecidable.
4. [[Peano arithmetic|HACXBCM7SS]]: $\mathsf{Q}$ plus the first-order induction schema.
5. [[Δ0, Σn and Πn formulas|GE43CSZTS9]]: the classes of formulas by quantifier complexity.
6. [[Σ1-completeness of Robinson arithmetic|69234Z1V00]]: $\mathsf{Q}$ proves every true $\Sigma_1$ sentence.
7. [[Representability of recursive functions|BJF29XET83]]: $\mathsf{Q}$ computes every recursive function, and no consistent recursively axiomatized theory computes more.
8. [[Gödel's β-function lemma|BM6P0C2AC2]]: two numbers code any finite sequence, with a $\Delta_0$ decoding.
9. [[Primitive recursive arithmetic|WCXH72T8D8]]: a quantifier-free theory with a symbol for every primitive recursive function.
10. [[Elementary function arithmetic|2JD80TE553]]: induction for bounded formulas with exponentiation.
11. [[Fragments of Peano arithmetic|T70PYM1F36]]: induction restricted to $\Sigma_n$ formulas, and the hierarchy $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n$.
12. [[Peano arithmetic is not finitely axiomatizable|M5WNB181T7]]: no finite set of sentences has the same theorems as $\mathsf{PA}$.
13. [[True arithmetic|DX0KPMH4QZ]]: the set of all sentences true in $\N$, which no recursive set of axioms captures.

### Prerequisites

1. [[First-order languages and syntax|PSRDJXRW53]]: formulas, sentences, substitution and prenex form.
2. [[Structures, satisfaction and truth|NJDME6F845]]: truth of a sentence in a structure, and models of a theory.
3. [[Completeness, compactness and interpolation|FBWW4J1XWG]]: the completeness and compactness theorems, used for non-standard models.
4. [[Primitive recursive functions and subrecursive hierarchies|3S95HQ8Q17]]: the functions that representability and primitive recursive arithmetic are about.
5. [[The halting problem, Rice's theorem and undecidability by reduction|C86MFEQJN8]]: computably enumerable and undecidable sets.

The [[incompleteness theorems|HXHHWY3328]] build on rows 6 to 8.
