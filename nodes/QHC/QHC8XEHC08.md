## Problem

Reconcile the role of time in [[quantum mechanics|108MYT1R5Z]] with its role in [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]].
In quantum mechanics, time is an external parameter $t$, and states evolve by the Schrödinger equation $i \hbar \, \partial_t \psi = H \psi$.
In general relativity, there is no preferred time coordinate, and in the canonical formulation the dynamics is generated by the [[Hamiltonian constraint|D87R3GJMH6]] $\mathcal{H} \approx 0$.
Its quantum form, the [[Wheeler–DeWitt equation|TNKMDJB5FP]] $\hat{\mathcal{H}} \Psi = 0$, contains no time variable.
The problem is to explain how the observed evolution in time arises from such a timeless description.
It also asks for a consistent notion of time, probability and observables in quantum gravity.

## Remarks

The problem is open; it is part of the wider problem of [[quantum gravity|M1VXRT5X56]].
Proposed approaches include choosing an internal clock among the physical variables before or after quantisation, and relational descriptions in which one physical quantity is described as a function of another.
Each proposal meets difficulties, for example the absence of a clock variable that is good for all solutions.
DeWitt noted in his 1967 paper on the canonical theory of quantum gravity that the wave functional of the universe does not depend on an external time.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In ordinary quantum theory, a clock outside the system ticks and the system changes as it ticks.
In Einstein's gravity, time is part of the geometry of the universe, so there is no clock outside it.
When one tries to make gravity quantum in the most direct way, the resulting equation for the whole universe has no time in it at all.
The problem of time asks how the change we see around us can come out of such an equation.
