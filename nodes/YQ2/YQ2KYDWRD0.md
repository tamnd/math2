## Summary

The energy cascade is the transfer of kinetic energy in three-dimensional [[turbulence|VY307F8Z0S]] from large eddies to successively smaller ones.
Energy enters at the large scales, passes through an intermediate range of scales almost without loss, and is dissipated by [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] at the [[Kolmogorov scale|NYRS1BJKRQ]].
The picture goes back to Lewis Fry Richardson in the 1920s and was made quantitative by Kolmogorov in 1941.
It explains why the rate of dissipation in turbulence does not depend on the viscosity when the viscosity is small.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stir a bath vigorously: you make big swirls with your hand.
Each big swirl is unstable and breaks into smaller swirls, which break into still smaller ones.
Only the tiniest swirls are small enough for the water's friction to stop them and turn their motion into heat.
Richardson summed it up in a rhyme: big whirls have little whirls that feed on their velocity, and little whirls have lesser whirls, and so on to viscosity.
