## Summary

A surface plasmon polariton is an electromagnetic wave that travels along the interface between a metal and a dielectric and decays exponentially on both sides.
It is a coupled oscillation of the light field and of the free electrons at the metal surface.
It exists only when the real part of the metal permittivity is negative and larger in size than the dielectric permittivity.
Its wavelength is shorter than that of light of the same frequency, which lets it concentrate light below the diffraction limit.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Electrons on a metal surface can slosh back and forth like water in a bath.
Light at the surface can lock onto this sloshing, and the two travel together along the surface as one wave.
The wave clings to the surface: its field fades away within a fraction of a wavelength into the air and even faster into the metal.
Silver and gold support such waves for visible and near-infrared light, which is the basis of many biosensors.
