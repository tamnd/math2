## Summary

An inertial frame of reference is a system of time and space coordinates with one property.
In it, a body free of forces moves along a straight line at constant speed.
The laws of [[classical mechanics|68W1NWECX8]] take their simple Newtonian form only in inertial frames.
Any two inertial frames move relative to each other at a constant velocity, without rotation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a hockey puck sliding on perfectly smooth ice.
Seen from the ice, it moves in a straight line at a steady speed, so the ice is (nearly) an inertial frame.
Seen from a merry-go-round, the same puck seems to curve, although nothing pushes it sideways.
A frame where such phantom pushes appear is not inertial.
A train moving at constant speed on straight rails is inertial too: a ball dropped inside it falls straight down, as on the platform.
