## Statement

Let $F$ be a field and let $A$ and $B$ be $m \times n$ matrices over $F$.
Then
$$\rank(A + B) \le \rank A + \rank B,$$
where $\rank$ is the [[rank of a matrix|0ZC9YTXZRY]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The two matrices must have the same size, so that $A + B$ is defined.
No other hypothesis is needed: the statement holds over every field.

### Consequences

- $\lvert \rank A - \rank B \rvert \le \rank(A - B)$, by the statement for $B + (A - B)$ and for $A + (B - A)$.
- A matrix of rank $r$ is not a sum of fewer than $r$ matrices of rank $1$.
- Changing one entry of a matrix changes its rank by at most $1$, since the change is a matrix of rank at most $1$.

### Converse

The inequality is often strict.
For $B = -A$ with $A \ne 0$, the left side is $0$ and the right side is $2 \rank A$.
It is an equality for $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$: $\rank(A + B) = 2 = 1 + 1$.

### Generalizations

For linear maps $S, T \colon V \to W$, $\rank(S + T) \le \rank S + \rank T$.
Equality holds if and only if the column spaces meet only in $0$ and the row spaces meet only in $0$.

## History

The inequality is classical and appears in the lists of properties of the rank.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rank of a sum is at most the sum of the ranks.
Adding two matrices cannot create more independent columns than the two matrices have together.

### L2 human

Each column of $A + B$ is a column of $A$ plus a column of $B$.
So the column space of $A + B$ lies in the sum of the two column spaces.
The dimension of a sum of two subspaces is at most the sum of their dimensions.
