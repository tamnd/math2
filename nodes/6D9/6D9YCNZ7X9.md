## Summary

An open quantum system is a quantum system that interacts with another system, its environment or bath, whose details are not tracked.
The joint system evolves unitarily, but the system alone is described by its reduced [[density matrix|V1JTB52E8C]], obtained by the [[partial trace|SQ3J7AVMKM]] over the environment.
The reduced dynamics is in general not unitary: it can show dissipation, relaxation to equilibrium and [[decoherence|AN414B0M5X]].
Under suitable approximations it is described by a [[quantum dynamical semigroup|EQDPM8894B]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

No real quantum system is perfectly isolated: an atom feels the electromagnetic field, and a qubit feels the material around it.
The theory of open quantum systems describes the system of interest while averaging over everything else.
The price is that the system's state is no longer a single vector but a density matrix, and it can lose information to the environment.
For example, an excited atom decays to its ground state by emitting a photon into the surrounding field, which is an irreversible process for the atom alone.
