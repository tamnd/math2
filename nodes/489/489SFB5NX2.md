## Summary

*Discussive logic* (also *discursive logic*) is the [[paraconsistent logic|C7YWCR87QJ]] that Jaśkowski introduced in 1948, often called $D_2$.
It models a discussion: each participant asserts sentences, and a sentence holds in the discussion when some participant asserts it.
Formally, a sentence $A$ is read as $\Diamond A$ in the modal logic [[S5|VC3A2VWXND]], and consequence is S5 consequence between these readings.
Since $A$ and $\neg A$ can hold at different worlds, the logic is not [[explosive|TTKZGJEEK7]].
The price is that [[conjunction|025YZN3VJW]] introduction fails: $A$ and $\neg A$ do not give $A \land \neg A$.
The [[definition|2YA3PJNVMS]] states the translation.

## Motivation

In a debate, each speaker may be consistent, while the speakers together are not.
We want to reason with the whole discussion without deriving every sentence.
Jaśkowski's idea is that the contradiction arises only when we put together claims of different speakers.
So a logic of discussion should not join premises freely.
The modal reading makes this exact: each world is the view of one participant.

## Notation

We write $\Gamma \vDash_{D_2} B$ for discussive consequence.
The discussive conjunction is $A \land_d B := A \land \Diamond B$, and the discussive implication is $A \supset_d B := \Diamond A \supset B$.

## Remarks

### Examples

- [[Explosion fails in $D_2$|9X7ZPK15C5]], by a two-world S5 model.
- [[Adjunction fails, and discussive conjunction restores it|49048G0FD2]].
- Modus ponens holds for discussive implication: from $\Diamond A \supset B$ and $A$ we get $B$.

### Non-examples

- [[The reading of $A$ as $\Box A$|S92WKWKZPE]] gives classical consequence, so it is not paraconsistent.
- Classical logic with its usual conjunction is adjunctive and explosive.

### Basic properties

- $D_2$ is non-adjunctive: $A, \neg A \nvDash A \land \neg A$.
- A single contradictory premise still explodes: $A \land \neg A \vDash_{D_2} B$, because $\Diamond(A \land \neg A)$ has no model.
- Every classical inference from a single premise is valid in $D_2$.
- Modus ponens for the material conditional $\supset$ fails, which is why Jaśkowski introduced $\supset_d$.

### History

Jaśkowski, a student of Łukasiewicz, was the first formal logician to develop a paraconsistent logic.
He published discussive logic in 1948 and the discussive conjunction in 1949.
He chose S5 for simplicity.
Rescher and Manor proposed another non-adjunctive approach in 1970, based on maximal consistent subsets of the premises.

### Common errors

- To think that $D_2$ rejects all contradictions. It rejects only the step that joins $A$ and $\neg A$ into one premise.
- To think that discussive logic is a modal logic. It uses S5 only as a semantic tool; its language has no modal operator.
- To expect the ordinary conditional to work. Detachment for $\supset$ fails across participants.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a meeting where Ann says "it will rain" and Bob says "it will not rain".
The meeting as a whole contains both claims.
That does not mean the meeting claims "it will rain and it will not rain", and it surely does not claim everything.
Discussive logic counts a sentence as held when someone holds it, and never merges two people's claims into one.

### L2 human

We check explosion with two worlds.

1. Let $W = \set{w_1, w_2}$, with $p$ true at $w_1$ only and $q$ false at both.
2. $\Diamond p$ is true, since $p$ holds at $w_1$.
3. $\Diamond\neg p$ is true, since $\neg p$ holds at $w_2$.
4. $\Diamond q$ is false, since $q$ holds nowhere.

So $p, \neg p \nvDash_{D_2} q$.
A trap is to put both $p$ and $\neg p$ at one world; no world can hold both.

### L3 human

$D_2$ is defined by a translation into S5, so its consequence relation is decidable, because S5 consequence is decidable.
The non-adjunctive strategy also underlies the preservationism of Jennings and Schotch.
There the level of a premise set is the least number of consistent pieces that cover it, and consequence must hold in some piece of every such cover.
Axiomatizations of $D_2$ in its own language are more involved; the SEP entry points to Omori and Alama (2018) for their history.
