## Definition

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a vector space over $F$ of dimension $n \ge 1$, and let $B = (b_1, \dots, b_n)$ be a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $V$.
For vectors $v_1, \dots, v_n \in V$, let $M$ be the $n \times n$ matrix whose column $j$ is the [[coordinate vector|0BMVBHTWV2]] $[v_j]_B$.
The *determinant of $(v_1, \dots, v_n)$ in the basis $B$* is
$$\det_B(v_1, \dots, v_n) = \det M,$$
the [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]] of $M$.

## Notation

- $\det_B(v_1, \dots, v_n)$, or $\det_B(v)$ for the family $v = (v_1, \dots, v_n)$.
- $\det_B(B')$ for a second basis $B'$, taken in its order.

## Remarks

The map $\det_B \colon V^n \to F$ is the unique alternating multilinear form with $\det_B(b_1, \dots, b_n) = 1$, because $\det$ on matrices has this property on columns.
The vectors $v_1, \dots, v_n$ form a basis of $V$ if and only if $\det_B(v_1, \dots, v_n) \ne 0$; see the [[basis criterion|5D1CHPV9Z3]].
For another basis $B'$, $\det_{B'} = \det_{B'}(b_1, \dots, b_n) \cdot \det_B$; see the [[change of basis rule|T1WN2DHS63]].
The same definition works for a free module of rank $n$ over a commutative ring.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Write $n$ vectors in the coordinates of a fixed basis, and put the coordinates side by side as columns.
The determinant of this square matrix measures the signed volume of the box that the vectors span.
The unit is the box of the basis vectors.

### L2 human

In $\mathbb{R}^2$ with $B = ((1, 1), (0, 1))$, take $v_1 = (1, 1)$ and $v_2 = (1, 0)$.

1. $v_1 = 1 \cdot (1, 1) + 0 \cdot (0, 1)$, so $[v_1]_B = (1, 0)$.
2. $v_2 = 1 \cdot (1, 1) - 1 \cdot (0, 1)$, so $[v_2]_B = (1, -1)$.
3. So $\det_B(v_1, v_2) = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -1$.
4. Check in the standard basis $E$: $\det_E(v_1, v_2) = 1 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot 1 = -1$ and $\det_E(B) = 1$, so the values agree.

The typical use is to measure volumes or test for a basis in a space without a standard basis.
A trap: the coordinates go in the columns in the order of the family; another order can change the sign.

### L3 human

For the free module $K^n$ over a commutative ring $K$, $\det_B$ is a generator of the free rank one module of alternating $n$-linear forms.
