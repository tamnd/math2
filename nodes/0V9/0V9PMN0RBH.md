## Description

Let $F$ be a field and let $n \ge 1$.
The *special linear group* is the set
$$\mathrm{SL}_n(F) = \set{A \in \mathrm{GL}_n(F) : \det A = 1}$$
of $n \times n$ matrices over $F$ with [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]] $1$, with matrix multiplication.
It is a subgroup of the [[general linear group|0FCHE902D7]] $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$, as the [[subgroup example|PDX10T7N7Z]] checks.
The same definition works over each commutative ring $R$, and gives $\mathrm{SL}_n(R)$.

## Remarks

- It is the kernel of the group homomorphism $\det \colon \mathrm{GL}_n(F) \to F^{\times}$, so it is a [[normal subgroup|0CB0DX5BVV]].
- The determinant is surjective, since $\det \operatorname{diag}(c, 1, \dots, 1) = c$. So the quotient $\mathrm{GL}_n(F) / \mathrm{SL}_n(F)$ is isomorphic to $F^{\times}$.
- $\mathrm{SL}_n(F)$ is generated by the [[transvections|0DTCBCKEJP]] $T_{ij}(c)$, by the theorem on [[generation by transvections|~b06f837f]].
- Over $\mathbb{R}$, its elements are the linear maps that keep volume and orientation, since $\abs{\det A}$ is the factor by which $A$ scales volumes.
- $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is the group of integer $2 \times 2$ matrices with determinant $1$; the examples [[$\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$|Q0GBCB9RA4]] and [[$\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$|J58ED1GV6N]] treat the real and complex cases.
- For $n = 1$ the group is trivial, since the only $1 \times 1$ matrix with determinant $1$ is $(1)$.
- Over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathrm{SL}_n$ is a [[matrix Lie group|36KSZ7GA5S]]; this belongs to Lie theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The special linear group collects the matrices with determinant $1$.
In the real case, these maps keep the volume of every region, and they do not flip orientation.
Shears, such as $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & t \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, are typical elements.

### L2 human

Worked example: the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has $\det A = 4 - 3 = 1$, so $A \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$.

1. Its inverse is $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, again with determinant $1$.
2. The matrix $\operatorname{diag}(2, \tfrac{1}{2})$ is in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$, but $\operatorname{diag}(2, 1)$ is not.
3. $A$ is a product of three transvections: $A = T_{12}(1)\, T_{21}(1)\, T_{12}(1)$, as the theorem on generation by transvections shows.

A trap: $\mathrm{SL}_n$ is not the set of matrices with determinant $\pm 1$; that set is a larger group when $1 \ne -1$ in $F$.

### L3 human

$\mathrm{SL}_n(F)$ is the commutator subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$, except when $n = 2$ and $F$ has $2$ elements.
Its quotient by its centre, the projective special linear group, is simple for $n \ge 2$, except for $n = 2$ and $F$ with $2$ or $3$ elements.
This belongs to group theory.
