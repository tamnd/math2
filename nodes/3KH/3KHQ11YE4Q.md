## Summary

Systems theory studies systems as wholes: objects that receive inputs, have an internal state, and produce outputs.
In mathematics, general systems theory describes a system abstractly, for example by a map from input signals to output signals or by a state equation.
It studies properties such as [[linearity|21FN2KSTJZ]], [[time invariance|S4THJVE1AJ]] and [[causality|NSR7N8A71M]].
It gives the common language of [[control theory|JXZ0GZXET1]], signal processing and circuit theory.
The word is also used for a wider interdisciplinary field that studies systems in biology, engineering and the social sciences.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A system is anything that turns an input into an output over time, such as an amplifier, a heated room or a bank account with deposits.
Systems theory ignores what the system is made of and keeps only how inputs and outputs are related.
This lets one result serve many systems at once.
For example, the rule "the output at time $t$ is the input at time $t - 1$" describes a delay line, and it is linear, time-invariant and causal, whatever device realizes it.
