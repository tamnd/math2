## Summary

Koiter's theory is the asymptotic analysis of elastic structures near the point where they [[buckle|4GAM4KXSM8]].
It describes the initial post-buckling path of the perfect structure by a few coefficients.
From them it predicts how much small geometric imperfections lower the maximum load.
It explains why thin cylindrical shells under axial compression fail far below their classical buckling load while flat plates do not.
W. T. Koiter set it out in his Delft doctoral thesis of 1945.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two structures can have the same buckling load and still behave very differently once they start to bend.
A flat plate keeps carrying more load after it buckles, so a small dent hardly matters.
A thin can under end load loses strength as soon as it starts to buckle, so a tiny dent lets it collapse well before the ideal load.
Koiter's theory tells which case applies by looking at the shape of the load–deflection curve just after buckling.
