## Summary

A linearly ordered group is an [[ordered group|KRDR04RQ7N]] whose order is a [[total order|0FRXBVVR03]].
Any two of its elements are comparable, and the order is preserved by multiplication on both sides.
The additive groups of $\Z$, $\Q$ and $\R$ are the basic examples.
Linearly ordered abelian groups are the value groups of valuations, and they are described by Hölder's theorem and the Hahn embedding theorem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A linearly ordered group is a group whose elements stand in one line, from smaller to larger.
Multiplying by a fixed element on either side shifts the whole line without changing the order of its points.
The integers under addition are an example: adding $5$ to every integer keeps them in the same order.
The pairs of integers with the dictionary order are another example, where $(0, 1000) < (1, 0)$.
