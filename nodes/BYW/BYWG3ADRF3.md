## Summary

The Oseen–Frank energy is the elastic energy of a nematic liquid crystal in terms of its director field.
The director field is a unit vector field that gives the local direction of the molecules.
It is the most general quadratic energy in the first derivatives of the director that respects the symmetries of a nematic.
Its three main terms measure splay, twist and bend, with three elastic constants $K_1$, $K_2$, $K_3$.
Minimizers of this energy, with boundary conditions, model the textures and defects seen in liquid crystal cells and displays.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a nematic liquid crystal the rod-like molecules tend to point in the same direction.
Where this direction changes from place to place, the material stores elastic energy, like a bent spring.
There are three basic ways to change direction: spreading out like a fan (splay), turning like a staircase (twist), and curving like a bent bow (bend).
The Oseen–Frank energy charges each of these with its own stiffness.
A uniform alignment costs nothing; a helical arrangement whose direction turns at rate $q$ along an axis costs $K_2 q^2 / 2$ per unit volume.
