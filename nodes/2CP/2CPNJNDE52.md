## Summary

The Charney–Hasegawa–Mima equation is a nonlinear equation for a single stream function that appears both in geophysics and in plasma physics.
In geophysics it is the equivalent-barotropic form of the [[quasi-geostrophic equations|8Z4QBWTWVR]], a layer of finite [[deformation radius|MT3A0DDAA8]] on a [[beta plane|F2D7MXW8V1]].
In plasma physics it describes drift waves in a magnetised plasma with a density gradient.
It is a basic model for Rossby waves, two-dimensional turbulence and zonal jets.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The equation follows a single swirling layer of fluid on a rotating planet whose top surface can bulge up and down.
It adds one effect to the simplest vorticity equation: when the layer's surface moves, it squeezes or stretches the fluid columns.
This makes large eddies stiffer and slows the long Rossby waves.
Remarkably, the same equation was found to govern certain waves in hot magnetised plasmas, as in fusion devices.
