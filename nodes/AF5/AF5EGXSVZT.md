## Summary

Approximation theory studies how well general functions can be approximated by simple functions, such as polynomials, splines, rational functions and sums of basis elements.
The central quantity is the error of best approximation, which is the distance in a chosen norm from a function to a given set of approximants.
The theory gives the error bounds behind numerical methods for interpolation, quadrature and differential equations, and it is the base of computer-aided geometric design.
Chebyshev studied best uniform approximation by polynomials in the middle of the nineteenth century, and Weierstrass proved in 1885 that each continuous function on a closed interval is a uniform limit of polynomials.
This topic collects the approximation theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Harmonic analysis, numerical analysis, and integral transforms and asymptotic methods have their own topics.
