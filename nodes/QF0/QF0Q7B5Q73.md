## Example

We give two short [[proofs|0B94TKFQJX]] about the [[natural numbers|0MWKJ44V5A]] in the language of the proof assistant Lean 4.
In Lean the natural numbers are the type `Nat`, and a statement is a type whose elements are its proofs.
The first proof is the line `example : 2 + 2 = 4 := rfl`.
The second proof is the line `theorem add_comm_example (a b : Nat) : a + b = b + a := Nat.add_comm a b`.

1. The first line states $2 + 2 = 4$. The text after `:=` is the proof term `rfl`, which says "both sides are the same value".
2. Lean computes both sides of $2 + 2 = 4$, finds the same natural number, and accepts the term `rfl` as a proof.
3. The second line states that $a + b = b + a$ for all natural numbers $a$ and $b$.
4. Its proof term is `Nat.add_comm a b`. The name `Nat.add_comm` refers to a theorem that the Lean core library proves once for all $a$ and $b$.
5. Lean checks that the type of `Nat.add_comm a b` is exactly the statement $a + b = b + a$, and then accepts the theorem.
6. If we write `example : 2 + 2 = 5 := rfl` instead, Lean computes $4$ and $5$, finds two different values, and rejects the proof with an error.

## Remarks

The example shows a proof as a checked object.
The program does not trust the author: it checks each proof term against the statement with a small trusted part, the kernel.
Step 6 shows that a false claim gets no proof of this kind.

The proof in step 4 is short because it uses a result that is already proved, in the same way as a human proof cites a lemma.
Longer proofs in Lean are written with tactics, which are commands that build the proof term step by step.
This match between proofs and terms is the [[Curry-Howard correspondence|A77FER1Q5H]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A proof assistant is a computer program that checks proofs.
We type a statement, such as "two plus two is four", and then a proof of it.
The program checks each step and says "accepted" or shows an error.
For "two plus two is four" the program just computes both sides and sees the same number.
For "two plus two is five" it computes, sees two different numbers, and refuses the proof.

### L2 human

We read the second line of the example as a statement with a proof.

1. The part `(a b : Nat)` fixes two arbitrary natural numbers $a$ and $b$.
2. The part `a + b = b + a` is the statement to prove for these $a$ and $b$.
3. The part `Nat.add_comm a b` applies a general theorem to $a$ and $b$.
4. Lean compares the statement of the applied theorem with the statement in step 2. They agree, so Lean accepts.

The typical use is to check a proof that is long or delicate, where a human reader can miss an error.
A trap is to think that the program checks the meaning of the statement.
It checks only that the proof proves the statement as written, so a wrongly stated theorem can have a correct formal proof.
