## Statement

Let $A$ be a real $m \times n$ matrix and let $b \in \R^m$.
Suppose that the columns of $A$ are [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]].
Then the equation $Ax = b$ has exactly one [[least squares|0BYTQ4X1HC]] approximate solution $\hat x$, that is, exactly one vector that makes $\norm{Ax - b}$ as small as possible.
This vector is the solution of the normal equation $A^T A \hat x = A^T b$, where $A^T$ is the [[transpose|09DXWYRD43]] of $A$.

## Remarks

The independence of the columns is what gives uniqueness.
Without it, many vectors $\hat x$ can give the same closest point $A\hat x$.
The normal equation says that the residual $b - A\hat x$ is orthogonal to every column of $A$.
