## Summary

A rigid body rolls without slipping on a surface when its contact point does not slide.
At each instant, the point of the body touching the surface has the same velocity as the surface there.
For a fixed surface this means that the contact point of the body is momentarily at rest.
It is the basic model of wheels, balls and rolling coins.
In general it is a [[nonholonomic constraint|PTRV0QFSB9]]: it restricts velocities without restricting positions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bicycle wheel on a road touches the ground at one point, and if it does not skid, that point of the tyre is at rest for an instant.
So the centre of a wheel of radius $r$ turning at angular speed $\omega$ moves forward at speed $r\omega$.
A coin rolling on a table obeys the same rule, but it can steer.
Because of the steering, a rolling coin can reach any position and orientation, even though at each moment it can only move in some directions.
