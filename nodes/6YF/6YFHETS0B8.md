## Summary

The Laughlin wavefunction is a trial ground state for the fractional [[quantum Hall effect|11AZ4RGXVN]] at filling fractions $\nu = 1/m$, $m$ odd.
Robert Laughlin proposed it in 1983.
It is a product of the factors $(z_i - z_j)^m$ over all pairs of electrons, times a Gaussian.
Here $z_j$ is the position of electron $j$ as a complex number.
Its excitations carry fractional charge $e/m$ and have fractional exchange statistics, so they are [[anyons|CSX5R1RAPN]].
Laughlin shared the 1998 Nobel Prize in Physics for this explanation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a strong magnetic field, electrons confined to a plane avoid each other in a very particular way.
Laughlin guessed a formula for their joint state: whenever two electrons approach, the wavefunction vanishes like the $m$-th power of their distance.
This keeps the electrons apart and lowers their repulsion.
The formula has no free parameters, and it matched the experiments at $\nu = 1/3$ remarkably well.
For $m = 1$ it is the state of a completely filled lowest Landau level, with no interactions needed.
