## Summary

A black hole is a region of spacetime from which nothing, not even light, can escape to far-away observers.
Its boundary is the [[event horizon|D8NZ32Z3SQ]].
In general relativity black holes form by gravitational collapse, as in the [[Oppenheimer–Snyder model|WYV7PXCXJ7]].
By the [[no-hair theorem|RAEHHEPX7F]] the stationary ones in electrovacuum are described by the [[Kerr–Newman metric|NM34PFSE8A]] family.
Black holes are central objects of both mathematical relativity and astrophysics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Throw a ball upwards and it falls back, unless you throw it faster than the escape speed.
Near a very compact mass the escape speed exceeds the speed of light, and then nothing can get out.
In relativity this is described through spacetime itself: inside the black hole every future direction, even that of light, points further inwards.
The simplest example is the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]], where the black hole is the region $r \le 2m$, a sphere of radius about $3$ km for each solar mass.
Astronomers observe black holes through the motion of stars and gas around them and through the gravitational waves emitted when two of them merge.
