## Summary

Ergodic theory is the study of dynamical systems that preserve a measure, of the average behaviour of their orbits, and of the question of when two such systems are isomorphic.
A measure-preserving transformation of a probability space is a measurable map such that the inverse image of each measurable set has the same measure as the set.
The subject gives a mathematical base for the ergodic hypothesis of statistical mechanics, and its methods give proofs of deep results in combinatorics and number theory, such as Szemerédi's theorem on arithmetic progressions.
Birkhoff and von Neumann proved the first ergodic theorems in the early 1930s.
This topic collects the ergodic theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Dynamical systems, probability theory and operator algebras have their own topics.
