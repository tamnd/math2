## Summary

The groundwater flow equation is the partial differential equation for the hydraulic head of water in an aquifer.
It combines [[Darcy's law|DX8RV2XG6H]], which gives the flux of water from the gradient of the head, with conservation of mass.
Storage comes from the small compressibility of the water and the rock.
For a uniform aquifer it is the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]] for the head, and in steady state it is [[Laplace's equation|S8RG6N1PRT]].
It is the basic model of hydrogeology, used to predict the effect of pumping wells and the spread of contaminants.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Water underground moves slowly through the tiny pores of sand and rock, from places of high pressure to places of low pressure.
The hydraulic head is the height to which water would rise in a pipe pushed into the ground at that point; water flows from high head to low head.
When a well pumps water out, the head drops near the well, and the drop spreads outward over time, like heat spreading from a cold spot.
The groundwater flow equation describes exactly this spreading.
