## Summary

A restricted Lie algebra is a [[modular Lie algebra|HKYYR1SZQW]] with an extra operation $x \mapsto x^{[p]}$ that behaves like the $p$-th power in an associative algebra.
The notion is due to Nathan Jacobson.
Lie algebras of algebraic groups in characteristic $p$, and algebras of derivations, carry such an operation.
The $p$-operation gives restricted enveloping algebras, which are finite-dimensional when the Lie algebra is.
It is basic for the representation theory of modular Lie algebras.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In characteristic $p$, the $p$-th power of a matrix behaves very well: $(a + b)^p = a^p + b^p$ when $a$ and $b$ commute.
A restricted Lie algebra keeps a $p$-th power map as part of its structure, even though a Lie algebra has only a bracket and no associative product.
For a Lie algebra of matrices, the $p$-power map is the usual $p$-th power of matrices.
For example, $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\F_p)$ is restricted with $x^{[p]} = x^p$.
