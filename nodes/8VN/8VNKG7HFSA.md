## Summary

Gravitoelectromagnetism is a formulation of weak-field general relativity for slowly moving matter.
It splits the gravitational field into a gravitoelectric field and a gravitomagnetic field.
These fields obey equations of the same shape as [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]].
Mass density takes the place of charge density, and mass current that of electric current.
The gravitoelectric field is the Newtonian gravitational field, while the gravitomagnetic field is produced by moving or rotating masses.
It gives a simple picture of frame-dragging effects near rotating bodies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A static electric charge makes an electric field, and a moving charge also makes a magnetic field.
General relativity says that mass behaves in a similar way when gravity is weak.
A mass at rest gives the ordinary Newtonian pull, and a moving or spinning mass adds a small extra field that pushes sideways on moving objects.
For example, the rotating Earth drags nearby gyroscopes very slightly, and this was measured by the Gravity Probe B satellite.
The analogy is not perfect: signs and numerical factors differ from electromagnetism, because like masses attract.
