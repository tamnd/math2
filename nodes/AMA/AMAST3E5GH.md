## Summary

Mathematical biology and chemistry is the use of mathematical models to study living systems and chemical processes.
A typical model is a system of differential equations, difference equations or random processes whose variables are the sizes of populations, the frequencies of genes, or the concentrations of chemical species.
Such models explain observed patterns, such as the cycles of predators and prey or the spread of an epidemic, and they help decisions in public health, fisheries and conservation.
Malthus wrote on population growth in 1798, Lotka and Volterra proposed their predator-prey equations in the 1920s, and Fisher, Haldane and Wright built mathematical population genetics in the 1920s and 1930s.
This topic collects the mathematical biology and chemistry pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Dynamical systems, ordinary and partial differential equations, stochastic processes and game theory have their own topics.
