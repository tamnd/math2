## Summary

The upper critical dimension of a family of models is the dimension above which their critical behaviour is described exactly by [[mean-field theory|1DGJYHG10E]].
Above it, fluctuations are too weak to change the critical exponents.
At the upper critical dimension itself, mean-field exponents hold up to logarithmic corrections.
It is $4$ for the Ising and $O(n)$ models with short-range interactions and $6$ for percolation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a space with many dimensions, each site has many neighbours, and the average of their influence is very reliable.
Then the simple mean-field picture, where each spin only feels the average of the others, gives the right answer near the critical point.
In few dimensions, the neighbours are few and their random fluctuations matter a lot.
The upper critical dimension is the border between these two worlds.
For ordinary magnets it is $4$, just one more than the dimension of our space, which is why mean-field exponents are close but not exact for real magnets.
