## Summary

The paraxial wave equation describes a light beam that travels mostly along one axis and spreads only slowly sideways.
It comes from the [[Helmholtz equation|MNXKTXJ35G]] when the field is written as a fast plane wave along the axis times a slowly varying envelope.
It has the form of the free [[Schrödinger equation|EPZR6H2WJN]] in two dimensions, with the distance along the beam playing the role of time.
Its solutions include the [[Gaussian beam|Y0QE726BE3]] and the higher-order laser modes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A laser beam is long and thin: it moves forward fast and widens only a little.
So one can split the light into a rapid wave going forward and a slowly changing profile across the beam.
The paraxial wave equation tells how this profile changes as the beam moves forward.
It is the same equation that tells how a free quantum particle spreads in a plane, so the beam spreads the way a wave packet does.
It is accurate as long as the beam is not focused too tightly.
