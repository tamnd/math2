## Statement

Let $L$ be a finite relational signature, and let $M$ be a countably infinite $L$-structure with theory $T = \operatorname{Th}(M)$.
Then $M$ is [[ultrahomogeneous|H8VM37SSAX]] if and only if $T$ is [[$\aleph_0$-categorical|A22K1X8GKD]] and has [[quantifier elimination|7FDDXV3JK7]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Finite signature: let $L = \set{P_i : i \in \N}$ be unary and $M = \set{a_i : i \in \N}$ with $P_i = \set{a_i}$. Every partial isomorphism is an identity map, so $M$ is ultrahomogeneous. But $T$ has a countable model with extra points outside all $P_i$, so $T$ is not $\aleph_0$-categorical.
- Relational: $(\Z, <, s)$ with the successor function $s$ is ultrahomogeneous, since its finitely generated substructures are the sets $m + \N$. Its theory has a countable model with two copies of $\Z$, so it is not $\aleph_0$-categorical.
- Countable, for the converse: $\R + \Q$, the reals followed by a copy of the rationals, is a dense order without endpoints. Its theory is $\aleph_0$-categorical with quantifier elimination. A point of $\R$ cannot be moved to a point of $\Q$, since the final segments have different cardinalities.

### Consequences

- The [[Rado graph|RMXR0FEF54]], the [[Henson graphs|TZT25G2YB3]] and $(\Q, <)$ have $\aleph_0$-categorical theories with quantifier elimination.
- Every Fraïssé limit of a class of finite structures in a finite relational signature has such a theory.
- In such an $M$, the definable sets are finite Boolean combinations of atomic formulas.

### Converse

The statement is an equivalence.
In a finite relational signature, quantifier elimination alone gives finitely many $n$-types for each $n$, hence $\aleph_0$-categoricity.
So the right side reduces to quantifier elimination.
$\aleph_0$-categoricity alone does not suffice.
The graph $K_1 \sqcup \aleph_0 \cdot K_2$, one isolated vertex beside infinitely many disjoint edges, has an $\aleph_0$-categorical theory but is not ultrahomogeneous.

### Generalizations

The forward direction and the equivalence extend to countable structures in a finite signature that are uniformly locally finite: the size of the substructure generated by $n$ elements is bounded by a function of $n$.

## History

The result joins two lines of work: Fraïssé's theory of homogeneous relational structures and the Ryll-Nardzewski characterization of $\aleph_0$-categorical theories.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a countable structure built from finitely many relations, three things go together.
Every finite matching comes from a symmetry; every property is decided by the finite pattern of a tuple; and the theory has only one countable model.
The first is the same as the other two together.

### L2 human

We test the theorem on $(\Q, <)$.

1. $(\Q, <)$ is ultrahomogeneous, by stretching gaps between points.
2. The theory of dense linear orders without endpoints has quantifier elimination: a formula in $x_1, \dots, x_n$ is decided by the order pattern of the $x_i$.
3. Cantor's theorem says that the theory is $\aleph_0$-categorical.

The typical use is to get quantifier elimination for a Fraïssé limit for free.
A trap is to apply the theorem with function symbols, where it can fail.

### L3 human

The forward direction counts orbits: ultrahomogeneity makes the orbits on $n$-tuples equal to the quantifier-free types, and there are finitely many.
The [[Ryll-Nardzewski theorem|1513J40TKA]] turns "finitely many $n$-types for every $n$" into $\aleph_0$-categoricity.
The converse uses only quantifier elimination and countability, through the extension property and a back-and-forth argument.
