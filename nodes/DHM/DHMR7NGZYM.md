## Summary

The Kelvin–Helmholtz mechanism is the heating and shining of a star or planet caused by its slow gravitational contraction.
As the body shrinks, its gravitational energy decreases.
By the [[virial theorem|6T0BTVNMRF]], about half of the released energy heats the interior and the other half is radiated away.
It powers stars before they start nuclear fusion, and it explains why Jupiter emits more heat than it receives from the Sun.
In the nineteenth century it was proposed as the energy source of the Sun.
But the lifetime it gives, the [[Kelvin–Helmholtz timescale|KR6BPP5WHY]], is far shorter than the age of the Earth.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a ball of gas held together by its own gravity shrinks a little, its layers fall inward and speed up.
Collisions turn this motion into heat, so the gas gets hotter.
The hot gas glows and loses energy, so it shrinks a little more, and the cycle goes on.
Surprisingly, losing energy makes the star hotter inside, not cooler.
Young stars that have not yet started burning hydrogen shine in this way.
