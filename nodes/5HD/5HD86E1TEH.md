## Summary

Declination is the latitude coordinate of the [[equatorial coordinate system|QBEJJPMWV1]].
It is the angle of a direction north or south of the celestial equator, from $-90^\circ$ to $+90^\circ$.
With [[right ascension|RHNFB0HEW4]] it fixes a direction in the sky, and with the observer's latitude it decides whether a star rises and sets.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Declination is the sky's version of geographic latitude.
A star with declination $+40^\circ$ passes straight overhead for observers at latitude $40^\circ$ north.
The Sun's declination moves between about $-23.4^\circ$ and $+23.4^\circ$ during the year, and this is why the seasons change.
Polaris has declination close to $+89^\circ$, so it stays almost fixed above the north pole of the sky.
