## Definition

The dimension of a vector space is the size of any of its bases.
Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a vector space over $F$.
If $V$ is [[finite-dimensional|018W3V3TCT]], the *dimension* of $V$ over $F$, written $\dim_F V$ or $\dim V$, is the number of vectors in a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $V$.
In general, the dimension of $V$ is the cardinality of a basis of $V$.

## Notation

We write $\dim V$ when the field is clear and $\dim_F V$ when it is not.
Mathlib has two versions: `Module.rank F V` is the dimension as a cardinal, and matches the general case.
The natural number `Module.finrank F V` matches the finite case, and it takes the value $0$ for every infinite-dimensional space.

## Remarks

The definition says "a basis", so it needs two facts to make sense.
First, a basis must exist: a finite-dimensional space has a finite spanning set, and [[every spanning set contains a basis|06MRNP4BNT]]; in general [[every vector space has a basis|00MS9VMQ9Z]].
Second, the size must not depend on the choice, which is the [[dimension theorem|0WK0Y8RH1M]].

For example, $\mathbb{R}^2$ has the bases $(1, 0), (0, 1)$ and $(1, 1), (1, -1)$, and both have two vectors, so $\dim \mathbb{R}^2 = 2$.
The zero space $\{0\}$ has the empty basis, so its dimension is $0$.

The field is part of the definition.
The set $\mathbb{C}$ has dimension $1$ over $\mathbb{C}$, with basis $1$, and dimension $2$ over $\mathbb{R}$, with basis $1, i$.
The [[concept page on dimension|03GSZ8RTZJ]] gives more examples and the rules for computing dimensions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The dimension counts the independent directions in a space.
A line has dimension $1$, a plane has dimension $2$, and ordinary space has dimension $3$.
Every basis of a space has this same number of vectors, so it does not matter which basis we count.

### L2 human

To find a dimension, find one basis and count it.
The polynomials $a + bx + cx^2$ of degree at most $2$ have the basis $1, x, x^2$, because each such polynomial is $a \cdot 1 + b \cdot x + c \cdot x^2$ in exactly one way.
So this space has dimension $3$, even though its elements are not lists of numbers.
Watch out: the zero space has dimension $0$, because its basis is empty.
