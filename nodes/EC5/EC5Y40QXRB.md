## Summary

Monin–Obukhov similarity theory describes the mean wind and temperature profiles in the surface layer of the [[planetary boundary layer|4R7HASDQ6Q]].
It covers neutral conditions as well as air that is heated or cooled from below.
It asserts that the vertical gradients, scaled by the surface fluxes, depend on height only through $z / L$.
Here $L$ is the Obukhov length.
It extends the logarithmic [[law of the wall|7AX1EA410X]] to stratified conditions and is used in nearly all weather and climate models to compute surface fluxes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Near the ground, wind speed grows with height, quickly at first and then slowly.
On a sunny afternoon the heated ground stirs the air, and the wind changes less with height.
On a clear night the cooled ground calms the air, and the wind changes more with height.
The theory says that all these cases follow one universal rule, once heights are measured in units of a single length, the Obukhov length.
This length tells at what height the stirring by heat becomes as important as the stirring by wind.
It lets models turn the wind and temperature at one height into the exchange of heat and momentum with the surface.
