## Definition

An *unrestricted grammar*, or *type-0 grammar*, is a [[formal grammar|DP1HXZGPCM]] $G = (N, \Sigma, P, S)$ with no condition on its rules beyond the general one.
So each rule is $\alpha \to \beta$ with $\alpha, \beta \in (N \cup \Sigma)^*$ and $\alpha$ containing at least one nonterminal.

## Remarks

Example: $N = \set{S, B, C}$, $\Sigma = \set{a, b, c}$ and the rules $S \to aSBC$, $S \to aBC$, $CB \to BC$, $aB \to ab$, $bB \to bb$, $bC \to bc$, $cC \to cc$.
This grammar generates $\set{a^n b^n c^n : n \ge 1}$.
The first two rules make $a^n (BC)^n$, the rule $CB \to BC$ sorts the $B$s before the $C$s, and the last four turn them into terminals from left to right.
Non-example: a rule $ab \to c$ with $a, b, c \in \Sigma$ is not allowed, because its left side has no nonterminal.
A language is generated by an unrestricted grammar if and only if it is [[recursively enumerable|67R1A5Y54R]], that is, recognised by a [[Turing machine|758EYCN27B]].
It follows that membership in $L(G)$ is undecidable for some unrestricted grammars $G$.
