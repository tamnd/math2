## Summary

The permeability of a material measures how strongly it supports a [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] in response to a given magnetizing field.
For a linear isotropic material it is the constant $\mu$ in the relation $B = \mu H$.
The permeability of vacuum, $\mu_0$, is a constant of nature that fixes the strength of magnetic forces between currents.
It is the magnetic counterpart of the [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put a coil of wire around a rod and run a current through the coil.
With an iron rod the magnetic field inside is hundreds or thousands of times stronger than with a wooden rod, because the iron becomes [[magnetized|A2WZA4QA3V]] and adds its own field.
The permeability of a material is the number that says how much stronger.
Iron has a large permeability, and air, wood and copper have a permeability very close to that of empty space.
