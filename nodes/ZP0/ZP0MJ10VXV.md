## Summary

The Hartmann number $\mathrm{Ha} = B_0 L \sqrt{\sigma / \mu}$ is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] of [[magnetohydrodynamics|T8JWCBK9M6]] that compares magnetic braking with viscous friction.
Its square is the ratio of the Lorentz force to the viscous force.
At large Hartmann number, flows of liquid metals across a magnetic field become flat in the core.
They have thin boundary layers, the Hartmann layers, of thickness about $L / \mathrm{Ha}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a liquid metal flows across a magnetic field, induced currents slow it down, like a magnetic brake.
Viscosity also slows the flow, through friction.
The Hartmann number says which of the two wins.
For liquid metal in the strong magnets of a fusion reactor, it can be in the thousands, and the magnetic brake dominates.
