## Summary

Geostrophic balance is the balance between the Coriolis force and the horizontal pressure gradient force in a rotating fluid.
It holds approximately for large-scale, slow flows in the atmosphere and the ocean, where the [[Rossby number|SCYK4FMY59]] is small.
The flow it gives, the geostrophic flow, runs along the isobars instead of across them.
It is the leading-order balance of [[geophysical fluid dynamics|2MNWD70JN3]] and the starting point of the [[quasi-geostrophic equations|8Z4QBWTWVR]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a non-rotating planet, air would rush straight from high pressure to low pressure.
On the rotating Earth the Coriolis force turns moving air to the right in the northern hemisphere.
The air ends up flowing along the lines of equal pressure, with the push of the pressure and the turn by rotation in balance.
That is why winds in the northern hemisphere circle anticlockwise around low pressure centres.
