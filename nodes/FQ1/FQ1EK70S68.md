## Statement

Consider a quantitative trait with population mean $\mu$.
Let a group of mating pairs be selected as parents.
Let the selection differential $S$ be the mean of the midparent values $(P_m + P_f)/2$ over the selected pairs, minus $\mu$.
Assume that, for every pair, the [[expected|0WQNRCNY3M]] phenotype of an offspring is
$$\mu + b \left( \frac{P_m + P_f}{2} - \mu \right),$$
for a constant $b$, the [[linear regression|41FVC4P5JB]] slope of offspring on midparent.
Then the response $R$, the expected mean of the offspring minus $\mu$, is
$$R = b S.$$
If moreover mating is random, there is no epistasis, and parents and offspring share no environmental effects, then $b = h^2$, the narrow-sense [[heritability|9GGRK9ESD6]], and $R = h^2 S$.

## Remarks

The first part follows by averaging the linear conditional mean over the selected pairs.
For the second part, the offspring breeding value is the mean of the parents' breeding values plus independent noise.
So the covariance of offspring and midparent is $V_A/2$, while the variance of the midparent is $V_P/2$.
Their quotient is $h^2$.
Example: if $h^2 = 0.4$ and breeders choose parents whose mean exceeds the population mean by $10$ units, then the next generation is expected to exceed it by $4$ units.
If the parent–offspring regression is not linear, as can happen under strong dominance or epistasis, the formula is only an approximation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Breeders select the best parents, but the offspring are on average less extreme than their parents.
The breeder's equation says by how much: the gain equals the heritability times the selection advantage of the parents.
With heritability $0$, selection gains nothing; with heritability $1$, the offspring keep the whole advantage.

### L2 human

This equation is the basic tool of animal and plant breeding and of the study of evolution of quantitative traits.
Repeated over generations, it predicts a steady change of the mean as long as $h^2$ stays roughly constant.
Its multivariate form, $\Delta \bar z = G \beta$ with the genetic covariance matrix $G$ and the selection gradient $\beta$, is the Lande equation.
It can also be read as a special case of the Price equation in which the covariance between fitness and breeding value is expressed through the regression on phenotype.
