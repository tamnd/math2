## Definition

Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space and let $V \to X$ be a real [[vector bundle|P4DZM975BV]] of rank $n$.
Let $\operatorname{Th}(V)$ be its [[Thom space|5MNYT3AFPH]].
For $x \in X$, let $j_x \colon \operatorname{Th}(V_x) \to \operatorname{Th}(V)$ be the inclusion of the Thom space of the fibre, which is homeomorphic to $S^n$.
Let $\tilde{K}^n$ be reduced complex K-theory in degree $n$, viewed as a [[generalized cohomology theory|677EDH782C]], so $\tilde{K}^n(S^n) \cong \Z$.
A *K-orientation* of $V$ is a class $u \in \tilde{K}^n(\operatorname{Th}(V))$ such that, for every $x \in X$, the class $j_x^*(u)$ generates $\tilde{K}^n(\operatorname{Th}(V_x)) \cong \Z$.
The bundle $V$ is *K-orientable* if it has a K-orientation.

## Remarks

Example: a complex vector bundle $E$ of complex rank $m$ is a real bundle of rank $2m$.
The K-theory Thom class $\lambda_E$ of the [[Thom isomorphism in K-theory|JBX99NRRB6]] lies in $\tilde{K}^0(\operatorname{Th}(E)) \cong \tilde{K}^{2m}(\operatorname{Th}(E))$ and restricts to a generator on each fibre, so it is a K-orientation.

Non-example: the Möbius line bundle over $S^1$ is not orientable.
A K-orientable bundle is orientable, so the Möbius bundle has no K-orientation.

A real vector bundle is K-orientable exactly when it admits a spin$^c$ structure.
This means that its first Stiefel–Whitney class vanishes and its second Stiefel–Whitney class is the reduction of an integral class.
Bundles with a [[spin structure|RSPZ9TCFQ8]] are spin$^c$, hence K-orientable.
A K-orientation $u$ gives a Thom isomorphism $K^*(X) \to \tilde{K}^{*+n}(\operatorname{Th}(V))$, $a \mapsto a \cdot u$, by the same argument as for complex bundles.
