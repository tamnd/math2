## Summary

The Elsasser number is a dimensionless number that compares the Lorentz force with the [[Coriolis force|6HZRX3WSQD]] in a rotating, electrically conducting fluid.
It is one of the [[dimensionless numbers of fluid mechanics|T3R5FWV91P]] used in [[magnetohydrodynamics|T8JWCBK9M6]].
In the Earth's outer core it is of order one, which means that magnetic and rotational forces are comparable there.
This balance is called the magnetostrophic regime and is central to theories of the [[geodynamo|19AESAFHF9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the liquid core two forces fight over the motion of the metal: the twist from the Earth's rotation and the push of the magnetic field on electric currents.
The Elsasser number is the ratio of these two forces.
If it is small, rotation wins and the flow forms tall columns along the axis.
If it is about one, as in the Earth's core, both forces shape the flow together.
