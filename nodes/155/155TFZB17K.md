## Definition

Let $X$ be a non-negative [[random variable|0PNRKMRC55]] with [[distribution function|0H8H7ZTHW2]] $F$ and finite positive [[mean|0WQNRCNY3M]] $\mu$.
Let $F^{-1}(u) = \inf \set{x : F(x) \ge u}$ be its [[quantile function|0X60VE98HN]].
The *Lorenz curve* of $X$ is the function $L \colon [0, 1] \to [0, 1]$ given by
$$L(p) = \frac{1}{\mu} \int_0^p F^{-1}(u) \, du.$$
For finitely many values $0 \le x_1 \le \dots \le x_n$ with positive sum, it is the polygon through the points
$$\left( \frac{i}{n}, \frac{x_1 + \dots + x_i}{x_1 + \dots + x_n} \right), \quad i = 0, 1, \dots, n.$$

## Remarks

Every Lorenz curve satisfies $L(0) = 0$ and $L(1) = 1$, is non-decreasing and convex, and satisfies $L(p) \le p$.
Convexity holds because $F^{-1}$ is non-decreasing.
Equality $L(p) = p$ for all $p$ holds exactly when $X$ is almost surely constant.

Example: for incomes $1, 2, 3, 4$, the total is $10$ and the curve passes through $(0, 0)$, $(1/4, 1/10)$, $(1/2, 3/10)$, $(3/4, 6/10)$ and $(1, 1)$.
Each point lies below the diagonal, and the slopes $2/5, 4/5, 6/5, 8/5$ increase, so the polygon is convex.

Non-example: $p \mapsto \sqrt{p}$ maps $[0, 1]$ onto $[0, 1]$, but it is not a Lorenz curve, since it is concave and lies above the diagonal.

The Lorenz curve does not change when all incomes are multiplied by the same positive number.
