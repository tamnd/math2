## Definition

Consider a spacecraft of negligible mass moving under the gravity of the Sun and of planets, a case of the [[n-body problem|62SNWC5E45]].
Let a planet of mass $m$ move at distance $a$ from the Sun of mass $M$.
Its sphere of influence is the ball about the planet of radius
$$r_{\mathrm{SOI}} = a \left( \frac{m}{M} \right)^{2/5}.$$
The *patched conic approximation* replaces the trajectory by a sequence of arcs such that:

1. inside the sphere of influence of a planet, the arc is a [[Kepler orbit|BXFSBB8Q46]] about that planet alone;
2. outside all spheres of influence, the arc is a Kepler orbit about the Sun alone;
3. at the boundary of a sphere of influence, consecutive arcs share the position; their velocities differ by the planet's velocity relative to the Sun.

## Remarks

Since the spheres are small compared with the planetary orbits, one often treats them as points in the solar arc and as infinitely large in the planetary arcs.
Example: for the Earth, $m/M \approx 3.0 \times 10^{-6}$ and $a \approx 1.496 \times 10^8$ km, so $r_{\mathrm{SOI}} \approx 9.2 \times 10^5$ km.
Non-example: the motion of a spacecraft near a Lagrange point of the Earth and the Sun is not a patched conic.
There both attractions are of the same size, and neither can be neglected.
The approximation is refined afterwards by numerical integration of the full equations.
