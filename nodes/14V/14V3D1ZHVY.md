## Summary

The compressibility factor $Z = pV_m/(RT)$ of a gas measures how far it departs from the [[ideal gas law|VXVGVDSJ0W]].
It equals $1$ for an [[ideal gas|N7R0H2VNDJ]] and differs from $1$ for a real gas.
It is the standard way to record and compare the [[equation of state|YPT3SJSS5T]] of real gases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For an ideal gas, the product of pressure and molar volume divided by $RT$ is always exactly one.
Real molecules attract each other and take up room, so for a real gas this ratio drifts away from one.
A value below one means the attractions win and the gas is easier to squeeze than an ideal gas.
A value above one means the size of the molecules wins and the gas is harder to squeeze.
At low pressure every gas has $Z$ close to $1$, because the molecules are far apart.
