## Example

We use $K = \mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge q)$ and the ranking $11 < 10 \approx 01 < 00$ of [[the revision on two atoms|CC604TAP5T]].
For $p$ we compute the contraction $K \div p$ by the Harper identity, and we recover the revision $K * \neg p$ by the Levi identity of [[AGM belief revision|Y05YQN7HGQ]].

1. $\mathrm{Mod}(\neg p) = \set{01, 00}$, and its minimal model is $01$. So $K * \neg p = \mathrm{Cn}(\neg p \wedge q)$.
2. Harper identity: $K \div p = K \cap (K * \neg p)$. The models of an intersection of belief sets are the union of their models.
3. So $\mathrm{Mod}(K \div p) = \set{11} \cup \set{01} = \set{11, 01}$, and $K \div p = \mathrm{Cn}(q)$.
4. Success: $p \notin \mathrm{Cn}(q)$, since $01$ satisfies $q$ and not $p$.
5. Inclusion: $\mathrm{Cn}(q) \subseteq \mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge q) = K$.
6. Recovery: $(K \div p) + p = \mathrm{Cn}(q \wedge p) = K$. So $K \subseteq (K \div p) + p$.
7. Levi identity: $(K \div \neg\neg p) + \neg p = (K \div p) + \neg p = \mathrm{Cn}(q \wedge \neg p)$, using extensionality for $\neg\neg p$.
8. This equals $K * \neg p$ from step 1, so the two identities fit together.

## Remarks

The contraction keeps $q$ because the most plausible world without $p$ still has $q$.
With the ranking $11 < 01 < 10 \approx 00$, the result is the same, since only the minimal $\neg p$-worlds matter.
With the ranking $11 < 10 \approx 00 < 01$, the minimal $\neg p$-world is $00$, and $K \div p = \mathrm{Cn}(p \leftrightarrow q)$: the agent keeps only the link between $p$ and $q$.
The recovery postulate holds in both cases: adding $p$ back restores $K$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To stop believing "Tweety is a bird", we look at the most believable situation where Tweety is not a bird.
There, Tweety still flies (say, Tweety is a plane).
So after giving up "bird", we still believe "flies".
If we later learn "bird" again, we get back exactly our old beliefs.

### L2 human

1. To contract by $\varphi$, add the minimal $\neg\varphi$-models to the models of $K$.
2. To revise by $\varphi$, contract by $\neg\varphi$ and then add $\varphi$.
3. Check the postulates on the sets of models.

The typical use of the identities is to define one operation from the other when one of them is easier to specify.
A trap: $K \div p$ is not $K \cap \mathrm{Cn}(\neg p)$ in general; that is full meet contraction, which here gives $\mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge q) \cap \mathrm{Cn}(\neg p) = \mathrm{Cn}(p \to q)$, losing $q$.
