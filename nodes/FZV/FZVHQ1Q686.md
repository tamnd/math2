## Summary

Stackelberg competition is a model of an industry in which a leader firm chooses its quantity first.
A follower firm observes it and then chooses its own quantity.
Its solution is the [[subgame perfect equilibrium|HE8K9M4EXN]] of this two-stage game, found by backward induction.
Heinrich von Stackelberg introduced it in 1934.
Moving first is an advantage: the leader commits to a large quantity, and the follower adapts by producing less than in [[Cournot competition|S5HGX1PNZR]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A large firm announces how much it will produce before a smaller rival decides.
The rival then simply picks its best amount given the announcement.
The leader knows this and picks its amount with the rival's reaction in mind.
For example, with linear demand and equal costs, the leader produces twice as much as the follower and earns more than it would if both chose at the same time.
