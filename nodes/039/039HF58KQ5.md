## Definition

Let $K$ be a commutative ring, let $n \ge 1$, and let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $K$.
The *characteristic polynomial* of $A$ is the polynomial
$$p_A(t) = \det(t I_n - A) \in K[t],$$
where $\det$ is the [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]] of a matrix with entries in the [[polynomial ring|0Q9EZS90GY]] $K[t]$.

## Notation

The matrix $t I_n - A$ has the entry $t - a_{ii}$ at $(i, i)$ and the entry $-a_{ij}$ at $(i, j)$ for $i \ne j$.
It is a matrix over the commutative ring $K[t]$, so its determinant is defined by the Leibniz formula over $K[t]$.
Mathlib writes `Matrix.charpoly A`, with the same sign convention.
Some authors write $\det(A - t I_n)$, which equals $(-1)^n p_A(t)$.

## Remarks

The polynomial $p_A$ is monic of degree $n$.
For $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$, $p_A(t) = t^2 - (a + d) t + (ad - bc)$.
Over a field, the roots of $p_A$ are the [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]] of $A$.
For $n = 0$, the determinant of the empty matrix is $1$, so $p_A = 1$; Mathlib allows this case.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The number $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue exactly when $A - \lambda I$ is singular, that is, when $\det(\lambda I - A) = 0$.
If we replace $\lambda$ by a variable $t$, then we get a polynomial.
Its roots are the eigenvalues.

### L2 human

We compute $p_A$ for $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. $tI - A$ is lower triangular with diagonal entries $t - 2$, $t - 3$ and $t - 6$.
2. The determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, also over $K[t]$.
3. So $p_A(t) = (t - 2)(t - 3)(t - 6) = t^3 - 11t^2 + 36t - 36$.
4. Check: the coefficient $-11$ is $-\operatorname{tr} A$, and the constant $-36$ is $(-1)^3 \det A = -(2 \cdot 3 \cdot 6)$.

The typical use is to compute $p_A$ and then find its roots.
A trap: $tI - A$ subtracts $A$ from $t$ only on the diagonal; the off-diagonal entries become $-a_{ij}$.
