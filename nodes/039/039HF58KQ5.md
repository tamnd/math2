## Definition

The characteristic polynomial of a square matrix is the determinant of $tI - A$, read as a polynomial in the variable $t$.
Let $K$ be a commutative ring, let $n \ge 1$, and let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $K$.
The *characteristic polynomial* of $A$ is
$$p_A(t) = \det(t I_n - A) \in K[t],$$
where $\det$ is the [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]] of the matrix $t I_n - A$ over the [[polynomial ring|0Q9EZS90GY]] $K[t]$.

## Notation

The matrix $t I_n - A$ has the entry $t - a_{ii}$ in place $(i, i)$ and the entry $-a_{ij}$ in place $(i, j)$ for $i \ne j$.
Mathlib writes `Matrix.charpoly A`, with the same sign.
Some books write $\det(A - t I_n)$, which equals $(-1)^n p_A(t)$, so the two agree only for even $n$.

## Remarks

Each condition has a job.
The ring $K$ must be commutative, because the determinant is defined by the Leibniz formula, and that formula needs commutative entries.
The variable $t$ commutes with every element of $K$, so $K[t]$ is again commutative and $\det(tI_n - A)$ makes sense.
The matrix must be square, since $t I_n - A$ needs $A$ to have the same size as $I_n$.

For $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ the definition gives
$$p_A(t) = (t - a)(t - d) - bc = t^2 - (a + d)\, t + (ad - bc).$$
A $2 \times 3$ matrix has no characteristic polynomial, because $t I - A$ is not defined for it.
For $n = 0$ the empty matrix has determinant $1$, so $p_A = 1$, and Mathlib allows this case.
Over a field, the roots of $p_A$ are the [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]] of $A$.
The [[concept page|0VSSPAFM2C]] gives examples, the sign convention and the first properties.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A number $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ when $\lambda I - A$ has no inverse, that is, when $\det(\lambda I - A) = 0$.
If we write a letter $t$ in place of $\lambda$ and keep it as an unknown, the determinant is a polynomial in $t$.
That polynomial is the characteristic polynomial, and its roots are the eigenvalues.

### L2 human

We compute $p_A$ for
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}.$$

1. The matrix $tI - A$ is lower triangular, with diagonal entries $t - 2$, $t - 3$ and $t - 6$.
2. The determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, and this holds over $K[t]$ too.
3. So $p_A(t) = (t - 2)(t - 3)(t - 6) = t^3 - 11t^2 + 36t - 36$.
4. Check: the coefficient $-11$ is $-\operatorname{tr} A$, and the constant $-36$ is $(-1)^3 \det A = -(2 \cdot 3 \cdot 6)$.

Watch out: $tI - A$ changes only the diagonal by $t$, and the entries off the diagonal become $-a_{ij}$, not $t - a_{ij}$.
