## Summary

An inelastic collision is a collision in which some [[kinetic energy|C9YCMAPKMT]] is lost, turned into heat, sound or deformation.
The total [[momentum|37Q4BTJMXW]] is still conserved, because the contact forces act in equal and opposite pairs.
In a perfectly inelastic collision the bodies stick together, and the loss of kinetic energy is as large as momentum conservation allows.
It is the opposite case to an [[elastic collision|NSF6MKF91K]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a lump of clay hits another lump and they stick, the pair moves on more slowly than the first lump did.
Momentum is shared, but some energy of motion has gone into squashing the clay.
A $1$ kg lump at $2$ m/s hitting a resting $1$ kg lump gives a $2$ kg pair at $1$ m/s.
The kinetic energy drops from $2$ joules to $1$ joule.
