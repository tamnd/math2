## Definition

A matrix times a column vector is the column of the sums of row entries times vector entries.
Let $R$ be a ring, let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $m \times n$ [[matrix|0N1MY1T0W9]] over $R$, and let $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)^{\mathsf{T}} \in R^n$ be a column vector.
The *matrix-vector product* $Ax$ is the column vector in $R^m$ with entries
$$(Ax)_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} x_j, \qquad 1 \le i \le m.$$
The product is defined only when the number of columns of $A$ equals the number of entries of $x$.

## Notation

The product is written $Ax$ with no sign between the factors, and the vector stands on the right.
In Mathlib the product is `Matrix.mulVec A x`, also written `A *ᵥ x`, for `x : n → α`.
It needs `Fintype n`, so that the sum over the column index is a finite sum.

## Remarks

Each part of the definition has a job.
The sum runs over $j$, the column index of $A$ and the index of $x$, so these two ranges must agree; the [[2 × 2 matrix times a vector with 3 entries|VZN1T7QCWE]] shows the failure.
The row index $i$ is free, so the result has one entry for each row of $A$.
The factors are written in the order $a_{ij} x_j$, which matters only over a noncommutative ring.

For a small case, the definition gives
$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 1 \\ 3 \cdot 1 + 4 \cdot 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Grouping the same sum by columns gives a second form.
If $a_1, \dots, a_n$ are the columns of $A$, then $Ax = a_1 x_1 + \dots + a_n x_n$, a [[linear combination|0V8F3E48E6]] of the columns.
In the example, $1 \cdot (1, 3)^{\mathsf{T}} + 1 \cdot (2, 4)^{\mathsf{T}} = (3, 7)^{\mathsf{T}}$ again.

A [[system of linear equations|0GT4WWGWH2]] with coefficient matrix $A$ and right side $b$ is the equation $Ax = b$, and the map $x \mapsto Ax$ is linear; see [[multiplication by a matrix as a linear map|094V2VBJXJ]].
The [[concept page|0PDVQEP0ZC]] treats both views in more detail.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To multiply a matrix by a vector, take each row of the matrix in turn.
Multiply its entries by the entries of the vector, position by position, and add the results.
Each row gives one entry of the answer.

### L2 human

The product is the special case $p = 1$ of the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]] of an $m \times n$ and an $n \times p$ matrix.
For example, with $x = (2, -1)^{\mathsf{T}}$ the matrix above gives $(2 - 2, 6 - 4)^{\mathsf{T}} = (0, 2)^{\mathsf{T}}$.
The typical use is to write a system of equations as $Ax = b$.
The trap is to pair the rows of $A$ with a vector of the wrong length.
