## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring, let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $m \times n$ [[matrix|0N1MY1T0W9]] over $R$, and let $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)^{\mathsf{T}} \in R^n$ be a column vector.
The *matrix-vector product* $Ax$ is the column vector in $R^m$ with entries
$$(Ax)_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} x_j, \qquad 1 \le i \le m.$$
The product is defined only when the number of columns of $A$ equals the number of entries of $x$.

## Remarks

- If $a_1, \dots, a_n$ are the columns of $A$, then $Ax = a_1 x_1 + \dots + a_n x_n$, a [[linear combination|0V8F3E48E6]] of the columns.
- A [[system of linear equations|0GT4WWGWH2]] is the equation $Ax = b$.
- The map $x \mapsto Ax$ is linear; see [[multiplication by a matrix as a linear map|094V2VBJXJ]].
- For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix}$.
- Mathlib's `Matrix.mulVec` takes `x : n → α` and needs `Fintype n` for the sum.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To multiply a matrix by a vector, take each row of the matrix in turn.
Multiply its entries by the entries of the vector, position by position, and add the results.
Each row gives one entry of the answer.

### L2 human

The product is the special case $p = 1$ of the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]] of an $m \times n$ and an $n \times p$ matrix.
