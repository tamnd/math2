## Definition

Let $G$ be a [[locally compact group|AHV05ZBCYW]], not assumed abelian, with a left [[Haar measure|VY0HXD3GC4]].
*Noncommutative harmonic analysis* is the part of [[harmonic analysis on groups|5E6KB3V8R0]] that studies $G$ through its irreducible [[unitary representations|74BPYJNZTM]].
Its main questions are:

1. how the [[left regular representation|PR8C073VWY]] of $G$ on $L^2(G)$ decomposes as a direct sum or direct integral of irreducible unitary representations;
2. how a function $f$ on $G$ is recovered from the operators $\pi(f) = \int_G f(x) \pi(x) \, dx$, where $\pi$ runs over the irreducible unitary representations;
3. how the $L^2$ norm of $f$ is expressed through these operators, by a Plancherel formula.

## Remarks

Example: for a compact group $G$, the [[Peter–Weyl theorem|6VC9QE39XH]] answers question 1.
The regular representation is the Hilbert direct sum of the irreducible representations $\pi$, each with multiplicity $\dim \pi$.
For a finite group of order $n$ this gives the identity $n = \sum_\pi (\dim \pi)^2$.

Non-example: for $G$ abelian, every irreducible unitary representation is one-dimensional, given by a character.
The theory is then Fourier analysis on the dual group, which is the commutative case and not what the term refers to.

The term abstract harmonic analysis is often used for the abelian and the general case together.
