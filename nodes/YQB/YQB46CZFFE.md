## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a [[thermodynamic system|GYNNJBMTCP]] and let $P$ be a process of $\Sigma$ from a state $A$ to a state $B$.
$P$ is *reversible* if there is a process from $B$ back to $A$ that restores both $\Sigma$ and its surroundings to their initial states.
Equivalently, the direction of $P$ can be reversed at every moment by an infinitesimal change of the external conditions.

## Remarks

Every reversible process is [[quasistatic|PVDWD7HGFG]] and free of dissipation.
The slow, frictionless isothermal expansion of an ideal gas in contact with a reservoir at its own temperature is reversible.
Running it backwards compresses the gas, and the heat taken from the reservoir goes back to it.
Heat flow from a body at $350$ K to a body at $300$ K is not reversible.
No process restores both bodies without leaving a change elsewhere, by the second law.
In a reversible process the total entropy of the system and its surroundings stays constant.
