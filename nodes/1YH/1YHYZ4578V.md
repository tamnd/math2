## Summary

Fields and Galois theory is the study of fields, of their extensions and of the polynomials over them.
A field is a commutative ring in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse, such as the rational numbers, the real numbers or the integers modulo a prime.
Galois theory attaches a group of automorphisms to a field extension, so that questions about the roots of polynomials become questions about finite groups.
Galois used groups of permutations of the roots in about 1830 to decide which polynomial equations are solvable by radicals, and Dedekind, Artin and others later gave the theory its modern form in terms of fields.
This topic collects the fields and Galois theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Commutative algebra, algebraic number theory, group theory and model theory have their own topics.
