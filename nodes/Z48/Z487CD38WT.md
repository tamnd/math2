## Definition

Let $\Lambda$ be the set of terms of the [[lambda calculus|BD756KR8FM]], taken up to renaming of bound variables.
For terms $M, N$ and a variable $x$, let $M[x := N]$ denote the capture-avoiding substitution of $N$ for the free occurrences of $x$ in $M$.
A *$\beta$-redex* is a term of the form $(\lambda x.\, M)\, N$, and its *contractum* is $M[x := N]$.

*One-step $\beta$-reduction* $\to_\beta$ is the least binary relation on $\Lambda$ with these properties.

1. $(\lambda x.\, M)\, N \to_\beta M[x := N]$ for all $x$, $M$ and $N$.
2. If $M \to_\beta M'$, then $M\, N \to_\beta M'\, N$, $N\, M \to_\beta N\, M'$ and $\lambda x.\, M \to_\beta \lambda x.\, M'$.

*Multi-step $\beta$-reduction* $\twoheadrightarrow_\beta$ is the reflexive [[transitive closure|05J9NWX07X]] of $\to_\beta$.
*$\beta$-conversion* $=_\beta$ is the least equivalence relation that contains $\to_\beta$.

## Remarks

Example: $(\lambda x.\, x\, y)(\lambda z.\, z) \to_\beta (\lambda z.\, z)\, y \to_\beta y$.
Each step contracts a redex by clause 1, so $(\lambda x.\, x\, y)(\lambda z.\, z) \twoheadrightarrow_\beta y$.

Non-example: the term $x\, (\lambda y.\, y)$ has no reduction step.
It is an application, but its function part is the variable $x$ and not an abstraction, so it is not a redex, and its subterms contain no redex either.

Capture avoidance matters: $(\lambda x.\, \lambda y.\, x)\, y$ reduces to $\lambda z.\, y$, not to $\lambda y.\, y$.
