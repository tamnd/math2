## Summary

Geodynamics is the part of geophysics that studies how the solid Earth deforms and flows, and what forces and heat drive these motions.
It applies continuum mechanics, the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]] and fluid dynamics to the crust, the mantle and the core.
Its main subjects include [[plate tectonics|AYQQM6PBMR]], [[mantle convection|7QQVSQAY85]], [[flexure of the lithosphere|K7A4FT1T6T]] and [[postglacial rebound|A4YSEXMJ4P]].
It links observations at the surface, such as heat flow, gravity, topography and earthquakes, to processes deep inside the Earth.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Rock looks rigid, but over millions of years the hot mantle flows like a very thick liquid.
Geodynamics asks how this happens and what it does at the surface.
It explains why continents drift, why the ocean floor gets deeper as it gets older, and why land that carried an ice sheet is still rising today.
A typical model treats the mantle as a fluid slowly stirred by heat from below.
Its viscosity is about $10^{21}$ pascal seconds, roughly $10^{24}$ times that of water.
