## Example

We derive $\neg p \vee \neg \neg p$ in [[Gödel-Dummett logic|FA88H7FDPY]], and we show that $\mathsf{KC} \subsetneq \mathsf{LC}$.

1. The instance $(p \to \neg p) \vee (\neg p \to p)$ of prelinearity is in $\mathsf{LC}$.
2. $p \to \neg p \vdash \neg p$: from the hypothesis $p$ we get $\neg p$, and then $\bot$. The [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] gives $p \to \bot$.
3. $\neg p \to p \vdash \neg \neg p$: from the hypothesis $\neg p$ we get $p$, and with $\neg p$ again $\bot$. The deduction theorem gives $\neg p \to \bot$.
4. By steps 2 and 3, $\vdash (p \to \neg p) \to \neg p \vee \neg \neg p$ and $\vdash (\neg p \to p) \to \neg p \vee \neg \neg p$ in $\mathsf{IPC}$.
5. The disjunction axiom of [[IPC|52XCYMK0WW]] and step 1 give $\mathsf{LC} \vdash \neg p \vee \neg \neg p$.
6. $\mathsf{LC}$ is closed under substitution, so it contains [[the logic KC|H9Z8BWRN69]] $= \mathsf{IPC} + \neg p \vee \neg \neg p$.
7. For strictness, take the diamond: a root $r$, two incomparable worlds $a, b$ above $r$, and a top $t$ above both.
8. Put $V(p) = \set{a, t}$ and $V(q) = \set{b, t}$. Then $a \Vdash p$ and $a \nVdash q$, so $r \nVdash p \to q$. Symmetrically $r \nVdash q \to p$.
9. The diamond validates $\neg \varphi \vee \neg \neg \varphi$ for every $\varphi$. If $t \Vdash \varphi$, every world sees $t$, so every world forces $\neg \neg \varphi$.
10. If $t \nVdash \varphi$, persistence shows that no world forces $\varphi$, so every world forces $\neg \varphi$.
11. So the diamond is a frame of $\mathsf{KC}$ that refutes prelinearity, and $\mathsf{LC} \not\subseteq \mathsf{KC}$.

## Remarks

The derivation uses only one instance of prelinearity, with $q := \neg p$.
The diamond is not locally linear, which matches the frame correspondence in [[the completeness theorem|DGJ176J5CT]].
The [[hypersequent derivation of weak excluded middle|Q50NC2F1Y1]] gives a cut-free proof of the same formula.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If, of $p$ and "not $p$", one always implies the other, then we can decide which of "not $p$" and "not not $p$" holds.
The first case gives "not $p$", the second gives "not not $p$".
So the linear principle is at least as strong as the weak excluded middle.

### L2 human

1. Choose the instance of prelinearity with $q := \neg p$.
2. Treat each disjunct by a short natural deduction.
3. Combine the two cases by disjunction elimination.

The typical use is to compare $\mathsf{LC}$ and $\mathsf{KC}$ in the lattice of intermediate logics.
A trap: the other inclusion fails, so weak excluded middle does not give prelinearity.
