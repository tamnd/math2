## Summary

A loop and a two-cycle satisfy exactly the same modal formulas, although one point sees itself and the other does not.
That observation starts the model theory of modal logic, a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]].
Bisimulation explains it, and the van Benthem characterization theorem turns it into a description of modal logic as the bisimulation-invariant part of first-order logic.
On frames, modal formulas become second-order conditions, and correspondence theory asks when they are first-order, as $\square p \to p$ is reflexivity.
Sahlqvist's theorem answers for a large class of formulas, and the Goldblatt-Thomason theorem says which first-order frame classes are modally definable.
Some logics escape all frames: Kripke incompleteness needs general frames to repair it.
The second half extends the language with quantifiers, fixed points, programs, nominals, topology, two dimensions and impossible worlds.
Each extension keeps the idea of local evaluation at a point and changes what a point can see.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Bisimulation|X27BR4YP2Y]]: you learn the zig and zag conditions and why modal formulas cannot tell bisimilar worlds apart.
2. [[Hennessy-Milner theorem|DBDNYXA7ME]]: you learn that on image-finite models modal equivalence is bisimilarity, and how to extract a separating formula.
3. [[Van Benthem characterization theorem|Y89MGMWE4H]]: you learn that modal logic is the bisimulation-invariant fragment of first-order logic.
4. [[Modal correspondence theory|3RYBWYPJVD]]: you learn to compute frame conditions such as reflexivity and density from axioms.
5. [[Sahlqvist's theorem|S4SSDMETJM]]: you learn the minimal valuation method, and why Sahlqvist logics are canonical and complete.
6. [[Goldblatt-Thomason theorem|GX5E3GNRCS]]: you learn the four closure tests that decide when a first-order frame class is modally definable.
7. [[General and descriptive frames|6W9MEGN982]]: you learn frames with admissible sets, the duals of modal algebras.
8. [[Kripke incompleteness|W67YJQTT50]]: you see a consistent normal logic that no class of Kripke frames characterizes.
9. [[McKinsey formula|4N4ZNWZRE2]]: you see a short formula whose frame class is not first-order definable.
10. [[Lattice of normal modal logics|EZ011FT3GT]]: you learn the structure of $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{K})$, Makinson's theorem and splittings.
11. [[Quantified modal logic|D2H3S12V7X]]: you learn Kripke models with domains, de re and de dicto readings, and where completeness fails.
12. [[Barcan formula|9PYDNBA5X4]]: you learn how exchanging $\forall x$ and $\square$ corresponds to conditions on domains.
13. [[Modal mu-calculus|MZ0M2KR9S0]]: you learn least and greatest fixed points, and the evaluation game.
14. [[Propositional dynamic logic|VJ78A0Q22R]]: you learn a box for each regular program, Segerberg's axioms and the Fischer-Ladner closure.
15. [[Hybrid logic|NVPQ9W9DED]]: you learn nominals, satisfaction operators and the binder, and completeness for pure axioms.
16. [[Topological semantics of modal logic|HWJXMBNHQ0]]: you learn to read the box as interior, with $\mathsf{S4}$ as the logic of all spaces.
17. [[McKinsey-Tarski theorem|2VF0546VXK]]: you learn that $\mathsf{S4}$ is already the logic of the real line.
18. [[Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation|BXVNEN55MR]]: you learn the faithful embedding of intuitionistic logic into $\mathsf{S4}$.
19. [[Blok-Esakia theorem|BB9TA5Q9PN]]: you learn that superintuitionistic logics correspond one to one to normal extensions of $\mathsf{Grz}$.
20. [[Products of modal logics|JA2EBWVW8A]]: you learn two-dimensional frames, commutativity, and why three dimensions are undecidable.
21. [[Impossible world|8PAJMAN8DM]]: you learn non-normal worlds, Rantala models and the problem of logical omniscience.

### Prerequisites

1. [[Syntax, Kripke semantics and completeness of modal logic|SKY7AQFG1V]]: [[modal logic|C3RZY20ETK]], [[Kripke semantics|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]], [[canonical models|TVE4F04KME]], [[filtration|6ZZSHAKG5F]] and the [[standard translation|YW6YGBMVQY]] into first-order logic.
2. [[Structures, satisfaction and truth|NJDME6F845]]: first-order structures and the satisfaction relation.
3. [[Completeness, compactness and interpolation|FBWW4J1XWG]]: [[compactness|1NR3HAWG9D]], [[ultraproducts|AX9Q86GB8M]] and the [[Löwenheim-Skolem theorem|484M0TZ1S7]].
4. [[Algebraic logic|QYA4H5Y7VR]] and [[intuitionistic and intermediate logics|38EQ20GR7M]]: Boolean, Heyting and interior algebras, used in items 7, 18 and 19 of the reading order.
