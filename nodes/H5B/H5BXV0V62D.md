## Summary

The invariant mass of a particle or of a system of particles is the Minkowski length of its total [[four-momentum|67N6B8MJ3X]], divided by $c$.
It has the same value in every inertial frame.
For a single particle it is the rest mass.
For a system it is the total energy in the frame where the total momentum is zero, divided by $c^2$.
In particle physics it is used to identify a decayed particle from the energies and momenta of its decay products.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Different observers measure different energies and momenta for the same particle, but one combination of them is the same for everybody.
This combination is the invariant mass.
For a system it is not just the sum of the masses of the parts: their kinetic energy and their binding energy count too.
For example, two photons, each of which has zero mass, flying apart in opposite directions with energy $E$ each, form a system of invariant mass $2E/c^2$.
