## Summary

Topological order is a kind of order in gapped quantum phases at zero temperature that no local order parameter detects.
One sign is a ground-state degeneracy that depends on the topology of the surface carrying the system.
Another is that no local measurement can tell the ground states apart.
It arises in fractional quantum Hall states and in models such as the toric code.
Its excitations can be anyons, and its protected degeneracy is a resource for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary order, as in a magnet, can be seen by looking at one small region: the spins there point the same way.
Topological order is hidden: every small region looks the same in all the ground states.
The information sits in global features, such as what happens when you go once around a hole of the surface.
On a torus, the toric code has $4$ ground states, and on a sphere it has $1$; the number depends only on the shape of the surface.
Because no local disturbance can see the difference, local noise cannot destroy the information stored in this choice.
