## Definition

Let $g = -dt^2 + a(t)^2 \gamma_k$ be a [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric|ECJMS4VDTF]] on $(t_b, t_\infty) \times \Sigma$ with $t_b > -\infty$, filled with matter of energy density $\rho(t)$, where $a$ is the [[scale factor|J70F32P86W]].
The model has a *Big Bang* at $t_b$ when
$$\lim_{t \to t_b^+} a(t) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{t \to t_b^+} \rho(t) = \infty.$$

## Remarks

If the Einstein equations hold, then at a Big Bang the curvature invariant $R_{ab}R^{ab}$ blows up together with the density.
So the metric has no $C^2$ extension through $t = t_b$, and $t_b$ is an initial [[singularity|45AE2YXWT3]].
Example: the Einstein–de Sitter model, $a(t) = t^{2/3}$ and $\rho = 1/(6\pi G t^2)$ on $(0, \infty)$, has a Big Bang at $t_b = 0$.
Non-example: the de Sitter model $a(t) = e^{Ht}$ is defined for all $t \in \R$ and $a$ never tends to $0$ at a finite time, so it has no Big Bang.
A Big Bang is forced under mild conditions: if $\rho + 3p > 0$, $\Lambda \le 0$ and $H(t_0) > 0$, then the second [[Friedmann equation|PPHFS2P4G4]] gives $\ddot a < 0$.
So $a$ is concave and lies below its tangent line at $t_0$, which reaches zero at $t_0 - 1/H(t_0)$; hence the solution breaks down at some time $t_b \ge t_0 - 1/H(t_0)$.
