## Summary

In Dirac's theory of constrained Hamiltonian systems, a constraint is first class when its [[Poisson bracket|2S0MXDT4YH]] with every constraint vanishes on the constraint surface.
Otherwise it is second class.
First class constraints generate gauge transformations: motions along them change the description of the state but not its physical content.
Second class constraints instead remove pairs of variables, and they are handled with the [[Dirac bracket|QYP4KYN248]].
This distinction is central in the Hamiltonian treatment of gauge theories and general relativity.
See the [[definition|F3D3X49ZBW]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some mechanical systems, often written with more variables than needed, come with equations that the positions and momenta must satisfy at all times.
These constraint equations come in two kinds.
A first class constraint, such as "the momentum $p_2$ is zero" when nothing depends on $q_2$, leaves the variable $q_2$ completely free and meaningless.
A second class pair, such as $q_2 = 0$ and $p_2 = 0$ together, simply removes the pair $(q_2, p_2)$ from the system.
Telling the two kinds apart tells one how many true degrees of freedom the system has.
