## Summary

A two-way finite automaton is a finite automaton whose reading head can move both left and right on the input word.
The input lies between two end markers.
Despite this freedom, two-way deterministic automata accept exactly the [[regular languages|18WVBDCS38]], a result of Rabin and Scott and of Shepherdson.
They can be much smaller than equivalent one-way [[deterministic finite automata|W65B6BKZRY]].
The question of how many states are needed to remove nondeterminism from two-way automata is a well-known open problem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An ordinary finite automaton reads its input once, from left to right, and never looks back.
A two-way automaton may walk back and forth over the input as often as it likes, but it still has only finitely many states and cannot write.
For example, to test whether the $n$-th letter from the end is $a$, it can walk to the right end marker.
Then it steps back $n$ letters, using about $n$ states.
A one-way DFA for the same language needs $2^n$ states.
