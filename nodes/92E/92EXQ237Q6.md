## Statement

Let $F$ be a field of [[characteristic|1ZB13N78XW]] $0$, and let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a finite-dimensional [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]] over $F$.
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-[[module|TZAZ163QGK]].
Then every submodule $W$ of $V$ has a complement: a submodule $W'$ with $V = W \oplus W'$.
Equivalently, $V$ is a direct sum of [[irreducible|HFW8642GBK]] submodules.

## Remarks

One proof first treats a submodule of codimension one, using the [[Casimir element|K6VVP66GKB]] of a faithful quotient of $\mathfrak{g}$, which acts on a nontrivial irreducible module by a nonzero scalar.
The general case then follows by applying this to a module of linear maps $\operatorname{Hom}(V, W)$.
Another proof uses [[Whitehead's first lemma|NE2K5HAEFN]], $H^1(\mathfrak{g}, M) = 0$ for finite-dimensional $M$, with $M = \operatorname{Hom}(V, W)$.
Example: the adjoint module of $\mathfrak{g}$ splits as the direct sum of the simple ideals of $\mathfrak{g}$.
Semisimplicity is needed: the one-dimensional Lie algebra $F$ acting on $F^2$ by $t \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} 0 & t \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ preserves the line $F e_1$, and no other line, so this line has no complement.
Finite dimension is needed: the [[Verma module|809XR5G0DG]] $M(0)$ of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\C)$ has a proper nonzero submodule with no complement.
Characteristic $0$ is needed: over a field of characteristic $p > 0$, $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ has finite-dimensional modules that are not direct sums of irreducible ones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem says that every finite-dimensional representation of a semisimple Lie algebra breaks up into irreducible pieces.
So to know all finite-dimensional representations, it is enough to know the irreducible ones.
It is the analogue for Lie algebras of Maschke's theorem for finite groups.

### L2 human

Together with the [[theorem of the highest weight|J1EFY61K87]], it reduces the finite-dimensional representation theory of a complex semisimple Lie algebra to the study of dominant integral weights.
Weyl first proved it with the unitary trick, by integration over a compact Lie group with the same complexified Lie algebra; algebraic proofs came later.
It is also used to show that every representation of a semisimple Lie algebra preserves the abstract Jordan decomposition of its elements.
The cohomological proof generalises: Whitehead's second lemma, $H^2(\mathfrak{g}, M) = 0$, gives the [[Levi decomposition|XMP8X1YVJ3]].
