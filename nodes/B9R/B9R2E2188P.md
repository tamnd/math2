## Definition

Let $n \ge 1$, $\hbar > 0$, and let $H$ be a quantum Hamiltonian on $L^2(\R^n)$ with [[Weyl symbol|RFNGJ09WDR]] $h(x, p)$ on the [[phase space|CPEC2NW2KF]] $\R^{2n}$.
The *phase space formulation* of the quantum system is the following description of it by functions on $\R^{2n}$:

1. a state, given by a [[density operator|V1JTB52E8C]] $\rho$, is represented by its [[Wigner function|563Y1P5HR8]] $W_\rho(x, p)$;
2. an observable $A$ is represented by its Weyl symbol $a$, so that $A = \mathrm{Op}^w(a)$;
3. the expectation value of $A$ in the state $\rho$ is $\langle A \rangle_\rho = \int_{\R^{2n}} a\, W_\rho\, dx\, dp$;
4. the product of observables $\mathrm{Op}^w(a)\mathrm{Op}^w(b)$ is represented by the [[Moyal product|J3A8CFHZZM]] $a \star b$;
5. the time evolution of the state is given by the Moyal equation
$$\frac{\partial W}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{i\hbar}\left(h \star W - W \star h\right).$$

## Remarks

Clause 5 is the translation of the von Neumann equation $i\hbar\, \partial_t \rho = [H, \rho]$, because $W_\rho$ is $(2\pi\hbar)^{-n}$ times the Weyl symbol of $\rho$.
Since $h \star W - W \star h = i\hbar\{h, W\} + O(\hbar^3)$, the equation reduces to the classical Liouville equation $\partial_t W = \{h, W\}$ up to terms of order $\hbar^2$.

Example: for the harmonic oscillator $h = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \frac{m\omega^2 x^2}{2}$, all terms of order $\hbar^2$ and higher vanish, because $h$ is quadratic.
So $\partial_t W = \{h, W\}$ exactly, and $W$ is transported along the classical elliptical orbits.

Non-example: a general probability density $f(x, p)$ on $\R^{2n}$ need not be the Wigner function of any state.
For instance, a Gaussian $f = \frac{1}{\pi\varepsilon} e^{-(x^2 + p^2)/\varepsilon}$ with $n = 1$ and $\varepsilon < \hbar$ violates the bound $\abs{W} \le 1/(\pi\hbar)$ at the origin.
