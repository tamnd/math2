## Summary

The row space of an $m \times n$ [[matrix|0VD9ZSWWJG]] $A$ over a field $F$ is the [[span|0DTY8B9HMS]] of its rows, a [[subspace|00YC1D652K]] of $F^n$.
Row operations do not change it, and the nonzero rows of any echelon form of $A$ are a basis of it.
It is the column space of the [[transpose|09DXWYRD43]] $A^\top$.

## Motivation

Each row of $A$ is one equation of the system $Ax = b$.
Combinations of the rows are the equations that follow from the system.
So the row space describes all the linear consequences of the equations, and row reduction is a search for a simple basis of it.

## Notation

The row space is written $\operatorname{Row}(A)$, $\operatorname{Row} A$ or $C(A^\top)$.
Strang writes $C(A^\top)$, which treats the rows of $A$ as the columns of $A^\top$.
Some books regard the rows as row vectors in $F^{1 \times n}$ rather than as vectors of $F^n$.

## Remarks

### Examples

- [[A $3 \times 4$ matrix|~ca0156db]]: the nonzero rows $(1, 2, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 0, 1, 2)$ of the reduced form are a basis.
- [[A matrix with a non-reduced echelon form|~b0a6d10e]]: the rows $(1, 1, 1)$ and $(0, 2, 4)$ of an echelon form are a basis, and the row space is the plane $x - 2y + z = 0$.
- The row space of an invertible $n \times n$ matrix is $F^n$.
- The row space of $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ is the line in $F^3$ spanned by $(1, 2, 3)$.

### Non-examples

- [[The column space of a square matrix|~c303e3ec]]: for $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, the column space is the line through $(1, 0)$, and the row space is the line through $(1, 1)$.
- The span of the rows after a column operation is in general a different subspace, as in the same example.

### Basic properties

- Row equivalent matrices have the same row space, by the [[theorem on row equivalence|E4GKK727ZM]].
- The nonzero rows of an echelon form of $A$ form a basis, by the [[theorem on the row space|~b802c546]]. So $\dim \operatorname{Row}(A)$ is the number of pivots.
- $\dim \operatorname{Row}(A) = \dim \operatorname{Col}(A) = \rank A$, by the theorem that [[row rank equals column rank|0TZAG60ZYT]].
- $\operatorname{Row}(BA) \subseteq \operatorname{Row}(A)$ for each $k \times m$ matrix $B$, since each row of $BA$ is a combination of the rows of $A$.
- Over $\R$, $\operatorname{Row}(A)$ is the orthogonal complement of the null space of $A$, by the [[theorem on the four fundamental subspaces|~c3d8720b]].

### History

The method of elimination for linear systems appears in the Chinese text The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art.
The row space is the space of all equations that elimination can produce from the given rows.

### Common errors

- Taking the first $r$ rows of $A$ as a basis, where $r$ is the rank. For the rows $(1, 1)$, $(2, 2)$, $(0, 1)$, the rank is $2$, but the first two rows are dependent.
- Confusing the row space, in $F^n$, with the column space, in $F^m$.
- Applying column operations and expecting the row space to stay the same.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The row space collects all the combinations of the rows of a matrix.
Row operations only mix the rows, so they keep the row space.
After row reduction, the nonzero rows give a basis of it.

### L2 human

The row space and the column space are computed in opposite ways.
For the row space, take the nonzero rows of the reduced matrix.
For the column space, take the pivot columns of the original matrix.
Both give the same number of vectors, the rank.
Over $\R$, the row space and the null space are orthogonal complements in $\R^n$.

### L3 human

The row space of $A$ is the image of the transpose map $F^m \to F^n$, $y \mapsto A^\top y$.
In the language of duality, it is the image of the dual map of $x \mapsto Ax$, identified with a subspace of $F^n$ through the standard basis.
The annihilator of $\operatorname{Row}(A)$ is the null space of $A$, which gives a proof of row rank equals column rank by [[duality|0ZPB3180AY]].
