## Example

Let $L$ be the empty signature, so the only atomic formulas are equations $u = v$ between variables.
For $n \ge 2$ let $\sigma_n$ be the sentence $\exists x_1 \cdots \exists x_n \bigwedge_{i < j} x_i \ne x_j$.
Let $T_\infty$ be the [[theory|92SWXST1ZM]] $\set{\sigma_n : n \ge 2}$, whose models are the infinite sets.
We show that $T_\infty$ has [[quantifier elimination|RE5TFTDP4D]].

1. Reduction. By the [[one-quantifier test|Q1CGE4DWXQ]], it suffices to treat $\exists y \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$, where $\varphi$ is a conjunction of atomic and negated atomic formulas.
2. Literals without $y$ do not depend on $y$, so we move them outside the quantifier. We delete the atoms $y = y$.
3. If $\neg(y = y)$ occurs, the formula is equivalent to $\bot$.
4. If $y = x_i$ occurs, then $\exists y \, \varphi$ is equivalent to $\varphi(\bar x, x_i)$, which is quantifier-free.
5. In the remaining case the formula is $\exists y \, \bigwedge_{j \in J} y \ne x_j$ for a finite set $J$.
6. In an infinite set, the elements $x_j$ with $j \in J$ take at most $\abs{J}$ values. So some element $y$ differs from all of them.
7. So the formula of step 5 is equivalent to $\top$ modulo $T_\infty$.

So every formula is equivalent modulo $T_\infty$ to a quantifier-free formula.

## Remarks

The example shows the simplest case of quantifier elimination: the unknown $y$ must only avoid finitely many values.
A consequence is that a subset of an infinite set $M$ defined with parameters is finite or cofinite.
So $T_\infty$ is [[strongly minimal|PNGY60EBDJ]].
The quantifier-free sentences are $\top$ and $\bot$, so $T_\infty$ is complete.

When the hypothesis "infinite" changes, the result changes in this way.

- For the theory of sets with exactly $n$ elements, step 6 changes. The formula $\exists y \, \bigwedge_{j \in J} y \ne x_j$ says that the $x_j$ take fewer than $n$ values, and this is a quantifier-free disjunction of equations. So this theory also has quantifier elimination.
- The theory of all nonempty sets does not have quantifier elimination. The formula $\exists y \, (y \ne x)$ is false in a one-element set and true in a two-element set. Every quantifier-free formula in $x$ is equivalent to $x = x$ or to its negation in all sets.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take an endless bag of different marbles.
The only question that one can ask about marbles is whether two of them are the same.
"Is there a marble different from marble $1$ and marble $2$?" always has the answer yes, since the bag never runs out.
So every question with "there is" can be answered without searching the bag.

### L2 human

We eliminate the quantifier in $\exists y \, (y \ne x_1 \wedge y = x_2 \wedge x_1 \ne x_3)$.

1. The literal $x_1 \ne x_3$ does not contain $y$, so it moves outside.
2. The equation $y = x_2$ forces the witness. We substitute $x_2$ for $y$.
3. The result is $x_2 \ne x_1 \wedge x_1 \ne x_3$, which is quantifier-free.

The typical use is as a base case: similar steps work for infinite vector spaces over a fixed field, where $y$ must avoid finitely many points.
A trap is to forget that the theory must fix the size of the models: without the axioms $\sigma_n$ the elimination fails.
