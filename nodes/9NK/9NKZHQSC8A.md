## Summary

The Hermite–Gaussian modes are a family of beam solutions of the [[paraxial wave equation|53DCTAEM5M]] in Cartesian coordinates.
Each one is a [[Gaussian beam|Y0QE726BE3]] multiplied by [[Hermite polynomials|MGACY98E9H]] in the two transverse coordinates, scaled by the beam width.
They keep their shape as they propagate, apart from a change of scale, and they form a complete orthogonal family of transverse profiles.
They are the transverse modes of laser resonators with rectangular symmetry, labelled $\mathrm{TEM}_{mn}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A laser does not always emit a single round spot.
Sometimes the spot breaks into a grid of bright lobes separated by dark lines, for example two lobes side by side.
These patterns are the Hermite–Gaussian modes: the numbers $m$ and $n$ count the dark lines across and down.
Each pattern keeps its look as the beam travels; it only grows in size.
Any beam profile can be written as a sum of these patterns, like a sound as a sum of tones.
