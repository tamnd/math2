## Definition

Fix a stellar mass $M$ and a chemical composition.
Consider the pre-main-sequence evolutionary path of a star of mass $M$ in the plane of $\log T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $\log L$.
The *Henyey track* of mass $M$ is the part of this path during which:

1. the star is in [[stellar structure|SBKFDNEMFF]] equilibrium with a radiative core;
2. the star contracts and is powered by the [[Kelvin–Helmholtz mechanism|DHMR7NGZYM]], before core hydrogen burning supplies its luminosity.

Here $L$ is the luminosity and $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the [[effective temperature|WETKRE94E0]].

## Remarks

The track starts where the star leaves its [[Hayashi track|GRQ41F2G55]] and ends at the zero-age main sequence.
For a radiative star of constant opacity, scaling arguments give a luminosity nearly independent of the radius, which is why $L$ changes little while $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ rises.
Example: a star of one solar mass leaves its Hayashi track when a radiative core appears.
It then moves to the left along its Henyey track until it reaches the main sequence.
Non-example: a star of $0.2$ solar masses stays fully convective until it reaches the main sequence, so its path is a Hayashi track and has no Henyey part.
