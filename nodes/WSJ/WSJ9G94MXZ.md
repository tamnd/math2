## Summary

The Eddington luminosity of a body of mass $M$ is the luminosity at which radiation and gravity balance.
At this luminosity the outward push of the radiation on the surrounding gas equals the inward pull of gravity.
It equals $4 \pi G M c / \kappa$, where $\kappa$ is the opacity of the gas.
A star or an accreting object that shines more strongly than this limit drives away its outer layers or the matter falling onto it.
It bounds the brightness of the most massive stars and the growth rate of black holes by accretion.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light carries momentum, so a very bright object pushes on the gas around it.
Gravity pulls the same gas inward.
Both forces fall off like the inverse square of the distance, so their ratio does not depend on distance, only on brightness and mass.
At the Eddington luminosity the two forces are equal.
For the Sun it is about thirty thousand times the actual brightness of the Sun.
