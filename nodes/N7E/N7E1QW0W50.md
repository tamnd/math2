## Summary

Recreational mathematics is mathematics done for pleasure.
Its objects are puzzles, games, curiosities and tricks, and each page states a puzzle, gives its solution and proves why the solution works.
Many puzzles lead to real mathematics: the knight's tour leads to Hamiltonian paths, and the Rubik's Cube leads to permutation groups.
The tradition goes back at least to the puzzle collection attributed to Alcuin of York, and Sam Loyd, Henry Dudeney and Martin Gardner made puzzles popular in the 19th and 20th centuries.
This topic collects the recreational mathematics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Game theory, problem solving and competitions, and mathematics and culture have their own topics.
