## Definition

*Finite-time thermodynamics* is the study of thermodynamic processes and [[cycles|AGRGCDWQF8]] under these conditions.

1. Each process, or each cycle, has a finite duration.
2. Transport laws give the rates of heat and mass transfer in terms of differences of intensive variables, such as $q = \kappa (T_1 - T_2)$ for heat.
3. A performance measure, such as power, efficiency at given power or entropy production, is optimised over the admissible processes.

A model in this theory is *endoreversible* if the system is [[reversible|F3DHZ45H6A]] inside and all [[irreversible|D36GZB2766]] effects occur in its coupling to the surroundings.

## Remarks

Example: a [[heat engine|S7XFW04T3V]] that takes heat from a reservoir at $T_h$ through a conductance $\kappa_h$ and runs a Carnot cycle inside between $T_1 < T_h$ and $T_2$ is an endoreversible model.
Its power is a function of $T_1$ and $T_2$, and optimising it is a problem of finite-time thermodynamics.
Non-example: the study of a Carnot cycle in the quasistatic limit, with zero temperature gaps, fails clause 1, because a quasistatic process takes infinite time.
The terms *endoreversible thermodynamics* and *finite-time thermodynamics* are often used for the same body of work.
