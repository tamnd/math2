## Summary

Fermi acceleration is the gain of energy of charged particles that are reflected again and again by moving magnetic structures in a plasma.
In the second-order form the reflectors are randomly moving magnetized clouds.
The mean gain per collision is of order $(V/c)^2$, where $V$ is the cloud speed and $c$ the speed of light.
In the first-order form, diffusive shock acceleration, particles cross a [[shock wave|JSDMGGTG7G]] back and forth.
The mean gain per crossing cycle is of order $\Delta u / c$, where $\Delta u$ is the jump of flow speed at the shock.
Both forms produce power-law energy spectra, which is why they are the standard explanation of cosmic rays.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A tennis ball that bounces off a racket moving toward it comes back faster.
A charged particle in space can bounce off moving magnetic fields in the same way.
Head-on bounces are slightly more frequent than overtaking ones, so on average the particle speeds up a little at each bounce.
After many bounces a few lucky particles reach enormous energies, and the number of particles above an energy $E$ falls like a power of $E$.
At a shock wave every round trip across the shock is a head-on bounce, which makes the process much faster.
