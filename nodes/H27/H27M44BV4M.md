## Summary

Fluid mechanics is the study of liquids, gases and plasmas and of the forces that act on them.
It models a fluid as a continuum, described by fields such as density, velocity and pressure.
It splits into fluid statics, for fluids at rest, and [[fluid dynamics|F2JRWJNYN4]], for fluids in motion.
Its equations, such as the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]], are a major source of partial differential equations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Water in a pipe, air around a wing and blood in a vein are all fluids.
A fluid is a material that keeps flowing as long as a shearing force acts on it, unlike a solid that only bends.
Fluid mechanics ignores single molecules and treats the fluid as a smooth substance with a density and a velocity at each point.
A simple example is a glass of still water: the pressure grows linearly with depth, by about one atmosphere for every ten metres.
