## Summary

This area studies methods that approximate the solutions of ordinary, partial, differential-algebraic, delay and stochastic differential equations, with the theory of their convergence, stability and errors.
A numerical method converges when the difference between the computed and the exact solution goes to zero as the step size or the mesh size goes to zero.
These methods make it possible to simulate weather, flows, structures, circuits and chemical reactions, and they are among the main uses of computers in science and engineering.
Euler described his method for the initial value problem in 1768, and Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy found their stability condition for difference schemes in 1928.
This topic collects the numerical methods for differential equations pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Numerical analysis, ordinary differential equations and partial differential equations have their own topics.
