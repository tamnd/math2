## Summary

A force-free magnetic field is a [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] whose current density is everywhere parallel to the field.
Then the magnetic part of the [[Lorentz force|S3RZRCH0T5]] on the current, $J \times B$, vanishes.
Such fields model the solar corona and other plasmas where the magnetic pressure dominates the gas pressure.
They also appear as states of least magnetic energy at fixed [[magnetic helicity|5V4YEMZ0EA]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A current in a magnetic field usually feels a sideways push.
If the current flows exactly along the field lines, the push is zero.
A force-free field is one in which this happens everywhere, so the field exerts no force on the plasma that carries it.
Such fields have field lines that twist like the strands of a rope, as one sees in the loops above the surface of the Sun.
