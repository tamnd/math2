## Statement

Let $a$, $b$, $c$ and $d$ be real numbers with $(a, b, c) \neq (0, 0, 0)$.
Let $P = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : a x + b y + c z = d\}$ be a [[plane|07SDJ5DNE4]], and let $X = (x_0, y_0, z_0) \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
The distance from $X$ to $P$ is the least [[Euclidean distance|0NPPGHQNXQ]] $d(X, Q)$ for $Q \in P$.
This least value exists, and
$$\operatorname{dist}(X, P) = \frac{|a x_0 + b y_0 + c z_0 - d|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}.$$

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The condition $(a, b, c) \neq (0, 0, 0)$ makes $P$ a plane with the [[normal vector|0WQJJCCTC8]] $n = (a, b, c)$.
Without it, the denominator is $0$, and $P$ is empty or all of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

### Consequences

- The least distance is reached at exactly one point of $P$, the foot $X - \dfrac{a x_0 + b y_0 + c z_0 - d}{a^2 + b^2 + c^2} \, n$.
- The number $(a x_0 + b y_0 + c z_0 - d) / \|n\|$ is the signed distance. Its sign tells on which side of $P$ the point lies.
- The parallel planes $n \cdot r = d_1$ and $n \cdot r = d_2$ have the distance $|d_1 - d_2| / \|n\|$.

### Converse

The distance is $0$ if and only if $X \in P$, since the numerator is $0$ exactly then.

### Generalizations

For a hyperplane $\{x : a \cdot x = d\}$ with $a \neq 0$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, the distance from $x_0$ is $|a \cdot x_0 - d| / \|a\|$, with the same proof.
In the plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ this is the [[distance from a point to a line|01AAYNSBN6]].
The [[distances concept|C6GAS92QHX]] collects the related formulas for lines.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The nearest point of a plane lies straight below or above the given point, along the normal vector.
For the floor plane $z = 0$, the formula gives $|z_0|$, the height of the point above the floor.
Tilted planes need the factor $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$, because $(a, b, c)$ need not be a unit vector.

### L2 human

Worked example: the point $(1, 2, 3)$ and the plane $6x + 3y + 2z = 6$.

1. The numerator is $|6 + 6 + 6 - 6| = 12$.
2. The denominator is $\sqrt{36 + 9 + 4} = 7$.
3. The distance is $\tfrac{12}{7}$.

The [[worked example|QJJHWYGDQN]] also finds the foot.
A trap: the equation must be in the form $ax + by + cz = d$; for $ax + by + cz + e = 0$ the numerator is $|a x_0 + b y_0 + c z_0 + e|$.
