## Statement

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]] with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ and positive roots $\Phi^+$.
Its [[Weyl group|YT1ZJ2S4EE]] $W$ is a [[Coxeter group|G6FND7X69J]] generated by the simple reflections.
Let $\rho$ be half the sum of the positive roots.
Let $\ell$ be the length function of $W$, and let $\le$ be the [[Bruhat order|A17Q3X6285]] on $W$.
For $y, w \in W$ let $P_{y,w}$ be the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of $W$, defined from the [[Hecke algebra|5J2P2AGY4N]] of $W$.
For $w \in W$ let $M_w$ be the [[Verma module|809XR5G0DG]] of highest weight $-w\rho - \rho$, and let $L_w$ be its irreducible quotient.
Then for every $w \in W$, the characters satisfy
$$\operatorname{ch} L_w = \sum_{y \in W,\ y \le w} (-1)^{\ell(w) - \ell(y)} P_{y,w}(1) \operatorname{ch} M_y.$$

## Remarks

Kazhdan and Lusztig stated the conjecture in 1979.
It was proved in 1981, independently by Beilinson and Bernstein and by Brylinski and Kashiwara.
Both proofs pass to $\mathcal{D}$-modules on the flag variety and use the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence.
There, $P_{y,w}$ is a generating function for the local intersection cohomology of Schubert varieties, a result of Kazhdan and Lusztig.
An equivalent form gives the multiplicities in [[category O|NM3KHQNYDB]]: $[M_w : L_y] = P_{w_0 w, w_0 y}(1)$, where $w_0$ is the longest element of $W$.
Example: for $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\C)$, $W = \set{1, s}$ and $P_{1,s} = P_{1,1} = P_{s,s} = 1$.
Here $M_1 = M(-2)$ is irreducible and $M_s = M(0)$ contains $M(-2)$, so $\operatorname{ch} L_s = \operatorname{ch} M_s - \operatorname{ch} M_1$, as the formula says.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Verma modules are well understood, while their irreducible quotients are much harder.
The conjecture expresses the characters of these irreducible modules through the Verma characters.
The coefficients come from polynomials defined purely in terms of the Weyl group as a group of symmetries.
It was a striking link between representation theory, combinatorics and the geometry of Schubert varieties.

### L2 human

For $w = w_0$ the highest weight $-w_0\rho - \rho = 0$ is dominant, all $P_{y,w_0}$ equal $1$, and the formula gives the trivial character as in the Weyl character formula.
The other $L_w$ are infinite-dimensional, and for them no earlier formula was known in general.
Through translation functors, it gives the characters of all simple modules of integral weight in category $\mathcal{O}$.
Its proof was a founding success of geometric representation theory.
