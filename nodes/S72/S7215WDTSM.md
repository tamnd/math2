## Summary

An electroactive polymer is a polymer material that changes its size or shape when it is stimulated by an electric field or an electric current.
Such materials can strain much more than [[piezoelectric|XYSSPAC1XG]] ceramics, so they are studied as artificial muscles, soft actuators and sensors.
Their mechanics couples large elastic deformation with electrostatics, which makes them a model problem of electromechanical coupling in [[solid mechanics|JRD6BDKT1X]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a thin rubber sheet coated on both faces with a flexible conducting paint.
When a voltage is put across the two faces, the opposite charges attract each other and squeeze the sheet.
The sheet gets thinner and, since rubber keeps its volume, it spreads out sideways.
Turning the voltage off lets it spring back.
This device is called a dielectric elastomer actuator, and it is the simplest kind of electroactive polymer.
