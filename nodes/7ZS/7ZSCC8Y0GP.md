## Definition

Fix an observer on the Earth.
The *apparent solar time* is the [[hour angle|20B8CQHH1S]] of the true Sun plus $12^{\mathrm h}$.
The *mean Sun* is a fictitious point that moves along the celestial equator at a constant rate.
Its period equals the mean period of the true Sun.
The *mean solar time* is the hour angle of the mean Sun plus $12^{\mathrm h}$.
The *equation of time* is the apparent solar time minus the mean solar time, as a function of the date.

## Remarks

Equivalently, it is the hour angle of the true Sun minus the hour angle of the mean Sun.
Some texts use the opposite sign.
Two effects add up.
The Earth's orbit is a [[Kepler orbit|BXFSBB8Q46]] of small eccentricity, so the true Sun moves along the [[ecliptic|3ND041H1AA]] faster near perihelion in early January.
The ecliptic is tilted against the equator, so even uniform motion along the ecliptic gives non-uniform motion in right ascension.
Example: in early November the equation of time is about $+16$ minutes, so a sundial is ahead of mean time.
Non-example: the difference between local mean time and the time of a time zone is not part of the equation of time; it depends only on longitude.
