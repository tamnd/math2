## Definition

Let $X$ be a quasi-compact quasi-separated [[scheme|5A35TYZD02]], and let $\ell \ge 2$ be an integer invertible on $X$, that is, invertible in $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$.
Let $X_{\mathrm{et}}$ be the small étale site of $X$, whose objects are the [[étale|H84VHC8PX2]] morphisms $U \to X$ with $U$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated, with the étale [[topology|GDRFSJ8E2B]].
Consider the presheaf of [[spectra|1GHF4EKBXS]] $U \mapsto K(U)/\ell$ on $X_{\mathrm{et}}$, where $K(U)/\ell$ is the [[mod $\ell$ K-theory|ANJ5719F7F]] spectrum of the [[Thomason–Trobaugh K-theory|ECFW7KP0SX]] of $U$.
The *étale K-theory* spectrum of $X$ with coefficients in $\Z/\ell$ is its étale hypercohomology spectrum:
$$K^{\mathrm{et}}(X; \Z/\ell) = \mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{et}}(X; K/\ell).$$
This is the value on $X$ of the étale hypersheafification of the presheaf.
Its [[homotopy groups|P976WYEFMB]] are the étale K-groups $K^{\mathrm{et}}_n(X; \Z/\ell)$.

## Remarks

There is a natural comparison map $K(X)/\ell \to K^{\mathrm{et}}(X; \Z/\ell)$.
Assume that the étale cohomological dimension of $X$ for $\ell$-torsion sheaves is finite.
Then a descent spectral sequence computes $K^{\mathrm{et}}_*(X; \Z/\ell)$ from the [[étale cohomology|7YX76G0838]] of $X$ with coefficients in the sheaves associated to $U \mapsto K_q(U; \Z/\ell)$.

Example: let $X = \operatorname{Spec} k$ with $k$ an algebraically closed field in which $\ell$ is invertible.
Every étale $k$-algebra is a finite product of copies of $k$, and K-theory turns finite products into products, so the presheaf already satisfies étale descent.
Hence $K^{\mathrm{et}}(k; \Z/\ell) = K(k)/\ell$, and $K^{\mathrm{et}}_n(k; \Z/\ell) \cong \Z/\ell$ for even $n \ge 0$ and $0$ otherwise, by Suslin's computation.

Non-example: the construction is not used when $\ell$ is divisible by the characteristic of a residue field of $X$.
For $\ell = p$ in characteristic $p$, the étale cohomology of $\Z/p$ sheaves behaves differently.
The comparison with K-theory then takes another form.

The original constructions, by Friedlander and by Dwyer and Friedlander, used the étale homotopy type of $X$ instead of hypercohomology.
