## Summary

*Intuitionistic propositional logic*, $\mathsf{IPC}$, is the set of formulas in $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\to$ and $\bot$ that are derivable in [[Heyting's Hilbert system|52XCYMK0WW]].
This system has the axioms of positive logic, the axiom $\bot \to \varphi$, and [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] as its only rule.
The negation $\neg \varphi$ abbreviates $\varphi \to \bot$.
Classical [[propositional calculus|09K9MWD2QQ]] is $\mathsf{IPC}$ plus the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]] $p \vee \neg p$.
Intuitionistic predicate logic $\mathsf{IQC}$ adds quantifiers in the same way; see [[intuitionistic predicate logic|~d18966da]].
Intuitionistic logic is the logic of constructive mathematics, and it is the base of a large family of logics between it and classical logic.

## Motivation

Brouwer rejected the law of excluded middle for statements about infinite domains.
On his reading, a statement is true when there is a proof of it, and a proof of $A \vee B$ must give a proof of one of the disjuncts.
For an open problem $A$, there is no proof of $A$ and no proof of $\neg A$, so $A \vee \neg A$ has no proof.
Heyting asked which laws of logic remain valid under this reading, and he gave a formal system for them.
The meaning of the connectives in terms of proofs is the [[Brouwer-Heyting-Kolmogorov interpretation|7B53ZGXXEQ]], a page of constructive mathematics.
Here intuitionistic logic is treated as a formal logic: its proof systems, its semantics and its metatheory.

## Notation

$\mathsf{IPC}$ (also $\mathsf{Int}$ or $\mathsf{IL}$) is the propositional logic, and $\mathsf{IQC}$ is the predicate logic.
$\vdash_{\mathsf{i}} \varphi$ or $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash \varphi$ means that $\varphi$ is derivable, and $\vdash_{\mathsf{c}}$ is classical derivability.
$\mathsf{CPC}$ is classical propositional logic.
$\neg \varphi := \varphi \to \bot$, $\top := \bot \to \bot$ and $\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi := (\varphi \to \psi) \wedge (\psi \to \varphi)$.
Gentzen's names for the intuitionistic [[natural deduction|6KT3BJZ7D1]] and [[sequent calculus|W72CA3R9MP]] are $\mathrm{NJ}$ and $\mathrm{LJ}$.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash p \to \neg\neg p$ and $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash \neg\neg\neg p \to \neg p$; see [[intuitionistic derivations with negation|~fc52f9a2]].
2. $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash \neg\neg(p \vee \neg p)$, although $p \vee \neg p$ is not derivable; see [[the double negation of excluded middle|~9ac335b9]].
3. $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash \neg(p \vee q) \leftrightarrow (\neg p \wedge \neg q)$ and $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash (\neg p \vee \neg q) \to \neg(p \wedge q)$. These are the [[De Morgan laws|09PKJKA6J0]] that remain valid.
4. $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash (p \to q) \to (\neg q \to \neg p)$, contraposition in one direction.

### Non-examples

1. The [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]] $p \vee \neg p$ is not derivable. A two-point [[Kripke model|~8db2fc65]] refutes it; see [[the two-point countermodel|~bc4c4586]].
2. [[Double negation elimination|0NFAGWH84K]] $\neg\neg p \to p$ and [[Peirce's law|GMSZ75D5WE]] $((p \to q) \to p) \to p$ are not derivable, by the same model.
3. The De Morgan law $\neg(p \wedge q) \to \neg p \vee \neg q$ and the [[weak excluded middle|CX032ACTMZ]] $\neg p \vee \neg\neg p$ are not derivable.
4. A classical proof of $p \vee \neg p$ uses reductio ad absurdum, which is not an intuitionistic rule; see [[a classical derivation that is not intuitionistic|~d1037047]].

### Basic properties

1. $\mathsf{IPC} \subseteq \mathsf{CPC}$, and the inclusion is proper.
2. Soundness and completeness hold for [[Kripke semantics|~ac029000]], for [[Heyting algebras|~32a20f35]], for [[topological spaces|~088e3e8d]] and for [[Beth models|~957fdb6a]].
3. $\mathsf{IPC}$ has the [[disjunction property|~59f11dc8]]: if $\vdash \varphi \vee \psi$, then $\vdash \varphi$ or $\vdash \psi$.
4. By [[Glivenko's theorem|~f74f3067]], $\mathsf{CPC} \vdash \varphi$ if and only if $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash \neg\neg\varphi$.
5. $\mathsf{IPC}$ has no finite characteristic matrix, by [[Gödel's theorem|~98b455d2]].
6. $\mathsf{IPC}$ is decidable. It has the finite model property for Kripke frames, and $\mathrm{LJ}$ has [[cut elimination|AYRZ98Y0NW]].
7. $\mathsf{IPC}$ has the Craig interpolation property, and it is one of the seven intermediate logics with it, by [[Maksimova's theorem|~a89c6574]].
8. The [[Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation|BXVNEN55MR]] embeds $\mathsf{IPC}$ into the modal logic $\mathsf{S4}$.

### History

Heyting published the formal rules of intuitionistic logic in 1930.
Glivenko had proved his theorem on double negation in 1929, for the propositional fragment.
Gentzen introduced the calculi $\mathrm{NJ}$ and $\mathrm{LJ}$ in 1934 and 1935, and proved cut elimination for $\mathrm{LJ}$.
Kripke introduced his semantics for intuitionistic logic in 1965.

### Common errors

1. Reading "not derivable" as "false". Intuitionistic logic does not assert $\neg(p \vee \neg p)$; it proves $\neg\neg(p \vee \neg p)$.
2. Assuming that intuitionistic logic is a three-valued logic. By Gödel's theorem, no finite truth table characterizes $\mathsf{IPC}$.
3. Assuming that every classical law with $\neg$ fails. The laws $p \to \neg\neg p$ and $\neg\neg\neg p \leftrightarrow \neg p$ hold.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Intuitionistic logic keeps the ordinary rules of reasoning, with one change.
It reads "true" as "proved", and to prove "$A$ or $B$" one must prove $A$ or prove $B$.
Take the statement "there are infinitely many twin primes", which nobody has proved or refuted.
Intuitionistic logic does not assert "this statement is true or false", because that would need a proof of one of the two.
But it still proves that "the statement is true or false" can not be refuted.
Everything that intuitionistic logic proves is also a law of ordinary logic.

### L2 human

We derive $\neg(p \vee q) \to \neg p$ in [[natural deduction|6KT3BJZ7D1]] without excluded middle.

1. Assume $\neg(p \vee q)$, that is, $(p \vee q) \to \bot$.
2. Assume $p$, to prove $\neg p$.
3. From $p$, introduce the disjunction $p \vee q$.
4. Apply step 1 to step 3, and get $\bot$.
5. Discharge the assumption $p$, and get $p \to \bot$, that is, $\neg p$.
6. Discharge step 1, and get $\neg(p \vee q) \to \neg p$.

The typical use of intuitionistic logic is to check that a proof gives a construction: the derivation above uses no rule that loses information.
A trap: the converse direction $\neg(p \wedge q) \to \neg p \vee \neg q$ of a De Morgan law is not derivable, although the classical proof looks short.

### L3 human

Intuitionistic logic is the logic of [[Heyting algebras|NG943XNQWH]], just as classical logic is the logic of Boolean algebras.
The open sets of a topological space, the up-sets of a partial order and the subobjects in a topos form Heyting algebras, so the logic appears in topology, in order theory and in category theory.
Through the [[Curry-Howard correspondence|A77FER1Q5H]], natural deduction proofs in the implication fragment are terms of the simply typed lambda calculus.

The logics between $\mathsf{IPC}$ and $\mathsf{CPC}$ form the [[intermediate logics|~57af80cc]], a lattice of the size of the continuum.
Later sub-areas treat other weakenings of classical logic: many-valued, substructural and paraconsistent logics.
The constructive reading by proofs, realizability and Heyting arithmetic belong to the pages on [[constructive logic|MEEG709X2W]].
