## Example

We know $\varphi$ to degree at least $0.7$ and $\varphi \to \psi$ to degree at least $0.8$, and we ask how well we know $\psi$.
In [[rational Pavelka logic|YG62F8YC0P]] the two premises form this theory:
$$T = \{\overline{0.7} \to \varphi, \ \overline{0.8} \to (\varphi \to \psi)\}.$$
We show that $T \vdash \overline{0.5} \to \psi$, and that $0.5$ is the best bound.

The bound comes from the Łukasiewicz [[t-norm|BHGCVPAJW0]]:
$$0.7 \odot 0.8 = \max(0, 0.7 + 0.8 - 1) = 0.5.$$

The bound is sound in [[Łukasiewicz semantics|JQ713VVD0G]].
Let $e$ be a model of $T$, with $x = e(\varphi)$ and $y = e(\psi)$.
Then $x \ge 0.7$ and $x \Rightarrow y = \min(1, 1 - x + y) \ge 0.8$.
The t-norm $\odot$ is monotone, so $x \odot (x \Rightarrow y) \ge 0.7 \odot 0.8 = 0.5$.
By residuation $x \odot (x \Rightarrow y) \le y$, and so $y \ge 0.5$.

The derivation in RPL follows the same four steps.

1. From the two premises, $(\overline{0.7} \odot \overline{0.8}) \to (\varphi \odot (\varphi \to \psi))$ is derivable, since $\odot$ is monotone in Łukasiewicz logic.
2. The formula $(\varphi \odot (\varphi \to \psi)) \to \psi$ is a theorem of Łukasiewicz logic.
3. The bookkeeping axiom gives $\overline{0.5} \to (\overline{0.7} \odot \overline{0.8})$.
4. By transitivity of $\to$ and [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]], $T \vdash \overline{0.5} \to \psi$.

The bound is optimal.
Put $e(\varphi) = 0.7$ and $e(\psi) = 0.5$.
Then $e(\varphi \to \psi) = \min(1, 1 - 0.7 + 0.5) = 0.8$, so $e$ is a model of $T$ with $e(\psi) = 0.5$.
No bound above $0.5$ is sound, and the truth degree of $\psi$ in $T$ is exactly $0.5$.

## Remarks

The general rule reads: from $\overline{r} \to \varphi$ and $\overline{s} \to (\varphi \to \psi)$, derive $\overline{r \odot s} \to \psi$.
The same model, with $e(\varphi) = r$ and $e(\psi) = r \odot s$, shows that the bound is always optimal when $r + s \ge 1$.
Check with $r = 0.9$ and $s = 0.6$: the bound is $0.5$, and $\min(1, 1 - 0.9 + 0.5) = 0.6$.

Pavelka's evaluated syntax attaches a degree to each formula.
In RPL the graded formula "$\varphi$ to degree $r$" is the ordinary formula $\overline{r} \to \varphi$, so graded modus ponens is a derived rule, not a new one.

The rule that takes the minimum of the two degrees would claim $0.7$ here, and the model above refutes it; see [[the minimum rule|KYG8T7KQQ9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

We know $\varphi$ to degree $0.7$ and "$\varphi$ implies $\psi$" to degree $0.8$.
Then we know $\psi$ to degree $0.5$, and no more.
The first premise is $0.3$ short of true, the second is $0.2$ short, and the losses add up to $0.5$.

### L2 human

To find the bound, combine the two degrees with the Łukasiewicz t-norm, $\max(0, r + s - 1)$.
To check that the bound is best, set $\varphi$ to $r$ and $\psi$ to $r \odot s$, and compute the value of $\varphi \to \psi$.
Watch out: if $r + s \le 1$, the rule gives the degree $0$, which carries no information.
