## Summary

The Cramér–Lundberg model is the classical model of [[ruin theory|YSXZZ1HQR5]].
Claims arrive at the jump times of a Poisson process, their sizes are independent and identically distributed, and premiums come in at a constant rate.
It goes back to Filip Lundberg in 1903 and was developed by Harald Cramér in the 1930s.
It is the setting of the [[Lundberg inequality|TSN5GZV241]] and of many explicit formulas for the ruin probability.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture the bank balance of an insurer as a line that rises steadily as premiums come in.
At random moments, on average $\lambda$ times per year, a claim arrives and the line drops by a random amount.
The claim amounts are drawn independently from one fixed distribution.
For example, with $\lambda = 10$ claims per year of mean $1000$, the insurer must charge more than $10\,000$ per year, or ruin is certain in the long run.
