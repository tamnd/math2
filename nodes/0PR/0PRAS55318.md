## Definition

Let $n$ be a positive integer, and let $u$ and $v$ be nonzero [[vectors|0QN3D80AWP]] in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
Let $u \cdot v = u_1 v_1 + \dots + u_n v_n$ be their [[dot product|0S52TNNTH9]], and let $\|u\|$ and $\|v\|$ be their [[lengths|0XKBKGTCPC]].
The angle between $u$ and $v$ is the unique real number $\theta$ with $0 \leq \theta \leq \pi$ such that
$$\cos \theta = \frac{u \cdot v}{\|u\| \, \|v\|}.$$
The [[Cauchy-Schwarz inequality|0RFWW56Y22]] $|u \cdot v| \leq \|u\| \, \|v\|$ makes sure that the quotient is in $[-1, 1]$.
The cosine is a bijection from $[0, \pi]$ onto $[-1, 1]$, so $\theta = \arccos\left(\frac{u \cdot v}{\|u\| \, \|v\|}\right)$ exists and is unique.

## Notation

The angle is written $\theta$ or $\angle(u, v)$.
In Mathlib the angle is `InnerProductGeometry.angle u v`, defined by the same formula in any real inner product space.

## Remarks

In the plane and in space, this angle is the geometric angle between the arrows, by the [[geometric formula for the dot product|070TXS9AXM]].
The angle is $0$ if and only if $v = c u$ for a real number $c > 0$, and it is $\pi$ if and only if $v = c u$ for a real number $c < 0$.
The angle does not depend on the order of the vectors, and it does not change when $u$ or $v$ is multiplied by a positive number.
Mathlib uses the formula for all vectors, with the convention $0 / 0 = 0$, so the angle between the zero vector and any vector is $\pi / 2$ there.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put the two arrows at the same start point.
The angle between them is the opening between the arrows, measured in radians from $0$ to $\pi$.
For example, $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ have dot product $0$, so the angle between them is $\arccos 0 = \pi / 2$, a right angle.

### L2 human

For $u = (1, 0, 1, 0)$ and $v = (1, 1, 1, 1)$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$:

1. $u \cdot v = 2$, $\|u\| = \sqrt{2}$ and $\|v\| = 2$.
2. $\cos \theta = \frac{2}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, so $\theta = \frac{\pi}{4}$.

A trap: the definition needs the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality first, or the arccosine may be undefined.
