## Definition

The angle between two nonzero vectors is the number in $[0, \pi]$ whose cosine is their dot product divided by the product of their lengths.
Let $n$ be a positive integer, and let $u$ and $v$ be nonzero [[vectors|0QN3D80AWP]] in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
Let $u \cdot v = u_1 v_1 + \dots + u_n v_n$ be their [[dot product|0S52TNNTH9]], and let $\|u\|$ and $\|v\|$ be their [[lengths|0XKBKGTCPC]].
The angle between $u$ and $v$ is the unique real number $\theta$ with $0 \le \theta \le \pi$ such that
$$\cos \theta = \frac{u \cdot v}{\|u\| \, \|v\|}.$$
The [[Cauchy-Schwarz inequality|0RFWW56Y22]] $|u \cdot v| \le \|u\| \, \|v\|$ puts the quotient in $[-1, 1]$.
The cosine is a bijection from $[0, \pi]$ onto $[-1, 1]$, so $\theta = \arccos\left(\frac{u \cdot v}{\|u\| \, \|v\|}\right)$ exists and is unique.

## Notation

The angle is written $\theta$ or $\angle(u, v)$.
In Mathlib the angle is `InnerProductGeometry.angle u v`, defined by the same formula in any real inner product space.

## Remarks

Each part of the definition has a job.
The vectors must be nonzero because their lengths stand in the denominator.
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is needed because the arccosine is defined only on $[-1, 1]$; without it the formula could ask for the angle whose cosine is $1.2$.
The range $[0, \pi]$ makes the answer unique, since on that interval each value of the cosine occurs exactly once.

For $u = (1, 0, 1, 0)$ and $v = (1, 1, 1, 1)$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$ we get $u \cdot v = 2$, $\|u\| = \sqrt{2}$ and $\|v\| = 2$.
So $\cos \theta = 2 / (2\sqrt{2}) = 1 / \sqrt{2}$, and $\theta = \tfrac{\pi}{4}$.
For $u = (1, 0)$ and $v = (0, 0)$ the definition does not apply, because the denominator is $0$.
Mathlib uses the formula for all vectors with the convention $0 / 0 = 0$, so there the angle with the zero vector is $\arccos 0 = \pi / 2$.

In the plane and in space this angle is the geometric angle between the arrows, by the [[geometric formula for the dot product|070TXS9AXM]], and the [[concept page|0ZEHZY85E6]] develops its properties.
The angle does not depend on the order of the vectors, and it does not change when $u$ or $v$ is multiplied by a positive number.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put the two arrows at the same start point.
The angle between them is the opening between the arrows, measured in radians from $0$ to $\pi$.
For example, $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ have dot product $0$, so the angle between them is $\arccos 0 = \pi / 2$, a right angle.

### L2 human

To find an angle, compute three numbers and one arccosine.
For $u = (1, 2, 2)$ and $v = (2, 0, 0)$:
$$u \cdot v = 2, \qquad \|u\| = 3, \qquad \|v\| = 2, \qquad \cos \theta = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3},$$
so $\theta = \arccos \tfrac{1}{3} \approx 70.5^\circ$.
Watch out: in $\mathbb{R}^n$ the definition needs the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality first, or the arccosine may be undefined.
