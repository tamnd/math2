## Definition

A *flow network* is a tuple $N = (V, A, c, s, t)$, where $(V, A)$ is a finite [[directed graph|0KFYGSHHF5]], $c \colon A \to [0, \infty)$ is the capacity function, and $s, t \in V$ are distinct vertices.
The vertex $s$ is the source and $t$ is the sink.
For $v \in V$, let $\delta^{\mathrm{in}}(v)$ and $\delta^{\mathrm{out}}(v)$ be the sets of edges entering and leaving $v$.
An *$s$–$t$ flow* in $N$ is a function $f \colon A \to \R$ such that:

1. $0 \le f(a) \le c(a)$ for every edge $a \in A$ (capacity constraint).
2. $\sum_{a \in \delta^{\mathrm{in}}(v)} f(a) = \sum_{a \in \delta^{\mathrm{out}}(v)} f(a)$ for every vertex $v \ne s, t$ (conservation).

The *value* of $f$ is $\abs{f} = \sum_{a \in \delta^{\mathrm{out}}(s)} f(a) - \sum_{a \in \delta^{\mathrm{in}}(s)} f(a)$.

## Remarks

Example: let $V = \set{s, a, t}$ with edges $s a$, $a t$, $s t$ of capacities $2$, $3$, $1$.
The function with $f(sa) = 2$, $f(at) = 2$, $f(st) = 1$ satisfies both clauses, and its value is $3$.
At $a$ the inflow $2$ equals the outflow $2$.

Non-example: in the same network, $f(sa) = 2$, $f(at) = 3$, $f(st) = 0$ respects the capacities but fails conservation at $a$, where $2 \ne 3$.

Summing conservation over all vertices shows that the value also equals the net flow into $t$.
Some texts use instead a skew-symmetric function on all ordered pairs, with $f(u, v) = -f(v, u)$.
That form records only the net flow between two vertices, and it gives the same maximum flow values.
