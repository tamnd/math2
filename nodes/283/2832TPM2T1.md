## Example

A *planar four-bar linkage* consists of four rigid bars joined in a closed loop by four revolute joints in the plane.
One bar, the ground link, is fixed, and the bar opposite to it is the coupler.
The two others are the crank and the rocker, each pivoted on the ground.

Mobility check: there are $N = 4$ links and $j = 4$ joints, each with $f_i = 1$.
The [[Chebychev–Grübler–Kutzbach criterion|H3E119CTY3]] gives $M = 3(4 - 1 - 4) + 4 = 1$.
Indeed, for generic bar lengths, the angle of the crank fixes the other positions up to at most two choices.
These are the two ways of closing a triangle.

Grashof condition: let $s$ and $l$ be the shortest and the longest bar lengths, and $p$, $q$ the other two.
If $s + l < p + q$, then the shortest bar can make full turns relative to its two neighbours.
If $s + l > p + q$, no bar makes a full turn relative to another.

## Remarks

Points of the coupler trace coupler curves, which are algebraic curves of degree at most $6$.
Watt's linkage, a four-bar linkage, traces an approximate straight line; an exact straight line needs more bars, as in the [[Peaucellier–Lipkin linkage|PP2CAZ7QKE]].
The [[instant centre|WQT8X3V2JQ]] of the coupler relative to the ground is the intersection of the crank line and the rocker line.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Four sticks hinged at their ends into a loop, with one stick nailed down, form the simplest moving mechanism.
Turning one stick moves all the others in a definite way, so the mechanism has one degree of freedom.
Such linkages are found in windscreen wipers, pumps and car suspensions.
