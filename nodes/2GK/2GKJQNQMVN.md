## Summary

A gauge theory is a field theory whose action does not change under local symmetry transformations, chosen independently at each point of spacetime.
In geometric terms, its basic field is a [[connection|NJ21PN5ZGJ]] on a [[principal bundle|8E37E74XVP]], and its symmetry group is the [[gauge group|707DEWCCFX]] of that bundle.
Electromagnetism is the gauge theory of the circle group $U(1)$, and the Standard Model of particle physics is a gauge theory with group $SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)$.
In mathematics, gauge theory gave the [[Yang–Mills equations|JJWETW5AT8]], [[instantons|YWBTGC7MFA]] and new invariants of four-manifolds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A symmetry of a physical law is a change that leaves the law the same, such as rotating the whole laboratory.
A gauge symmetry is much stronger: one may make a different change at every point of space and time.
To compare fields at nearby points after such changes, one needs an extra field, the gauge field.
It plays the role of a rule for moving information from point to point.
For electromagnetism the change is a rotation of the phase of a complex wave function, and the gauge field is the electromagnetic potential.
Light is the wave of this gauge field.
