## Definition

Let $g$ be the acceleration of gravity and $U > 0$ the wind speed at a height of $19.5$ m above the sea surface.
Set $\omega_0 = g / U$.
The *Pierson–Moskowitz spectrum* is the [[ocean wave spectrum|GYQCDP4VN8]]
$$S(\omega) = \frac{\alpha g^2}{\omega^5} \exp\!\Big( -\beta \Big( \frac{\omega_0}{\omega} \Big)^4 \Big), \qquad \omega > 0,$$
with the dimensionless constants $\alpha = 8.1 \times 10^{-3}$ and $\beta = 0.74$.

## Remarks

Example: the peak of $S$ is where $\frac{d}{d\omega} \ln S = -5/\omega + 4 \beta \omega_0^4 / \omega^5 = 0$, that is at $\omega_p = (4\beta/5)^{1/4} \omega_0 \approx 0.877 \, g / U$.
The substitution $s = \beta \omega_0^4 \omega^{-4}$ gives the variance
$$m_0 = \int_0^\infty S(\omega) \, d\omega = \frac{\alpha g^2}{4 \beta \omega_0^4} = \frac{\alpha U^4}{4 \beta g^2},$$
so the significant wave height is $H_s = 4 \sqrt{m_0} = 2 \sqrt{\alpha / \beta} \, U^2 / g \approx 0.21 \, U^2 / g$.

Non-example: a pure swell from a distant storm has a peak much narrower than the Pierson–Moskowitz shape.
It is not tied to the local wind speed, and it is not of this form for any $U$.

The tail $\alpha g^2 \omega^{-5}$ is Phillips' saturation range.
The JONSWAP spectrum multiplies a spectrum of this form by a peak enhancement factor, to describe seas that are still growing.
