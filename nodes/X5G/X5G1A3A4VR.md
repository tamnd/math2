## Summary

The reduced mass of two bodies with masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ is $\mu = m_1 m_2 / (m_1 + m_2)$.
It is the mass to use when two interacting bodies are replaced by one body, their relative position, moving around a fixed centre.
It turns the two-body problem into a one-body problem in a central field.
It appears in the same way in classical and in quantum mechanics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When two bodies pull on each other, both of them move, so the distance between them changes faster than if one were fixed.
The reduced mass is a smaller effective mass that takes this into account.
It is always less than each of the two masses.
For two equal masses $m$, the reduced mass is $m/2$; for a planet much lighter than its star, it is almost the mass of the planet.
