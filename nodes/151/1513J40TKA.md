## Statement

Let $T$ be a [[complete theory|655K8Y7VS8]] with infinite models in a countable signature $L$.
The following are equivalent.

1. $T$ is [[$\aleph_0$-categorical|1JJCGNX240]]: any two countable models of $T$ are isomorphic.
2. For every $n \ge 1$, the set $S_n(T)$ of complete $n$-[[types|9V71Q3SD1G]] of $T$ is finite.
3. For every $n \ge 1$, there are only finitely many $L$-formulas in $x_1, \dots, x_n$ up to equivalence modulo $T$.
4. For every $n \ge 1$, every complete $n$-type of $T$ is isolated, in the sense of the [[omitting types theorem|7MFGBVDVG9]].
5. $T$ has a countable model $M$ whose [[automorphism group|0K1SCPBNKJ]] has finitely many [[orbits|0YQPZB5Q1G]] on $M^n$ for every $n \ge 1$.

The proof is [[here|KSAWMBQVZK]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Countable signature. Let $L$ have constants $c_\alpha$ for $\alpha < \omega_1$, and let $T$ say that they are pairwise distinct. $T$ has no countable models, so clause 1 holds vacuously. But the formulas $x_1 = c_\alpha$ give uncountably many $1$-types, so clause 2 fails.
- Completeness. The theory of dense linear orders, with endpoints allowed, has finitely many $n$-types for each $n$. It is not complete, and it has four countable models up to isomorphism.

### Consequences

- If $T$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical, every model of $T$ is [[atomic|HSJ4F9J8SM]], and the countable model is both prime and saturated.
- Adding finitely many constants keeps $\aleph_0$-categoricity: types of $\operatorname{Th}(M, \bar a)$ are types of $T$ in more variables, so there are still finitely many.
- In the countable model $M$, two tuples have the same type exactly when an automorphism maps one to the other. So the definable subsets of $M^n$ are the finite unions of orbits.
- Reducts of an $\aleph_0$-categorical structure to a smaller signature are $\aleph_0$-categorical, since clause 5 passes to reducts: the automorphism group can only grow.

### Converse

The theorem is a list of equivalences, so each clause implies the others.
In uncountable cardinals there is no analogue by counting types over the empty set; there [[Morley's categoricity theorem|V7N84P60SJ]] uses types over models.

### Generalizations

Clause 5 links model theory with permutation groups.
A permutation group on a countable set is *oligomorphic* when it has finitely many orbits on $n$-tuples for each $n$.
The countable $\aleph_0$-categorical structures are, up to choice of signature, the countable sets with an oligomorphic group of permutations.

## History

Erwin Engeler, Czesław Ryll-Nardzewski and Lars Svenonius found the theorem independently, all in 1959.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A theory is $\aleph_0$-categorical when it has only one countable world up to isomorphism.
The theorem says that this happens exactly when the language can say only finitely many different things about any fixed number of elements.
For the rational numbers with their order, all one can say about two numbers is whether the first is smaller, equal or larger than the second.

### L2 human

We check clause 5 for the [[theory of an equivalence relation with infinitely many infinite classes|N232QBKCAA]], in its countable model $M$.

1. An $n$-tuple $\bar a$ has a pattern: which entries are equal, and which are in the same class.
2. Two tuples with the same pattern are mapped to each other by an automorphism: permute the classes, then permute inside each class.
3. There are finitely many patterns for each $n$, so there are finitely many orbits.

The typical use is clause 5 to prove $\aleph_0$-categoricity by an orbit count, or clause 3 to disprove it.
For $(\Z, <)$, the formulas "at least $k$ elements lie between $x_1$ and $x_2$" are pairwise inequivalent, so clause 3 fails.

### L3 human

The theorem makes $\aleph_0$-categoricity a property of the automorphism group of one countable structure.
This is why [[Fraïssé limits|HJVRBDQSGG]] of classes with finitely many structures of each size in a finite relational signature, such as the [[Rado graph|RMXR0FEF54]], are $\aleph_0$-categorical.
