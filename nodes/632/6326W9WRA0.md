## Statement

Let $\Omega \subseteq \R^3$ be a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary $\partial \Omega$ and outward unit normal $n$.
Let $C$ be an [[elasticity tensor|SX32X1D537]] field on $\Omega$.
Let $u$ be a displacement field of class $C^2$ on $\overline\Omega$ in static equilibrium in [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] under body force $f$.
So $\nabla \cdot \sigma + f = 0$ with $\sigma = C : \varepsilon(u)$.
Let $t = \sigma n$ be the surface traction.
Then the strain energy of the body is half the work of the external loads acting through the final displacements:
$$\frac12 \int_\Omega \varepsilon(u) : C : \varepsilon(u) \, dV = \frac12 \left( \int_\Omega f \cdot u \, dV + \int_{\partial \Omega} t \cdot u \, dS \right).$$

## Remarks

The proof is one application of the [[divergence theorem|0JX86PSSA9]]: $\int_{\partial \Omega} t \cdot u \, dS = \int_\Omega \nabla \cdot (\sigma u) \, dV = \int_\Omega \big( (\nabla \cdot \sigma) \cdot u + \sigma : \nabla u \big) dV$, and $\sigma : \nabla u = \sigma : \varepsilon(u)$ because $\sigma$ is symmetric.
The factor $\tfrac12$ appears because the loads grow from $0$ to their final values while the displacements grow in proportion.
So the work done is half of final force times final displacement.
Example: a linear spring of stiffness $k$ stretched by $x$ under the force $F = kx$ stores $\tfrac12 k x^2 = \tfrac12 F x$.
A consequence is uniqueness in linear elastostatics.
The difference of two solutions with the same data does zero external work, so its strain energy vanishes.
When $C$ is positive definite, its strain is then zero.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When loads deform an elastic body slowly, part of their work is stored as elastic energy.
Clapeyron's theorem says that this energy is exactly half of the final load times the final displacement.
The other half is not lost: it reflects the fact that the load was smaller at the start of the loading.

### L2 human

The theorem links the energy and the force descriptions of [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]].
It is used to prove uniqueness of solutions and to compute energies from boundary data.
It is the special case of [[Betti's reciprocal theorem|E0WD2ATFZD]] with both states equal.
