## Definition

Let $\varepsilon > 0$ and let $g \colon (0, \varepsilon) \to (0, \infty)$ be a function.
The *critical exponent* of $g$ at $0$ is the limit
$$\lambda = \lim_{t \to 0^+} \frac{\log g(t)}{\log t},$$
when this limit exists in $\R$, and one writes $g(t) \sim t^{\lambda}$.

For a system near a [[critical point|57789VKKAY]] at temperature $T_c$, let $t = (T - T_c)/T_c$.
The standard exponents are defined by the following laws, at $h = 0$ unless stated otherwise:

1. specific heat $C \sim \abs{t}^{-\alpha}$;
2. order parameter $m \sim (-t)^{\beta}$ for $t < 0$;
3. susceptibility $\chi \sim \abs{t}^{-\gamma}$;
4. order parameter at $t = 0$: $\abs{m} \sim \abs{h}^{1/\delta}$ as $h \to 0$;
5. correlation length $\xi \sim \abs{t}^{-\nu}$;
6. two-point [[correlation function|ENWMTRJRND]] at $t = 0$: $G(x) \sim \abs{x}^{-(d - 2 + \eta)}$ as $\abs{x} \to \infty$, in dimension $d$.

## Remarks

Example: $g(t) = 5 \, t^{1/2} \abs{\log t}$ has $\log g(t) / \log t = 1/2 + (\log 5 + \log \abs{\log t}) / \log t \to 1/2$, so its exponent is $1/2$.
Constant factors and logarithms do not change the exponent.
A logarithmic divergence $C \sim \abs{\log \abs{t}}$, as for the two-dimensional Ising model, gives $\alpha = 0$.

Non-example: $g(t) = e^{1/\sqrt{t}}$ has $\log g(t) / \log t = t^{-1/2} / \log t \to -\infty$, so no finite exponent exists.
Correlation lengths of this kind occur at the Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition.

Exponents for $t < 0$ are sometimes written with a prime, such as $\alpha'$ and $\gamma'$, when they may differ from those for $t > 0$.
For the two-dimensional Ising model, $\alpha = 0$, $\beta = 1/8$, $\gamma = 7/4$, $\delta = 15$, $\nu = 1$ and $\eta = 1/4$.
