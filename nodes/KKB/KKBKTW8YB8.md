## Summary

Commutative algebra is the study of commutative rings, their ideals and their modules.
A commutative ring is a ring in which the product of two elements does not depend on their order, such as the ring of integers or a ring of polynomials.
Commutative algebra gives the algebraic tools of algebraic geometry and algebraic number theory, because the rings of polynomial functions on varieties and the rings of algebraic integers are commutative rings.
The subject grew from the ideal theory of Dedekind and Kronecker and from the work of Hilbert on polynomial rings and invariants, and Emmy Noether gave it its abstract form in the 1920s.
This topic collects the commutative algebra pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Rings and modules, algebraic geometry, algebraic number theory and homological algebra have their own topics.
