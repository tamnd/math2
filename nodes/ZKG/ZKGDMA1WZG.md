## Summary

The Weber number $\mathrm{We} = \rho U^2 L / \sigma$ is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] that compares the inertia of a fluid with its surface tension.
It governs flows with interfaces between two fluids, such as drops, bubbles, sprays and thin films.
When the Weber number is large, inertia can overcome surface tension and break drops and jets into smaller pieces.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Surface tension acts like a thin elastic skin on the surface of a liquid that tries to keep drops round.
When a drop moves fast through air, the air pushes on it and tries to flatten and tear it.
The Weber number compares the push with the skin.
A raindrop falling slowly stays whole; a drop hit by a fast air jet, at a large Weber number, shatters into a mist.
