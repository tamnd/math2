## Summary

Category $\mathcal{O}$ is a category of modules of a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]] that contains the finite-dimensional modules and the [[Verma modules|809XR5G0DG]].
It was introduced by Bernstein, Gelfand and Gelfand.
It is an [[abelian category|Y9CZ3MTNF1]] in which every object has finite length.
So it is large enough to be flexible and small enough to be studied in detail.
For integral weights, the multiplicities of simple modules in Verma modules are given by the [[Kazhdan–Lusztig conjecture|ZPE6XR02YQ]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Finite-dimensional representations of a semisimple Lie algebra are well understood, but they are too few for some purposes.
Category $\mathcal{O}$ also allows infinite-dimensional modules, while keeping the good features of highest weight theory.
Its modules split into weight spaces of finite dimension, and the raising operators act on each vector in a finite way.
The main examples are the Verma modules, built from one highest weight vector by applying the lowering operators freely.
