## Summary

Kaluza–Klein theory is a five-dimensional version of [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]] in which the fifth dimension is a small circle.
Theodor Kaluza (1921) showed that the five-dimensional Einstein equations contain the four-dimensional Einstein equations together with [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] and an extra scalar field.
Oskar Klein (1926) proposed that the extra dimension is compact and very small, which explains why it is not seen.
The idea of getting gauge fields from the geometry of extra dimensions is central in [[string theory|GM58M1HFY0]] and [[supergravity|VWBEWVT29C]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a garden hose seen from far away: it looks like a line, but up close each point of the line is a small circle.
Kaluza–Klein theory imagines that every point of our four-dimensional spacetime carries such a tiny circle.
Gravity in the five-dimensional space then looks, to a four-dimensional observer, like gravity plus electromagnetism.
The way the metric tilts the circle as one moves in spacetime becomes the electromagnetic potential.
Motion around the circle comes in whole-number steps, and this gives a reason why electric charge comes in whole multiples of a basic charge.
