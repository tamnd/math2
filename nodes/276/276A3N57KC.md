## Summary

Morava K-theory is a family of [[generalized cohomology theories|H28PSZR7XG]] $K(n)$, one for each prime $p$ and each integer $n \ge 1$.
Its coefficient ring $\F_p[v_n, v_n^{-1}]$ is a graded field, so it behaves almost like ordinary cohomology with coefficients in a field.
Its [[formal group law|TNHP7XXYZT]] has height exactly $n$.
These theories are the basic building blocks of [[chromatic homotopy theory|300V40FC1B]], where they detect the layers of the stable homotopy category.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary cohomology with coefficients in $\F_p$ sees a space in a simple way, because every graded vector space over a field is free.
Morava K-theory keeps this good property but sees more of the stable homotopy of a space.
Think of a prism: each $K(n)$ is one colour of the light, and $n$ measures how fine a detail that colour can see.
For $n = 1$ and $p = 2$, the theory $K(1)$ is mod $2$ complex [[topological K-theory|6XCFW7YGX8]].
For larger $n$ the theories are new and are built from [[complex cobordism|5CZHFNY9F3]].
