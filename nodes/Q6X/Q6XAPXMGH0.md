## Summary

The bending moment at a cross-section of a beam is the turning effect of the internal stresses acting on the cross-section.
By equilibrium it equals the total moment of the external forces on either side of the section.
It is the quantity that makes a beam curve, and the largest stresses in a beam occur where the bending moment is largest.
Together with the shear force it is the basic internal force of [[beam theory|GDM0J9ZCBT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hold a ruler at one end and press on the other end.
The ruler bends most near your hand, because there the push at the far end acts with the longest lever arm.
The bending moment at a point measures this: force times lever arm, added up over all the forces on one side of the point.
For a ruler of length $L$ with a force $F$ at its free end, the bending moment at the hand is $FL$, and at the free end it is $0$.
