## Summary

Game theory is the mathematical study of strategic interaction between decision makers.
A game in strategic form consists of a set of players, a set of strategies for each player, and a payoff function for each player on the profiles of strategies.
Game theory gives models for economics, political science, evolutionary biology and computer science, and the Nash equilibrium is its central solution concept.
Von Neumann proved the minimax theorem for zero-sum games in 1928, and his 1944 book with Morgenstern and the work of Nash around 1950 started the modern subject.
This topic collects the game theory pages of math2: strategic, zero-sum, extensive and Bayesian games, equilibrium refinements, repeated and stochastic games, cooperative games and values, bargaining, evolutionary and learning dynamics, algorithmic game theory, mechanism design and auctions, and combinatorial games.
Mathematical economics, optimization, the calculus of variations and optimal control, and recreational mathematics have their own topics.
