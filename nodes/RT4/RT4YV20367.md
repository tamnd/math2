## Summary

The associator of three elements $x, y, z$ of a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] is $(x, y, z) = (xy)z - x(yz)$.
It measures how far the product is from being [[associative|0K8F7PVDMX]], in the same way as the [[commutator|T2HH02QC2M]] measures how far it is from being commutative.
Many classes of nonassociative algebras are defined by conditions on the associator, such as [[alternative algebras|N8K9SSVMRN]] and [[flexible algebras|R1Z4P70285]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an associative algebra you can drop brackets: $(xy)z = x(yz)$.
The associator is the difference between the two ways of bracketing.
It is zero for all triples exactly when the algebra is associative.
For the cross product on $\R^3$, the associator of $e_1, e_1, e_2$ is $e_2$, not zero.
