## Summary

A map projection is a map from a region of the [[sphere|P9CM4GQ2T0]] or of a [[reference ellipsoid|R9XX7TNJCH]] to the plane.
It turns latitude and longitude into flat coordinates $(x, y)$ for drawing maps.
By the [[Theorema Egregium|YKSA8SJ81V]], no projection keeps all distances, so each one distorts something.
Projections are classified by what they keep: angles (conformal), areas (equal-area) or some distances (equidistant).

## Explanations

### L1 human

A map projection is a rule for flattening the curved Earth onto paper.
Peeling an orange and pressing the peel flat always tears or stretches it; a projection chooses how to stretch.
The simplest rule plots longitude as $x$ and latitude as $y$, which gives a rectangle of the whole world.
In that map, Greenland and other places near the poles are stretched sideways.
Other projections keep shapes right, or keep areas right, but never both at once.
