## Summary

Rheology is the study of how materials deform and flow under applied forces.
Its central objects are constitutive equations, which say how the stress in a material depends on its deformation and on the history of that deformation.
It covers materials between the ideal elastic solid and the [[Newtonian fluid|T4ZV12BVHN]], such as polymers, suspensions, gels and [[non-Newtonian fluids|8DT9MB9V6R]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Honey, bread dough, silly putty and rubber all respond differently when you push them.
Honey flows slowly, dough stretches and partly springs back, silly putty bounces when thrown but spreads out when left alone.
Rheology asks, for each material, which rule links the force on it to the way it moves and changes shape.
For water the rule is simple: stress is proportional to the rate of deformation.
For silly putty the rule must include memory, since the response depends on how fast and for how long the material is pushed.
