## Statement

Let $K$ be a commutative ring, let $A$ be an [[invertible|0TWTS88TWN]] $n \times n$ matrix over $K$, and let $u, v \in K^n$ be column vectors.
Then
$$\det(A + u v^T) = (1 + v^T A^{-1} u) \det A,$$
where $\det$ is the [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The formula uses $A^{-1}$.
For singular $A$, use the form without inverses: $\det(A + u v^T) = \det A + v^T \operatorname{adj}(A)\, u$, which holds for every square $A$ over $K$.
It follows from the stated form for the generic matrix and from naturality under ring homomorphisms.
For example, $A = 0$ gives $\det(u v^T) = 0$ for $n \ge 2$, as a matrix of rank at most $1$ must.

### Consequences

- The matrix $A + u v^T$ is invertible if and only if $1 + v^T A^{-1} u$ is a unit.
- The [[Sherman-Morrison formula|MDZDEFE53C]] for $(A + u v^T)^{-1}$ has the same factor $1 + v^T A^{-1} u$ in its denominator.

### Converse

The lemma is an identity for all $u$ and $v$, so a converse in the usual sense does not apply.

### Generalizations

Let $U$ and $V$ be $n \times m$ matrices.
Then $\det(A + U V^T) = \det(I_m + V^T A^{-1} U) \det A$.
This follows from the [[Weinstein-Aronszajn identity|0G24VQ1088]] in the same way as the case $m = 1$.
A low-rank update of rank $m$ thus needs only an $m \times m$ determinant.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Adding a matrix of rank one, $u v^T$, changes the determinant by a simple factor.
The factor is $1 + v^T A^{-1} u$, a single number.
So we can update a determinant cheaply when we know the inverse.

### L2 human

Take $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, $u = (1, 1)^T$ and $v = (1, 2)^T$.

1. $A^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(1/2, 1/3)$, so $v^T A^{-1} u = 1/2 + 2/3 = 7/6$.
2. The lemma gives $(1 + 7/6) \cdot 6 = 13$.
3. Check directly: $A + u v^T = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, with determinant $15 - 2 = 13$.
4. Check by the form without inverses: $\operatorname{adj} A = \operatorname{diag}(3, 2)$, so $\det A + v^T \operatorname{adj}(A)\, u = 6 + 3 + 4 = 13$.
The typical use is to update a determinant after one row or one column of $A$ changes, since such a change is a rank one update.
A trap: the factor is $1 + v^T A^{-1} u$, with $A^{-1}$, not $1 + v^T A u$.

### L3 human

The lemma is the rank one case of the Weinstein-Aronszajn identity.
In numerical work, it updates a known determinant after a rank one change in $O(n^2)$ operations.
