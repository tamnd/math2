## Summary

An observable is a physical quantity of a quantum system that can be measured, such as position, momentum, energy or spin along an axis.
In the Hilbert space formulation it is a [[self-adjoint operator|0NEGN5CRSC]], possibly [[unbounded|KRJX5RA0PW]], on the Hilbert space of the system.
The possible results of a measurement are the points of its [[spectrum|BMKATJ802A]], which is a subset of $\R$.
Through the [[spectral theorem|18KPYRRPZY]], each observable has a [[projection-valued measure|ATGT25E5FF]], which with the state gives the probability of each result.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In classical mechanics a measurable quantity is a number attached to each state, such as the energy of a moving ball.
In quantum mechanics it is an operator that acts on the state vectors.
Its eigenvalues are the values a measurement can give, and its eigenvectors are the states in which that value comes out with certainty.
For a spin, the vertical spin is the matrix $\mathrm{diag}(1, -1)$: the results are $1$ and $-1$, obtained with certainty in the states $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$.
