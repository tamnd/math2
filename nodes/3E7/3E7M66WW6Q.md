## Summary

This topic is a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]], and it asks what a formal theory can say about its own proofs.
The starting point is to read the modal box $\square p$ as "$\mathsf{PA}$ proves $p$", so that the derivability conditions behind Gödel's theorems become modal axioms.
Löb's theorem then becomes the axiom $\square(\square p \to p) \to \square p$, and the second incompleteness theorem is its instance with $p = \bot$.
The modal logic $\mathsf{GL}$ that these axioms give has a clean semantics on finite transitive irreflexive frames, so questions about provability reduce to checking small finite pictures.
Solovay's theorem shows that nothing is lost: $\mathsf{GL}$ proves exactly the modal laws that hold for every reading in $\mathsf{PA}$.
From there the story branches.
Self-reference becomes the computation of explicit fixed points, strong provability gives the Grzegorczyk logic, a hierarchy of stronger provability notions gives Japaridze's logic $\mathsf{GLP}$ and ordinal analysis, and Boolean algebras with a provability operator give the algebraic view.
A binary operator for relative interpretability leads to the logics $\mathsf{IL}$, $\mathsf{ILM}$ and $\mathsf{ILP}$, and adding quantifiers shows where the clean decidable picture ends.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Provability logic|WJK0YA4GP2]]: how to read $\square$ as provability in $\mathsf{PA}$, and why the logic $\mathsf{GL}$ is the right set of laws.
2. [[Löb's axiom in provability logic|QQ6PZFGGB3]]: how axiom 4 follows from Löb's axiom, and which frames make the axiom true.
3. [[Kripke completeness of GL|1DMFNR7FZT]]: how to refute a non-theorem of $\mathsf{GL}$ with a finite tree, which also makes $\mathsf{GL}$ decidable.
4. [[Solovay's arithmetical completeness theorem|1ZRWVDYPX0]]: why $\mathsf{GL}$ is exactly the provability logic of $\mathsf{PA}$, and which logic $\mathsf{GLS}$ describes truth.
5. [[De Jongh-Sambin fixed-point theorem|TDDX1NHG8D]]: how to solve a self-referential equation such as $p \leftrightarrow \neg \square p$ inside the modal logic.
6. [[Grzegorczyk logic|2Q8E4GQF3B]]: the logic of "true and provable", its finite partial orders, and its link with intuitionistic logic.
7. [[Japaridze's polymodal logic GLP|3EDRQJJHMT]]: a ladder of provability operators that has no Kripke semantics but measures the strength of $\mathsf{PA}$ by $\varepsilon_0$.
8. [[Magari algebra|PHWAN904PT]]: the algebraic models of $\mathsf{GL}$, where fixed points become equations in a Boolean algebra.
9. [[Interpretability logic|BGKZTSQN3W]]: the binary operator $\rhd$, its Veltman frames, and which logic belongs to which theory.
10. [[Quantified provability logic|5K9J8R8SCP]]: why the clean decidable picture breaks down once quantifiers enter.

2. [[Formal arithmetic and its fragments|QHCRN4JVG0]]: Peano arithmetic and its $\Sigma_1$-completeness.
3. [[Syntax, Kripke semantics and completeness of modal logic|SKY7AQFG1V]]: [[modal logic|C3RZY20ETK]] and [[Kripke semantics|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]].
