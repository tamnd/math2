## Summary

The shallow ice approximation is a simplified model of the flow of grounded glaciers and ice sheets.
It is valid when the ice is much thinner than it is wide.
In it, the weight of the ice is balanced only by vertical shear stress.
So the ice velocity at each point depends only on the local thickness and surface slope.
With [[Glen's flow law|ZSWNS5BRS1]] this leads to a single nonlinear diffusion equation for the ice thickness, which is the basis of many large-scale ice-sheet models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An ice sheet such as Antarctica is a few kilometres thick but thousands of kilometres wide, like a pancake.
In such a thin layer, the ice at each place simply shears downhill, in the direction in which the surface slopes down.
It behaves as if its neighbours did not matter.
Thicker ice and steeper surface slopes give much faster flow.
The ice thickness then spreads out like heat in a strongly nonlinear diffusion process, fed by snowfall and reduced by melting.
