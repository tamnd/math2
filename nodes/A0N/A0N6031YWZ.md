## Statement

Let $\phi$ be a field theory on Minkowski space $\R^{1,3}$ satisfying the [[Wightman axioms|5QTZQV6TNG]].
Let $H$ be its [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]], $\Omega$ its vacuum vector and $D$ the common dense domain of the fields.
Let $O \subseteq \R^{1,3}$ be a nonempty open set.
Let $\mathcal{P}(O)$ be the algebra of operators on $D$ generated by the smeared fields $\phi(f)$ with $\operatorname{supp} f \subseteq O$.
Then the set $\mathcal{P}(O)\Omega = \set{A\Omega : A \in \mathcal{P}(O)}$ is dense in $H$.

## Remarks

The idea of the proof is analyticity.
Take $\Psi \in H$ orthogonal to $\mathcal{P}(O)\Omega$.
By the spectrum condition, the function $(x_1, \dots, x_n) \mapsto \ip{\Psi}{\phi(x_1) \cdots \phi(x_n)\Omega}$ is the boundary value of a function analytic in a complex tube.
It vanishes when all $x_i$ lie in $O$, so by [[analytic continuation|0M3YQJ81NB]] it vanishes for all $x_i$.
So $\Psi$ is orthogonal to the dense set given by the cyclicity axiom; hence $\Psi = 0$.

A consequence: suppose the set of points spacelike to all of $O$ has a nonempty interior $O'$.
If $A \in \mathcal{P}(O)$ and $A\Omega = 0$, then $A$ vanishes on the dense subspace $\mathcal{P}(O')\Omega$.
Indeed $A B \Omega = B A \Omega = 0$ for $B \in \mathcal{P}(O')$, by locality.
In this sense $\Omega$ is separating for the local fields.
The same results hold for the local von Neumann algebras of a net satisfying the [[Haag–Kastler axioms|MGBCX7FH2G]] with a vacuum state, under a weak additivity assumption.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The vacuum of a quantum field is not empty in a naive sense.
Act on it only with operations inside a tiny region, such as a small laboratory for a short time.
In this way one can approximate any state of the whole universe as closely as one wants.
This shows that the vacuum is highly entangled across space.

### L2 human

The theorem is a central structural result of axiomatic and algebraic quantum field theory.
The separating property means no local operator can annihilate the vacuum, so there are no strictly local projections onto "a particle here".
In the algebraic setting the cyclic and separating vector makes Tomita–Takesaki modular theory available, and the Bisognano–Wichmann theorem identifies the modular group of a wedge algebra with Lorentz boosts.
The theorem is also used in the proofs of the [[spin–statistics theorem|5QYRVQY3T7]] and of results on entanglement entropy in field theory.
