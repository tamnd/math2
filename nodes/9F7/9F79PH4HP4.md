## Definition

Let $Q$ be a finite nonempty set, the *domain*, whose elements are called items or problems.
A *knowledge structure* on $Q$ is a [[family of subsets|0C21Q694AG]] $\mathcal{K}$ of $Q$ with $\emptyset \in \mathcal{K}$ and $Q \in \mathcal{K}$; its members are the *knowledge states*.
A *knowledge space* is a knowledge structure $\mathcal{K}$ that is closed under union: $K \cup L \in \mathcal{K}$ for all $K, L \in \mathcal{K}$.

## Remarks

Because $Q$ is finite, closure under union of two states gives closure under union of any subfamily, the empty union being $\emptyset$.
Example: $Q = \set{a, b}$ and $\mathcal{K} = \set{\emptyset, \set{a}, \set{a, b}}$; the unions of states are $\emptyset$, $\set{a}$ and $\set{a, b}$, all in $\mathcal{K}$.
Non-example: $Q = \set{a, b, c}$ and $\mathcal{K} = \set{\emptyset, \set{a}, \set{b}, Q}$ is a knowledge structure but not a knowledge space, since $\set{a} \cup \set{b} = \set{a, b} \notin \mathcal{K}$.
A knowledge structure closed under both union and intersection is called quasi-ordinal.
By Birkhoff's theorem, such structures correspond to [[preorders|0VABYWJWH9]] on $Q$.
A [[partial order|0QV2JS9RJ3]] arises when distinct items have distinct sets of prerequisites.
