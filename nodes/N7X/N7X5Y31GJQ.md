## Summary

A transit light curve is the brightness of a star as a function of time while a planet passes in front of it.
It shows a small dip whose depth gives the ratio of the planet radius to the star radius.
The shape and duration of the dip constrain the orbit.
The transit method has found most of the known exoplanets.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a planet crosses the face of its star, it blocks a tiny part of the light, like a fly crossing a lamp.
A telescope that measures the brightness of the star sees a shallow, flat-bottomed dip that repeats every orbit.
The fraction of light lost is about the fraction of the stellar disk covered, $(R_p/R_\star)^2$.
A Jupiter-size planet in front of a Sun-like star blocks about $1\%$ of the light, and an Earth-size planet only about $0.008\%$.
