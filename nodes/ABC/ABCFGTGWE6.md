## Summary

A seismic shadow zone is a range of distances from an earthquake at which a given type of direct wave does not arrive.
For the Earth, direct P-waves are missing between about $104°$ and $140°$ from the epicentre, because they are bent sharply at the core.
Direct S-waves are missing beyond about $104°$, because they do not pass through the liquid outer core.
These shadow zones were the first evidence for the Earth's core and for the liquidity of its outer part.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine standing on the far side of the Earth from an earthquake.
Waves that dive deep reach the core, where P-waves slow down suddenly and bend inward, like light through a lens.
The bending sends them past a belt of the surface, which gets no direct P-waves: a shadow.
Shear waves cannot travel through liquid, so they make an even larger shadow covering the whole far side.
Faint waves found inside the P shadow led to the discovery of the solid inner core.
