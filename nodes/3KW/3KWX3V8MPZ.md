## Summary

The unified neutral theory of biodiversity was proposed by Stephen Hubbell in 2001.
It models an ecological community in which all individuals have the same chances of birth, death, dispersal and speciation.
Differences in species abundance then come only from chance, from dispersal and from speciation.
The theory gives explicit predictions for species abundance distributions and serves as a null model against which niche-based explanations are tested.
Mathematically it is a [[Moran process|A9M7VKRRV9]] with many types, in which new types enter by speciation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a forest that is always full: a tree grows only where another tree has died.
When a tree dies, its place goes to the seedling of a random tree in the forest, or, very rarely, to a brand new species.
No species is better than another, and luck alone decides which species becomes common and which disappears.
Even so, this simple game produces a few very common species and many rare ones, much like real tropical forests.
