## Summary

Elastic instability is the loss of stability of an equilibrium of an elastic structure when the load passes a critical value.
Below that value a small disturbance only makes the structure wobble about its equilibrium; above it the structure moves away to a different shape.
For conservative loads the test is energetic: an equilibrium is stable when it is a strict local minimum of the total potential energy.
[[Buckling|4GAM4KXSM8]] of columns, plates and shells is the main example, and the subject is a part of [[bifurcation theory|D9TW46P19Y]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Press down on the top of a plastic ruler standing on a table.
For a small push it stays straight, and if it is nudged sideways it springs back.
Past a certain push, a nudge no longer comes back: the ruler bows out to one side.
The straight shape is still an equilibrium, but it has become unstable, like a ball balanced on top of a hill.
