## Summary

The principle of material frame-indifference, or objectivity, requires that the response of a material does not depend on the observer.
Two observers who differ by a time-dependent rigid motion must find the same [[constitutive equation|Q4C55V47A8]].
It restricts the form of constitutive equations strongly.
For an elastic material, the stress is then fixed by its values on pure stretches.
The rotation part of the deformation gradient only turns that stress.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rubber band behaves the same whether you watch it from the ground or from a merry-go-round.
So the law that gives its stress cannot depend on how the observer is turning.
In particular, rotating a stretched band as a whole must rotate its stress with it, and must not create new stress.
This simple requirement rules out many formulas that look plausible at first sight.
