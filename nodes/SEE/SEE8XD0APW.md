## Summary

Quantum chromodynamics, or QCD, is the quantum field theory of the strong interaction between quarks and gluons.
It is a [[Yang–Mills theory|A0J2688KZF]] with gauge group $SU(3)$, coupled to quark fields that each carry three "colours".
Its coupling becomes weak at high energies, a property called [[asymptotic freedom|RRN63RT562]], so perturbation theory works for high-energy collisions.
At low energies quarks are confined inside protons, neutrons and other hadrons, a fact supported by [[lattice computations|ZBJY59W3Z5]] but not proved.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Protons and neutrons are made of smaller particles, the quarks, held together by the gluons.
Each quark carries a kind of charge with three values, named after three colours, and gluons carry colour too.
Because gluons are charged, they attract one another, and the force between quarks does not weaken with distance as electric forces do.
This is why a single quark has never been seen alone.
At very short distances the force becomes weak, and quarks behave almost like free particles.
