## Definition

Consider an electrolyte at absolute temperature $T$ in a solvent of permittivity $\varepsilon$.
It contains ion species $i = 1, \dots, s$ with charges $z_i e$ and bulk number densities $n_i > 0$, and it is electrically neutral: $\sum_i z_i n_i = 0$.
Here $e$ is the elementary charge and $k_B$ is Boltzmann's constant.
*Debye–Hückel theory* describes the mean electric potential $\varphi$ around a fixed ion by the following assumptions.

1. The charge density is $\rho = \sum_i z_i e \, n_i \exp(-z_i e \varphi / (k_B T))$, by the [[Boltzmann distribution|GYNMZ9201M]] of each species in the potential $\varphi$.
2. The potential obeys [[Poisson's equation|FC64B1A65B]] $\nabla^2 \varphi = -\rho / \varepsilon$ outside the fixed ion.
3. The exponentials are linearized, $\exp(-x) \approx 1 - x$, which is valid when $\abs{z_i e \varphi} \ll k_B T$.

With neutrality, clauses 1 to 3 give the *linearized Poisson–Boltzmann equation*
$$\nabla^2 \varphi = \kappa^2 \varphi, \qquad \kappa^2 = \frac{e^2}{\varepsilon k_B T} \sum_i z_i^2 n_i,$$
where $\kappa = 1/\lambda_D$ is the inverse [[Debye length|ZSNW9FC08R]].

## Remarks

Example: around a point ion of charge $Q$ at the origin, the radial solution that decays at infinity and behaves like $Q/(4\pi\varepsilon r)$ near $0$ is
$$\varphi(r) = \frac{Q}{4 \pi \varepsilon r} e^{-\kappa r}.$$
Check: for a radial function, $\nabla^2 \varphi = r^{-1} (r \varphi)''$, and $r \varphi = C e^{-\kappa r}$ gives $(r\varphi)'' = \kappa^2 r \varphi$.
The zeroth-order term of clause 1 vanishes by neutrality, which is why only the linear term $-\kappa^2 \varepsilon \varphi$ remains.
Non-example: in a concentrated solution, or near a highly charged surface, $\abs{z_i e \varphi}$ is not small compared to $k_B T$, so clause 3 fails and one must solve the nonlinear Poisson–Boltzmann equation.
The theory leads to the Debye–Hückel limiting law: $\ln \gamma_\pm$ is proportional to $-\abs{z_+ z_-} \sqrt{I}$.
Here $\gamma_\pm$ is the mean activity coefficient, and $I = \frac12 \sum_i c_i z_i^2$ is the ionic strength for molar concentrations $c_i$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each ion makes a potential, the other ions arrange themselves in that potential according to the Boltzmann law, and their charge changes the potential.
Solving this self-consistently, after linearizing, gives a potential that dies out exponentially.
