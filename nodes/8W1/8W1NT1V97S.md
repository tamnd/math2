## Summary

The radar cross-section of a target measures how strongly it scatters an incident radar wave toward a receiver.
It is an area: the area of an ideal isotropic scatterer that would send back the same power density at the receiver.
It depends on the target's shape and material, the frequency, the polarization and the directions of incidence and observation.
It enters the radar equation and is the quantity that stealth design tries to reduce.
See the [[definition|3HWDYB2FJ3]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A radar sends out a wave and listens for the echo.
A big flat plate facing the radar sends back a strong echo, while a smooth sphere of the same size sends back much less.
The radar cross-section turns the strength of the echo into an equivalent area.
A metal sphere much larger than the wavelength has radar cross-section equal to its shadow area $\pi a^2$, where $a$ is its radius.
