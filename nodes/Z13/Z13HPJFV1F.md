## Summary

Earth's magnetic field is the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] produced in and around the Earth, mainly by electric currents in the liquid iron outer core.
Smaller parts come from magnetised crustal rocks and from currents in the ionosphere and magnetosphere.
Near the surface it is roughly the field of a tilted [[dipole|2KPTTBM584]] at the Earth's centre, with an intensity of about $25\,000$ to $65\,000$ nanotesla.
In the source-free region between the surface and the ionosphere it is the gradient of a harmonic potential.
This allows its description by spherical harmonics in [[Gauss's analysis|PGPW2Y7GPD]].
It changes slowly in time by [[secular variation|JXVMPHF681]] and reverses its polarity at irregular intervals over geological time.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A compass needle points roughly north because the Earth behaves like a giant bar magnet.
The magnet is not a solid piece of iron but a flow of molten metal deep inside the Earth that generates electric currents, like a dynamo.
At the equator the field lines are nearly horizontal, and near the magnetic poles they point steeply into or out of the ground.
For example, in much of Europe the field points down into the ground at an angle of about $60$ degrees or more, which is why a free compass needle tilts.
