## Example

Take Peano arithmetic and add the false axiom "Peano arithmetic proves $0 = 1$".
The result never proves a contradiction, and yet it claims a proof that no number is.

Let $T := \mathsf{PA} + \neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, where $\neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is $\exists y \, \mathrm{Prf}_{\mathsf{PA}}(y, \ulcorner 0 = S0 \urcorner)$.
We show that $T$ is [[consistent|VM8TQ70CVJ]] but not [[ω-consistent|4S29X1GTV4]].

1. $\mathsf{PA}$ is consistent, because $\mathbb{N}$ is a model of it.
2. By the [[second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]], $\mathsf{PA} \nvdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.
3. Suppose $T$ were inconsistent.
   Then $\mathsf{PA} + \neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA}) \vdash 0 = S0$, and the deduction theorem gives $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA}) \to 0 = S0$.
   With $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \neg (0 = S0)$ this gives $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, against step 2, so $T$ is consistent.
4. Let $\varphi(y) := \mathrm{Prf}_{\mathsf{PA}}(y, \ulcorner 0 = S0 \urcorner)$.
   Then $T \vdash \exists y \, \varphi(y)$, since this is the new axiom of $T$.
5. Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
   By step 1, $n$ does not code a proof of $0 = S0$ in $\mathsf{PA}$.
6. By representability in $\mathsf{Q}$, $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \neg \varphi(\underline{n})$, and so $T \vdash \neg \varphi(\underline{n})$.
7. Steps 4 and 6 show that $\varphi$ witnesses $\omega$-inconsistency.

## Remarks

The theory is not even $1$-consistent.
The formula $\varphi$ is $\Sigma_1$, and $T$ proves the false $\Sigma_1$ sentence $\exists y \, \varphi(y)$.
All its models are [[non-standard|1QWN5CV4CG]], and in each of them the witness of $\exists y \, \varphi(y)$ is a non-standard number, as [[non-standard proofs|JP0RVCPF38]] shows.

The theory even refutes its own consistency.
$\mathsf{PA}$ proves that every proof in $\mathsf{PA}$ is a proof in $T$, so $T \vdash \neg \mathrm{Con}(T)$.
This is why Gödel's original first theorem needs more than consistency: the Gödel sentence $G_T$ of $T$ is $T$-equivalent to $\mathrm{Con}(T)$, so $T$ refutes $G_T$.

Replace $\mathsf{PA}$ by any consistent computably axiomatized $T_0 \supseteq \mathsf{PA}$, and the same steps show that $T_0 + \neg \mathrm{Con}(T_0)$ is consistent and $\omega$-inconsistent.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This theory takes the rules of arithmetic and adds the false claim "arithmetic proves $0 = 1$".
It contains no contradiction that can be written down, because arithmetic cannot rule out this claim by itself.
But it says "a proof of $0 = 1$ exists", while it can check that $0$, $1$, $2$ and every other number is not such a proof.
That is exactly $\omega$-inconsistency.

### L2 human

The argument splits into a consistency half and an inconsistency half.
For consistency, the second incompleteness theorem says that $\mathsf{PA}$ cannot refute $\neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, so adding it causes no contradiction.
For $\omega$-inconsistency, take the formula "$y$ codes a proof of $0 = S0$".
The theory proves that such a $y$ exists, and for each numeral it proves "this one is not it", because checking a single number is a computation that $\mathsf{Q}$ can do.
This example is the standard proof that consistency does not imply $\omega$-consistency.
Watch out: $T$ is consistent, so it has a model, and in that model the witness is not the value of any numeral.
