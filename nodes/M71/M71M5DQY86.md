## Definition

Let $(M, g)$ be an oriented [[pseudo-Riemannian manifold|GH6CXWFNDJ]] with [[Hodge star|1Y4WX4XQWJ]] $\star$.
Let $G$ be a [[compact|EVHCE5VR6Y]] Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, and fix an inner product $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$ on $\mathfrak{g}$ invariant under the adjoint action.
Let $P \to M$ be a [[principal $G$-bundle|8E37E74XVP]].
For a [[connection|NJ21PN5ZGJ]] $A$ on $P$, let $F_A$ be its [[curvature|ZAJFW1S078]], a $2$-form with values in the adjoint bundle $P \times_G \mathfrak{g}$.
Let $g_{\mathrm{YM}} > 0$ be a constant, the coupling constant.
*Pure Yang–Mills theory* with gauge group $G$ is the following [[gauge theory|2GKJQNQMVN]].
Its fields are the connections on $P$, and its action is
$$S(A) = -\frac{1}{2 g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} \int_M \ip{F_A}{\wedge \star F_A}.$$
Here $\ip{F_A}{\wedge \star F_A}$ combines the wedge product of forms with the inner product on $\mathfrak{g}$.
Its field equations are the [[Yang–Mills equations|JJWETW5AT8]] $d_A \star F_A = 0$.

## Remarks

In Riemannian (Euclidean) signature one uses instead $S_E(A) = \frac{1}{2 g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} \int_M \abs{F_A}^2 \, \mathrm{vol}_g$, the [[Yang–Mills functional|CKGDR9P9PA]].
Sign conventions for the Lorentzian action differ between books; the field equations do not depend on them.
A Yang–Mills theory with matter adds terms for fields in associated bundles, as in [[quantum chromodynamics|SEE8XD0APW]].

Example: $G = U(1)$ with $\mathfrak{g} = i\R$.
Then $F_A = dA$ is a closed form, $d_A = d$ on forms with values in $\mathfrak{g}$, and the Yang–Mills equations become $d \star F = 0$, Maxwell's equations in vacuum.
For a non-abelian $G$ the curvature in a local trivialization is $F = dA + \frac{1}{2}[A \wedge A]$, and the quadratic term makes the equations nonlinear.

Non-example: on a $3$-manifold, take the Chern–Simons action, which up to a constant factor is $\int_M \ip{A}{\wedge dA} + \frac{1}{3} \ip{A}{\wedge [A \wedge A]}$.
It is built from $A$ itself and not from $\abs{F_A}^2$, so it does not define a Yang–Mills theory.
Its field equations say that $A$ is flat.

In physics notation, with $\mathfrak{g}$ realized by Hermitian matrices and $\mathrm{tr}(T^a T^b) = \frac{1}{2}\delta^{ab}$, the Lorentzian Lagrangian density is $-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu}) = -\frac{1}{4} F^a_{\mu\nu} F^{a\,\mu\nu}$ after rescaling $A$ by the coupling.
