## Summary

Modified Newtonian dynamics (MOND) is a proposal by Mordehai Milgrom, published in 1983, to change Newton's law of gravity at very small accelerations.
Below an acceleration scale $a_0 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m/s}^2$, the gravitational acceleration is taken to be about $\sqrt{a_0 g_N}$ instead of the Newtonian value $g_N$.
It was designed to explain the flat rotation curves of galaxies without dark matter.
It is a non-relativistic rule, and relativistic versions belong to the [[alternatives to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stars far out in a spiral galaxy orbit faster than the visible matter can explain with [[Newton's law of gravitation|29WRN5J730]].
The usual answer is unseen dark matter.
MOND instead says that gravity itself behaves differently when the pull is extremely weak.
In that regime the pull falls off like $1/r$ instead of $1/r^2$, which makes the orbital speed constant far from the centre.
For example, a star whose Newtonian acceleration is $a_0/100$ gets an acceleration of about $a_0/10$ under MOND, ten times more.
