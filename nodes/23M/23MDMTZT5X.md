## Summary

An optical vortex is a point of a light field where the intensity is zero and the phase winds around it like a corkscrew.
Going once around the point, the phase changes by $2\pi \ell$ for an integer $\ell \ne 0$, the topological charge.
In three dimensions the vortex points form lines, and the wavefronts near them are helical.
[[Laguerre–Gaussian modes|8YGCTX64MT]] with $\ell \ne 0$ carry a vortex on their axis, and such beams carry orbital angular momentum.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The phase of a light wave tells where it is in its up-and-down cycle.
Around an optical vortex, the phase goes through a full cycle, or several, as one walks once around a point.
At the point itself the phase cannot have any single value, so the light must vanish there.
This makes a dark hole in the middle of the beam, like the eye of a storm.
The number of full cycles around the hole cannot change by small disturbances, so vortices are stable features of light.
