## Example

Let $T > 0$, $A > 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ with $4 \varepsilon A < 1$.
Let $u \colon [0, T] \to [0, \infty)$ be [[continuous|0X3WGX95FE]], with
$$u(0) \le A \qquad \text{and} \qquad u(t) \le A + \varepsilon\, u(t)^2 \quad \text{for each } t \in [0, T].$$
We prove by [[bootstrapping|YKA0ADKYFR]] that $u(t) \le 2A$ for each $t \in [0, T]$.
The bootstrap hypothesis is $u(t) \le 2A$, and the bootstrap conclusion is $u(t) < 2A$.

1. Let $S = \{ t \in [0, T] : u(t) \le 2A \}$.
2. Non-empty: $u(0) \le A < 2A$, so $0 \in S$.
3. Closed: $S$ is the preimage of the closed set $(-\infty, 2A]$ under the continuous map $u$, so $S$ is closed in $[0, T]$.
4. The bootstrap step: for $t \in S$, $0 \le u(t) \le 2A$ gives $u(t)^2 \le 4A^2$.
   The hypothesis then gives $u(t) \le A + 4\varepsilon A^2 = A(1 + 4\varepsilon A)$.
5. Since $4 \varepsilon A < 1$, we have $A(1 + 4 \varepsilon A) < 2A$, a strict gain.
6. Open: by step 5 and the continuity of $u$, there is $\delta > 0$ with $u(s) < 2A$ for all $s \in [0, T]$ with $\lvert s - t \rvert < \delta$.
   So a neighborhood of $t$ lies in $S$.
7. $S$ is non-empty, open and closed in the [[connected|0V032VWTHD]] interval $[0, T]$, so $S = [0, T]$ by the [[open-closed argument|1JJCDJB7FM]].
8. Hence $u(t) \le 2A$ for each $t$, and step 4 even gives $u(t) \le A(1 + 4\varepsilon A)$ for each $t$.

Check with $A = 1$ and $\varepsilon = \tfrac18$: $4 \varepsilon A = \tfrac12$, and the final bound is $u \le 1 + \tfrac12 = \tfrac32$.

## Remarks

The inequality $u \le A + \varepsilon u^2$ alone allows two ranges of values.
Let $r_\pm = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 4\varepsilon A}}{2\varepsilon}$ be the roots of $\varepsilon r^2 - r + A = 0$.
Then the inequality holds exactly when $u \le r_-$ or $u \ge r_+$.
Rationalizing gives $r_- = \frac{2A}{1 + \sqrt{1 - 4\varepsilon A}} \le 2A$, and $r_+ > \frac{1}{2\varepsilon} > 2A$.
So the forbidden zone $(r_-, r_+)$ contains $2A$, and continuity stops $u$ from crossing it.
For $A = 1$ and $\varepsilon = \tfrac18$, $r_- = 4 - 2\sqrt2 \approx 1.172$ and $r_+ = 4 + 2\sqrt2 \approx 6.828$.

Repeating the step sharpens the bound.
Put $b_0 = 2A$ and $b_{k+1} = A + \varepsilon b_k^2$.
Then $u \le b_k$ for each $k$, and the $b_k$ decrease to $r_-$.
Without continuity the result fails, as [[the non-example with a jump|5Y9D4SWRCF]] shows.
If $4\varepsilon A \ge 1$, the quadratic $\varepsilon r^2 - r + A$ is never negative, so the hypothesis holds for every value of $u$ and the argument proves nothing.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a quantity changes smoothly over time and starts at $1$.
A rule says: whenever the quantity is at most $2$, it is in fact at most $1.5$.
Then it can never reach $2$, because just before reaching $2$ it would be at most $1.5$, far from $2$.
Since it cannot jump, it stays at most $2$, and so at most $1.5$, for all time.

### L2 human

We run the iteration for $A = 1$ and $\varepsilon = \tfrac18$.

1. $b_0 = 2$ and $b_1 = 1 + \tfrac18 \cdot 4 = 1.5$.
2. $b_2 = 1 + \tfrac18 \cdot 2.25 = 1.28125$.
3. $b_3 = 1 + \tfrac18 \cdot 1.6416015625 \approx 1.2052$.
4. The sequence decreases, because $b \mapsto 1 + b^2/8$ is increasing on $[0, \infty)$ and $b_1 < b_0$.
5. It stays at or above $r_- = 4 - 2\sqrt2$, because $b \ge r_-$ gives $1 + b^2/8 \ge 1 + r_-^2/8 = r_-$.
6. So it converges to some $L$ with $L = 1 + L^2/8$ and $r_- \le L \le 2 < r_+$, hence $L = r_- \approx 1.1716$.

The iteration turns a crude bound into the sharp bound of the inequality.
Watch out: the iteration needs the first bound $u \le b_0$, and only the continuity argument provides it.
