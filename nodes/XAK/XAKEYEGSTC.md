## Summary

Metamaterial cloaking is the use of an engineered medium to make an object invisible to electromagnetic waves.
A shell of [[metamaterial|CQ9A45JF68]] around a region guides the waves around it.
An observer outside then sees the same field as if the region and the shell were empty space.
The main design tool is [[transformation optics|HBCRG5V2BD]], which maps a punctured ball onto a shell.
It is an example of non-uniqueness in [[electromagnetic scattering|8BQYB8H5CT]] and in inverse problems: different media can give the same measurements outside.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of water flowing around a rock in a stream: behind the rock the flow closes up again.
A cloak makes light flow around a hidden region in the same way, and then rejoin its original path.
To someone looking from outside, the light seems to have crossed empty space.
Ideal cloaks need extreme material parameters, so real cloaks work only for some frequencies or directions.
