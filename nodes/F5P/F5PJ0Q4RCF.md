## Summary

Spontaneous symmetry breaking happens when the laws of a system have a symmetry but its lowest-energy states do not.
The symmetry group then moves one ground state to other ground states, which form an [[orbit|0YQPZB5Q1G]] of the group.
When the broken symmetry is continuous, [[Goldstone's theorem|H028V9A14M]] predicts massless excitations.
In a gauge theory these are absorbed by the gauge fields through the [[Higgs mechanism|F985ZCF9SZ]].
Magnetization below the Curie temperature and the masses of the $W$ and $Z$ bosons are two standard instances.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a ball resting on top of a round hill shaped like the bottom of a wine bottle, with a ring-shaped valley around a central bump.
The landscape looks the same after any rotation about the centre, but the ball must roll down to some point of the valley, and that point breaks the rotation symmetry.
All points of the valley are equally good, and rotations move one of them to another.
Moving the ball along the valley costs no energy, which is the classical picture of a massless Goldstone mode.
