## Proof

We prove [[the finite model property theorem|XNPJK6B921]] by taking a countermodel from completeness and filtering it through the subformulas of $\varphi$, with a relation chosen to keep the frame class.

Let $\Lambda$ be one of the seven logics, let $\mathsf{C}$ be its frame class, and let $\Lambda \nvdash \varphi$.
Put $\Sigma = \operatorname{Sub}(\varphi)$, a finite set closed under subformulas with $n$ elements.

### The finite countermodel

1. By [[the completeness theorem|FXTCKYR3AQ]], there are a model $\mathfrak{M} = (W, R, V)$ on a frame of $\mathsf{C}$ and a world $w$ with $\mathfrak{M}, w \not\models \varphi$.
2. We choose the filtration by the logic.
   For $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{T} = \mathsf{KT}$, $\mathsf{D} = \mathsf{KD}$ and $\mathsf{B} = \mathsf{KTB}$ we take the smallest filtration $R^s$.
   For $\mathsf{K4}$ and $\mathsf{S4} = \mathsf{KT4}$ we take the transitive filtration $R^t$, and for $\mathsf{S5} = \mathsf{KT5}$ we take $R^e$.
3. Each choice is a filtration.
   For $R^s$ this is part 2 of [[the filtration theorem|T99DK51S0W]].
   For $R^t$ it is part 4, which uses that $R$ is transitive, and for $R^e$ it is part 6, which uses that $R$ is an equivalence relation.
4. The finite frame lies in $\mathsf{C}$.
   For $\mathsf{T}$ and $\mathsf{D}$, condition (min) carries reflexivity and seriality from $R$ to $R^s$, as on the [[filtration|6ZZSHAKG5F]] page.
   For $\mathsf{B}$, $R^s$ is reflexive by (min) and symmetric by part 5.
5. For $\mathsf{K4}$, $R^t$ is transitive by part 4, and for $\mathsf{S4}$ it is also reflexive by part 4.
   For $\mathsf{S5}$, $R^e$ is an equivalence relation by part 6, and for $\mathsf{K}$ every frame lies in $\mathsf{C}$.
6. By part 3, $\mathfrak{M}^f$ has at most $2^n$ worlds.
   By part 1, the filtration lemma, $\mathfrak{M}^f, [w] \not\models \varphi$, since $\varphi \in \Sigma$.
   This is part 1 of the theorem.

### The logic of the finite frames

7. Each frame of $\mathsf{C}$ validates $\Lambda$, by [[soundness|E87G6ZTMTN]] and [[common modal axiom schemata|FDFQFWPM4X]].
   So $\Lambda \subseteq \operatorname{Log}(\mathsf{C}_{\mathrm{fin}})$.
8. By step 6, every non-theorem of $\Lambda$ fails on a frame of $\mathsf{C}_{\mathrm{fin}}$, so $\operatorname{Log}(\mathsf{C}_{\mathrm{fin}}) \subseteq \Lambda$.
   This is part 2.

### The decision method

9. Up to isomorphism, there are finitely many frames with at most $2^n$ worlds, and for each one we can check the frame condition of $\mathsf{C}$.
10. On each such frame, the truth of $\varphi$ depends only on the values of the variables of $\varphi$, so there are finitely many valuations to test.
11. By steps 6 and 7, $\Lambda \vdash \varphi$ holds if and only if all these tests succeed.
    This is part 3.

## Idea

Completeness gives a countermodel on a frame of the class, and filtration makes it finite.
The one real choice is the relation on the quotient, which must keep the frame property of the logic.
For the transitive logics only the transitive filtration does so.

## Variants

One can filter the canonical model directly.
Then the classes are the maximal consistent subsets of $\Sigma \cup \{ \neg\psi : \psi \in \Sigma \}$.

Tableau methods give the same result with countermodels built from the formula, and they also give the better space bounds of [[the complexity theorem|1P2B0C7CQ4]].
