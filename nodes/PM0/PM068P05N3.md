## Definition

Let $\kappa$ be a finite two-dimensional oriented cell complex, with vertices $v$, edges $e$ and faces $f$, whose boundary $\partial \kappa$ is a graph.
A *spin foam* on $\kappa$ is an assignment of:

1. a nonzero spin $j_f$, labelling an irreducible representation of $SU(2)$ (see [[representation theory of SU(2)|9V6PRD1Z90]]), to each face $f$;
2. an intertwiner $\iota_e$ between the representations of the faces that meet at $e$ to each edge $e$.

The restriction to $\partial \kappa$ is then a [[spin network|B173ESJ4YX]].
A *spin foam model* assigns amplitudes $A_f(j_f)$, $A_e(j, \iota)$ and $A_v(j, \iota)$ to faces, edges and vertices.
It defines the amplitude of $\kappa$ with a fixed boundary spin network as
$$Z(\kappa) = \sum_{j, \iota} \prod_f A_f \prod_e A_e \prod_v A_v.$$
The sum runs over all spin foams on $\kappa$ that restrict to the given boundary spin network.

## Remarks

This is a discrete analogue of the gravitational [[path integral|1NYTFT5NPE]]: the sum over spins and intertwiners replaces the integral over geometries.
Example: the Ponzano–Regge model takes $\kappa$ dual to a triangulation of a three-manifold, so each vertex of $\kappa$ corresponds to a tetrahedron.
Its amplitudes are $A_f = (-1)^{2 j_f} (2 j_f + 1)$ and $A_v$ the $6j$-symbol of the six spins around the tetrahedron, up to sign conventions.
It is a model of three-dimensional Euclidean quantum gravity.
Non-example: a spin network is not a spin foam, since it is a one-dimensional graph with spins on edges, not a two-complex.
In four dimensions, the main models are the Barrett–Crane model and the EPRL model, whose vertex amplitudes use $SU(2) \times SU(2)$ or $SL(2, \C)$ representations.
