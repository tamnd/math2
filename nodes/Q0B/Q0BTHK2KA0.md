## Summary

A Hertz vector, or polarization potential, is one vector field that yields both the [[electric potential|SAEDXNM3Y1]] and the [[magnetic vector potential|8PEMR2J7EF]] by differentiation.
The potentials built from it satisfy the [[Lorenz gauge condition|YBHAVTWF82]] automatically, and Maxwell's equations reduce to one vector wave equation for the Hertz vector.
It is a convenient tool for radiation problems, especially for dipole antennas, where it is the field of the source dipole moment delayed in time.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Maxwell's equations involve two potentials, a scalar one and a vector one, linked by a gauge condition.
The Hertz vector is a trick that replaces both by one vector field.
The scalar potential is minus its divergence, up to a constant, and the vector potential is its time derivative, up to a constant.
For a small oscillating dipole, the Hertz vector is just the dipole moment, read at the delayed time and divided by $4\pi$ times the distance.
