## Summary

An $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple in a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] is a triple of elements $(h, e, f)$ that satisfy the bracket relations of the standard basis of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$.
Such a triple spans a subalgebra that is a homomorphic image of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, so the representation theory of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ applies to it.
In the structure theory of semisimple Lie algebras, each root gives an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple.
The Jacobson–Morozov theorem attaches such triples to nilpotent elements.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ has three basis elements $e, h, f$ with $[h, e] = 2e$, $[h, f] = -2f$ and $[e, f] = h$.
An $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple is a copy of these three elements inside a larger Lie algebra.
Finding many such copies lets one study a big Lie algebra through the well understood $\mathfrak{sl}_2$.
For example, in $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ the matrices $E_{12}$, $E_{11} - E_{22}$ and $E_{21}$ form one.
