## Example

Let $F$ be a field.
Each map below is a [[linear map|0RJF668P1T]].
For each map, we check additivity $T(u + v) = T(u) + T(v)$ and homogeneity $T(cu) = cT(u)$, and we compute one value.

1. Differentiation. Let $D \colon F[x] \to F[x]$ be the [[formal derivative|0EGN1E1JPB]] on the [[polynomial ring|0SDC934R52]], $D\left(\sum_k a_k x^k\right) = \sum_{k \ge 1} k a_k x^{k-1}$. The coefficient of $x^{k-1}$ in $D(p + q)$ is $k(a_k + b_k) = k a_k + k b_k$. The coefficient in $D(cp)$ is $k(c a_k) = c(k a_k)$. So $D$ is linear. For example, $D(x^3 + 2x) = 3x^2 + 2$.
2. Integration. Let $J \colon \R[x] \to \R[x]$ be $J(p)(x) = \int_0^x p(t)\, dt$, so $J(x^k) = x^{k+1}/(k+1)$. The integral of a sum is the sum of the integrals, and constants move out of the integral. So $J$ is linear. For example, $J(3x^2 + 1) = x^3 + x$.
3. Shifts. Let $F^{\N}$ be the [[space of sequences|XYTN19EPF2]] $(x_0, x_1, x_2, \dots)$ with entrywise operations. The right [[shift operator|MBK6AE6734]] is $R(x_0, x_1, \dots) = (0, x_0, x_1, \dots)$, and the left shift is $L(x_0, x_1, \dots) = (x_1, x_2, \dots)$. Each entry of $R(x + y)$ is an entry of $x + y$ or $0$, so $R(x + y) = R(x) + R(y)$. The same check gives $R(cx) = cR(x)$, and the same checks work for $L$. For example, $L(1, 2, 3, 4, \dots) = (2, 3, 4, \dots)$.
4. Evaluation. Fix $a \in F$ and let $\mathrm{ev}_a \colon F[x] \to F$ be $\mathrm{ev}_a(p) = p(a)$, as in the [[evaluation homomorphism|0FCCDXH97W]]. Then $(p + q)(a) = p(a) + q(a)$ and $(cp)(a) = c\,p(a)$. So $\mathrm{ev}_a$ is linear. For example, $\mathrm{ev}_2(x^2 - 3) = 1$.
5. Trace. The [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]] $\tr \colon M_n(F) \to F$ is the sum of the diagonal entries. The diagonal of $A + B$ is the sum of the diagonals, and the diagonal of $cA$ is $c$ times the diagonal of $A$. For example, $\tr \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = 5$.
6. Transpose. The [[transpose|09DXWYRD43]] $A \mapsto A^{\mathsf T}$ is a map $M_{m \times n}(F) \to M_{n \times m}(F)$. The $(i, j)$ entry of $(A + B)^{\mathsf T}$ is $a_{ji} + b_{ji}$, and the $(i, j)$ entry of $(cA)^{\mathsf T}$ is $c\,a_{ji}$. So the transpose is linear. For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf T}$ is the column with entries $1, 2, 3$.
7. Multiplication by a matrix. Fix $A \in M_{m \times n}(F)$ and let $T_A \colon F^n \to F^m$ be $T_A(x) = Ax$, the [[matrix–vector product|0PDVQEP0ZC]]. Then $A(x + y) = Ax + Ay$ and $A(cx) = c\,Ax$. For $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $T_A(1, 1) = (3, 1)$.

## Remarks

- Over a field of characteristic $0$, the [[kernel|0S23E6YE83]] of $D$ is the space of constant polynomials. Over $\mathbb{F}_p$, the kernel also contains $x^p$, since $D(x^p) = p x^{p-1} = 0$.
- On the space $P_n$ of [[polynomials of degree at most $n$|0JGZCDT5DN]], $D$ maps $P_n$ to $P_n$ and $D^{n+1} = 0$. In the basis $1, x, x^2$ of $P_2$, the [[matrix of $D$|0YQSGNYE3K]] is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
- $D \circ J$ is the identity on $\R[x]$, but $J \circ D$ is not, since $J(D(1)) = 0$. See [[the composites of differentiation and integration|~71de384c]].
- The shifts give the standard [[counterexample on infinite-dimensional spaces|~834be223]]: $R$ is injective but not surjective.
- By the [[theorem on matrix transformations|W6KB0J3SZZ]], item 7 gives every linear map $F^n \to F^m$.
- Over $\R$, differentiation is also linear on the space of differentiable functions, by the [[linearity of differentiation|0M0TKFRX2A]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Many operations in mathematics are linear maps.
Taking a derivative, taking an integral, adding up a diagonal and flipping a matrix all respect sums and scalar multiples.
The check is always the same: apply the map to a sum, and compare with the sum of the images.

### L2 human

In each item, the check is done one coordinate, coefficient or entry at a time.
This works because the operations in these spaces are also defined one coordinate at a time.
Items 1 and 2 show that a linear map can lower or raise the degree of a polynomial.
Items 3 and 4 show that the source and target can have different dimensions, or even infinite dimension.
The trap is to think that a linear map must be given by a matrix formula; items 1 to 6 are linear without any matrix in sight.
