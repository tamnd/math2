## Definition

Let $f_0 \ne 0$ be the Coriolis parameter and $g$ the gravitational acceleration.

1. For a single layer of fluid of mean depth $H > 0$ described by the [[shallow water equations|NT73Y95RY5]], the *barotropic Rossby radius of deformation* is $L_d = \sqrt{g H} / \abs{f_0}$.
2. For a fluid of depth $H$ with constant [[Brunt–Väisälä frequency|XQ82TE42FH]] $N > 0$, the *baroclinic Rossby radius of deformation* is $L_d = N H / \abs{f_0}$.

## Remarks

In both cases $L_d = c / \abs{f_0}$, where $c$ is a gravity wave speed: $c = \sqrt{gH}$ for surface waves and $c = N H$ for internal waves.
Many authors define the baroclinic radius of the $m$-th vertical mode as $N H / (m \pi \abs{f_0})$, so numerical values differ by a factor $\pi$ between texts.
For a two-layer fluid with reduced gravity $g'$ the same form $\sqrt{g' H} / \abs{f_0}$ is used.

Example: in the atmosphere $N \approx 10^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$, $H \approx 10^4$ m and $f_0 \approx 10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$, so $L_d \approx 1000$ km.
For the deep ocean, $H = 4000$ m gives $\sqrt{gH} \approx 200$ m/s and a barotropic radius of about $2000$ km, while the first baroclinic radius is only a few tens of kilometres.

Non-example: at the equator $f_0 = 0$ and the definition does not apply; an equatorial deformation radius $\sqrt{c / \beta}$, with $\beta$ the meridional gradient of $f$, is used instead.

In the shallow water equations linearised about rest, the steady geostrophically adjusted response to a localised disturbance decays exponentially with distance on the scale $L_d$.
Motions of horizontal scale much larger than $L_d$ are close to [[geostrophic balance|CZ1S1P465Y]].
