## Summary

A complete set of commuting observables is a family of pairwise commuting [[observables|DF5RN297GE]] whose joint eigenvalues determine a state vector up to a phase.
Measuring all of them at once fixes the pure state of the system completely.
Their common eigenvectors form an orthonormal basis labelled by the tuples of eigenvalues, which is how quantum numbers label states.
In operator-algebra terms, the observables generate a maximal abelian [[von Neumann algebra|PSFWQVZ0FS]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

One measurement often does not pin down a state, because several states can give the same value.
Adding more quantities that can be measured together, without disturbing each other, splits these ties.
When every combination of results belongs to at most one state, the set is complete.
For two spins, the vertical spin of the first and the vertical spin of the second form such a set: the four results $(\pm 1, \pm 1)$ correspond to the four basis states.
For the electron of a hydrogen atom without spin, the energy, the squared angular momentum and one component of the angular momentum form a complete set.
Their values give the usual quantum numbers.
