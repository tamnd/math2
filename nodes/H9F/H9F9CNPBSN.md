## Summary

An extensive property of a system is proportional to its size, like volume, mass, internal energy or entropy.
An intensive property does not depend on the size, like temperature, pressure or density.
The distinction organises the state variables of thermodynamics and leads to pairs of conjugate variables.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a bucket of water and pour half of it into a second bucket.
Each half has half the volume and half the mass: these are extensive.
Each half still has the same temperature and the same density as before: these are intensive.
Dividing one extensive quantity by another, such as mass by volume, gives an intensive one.
