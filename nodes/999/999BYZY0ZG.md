## Summary

The geoid is the level surface of the Earth's [[geopotential|5Y2BWNY6MF]] that best fits mean sea level.
It is the shape the ocean surface would take under gravity and rotation alone, extended under the continents.
It serves as the zero surface for heights above sea level.
It deviates from the [[reference ellipsoid|R9XX7TNJCH]] by up to about $100$ m, following the uneven distribution of mass inside the Earth.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine the oceans perfectly calm, without tides, winds or currents, and connected by canals under the land.
Their surface would be the geoid.
It is not a smooth ellipsoid: it is a little higher where there is extra mass below and lower where mass is missing.
These bumps are gentle, at most about $100$ m over thousands of kilometres.
A spirit level is always parallel to the local level surface of gravity, so surveyors measure heights relative to the geoid.
