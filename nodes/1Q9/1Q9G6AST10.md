## Summary

A composition algebra is an [[algebra over a field|1SENAHZW5A]] with a nondegenerate [[quadratic form|0RBQS1JJCR]] $N$ that is multiplicative: $N(xy) = N(x)N(y)$.
The model is $\C$ with $N(z) = \abs{z}^2$, where multiplicativity is the rule $\abs{zw}^2 = \abs{z}^2\abs{w}^2$.
By [[Hurwitz's theorem|WZ81H3S4KK]], a composition algebra with a unit has dimension $1$, $2$, $4$ or $8$.
Over a field of characteristic not $2$, every unital one is obtained from the field by repeated [[Cayley–Dickson construction|T9XW7RBEHY]].
The [[quaternions|0GE4DVQKR8]], the [[octonions|1HRMT3DSX2]] and their split forms are composition algebras, and so is the non-unital [[Okubo algebra|YYKQX7JX8K]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

[[Euler's four-square identity|08WTN6SCQ6]] says that a product of two sums of four squares is again a sum of four squares.
Such identities come from algebras whose "squared length" respects multiplication.
A composition algebra is exactly such an algebra.
There are very few of them, which explains why such bilinear product identities exist only for $1$, $2$, $4$ and $8$ squares.
