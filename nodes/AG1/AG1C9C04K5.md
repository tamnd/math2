## Statement

Let $F$ be a field.
Let $D$ be a [[division ring|0EA5BTA0CX]] that is a [[central simple algebra|5HRKJBAY2V]] of finite dimension over $F$, of [[degree|NNXSP5KKAC]] $n$.
Let $SK_1(D) = \set{d \in D^\times : \mathrm{Nrd}(d) = 1} / [D^\times, D^\times]$, where $\mathrm{Nrd}$ is the [[reduced norm|2FATVDAN2E]] and $[D^\times, D^\times]$ is the [[commutator subgroup|0ASGSTXSBW]] of $D^\times$.

1. If $n$ is squarefree, then $SK_1(D) = 1$.
2. If $F$ is an [[algebraic number field|MWBDFS2KJ5]], then $SK_1(D) = 1$.

In both cases, every element of $D$ of reduced norm $1$ is a product of commutators.

## Remarks

The group $SK_1(D)$ is the kernel of the reduced norm on $K_1(D) \cong D^\times/[D^\times, D^\times]$, so it is the group [[SK1|AAB05HS00Y]] of $D$.
Since $\mathrm{Nrd}$ is multiplicative with values in the commutative group $F^\times$, it is trivial on commutators, so the quotient makes sense.
The theorem is due to S. Wang (1950).
The hypotheses matter: Platonov later showed that $SK_1(D)$ can be nontrivial for suitable division algebras over other fields.
Example: for the Hamilton quaternions $\mathbb{H}$, of degree $2$, the elements of reduced norm $1$ are the unit quaternions.
Part 1 says each of them is a product of commutators.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a division algebra, the reduced norm plays the role of the determinant.
Products of commutators $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ always have reduced norm $1$.
Wang's theorem says that, in two common situations, the converse holds: reduced norm $1$ means product of commutators.
So in these cases the reduced norm detects everything about $K_1$ of the algebra.

### L2 human

The theorem computes $K_1(D)$: in both cases the reduced norm gives an injection $K_1(D) \to F^\times$.
It answers in these cases the question whether $SK_1$ of a central simple algebra is always trivial.
Platonov's counterexamples (1975) showed the answer is negative in general.
For the Hamilton quaternions one can also see the result directly: the unit quaternions form the [[perfect group|HWPXM2JSJ9]] $SU(2)$.
