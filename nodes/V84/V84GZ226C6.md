## Definition

Let a [[continuum body|X01DZTS224]] occupy an open set $\Omega \subseteq \R^3$ at some time, and let $S^2$ be the set of unit vectors of $\R^3$.
A *stress field* (in the sense of Cauchy) on $\Omega$ is a *traction field* $t \colon \Omega \times S^2 \to \R^3$ with the following meaning.
Let $x \in \Omega$, and take a smooth oriented surface through $x$ with unit normal $n$ at $x$.
The material on the side into which $n$ points exerts a force on the material on the other side.
The force per unit area of this action at $x$ is $t(x, n)$.
The *normal stress* on the surface is $t(x, n) \cdot n$, and the *shear stress* is the vector $t(x, n) - (t(x, n) \cdot n)\, n$.

## Remarks

In a fluid at rest with pressure $p$, the traction is $t(x, n) = -p(x)\, n$: the normal stress is $-p$ and the shear stress is $0$.
In a bar along the first axis under a tensile force $P$ spread uniformly over the cross-section area $A$, the traction on a cross-section with $n = e_1$ is $(P/A)\, e_1$.
Gravity is not a stress: it acts on the volume of the material, not across surfaces, and is described by a body force per unit mass.
The assumption that $t$ depends on the surface only through its normal $n$ is Cauchy's stress principle; Cauchy's stress theorem then shows that $t$ is linear in $n$.
Stress has the units of pressure, the pascal $\text{N}/\text{m}^2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stress describes the internal pulls and pushes in a material.
For each point and each direction of a cut, it gives the force per unit area across the cut.
