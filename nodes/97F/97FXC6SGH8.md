## Summary

Elastic-rebound theory explains how earthquakes happen on faults.
The motion of the plates slowly deforms the rocks on the two sides of a locked fault.
The rocks store elastic strain energy, as described by [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]].
When the [[stress|HXGCGHS070]] on the fault exceeds its frictional strength, the fault slips suddenly and the rocks spring back.
The stored energy is released as seismic waves and heat.
Harry Fielding Reid proposed the theory after the 1906 San Francisco earthquake.
It is the basis of the earthquake cycle and of models of recurrence.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Bend a ruler slowly between your hands: it stores energy and then snaps back when it slips.
A fault is like that: the two sides are stuck together while the ground far away keeps moving, so the rock near the fault bends.
When the bending becomes too large, the fault slips in a few seconds, the rock straightens, and the released energy shakes the ground.
For example, a fence built straight across a fault becomes curved over the years and is then offset in one jump during the earthquake.
