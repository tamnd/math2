## Example

A theorem of first-year analysis, with the weak base theory, proves that finitistic arithmetic is consistent, so it cannot be reduced to finitism.
Let $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$ be the arithmetic sentence "no number codes a proof of $0 = 1$ in [[primitive recursive arithmetic|WCXH72T8D8]]".
It has the form $\forall p\, \neg \mathrm{Prf}(p)$ with $\mathrm{Prf}$ primitive recursive, so it is a $\Pi^0_1$ sentence.
We show that $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{BW}$ proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$, where $\mathrm{BW}$ is the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem.
So $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{BW}$ is not $\Pi^0_2$-conservative over $\mathsf{PRA}$, in contrast with $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.

1. By the [[Bolzano-Weierstrass reversal|HV153Y95MQ]], $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{BW}$ proves every axiom of [[ACA0|K9XG0Z2N9V]].
2. $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ proves every instance of the induction scheme for arithmetical formulas.
   Arithmetical comprehension forms the set of numbers with the property, and the induction axiom applies to this set.
3. So $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ proves every theorem of [[Peano arithmetic|HACXBCM7SS]].
4. Lemma, cited: Peano arithmetic proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$.
5. By steps 1, 3 and 4, $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{BW} \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$.
6. $\mathsf{PRA}$ is consistent, recursively axiomatized and strong enough for the [[second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]].
   So $\mathsf{PRA} \nvdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$.
7. So $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{BW}$ proves a $\Pi^0_1$ sentence that $\mathsf{PRA}$ does not prove.
   The conclusion of the [[conservation theorem for WKL0|V609D9SZ05]] fails for it.

## Remarks

The example places the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem outside the part of analysis that $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ reduces to finitism.
The same argument applies to every theorem that implies $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, such as [[König's lemma|3P2J5NWCM1]].
The Heine-Borel theorem for $[0, 1]$, which is equivalent to $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, behaves differently: its $\Pi^0_2$ consequences are all provable in $\mathsf{PRA}$.
Watch out: $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$ is true, so the example does not cast doubt on the theorem.
It shows only that proofs with the theorem carry more than finitistic strength.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem says that every bounded sequence of numbers has a convergent subsequence.
Together with a weak base theory, it is strong enough to prove that primitive recursive arithmetic is consistent.
A finitistic system cannot prove its own consistency, so this theorem cannot be reduced to finitism in the way $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ is.

### L2 human

The typical use of this argument is a test for finitistic reducibility.
If a theorem implies $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, it proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PRA})$, and it is not reducible in the sense of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.
Compare the two compactness theorems of the unit interval.
Heine-Borel says that every open cover of $[0, 1]$ has a finite subcover, and it is reducible.
Bolzano-Weierstrass says that every sequence in $[0, 1]$ has a convergent subsequence, and it is not.
The trap is to think that the two must have the same strength, as they do in classical analysis with full comprehension.
