## Definition

Let $f \colon \R^n \to \R$ be continuously differentiable, with [[gradient|0ABK9V9Y64]] $\nabla f$, and let $\norm{\cdot}$ be the Euclidean [[norm|0V2WP9B1MF]].
Fix $\Delta_{\max} > 0$ and $\eta \in [0, 1/4)$.
A *trust region method* starts from $x_0 \in \R^n$ and a radius $\Delta_0 \in (0, \Delta_{\max}]$, and for $k \ge 0$:

1. chooses a [[symmetric|0VR671PHE5]] $n \times n$ matrix $B_k$ and the model $m_k(p) = f(x_k) + \nabla f(x_k)^T p + \tfrac12 p^T B_k p$;
2. computes a step $p_k$ that minimizes $m_k(p)$, exactly or approximately, subject to $\norm{p} \le \Delta_k$, with $m_k(p_k) < m_k(0)$;
3. computes the ratio $\rho_k = \dfrac{f(x_k) - f(x_k + p_k)}{m_k(0) - m_k(p_k)}$;
4. sets $x_{k+1} = x_k + p_k$ if $\rho_k > \eta$, and $x_{k+1} = x_k$ otherwise;
5. sets $\Delta_{k+1} = \Delta_k / 4$ if $\rho_k < 1/4$, sets $\Delta_{k+1} = \min(2\Delta_k, \Delta_{\max})$ if $\rho_k > 3/4$ and $\norm{p_k} = \Delta_k$, and sets $\Delta_{k+1} = \Delta_k$ otherwise.

## Remarks

The constants $1/4$, $3/4$, $2$ and $1/4$ in clause 5 are the usual textbook choices; other fixed constants of the same kind give the same theory.
With $B_k = \nabla^2 f(x_k)$ the method is a trust region Newton method.
The subproblem in clause 2 has a minimizer even when $B_k$ is indefinite, because the ball is compact.

Example: if $f$ is itself quadratic with Hessian $B_k$, then $m_k(p) = f(x_k + p)$ for all $p$.
So $\rho_k = 1$ at every step, every step is accepted, and the radius grows whenever the step reaches the boundary.

Non-example: a damped Newton step $x_k - \alpha_k \nabla^2 f(x_k)^{-1} \nabla f(x_k)$ chosen by a line search has no radius and no ratio test.
It fails clauses 2 to 5, so it is a line search method and not a trust region method.
