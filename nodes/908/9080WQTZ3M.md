## Summary

This topic is a part of [[linear algebra|GN8GYSDZFX]], and it studies the maps that respect the two operations of a vector space.
A linear map sends sums to sums and multiples to multiples, and the first pages show that rotations, derivatives, shifts and matrix products all have this form.
Each linear map comes with two subspaces, the kernel of vectors it kills and the image of values it takes.
A map is injective exactly when its kernel is zero, and it is fixed by its values on a basis, which can be chosen freely.
Counting dimensions leads to the rank-nullity theorem, which says that the rank plus the nullity is the dimension of the domain.
From there the story turns to isomorphisms, which show that a finite-dimensional space is determined by its dimension.
For an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional space, injective, surjective and bijective then mean the same thing, while the shifts show that this fails in infinite dimension.
The linear maps between two spaces form a space of their own, and the maps from a space to itself form an algebra, which in a basis is the algebra of square matrices.
The topic ends with semilinear maps, which twist the scalars by a field automorphism.
Later topics build on this one: [[direct sums and quotient spaces|QHKTJ89V6S]], [[matrices of linear maps|S6559D61BP]], [[dual spaces|9K7FFF26QR]] and [[eigenvalues|6R1D6QPY3S]].

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Linear map|0NRYHJPQG2]]: you learn the two rules and see maps that break them.
2. [[Examples of linear maps|0SS770NS8B]]: you meet derivatives, integrals, shifts, the trace and the transpose.
3. [[Matrix transformations of Fⁿ|W6KB0J3SZZ]]: you see why every linear map $F^n \to F^m$ is $x \mapsto Ax$.
4. [[Linear transformations of the plane|5BXHREC8C8]]: you draw rotations, reflections, shears and projections.
5. [[Composition of linear maps|00BKRZKMSS]]: you see composition become matrix multiplication.
6. [[Kernel of a linear map|0S23E6YE83]]: you find the subspace of vectors sent to $0$.
7. [[Image of a linear map|0WSFKKT6FF]]: you find the subspace of values.
8. [[Injectivity and the kernel|0BJBYCGMWE]]: you test injectivity by checking that the kernel is $\{0\}$.
9. [[Linear maps are determined by a basis|0R8X9XA83A]]: you build maps from free choices on a basis.
10. [[Rank of a linear map|0ZZXSBTR4J]]: you measure the image.
11. [[Nullity|0APCFW7639]]: you measure the kernel.
12. [[Rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]]: you add the two to get the dimension of the domain.
13. [[Linear isomorphism|0B8T10CE7N]]: you see why inverses of linear bijections are linear.
14. [[Classification of vector spaces by dimension|0VJBSK15SN]]: you see that $V \cong F^n$ when $\dim V = n$.
15. [[Injective, surjective and bijective endomorphisms|0912EEEVCT]]: you see that the three agree in finite dimension but not in general.
16. [[Space of linear maps|0X9MBPP2Z3]]: you treat $\Hom(V, W)$ as a vector space.
17. [[Dimension of Hom(V, W)|0Z9N1ER0W0]]: you compute $\dim \Hom(V, W) = \dim V \cdot \dim W$.
18. [[Endomorphism|0J7H0YBFDJ]]: you take powers of maps from a space to itself.
19. [[Algebra of endomorphisms|5JA4CYT1TB]]: you see $\End(V)$ as the algebra $M_n(F)$ with the scalars as center.
20. [[Semilinear and antilinear maps|BNPDDM95KF]]: you meet maps that twist the scalars.

### Prerequisites

The reader should know [[vector spaces, span, independence, bases and dimension|MVH97H8B2J]], in particular subspaces, bases and dimension.
The reader should also know [[matrices and the operations on them|Y1ESNQ2V52]], including matrix products, matrix units and the matrix ring.
Finally, the pages on kernels use [[systems of linear equations and row reduction|DXSXFVDMXQ]] to solve homogeneous systems.
