## Summary

This topic is a part of [[linear algebra|GN8GYSDZFX]], and it studies the maps that respect the two operations of a vector space.
A linear map sends sums to sums and multiples to multiples, and the first pages show that rotations, derivatives, shifts and matrix products all have this form.
Each linear map comes with two subspaces, the kernel of vectors it kills and the image of values it takes.
A map is injective exactly when its kernel is zero, and it is fixed by its values on a basis, which can be chosen freely.
Counting dimensions leads to the rank-nullity theorem, which says that the rank plus the nullity is the dimension of the domain.
From there the story turns to isomorphisms, which show that a finite-dimensional space is determined by its dimension.
For an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional space, injective, surjective and bijective then mean the same thing, while the shifts show that this fails in infinite dimension.
The linear maps between two spaces form a space of their own, and the maps from a space to itself form an algebra, which in a basis is the algebra of square matrices.
The topic ends with semilinear maps, which twist the scalars by a field automorphism.
Later topics build on this one: [[direct sums and quotient spaces|QHKTJ89V6S]], [[matrices of linear maps|S6559D61BP]], [[dual spaces|9K7FFF26QR]] and [[eigenvalues|6R1D6QPY3S]].

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Linear map|0NRYHJPQG2]]: the reader learns additivity and homogeneity, and sees maps that fail them.
2. [[Examples of linear maps|0SS770NS8B]]: the reader meets the derivative, the integral, shifts, evaluation, the trace and the transpose.
3. [[Matrix transformations of Fⁿ|W6KB0J3SZZ]]: the reader sees the maps $x \mapsto Ax$ and why every linear map $F^n \to F^m$ has this form.
4. [[Linear transformations of the plane|5BXHREC8C8]]: the reader draws rotations, reflections, shears and projections on the unit square.
5. [[Composition of linear maps|00BKRZKMSS]]: the reader learns that composites are linear and that composition matches matrix multiplication.
6. [[Kernel of a linear map|0S23E6YE83]]: the reader finds the vectors sent to $0$, a subspace of the domain.
7. [[Image of a linear map|0WSFKKT6FF]]: the reader finds the values of the map, a subspace spanned by the images of a spanning set.
8. [[Injectivity and the kernel|0BJBYCGMWE]]: the reader learns that a linear map is injective exactly when its kernel is $\{0\}$.
9. [[Linear maps are determined by a basis|0R8X9XA83A]]: the reader learns that values on a basis can be chosen freely and fix the map.
10. [[Rank of a linear map|0ZZXSBTR4J]]: the reader measures the image by its dimension.
11. [[Nullity|0APCFW7639]]: the reader measures the kernel by its dimension.
12. [[Rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]]: the reader learns that rank plus nullity equals the dimension of the domain.
13. [[Linear isomorphism|0B8T10CE7N]]: the reader studies bijective linear maps and why their inverses are linear.
14. [[Classification of vector spaces by dimension|0VJBSK15SN]]: the reader learns that $V \cong F^n$ for $\dim V = n$, through a choice of basis.
15. [[Injective, surjective and bijective endomorphisms|0912EEEVCT]]: the reader sees that the three agree in finite dimension, and why the shifts need infinite dimension.
16. [[Space of linear maps|0X9MBPP2Z3]]: the reader treats $\Hom(V, W)$ as a vector space under pointwise operations.
17. [[Dimension of Hom(V, W)|0Z9N1ER0W0]]: the reader computes $\dim \Hom(V, W) = \dim V \cdot \dim W$.
18. [[Endomorphism|0J7H0YBFDJ]]: the reader studies maps from a space to itself, their powers and the automorphisms.
19. [[Algebra of endomorphisms|5JA4CYT1TB]]: the reader sees $\End(V)$ as an associative algebra, isomorphic to $M_n(F)$, with the scalars as center.
20. [[Semilinear and antilinear maps|BNPDDM95KF]]: the reader meets maps that twist the scalars by a field automorphism.

### Prerequisites

The reader should know [[vector spaces, span, independence, bases and dimension|MVH97H8B2J]], in particular subspaces, bases and dimension.
The reader should also know [[matrices and the operations on them|Y1ESNQ2V52]], including matrix products, matrix units and the matrix ring.
Finally, the pages on kernels use [[systems of linear equations and row reduction|DXSXFVDMXQ]] to solve homogeneous systems.
