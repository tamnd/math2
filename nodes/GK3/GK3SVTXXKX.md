## Summary

The Fermi surface of a metal is the surface in wave-vector space that separates the occupied electron states from the empty ones at zero temperature.
It is the level set at the Fermi energy of the band functions of the [[electronic band structure|QP0TK2M29V]].
Electrons near the Fermi surface carry the low-energy physics of a metal: conduction, heat capacity and the response to magnetic fields.
Insulators have no Fermi surface, because their Fermi energy lies in a gap.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Electrons fill the available states from the lowest energy up, one per state and spin, as [[Fermi–Dirac statistics|FM6XWC4BST]] require at zero temperature.
In wave-vector space the filled states form a solid region, the Fermi sea.
The Fermi surface is the boundary of this region.
For free electrons the Fermi sea is a ball and the Fermi surface is a sphere of radius $k_F$.
In real metals the periodic lattice bends and cuts this sphere into more complicated shapes.
