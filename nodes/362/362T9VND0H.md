## Summary

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] and $M$ a $\mathfrak{g}$-module.
The Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of $\mathfrak{g}$ with coefficients in $M$ is a cochain complex of alternating multilinear maps from $\mathfrak{g}$ to $M$.
Its differential is built from the action and the bracket.
Its cohomology is the [[Lie algebra cohomology|69NARSMQCM]] $H^*(\mathfrak{g}, M)$.
It gives an explicit way to compute this cohomology.
For the Lie algebra of a compact connected Lie group, with trivial coefficients, it computes the de Rham cohomology of the group.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a cochain of degree $n$ as a machine that takes $n$ elements of the Lie algebra and returns an element of the module.
Its output changes sign when two inputs are swapped.
The differential turns an $n$-input machine into an $(n+1)$-input machine, using the bracket of inputs and the action of inputs on outputs.
Applying the differential twice always gives zero, so one can form cohomology.
In degree $0$, a cochain is just an element $m$ of the module, and its differential is the map $x \mapsto x \cdot m$.
So the elements with zero differential in degree $0$ are the invariant elements.
