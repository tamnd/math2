## Summary

$f(R)$ gravity is a family of [[alternatives to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]].
In it the scalar curvature $R$ in the [[Einstein–Hilbert action|JPE3A1SN7V]] is replaced by a function $f(R)$.
The choice $f(R) = R - 2\Lambda$ gives general relativity with a cosmological constant.
Other choices have been used to model cosmic inflation and the accelerated expansion of the universe without a cosmological constant.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Einstein's equations come from the simplest possible action, which just adds up the curvature $R$ over spacetime.
$f(R)$ gravity asks what happens if one adds up some other function of the curvature instead, such as $R + R^2/(6M^2)$ for a constant $M$.
Depending on the choice, the new terms matter only where the curvature is very large or very small.
So they can change the early universe or the far future while leaving the Solar System almost untouched.
