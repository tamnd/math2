## Definition

Let $\R^{2n}$ be the [[phase space|CPEC2NW2KF]] with coordinates $(q_1, \dots, q_n, p_1, \dots, p_n)$ and [[Poisson bracket|2S0MXDT4YH]] $\{f, g\} = \sum_j \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial q_j}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_j} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial p_j}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_j} \right)$, and let $\hbar > 0$.
*Canonical quantization* assigns:

1. the [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] $L^2(\R^n)$ as the space of states;
2. to $q_j$ the position operator $(Q_j \psi)(x) = x_j \psi(x)$, and to $p_j$ the momentum operator $P_j \psi = -i\hbar\, \partial \psi / \partial x_j$, both [[self-adjoint|0NEGN5CRSC]] and both mapping the [[Schwartz space|CG9JEATW3Q]] $\mathcal{S}(\R^n)$ into itself;
3. to the constant function $1$ the identity operator $I$.

On $\mathcal{S}(\R^n)$ these operators satisfy the canonical commutation relations
$$[Q_j, Q_k] = 0, \qquad [P_j, P_k] = 0, \qquad [Q_j, P_k] = i\hbar\, \delta_{jk}\, I,$$
which match $\{q_j, q_k\} = \{p_j, p_k\} = 0$ and $\{q_j, p_k\} = \delta_{jk}$ under the rule $\{f, g\} \mapsto \frac{1}{i\hbar}[\,\cdot\,, \cdot\,]$.
A further observable $f(q, p)$ is quantized as an operator $f(Q, P)$ after the choice of an ordering rule.

## Remarks

Example: for $n = 1$ and $H(q, p) = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V(q)$ there is no ordering ambiguity, since no term mixes $q$ and $p$.
The quantized Hamiltonian is the Schrödinger operator $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + V(x)$.

Non-example: the naive quantization of $f = qp$ as $QP$ is not symmetric, because $(QP)^* \supseteq PQ = QP - i\hbar I$.
The symmetric choice $\frac12(QP + PQ)$ is the one given by [[Weyl quantization|RFNGJ09WDR]].

By the Stone–von Neumann theorem, any irreducible representation of the canonical commutation relations in their exponentiated (Weyl) form is unitarily equivalent to the one in clause 2.
