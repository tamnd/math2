## Summary

Twistor theory is a programme proposed by Roger Penrose in 1967 that replaces spacetime points by objects in a complex space, twistor space.
Points of complexified compactified [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]] correspond to lines in the [[complex projective space|7GJ4STCNNM]] $\mathbb{CP}^3$.
Light rays correspond to points of a real hypersurface of $\mathbb{CP}^3$.
Under this correspondence, solutions of the massless field equations become holomorphic data on twistor space.
Twistor methods are used in differential geometry, in integrable systems and in the computation of scattering amplitudes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In twistor theory the basic objects are light rays, not points.
A point of spacetime is recovered as the set of all light rays through it, and in twistor space this set is a complex line.
Hard equations of physics, such as the wave equation, then become questions about holomorphic functions, which are rigid and easy to handle.
For example, every solution of the wave equation on a suitable region can be written as a contour integral of a holomorphic function on twistor space.
