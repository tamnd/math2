## Definition

Let $Q$ be a [[risk-neutral measure|858BHBY2BD]] and let $r$ be a short rate with default-free bond prices $P(t, T)$, as in a [[short-rate model|7TZ87AS1MW]].
Let $N$ be a [[Poisson process|0MTNR28K60]] with intensity $\lambda > 0$ under $Q$, independent of $r$.
In the *Jarrow–Turnbull model* the default time of a firm is the first jump time $\tau$ of $N$.
A defaultable zero-coupon bond with maturity $T$ pays $1$ at $T$ if $\tau > T$.
If $\tau \le T$ it pays a fixed recovery fraction $\delta \in [0, 1)$ at time $T$.

## Remarks

The default time $\tau$ has the [[exponential distribution|0E99F51GVB]] with rate $\lambda$, so $Q(\tau > T \mid \tau > t) = e^{-\lambda (T - t)}$.
By independence, for $t < \tau$ the defaultable bond price is $P(t, T) \big( \delta + (1 - \delta) e^{-\lambda (T - t)} \big)$.
Example: with $\delta = 0$, $\lambda = 0.02$ and $T - t = 5$ the price is $P(t, T) e^{-0.1}$.
Non-example: a model in which default happens when the firm's asset value falls below its debt is structural.
It is not of Jarrow–Turnbull type, since its default time is announced by the asset path.
Later work replaces the constant $\lambda$ by a random intensity process.
