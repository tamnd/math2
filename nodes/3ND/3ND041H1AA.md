## Summary

The ecliptic coordinate system is the [[celestial coordinate system|YT0G7RE2GM]] built on the ecliptic, the apparent yearly path of the Sun on the sky.
Its coordinates are ecliptic latitude and ecliptic longitude, counted from the March equinox.
It suits the study of the Sun, the Moon and the planets, whose orbits lie close to the ecliptic plane.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Over a year the Sun seems to travel once around the sky along a great circle, the ecliptic.
Using that circle as the equator gives the ecliptic coordinates.
The Sun always has ecliptic latitude about $0$, and its longitude grows by about one degree per day.
The planets stay within a few degrees of the ecliptic, so their latitudes are small numbers.
