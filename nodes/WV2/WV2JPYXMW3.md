## Summary

Bayesian search theory applies [[Bayes' theorem|0NM6E9VW04]] to the search for a lost object.
The searcher keeps a probability for each possible location, and after each unsuccessful search updates these probabilities, lowering the one of the searched area.
The next search is directed where it is most likely to succeed for its cost.
It has been used to find lost ships, submarines and aircraft.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A ship is lost at sea, and experts divide the sea into squares, giving each square a chance that the ship is there.
Searching a square may miss the ship even if it is there, for instance in deep water.
After a search that finds nothing, the chance for that square goes down and the chances for all other squares go up.
The searchers then always go where the chance of finding the ship, per hour spent, is highest.
