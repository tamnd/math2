## Summary

A Rossby wave, or planetary wave, is a large-scale wave in a rotating fluid.
Its restoring mechanism is the variation of the Coriolis parameter with latitude.
Fluid parcels that move north or south must change their relative [[vorticity|B3SXT9TWFA]] to conserve their potential vorticity.
This sets up an oscillation.
Their phase always travels westward relative to the mean flow.
They shape the meanders of the jet stream and the slow adjustment of ocean basins.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The spin that the Earth gives to a column of air is larger near the poles than near the equator.
When a column drifts north, it must spin less on its own to keep its total spin, and when it drifts south it must spin more.
These changes push neighbouring air back toward where it came from, making a wave.
The big loops of the jet stream on weather maps are Rossby waves, and they slowly drift westward relative to the wind that carries them.
