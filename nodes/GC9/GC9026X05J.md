## Statement

Let $f \colon A \to B$ be a surjective [[ring homomorphism|0VTH8KPCG2]] whose kernel $I$ is a [[nilpotent ideal|C2H26BYWHE]].
Write $K_n(A, I)$ for the [[relative K-groups|GYK9TGKG77]] and $HC_n(A, I)$ for the relative [[cyclic homology|676K167XS8]] groups over $\Z$.
Then for every $n$ there is a natural isomorphism
$$K_n(A, I) \otimes \Q \cong HC_{n-1}(A, I) \otimes \Q.$$

## Remarks

The isomorphism is given by a relative Chern character, a refinement of the Dennis trace map.
The proof works with simplicial rings and reduces to the rational homology of general linear groups.
Example: let $A = \Q[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ and $I = (\varepsilon)$.
Then $K_1(A, I) = 1 + I$, and $1 + a\varepsilon \mapsto a$ identifies it with $\Q$, while $HC_0(A, I) = I \cong \Q$, as the theorem predicts.
Nilpotence is needed: for $A = \Q[t]$ and $I = (t)$, homotopy invariance gives $K_n(\Q[t], (t)) = 0$, but $HC_0(\Q[t], (t)) = t\Q[t]$ is not zero.

## Explanations

### L1 human

K-groups are hard to compute, and cyclic homology is much easier, since it comes from explicit chain complexes.
The theorem says that, for small changes of a ring, the two theories see the same change, once torsion is ignored.
A small change means passing to a quotient by a nilpotent ideal, such as setting $\varepsilon = 0$ when $\varepsilon^2 = 0$.
So the rational K-theory of rings like $\Q[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ can be computed by linear algebra.

### L2 human

The theorem is the rational form of a general principle: relative K-theory for nilpotent extensions is controlled by trace invariants.
Its integral form is the Dundas–Goodwillie–McCarthy theorem, where cyclic homology is replaced by topological cyclic homology.
For $\Q$-algebras the relative K-groups of a nilpotent ideal are already rational vector spaces, so there the theorem computes them exactly.
