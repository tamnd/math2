## Summary

A solvable Lie algebra is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] whose derived series reaches $0$.
The derived series is the chain of iterated brackets $\mathfrak g \supseteq [\mathfrak g, \mathfrak g] \supseteq [\,[\mathfrak g, \mathfrak g], [\mathfrak g, \mathfrak g]\,] \supseteq \cdots$.
It is the Lie algebra analogue of a [[solvable group|0FXJ7YFMMX]].
Algebras of upper triangular matrices are the model examples.
By Lie's theorem, over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, every solvable algebra of matrices is of this kind.
Over a field of characteristic $0$, every finite-dimensional Lie algebra is built from a solvable part, its radical, and a semisimple part.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A solvable Lie algebra can be taken apart in finitely many steps, each step peeling off a commutative layer.
Take the upper triangular $2 \times 2$ matrices.
Brackets of such matrices are strictly upper triangular, and brackets of strictly upper triangular $2 \times 2$ matrices are zero.
So two steps reach $0$, and this algebra is solvable.
