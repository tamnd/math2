## Summary

The Prandtl boundary layer equations describe the thin layer near a wall where a fluid of small viscosity is slowed down by the [[no-slip condition|DF0M8YNQ3D]].
Ludwig Prandtl derived them in 1904 from the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] by a scaling argument in the thin layer.
They connect the inviscid outer flow, governed by the [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]], to the wall.
They are the foundation of [[boundary layer|YCFPD0GE5G]] theory and of matched asymptotics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air flowing past a wing is almost frictionless, except in a very thin layer right next to the surface.
In that layer the speed drops from the outside value to zero at the surface.
Prandtl noticed that the layer is so thin that the pressure across it is the same as just outside, and that friction acts only across the layer, not along it.
This turns the full flow equations into simpler ones for the layer alone.
