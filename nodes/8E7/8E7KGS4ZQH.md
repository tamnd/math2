## Summary

Normal moveout is the increase of the arrival time of a reflection with the distance between source and receiver.
For a flat reflector below a layer of constant speed, the reflection time is a [[hyperbola|0WN5G117YC]] in the offset.
Its curvature depends on the speed.
Correcting for normal moveout flattens the reflection across all offsets, which allows traces to be summed in common midpoint stacking.
Measuring the moveout is also the main way to estimate seismic velocities from reflection data.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A reflection travels down to a layer and back up.
When the source and the receiver are far apart, the path is longer and the echo arrives later than when they are at the same place.
This delay is the normal moveout.
Take a layer $1$ km deep in rock of speed $2$ km per second.
The echo returns after $1$ second when source and receiver coincide, and after about $1.41$ seconds when they are $2$ km apart.
