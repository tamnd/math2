## Summary

Homological algebra studies chain complexes, their homology, and the derived functors that measure how far a functor is from being exact.
A chain complex is a sequence of modules and maps in which the composite of two consecutive maps is zero.
K-theory attaches abelian groups to rings, schemes, spaces and categories, starting with the Grothendieck group of projective modules or of vector bundles, and it connects algebra with topology and number theory.
Homological algebra took its modern form in the book of Cartan and Eilenberg of 1956, and Grothendieck introduced the Grothendieck group in the 1950s for his work on the Riemann-Roch theorem.
This topic collects the homological algebra and K-theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Category theory, commutative algebra, algebraic topology, homotopy theory and operator algebras have their own topics.
