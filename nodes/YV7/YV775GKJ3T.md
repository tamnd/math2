## Summary

Crystal plasticity is the continuum theory of [[plasticity|K8BBMTN0KV]] for single crystals and for the grains of a polycrystal.
Plastic flow is not isotropic: it happens only by shear on a finite set of slip systems.
Each slip system is given by a slip plane and a slip direction of the crystal lattice.
A slip system becomes active when the shear stress resolved on it reaches a critical value.
The theory links the atomic lattice to macroscopic behaviour, such as anisotropy and the development of texture.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A crystal is a regular stack of atomic planes, and it deforms permanently by planes sliding over each other, like cards in a deck.
Only some planes and some directions within them allow easy sliding.
Each such pair is a slip system, and the crystal yields when the push along one of them gets large enough.
For example, copper and aluminium crystals have twelve slip systems, made of the four close-packed planes with three close-packed directions in each.
