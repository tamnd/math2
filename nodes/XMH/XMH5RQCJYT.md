## Summary

Cosmological perturbation theory studies small departures from a homogeneous and isotropic universe.
It writes the metric and the matter content as a [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker|ECJMS4VDTF]] background plus small perturbations.
It then solves the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] to first order in the perturbations.
It is the basic tool to explain how galaxies and the anisotropies of the cosmic microwave background grew from tiny initial fluctuations.
A main difficulty is the choice of coordinates, called the gauge, which changes the perturbations without changing the physics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On very large scales the universe looks the same everywhere, but on smaller scales it has clumps such as galaxies and voids.
Cosmological perturbation theory treats these clumps as small ripples on top of a smooth expanding universe.
Picture a calm sea with small waves: one first describes the calm sea, then the waves as small corrections to it.
For example, a region that is $0.001\%$ denser than average pulls in matter by gravity, and the theory computes how fast its density contrast grows as the universe expands.
While the ripples stay small, the equations become linear and each Fourier mode evolves on its own.
