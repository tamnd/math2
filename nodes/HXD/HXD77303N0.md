## Definition

Let $A$ be a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] over a commutative ring $R$.
$A$ is *power-associative* if for each $x \in A$ the [[subalgebra|7CHG6SD365]] of $A$ generated by $x$ is associative.

## Remarks

Equivalently, any two products of $n$ copies of $x$, with any bracketing, are equal for every $n$; their common value is written $x^n$, and then $x^m x^n = x^{m+n}$.
Example: every [[alternative algebra|N8K9SSVMRN]] is power-associative, by [[Artin's theorem|PBRCDXKQFR]].
Example: an [[anticommutative algebra|CCZ0W5WGWX]] is power-associative, since $xx = 0$ makes every product of two or more copies of $x$ zero.
Non-example: let $A$ have basis $e, f$ over a field, with $ee = f$, $ef = e$ and $fe = ff = 0$.
Then $(ee)e = 0$ and $e(ee) = e$, so the subalgebra generated by $e$ is not associative.
