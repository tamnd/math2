## Summary

Big Bang nucleosynthesis is the production of the light nuclei, deuterium, helium-3, helium-4 and a little lithium-7, in the first minutes after the Big Bang.
Its predictions depend mainly on one number, the ratio of baryons to photons.
About one quarter of the ordinary mass is in helium-4.
The agreement of predicted and observed abundances is a central test of the hot Big Bang model.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Very early, the universe was too hot for atomic nuclei to hold together.
As it expanded and cooled, protons and neutrons started to stick together, first into deuterium and then mostly into helium.
This lasted only a few minutes, because the universe soon became too cool and thin for more reactions.
Almost all the neutrons ended up in helium-4, so the amount of helium is fixed by how many neutrons there were.
With about one neutron for every seven protons, helium makes up about a quarter of the mass.
