## Example

The *Milne model* is the [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric|ECJMS4VDTF]] with $k = -1$ and scale factor $a(t) = t$.
It has no matter and no cosmological constant:
$$ds^2 = -dt^2 + t^2 \left( d\chi^2 + \sinh^2\chi \, d\Omega^2 \right), \qquad t > 0,$$
in units with $c = 1$.

Check with the [[Friedmann equations|PPHFS2P4G4]]: $\dot a / a = 1/t$, so the first equation reads $1/t^2 = -k/a^2 = 1/t^2$, and the second reads $\ddot a / a = 0$, both with $\rho = p = \Lambda = 0$.

## Remarks

The Milne model is flat: it is the interior of the future [[light cone|JZ9ADDZX8D]] of a point $O$ in [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]].
On that region, with Minkowski coordinates $(T, X)$ centred at $O$, put $t = \sqrt{T^2 - \abs{X}^2}$, the proper time from $O$.
The slices $t = \text{const}$ are the hyperboloids $T^2 - \abs{X}^2 = t^2$, and each carries the metric $t^2$ times the metric of hyperbolic space, as in the [[hyperboloid model|809288G50Y]].
The comoving observers are the inertial observers that pass through $O$, so the "expansion" is ordinary motion of test particles away from a common event.
Its Hubble parameter is $H = 1/t$ and its deceleration parameter is $q = 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture an explosion of tiny test particles from one point in empty flat spacetime, each flying off at its own constant speed.
Let each particle use its own clock to define "now".
Then the set of particles at the same "now" is a curved hyperbolic space, and that space grows in proportion to time.
This is the Milne model: an expanding universe that is really just empty space seen in unusual coordinates.
It is useful as a reference model that expands at a constant rate.
