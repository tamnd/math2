## Statement

Let $g > 0$, $h > 0$, let $D \subset \R^d$ be a bounded horizontal domain and $[t_0, t_1]$ a time interval.
For smooth functions $\eta(x, t) > -h$ on $D \times [t_0, t_1]$ and $\phi(x, z, t)$ on $\{(x, z, t) : -h \le z \le \eta(x, t)\}$, define the action
$$\mathcal{L}(\phi, \eta) = -\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\int_D \int_{-h}^{\eta(x, t)} \left(\partial_t\phi + \tfrac{1}{2}\abs{\nabla\phi}^2 + gz\right) dz\,dx\,dt,$$
where $\nabla$ is the gradient in $(x, z)$.
Consider variations of $\phi$ and $\eta$ that vanish at $t = t_0$, at $t = t_1$ and on the lateral boundary $\partial D \times \R$.
Then $(\phi, \eta)$ is a stationary point of $\mathcal{L}$ for all such variations if and only if the following hold.
These are the equations of the [[water wave problem|R9WC0Z7Z69]] with flat bottom at $z = -h$.

1. $\Delta\phi = 0$ for $-h < z < \eta$.
2. $\partial_z\phi = 0$ at $z = -h$.
3. $\partial_t\eta + \nabla_x\phi\cdot\nabla_x\eta = \partial_z\phi$ at $z = \eta$.
4. $\partial_t\phi + \tfrac{1}{2}\abs{\nabla\phi}^2 + g\eta = 0$ at $z = \eta$.

## Remarks

By the Bernoulli equation the integrand is minus the pressure divided by the density.
So the action is the integral of the pressure over the fluid and over time, divided by the density.
Varying $\eta$ changes only the upper limit of the inner integral.
Its first variation is the left side of the dynamic condition $\partial_t\phi + \tfrac{1}{2}\abs{\nabla\phi}^2 + g\eta = 0$ on the surface.
Varying $\phi$ and integrating by parts in $(x, z, t)$ gives a volume term $\Delta\phi$, a bottom term $\partial_z\phi$ and a surface term.
The surface term is the kinematic condition, and each of the three terms must vanish separately.
J. C. Luke published the principle in 1967.
With $\eta$ and the surface value of $\phi$ as variables, the principle leads to Zakharov's Hamiltonian formulation of water waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Many laws of physics say that nature makes some quantity stationary, like a ball rolling along the path of least action.
Luke found such a quantity for water waves: a single integral over the water whose stationary points are exactly the motions of waves.
All the equations of the wave problem, including the conditions at the free surface, follow from this one integral.

### L2 human

The principle is a tool of the [[calculus of variations|PQ25NHV0KS]] applied to a free boundary problem.
It is the starting point of Whitham's averaged variational method for slowly varying wave trains.
Many approximate models also come from it, by restricting $\phi$ to a simple form in $z$ before varying.
Such restrictions preserve the variational structure, and so conservation laws of energy and momentum carry over to the approximate equations.
