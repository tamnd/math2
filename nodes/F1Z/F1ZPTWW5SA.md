## Summary

Chandrasekhar's white dwarf equation is the [[ordinary differential equation|0NT64JV96A]] for a star held up by the pressure of a cold, degenerate electron gas.
It allows the electrons to move at relativistic speeds.
It replaces the power law of a [[polytrope|9YTGKNPMCP]] by the exact pressure of such a gas.
In the low-density limit it becomes the [[Lane–Emden equation|ANRZZMH2Q0]] of index $3/2$, and in the high-density limit the one of index $3$.
Its solutions give the relation between mass and radius of white dwarfs and the [[Chandrasekhar limit|J8C4PN2R4H]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A white dwarf is the dense remnant of a star like the Sun.
It does not burn fuel; it is held up because electrons packed very closely resist being squeezed, a quantum effect.
When the star is heavier, the electrons are squeezed harder and move close to the speed of light, and their resistance becomes weaker in relative terms.
Chandrasekhar's equation describes this whole range at once, from light white dwarfs to the heaviest ones.
