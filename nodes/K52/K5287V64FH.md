## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a finite [[alphabet|RABRP2KG81]] and let $n \ge 1$.
The *Hamming distance* of two [[strings|1P6EP01HZF]] $x = x_1 \cdots x_n$ and $y = y_1 \cdots y_n$ in $\Sigma^n$ is
$$d_H(x, y) = \abs{\set{i \in \set{1, \dots, n} : x_i \ne y_i}}.$$

## Remarks

Example: $d_H(10110, 11100) = 2$, since the strings differ exactly in positions $2$ and $4$.

Non-example: the strings $101$ and $10$ have no Hamming distance, because the definition needs strings of the same length.
Their edit distance, which also allows insertions and deletions, is $1$.

The function $d_H$ is a [[metric|063W5JMY85]] on $\Sigma^n$.
The triangle inequality holds because each position where $x$ and $z$ differ is a position where $x$ and $y$ differ or where $y$ and $z$ differ.
For binary strings, $d_H(x, y)$ is the number of ones in $x \oplus y$, the coordinatewise sum modulo $2$.
In Mathlib the notion is `hammingDist`.
