## Example

An ideal *Bose gas* is a system of non-interacting identical bosons obeying [[Bose–Einstein statistics|R5HWSRAK4H]].
Take spinless bosons of mass $m$ in a box of volume $V$ in three dimensions, at temperature $T$ and chemical potential $\mu < 0$.
Write $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$, $z = e^{\beta \mu} \in (0, 1)$ for the fugacity, and
$$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 \pi m k_B T}}$$
for the thermal wavelength, where $h$ is the Planck constant.
In the [[thermodynamic limit|FPYQWE8X12]], replacing the sum over states by an integral over momenta gives the density
$$n = \frac{1}{h^3} \int_{\R^3} \frac{d^3 p}{z^{-1} e^{\beta \abs{p}^2 / 2m} - 1} = \frac{g_{3/2}(z)}{\lambda^3}, \qquad g_s(z) = \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{z^k}{k^s}.$$
Check: expand $1/(z^{-1}e^{x} - 1) = \sum_{k \ge 1} z^k e^{-kx}$ and use the Gaussian integral $\frac{1}{h^3}\int e^{-k\beta \abs{p}^2/2m} d^3 p = k^{-3/2} \lambda^{-3}$.
The function $g_s$ is the [[polylogarithm|96CHDZKGQ0]] $\operatorname{Li}_s$.

## Remarks

The function $g_{3/2}$ is increasing on $(0, 1]$ with maximum $g_{3/2}(1) = \zeta(3/2) \approx 2.612$, a value of the [[Riemann zeta function|1XWBY4N0VT]].
So the integral can account for a density of at most $\zeta(3/2)/\lambda^3$.
At fixed density $n$ this bound fails below the temperature
$$T_c = \frac{2 \pi \hbar^2}{m k_B} \left( \frac{n}{\zeta(3/2)} \right)^{2/3}.$$
Below $T_c$ the missing particles collect in the lowest state: this is Bose–Einstein condensation.
For $z \ll 1$, $g_{3/2}(z) \approx z$ and the gas behaves like a classical ideal gas.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Bose gas is a cloud of particles that are happy to share states.
As the gas cools, the thermal wavelength $\lambda$ grows, and the excited states can hold fewer and fewer particles.
When $n \lambda^3$ reaches about $2.612$, the excited states are full, and any extra particles pile into the single lowest state.
Gases of ultracold alkali atoms behave much like this.
