## Summary

Coulomb stress transfer is the change of [[stress|KG2XWEMJ30]] that an earthquake causes on nearby faults, measured by the change of the Coulomb failure stress.
The slip of one fault deforms the surrounding crust elastically and so loads some faults and unloads others.
A positive change brings a fault closer to failure in the sense of the [[Mohr–Coulomb criterion|Q5CF1Z6179]], and a negative change moves it away.
It is used to explain where aftershocks occur and how one earthquake can trigger the next one on a nearby fault.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a fault slips, it pushes and pulls the rock around it.
Some nearby faults get pushed in the direction they would slip anyway, so they are closer to breaking; others are pushed the other way and become safer.
Seismologists compute this change for each nearby fault and draw maps with "lobes" of increased stress where aftershocks are more likely.
For example, if a nearby fault gains $0.1$ megapascal of shear stress in its slip direction and its clamping does not change, its Coulomb stress rises by $0.1$ megapascal.
