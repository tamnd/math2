## Summary

A phylogenetic tree is a [[tree|062D9KXJB1]] whose leaves are labelled by species, genes or other taxa, and which represents their evolutionary relationships.
Internal vertices stand for common ancestors, and in a rooted tree the root is the common ancestor of all the taxa.
Edges may carry lengths, such as time or expected number of substitutions.
Phylogenetic trees are the basic objects of phylogenetics, the study of the history of life.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A phylogenetic tree is a family tree of species.
The species we see today sit at the tips, and each branching point is an ancestor whose descendants split into separate lines.
For example, take a rooted tree on human, chimpanzee and gorilla in which human and chimpanzee form a pair.
It says that these two share an ancestor not shared by the gorilla.
Only the branching pattern matters, not the order in which the branches are drawn.
