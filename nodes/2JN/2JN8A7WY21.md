## Definition

Let $A$ be a real $m \times n$ [[matrix|0VD9ZSWWJG]] of [[rank|0ZC9YTXZRY]] $m$, and let $b \in \R^m$.
Consider the [[feasible region|1TTKMRW8PD]] $P = \set{x \in \R^n : A x = b, \ x \ge 0}$.
For $B \subseteq \set{1, \dots, n}$, let $A_B$ be the submatrix of $A$ formed by the columns with index in $B$.

1. A *basis* is a set $B$ of $m$ indices such that $A_B$ is [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]].
2. The *basic solution* of a basis $B$ is the $x \in \R^n$ with $x_B = A_B^{-1} b$ and $x_j = 0$ for $j \notin B$.
3. A *basic feasible solution* is a basic solution that lies in $P$, that is, with $x_B \ge 0$.

## Remarks

Equivalently, $x \in P$ is a basic feasible solution if and only if the columns $A_j$ with $x_j \neq 0$ are [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]].
A point of $P$ is a basic feasible solution if and only if it is a vertex, that is, an [[extreme point|3MSGFJM2NX]], of $P$.
There are at most $\binom{n}{m}$ bases, so there are finitely many basic feasible solutions.
A basic feasible solution with fewer than $m$ nonzero coordinates is *degenerate*, and then it can be the basic solution of several bases.

Example: let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $b = (2, 0)^T$.
For $B = \set{1, 2}$, solving $x_1 + x_2 = 2$ and $x_1 - x_2 = 0$ gives $x = (1, 1, 0, 0)$, which is $\ge 0$, so it is a basic feasible solution.

Non-example: the point $x = (1/2, 1/2, 1, 0)$ lies in $P$, since $A x = (2, 0)^T$ and $x \ge 0$.
It is not a basic feasible solution, because its three nonzero coordinates belong to three columns in $\R^2$, which are linearly dependent.
Also, for $b = (2, -1)^T$ the basis $B = \set{3, 4}$ gives $x = (0, 0, 2, -1)$, a basic solution that is not feasible.
