## Summary

The thermal contact conductance measures how well heat crosses the interface between two solids pressed together.
Real surfaces touch only at small spots, so the interface resists heat flow and the temperature jumps across it.
The conductance is the [[heat flux|1Y36434YTE]] across the interface divided by this temperature jump.
Its reciprocal is the contact [[thermal resistance|1CB45W4GA6]] per unit area.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put two metal blocks together and they seem to touch everywhere, but under a microscope their surfaces are rough.
They touch only at the tops of tiny bumps, and the gaps between are filled with air, which conducts heat poorly.
So heat crossing from one block to the other meets an extra obstacle at the joint.
Pressing harder, polishing the surfaces, or filling the gaps with thermal paste raises the contact conductance.
This is why computer chips are mounted on their coolers with a layer of paste.
