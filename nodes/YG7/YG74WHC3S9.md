## Summary

The proper time along a timelike [[world line|Z788G6MJ9E]] is the time measured by an ideal clock carried along it.
It is the Lorentzian length of the world line divided by $c$, so it is the same in every inertial frame.
It is the natural time parameter for a moving particle, and it differs from coordinate time by the factor of time dilation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each observer carries a wristwatch; proper time is what that wristwatch shows.
Different observers who travel between the same two events can show different elapsed times, depending on their paths.
The traveller who stays inertial shows the most time; anyone who speeds away and comes back shows less.
For example, a clock moving at $0.6 c$ for $10$ seconds of lab time records $10 \sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 8$ seconds of proper time.
