## Definition

The proof-theoretic ordinal of a theory is the supremum of the order types of the primitive recursive well-orders along which the theory proves transfinite induction.

Let $T$ be a recursively axiomatized theory that interprets [[Peano arithmetic|0QFT7Z76N5]] or a weaker standard system of arithmetic.
Let $X$ be a unary predicate symbol that is not in the language of $T$, and let $T(X)$ be $T$ with $X$ allowed in its axiom schemas.
For a [[primitive recursive|QQQRJA90EP]] binary relation $\prec$ on $\mathbb{N}$, let $\operatorname{Prog}(\prec, X)$ be the formula
$$\forall x \, \big( \forall y \, (y \prec x \to X(y)) \to X(x) \big),$$
and let $\operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$ be the formula $\operatorname{Prog}(\prec, X) \to \forall x \, X(x)$.
The proof-theoretic ordinal of $T$ is
$$\lvert T \rvert = \sup \{ \operatorname{otyp}(\prec) : \prec \text{ is a primitive recursive well-order on } \mathbb{N} \text{ and } T(X) \vdash \operatorname{TI}(\prec, X) \}.$$
Here a [[well-order|0F1D6PK9RZ]] is a linear order with no infinite descending sequence, and $\operatorname{otyp}(\prec)$ is the [[ordinal|0RDJK7EQYY]] isomorphic to $(\mathbb{N}, \prec)$.
For a theory of second-order arithmetic, $X$ is a set variable, $X(x)$ means $x \in X$, and the formula $\forall X \, \operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$ is used.

## Notation

The ordinal is written $\lvert T \rvert$ or $\lvert T \rvert_{\Pi^1_1}$, and it is also called the $\Pi^1_1$ ordinal of $T$.
Some books use computable instead of primitive recursive orders, and some allow orders on a primitive recursive subset of $\mathbb{N}$.
For the usual theories these variants give the same value.

## Remarks

### Reading the conditions

The predicate $X$ is new, so $T$ knows nothing special about it.
A proof of $\operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$ therefore works for every property at once, and that is the formal content of "$\prec$ is well-founded".
In second-order form, $\forall X \, \operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$ is a $\Pi^1_1$ sentence of the [[analytical hierarchy|M6JGF4TPM6]], and it is true exactly when $\prec$ is well-founded.

The condition "$\prec$ is a well-order" is checked outside $T$, in the real world.
It is needed because an unsound theory can prove $\operatorname{TI}$ along an ill-founded order, as [[PA plus the negation of Con(PA)|FWY2SE83Q4]] shows.
The orders are primitive recursive so that $T$ can talk about them through a formula that it can evaluate.

For example, $\lvert \mathrm{PA} \rvert = \varepsilon_0$, the ordinal [[epsilon nought|KT50SMH3EC]].
PA proves [[transfinite induction|0ZHQMQ2C1N]] along the standard notation system restricted to each ordinal below $\varepsilon_0$, and along no primitive recursive well-order of type $\varepsilon_0$ or more.
On the other side, $\omega_1$ is never a value, because each $\operatorname{otyp}(\prec)$ is [[countable|01TQB88H4C]].

### The bound by the Church-Kleene ordinal

Let $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ be the least ordinal that is not the order type of a [[computable|KZSB5E471K]] well-order on $\mathbb{N}$.
Call $T$ $\Pi^1_1$-sound if every $\Pi^1_1$ sentence that $T$ proves is true.
If $T$ is $\Pi^1_1$-sound, then $\lvert T \rvert < \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$.
We use the standard fact that every computable well-founded relation has a rank below $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$.

1. Let $I$ be the set of codes $i$ of proofs in $T(X)$ that end in a formula $\operatorname{TI}(\prec_i, X)$.
   The set $I$ is decidable and $i \mapsto \prec_i$ is computable, since proofs can be checked mechanically.
2. Each $\prec_i$ is well-founded, by $\Pi^1_1$-soundness.
3. On the pairs $(i, x)$ with $i \in I$, put $(i, x) \triangleleft (j, y)$ if $i = j$ and $x \prec_i y$.
   This relation is computable by step 1, and it is well-founded by step 2, since a descending sequence stays in one $\prec_i$.
4. Let $\rho$ be the rank of $\triangleleft$.
   The rank of $(i, x)$ in $\triangleleft$ is the rank of $x$ in $\prec_i$.
   When $\prec_i$ is a well-order, these ranks run through all ordinals below $\operatorname{otyp}(\prec_i)$, so $\operatorname{otyp}(\prec_i) \le \rho$.
5. So $\lvert T \rvert \le \rho$, and $\rho < \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ by the standard fact.

### Notation dependence

The definition takes the supremum over all primitive recursive well-orders, so it does not depend on one chosen notation system.
A claim "$T$ proves transfinite induction up to $\alpha$" does depend on the order used to represent $\alpha$.
By [[the artificial notation system|PXMATF6B16]], PA does not prove induction along a certain primitive recursive order of type $\omega$.
So a theorem $\lvert T \rvert = \alpha$ needs a natural notation system for the lower bound and an argument about all orders for the upper bound.
Other definitions, such as the $\Pi^0_2$ ordinal from the provably total functions, can give other values.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a theory, for example Peano arithmetic, and look at the computable ways of ordering the natural numbers in which every walk downward stops.
For some of these orderings, the theory proves that induction along them works.
Each ordering has a length, which is an ordinal.
The proof-theoretic ordinal is the least ordinal that is at least each of these lengths.
For Peano arithmetic it is $\varepsilon_0$, the limit of $\omega$, $\omega^\omega$, $\omega^{\omega^\omega}$, and so on.

### L2 human

We check that an order of type $\omega \cdot 2$ gives the lower bound $\omega \cdot 2 \le \lvert \mathrm{PA} \rvert$.
Put the even numbers first, in their usual order, and then the odd numbers:
$$x \prec y \iff (x \text{ even and } y \text{ odd}) \text{ or } (x \equiv y \pmod 2 \text{ and } x < y).$$
This relation is primitive recursive, and its order type is $\omega + \omega = \omega \cdot 2$.

Assume $\operatorname{Prog}(\prec, X)$.
Every even number has only smaller even numbers below it, so strong induction on $n$ gives $X(2n)$ for all $n$.
Every odd number has all even numbers and the smaller odd numbers below it.
With the first part, strong induction on $n$ gives $X(2n + 1)$ for all $n$.
So PA proves $\operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$, using the induction schema for formulas that contain $X$.

The typical use is a lower bound: exhibit natural orders and prove induction along them.
Watch out: the bound $\lvert T \rvert \ge \alpha$ needs only one order of type $\alpha$, but the bound $\lvert T \rvert \le \alpha$ is a claim about every primitive recursive well-order.
