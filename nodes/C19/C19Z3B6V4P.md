## Definition

Let $T$ be a recursively axiomatized theory that interprets [[Peano arithmetic|0QFT7Z76N5]] or a weaker standard system of arithmetic.
Let $X$ be a unary predicate symbol that is not in the language of $T$, and let $T(X)$ be $T$ with $X$ allowed in its axiom schemas.

For a [[primitive recursive|QQQRJA90EP]] binary relation $\prec$ on $\N$, define:

- $\operatorname{Prog}(\prec, X)$ is the formula $\forall x \, (\forall y \, (y \prec x \to X(y)) \to X(x))$;
- $\operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$ is the formula $\operatorname{Prog}(\prec, X) \to \forall x \, X(x)$.

The *proof-theoretic ordinal* of $T$ is

$$\abs{T} = \sup \{ \operatorname{otyp}(\prec) : \prec \text{ is a primitive recursive well-order on } \N \text{ and } T(X) \vdash \operatorname{TI}(\prec, X) \}.$$

Here a [[well-order|0F1D6PK9RZ]] is a linear order with no infinite descending sequence, and $\operatorname{otyp}(\prec)$ is the [[ordinal|0RDJK7EQYY]] isomorphic to $(\N, \prec)$.
For a theory of second-order arithmetic, $X$ is a set variable, and $\operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$ is read with $X(x)$ as $x \in X$ and a universal quantifier over $X$ in front.

## Notation

The ordinal is written $\abs{T}$ or $\abs{T}_{\Pi^1_1}$.
Some books use computable instead of primitive recursive orders; for the usual theories this gives the same value.
Some books require the order only on a primitive recursive subset of $\N$, which also gives the same value.

## Remarks

- The condition "$\prec$ is a well-order" is needed. Without it, the supremum can include ill-founded orders when $T$ is unsound, as in [[PA plus the negation of Con(PA)|FWY2SE83Q4]].
- The statement $\forall X \, \operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$ is a $\Pi^1_1$ sentence of the [[analytical hierarchy|M6JGF4TPM6]], and it is equivalent to the well-foundedness of $\prec$. So $\abs{T}$ measures the $\Pi^1_1$ sentences of this form that $T$ proves.
- Example: $\abs{\mathrm{PA}} = \varepsilon_0$, the ordinal [[epsilon nought|KT50SMH3EC]]. PA proves [[transfinite induction|0ZHQMQ2C1N]] along the standard notation system for each ordinal below $\varepsilon_0$, and no primitive recursive well-order of type $\varepsilon_0$ or more.
- Non-example: the least uncountable ordinal $\omega_1$ is never $\abs{T}$, since each $\operatorname{otyp}(\prec)$ is [[countable|01TQB88H4C]] and $T$ has only countably many proofs.

### The bound by the Church-Kleene ordinal

Let $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ be the least ordinal that is not the order type of a [[computable|KZSB5E471K]] well-order on $\N$.
Call $T$ $\Pi^1_1$-sound if every $\Pi^1_1$ sentence that $T$ proves is true.
If $T$ is $\Pi^1_1$-sound, then $\abs{T} < \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$.

1. Let $I$ be the set of codes $i$ of proofs in $T(X)$ that end in a formula $\operatorname{TI}(\prec_i, X)$. The set $I$ is decidable and $i \mapsto \prec_i$ is computable, since proofs can be checked.
2. Each $\prec_i$ is a well-order, by $\Pi^1_1$-soundness.
3. On the pairs $(i, x)$ with $i \in I$ and $x \in \N$, define $(i, x) \triangleleft (j, y)$ if $i < j$, or $i = j$ and $x \prec_i y$.
4. The relation $\triangleleft$ is computable on a decidable set of pairs, by step 1. It is a well-order, since it is a sum of well-orders along the well-order $<$ on $I$.
5. Its order type $\sum_i \operatorname{otyp}(\prec_i)$ is at least each $\operatorname{otyp}(\prec_i)$, and it is below $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ by step 4.
6. So $\abs{T} \le \sum_i \operatorname{otyp}(\prec_i) < \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$.

### Notation dependence

The definition takes the supremum over all primitive recursive well-orders, so it does not depend on a chosen notation system.
A statement "$T$ proves transfinite induction up to $\alpha$" does depend on the order used to represent $\alpha$.
By [[the artificial notation system|PXMATF6B16]], PA does not prove transfinite induction along a certain primitive recursive order of type $\omega$.
So a theorem of the form $\abs{T} = \alpha$ is shown with a natural notation system for the lower bound and an argument about all orders for the upper bound.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a theory, for example Peano arithmetic, and look at all the computable ways of ordering the natural numbers that have no endless descending chains.
For some of these orderings, the theory can prove that induction along them works.
Each ordering has a length, which is an ordinal.
The proof-theoretic ordinal is the least ordinal that is at least each of these lengths.
For Peano arithmetic it is $\varepsilon_0$, the limit of $\omega$, $\omega^\omega$, $\omega^{\omega^\omega}$, and so on.

### L2 human

We check that the order of type $\omega \cdot 2$ below gives a lower bound $\omega \cdot 2 \le \abs{\mathrm{PA}}$.

1. Order $\N$ by putting the even numbers first, in their usual order, and then the odd numbers: $x \prec y$ if $x$ is even and $y$ is odd, or $x \equiv y \pmod 2$ and $x < y$.
2. The relation is primitive recursive, and its order type is $\omega + \omega = \omega \cdot 2$.
3. Assume $\operatorname{Prog}(\prec, X)$. Every even number has only even numbers below it, so ordinary strong induction on the even numbers gives $X(2n)$ for all $n$.
4. Every odd number has all even numbers and the smaller odd numbers below it. With step 3, strong induction gives $X(2n + 1)$ for all $n$.
5. So PA proves $\operatorname{TI}(\prec, X)$ with the induction schema for formulas that contain $X$.

The typical use is a lower bound: exhibit natural orders and prove induction along them.
The trap: the bound $\abs{T} \ge \alpha$ needs only one order of type $\alpha$, but the bound $\abs{T} \le \alpha$ is about every primitive recursive well-order.
