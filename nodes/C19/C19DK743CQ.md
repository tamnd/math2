## Definition

Consider a [[model of an infectious disease|AKP63KCK02]] in a host population.
The *basic reproduction number* $R_0$ is the [[expected value|0DCT0QZVQ1]] of the number of secondary infections caused by one typical infectious host.
The expectation is taken over the whole infectious period of that host, in a population where every other host is susceptible.

## Remarks

Example: in the [[SIR model|J5FHBDJGJA]] with transmission rate $\beta$ and recovery rate $\gamma$, an infectious host in a fully susceptible population causes new infections at rate $\beta$.
Its infectious period has mean $1/\gamma$, so $R_0 = \beta \cdot (1/\gamma) = \beta / \gamma$.

Non-example: the mean number of secondary infections at a time when part of the population is already immune is not $R_0$.
It fails the condition that all other hosts are susceptible, and it is called the effective reproduction number $R_t$.
In the SIR model, $R_t = R_0 \, S(t)/N$.

When hosts are of several types, "typical" means averaged with the right weights.
Then $R_0$ is the spectral radius of the [[next-generation matrix|KY7W9E9ECS]].
The number $R_0$ is a property of the pathogen in a given host population, not of the pathogen alone.
