## Summary

This topic is a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]].
It covers the syntax of first-order logic: signatures, terms, formulas, free and bound variables, substitution and the standard normal forms.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[First-order logic|086QKPF0A6]]: formulas about elements, with quantifiers over elements only.
2. [[Signature|WDVVEYD6AT]]: the function, relation and constant symbols, each with its arity.
3. [[Term|6D3WA7N2W4]]: names built from variables and constants by function symbols, read in one way only.
4. [[Atomic formula|~de213704]]: an equation or a relation symbol applied to terms.
5. [[Well-formed formula|HYJ10AF6RT]]: atomic formulas closed under connectives and quantifiers.
6. [[Free and bound variables|~2fd0cd75]]: which variable occurrences a quantifier binds.
7. [[Sentence|DKFGQTRYZE]]: a formula without free variables.
8. [[Substitution|NCDZ969V7X]]: replacing a free variable by a term without capture.
9. [[First-order theory|92SWXST1ZM]]: a set of sentences over a signature.
10. [[First-order logic with equality|~cc7adc48]]: equality as a logical symbol, and its axioms.
11. [[Many-sorted logic|~4886a7ff]]: several domains, and the reduction to one sort.
12. [[Prenex normal form|CS82V969DE]]: all quantifiers in front of a quantifier-free matrix.
13. [[Skolem normal form|GCPQGMJYEV]]: existential quantifiers replaced by new function symbols.
14. [[Hilbert's epsilon calculus|~41c81e04]]: a choice term for each formula, and the epsilon theorems.
15. [[Definite description|PD2R4XT79Q]]: "the $x$ such that", and Russell's analysis by quantifiers.
16. [[Standard first-order languages|~1cac8c60]]: the signatures of orders, groups, rings, arithmetic and set theory.

### Prerequisites

1. The [[propositional calculus|JDK63589SA]]: connectives, truth tables and propositional formulas.
2. [[Propositions, connectives and quantifiers|PM1AYWABA7]]: reading quantified statements in mathematics.
3. [[Logic, formal systems and foundations|H8QZY0RZXZ]]: object language, metalanguage and formal systems.
4. [[Structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]], used in most proofs about terms and formulas.
