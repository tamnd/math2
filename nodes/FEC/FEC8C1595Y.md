## Summary

M-theory is a conjectured eleven-dimensional quantum theory that unifies the five consistent ten-dimensional [[superstring theories|N993FCEJY2]].
Edward Witten proposed it in 1995, after evidence that the five string theories are related to each other by dualities.
Its low-energy limit is [[eleven-dimensional supergravity|CRHHG45BVB]], and it contains extended objects of dimension two and five, the M2-brane and the M5-brane.
No complete formulation of the theory is known.

## Explanations

### L1 human

By the early 1990s physicists had five different string theories, all living in ten dimensions.
It turned out that each of them can be turned into another one by changing parameters such as the size of a hidden dimension or the strength of interactions.
Witten suggested that all five are limits of one larger theory, called M-theory, which lives in eleven dimensions.
For example, when the strings of one theory interact strongly, an eleventh dimension, a small circle, opens up.
The full theory is still not written down; physicists know only its limits and many of its consequences.
