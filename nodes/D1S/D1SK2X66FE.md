## Summary

Regge calculus is a discrete version of [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]], introduced by Tullio Regge in 1961.
Spacetime is approximated by flat simplices glued together, and the geometry is given by the lengths of the edges.
Curvature is concentrated on the faces of codimension two, where it is measured by a deficit angle.
It is used in numerical relativity and as a starting point for discrete approaches to [[quantum gravity|M1VXRT5X56]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A curved surface can be approximated by flat triangles, like a geodesic dome.
Each triangle is flat, and the curvature hides at the corners: the angles of the triangles meeting at a corner add up to less than a full turn.
The missing angle, the deficit angle, measures how curved the surface is there.
Regge calculus does the same in four dimensions with flat four-dimensional simplices, and writes Einstein's theory as equations for the edge lengths.
For example, the surface of a tetrahedron is a crude sphere whose four corners each miss half a turn.
