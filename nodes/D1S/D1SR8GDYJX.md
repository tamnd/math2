## Summary

Euclidean geometry is the study of the plane and of space with the notions of distance and angle that Euclid set out in his Elements.
Its central objects are points, lines, circles, triangles and polygons, and two figures are congruent when an isometry takes one onto the other.
Euclidean geometry is the first part of mathematics in which most students meet proofs, and it is the model for the axiomatic method.
Euclid wrote the Elements in Alexandria around 300 BC, and Hilbert gave a complete modern system of axioms for the subject in 1899.
This topic collects the Euclidean geometry pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Classical geometries, convex and discrete geometry and differential geometry have their own topics.
