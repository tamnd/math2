## Summary

The WKB approximation in optics describes waves in a medium whose [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] changes slowly on the scale of a wavelength.
It is the [[WKB approximation|YJEBN8N3G8]] applied to the [[Helmholtz equation|MNXKTXJ35G]] $\nabla^2 u + k_0^2 n^2 u = 0$ with the large parameter $k_0$.
Its phase satisfies the [[eikonal equation|HGMX1QGTAZ]] and its amplitude a transport equation, which together give geometrical optics.
In layered media it gives explicit approximate fields and the mode conditions of graded-index waveguides.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If the glass in front of a light wave changes only a little over each wavelength, the wave adapts as it goes.
Locally it looks like an ordinary wave with the local wavelength $\lambda / n$.
Its phase is the sum of the small phase steps along the way, and its amplitude adjusts so that the energy flow stays constant.
The approximation breaks down where the index changes abruptly, or where a ray turns back.
