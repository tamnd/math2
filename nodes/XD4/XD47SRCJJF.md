## Summary

Work hardening, also called strain hardening, is the increase of the stress needed for further plastic deformation as a material is deformed plastically.
In [[plasticity|K8BBMTN0KV]] models it appears as a [[yield surface|CWWCEXZ8BH]] that grows or moves with the plastic strain.
In metals it comes from the multiplication and tangling of dislocations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Bend a copper wire back and forth a few times and it becomes harder to bend.
The wire has work-hardened: each plastic deformation raised the stress needed for the next one.
Blacksmiths and wire makers use this to make metal stronger.
Too much work hardening makes the metal brittle, and heating it (annealing) undoes the effect.
