## Summary

Seismic tomography is the imaging of the Earth's interior by [[geophysical inversion|C54MFNX576]] of seismic data recorded for many sources and receivers.
The data are travel times, amplitudes or whole waveforms of seismic waves, and the unknowns are maps of wave speed, attenuation or anisotropy.
Many wave paths cross each region from different directions.
So the inversion can locate where the material differs from a reference model such as the [[Preliminary reference Earth model|20FVNS391E]].
It has produced images of subducting slabs, mantle plumes and the structure of the crust.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A medical CT scanner sends X-rays through the body from many directions and builds a picture from how much each ray is weakened.
Seismic tomography does the same with earthquake waves and the Earth.
Waves that arrive earlier than expected passed through fast rock, and waves that arrive late passed through slow rock.
Combining thousands of such paths, crossing in many directions, shows where the fast and the slow regions are.
An example is a cold, fast slab of ocean floor sinking into the mantle.
