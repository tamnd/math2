## Definition

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]] with [[Cartan subalgebra|BB098JPWGA]] $\mathfrak{h}$ and a choice of positive roots $\Phi^+$.
Let $\mathfrak{n}^+ = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, so that $\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^+$ is a [[Borel subalgebra|AP4MMD8FPR]].
*Category $\mathcal{O}$* is the full subcategory of the [[category|A2DE66937D]] of $\mathfrak{g}$-[[modules|TZAZ163QGK]] whose objects are the modules $M$ such that:

1. $M$ is finitely generated as a module over the [[universal enveloping algebra|SCZ8PRVY00]] $U(\mathfrak{g})$.
2. $M$ is the direct sum of its [[weight spaces|KFVNC2W720]] $M_\mu$, $\mu \in \mathfrak{h}^*$.
3. $M$ is locally $\mathfrak{n}^+$-finite: for each $v \in M$, the subspace $U(\mathfrak{n}^+) v$ is finite-dimensional.

## Remarks

Example: a Verma module $M(\lambda)$ lies in $\mathcal{O}$.
It is generated by one vector, it is the direct sum of its weight spaces, and its weights lie in $\lambda - \Z_{\ge 0}\Phi^+$ with finite-dimensional weight spaces.
Let $v$ be a weight vector of weight $\mu$.
Then $U(\mathfrak{n}^+) v$ lies in the sum of the finitely many weight spaces of weights between $\mu$ and $\lambda$, so it is finite-dimensional.
Every finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-module also lies in $\mathcal{O}$.
Non-example: with $\mathfrak{b}^- = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^-$, the lowest weight module $U(\mathfrak{g}) \otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b}^-)} \C_\lambda$ satisfies clauses 1 and 2.
It fails clause 3, since $U(\mathfrak{n}^+)$ acts freely on its generator, so $U(\mathfrak{n}^+) v$ is infinite-dimensional.
In $\mathcal{O}$ each weight space is finite-dimensional, each module has finite length, and the simple objects are the modules $L(\lambda)$, $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$.
$\mathcal{O}$ is abelian, has enough projectives, and is the direct sum of subcategories indexed by central characters.
