## Proof

We prove $\mathrm{BW}$ in $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ by halving the interval, and we prove the reversal by reading the range of a one-to-one $f$ off the limit of a sum of distinct powers of $2$.

For $\sigma \in 2^{<\mathbb{N}}$ of length $k$, let $c_\sigma = \sum_{i < k} \sigma(i)\, 2^{-(i+1)}$ and $D_\sigma = [c_\sigma, c_\sigma + 2^{-k}]$.
So $D_\sigma$ is a closed dyadic interval of length $2^{-k}$, and $D_\sigma = D_{\sigma 0} \cup D_{\sigma 1}$.

### Part 1: ACA0 proves BW

1. Let $(x_n)$ be a sequence of reals in $[0, 1]$.
   The relation $x_n \in D_\sigma$ is $\Pi^0_1$, so [[arithmetical comprehension|K9XG0Z2N9V]] gives $E = \{ (n, \sigma) : x_n \in D_\sigma \}$.
2. Arithmetical comprehension also gives $A = \{ \sigma : \forall m\, \exists n > m\, (n, \sigma) \in E \}$.
   This is the set of $\sigma$ such that $D_\sigma$ contains $x_n$ for infinitely many $n$.
3. The empty sequence is in $A$, since $D_{\langle \rangle} = [0, 1]$.
   If $\sigma \in A$, then $\sigma 0 \in A$ or $\sigma 1 \in A$, since $D_\sigma = D_{\sigma 0} \cup D_{\sigma 1}$.
4. Define $g \colon \mathbb{N} \to \{ 0, 1 \}$ by recursion: $g(k) = 0$ if $(g {\restriction} k)\, 0 \in A$, and $g(k) = 1$ otherwise.
   By induction on $k$, using step 3, we have $g {\restriction} k \in A$ for all $k$.
5. Let $n_0$ be the least $n$ with $(n, g {\restriction} 0) \in E$, and let $n_{k+1}$ be the least $n > n_k$ with $(n, g {\restriction} (k+1)) \in E$.
   Each search ends, by step 4.
6. Let $x$ be the real with code $(c_{g \restriction k})_k$.
   Then $x \in D_{g \restriction k}$ and $x_{n_k} \in D_{g \restriction k}$, so $\lvert x_{n_k} - x \rvert \le 2^{-k}$.
7. So $(x_{n_k})$ converges to $x$, with rate $2^{-k}$.

### Part 2: RCA0 plus BW proves ACA0

8. By the [[range lemma|3MQ39PRCYJ]], it suffices to show that every one-to-one $f \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ has a range.
   Fix $f$.
9. Let $c_n = \sum_{i \le n} 2^{-f(i) - 1}$, a rational.
   The terms are distinct powers of $2$, so $0 < c_n < 1$, and $(c_n)$ is increasing.
10. $\mathrm{BW}$ gives a subsequence $(c_{n_k})$ that converges to a real $L$ with code $(q_k)$, so that $\lvert q_k - L \rvert \le 2^{-k}$.
11. For every $n$, $c_n \le c_{n_k}$ once $n_k \ge n$, so $c_n \le L$.
    For every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $k$ with $L - c_{n_k} < \varepsilon$, and then $L - c_n < \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge n_k$.
12. Let $h(m)$ be the least $n$ with $q_{m+3} - c_n < 2^{-m-2}$.
    It exists: choose $n$ with $L - c_n < 2^{-m-3}$, and then $q_{m+3} - c_n \le L + 2^{-m-3} - c_n < 2^{-m-2}$.
13. The condition compares rationals, so $h$ exists by $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension.
    Also $L - c_{h(m)} \le q_{m+3} + 2^{-m-3} - c_{h(m)} < 2^{-m-2} + 2^{-m-3} < 2^{-m-1}$.
14. We claim that $m$ is in the range of $f$ exactly when $f(i) = m$ for some $i \le h(m)$.
15. Assume $f(i) = m$ with $i > h(m)$.
    Then $c_i \ge c_{h(m)} + 2^{-m-1}$, since the sum $c_i$ has the term $2^{-f(i)-1} = 2^{-m-1}$ that $c_{h(m)}$ lacks.
16. So $L - c_{h(m)} \ge c_i - c_{h(m)} \ge 2^{-m-1}$, against step 13.
    This proves the claim.
17. The condition of step 14 is $\Sigma^0_0$ in $f$ and $h$, so $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension gives the range of $f$.

## Idea

The forward proof makes the classical choice of a half with infinitely many terms, and arithmetical comprehension decides this choice.
The reversal codes the range of $f$ into the speed of convergence of an increasing sequence.
The limit gives a modulus $h$, and the modulus bounds the search for preimages: a value $m$ that has not appeared by stage $h(m)$ never appears.

## Variants

The reversal also proves that the [[monotone convergence theorem|G2S6H789RG]] implies $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, since $(c_n)$ itself is increasing and bounded.
Part 1 is the proof of [[König's lemma|3P2J5NWCM1]] in $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, applied to the binary tree $A$.
Weak König's lemma does not help here, because $A$ is defined by a $\Pi^0_2$ formula and need not exist in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
