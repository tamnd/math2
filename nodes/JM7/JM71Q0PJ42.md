## Proof

For $\sigma \in 2^{<\N}$ of length $k$, let $c_\sigma = \sum_{i < k} \sigma(i)\, 2^{-(i+1)}$ and $D_\sigma = [c_\sigma, c_\sigma + 2^{-k}]$.

Part 1: $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ proves $\mathrm{BW}$.

1. Let $(x_n)$ be a sequence of reals in $[0, 1]$. The relation $x_n \in D_\sigma$ is $\Pi^0_1$, so arithmetical comprehension gives $E = \set{(n, \sigma) : x_n \in D_\sigma}$.
2. Arithmetical comprehension also gives $A = \set{\sigma : \forall m\, \exists n > m\, (n, \sigma) \in E}$, the set of $\sigma$ such that $D_\sigma$ contains $x_n$ for infinitely many $n$.
3. The empty sequence is in $A$. If $\sigma \in A$, then $\sigma 0 \in A$ or $\sigma 1 \in A$, since $D_\sigma = D_{\sigma 0} \cup D_{\sigma 1}$.
4. Define $g \colon \N \to \set{0, 1}$ by recursion: $g(k) = 0$ if $(g {\restriction} k) 0 \in A$, and $g(k) = 1$ otherwise. By induction, $g {\restriction} k \in A$ for all $k$.
5. Define $n_0$ as the least $n$ with $(n, g {\restriction} 0) \in E$, and $n_{k+1}$ as the least $n > n_k$ with $(n, g {\restriction} (k+1)) \in E$. Step 4 shows that each search ends.
6. Let $x$ be the real with code $(c_{g \restriction k})_k$. Then $x \in D_{g \restriction k}$ and $x_{n_k} \in D_{g \restriction k}$, so $\abs{x_{n_k} - x} \le 2^{-k}$.
7. So $(x_{n_k})$ converges to $x$.

Part 2: $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{BW}$ proves $\mathsf{ACA}_0$.

8. By the [[range lemma|3MQ39PRCYJ]], it suffices to show that every one-to-one $f \colon \N \to \N$ has a range. Fix $f$.
9. Let $c_n = \sum_{i \le n} 2^{-f(i) - 1}$, a rational. The terms are distinct powers of $2$, so $0 < c_n < 1$, and $(c_n)$ is increasing.
10. $\mathrm{BW}$ gives a subsequence $(c_{n_k})$ that converges to a real $L$ with code $(q_k)$.
11. For every $n$, $c_n \le c_{n_k}$ once $n_k \ge n$, so $c_n \le L$. For every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $k$ with $L - c_{n_k} < \varepsilon$, and then $L - c_n < \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge n_k$.
12. Let $g(m)$ be the least $n$ with $q_{m+3} - c_n < 2^{-m-2}$. It exists: choose $n$ with $L - c_n < 2^{-m-3}$, then $q_{m+3} - c_n \le L + 2^{-m-3} - c_n < 2^{-m-2}$.
13. The condition compares rationals, so $g$ exists by $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension. Also $L - c_{g(m)} \le q_{m+3} + 2^{-m-3} - c_{g(m)} < 2^{-m-1}$.
14. Claim: $m$ is in the range of $f$ exactly when $f(i) = m$ for some $i \le g(m)$.
15. Assume $f(i) = m$ with $i > g(m)$. Then $c_i \ge c_{g(m)} + 2^{-m-1}$, since $c_i$ has the term $2^{-f(i)-1}$ that $c_{g(m)}$ lacks.
16. So $L - c_{g(m)} \ge c_i - c_{g(m)} \ge 2^{-m-1}$, against step 13. This proves the claim.
17. The condition of step 14 is $\Sigma^0_0$ in $f$ and $g$, so $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension gives the range of $f$.

## Idea

The forward proof makes the classical choice of a half with infinitely many terms, and arithmetical comprehension decides this choice.
The reversal codes the range of $f$ into the speed of convergence of an increasing sequence; the limit gives a modulus, and the modulus bounds the search for preimages.

## Variants

The reversal also proves that the monotone convergence theorem implies $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, since $(c_n)$ itself is increasing and bounded.
Part 1 is the same as the proof of König's lemma in $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, applied to the tree $A$.
