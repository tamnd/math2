## Summary

The radiosity of a surface is the total radiant power leaving it per unit area.
It counts radiation emitted by the surface and radiation reflected or transmitted by it.
It is what another surface actually receives from it, so it is the natural unknown in enclosure radiation problems.
For an opaque grey diffuse surface it combines the emitted part $\varepsilon \sigma T^4$ with the reflected part of the incoming radiation.
The net radiative flux of a surface is its radiosity minus the incident flux.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When you look at a white wall in a lit room, the light you see is not made by the wall: the wall reflects it.
A hot wall would also send out its own heat radiation.
Radiosity counts both: all the radiation that leaves a surface, whatever its origin.
For a perfectly black surface nothing is reflected, and the radiosity is just the emitted radiation.
