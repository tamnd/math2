## Definition

Let a star of mass $M$ rotate with angular velocity $\Omega$ and have total [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] $J$.
Let it lose mass at the rate $\dot M > 0$ through a steady, axisymmetric wind, as in the [[Parker solar wind model|GMWEA8Q83B]].
Let the wind carry a magnetic field that is [[frozen into the plasma|3C3BXAPJQA]].
The *Alfvén radius* $r_A$ is the distance from the star at which the radial wind speed equals the radial [[Alfvén speed|8CQDTE19FW]].
*Magnetic braking* is the resulting loss of angular momentum of the star.
In the equatorial model of Weber and Davis, the angular momentum carried away per unit mass is $\Omega r_A^2$, so
$$\frac{dJ}{dt} = -\dot M \, \Omega \, r_A^2.$$

## Remarks

The wind acts as if it corotated rigidly with the star out to $r_A$ and then left freely.
Since $r_A$ is much larger than the stellar radius $R$, the torque is larger by the factor $(r_A/R)^2$ than for an unmagnetized wind that leaves with the surface rotation.
For a wind leaving in all directions, an average over latitude gives the commonly used form $dJ/dt = -\frac{2}{3} \dot M \, \Omega \, r_A^2$.

Example: for the present Sun, $r_A$ is of the order of $10$ to $20$ solar radii.
So each kilogram of wind removes more than $100$ times the angular momentum $\Omega R^2$ of a kilogram of equatorial surface gas.

Non-example: a wind without a magnetic field has no Alfvén radius.
Each parcel leaves with the specific angular momentum $\Omega R^2 \sin^2\theta$ of its starting point at colatitude $\theta$, so the lever arm effect is absent.

For Sun-like stars, magnetic braking leads to the empirical Skumanich relation, under which the rotation rate decreases roughly like $t^{-1/2}$ with age $t$.
