## Summary

The barotropic vorticity equation describes a two-dimensional, non-divergent flow on a rotating planet.
It says that the absolute vorticity of each fluid column is carried along unchanged.
It is the simplest member of the family of [[quasi-geostrophic equations|8Z4QBWTWVR]], with one unknown, the stream function.
It describes [[Rossby waves|6N1MQMA9TM]], and it was the model used in the first successful numerical weather forecast in 1950.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine the atmosphere as a single thin sheet of fluid that swirls but never piles up or thins out.
Each column of air carries its own spin, made of the spin of the wind around it plus a share of the Earth's spin that depends on latitude.
The equation says this total spin travels with the air and does not change.
When air moves north, it gains planetary spin and must lose some of its own, which sends it back south; this back-and-forth is a Rossby wave.
