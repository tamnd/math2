## Definition

A *Helmert transformation* is a map $T \colon \R^3 \to \R^3$ of the form
$$T(X) = C + (1 + s) R X.$$
Here $C \in \R^3$ is a translation vector and $s > -1$ is a real number, the scale change.
The matrix $R \in SO(3)$ is a [[rotation|0HFREYRSZP]] matrix.
In geodesy $R$ is given by three small angles $r_x, r_y, r_z$, in radians, and replaced by its linear approximation
$$R \approx \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -r_z & r_y \\ r_z & 1 & -r_x \\ -r_y & r_x & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
The seven numbers $C_x, C_y, C_z, s, r_x, r_y, r_z$ are its parameters.

## Remarks

Example: $C = (100, 0, 0)$ m, $s = 0$, $R = I$ moves every point $100$ m along the $x$-axis; it preserves all distances, as $s = 0$.
Non-example: the map $X \mapsto \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 1 + 10^{-6}) X$ is not a Helmert transformation, since it scales the axes by different factors.
A Helmert transformation is a [[similarity transformation|D3GYHF6NAX]]: it multiplies all distances by $1 + s$ and preserves angles.
Two sign conventions for the angles are in use, the position vector and the coordinate frame conventions; they differ by the sign of $r_x, r_y, r_z$.
It is applied to Cartesian coordinates; [[geodetic coordinates|6QBV95NG6E]] $(\varphi, \lambda, h)$ are converted to Cartesian form first.
