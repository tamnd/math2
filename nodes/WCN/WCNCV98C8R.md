## Summary

This area studies the long-run behaviour of the orbits of iterated maps and of flows, how this behaviour changes with parameters, and which systems are the same up to a change of coordinates.
A dynamical system is a space together with a map of the space to itself, or a flow, and the orbit of a point is the sequence or curve of its images.
Dynamical systems model the motion of planets, the evolution of populations and many other systems that change in time, and they show that simple deterministic rules can give chaotic behaviour.
Poincaré started the qualitative theory around 1890 in his work on the three-body problem of celestial mechanics.
This topic collects the dynamical systems pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Ergodic theory, ordinary differential equations, and symplectic and Poisson geometry have their own topics.
