## Summary

Mantle convection is the slow creeping flow of the Earth's solid mantle, driven by the buoyancy of hot rock and the weight of cold rock.
It carries heat from the core and from radioactive decay to the surface.
On time scales of millions of years the mantle behaves as a fluid of very high viscosity.
Its [[Rayleigh number|CEKBV27MP0]] is far above the critical value for convection.
[[Plate tectonics|AYQQM6PBMR]] is its surface expression: cold plates sinking at trenches are the main downwellings.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A pot of thick soup heated from below turns over: hot soup rises, cools at the top and sinks again.
The Earth's mantle does the same, but it is solid rock that creeps at a few centimetres per year.
Hot plumes may rise from deep down, and cold ocean plates sink back into the mantle at trenches.
One full overturn takes on the order of a hundred million years.
