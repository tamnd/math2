## Statement

Consider the [[Solow–Swan model|223GCENAVJ]] with intensive production function $f$ and effective depreciation rate $m = n + g + \delta > 0$.
Assume that $f(0) = 0$, that $f$ is strictly increasing and strictly [[concave|0GCM7B6PBK]], and that $f$ satisfies the Inada conditions.
For $s \in (0, 1)$, let $k^*(s)$ be the steady state and let $c^*(s) = (1 - s) f(k^*(s))$ be steady-state consumption per effective worker.
Then there is a unique $k_G > 0$ with $f'(k_G) = m$.
The function $c^*$ has a unique maximum on $(0, 1)$, at the *golden rule saving rate*
$$s_G = \frac{m k_G}{f(k_G)} = \frac{k_G f'(k_G)}{f(k_G)}.$$

## Remarks

In a steady state $s f(k^*) = m k^*$, so $c^* = f(k^*) - m k^*$.
The map $s \mapsto k^*(s)$ is an increasing bijection from $(0, 1)$ onto an interval $(0, \bar k)$.
The concave function $k \mapsto f(k) - mk$ is maximised where $f'(k) = m$.
Strict concavity with $f(0) = 0$ gives $f(k) > k f'(k)$, so $s_G < 1$ and $k_G$ is reached by an admissible saving rate.

Example: for $f(k) = k^\alpha$ with $0 < \alpha < 1$, the condition $\alpha k_G^{\alpha - 1} = m$ gives $k_G^{1 - \alpha} = \alpha/m$, and then $s_G = m k_G^{1 - \alpha} = \alpha$.
So the golden rule saving rate equals the capital share $\alpha$.

If $s > s_G$, a lower saving rate raises consumption both now and in the new steady state.
Such an economy is called dynamically inefficient.
The rule is usually credited to Edmund Phelps (1961).

## Explanations

### L1 human

Saving more builds more capital, and more capital gives more output.
But saving more also leaves less to consume, and a large capital stock costs a lot to maintain.
The golden rule saving rate is the one that makes long-run consumption as large as possible.
At that rate, one extra unit of capital produces exactly what it costs to maintain it.

### L2 human

The golden rule compares steady states only; it says nothing about the path from one steady state to another.
In the Ramsey–Cass–Koopmans model, where saving is chosen by a household that discounts the future, the steady state satisfies $f'(k) = \rho + \delta$ instead.
This "modified golden rule" has less capital than the golden rule because of impatience.
The condition $f'(k_G) = n + g + \delta$ also says that the net marginal product of capital equals the growth rate of the economy.
