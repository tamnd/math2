## Summary

A geomagnetic reversal is a change in the polarity of the [[Earth's magnetic field|Z13HPJFV1F]], after which magnetic north and magnetic south have swapped.
The axial dipole part of the field changes sign and keeps the new sign for a long time.
Reversals are recorded in rocks and in the magnetic stripes of the ocean floor.
They are a main test for models of the [[geodynamo|19AESAFHF9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Today a compass needle points roughly north.
Many times in the past, the Earth's field flipped, and a compass would have pointed south.
Volcanic rocks freeze the direction of the field when they cool, so old lava flows keep a record of these flips.
The last full reversal happened about 780,000 years ago.
The intervals between reversals are irregular, from tens of thousands to tens of millions of years.
