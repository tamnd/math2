## Proof

We show that every logical axiom is valid and that modus ponens keeps truth, and then we walk down a derivation line by line.
We use the system $H$ and the notation of the [[soundness theorem|AVD7T7J063]].
For a [[structure|983GZBQYT4]] $\mathcal{A}$ with universe $A$, an assignment $s$ and an element $a \in A$, we write $s(x \mid a)$ for the assignment that agrees with $s$ except that it sends $x$ to $a$.
We write $\bar s(t)$ for the value of a [[term|6D3WA7N2W4]] $t$ in $\mathcal{A}$ under $s$.
We use two facts about [[satisfaction|2RBQPV02AS]], each proved by induction on formulas.
The [[coincidence lemma|FM580NZ9W6]] says that if $s_1$ and $s_2$ agree on the free variables of $\alpha$, then $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s_1]$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s_2]$.
The [[substitution lemma|A7S460KHBM]] says that if $t$ is substitutable for $x$ in $\alpha$, then $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha^x_t[s]$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s(x \mid \bar s(t))]$.

**Part A: every logical axiom is valid.**

1. If $\lambda$ is valid, then $\forall x \, \lambda$ is valid.
   By the clause for $\forall$, $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \lambda[s]$ means $\mathcal{A} \models \lambda[s(x \mid a)]$ for every $a \in A$, and each of these holds because $\lambda$ is valid.
2. By step 1 and induction on $k$, it is enough to show that each formula $\lambda$ of the six groups is valid.
   We fix $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ for steps 3 to 13.
3. Group 1. We define a truth assignment $v$ on the prime formulas by $v(\beta) = 1$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \beta[s]$.
4. Let $\bar v$ extend $v$ to all formulas by the truth tables of $\neg$ and $\to$.
   The satisfaction clauses for $\neg$ and $\to$ are the same truth tables, so [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] over the prime parts gives $\bar v(\gamma) = 1$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \gamma[s]$.
5. A [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]] $\alpha$ has $\bar v(\alpha) = 1$ for every $v$, so $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s]$ by step 4.
6. Group 2. Assume $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \alpha[s]$.
   Then $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s(x \mid a)]$ for every $a$, in particular for $a = \bar s(t)$.
7. The term $t$ is substitutable for $x$ in $\alpha$ by the side condition of group 2.
   So the substitution lemma turns step 6 into $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha^x_t[s]$, and the implication of group 2 is true at $s$.
8. Group 3. Assume $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, (\alpha \to \beta)[s]$ and $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \alpha[s]$.
   For each $a \in A$, both $\alpha \to \beta$ and $\alpha$ are true at $s(x \mid a)$, so $\beta$ is true at $s(x \mid a)$, and hence $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \beta[s]$.
9. Group 4. Assume $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s]$, where $x$ is not free in $\alpha$.
   For each $a$, the assignments $s$ and $s(x \mid a)$ agree on the free variables of $\alpha$.
10. The coincidence lemma gives $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s(x \mid a)]$ for every $a$, so $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \alpha[s]$.
11. Group 5. The formula $x = x$ is true at $s$, because $s(x) = s(x)$.
12. Group 6. Assume $s(x) = s(y)$, and let $u'$ come from a term $u$ by replacing some occurrences of $x$ by $y$.
    Then $\bar s(u') = \bar s(u)$ by induction on $u$, because the replaced variable has the same value, and a function symbol applied to arguments with equal values gives equal values.
13. An atomic $\alpha$ is $P u_1 \dots u_n$ or $u_1 = u_2$, and then $\alpha'$ is $P u'_1 \dots u'_n$ or $u'_1 = u'_2$.
    By step 12 the arguments have the same values, so $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ have the same truth value at $s$, and $x = y \to (\alpha \to \alpha')$ is true at $s$.

**Part B: derivations keep truth.**

14. [[Modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] keeps truth: if $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s]$ and $\mathcal{A} \models (\alpha \to \beta)[s]$, then $\mathcal{A} \models \beta[s]$, by the clause for $\to$.
15. Let $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ with a derivation $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n = \varphi$, and let $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ satisfy every member of $\Gamma$.
16. We show $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi_i[s]$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$ by [[strong induction|0KMVFSHFGZ]] on $i$, and we assume it for all $j < i$.
17. If $\varphi_i$ is a logical axiom, it is valid by Part A, so it is true at $s$.
18. If $\varphi_i \in \Gamma$, it is true at $s$ by the choice of $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ in step 15.
19. If $\varphi_i$ follows by MP from $\varphi_j$ and $\varphi_k = (\varphi_j \to \varphi_i)$ with $j, k < i$, both are true at $s$ by the induction hypothesis, so $\varphi_i$ is true at $s$ by step 14.
20. Steps 17 to 19 cover every line of a derivation, so $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi[s]$.
    Since $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ were arbitrary, $\Gamma \models \varphi$, which is part 1.
21. Part 2 is part 1 with $\Gamma = \emptyset$.
22. For part 3, let $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ satisfy $\Gamma$, and suppose $\Gamma \vdash \psi$ and $\Gamma \vdash \neg \psi$.
    Part 1 gives $\mathcal{A} \models \psi[s]$ and $\mathcal{A} \models \neg \psi[s]$, which the clause for $\neg$ forbids, so $\Gamma$ is [[consistent|VM8TQ70CVJ]].

## Idea

Think of the valid formulas as a safe region.
All axioms lie inside it, and modus ponens never leads out of it.
So an induction along a derivation shows that each line is true in every structure, under every assignment, that satisfies the hypotheses.
The only delicate cases are the quantifier axioms, and there the side conditions on substitution and on free variables do their work through the substitution and coincidence lemmas.

## Variants

In a Hilbert system with the rule of generalization, the induction has a fourth case.
That case is sound when the generalized variable is not free in any hypothesis used above it, and the coincidence lemma proves this.
For natural deduction and the sequent calculus, the induction runs over the derivation tree instead of a list, and each rule is checked once, for example the right rule for $\forall$ with its eigenvariable condition.
The [[soundness proof for propositional logic|WFBG5RAG36]] is the same induction without steps 6 to 13.
