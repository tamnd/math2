## Summary

The inverse of an implication $p \to q$ is the implication $\neg p \to \neg q$, in which both parts are negated and keep their places.
The inverse does not follow from $p \to q$: "if $n$ is divisible by $4$, then $n$ is even" is true, but its inverse fails for $n = 2$.
The inverse is the contrapositive of the [[converse|09A3N7ZN5G]] $q \to p$, so the inverse and the converse are logically equivalent.
The main page [[converse and inverse of an implication|09A3N7ZN5G]] treats the converse and the inverse together.

## Remarks

Inferring $\neg q$ from $p \to q$ and $\neg p$ uses the inverse as if it were true, and this is the fallacy of [[denying the antecedent|J7KFCFQRBT]].
The [[contrapositive|04T1CT7212]] $\neg q \to \neg p$ is the form with negations that is equivalent to $p \to q$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"If it is a square, then it has four sides" is true.
The inverse says "if it is not a square, then it does not have four sides".
A rectangle that is not a square has four sides, so the inverse is false.
