## Summary

"If it is a square, then it has four sides" is true, but "if it is not a square, then it does not have four sides" is false.
The second sentence is the inverse of the first.
The inverse of an implication $p \to q$ is the implication $\neg p \to \neg q$, in which both parts are negated and keep their places.
The inverse does not follow from $p \to q$, as the rectangle shows.
The inverse is the contrapositive of the [[converse|09A3N7ZN5G]] $q \to p$, so classically the inverse and the converse are logically equivalent.
The main page [[converse and inverse of an implication|09A3N7ZN5G]] treats the two together.

## Remarks

### Watch out

To infer $\neg q$ from $p \to q$ and $\neg p$ uses the inverse as if it were true, and this is the fallacy of [[denying the antecedent|J7KFCFQRBT]].
The [[contrapositive|04T1CT7212]] $\neg q \to \neg p$ is the form with negations that is equivalent to $p \to q$.

### Try it

Is the inverse of "if $n$ is divisible by $4$, then $n$ is even" true?
No: $n = 2$ is not divisible by $4$, and it is even.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"If it is a square, then it has four sides" is true.
The inverse says "if it is not a square, then it does not have four sides".
A rectangle that is not a square has four sides, so the inverse is false.
