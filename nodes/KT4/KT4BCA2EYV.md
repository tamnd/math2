## Summary

Consider a rotating, magnetized, highly conducting star such as a pulsar.
The Goldreich–Julian density is the charge density its magnetosphere must have so that the plasma there corotates with the star.
For a star with angular velocity $\Omega$ and magnetic field $B$, it equals $-2\varepsilon_0 \, \Omega \cdot B$ in SI units, up to relativistic corrections.
It sets the natural scale for the density of charged particles in pulsar magnetospheres, and comparisons with it decide where gaps and particle acceleration occur.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A pulsar is a spinning neutron star with a huge magnetic field.
A conductor that spins in a magnetic field builds up an electric field, like a dynamo.
If the space around the star is filled with plasma that turns with the star, this electric field must be produced by charges in the plasma.
The Goldreich–Julian density says how much charge that is: it is proportional to the spin rate times the field.
It changes sign where the field is perpendicular to the spin axis.
