## Proof

First, the shape of the argument on one case.
Let $\Gamma$ be the group axioms and let $\varphi$ be the cancellation law $\forall x \, \forall y \, \forall z \, (x \cdot y = x \cdot z \to y = z)$, which is true in every group.
If $\Gamma \nvdash \varphi$, then $\Gamma \cup \{ \neg \varphi \}$ would be consistent, and the model existence theorem would give a group in which cancellation fails.
No such group exists, so a derivation of $\varphi$ from $\Gamma$ exists.
The proof below runs this argument for every $\Gamma$ and $\varphi$.

We use the [[model existence theorem|G0DTR159RN]], proved by the [[Henkin construction|FMZMD0GGYK]].
We also use fact (P5) of the [[enumeration proof of Lindenbaum's lemma|DCR0S91GYY]]: if $\Gamma \cup \{ \neg \varphi \}$ is inconsistent, then $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$.

**Strong form.**

1. Let $\Gamma$ be a set of [[sentences|DKFGQTRYZE]] and $\varphi$ a sentence, with $\Gamma \models \varphi$.
2. Suppose that $\Gamma \cup \{ \neg \varphi \}$ is consistent.
3. The model existence theorem gives a [[model|MVCH6N9SNW]] $\mathcal{A}$ of $\Gamma \cup \{ \neg \varphi \}$.
4. $\mathcal{A}$ is a model of $\Gamma$, so $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi$ by step 1.
5. Also $\mathcal{A} \models \neg \varphi$, so $\mathcal{A} \not\models \varphi$ by the clause for $\neg$ in the definition of [[satisfaction|2RBQPV02AS]].
   This contradicts step 4.
6. Hence $\Gamma \cup \{ \neg \varphi \}$ is inconsistent.
7. By (P5), $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$, which proves the strong form.

**Weak form.**

8. A valid sentence $\varphi$ satisfies $\emptyset \models \varphi$.
   Step 7 with $\Gamma = \emptyset$ gives $\vdash \varphi$.

**Formulas with free variables.**

9. Let $\Gamma$ be a set of sentences and $\varphi$ a formula with free variables $x_1, \dots, x_k$, with $\Gamma \models \varphi$.
10. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a model of $\Gamma$.
    By step 9, $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi[s]$ for every assignment $s$, so $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x_1 \cdots \forall x_k \, \varphi$ by the clause for $\forall$.
11. So $\Gamma \models \forall x_1 \cdots \forall x_k \, \varphi$, and step 7 gives $\Gamma \vdash \forall x_1 \cdots \forall x_k \, \varphi$.
12. Each $x_i$ is substitutable for itself, so $\forall x_i \, \alpha \to \alpha$ is an axiom of group 2.
    With MP $k$ times, $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$.

## Idea

Completeness says that a sentence that is not derivable is not a consequence.
If $\Gamma \nvdash \varphi$, then $\Gamma$ together with $\neg \varphi$ is consistent, and a consistent set has a model.
That model satisfies $\Gamma$ and fails $\varphi$, so $\varphi$ is not a consequence of $\Gamma$.
All the work lies in the model existence theorem, and this proof only turns it around.

Watch out: step 7 needs (P5), which is the deduction theorem in disguise.
Without it, the inconsistency of $\Gamma \cup \{ \neg \varphi \}$ does not give a derivation of $\varphi$ from $\Gamma$ alone.

## Variants

- [[Gödel's original proof|CS0R0WW2V9]] reduces to prenex sentences of the form $\forall \bar x \, \exists \bar y \, \psi$ and uses propositional completeness with a compactness argument.
- Tableau proof: build a systematic tableau for $\Gamma \cup \{ \neg \varphi \}$.
  If it does not close, an infinite open branch is a Hintikka set, and its terms form a model.
  If it closes, the closed tableau is a derivation.
- Algebraic proof: for a countable language, Rasiowa and Sikorski find an ultrafilter of the [[Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra|QC9M3Q8B8Q]] that respects the infinite joins which the quantifiers define.
- For the cut-free [[sequent calculus|W72CA3R9MP]], a failed proof search gives a countermodel.
  This proves completeness without cut, and so [[cut elimination|AYRZ98Y0NW]].
