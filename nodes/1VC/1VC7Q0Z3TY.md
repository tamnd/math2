## Definition

An *electroactive polymer* is a polymer material that deforms in a reversible way under an applied electric field or electric current.
Two classes are usual.

1. *Electronic* electroactive polymers deform under the electric field itself, for example dielectric elastomers and electrostrictive or ferroelectric polymers.
2. *Ionic* electroactive polymers deform because ions move inside the material, for example ionic polymer–metal composites, conducting polymers and ionic gels.

## Remarks

Example: a dielectric elastomer film of thickness $d$ with compliant electrodes on both faces.
A voltage $V$ across the film gives an electric field $E = V/d$.
The attraction of the electrodes acts as an effective pressure $p = \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r E^2$ on the film, where $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity and $\varepsilon_r$ the relative permittivity of the polymer.
The film is a polymer and it deforms reversibly under the field, so it satisfies the definition.

Non-example: a ceramic piezoelectric crystal such as lead zirconate titanate also deforms under an electric field, but it is not a polymer.

The terms "artificial muscle" and "EAP" are common names for these materials.
