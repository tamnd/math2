## Summary

The vorticity equation is the evolution equation for the [[vorticity|B3SXT9TWFA]] $\omega = \nabla \times u$ of a flow, obtained by taking the curl of the momentum equation.
For an incompressible fluid it says that vorticity is carried by the flow, stretched and tilted by velocity gradients, and diffused by viscosity.
The pressure drops out, which makes this form central in vortex dynamics, turbulence and the regularity theory of the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] and [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The vorticity equation tracks how the spin of fluid parcels changes in time.
A spinning parcel that is stretched along its axis spins faster, like a skater pulling in the arms; this is vortex stretching.
Viscosity spreads spin to the neighbours and slowly weakens it.
In a flat, two-dimensional flow there is no stretching, so spin is only carried along and diffused.
