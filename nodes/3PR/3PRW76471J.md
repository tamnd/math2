## Statement

The diagonal lemma says that for every property of sentences that arithmetic can express, some sentence says of itself that it has the property.

Let $T$ be a [[theory|92SWXST1ZM]] in the language of arithmetic that contains [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]] $\mathsf{Q}$.
Fix a [[Gödel numbering|MFJXPAF0R0]], and let $\ulcorner \psi \urcorner$ be the numeral of the code of $\psi$.
For every [[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] $\varphi(x)$ with exactly one free variable $x$, there is a [[sentence|DKFGQTRYZE]] $\psi$ such that
$$T \vdash \psi \leftrightarrow \varphi(\ulcorner \psi \urcorner).$$
The sentence $\psi$ is called a fixed point of $\varphi$.

## Remarks

### The sentence that says "I am not provable"

Apply the lemma to the formula $\neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(x)$, where $\mathrm{Prov}_T$ is the [[provability predicate|NB2BCZR40F]] of $T$.
It gives a sentence $G$ with
$$T \vdash G \leftrightarrow \neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner).$$
Read through the coding, $G$ says "the sentence with my code is not provable in $T$", that is, "I am not provable".
This is the Gödel sentence, and [[Gödel's first incompleteness theorem|X9WYNYE29C]] shows that a consistent $T$ cannot prove it.
The [[proof|SFPSB6JPTC]] builds $G$ explicitly: it is $\exists y \, (D(\underline{k}, y) \wedge \neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(y))$, where $D$ represents a substitution function and $k$ is a code chosen so that the $y$ in question is the code of $G$ itself.

### One lemma, many theorems

Most limits on formal theories come from this lemma, with different formulas $\varphi$.
With a supposed truth definition and $\varphi(x) := \neg \mathrm{Tr}(x)$, it gives a liar sentence and so [[Tarski's undefinability theorem|YRV6SYK2BY]].
With $\varphi(x) := \mathrm{Prov}_T(x) \to \sigma$, it gives the sentence in the proof of [[Löb's theorem|3HD30YG3DG]].
With $\varphi(x) := \mathrm{Prov}_T(x)$, it gives a [[Henkin sentence|SBMQ5QHG9W]], which says "I am provable".
With the Rosser formula, it gives the sentence of [[Rosser's theorem|DH9RQ61GVD]].

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The theory must contain $\mathsf{Q}$ only so that it can represent the diagonal function $d$ of the [[proof|SFPSB6JPTC]].
Any theory that represents $d$ in the same sense satisfies the lemma.

The formula must have exactly one free variable, so that $\varphi(\ulcorner \psi \urcorner)$ is a sentence.
Formulas with more free variables need the [[diagonal lemma with parameters|P3XFQC6ACX]].

No consistency hypothesis is needed.
If $T$ is inconsistent, it proves every equivalence, and every sentence is a fixed point.

### Watch out: equivalence, not identity

The conclusion is a provable equivalence, and $\psi$ is not literally the sentence $\varphi(\ulcorner \psi \urcorner)$.
With prime-power codes, $\# \psi$ is larger than the length of $\psi$, and the numeral $\ulcorner \psi \urcorner$ has $\# \psi$ symbols $S$.
So $\varphi(\ulcorner \psi \urcorner)$ is longer than $\psi$, and the two cannot be the same string.
A property of sentences that is not preserved by provable equivalence, such as length, does not pass from one to the other.

### The converse

Is every sentence a fixed point of some formula?
Yes, and for a dull reason: $\psi$ is a fixed point of $\varphi(x) := \psi \wedge x = x$, since $T \vdash \psi \leftrightarrow (\psi \wedge \ulcorner \psi \urcorner = \ulcorner \psi \urcorner)$.
Fixed points of a given formula are not unique as sentences.
For $\varphi(x) := \neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(x)$ and $T \supseteq \mathsf{PA}$, all fixed points are $T$-equivalent to $\mathrm{Con}(T)$, as the [[second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]] shows.

### Generalizations

The [[diagonal lemma with parameters|P3XFQC6ACX]] gives a formula $\psi(\vec{y})$ with $T \vdash \psi(\vec{y}) \leftrightarrow \varphi(\ulcorner \psi(\vec{y}) \urcorner, \vec{y})$.
The [[uniform diagonal lemma|YEHH5WV8B6]] gives such a $\psi$ that works for every tuple of numerals.
The [[simultaneous diagonal lemma|R8TPVT9G5Z]] gives two sentences that refer to each other's codes.
In languages with a function symbol for each primitive recursive function, as in $\mathsf{PRA}$, the [[strong diagonal lemma|7PZDPCVK7Z]] gives a closed term $t$ with $T \vdash t = \ulcorner \varphi(t) \urcorner$.
The lemma is an arithmetic form of [[Kleene's recursion theorem|F3Z30M606H]] and of [[Lawvere's fixed point theorem|N84M16W19X]].

### Try it

Find a fixed point of $\varphi(x) := x = x$.
Any sentence provable in $T$ is one, since both sides of the equivalence are provable, so $0 = 0$ works.

Is the sentence $0 = S0$ a fixed point of $\neg \mathrm{Prov}_{\mathsf{PA}}(x)$ for $T = \mathsf{PA}$?
No: $\mathsf{PA}$ refutes $0 = S0$ but does not refute $\neg \mathrm{Prov}_{\mathsf{PA}}(\ulcorner 0 = S0 \urcorner)$, because that sentence is $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, which is true.

## History

Gödel used a diagonal construction for the one sentence he needed in his 1931 paper on incompleteness.
Carnap published the lemma in general form in 1934.
The lemma is also called the fixed point lemma.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The lemma says that arithmetic can build sentences that talk about themselves.
Take any property of sentences that a formula can express, such as "has an even code".
The lemma gives a sentence that says, in effect, "my own code is even".
The sentence does not contain its own code literally, but describes a calculation whose result is its code.
The phrase "yields a falsehood when appended to its own quotation", appended to its own quotation, works the same way.

### L2 human

We follow the construction for $\varphi(x) := \neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(x)$.
Let $d(n)$ be the code of the result of putting the numeral of $n$ into the formula with code $n$, and let $D(x, y)$ represent $d$ in $\mathsf{Q}$.
Set $\theta(x) := \exists y \, (D(x, y) \wedge \neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(y))$, which says "the diagonalization of $x$ is not provable".
Now put $\psi := \theta(\ulcorner \theta \urcorner)$.
The diagonalization of $\ulcorner \theta \urcorner$ is $\theta(\ulcorner \theta \urcorner)$, which is $\psi$ itself, so $\psi$ says "$\psi$ is not provable".
Formally, representability of $d$ gives $T \vdash \psi \leftrightarrow \neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \psi \urcorner)$.
This is the typical use: a sentence with a prescribed relation to its own provability or truth.

### L3 human

The lemma is a fixed point theorem: the map $\psi \mapsto \varphi(\ulcorner \psi \urcorner)$ on sentences, taken up to provable equivalence, has a fixed point.
Lawvere's theorem gives the categorical form: in a cartesian closed category, a point-surjective map $A \to B^A$ forces every endomap of $B$ to have a fixed point.
Here $A$ is the set of formulas with one free variable, $B$ is the set of sentences up to $T$-equivalence, and substitution of codes plays the role of evaluation.
The same pattern gives Cantor's theorem, Kleene's recursion theorem and the Y combinator of the lambda calculus.
