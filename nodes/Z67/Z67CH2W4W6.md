## Problem

Let $A$ be a real $n \times n$ matrix, $b \in \R^{n \times 1}$ and $c \in \R^{1 \times n}$, and let $\alpha < \beta$ be real numbers.
Let $\psi \colon [0, \infty) \times \R \to \R$ be piecewise continuous in $t$ and locally Lipschitz in $y$.
It belongs to the sector $[\alpha, \beta]$ if $\psi(t, 0) = 0$ and $\alpha y^2 \le y\, \psi(t, y) \le \beta y^2$ for all $t \ge 0$ and $y \in \R$.
Consider the Lur'e system, a linear system in [[feedback|7NYES2D0Z4]] with a nonlinearity:
$$x' = A x + b u, \qquad y = c x, \qquad u = -\psi(t, y).$$
The system is *absolutely stable* for the sector $[\alpha, \beta]$ if the following holds for every $\psi$ in the sector.
The origin is a globally uniformly [[asymptotically stable|0NB689782W]] equilibrium of this [[ordinary differential equation|0P97V20RW7]].
The problem: find conditions on $A$, $b$, $c$, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ under which the system is absolutely stable.
The conditions should preferably be stated in terms of the [[transfer function|86JKV3TRFD]] $G(s) = c(sI - A)^{-1} b$.

## Remarks

A necessary condition is that $A - k\, b c$ is Hurwitz for every constant gain $k \in [\alpha, \beta]$, since the linear functions $\psi(t, y) = k y$ lie in the sector.
Aizerman conjectured in 1949 that this condition is also sufficient for time-invariant nonlinearities, but the conjecture is false in general.
The circle criterion and the Popov criterion give sufficient conditions in the frequency domain.
Their proofs build quadratic Lyapunov functions, or quadratic plus integral terms, through the Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Many control loops contain a linear part that is well known and one nonlinear part, such as a valve that saturates.
Often the nonlinear part is only known to lie between two straight lines.
The question is whether the loop is stable for every nonlinearity in that range at once.
Checking straight lines alone is not enough, which makes the problem subtle, and the classical answers are tests on the frequency response of the linear part.
