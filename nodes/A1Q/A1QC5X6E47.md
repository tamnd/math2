## Summary

The relativistic Euler equations describe a perfect fluid, without viscosity or heat conduction, in special or general relativity.
They state that the energy–momentum tensor of the fluid and the number of its particles are conserved.
They reduce to the classical [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]] when speeds are small compared with the speed of light and pressure is small compared with energy density.
They model fluids in astrophysics, such as neutron stars, accretion flows and the early universe, and the quark–gluon plasma of heavy-ion collisions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a fluid moves close to the speed of light, or is so hot that its pressure is comparable to its energy, Newton's laws are not enough.
Relativity mixes mass, energy and momentum, so the fluid equations must count all three together.
The relativistic Euler equations do this for an ideal fluid without friction.
A key feature is that sound can never travel faster than light, which the equations guarantee when the equation of state is physical.
