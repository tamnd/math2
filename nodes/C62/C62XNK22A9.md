## Definition

Let $F$ be a field, such as $\R$, $\C$ or the field of rational functions in $s$.
A *signal-flow graph* over $F$ is a finite [[directed graph|0KFYGSHHF5]] with nodes $x_1, \ldots, x_N$ and a nonzero *gain* $a_{ij} \in F$ on each edge $x_j \to x_i$.
Loops are allowed, and there is at most one edge from each node to each node.
Put $a_{ij} = 0$ when there is no edge from $x_j$ to $x_i$.
A node with no incoming edges is a *source node* (an input).
The graph represents the system of linear equations
$$x_i = \sum_{j=1}^{N} a_{ij} \, x_j \quad \text{for each node } x_i \text{ that is not a source node},$$
in which the source nodes are given and the other nodes are unknowns.

## Remarks

The *gain* of a directed path or a directed cycle is the product of the gains of its edges.
Example: the nodes $r, e, y$ with edges $r \to e$ of gain $1$, $e \to y$ of gain $G$ and $y \to e$ of gain $-H$ encode $e = r - H y$ and $y = G e$, the negative feedback loop.
Solving gives $y = \frac{G}{1 + GH} r$ when $1 + GH \ne 0$.
Non-example: the relation $x_3 = x_1 x_2$ cannot be encoded, since each node must be a linear combination of nodes with constant gains.
If several edges join the same pair of nodes in one direction, they are merged into one edge whose gain is the sum of their gains.
