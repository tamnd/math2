## Summary

A travel-time curve is drawn for one type of seismic wave.
It gives the time the wave takes to reach a station as a function of the distance from the source.
Its slope at each distance is the [[ray parameter|JAFNCJNF6E]] of the arriving ray.
Travel-time curves are used to locate earthquakes and, by inversion, to find the speed of waves at each depth.
Tables of such curves, such as the Jeffreys–Bullen tables, were among the first quantitative models of the Earth's interior.

## Explanations

### L1 human

After an earthquake, stations near the source feel the shaking first and distant stations later.
Plot the arrival time against distance for many stations, and the points line up on a curve.
In a uniform medium the curve is a straight line through the origin with slope one over the speed.
In the real Earth the curve bends downward with distance, because rays to distant stations dive into deeper, faster rock.
Each kind of wave, direct, reflected or refracted, has its own branch of the curve.
