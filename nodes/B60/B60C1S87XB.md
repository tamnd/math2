## Summary

Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics describes how non-interacting classical particles share out among the energy levels of a system in thermal equilibrium.
Let $T$ be the temperature and $k_B$ the Boltzmann constant.
The mean number of particles in a level of energy $\varepsilon$ is proportional to $e^{-\varepsilon / k_B T}$.
It is the classical counterpart of [[Fermi–Dirac statistics|FM6XWC4BST]] and [[Bose–Einstein statistics|R5HWSRAK4H]], and both reduce to it when the occupation of every state is small.
Applied to the kinetic energy of a gas, it gives the [[Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution|3QSVWQJX11]] of molecular speeds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a crowd of molecules that keep bumping into each other and exchanging energy.
High-energy states are expensive, so fewer molecules sit in them, and the drop-off is exponential in the energy.
The temperature sets the scale: at high temperature the drop-off is gentle, at low temperature it is steep.
For example, with two levels at energies $0$ and $\varepsilon$, the ratio of the populations is $e^{-\varepsilon / k_B T}$.
If $\varepsilon = k_B T \ln 2$, the upper level holds half as many particles as the lower one.
