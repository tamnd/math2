## Summary

A phase transition is a change of a substance from one phase to another, such as melting, boiling or the onset of magnetism.
In thermodynamics it shows up as a point where the equilibrium free energy is not analytic in the control parameters, such as temperature and pressure.
Transitions are sorted by the [[Ehrenfest classification|5XVRB6MTK4]] or, today, into first-order and continuous ones.
They are mapped in a [[phase diagram|R6XCQYXKRP]] and studied near critical points by [[Landau theory|XJ4NRM74W6]] and the theory of [[critical phenomena|57789VKKAY]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Water can be ice, liquid or steam, and these are its phases.
When you heat ice at normal pressure, it stays ice until $0$ degrees Celsius and then suddenly turns into liquid.
That sudden change is a phase transition.
During melting, the ice takes in heat, the [[latent heat|FN6Z9QKAH5]], without getting warmer.
Other examples are a magnet that loses its magnetism above a certain temperature, and a metal that becomes superconducting when cooled.
