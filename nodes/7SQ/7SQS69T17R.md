## Summary

*Reverse mathematics* is the study of which set existence axioms are needed to prove the theorems of ordinary mathematics.
It works in subsystems of [[second-order arithmetic|DC60FYCSPM]], with the weak base theory $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
A theorem $\tau$, coded as a sentence of $L_2$, is *equivalent over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$* to a subsystem $S \supseteq \mathsf{RCA}_0$ when $S \vdash \tau$ and $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \tau$ proves every axiom of $S$.
The proof of the second half is the *reversal*: it derives the axioms from the theorem.
A main finding is that most theorems of countable mathematics are provable in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ or equivalent over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ to one of four stronger systems, and these five systems are called the big five.

## Motivation

A proof of a theorem shows that some axioms suffice.
It does not show that weaker axioms do not suffice.
Reverse mathematics asks for the weakest natural axioms, and it shows optimality by proving the axioms back from the theorem.
In second-order arithmetic, the axioms that vary are comprehension axioms, which say which sets exist.
So an equivalence measures how much set existence a theorem needs, and also how far from computable its proofs must be.

## Notation

For theories $S$ and $T$ in $L_2$, $S \equiv T$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ means that $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + S$ and $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + T$ prove the same sentences.
The big five, in increasing strength, are $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$.
The subscript $0$ marks restricted induction: the system has the set induction axiom and $\Sigma^0_1$ induction, but not the full induction scheme.

## Remarks

### Examples

- The [[existence of Turing jumps|10NR4PGXHW]] is equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
- The [[infinite pigeonhole principle for two colors|9EB4DXQMWK]] is provable in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ itself.
- $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves the [[intermediate value theorem|66WH4PQ392]].
- The [[Heine-Borel theorem for $[0, 1]$|KRS3VTWCHJ]] is equivalent to $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
- The [[Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem|HV153Y95MQ]] and [[König's lemma|3P2J5NWCM1]] are equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
- [[Ramsey's theorem for pairs|ZT5KV6KJ3E]] is a theorem of $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ that is not equivalent to any of the big five.

### Non-examples

- An [[equivalence over a strong base theory|E007FEBHYD]] is not a reverse mathematics result: over $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ the Heine-Borel and Bolzano-Weierstrass theorems are both provable, so they are equivalent, and this gives no information.
- A theorem about arbitrary functions from $\R$ to $\R$ has no direct statement in $L_2$, so its classification needs higher-order reverse mathematics.

### Basic properties

- If $\tau$ is equivalent over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ to $S$, then the $\omega$-models of $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \tau$ are the $\omega$-models of $S$. For example, $\tau$ fails in the $\omega$-model of the computable sets when $S$ is $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ or stronger.
- The lower systems have known first-order parts. $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ and $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ are [[conservative|N238J43XNE]] over $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ for arithmetic sentences, and $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ is conservative over Peano arithmetic.
- The big five are linearly ordered by strength. Many principles from combinatorics, such as Ramsey's theorem for pairs, fall outside this linear order.

### History

Harvey Friedman started the program in the 1970s.
Stephen Simpson and his students developed it into a systematic study.
Simpson's monograph *Subsystems of Second Order Arithmetic* (1999) is the standard reference.
Older parts of the program come from Hilbert and Bernays, who developed analysis in second-order arithmetic, and from Weyl's predicative analysis.

### Common errors

- An equivalence over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is a statement about provability in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$. It is not a claim that the theorem and the axiom are equivalent in the true model $(\N, \mathcal{P}(\N))$, where both are simply true.
- The result depends on the coding. A change of coding of reals or of continuous functions can change the classification, so each page fixes the coding of the [[definition of second-order arithmetic|Y92NXGEQBD]].
- The reversal must be proved in $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \tau$, without any comprehension beyond $\Delta^0_1$. A reversal that uses arithmetical comprehension proves nothing about $\mathsf{ACA}_0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Usually one starts from axioms and proves theorems.
Reverse mathematics also goes the other way: it starts from a theorem and proves the axioms from it.
If both directions work over a weak base, the theorem needs exactly those axioms.
For example, the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem says that every bounded sequence of reals has a convergent subsequence.
It is equivalent to the system $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ over the base system $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.

### L2 human

A reverse mathematics result has three parts.

1. Code the theorem in $L_2$. For the Heine-Borel theorem, an open cover is a sequence of open intervals with rational endpoints.
2. Prove the theorem in the target system $S$. For the Heine-Borel theorem, $S$ is $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.
3. Prove the axioms of $S$ in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ plus the theorem. For the Heine-Borel theorem, one builds a cover from a binary tree.

A trap in step 3 is to use a set whose existence needs more than $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension.
For example, the set of nodes of a tree with infinitely many extensions exists in $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, but not in general in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.

### L3 human

The big five correspond to levels of computability.
$\mathsf{RCA}_0$ matches computable mathematics, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ matches the $\mathrm{PA}$ degrees, $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ matches closure under the Turing jump, $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ matches transfinite iterations of the jump, and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ matches the hyperjump.
Two of them also correspond to foundational programs: $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ to a partial realization of Hilbert's program, and $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ to predicative reductionism.
Finer measures, such as Weihrauch reducibility and computable reducibility, refine the implications over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ into uniform reductions between problems.
