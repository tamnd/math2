## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a [[thermodynamic system|GYNNJBMTCP]].
A *state variable* of $\Sigma$ is a macroscopic quantity of $\Sigma$ that can be measured at a given time.
Examples are pressure, volume, temperature and amount of substance.
The *thermodynamic state* of $\Sigma$ at a given time is the list of values of its state variables at that time.
For a system in equilibrium, the state is fixed by finitely many independent state variables.
All other state variables are then functions of them.

## Remarks

For a fixed amount of a simple fluid in equilibrium, the pressure $p > 0$ and the volume $V > 0$ determine the state.
The temperature is then a function of $(p, V)$, given by the equation of state.
The full list of positions and velocities of the molecules is not a thermodynamic state: it is a microscopic state, and many such microstates correspond to the same thermodynamic state.
The thermodynamic state is also called the *macrostate*.
