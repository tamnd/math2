## Definition

The row space of a matrix is the subspace spanned by its rows.
Let $F$ be a field and let $A$ be an $m \times n$ [[matrix|0N1MY1T0W9]] over $F$ with rows $r_1, \dots, r_m$, each read as a vector of $F^n$.
The *row space* of $A$ is the [[linear span|0HR573CHNM]] of the rows,
$$\operatorname{Row}(A) = \operatorname{span}(r_1, \dots, r_m) = \{c_1 r_1 + \dots + c_m r_m : c_1, \dots, c_m \in F\} \subseteq F^n.$$

## Remarks

Each row has one entry for each column, so the row space lies in $F^n$, the space of the unknowns of $Ax = b$, and not in $F^m$.
The coefficients $c_i$ come from the field $F$, so the row space contains every combination of the rows and not only the rows themselves.
For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ has the row space $\{(t, 2t, 3t) : t \in F\}$, a line in $F^3$.
A case that fails: for $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, the vector $(1, 0)$ is a column but not a combination of the rows, so it is not in the row space.

Writing the rows as columns turns the row space of $A$ into the [[column space|03AJKXH1AV]] of $A^{\mathsf{T}}$.
[[Elementary row operations|04XWH28452]] do not change the row space, so [[row equivalent|006BHFWMAP]] matrices have the same row space.
The nonzero rows of a row echelon form of $A$ form a basis, by the [[echelon basis theorem|1JGMW9DM4Z]], and the [[concept page|069MCGX635]] works examples.
Its dimension equals the dimension of the column space, by the theorem that [[row rank equals column rank|0TZAG60ZYT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The row space collects all the mixtures of the rows of a matrix.
Row operations only mix the rows, so they keep the row space.
After row reduction, the nonzero rows that remain are a basis of it.
For the rows $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 6)$, only one row survives reduction, so the row space is the line through $(1, 2)$.
