## Summary

A Kac–Moody algebra is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]], usually infinite-dimensional, defined by generators and relations from a [[generalized Cartan matrix|38Z0HSXTCA]].
The relations copy the Chevalley–Serre presentation of a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra.
When the matrix is the Cartan matrix of a semisimple Lie algebra, the construction gives back that Lie algebra.
The [[affine Lie algebras|9A6RGRV056]] are the most studied infinite-dimensional examples, with links to modular forms, combinatorial identities and conformal field theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra over $\C$ is determined by a small integer matrix, its Cartan matrix.
One can write down generators and relations that read off the Lie algebra from this matrix.
Kac and Moody fed the same machine with more general integer matrices.
The output is a Lie algebra with a root system, a Weyl group and a theory of highest weight modules, but usually of infinite dimension.
For the $1 \times 1$ matrix $(2)$ the machine gives $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, while for the matrix with rows $(2, -2)$ and $(-2, 2)$ it gives an infinite-dimensional algebra built from loops in $\mathfrak{sl}_2$.
