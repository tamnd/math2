## Summary

Geodetic triangulation determines the positions of points by measuring angles in a network of triangles.
Only one side of the network, the baseline, needs to be measured as a length.
The other sides follow from the angles by the [[law of sines|0PASQ34GHG]], triangle after triangle.
Triangulation networks were the main tool of large surveys and of measurements of the [[figure of the Earth|4W7PGZ0B37]] before satellites.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Measuring long distances on the ground is hard, but measuring angles with a telescope is easy.
Measure one distance carefully, then measure the angles towards a third point from both ends.
This fixes a triangle, and its new sides become baselines for the next triangles.
A chain of triangles can cross a whole country with only one measured length.
