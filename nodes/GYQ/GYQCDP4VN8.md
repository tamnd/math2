## Summary

The ocean wave spectrum describes a random sea as a sum of many [[water waves|R9WC0Z7Z69]] of different frequencies and directions.
It tells how the energy of the sea surface is shared among them.
It is the spectral density of the elevation of the sea surface, viewed as a [[stationary random process|DT0QXJE705]].
From it one computes the significant wave height, the dominant period and the loads on ships and offshore structures.
Wave forecasting models predict the evolution of the spectrum rather than of individual waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The sea surface looks chaotic, but it can be thought of as many smooth regular waves of different lengths added together.
The spectrum is a graph that shows how much energy sits at each wave period.
A storm far away sends a narrow peak of long, regular swell; local wind makes a broad hump of short, choppy waves.
For example, a spectrum with a single sharp peak at a period of $10$ seconds describes a nearly regular swell, with crests passing every $10$ seconds.
