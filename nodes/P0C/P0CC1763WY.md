## Problem

Let a linear, homogeneous and non-dispersive dielectric medium at rest have permittivity $\varepsilon$ and permeability $\mu$.
Let $E$, $B$ be the [[electric|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] and [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]], $D = \varepsilon E$ the [[electric displacement field|NJVXQZ780B]] and $H = B / \mu$.
The problem is to decide which density is the momentum density of the electromagnetic field in the medium:
$$g_{\mathrm{M}} = D \times B \quad \text{(Minkowski)}, \qquad g_{\mathrm{A}} = \frac{1}{c^2} \, E \times H \quad \text{(Abraham)}.$$
For a light pulse of energy $\hbar \omega$ in a medium of [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] $n$, the two choices give the momenta $n \hbar \omega / c$ and $\hbar \omega / (n c)$.

## Remarks

The two densities differ by the factor $n^2 = \varepsilon \mu / (\varepsilon_0 \mu_0)$, so they agree only in vacuum.
Both come from energy–momentum tensors of the field in matter that are consistent with [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] in the medium; the Minkowski tensor is not symmetric, the Abraham tensor is.
The difference is a momentum carried by the medium, so the question is how the total momentum of the light and the medium is split.
A widely accepted view is that both answers are correct for different notions.
The Abraham momentum is the kinetic momentum of the light, and the Minkowski momentum is its canonical momentum.
Experiments measure the total momentum transfer, and different experiments are explained by one or the other expression once the force on the medium is included.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light carries momentum, so it pushes on what it hits.
Inside glass or water, two great physicists of the early twentieth century wrote down two different formulas for this momentum.
One formula says the momentum grows by the refractive index and the other says it shrinks by the same factor.
The debate is about how to split the momentum between the light and the material it travels through.
