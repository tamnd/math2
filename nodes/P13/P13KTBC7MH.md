## Summary

The Einstein tensor of a pseudo-Riemannian metric $g$ is the symmetric tensor $G = \mathrm{Ric} - \frac12 R \, g$, built from the [[Ricci curvature|4GTNM0WHDH]] and the [[scalar curvature|ZHX2VYQDZ3]].
Its divergence vanishes identically, by the contracted Bianchi identity.
This makes it the curvature side of the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] $G + \Lambda g = \kappa T$, whose matter side, the stress–energy tensor, also has zero divergence.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Einstein looked for a quantity built from the curvature of spacetime that could be set equal to the energy and momentum of matter.
Since energy and momentum are conserved, this quantity had to obey the same conservation law automatically.
The Ricci curvature alone does not, but subtracting half of the scalar curvature times the metric fixes it.
For the round sphere of radius $1$ in two dimensions, the Ricci tensor is $g$ and the scalar curvature is $2$, so $G = g - g = 0$.
In two dimensions the Einstein tensor is always zero.
