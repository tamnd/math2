## Definition

*Seismology* is the study of:

1. the propagation of elastic waves through the Earth and other planetary bodies, modelled by the equations of [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] with variable density and elastic moduli;
2. the sources of these waves, chiefly earthquakes, described as dislocations or equivalent forces inside the body;
3. the [[inverse problems|PCMW7KN5P4]] of recovering the sources and the internal structure from records of ground motion at the surface.

## Remarks

The two basic body waves are the [[P-wave|EEPJJHC5JZ]] and the [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]]; the other waves of seismology arise from them at boundaries.
Example: finding the epicentre of an earthquake from the arrival times of P-waves at several stations is a seismological problem.
It combines a wave model (clause 1) with a source (clause 2) and an inversion (clause 3).
Non-example: mapping the Earth's gravity field from satellite orbits is part of geodesy, not seismology, because no elastic waves are involved.
The word comes from the Greek *seismos*, an earthquake.
