## Summary

Euclidean quantum gravity is an approach to [[quantum gravity|M1VXRT5X56]] that writes the gravitational path integral as a sum over Riemannian metrics.
These metrics come from Lorentzian ones by a [[Wick rotation|G686S8SEK0]].
Each metric is weighted by $e^{-I/\hbar}$, where $I$ is the Euclidean Einstein–Hilbert action.
It was developed mainly by Stephen Hawking and Gary Gibbons in the late 1970s.
It gives a quick derivation of the temperature and entropy of black holes and underlies the [[Hartle–Hawking state|TDGEHS585Q]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In quantum mechanics, a [[path integral|1NYTFT5NPE]] adds up all possible histories, each with a complex phase.
Replacing real time by imaginary time turns the phases into real weights, much as in statistical physics.
For gravity the histories are whole geometries, and imaginary time turns spacetime into an ordinary curved space with no distinction between time and space.
For example, imaginary time around a black hole must be periodic, and the period is exactly the inverse of the Hawking temperature.
The method gives striking results, but the sum over geometries is not mathematically defined in general.
