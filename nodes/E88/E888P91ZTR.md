## Example

Use units where the gravitational constant and the speed of light equal $1$.
Fix a mass parameter $m \in \R$ and a NUT parameter $n > 0$, and set
$$f(r) = \frac{r^2 - 2mr - n^2}{r^2 + n^2}.$$
In coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$, on a region where $f(r) > 0$, the *Taub–NUT metric* is
$$g = -f(r)\left(dt + 2n\cos\theta\, d\phi\right)^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f(r)} + \left(r^2 + n^2\right)\left(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2\right).$$
A direct computation shows that its [[Ricci curvature|4GTNM0WHDH]] vanishes, so it is a [[vacuum solution|JRV1T69FYJ]].

Checks: for $n = 0$, $f = 1 - 2m/r$ and $g$ becomes the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]].
The zeros of $r^2 - 2mr - n^2$ are $r_\pm = m \pm \sqrt{m^2 + n^2}$, so for $n \ne 0$ there are always two of them, of opposite sign.
In the region $r_- < r < r_+$ one has $f < 0$, so $r$ is a time coordinate.
There the metric describes a homogeneous cosmology, the Taub region; outside it lie the NUT regions.

The term $2n\cos\theta\, d\phi$ is singular on the axis $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$, the Misner strings.
Charles Misner showed that the singularity disappears if $t$ is made periodic with period $8\pi n$.
Then the surfaces $r = \text{const}$ are three-spheres fibred over $S^2$ by the Hopf fibration, and the NUT regions contain [[closed timelike curves|DCM7BJ25R8]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Taub–NUT space is a vacuum solution of Einstein's equations with an extra parameter $n$ besides the mass.
The parameter $n$ acts like a gravitational analogue of a magnetic monopole charge: it twists time around the axis.
To remove the bad behaviour on the axis, time has to be made periodic, which leads to closed timelike curves.
Charles Misner called it a counterexample to almost anything, because it violates many properties one might hope a reasonable spacetime has.
