## Summary

A Bernstein algebra is a commutative [[baric algebra|A6HRHW6CBY]] whose weight $w$ satisfies $(x^2)^2 = w(x)^2 x^2$ for every element $x$.
The identity expresses the stationarity principle of population genetics: after one generation of random mating the gene frequencies no longer change.
The notion goes back to Sergei Bernstein's work of the 1920s on the Hardy–Weinberg law.
Bernstein's problem asks for a classification of the inheritance laws of this kind.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a population as a vector of weight $1$, and of squaring as producing the next generation by random mating.
In the simplest genetics, the generation after next has the same frequencies as the next generation: nothing changes after one step.
In formulas, the square of the square equals the square, for vectors of weight $1$.
The Bernstein identity is this rule written for vectors of any weight.
Mendel's law for one gene with two alleles is the standard example.
