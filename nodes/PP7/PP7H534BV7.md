## Definition

A *finite-horizon stochastic dynamic program* consists of:

1. a number $T \ge 1$ of stages and, for each $t = 0, \dots, T$, a finite set $S_t$ of states;
2. for each $t < T$ and $s \in S_t$, a finite nonempty set $A_t(s)$ of actions;
3. for each $t < T$, $s \in S_t$ and $a \in A_t(s)$, a probability distribution $P_t(\cdot \mid s, a)$ on $S_{t+1}$ and a cost $c_t(s, a) \in \R$;
4. a terminal cost $c_T \colon S_T \to \R$.

A policy chooses at each stage $t$ an action in $A_t(s_t)$ as a function of the states seen so far.
The next state $s_{t+1}$ is then drawn from $P_t(\cdot \mid s_t, a_t)$.
The problem is to find a policy that minimizes the expected total cost $\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} c_t(s_t, a_t) + c_T(s_T)\big]$ from a given initial state $s_0$.
The *stochastic dynamic programming method* computes the functions $f_t \colon S_t \to \R$ by backward recursion,
$$f_T = c_T, \qquad f_t(s) = \min_{a \in A_t(s)} \Big( c_t(s, a) + \sum_{s' \in S_{t+1}} P_t(s' \mid s, a) \, f_{t+1}(s') \Big).$$
At stage $t$ in state $s$ it chooses an action that attains the minimum.

## Remarks

Example: a gambler has $T$ bets left and $s$ coins, and may stake any whole number $a \le s$ of coins on a bet won with probability $p$.
Then $s_{t+1} = s + a$ or $s - a$ with probabilities $p$ and $1 - p$, and minimizing the cost $c_T(s) = -\mathbf{1}[s \ge N]$ maximizes the probability of ending with at least $N$ coins.
With $S_t = \set{0, 1, \dots, 2^t s_0}$, all four clauses hold, and $f_0(s_0)$ is minus that maximal probability.

Non-example: if the law of $s_{t+1}$ depends on $s_{t-1}$ as well as on $s_t$ and $a_t$, clause 3 fails.
Then the recursion above in the state $s_t$ alone is no longer correct, and one must enlarge the state to the pair $(s_{t-1}, s_t)$.

The value $f_0(s_0)$ equals the minimal expected total cost by the [[principle of optimality|5K2BKE7W93]].
Infinite-horizon problems with discounting lead to the stationary [[Bellman equation|Z94Z6BY8JG]] of a [[Markov decision process|WCNHWTJXYD]].
