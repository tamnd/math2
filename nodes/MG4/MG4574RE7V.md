## Example

We show $\mathsf{IPC} \subsetneq \mathsf{ML}$.

1. $\mathsf{IPC} \subseteq \mathsf{ML}$: every theorem of $\mathsf{IPC}$ is valid on every frame, by soundness in [[the completeness theorem|480TCT5CJ9]].
2. Let $\kappa$ be the Kreisel-Putnam axiom $(\neg p \to q \vee r) \to (\neg p \to q) \vee (\neg p \to r)$.
3. $\kappa \in \mathsf{ML}$, by [[the Medvedev frame argument|28FPR0F5WK]].
4. $\kappa \notin \mathsf{IPC}$: [[the frame with a root below three maximal worlds|ZBTCC4C61S]] refutes it.
5. So $\kappa$ separates the two logics.

## Remarks

The countermodel of step 4 lives on a frame that is not a Medvedev frame.
That frame has three maximal worlds but only one other world, while a rooted generated subframe of a Medvedev frame with three maximal worlds is $M_3$, with seven worlds.
So intuitionistic logic needs more frames than the Medvedev frames, and the extra frames refute $\kappa$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Intuitionistic logic allows any pattern of growth of knowledge.
Medvedev's logic allows only the patterns of narrowing down a finite set of candidates.
Fewer patterns means more laws.
The Kreisel-Putnam principle is one of the extra laws.

### L2 human

1. Prove $\kappa$ on every Medvedev frame using the least world above $I$ that forces $\neg p$.
2. Refute $\kappa$ on the three-pronged fork: $p$ at one leaf, $q$ and $r$ at the other two.
3. Conclude that $\mathsf{IPC}$ is a proper sublogic of $\mathsf{ML}$.

The typical use is to see that $\mathsf{ML}$ is a proper intermediate logic.
A trap: both logics have the disjunction property, so that property does not separate them.
