## Summary

Poroelasticity is the theory of elastic solids whose connected pores are filled with a moving fluid, such as saturated soils, rocks, bone and hydrogels.
The deformation of the skeleton and the pressure of the pore fluid act on each other.
Squeezing the solid raises the pore pressure, and the pore pressure pushes the solid apart.
The fluid flows according to [[Darcy's law|DX8RV2XG6H]], so the response is delayed in time by diffusion of pressure.
The linear theory was founded by Maurice Biot and its quasi-static form is [[Biot consolidation theory|AX26BVMSXQ]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Press on a wet sponge: at first the water resists, then it slowly escapes and the sponge settles.
Soil under a new building behaves the same way, settling for months or years as water is squeezed out.
Poroelasticity describes this interplay between an elastic frame and the fluid in its pores.
How fast the settling happens depends on how easily the fluid can flow and on how far it has to go.
