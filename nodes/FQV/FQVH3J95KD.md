## Summary

The Einstein–Infeld–Hoffmann equations are the equations of motion of $N$ gravitating point masses in general relativity at the first post-Newtonian order.
They add to Newton's inverse-square accelerations correction terms of relative size $v^2/c^2$ and $GM/(rc^2)$, which depend on the velocities and on the other bodies.
They were derived from the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] themselves, showing that the motion of bodies follows from the field equations.
They are used for precise solar-system ephemerides and as the starting point of the relativistic [[n-body problem|62SNWC5E45]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Newton's law says each body is pulled towards every other body with a force that falls off as the square of the distance.
General relativity changes this slightly: the pull also depends on how fast the bodies move and on the gravity of the other bodies around.
The Einstein–Infeld–Hoffmann equations write down these small corrections for a system of many bodies, like the Sun and the planets.
The corrections are tiny for the solar system, about one part in a hundred million for the Earth.
They are still large enough to explain effects such as the extra turning of Mercury's orbit.
