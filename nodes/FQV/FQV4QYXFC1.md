## Summary

Near a [[fixed point of the renormalization group|H0B5TN4139]], a small perturbation of the couplings either grows or shrinks under repeated coarse-graining.
Perturbations that grow are called relevant, those that shrink are called irrelevant, and those that neither grow nor shrink to first order are called marginal.
The relevant directions are the parameters one must tune to reach a critical point, such as the temperature and the magnetic field of a magnet.
Their growth rates give the [[critical exponents|SQ41AZGZ2P]], and the irrelevant directions explain [[universality|GWXP0KSW9Y]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture the space of all possible models as a landscape, and the renormalization group as a flow on it.
A critical fixed point is like a mountain pass: along some directions the flow runs away from it, and along others it runs into it.
The directions that run away are relevant: a tiny change in them becomes large at long distances.
The directions that run in are irrelevant: details of the model, such as the exact shape of the lattice, fade away at long distances.
For the Ising magnet there are two relevant directions, the temperature and the external magnetic field.
