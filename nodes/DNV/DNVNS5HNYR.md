## Summary

A signaling game is a two-player game in which an informed sender sends a message and an uninformed receiver then acts.
The sender knows her type, the receiver only knows the prior distribution of types and sees the message.
The receiver's action depends on what the message reveals, so the sender chooses the message with this in mind.
Signaling games model education as a signal of ability, warranties as signals of quality, and many signals in biology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A job applicant knows how able she is, but the employer does not.
The applicant can choose to earn a degree, and the employer sees the degree before offering a wage.
If earning the degree is easier for able applicants, the degree can tell the employer something true about ability.
This interplay, where actions are chosen partly for what they reveal, is what a signaling game captures.
An equilibrium where different types send different messages is called separating, and one where all types send the same message is called pooling.
