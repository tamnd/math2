## Summary

Take a [[function|0XHDTE5YMZ]] $f$ whose values can be compared, and a point $c$ of its domain.

The number $f(c)$ is a global (or absolute) maximum of $f$ when no value of $f$ on the whole domain is larger than $f(c)$. That is, $f(c) \ge f(x)$ for every $x$ in the domain.

The number $f(c)$ is a global (or absolute) minimum of $f$ when no value of $f$ on the whole domain is smaller than $f(c)$. That is, $f(c) \le f(x)$ for every $x$ in the domain.

The word global means that the comparison runs over the entire domain, not only over points near $c$.
