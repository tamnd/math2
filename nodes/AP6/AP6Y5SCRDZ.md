## Summary

The Landau–Lifshitz pseudotensor is a symmetric array $t^{\mu\nu}_{LL}$ built from the metric and its first derivatives.
It represents the energy, momentum and stress of the gravitational field in a given coordinate system.
Added to the matter [[stress–energy tensor|REDZ92MKXX]], weighted by $-\det g$, it gives a total that obeys an ordinary conservation law $\partial_\nu \bigl( (-g)(T^{\mu\nu} + t^{\mu\nu}_{LL}) \bigr) = 0$.
It is not a tensor: it can be made zero at any one point by a choice of coordinates.
Its integrals over all of space in asymptotically flat coordinates give the total energy and momentum of an isolated system.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The energy of gravity cannot be assigned to points in a way that all observers agree on, because a freely falling observer feels no gravity at all near them.
Landau and Lifshitz found a formula that still assigns an energy density to gravity, but one that depends on the coordinates.
With it, the total of matter energy and gravity energy is conserved, just as in other field theories.
In flat space with inertial coordinates the pseudotensor is zero, but in spherical coordinates on the same flat space it is not.
