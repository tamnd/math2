## Summary

An identity of a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] is a nonassociative polynomial that vanishes when its variables are replaced by any elements of the algebra.
The commutative law $xy - yx$, the associative law $(xy)z - x(yz)$, the [[Jacobi identity|ZKFN197MBR]] and the Jordan identity are examples.
Classes of algebras such as associative, [[alternative|N8K9SSVMRN]], Lie and Jordan algebras are defined by identities; each such class is a variety of algebras.
For associative algebras, the theory of identities is the theory of [[polynomial identity rings|C0EDT81EGH]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An identity is a formula in some letters that comes out zero in the algebra, whatever elements you put in for the letters.
For example $xy - yx$ is an identity of every commutative algebra.
Identities are the laws that an algebra obeys.
Choosing a list of laws picks out a family of algebras: all algebras that obey them.
