## Summary

Thermodynamic temperature is the absolute [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]] scale defined from the second law of thermodynamics, independent of any thermometric material.
For ordinary systems it is positive, its zero is absolute zero, and its unit is the kelvin.
It is the temperature $T$ that appears in the formula $dS = \delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}} / T$ and in the efficiency of the Carnot engine.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A mercury thermometer and an alcohol thermometer can disagree a little, because the two liquids expand differently.
Thermodynamic temperature avoids this by using an ideal reversible engine instead of a liquid.
The ratio of two temperatures is the ratio of the heats such an engine takes in and gives out between them.
On this scale water freezes at about $273$ kelvin and boils at about $373$ kelvin at normal pressure.
Nothing can be colder than $0$ kelvin.
