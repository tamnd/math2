## Summary

A proper equilibrium is a refinement of the [[trembling hand perfect equilibrium|GTKFGTX0B3]] introduced by Roger Myerson.
Players still make small mistakes, but costly mistakes are much less likely than cheap ones.
Every proper equilibrium is trembling hand perfect, and every finite game has a proper equilibrium.
It removes some perfect equilibria that depend on implausible patterns of mistakes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the trembling hand story, every mistake may happen with some small probability.
Proper equilibrium adds one sensible rule: a player is far less likely to make a mistake that costs a lot than one that costs a little.
So the worse a wrong move is, the more careful the player is to avoid it.
This rule matters when a game contains some very bad moves that nobody should play.
Adding such moves to a game can create new perfect equilibria, and the properness rule removes them again.
