## Definition

Fix a constant $\hbar > 0$, the reduced Planck constant.
A *quantum-mechanical system* is a pair $(\mathcal{H}, H)$, where $\mathcal{H}$ is a separable complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] and $H$ is a self-adjoint operator on $\mathcal{H}$, the [[Hamiltonian|VXDEQB46GV]].
It is read through the [[Dirac–von Neumann axioms|J1YDW3BE56]]:

1. the states of the system are the [[density operators|V1JTB52E8C]] on $\mathcal{H}$;
2. the measurable quantities are the [[observables|DF5RN297GE]], the self-adjoint operators on $\mathcal{H}$;
3. a state $\rho$ at time $0$ becomes $U(t) \rho\, U(t)^*$ at time $t$, where $U(t) = e^{-itH/\hbar}$ is a [[unitary operator|MM0EJ2GDE4]].

*Quantum mechanics* is the theory of such systems.

## Remarks

Example: take $\mathcal{H} = \C^2$ and $H = \mathrm{diag}(E_0, E_1)$ with real $E_0, E_1$.
Then $H$ is self-adjoint, and $U(t) = \mathrm{diag}(e^{-iE_0 t/\hbar}, e^{-iE_1 t/\hbar})$ is unitary, so $(\C^2, H)$ is a quantum-mechanical system, a two-level system.

Non-example: on $\C^2$, the matrix $H = iI$ is not self-adjoint.
Then $e^{-itH/\hbar} = e^{t/\hbar} I$ is not unitary, and it does not keep the trace of a density operator equal to $1$.

When the state is pure and given by a unit vector $\psi$, clause 3 becomes $\psi(t) = U(t)\psi$, the solution of the abstract Schrödinger equation $i\hbar\, \psi'(t) = H\psi(t)$ for $\psi \in D(H)$.
