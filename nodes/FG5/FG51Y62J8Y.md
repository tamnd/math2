## Summary

Constructive quantum field theory is the branch of mathematical physics that builds interacting [[quantum field theories|W031YNVRE3]] as rigorous mathematical objects.
A construction must satisfy a set of axioms, such as the [[Wightman axioms|5QTZQV6TNG]] or the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms.
Its main successes are interacting models in two and three spacetime dimensions, such as the [[quartic interaction|HVGDXMPB29]].
Whether interacting gauge theories exist in four dimensions is a major open problem, the [[Yang–Mills existence and mass gap|VXC1Y32FD6]] problem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Physicists compute with quantum fields using formal series and integrals that often do not make sense as written.
Constructive field theory asks whether there is a true mathematical object behind these computations.
The usual method is to cut the problem down to a finite box and a finite lattice.
Then one proves that the results have a limit as the cuts are removed.
For the $\phi^4$ model in a two-dimensional spacetime, this program succeeded and gives a theory of interacting particles.
