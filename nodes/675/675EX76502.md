## Definition

Let $A$ be a set, and let $M(A)$ be the [[free magma|ZZF8XPBG3Z]] on $A$.
Its elements are the binary trees with leaves labelled by $A$.
A tree that is not a single leaf is a pair $t = (t', t'')$ of a left and a right subtree.
A *Hall set* on $A$ is a subset $H \subseteq M(A)$ with a [[total order|0FRXBVVR03]] $<$ such that:

1. $A \subseteq H$.
2. If $t = (t', t'') \in H \setminus A$, then $t'' \in H$ and $t < t''$.
3. A tree $t = (t', t'') \in M(A) \setminus A$ lies in $H$ if and only if $t', t'' \in H$, $t' < t''$, and either $t' \in A$ or $t' = (u, v)$ with $v \ge t''$.

Let $k$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]] and let $L(A)$ be the [[free Lie algebra|NNAXK7BM3H]] over $k$ on $A$.
For $t \in M(A)$ let $P_t \in L(A)$ be given by $P_a = a$ for $a \in A$ and $P_{(t', t'')} = [P_{t'}, P_{t''}]$.
The *Hall basis* attached to $H$ is the family $(P_h)_{h \in H}$.
A standard theorem states that for every Hall set $H$ this family is a basis of $L(A)$ as a vector space.

## Remarks

Example: let $A = \set{a, b}$.
There is a Hall set on $A$ whose elements of degree at most $3$ are, in increasing order, $a < (a, (a, b)) < (a, b) < ((a, b), b) < b$.
The tree $(a, b)$ satisfies clause 3 since $a \in A$ and $a < b$, and clause 2 since $(a, b) < b$.
The tree $((a, b), b)$ satisfies clause 3 since $(a, b) < b$ and the right subtree $b$ of $(a, b)$ satisfies $b \ge b$.
The basis elements of degree at most $3$ are $a$, $b$, $[a, b]$, $[a, [a, b]]$ and $[[a, b], b]$.

Non-example: the tree $(b, a)$ is not in this Hall set, because $b < a$ fails in clause 3.
This matches the relation $[b, a] = -[a, b]$, which makes $P_{(b, a)}$ redundant.

Some texts use the mirror-image convention, with $t' > t''$ and the order reversed.
The set of Lyndon words with their standard bracketing, ordered lexicographically, is a Hall set; it gives the [[Lyndon basis|EERQK96TFT]].
