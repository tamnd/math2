## Definition

Fix an angular frequency $\omega > 0$ and the time factor $e^{-i\omega t}$, and let the background be vacuum with wavenumber $k = \omega/c$.
Let the scatterer be one of two kinds.
It is either a bounded obstacle $D \subset \R^3$ with perfectly conducting boundary, or a medium with [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]] $\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})$ equal to $\varepsilon_0$ outside a bounded set $D$.
Let the *incident field* $\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{inc}}$ be a solution of the source-free [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] in vacuum on all of $\R^3$, such as a [[plane wave|NM024G378A]].
The *electromagnetic scattering problem* is to find the *total field* $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{inc}} + \mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{sc}}$ such that:

1. $\mathbf{E}$ satisfies the time-harmonic Maxwell equations with the scatterer, outside $D$ for an obstacle or in all of $\R^3$ for a medium;
2. for an obstacle, $\hat{\mathbf{n}} \times \mathbf{E} = 0$ on $\partial D$;
3. the *scattered field* $\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{sc}}$ satisfies the [[Silver–Müller radiation condition|HSPJC4VVQ8]].

## Remarks

The scattered field has the asymptotic form $\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{sc}}(r\hat{\mathbf{x}}) = \dfrac{e^{ikr}}{r}\left( \mathbf{E}_\infty(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) + O(1/r) \right)$, and $\mathbf{E}_\infty$ is the *far-field pattern*.
For an incident plane wave of unit amplitude, the *scattering cross-section* is $C_{\mathrm{sca}} = \int_{S^2} \abs{\mathbf{E}_\infty}^2 \, d\Omega$, the scattered power divided by the incident intensity.
The radiation condition singles out outgoing waves and makes the problem uniquely solvable for a perfect conductor.
It does the same for a medium whose permittivity has non-negative imaginary part, under mild regularity assumptions.
Example: a small dielectric sphere in a plane wave gives [[Rayleigh scattering|CVKPJ23EYR]], whose far-field pattern is that of an oscillating dipole.
Non-example: the field $\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{inc}}$ itself is not a scattered field, since a plane wave does not satisfy the radiation condition.
The [[optical theorem|ZJG1YAGANG]] relates the total power removed from the incident wave to the forward value of $\mathbf{E}_\infty$.
