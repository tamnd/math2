## Summary

The Press–Schechter formalism predicts how many dark matter halos of each mass exist in the universe at a given time.
It starts from a Gaussian linear density field and smooths it on the scale that contains the mass $M$.
Every region whose linear overdensity exceeds the threshold $\delta_c \approx 1.686$ of the [[spherical collapse model|A8YJ7EKMV7]] counts as collapsed.
William Press and Paul Schechter introduced it in 1974.
It gives an analytic [[halo mass function|6K40RAVSAT]] that is still the starting point of more accurate fits.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture the early density field as a landscape of hills and valleys, made smoother or rougher depending on how much you blur it.
Blur it on the size of a galaxy cluster and count the hills higher than a fixed level: these regions have collapsed into clusters.
Blur it less, on the size of a galaxy, and you count galaxy-sized halos instead.
Because large blurs leave only gentle hills, massive halos are rare, and their number falls off exponentially with mass.
