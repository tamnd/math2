## Definition

Consider a single crystal under small strains, with [[Cauchy stress|KG2XWEMJ30]] $\sigma$.
Its [[infinitesimal strain|X121AYEY67]] is split as $\varepsilon = \varepsilon^e + \varepsilon^p$ into elastic and plastic parts.
A *slip system* $\alpha$, for $\alpha = 1, \dots, N$, is a pair of orthogonal unit vectors $(s^\alpha, m^\alpha)$ fixed by the crystal lattice.
Here $s^\alpha$ is the slip direction and $m^\alpha$ is the normal of the slip plane.
A *crystal plasticity* model is a constitutive model with the following three parts.

1. Kinematics: the plastic strain rate is a sum of shears on the slip systems. It is $\dot\varepsilon^p = \sum_{\alpha=1}^N \dot\gamma^\alpha \tfrac12 (s^\alpha \otimes m^\alpha + m^\alpha \otimes s^\alpha)$, where $\dot\gamma^\alpha$ is the slip rate of system $\alpha$.
2. Driving force: the *resolved shear stress* on system $\alpha$ is $\tau^\alpha = s^\alpha \cdot \sigma m^\alpha$.
3. Flow rule: system $\alpha$ can slip only when $\abs{\tau^\alpha}$ reaches its critical resolved shear stress $\tau_c^\alpha > 0$ (Schmid's law). In rate-dependent versions, $\dot\gamma^\alpha$ is a given increasing function of $\tau^\alpha / \tau_c^\alpha$. Hardening laws describe how the $\tau_c^\alpha$ evolve.

## Remarks

Example: a face-centred cubic crystal has the $12$ slip systems with $m^\alpha$ along a $\{111\}$ normal and $s^\alpha$ along a $\langle 110 \rangle$ direction in that plane.
Each pair is orthogonal, since $(1,1,1) \cdot (1,-1,0) = 0$.
Under uniaxial tension $\sigma = \sigma_0 \, e \otimes e$, the resolved shear stress is $\tau^\alpha = \sigma_0 (e \cdot s^\alpha)(e \cdot m^\alpha)$, the product of $\sigma_0$ with the Schmid factor.
Non-example: isotropic von Mises plasticity is not a crystal plasticity model.
Its plastic flow is along the stress deviator and is not restricted to a finite set of slip directions.
At finite strains the kinematics uses the [[multiplicative decomposition|S6XKB3012V]] $F = F^e F^p$ with plastic velocity gradient $\dot F^p (F^p)^{-1} = \sum_\alpha \dot\gamma^\alpha s^\alpha \otimes m^\alpha$.
