## Summary

The virial expansion writes the [[equation of state|YPT3SJSS5T]] of a gas as a power series in the density.
Its coefficients depend only on the temperature.
The first term gives the ideal gas, and the following terms are corrections from interactions between two, three and more molecules.
The coefficients, called virial coefficients, can be measured and also computed from the intermolecular forces in statistical mechanics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An ideal gas obeys $pV = nRT$ exactly, but a real gas does not.
At low density the error is small, and the virial expansion describes it step by step.
The first correction comes from pairs of molecules that meet, the next from triples, and so on.
A gas with strong attraction has a negative first correction, so its pressure is a bit lower than the ideal value.
