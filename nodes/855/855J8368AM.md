## Example

We prove two sentences in [[Peano arithmetic|Z8YDBW2857]], each by a single instance of the [[induction schema|G55NMADJ6G]].
Neither needs a lemma, and neither is provable from the other axioms of [[Robinson arithmetic|TT8MXRTVBM]] alone.

Claim 1: $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \forall x\,(S x \ne x)$.

1. We use induction on $x$ for the open formula $\varphi(x) := S x \ne x$.
2. The base $S 0 \ne 0$ is an instance of $\mathsf{Q}1$.
3. For the step, assume $S x \ne x$, and suppose that $S S x = S x$.
4. Then $\mathsf{Q}2$, applied to $S x$ and $x$, gives $S x = x$, which contradicts the assumption.
   So $S S x \ne S x$.
5. The induction axiom for $\varphi$ gives $\forall x\,(S x \ne x)$.

Claim 2: the axiom $\mathsf{Q}3$, $\forall x\,(x \ne 0 \to \exists y\,(x = S y))$, follows from induction without using $\mathsf{Q}3$.

6. We use induction on $x$ for $\psi(x) := x = 0 \lor \exists y\,(x = S y)$.
7. The base $\psi(0)$ holds, because $0 = 0$.
8. For the step, $S x = S x$, so $\exists y\,(S x = S y)$ holds with witness $y = x$.
   So $\psi(S x)$ holds, even without the induction hypothesis.
9. The induction axiom gives $\forall x\, \psi(x)$, which is logically equivalent to $\mathsf{Q}3$.

## Remarks

Claim 1 separates $\mathsf{PA}$ from $\mathsf{Q}$.
In [[this model of Q|PR8Z1YPJDK]] the extra element $a$ has $S(a) = a$, so $\mathsf{Q}$ does not prove $\forall x\,(S x \ne x)$.

Claim 2 shows that $\mathsf{Q}3$ is redundant in $\mathsf{PA}$, but it is not redundant in $\mathsf{Q}$.
For a witness, take the polynomials in one variable $X$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{N}$, with $S(p) = p + 1$ and the usual sum and product.
This structure satisfies $\mathsf{Q}1$, $\mathsf{Q}2$ and $\mathsf{Q}4$ to $\mathsf{Q}7$, with $<$ defined by $\mathsf{Q}8$.
But $X \ne 0$, and $X$ is not of the form $p + 1$, since $p = X - 1$ has a negative coefficient.

Claim 1 inducts on an open formula and Claim 2 on a $\Sigma_1$ formula, so both proofs work already in the fragment $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"No number is its own next number" sounds certain, but the rules of Robinson arithmetic do not prove it.
With induction it is quick.
The number $0$ is not its own next number, and if $x$ is not, then neither is $x + 1$, since equal successors have equal predecessors.
The second claim says that every number other than $0$ has a predecessor, and induction proves it in one line.

### L2 human

We check the step of Claim 1 at $x = \overline{2}$ in the standard model.
The hypothesis $S\,\overline{2} \ne \overline{2}$ says $3 \ne 2$.
Suppose that $S S\,\overline{2} = S\,\overline{2}$, that is, $4 = 3$.
Then $\mathsf{Q}2$ would give $3 = 2$, against the hypothesis, so $4 \ne 3$, which is $\varphi(\overline{3})$.
The typical use of Claim 2 is to shorten the axiom list of $\mathsf{PA}$.
The trap is to think that Claim 2 proves $\mathsf{Q}3$ in $\mathsf{Q}$, since it uses an induction axiom, which $\mathsf{Q}$ lacks.
