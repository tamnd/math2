## Definition

Let a pure substance have phases with [[chemical potentials|WVH4PVW64P]] $\mu_1(T, p), \dots, \mu_k(T, p)$.
A *triple point* of the substance is a pair $(T, p)$ at which three distinct phases $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ satisfy
$$\mu_\alpha(T, p) = \mu_\beta(T, p) = \mu_\gamma(T, p) = \min_{1 \le i \le k} \mu_i(T, p).$$
Then the three phases coexist in [[thermodynamic equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]].

## Remarks

In the [[phase diagram|R6XCQYXKRP]], a triple point is a point labelled by three phases.
Water has a triple point of ice, liquid and vapour at $T = 273.16\,\mathrm{K}$ and $p \approx 611.657\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
A non-example: the normal melting point of ice, $T = 273.15\,\mathrm{K}$ and $p = 101.325\,\mathrm{kPa}$, is not a triple point.
Only ice and liquid have the minimal chemical potential there; vapour is not stable at that pressure.
A substance can have several triple points; water has more, which involve different crystalline forms of ice.
