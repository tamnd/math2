## Summary

The LIBOR market model models a finite set of forward interest rates for consecutive periods, the forward LIBOR rates, as lognormal processes.
Each forward rate is a martingale under its own forward measure.
This measure uses the bond maturing at the end of the period as numeraire.
In this model caplets are priced by Black's formula, which matches market practice.
It is also called the BGM model, after Brace, Gatarek and Musiela, who developed it in 1997.
Jamshidian and Miltersen, Sandmann and Sondermann did related work at the same time.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Interest rate markets quote rates for fixed periods, such as three months, rather than instantaneous rates.
The LIBOR market model works directly with these quoted rates for a grid of future periods.
Each rate moves randomly in proportion to its own level, so it stays positive, like a stock price in the Black–Scholes model.
For example, a cap on the three-month rate a year from now is priced by the same kind of formula as a stock option.
