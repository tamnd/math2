## Summary

Damping is the loss of energy of an oscillating system through friction or other resistance, which makes the oscillations decrease.
The basic model is viscous damping, a force proportional to the velocity, which gives the [[damped harmonic oscillator|0RNXVVTJTJ]].
Its strength is measured by the damping ratio, which separates [[underdamped|0JJ6GDDE7Z]], critically damped and overdamped behaviour.
Damping governs how fast vibrations die out and how sharp a resonance is, as measured by the [[Q factor|NSJ6GVVP16]].
See the [[definition|99PP8V3619]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A swing that is pushed once slows down and stops, because air and the ropes take away its energy.
This is damping.
With light damping, the swing goes back and forth many times with smaller and smaller swings.
With heavy damping, like a door closer, the motion creeps back to rest without overshooting.
Critical damping is the borderline case, which returns to rest fastest without any overshoot.
