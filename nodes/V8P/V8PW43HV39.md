## Proof

We build a basis of $W$ one vector at a time, and bound its length with the [[Steinitz exchange lemma|08ZDRN6H41]].

1. Let $n = \dim V$, the [[dimension|05G2G0P224]] of $V$. Let $c_1, \dots, c_n$ be a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $V$. In particular, $c_1, \dots, c_n$ [[span|0BJKVF90Z6]] $V$.
2. Let $v_1, \dots, v_k \in V$ be [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]]. By the Steinitz exchange lemma with the spanning family $c_1, \dots, c_n$, we get $k \le n$.
3. Let $v_1, \dots, v_k \in W$ be linearly independent, and let $v \in W$ be outside $\operatorname{span}(v_1, \dots, v_k)$, the [[linear span|0HR573CHNM]]. We claim that $v_1, \dots, v_k, v$ are linearly independent.
4. Let $a_1 v_1 + \cdots + a_k v_k + a v = 0$ with all coefficients in $F$. If $a \ne 0$, then $v = -a^{-1}(a_1 v_1 + \cdots + a_k v_k)$ lies in the span, a contradiction.
5. So $a = 0$. Then $a_1 v_1 + \cdots + a_k v_k = 0$, and all $a_i = 0$ by independence. This proves the claim of step 3.
6. Start with the empty family, which is linearly independent. While the current family $v_1, \dots, v_k$ does not span $W$, pick $v \in W$ outside its span and append it.
7. By step 3, each family produced in step 6 is linearly independent in $W$, hence in $V$. By step 2, its length is at most $n$.
8. Each append increases the length by one. So by step 7, the process stops after at most $n$ appends.
9. It stops only when the current family $v_1, \dots, v_k$ spans $W$. This family is linearly independent, so it is a basis of $W$, with $k \le n$.
10. By step 9, $W$ has a finite spanning set, so $W$ is [[finite-dimensional|018W3V3TCT]].
11. By the [[dimension theorem|0WK0Y8RH1M]], all bases of $W$ have $k$ vectors, so $\dim W = k$. By step 9, $\dim W \le n = \dim V$.

## Idea

Inside $V$, an independent family can never have more than $\dim V$ vectors, by the exchange lemma. So if we keep adding vectors of $W$ that lie outside the current span, we must stop. When we stop, we have a basis of $W$ with at most $\dim V$ vectors.
