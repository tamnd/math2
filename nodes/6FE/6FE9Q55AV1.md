## Example

Let $p = (x - 1)^2 = x^2 - 2x + 1$, so $c_0 = 1$ and $c_1 = -2$.
Its companion matrix and the [[Jordan block|0PBFS92AJP]] $J_2(1)$ are
$$C = C(p) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad J_2(1) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
We show that $C = P J_2(1) P^{-1}$ with $P = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. The trace is $\operatorname{tr} C = 2$ and the determinant is $\det C = 0 \cdot 2 - (-1) \cdot 1 = 1$, so $\chi_C = x^2 - 2x + 1 = p$.
2. $C - I = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is not zero, so the minimal polynomial is not $x - 1$, and it is $p$, as the [[theorem on companion matrices|XYR7GPH20K]] says.
3. The kernel of $C - I$ is spanned by $p_1 = (-1, 1)$. So the eigenvalue $1$ has geometric multiplicity $1$, and $C$ is not diagonalizable.
4. Solve $(C - I) p_2 = p_1$. The vector $p_2 = (1, 0)$ works, since $(C - I)(1, 0) = (-1, 1)$.
5. Let $P$ have columns $p_1$ and $p_2$. Then $\det P = 0 - 1 = -1 \ne 0$, so $P$ is invertible.
6. Multiply: $CP = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \cdot (-1) + (-1) \cdot 1 & 0 \cdot 1 + (-1) \cdot 0 \\ 1 \cdot (-1) + 2 \cdot 1 & 1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
7. Multiply the other way: $P J_2(1) = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 + 1 \\ 1 & 1 + 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
8. So $CP = P J_2(1)$, and $C$ is [[similar|027KDW2K72]] to $J_2(1)$.

## Remarks

A companion matrix whose polynomial has a repeated root is a single Jordan block for that root, not a diagonal matrix.
The reason is that a companion matrix is cyclic, and a cyclic matrix has only one Jordan block for each eigenvalue.

Change the polynomial to $p = (x - 1)(x - 2) = x^2 - 3x + 2$, and the picture changes.
Then $C(p) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ has the two distinct eigenvalues $1$ and $2$, and it is similar to $\operatorname{diag}(1, 2)$.
For $p = (x - \lambda)^n$ the same method gives $C(p)$ similar to $J_n(\lambda)$ for every $n$, because both are cyclic with characteristic polynomial $(x - \lambda)^n$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The polynomial $(x - 1)^2$ has the root $1$ twice.
Its companion matrix has only one line of eigenvectors, not two.
So it cannot be made diagonal, and the best you can do is a Jordan block, with a single $1$ above the diagonal.

### L2 human

Here is the meaning of the columns of $P$.
The first column is an eigenvector: $C(-1, 1) = (0 - 1, -1 + 2) = (-1, 1) = p_1$.
The second column is the next link of a chain: $C(1, 0) = (0, 1) = (-1, 1) + (1, 0) = p_1 + p_2$.
These two equations are exactly the two columns of $CP = P J_2(1)$.
So similarity to a Jordan block comes down to finding a chain $p_2 \mapsto p_1 \mapsto 0$ under $C - I$.
Watch out for the order: if $p_2$ comes first in $P$, the $1$ appears below the diagonal instead of above it.
