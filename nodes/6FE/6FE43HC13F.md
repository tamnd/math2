## Proof

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be the scalar field, $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$.
Write $\bar a$ for the conjugate of $a \in \mathbb{K}$, so $\bar a = a$ over $\mathbb{R}$.

1. By axioms 1 and 2 of the [[inner product|0VGSYBWHV2]], for $u, v, w \in V$ we get $\ip{w}{u + v} = \overline{\ip{u}{w} + \ip{v}{w}} = \ip{w}{u} + \ip{w}{v}$.
2. In the same way, $\ip{w}{-v} = \overline{-\ip{v}{w}} = -\ip{w}{v}$.
3. By the definition of the [[induced norm|0KG4D8AP6G]], axiom 1 and step 1, $\norm{x + y}^2 = \ip{x + y}{x + y} = \ip{x}{x} + \ip{x}{y} + \ip{y}{x} + \ip{y}{y}$.
4. By axiom 1 and steps 1 and 2, $\norm{x - y}^2 = \ip{x}{x} - \ip{x}{y} - \ip{y}{x} + \ip{y}{y}$.
5. We add steps 3 and 4. The mixed terms cancel, so $\norm{x + y}^2 + \norm{x - y}^2 = 2\ip{x}{x} + 2\ip{y}{y} = 2\norm{x}^2 + 2\norm{y}^2$.

## Idea

Expand both squared norms as inner products.
The mixed terms $\ip{x}{y} + \ip{y}{x}$ appear with a plus sign in one expansion and with a minus sign in the other.
So they cancel in the sum.

## Variants

- With the formula $\norm{x \pm y}^2 = \norm{x}^2 \pm 2\operatorname{Re}\ip{x}{y} + \norm{y}^2$ of the [[induced norm|0MFJJV97RJ]], the proof is one line.
- In $\mathbb{R}^2$ the law follows from the law of cosines applied to the two triangles cut off by each diagonal. The two angles at adjacent vertices have opposite cosines.
