## Proof

We expand $\norm{x + y}^2$ and $\norm{x - y}^2$ as inner products and add the two expansions.
Let $\mathbb{K}$ be the scalar field, $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, and write $\bar a$ for the conjugate of $a \in \mathbb{K}$, so that $\bar a = a$ over $\mathbb{R}$.
The [[inner product|0VGSYBWHV2]] is linear in the first slot and conjugate symmetric.

1. The second slot is additive and changes sign with its argument.
   For $u, v, w \in V$, conjugate symmetry and linearity in the first slot give
$$\ip{w}{u + v} = \overline{\ip{u}{w} + \ip{v}{w}} = \ip{w}{u} + \ip{w}{v}, \qquad \ip{w}{-v} = \overline{-\ip{v}{w}} = -\ip{w}{v}.$$
2. By the definition of the [[induced norm|0KG4D8AP6G]], linearity in the first slot and step 1,
$$\norm{x + y}^2 = \ip{x + y}{x + y} = \ip{x}{x} + \ip{x}{y} + \ip{y}{x} + \ip{y}{y}.$$
3. In the same way, with the signs from step 1,
$$\norm{x - y}^2 = \ip{x}{x} - \ip{x}{y} - \ip{y}{x} + \ip{y}{y}.$$
4. Add steps 2 and 3.
   The mixed terms cancel, so
$$\norm{x + y}^2 + \norm{x - y}^2 = 2\ip{x}{x} + 2\ip{y}{y} = 2\norm{x}^2 + 2\norm{y}^2.$$

## Idea

We expand both squared norms the way we expand $(a + b)^2$ and $(a - b)^2$ for numbers.
The mixed terms $\ip{x}{y} + \ip{y}{x}$ come with a plus sign in one expansion and a minus sign in the other, so they cancel in the sum.
Nothing else is left but twice the squared sides.
The proof never needs the mixed terms to be real, so the complex case costs nothing extra.

## Variants

With the formula $\norm{x \pm y}^2 = \norm{x}^2 \pm 2\operatorname{Re}\ip{x}{y} + \norm{y}^2$ for the [[induced norm|0MFJJV97RJ]], the proof is one line.
In $\mathbb{R}^2$ there is a proof by the law of cosines.
Apply it to the two triangles that the diagonals cut off at two adjacent corners.
The angles of the parallelogram at those corners add up to a straight angle, so their cosines are opposite, and the two cosine terms cancel in the sum.
