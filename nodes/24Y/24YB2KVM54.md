## Definition

Let $y = \sin(\text{latitude}) \in [-1, 1]$ and let $T(y, t)$ be the annual mean surface temperature at $y$.
A *Budyko–Sellers model* is a one-dimensional [[energy balance model|M182YC20JJ]]
$$C \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = Q s(y) \big( 1 - \alpha(T) \big) - (A + B T) + \mathcal{T}[T],$$
with the following data.

1. $Q > 0$ is the global mean incoming solar flux, $s(y) \ge 0$ its distribution in latitude with mean $1$, and $A$, $B > 0$ and $C > 0$ are constants.
2. The albedo depends on temperature through an ice threshold $T_c$: $\alpha(T) = \alpha_i$ for $T < T_c$ and $\alpha(T) = \alpha_w$ for $T > T_c$, with $\alpha_i > \alpha_w$. Smooth ramps are also used.
3. $\mathcal{T}[T]$ is the meridional heat transport: either Budyko's relaxation $\mathcal{T}[T] = \gamma (\bar T - T)$, with $\gamma > 0$ and $\bar T$ the global mean of $T$, or the diffusion $\mathcal{T}[T] = \partial_y \big( D (1 - y^2) \partial_y T \big)$, with $D > 0$.

The *ice line* is a latitude $y_s$ where $T(y_s) = T_c$.
The *ice–albedo feedback* is the dependence of $\alpha$ on $T$ in clause 2.
A decrease of $T$ can only increase the albedo, and so decrease the absorbed flux.

## Remarks

Example: take a symmetric equilibrium of Budyko's model with a single ice line $y_s \in (0, 1)$.
The equation is linear in $T$ on each side of $y_s$.
Integrating it over $[0, 1]$ gives $A + B \bar T = Q \int_0^1 s(y) (1 - \alpha) \, dy$, with $\alpha = \alpha_w$ for $y < y_s$ and $\alpha = \alpha_i$ for $y > y_s$.
The condition $T(y_s) = T_c$ then gives an explicit relation between $Q$ and $y_s$, whose graph shows the [[equilibria|0N8NGKDZDZ]] for each $Q$.

Non-example: with constant albedo $\alpha_i = \alpha_w$, clause 2 has no feedback; the model is then linear and has exactly one equilibrium for each $Q$.

Budyko's model uses the linear fit $A + BT$ to observed outgoing radiation and the relaxation transport; Sellers' model uses a diffusive transport with more detailed physics.
