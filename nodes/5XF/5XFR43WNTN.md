## Summary

The free-fall time is the time a body of gas needs to collapse under its own gravity when nothing resists the collapse.
For a uniform sphere starting at rest it depends only on the initial density, not on the size of the sphere.
It is the basic time scale of gravitational collapse in star formation and the dynamical time scale of stars and galaxies.
Comparing other time scales with it, such as the sound crossing time or the cooling time, shows which process controls a cloud.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a ball of dust floating at rest in space, with no pressure and no rotation.
Gravity pulls every grain toward the centre, and all of them arrive at the centre at the same moment.
That moment comes sooner for a denser ball, and the size of the ball does not matter.
For a cloud with the mean density of the Sun the free-fall time is about half an hour; for a typical molecular cloud it is about a million years.
