## Statement

Let $K$ be a commutative ring, let $n \ge 3$, and let $M$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $K$.
Let $M^{i}_{j}$ be the matrix that results when row $i$ and column $j$ of $M$ are deleted.
Let $M^{1,n}_{1,n}$ be the $(n-2) \times (n-2)$ matrix that results when rows $1, n$ and columns $1, n$ are deleted.
Then
$$\det M \cdot \det M^{1,n}_{1,n} = \det M^{1}_{1} \det M^{n}_{n} - \det M^{1}_{n} \det M^{n}_{1},$$
where $\det$ is the [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]], and the four determinants on the right are [[minors|0SD528P0EK]] of order $n - 1$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The ring must be commutative, as in every use of the determinant.
The size must be at least $3$, so that the interior minor $M^{1,n}_{1,n}$ exists.
For $n = 2$, with the convention that the empty determinant is $1$, the identity reads $\det M = m_{22} m_{11} - m_{21} m_{12}$.

### Consequences

- The right side is the $2 \times 2$ determinant of the four corner minors $\begin{pmatrix} \det M^1_1 & \det M^1_n \\ \det M^n_1 & \det M^n_n \end{pmatrix}$.
- When the interior minor is a unit, $\det M = (\det M^1_1 \det M^n_n - \det M^1_n \det M^n_1) / \det M^{1,n}_{1,n}$.
- This is the step of [[Dodgson condensation|RQ44FKS5QM]].

### Converse

The identity holds for all square matrices of size at least $3$, so a converse in the usual sense does not apply.
It does not determine $\det M$ when the interior minor is $0$: then both sides are $0$ for every value of $\det M$.

### Generalizations

The identity is the case $k = n - 2$ of [[Sylvester's determinant identity|7SSKBQYFW1]], after the interior rows and columns are moved to the front.
The [[proof|DBJZFHD9PR]] multiplies $M$ by a matrix built from two columns of the adjugate.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a square matrix, its four corner minors of size $n - 1$, and its central minor of size $n - 2$.
The determinant times the central minor equals the $2 \times 2$ determinant of the corner minors.

### L2 human

Take $M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. The corner minors are $\det M^1_1 = -3$, $\det M^4_4 = -7$, $\det M^1_4 = 1$ and $\det M^4_1 = 7$.
2. The right side is $(-3)(-7) - 1 \cdot 7 = 14$.
3. The interior minor is $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = -1$.
4. So the identity predicts $\det M = 14 / (-1) = -14$.
5. Check by the [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]] along row 1, with the minors $-3, 5, 7, 1$ of row 1.
6. It gives $1 \cdot (-3) - 2 \cdot 5 + 0 \cdot 7 - 1 \cdot 1 = -14$, which agrees.
The typical use is Dodgson condensation, which applies the identity to connected submatrices.
A trap: the minor $M^1_n$ deletes row $1$ and column $n$, so it is not the same as $M^n_1$.

### L3 human

The identity is a quadratic relation among minors.
Read as a recurrence, it is the octahedron recurrence, which links condensation with alternating sign matrices in combinatorics.
