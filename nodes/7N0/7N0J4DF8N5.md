## Summary

Creation and annihilation operators act on the [[Fock space|1TJ8TNH331]] of a system of [[identical particles|Y64G7W4BEC]].
A creation operator $a^*(f)$ adds one particle in the one-particle state $f$, and the annihilation operator $a(f)$, its adjoint, removes one.
For bosons they satisfy canonical commutation relations, and for fermions the [[canonical anticommutation relations|6TW5A09KCB]].
Every operator of a many-particle system can be written with them, which is the language of [[second quantization|SZWQ1A69V6]] and of quantum field theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a list of boxes, one for each possible state of a single particle, and count how many particles sit in each box.
A creation operator puts one more particle in a chosen box, and an annihilation operator takes one out.
For a single box of bosons, with basis states $e_0, e_1, e_2, \dots$ counting the particles, the rules are $a^* e_n = \sqrt{n+1}\, e_{n+1}$ and $a\, e_n = \sqrt{n}\, e_{n-1}$.
Then $a^* a\, e_n = n\, e_n$, so $a^* a$ counts the particles.
The same operators raise and lower the energy levels of the quantum harmonic oscillator, where they are called ladder operators.
