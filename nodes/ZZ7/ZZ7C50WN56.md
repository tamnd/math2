## Summary

Branch and bound is a general method for finding an exact optimum of a discrete or nonconvex [[optimization problem|NS0Q42Y3FX]].
It splits the feasible set into smaller pieces (branching).
For each piece it computes a bound on the best value the piece can contain (bounding).
It discards pieces whose bound shows they cannot beat the best solution found so far (pruning).
For [[integer programs|9GX3WH5EQF]] the bounds come from the [[linear programming relaxation|PYMHHM5Z6F]], and one branches on a variable with a fractional value.
This form goes back to Ailsa Land and Alison Doig in 1960.
In the worst case the search tree is exponential, but good bounds often keep it small.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Searching every possible answer of a large discrete problem is impossible.
Branch and bound divides the answers into groups, and for each group quickly estimates the best it could possibly offer.
If that estimate is already worse than an answer in hand, the whole group is thrown away unseen.
Otherwise the group is split further, and the search goes on.
In the end the answer in hand is guaranteed to be the best.
