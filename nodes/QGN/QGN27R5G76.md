## Definition

Let $G$ be the [[transfer function|86JKV3TRFD]] of a single-input single-output linear time-invariant system, a real [[rational function|0XDK9CGYKJ]] of $s$ that is not identically zero.
Write $G = n/d$ with real polynomials $n$ and $d$ that have no common root.
The *poles* of the system are the complex roots of $d$, and its *(finite) zeros* are the complex roots of $n$.
The multiplicity of a pole or zero is its multiplicity as a root of $d$ or $n$.

## Remarks

These are the [[zeros and poles|1TE3QF8EYC]] of $G$ in the sense of complex analysis, restricted to $\C$.
If $(A, B, C, D)$ is a minimal [[realization|WDVJXWV3RF]] of $G$, the poles are exactly the [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]] of $A$.
For a non-minimal realization, the eigenvalues of $A$ include the poles but may contain more.

Example: $G(s) = (s - 1)/(s^2 + 3s + 2)$ has $n(s) = s - 1$ and $d(s) = (s + 1)(s + 2)$ with no common root, so its poles are $-1, -2$ and its zero is $1$.

Non-example: for $G(s) = (s + 1)/\big((s + 1)(s + 2)\big)$, the point $-1$ is neither a pole nor a zero, because the fraction is not in lowest terms; after cancelling, $G(s) = 1/(s + 2)$.
The realization $A = \operatorname{diag}(-1, -2)$, $B = (0, 1)^T$, $C = (1, 1)$ of this $G$ has the eigenvalue $-1$, which is not a pole of $G$.

For a multivariable system with a $p \times m$ rational matrix $G$, poles and transmission zeros are defined through the Smith–McMillan form of $G$.
For a minimal realization, the transmission zeros are the points where the Rosenbrock matrix $\begin{pmatrix} sI - A & -B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}$ drops below its normal rank.
