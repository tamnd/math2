## Summary

The Palatini variation is a way to derive the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] from the [[Einstein–Hilbert action|JPE3A1SN7V]].
In it, the metric and the [[affine connection|JX65KSVHSJ]] are treated as independent fields.
Varying the connection forces it to be the [[Levi-Civita connection|CAZYGE1T4E]] of the metric, and varying the metric then gives the Einstein equations.
It shows that the link between metric and connection need not be assumed: it comes out of the action principle.
The method is a starting point for first-order formulations of gravity and for some modified theories.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In general relativity, the metric measures lengths and times, and the connection tells how to carry vectors from point to point.
Usually the connection is computed from the metric before anything else.
In the Palatini approach, both are first treated as unrelated unknowns.
The principle of least action is then applied to each of them separately.
The first equation says that the connection must be the one built from the metric, so nothing new appears.
The second equation is the usual Einstein equation, so the two approaches agree for ordinary general relativity.
