## Summary

Denavit–Hartenberg parameters are four numbers per joint that describe how consecutive links of a serial robot arm are placed relative to each other.
They are a joint angle $\theta$, an offset $d$, a link length $a$ and a twist angle $\alpha$.
They are read off from the common normal of two consecutive joint axes.
With them, each joint contributes one standard $4 \times 4$ matrix, and [[forward kinematics|1DQ5AE8D8E]] becomes a product of such matrices.
The convention was introduced by Jacques Denavit and Richard Hartenberg and is a standard in robotics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A robot arm has a chain of joint axes, each a line in space.
Two consecutive lines are related by how far apart they are and how much they are tilted against each other.
Two more numbers say where and at what angle the next link sits along the first line.
Recording these four numbers for every joint describes the whole arm.
For a flat arm with two hinges whose axes are parallel, the twist and offset are zero and the link length is the distance between the hinges.
