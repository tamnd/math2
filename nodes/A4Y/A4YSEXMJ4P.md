## Summary

Postglacial rebound is the slow rise of land that was pressed down by an ice sheet, after the ice has melted.
The mantle under the load flows back as it returns to [[isostatic|7CJTGB6M0V]] equilibrium, over thousands of years.
Regions such as Scandinavia and Hudson Bay are still rising today, about ten thousand years after the last ice age ended.
The rate of rebound is one of the main ways to measure the [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] of the mantle.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Press your thumb into a memory-foam mattress and let go: the dent fills in slowly.
A thick ice sheet does the same to the Earth, pushing the crust down by hundreds of metres.
When the ice melts, the land slowly springs back, because the hot rock below has to flow back underneath it.
Old beaches in northern Canada and Sweden now sit high above the sea, lifted by this rebound.
