## Summary

An evolutionarily stable strategy is a strategy with the following property.
Once a whole population adopts it, a small group of mutants playing a different strategy cannot invade.
John Maynard Smith and George Price introduced it in 1973 to study animal conflicts.
Every evolutionarily stable strategy gives a symmetric [[Nash equilibrium|745WYP9YJ0]], but not conversely.
It is the basic equilibrium notion of evolutionary game theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a large population of animals that meet in random pairs and fight over food.
Each animal follows an inherited strategy, and strategies that earn more leave more offspring.
A strategy is evolutionarily stable if a population using it cannot be taken over by a few newcomers with another strategy.
Either the newcomers do worse against the residents, or they do equally well against the residents but worse against each other.
In the Hawk–Dove game, when the cost of a fight exceeds the prize, a mix of aggressive and peaceful behaviour is evolutionarily stable.
