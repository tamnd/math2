## Example

Let $n \geq 1$ be an integer.
The cyclic group of order $n$ is a [[cyclic group|0KVGH0J5B3]] with exactly $n$ elements.
By [[the classification of cyclic groups|0736AXH2ME]], it is unique up to [[isomorphism|08MG7H8JW5]], and we write it $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ or $C_n$.
Three standard models are the following.

1. The [[integers modulo $n$|03VFZ2WETT]] under addition: $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} = \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$, with $j + k$ reduced modulo $n$. The residue $1$ generates it, because $k = 1 + 1 + \cdots + 1$ with $k$ terms.
2. The [[group of $n$th roots of unity|0WX11EA3F3]]: $\mu_n = \{\zeta^k : 0 \leq k \leq n-1\}$ in $\mathbb{C}^\times$, where $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/n}$. The number $\zeta$ generates it.
3. The rotations of a [[regular polygon|01KJJ9A400]] with $n$ vertices about its center: $R_n = \{r_0, r_1, \ldots, r_{n-1}\}$, where $r_k$ is the counterclockwise [[rotation|0HFREYRSZP]] through the angle $2\pi k/n$. The operation is composition, and $r_1$ generates it.

The three models are isomorphic through explicit maps.

1. Define $f \colon \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to \mu_n$ by $f(k) = \zeta^k$. The map is well defined, because $\zeta^n = e^{2\pi i} = 1$, so $\zeta^k$ depends only on $k$ modulo $n$.
2. The map $f$ is a homomorphism: $f(j + k) = \zeta^{j+k} = \zeta^j \zeta^k$ by the laws of exponents.
3. The map $f$ is a bijection: for $0 < k < n$ the angle $2\pi k/n$ lies strictly between $0$ and $2\pi$, so $\zeta^k \neq 1$. Thus $\zeta$ has order $n$, and $\zeta^0, \ldots, \zeta^{n-1}$ are distinct.
4. Place the polygon in $\mathbb{C}$ with vertices $\zeta^0, \zeta^1, \ldots, \zeta^{n-1}$. Multiplication by $\zeta^k$ rotates $\mathbb{C}$ through $2\pi k/n$ about $0$, so it is $r_k$. This gives the isomorphism $\mu_n \to R_n$, $\zeta^k \mapsto r_k$.
5. The composite $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to R_n$ is $k \mapsto r_k$. In particular, $r_j r_k = r_{j+k}$ with $j + k$ taken modulo $n$.

## Remarks

1. The [[order|0WE2NHZJ7B]] of $k \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is $n/\gcd(k, n)$, by [[the order of a power|0VVPQNVM8S]]. For $n = 6$, the orders of $0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ are $1, 6, 3, 2, 3, 6$.
2. The generators are the residues $k$ with $\gcd(k, n) = 1$, so there are $\varphi(n)$ of them. The proof is on the page of the [[cyclic group|0KVGH0J5B3]].
3. For each divisor $d$ of $n$, there is exactly one subgroup of order $d$, namely $\langle n/d \rangle$, by [[the fundamental theorem of cyclic groups|0423TXR9AN]]. It contains [[exactly $\varphi(d)$ elements of order $d$|0XCYGCCX8V]].
4. If $n = p$ is prime, each element other than $0$ generates the group.
5. For $n = 1$ the group is trivial, and for $n = 2$ it is [[isomorphic to $\{1, -1\}$|7FCN2KNWC0]].
6. In the [[dihedral group|0JZ27DMJ5D]] of symmetries of the regular $n$-gon, with $n \geq 3$, the rotations $R_n$ form a cyclic subgroup of [[index|0DTXBQPA2S]] $2$.
7. The group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is [[cyclic and abelian|FB79N7AHZA]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The cyclic group of order $n$ is clock arithmetic with $n$ hours.
The same group appears in other places.
The $n$ turns of a regular polygon that bring it back onto itself form this group.
The $n$ complex numbers whose $n$th power is $1$ also form this group.
In each case, one basic step repeated $n$ times brings us back to the start.

### L2 human

We write out the isomorphism $f \colon \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to \mu_4$, with $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/4} = i$.

1. The values are $f(0) = 1$, $f(1) = i$, $f(2) = i^2 = -1$ and $f(3) = i^3 = -i$.
2. Check one sum: $3 + 3 = 6 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, and $f(3) f(3) = (-i)^2 = -1 = f(2)$.
3. In the square model, $r_1$ is the quarter turn, and $r_3 r_3 = r_6 = r_2$ is the half turn.

The typical use: compute in the model that is easiest, then move the result to the other models through the isomorphisms.
A trap: the isomorphism depends on the choice of generators.
The map $k \mapsto \zeta^{-k}$ is also an isomorphism $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to \mu_n$, and for $n \geq 3$ it differs from $f$.

### L3 human

The isomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to \mu_n$ are the maps $k \mapsto \omega^k$ with $\omega$ a primitive $n$th root of unity, so there are $\varphi(n)$ of them.
The homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ are the $n$ characters $k \mapsto \zeta^{jk}$, and the [[discrete Fourier transform|TST0GH66A5]] expands functions on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ in these characters.
The group $C_n$ appears as a Galois group: the Galois group of the field extension $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}/\mathbb{F}_q$ is cyclic of order $n$, generated by the [[Frobenius map|0T3WVTD675]] $x \mapsto x^q$.
In terms of generators and relations, $C_n = \langle r \mid r^n = 1 \rangle$.
