## Statement

Let $G$ be a [[locally compact abelian group|ZR09S9R3AW]] with a [[Haar measure|VY0HXD3GC4]], and let $\widehat{G}$ be its [[Pontryagin dual|FMW57WNPDB]].
Let $f \in L^1(G)$, and let $\widehat{f}$ be its [[Fourier transform|VHN5VW5Q92]].
Then $\widehat{f}$ is continuous on $\widehat{G}$ and vanishes at infinity.
That is, for every $\varepsilon > 0$ the set $\set{\chi \in \widehat{G} : \abs{\widehat{f}(\chi)} \ge \varepsilon}$ is compact.

## Remarks

Proof idea: by the [[description of the Gelfand spectrum of L1(G)|29PHKDB4PT]], $\widehat{f}$ is the Gelfand transform of $f$ in the commutative Banach algebra [[L1(G)|6GD25S53ND]].
Gelfand transforms are continuous and vanish at infinity on the character space, which is $\widehat{G}$.
A direct proof approximates $f$ in $L^1$ by functions whose transforms are known to vanish at infinity, and uses $\abs{\widehat{f}(\chi) - \widehat{g}(\chi)} \le \norm{f - g}_1$.

Example: for $G = \R$ this is the classical [[Riemann–Lebesgue lemma|WVKDC011KP]].
For $G = \mathbb{T}$ it says that the Fourier coefficients of an integrable function on the circle tend to $0$.
For $G = \Z$ the dual $\mathbb{T}$ is compact, and the statement only says that $\widehat{f}$ is continuous.

The hypothesis $f \in L^1(G)$ cannot be replaced by a finite measure.
The Dirac measure at $0$ has Fourier–Stieltjes transform equal to $1$, which does not vanish at infinity on a non-compact dual.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The transform of an integrable function on a group is a continuous function on the dual group that becomes small far out.
Far out in the dual means waves that oscillate fast on the group, and integrating against them causes cancellation.

### L2 human

The lemma says that the Fourier transform maps $L^1(G)$ into $C_0(\widehat{G})$, the continuous functions vanishing at infinity.
This map is injective, but it is not onto when $\widehat{G}$ is infinite.
Together with the bound $\norm{\widehat{f}}_\infty \le \norm{f}_1$, it makes the Fourier transform a norm-decreasing algebra homomorphism $L^1(G) \to C_0(\widehat{G})$.
