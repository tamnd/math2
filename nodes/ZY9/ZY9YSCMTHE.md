## Summary

This area studies large random objects in space, such as random media, random geometry, random matrices and random combinatorial structures, with their phase transitions, scaling limits and universality.
In Bernoulli bond percolation on a lattice, each edge is open with probability p independently of the other edges, and the main question is whether an infinite connected cluster of open edges exists.
Many of these models come from statistical physics, and their study joins probability with combinatorics, complex analysis and mathematical physics.
Broadbent and Hammersley introduced percolation in 1957, and Wigner introduced random matrices in the 1950s as models for the energy levels of heavy nuclei.
This topic collects the random structures and probabilistic models pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Extremal and probabilistic combinatorics, stochastic processes, and thermodynamics and statistical mechanics have their own topics.
