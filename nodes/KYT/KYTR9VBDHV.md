## Summary

The normalizer of a [[Lie subalgebra|WKSSTQ69AK]] $\mathfrak k$ of a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] $\mathfrak g$ is the set of elements $x$ with $[x, \mathfrak k] \subseteq \mathfrak k$.
It is the largest subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ that contains $\mathfrak k$ as an [[ideal|JWMTXQ8A3A]].
It is the Lie algebra analogue of the [[normaliser|0YGN3VAA0D]] of a subgroup.
Cartan subalgebras are defined as nilpotent subalgebras that equal their own normalizer.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A subalgebra is usually not an ideal of the whole algebra.
The normalizer collects the elements that, under the bracket, still send the subalgebra into itself.
It is the largest region in which the subalgebra behaves like an ideal.
For example, in $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ the line spanned by $h = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1)$ is its own normalizer.
