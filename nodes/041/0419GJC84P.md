## Definition

A nilpotent matrix is a square matrix with a power equal to zero.
Let $R$ be a ring and let $N$ be an $n \times n$ [[square matrix|0CP7YY6NAG]] over $R$.
We call $N$ *nilpotent* when $N^k = 0$ for some integer $k \ge 1$, where $N^k$ is the product of $k$ factors $N$ under [[matrix multiplication|076JGP6R3T]].
The least such $k$ is the *index* of $N$.

## Notation

The index is also called the index of nilpotency, the degree or the order.
In Mathlib, `IsNilpotent N` says that some power of `N` is `0`.

## Remarks

The matrix must be square, because only then is $N^2 = N N$ defined, and so are all higher powers.
The condition asks for one power that vanishes, and then all later powers vanish too, since $N^m = N^{m-k} N^k = 0$ for $m \ge k$.
So the index is the point from which all powers are zero.
The zero matrix is nilpotent of index $1$, and every other nilpotent matrix has index at least $2$.

For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is nilpotent of index $2$, because its square is the zero matrix.
In contrast, $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is not nilpotent, although its diagonal is zero: its square is $I$, so its powers alternate between itself and $I$.

Over a field, an $n \times n$ matrix is nilpotent if and only if its characteristic polynomial is $x^n$, and then the index is at most $n$.
The concept page on [[nilpotent matrices|07BSPZPSMF]] gives the examples and the rules.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A nilpotent matrix becomes the zero matrix after we multiply it by itself enough times.
The index counts how many factors we need.
For the matrix with a single $1$ in the top right corner of a $2 \times 2$ table, two factors are enough.

### L2 human

To test a small matrix, compute its powers until one is zero or until the power $N^n$, where $n$ is the size.
Over a field, if $N^n \ne 0$ then no later power is zero either, so $N$ is not nilpotent.
For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, so this matrix is nilpotent of index $2$.
