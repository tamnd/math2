## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring and let $N$ be an $n \times n$ [[square matrix|0CP7YY6NAG]] over $R$.
The matrix $N$ is *nilpotent* when $N^k = 0$ for some integer $k \ge 1$, where the powers are [[matrix products|076JGP6R3T]].
The least such $k$ is the *index* of $N$.

## Notation

- In Mathlib, `IsNilpotent N` means that some power of `N` is `0`.

## Remarks

- The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is nilpotent of index $2$.
- Each [[strictly upper triangular matrix|0MKABWDRNC]] is nilpotent.
- Over a field, an $n \times n$ matrix $N$ is nilpotent if and only if its characteristic polynomial is $x^n$; then $N^n = 0$.
- The only eigenvalue of a nilpotent matrix over a field is $0$, so its trace and determinant are $0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A nilpotent matrix becomes the zero matrix after we multiply it by itself enough times.
The index counts how many factors we need.

### L2 human

If $N^k = 0$, then $N^m = 0$ for every $m \ge k$.
So the index is the point after which all powers vanish.
