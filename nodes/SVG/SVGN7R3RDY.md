## Summary

The Arrow–Debreu model is the standard mathematical model of a whole competitive economy, with finitely many goods, consumers and firms.
Consumers own endowments and shares of firms, firms choose production plans to maximize profit, and prices make all markets clear.
Kenneth Arrow and Gérard Debreu proved in 1954 that under convexity and continuity assumptions it has a [[competitive equilibrium|YTBPB52K27]].
Goods can be indexed by date and state of the world, so the model also covers time and uncertainty.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The model is a precise picture of a market economy.
People start with some goods and some ownership of companies; companies turn inputs into outputs; everyone faces the same prices.
Each person buys the best bundle they can afford with the value of their goods plus their share of company profits.
An equilibrium is a list of prices at which all these plans fit together exactly.
For instance, "an umbrella delivered tomorrow if it rains" counts as a separate good with its own price.
