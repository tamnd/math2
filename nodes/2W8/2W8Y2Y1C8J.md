## Summary

Coalescent theory studies the genealogy of a sample of genes by following their ancestral lines backward in time until they merge in common ancestors.
It replaces the forward simulation of a whole population by a random tree on the sample only.
Its central model is [[Kingman's coalescent|P2H42E2V2N]], the limit of the genealogies of many neutral population models.
It is the main tool for the statistical analysis of DNA sequence variation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a few people today and ask for their parents, grandparents, and so on.
Going back, two lines sooner or later meet in a shared ancestor; this merging is a coalescence.
In the end all lines meet in one most recent common ancestor of the sample.
The pattern of mergings is a family tree of the sample, and mutations that fell on its branches explain the genetic differences we see today.
For example, two genes in a population of $N$ haploid individuals that reproduce as in the Wright–Fisher model share a parent in the previous generation with probability $1/N$.
