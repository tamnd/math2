## Summary

In general relativity the energy of the gravitational field has no local density that is independent of coordinates.
So the mass of a system cannot be found by integrating a density.
Instead, mass is defined for isolated systems by the behaviour of the metric far away.
The main definitions are the [[ADM energy|XQNV97Z79Q]] at spatial infinity, the [[Bondi mass|XYFKKJM9FD]] at null infinity and the [[Komar mass|7V112F6KVB]] for stationary spacetimes.
For stationary spacetimes they all agree.
The [[positive mass theorem|PYRPXHNQTB]] shows that the ADM and Bondi masses are non-negative under physical conditions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In Newton's theory, the mass of a star is the sum of the masses of its parts.
In general relativity the gravitational field itself carries energy, and this energy cannot be located at points.
So one measures the mass from far away, by how strongly the system pulls on distant objects.
For the Schwarzschild metric, all the usual definitions give the same number, the parameter $M$ in the metric.
