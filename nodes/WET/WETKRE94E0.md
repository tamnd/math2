## Summary

The effective temperature of a star or planet is the temperature of a [[black body|A2RMYX8A65]] that would emit the same total power per unit area.
By the [[Stefan–Boltzmann law|6JQVMS9Y7R]], it is fixed by the luminosity and the radius of the body.
It is one of the two coordinates of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram and a basic boundary condition for models of stellar structure.
A real star is not a black body, so its effective temperature is a convenient summary rather than the temperature of any particular layer.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A hot object glows, and the hotter it is, the more power each square metre gives off.
For a perfect emitter the power per square metre is $\sigma T^4$, where $\sigma$ is the Stefan–Boltzmann constant.
To give a star a single temperature, one measures the power per square metre it actually emits and asks which $T$ gives that value.
The answer for the Sun is about $5772$ kelvin.
