## Summary

Magnetic reconnection is a change in the connectivity of magnetic field lines in a conducting fluid or plasma.
In an ideal conductor field lines are frozen into the fluid by [[Alfvén's theorem|3C3BXAPJQA]], so their topology cannot change.
Reconnection happens in thin regions where resistivity or other non-ideal effects break this rule.
It quickly converts magnetic energy into heat, flows and fast particles.
It drives solar flares, magnetic substorms in the Earth's magnetosphere and sawtooth crashes in tokamaks.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture two bundles of magnetic field lines pointing in opposite directions and pushed against each other.
Where they meet, the lines can break and join up with the lines of the other bundle, forming an X shape.
The newly joined lines are sharply bent and snap away like stretched rubber bands, flinging the gas with them.
This is how a solar flare releases in minutes energy that was stored in twisted magnetic fields for days.
