## Summary

The modes of fracture are the three basic ways in which the faces of a crack move relative to each other.
Mode I is opening, mode II is sliding in the direction perpendicular to the crack front, and mode III is tearing along the crack front.
Every crack-tip displacement field in [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] is a superposition of the three modes, and each mode has its own [[stress intensity factor|8D619PN75J]].
In [[fracture mechanics|SNRXGRZWR5]] most cracks in brittle materials grow so that the loading at the tip is pure mode I.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a sheet of paper with a cut in it.
Pull the two sides of the cut apart: that is mode I.
Slide one side forward and the other back, in the plane of the sheet: that is mode II.
Move one side up and the other down, out of the plane of the sheet, as when tearing paper: that is mode III.
