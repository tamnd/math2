## Summary

A mathematical program with equilibrium constraints (MPEC) is an optimization problem with an equilibrium constraint.
Some variables must solve a [[variational inequality|6QS7X5GVMY]] or a [[complementarity problem|JMTCEQ2J3Q]] that depends on the other variables.
It models a leader who chooses a design while followers react by reaching an equilibrium.
Examples are Stackelberg games, toll setting and network design.
It is closely related to [[bilevel optimization|3VE9BSJQJA]].
MPECs are hard because their feasible sets are not convex and the usual constraint qualifications fail at every feasible point.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A city sets prices on roads, and drivers then choose routes that form a traffic equilibrium.
The city wants the best prices, knowing how drivers will react.
The drivers' equilibrium is a constraint of the city's problem, and it is written with conditions of the form "a road is used only if its cost is minimal".
Such "either-or" conditions make the feasible set look like a union of pieces, which is why these problems are hard.
