## Statement

Let $G$ be the gravitational constant and $c$ the speed of light.
Consider a static, spherically symmetric spacetime with metric
$$ds^2 = -e^{2\nu(r)} c^2 dt^2 + \Big(1 - \frac{2G m(r)}{r c^2}\Big)^{-1} dr^2 + r^2 \left(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta \, d\varphi^2\right),$$
filled with a static [[perfect fluid|H4FNPG6ZY1]] of mass density $\rho(r)$ (energy density $\rho c^2$) and pressure $p(r)$.
Assume the metric solves the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] with zero cosmological constant, is regular at the centre, and $2Gm(r) < rc^2$.
Then
$$\frac{dm}{dr} = 4\pi r^2 \rho, \qquad m(0) = 0,$$
$$\frac{dp}{dr} = -\frac{G \left(\rho + p/c^2\right)\left(m + 4\pi r^3 p/c^2\right)}{r^2 \left(1 - 2Gm/(rc^2)\right)},$$
$$\frac{d\nu}{dr} = -\frac{1}{\rho c^2 + p} \frac{dp}{dr}.$$
The equation for $dp/dr$ is the *Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation*; with the equation for $m$ and an equation of state it determines the structure of the star.

## Remarks

The $tt$ component of the Einstein equations gives the equation for $m$, the $rr$ component gives $d\nu/dr = G\left(m + 4\pi r^3 p/c^2\right)/\left(r^2 c^2 (1 - 2Gm/(rc^2))\right)$, and the conservation law $\nabla_a T^{ab} = 0$ gives $dp/dr = -(\rho c^2 + p)\, d\nu/dr$.
Combining them gives the equation for $p$.

To build a star one adds an equation of state $p = f(\rho)$, chooses a central density, and integrates outward until $p = 0$ at a radius $R$.
Outside, the solution is matched to the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]] with mass $M = m(R)$.

When $p \ll \rho c^2$ and $Gm \ll rc^2$, the equation reduces to the Newtonian hydrostatic equilibrium $dp/dr = -G m \rho / r^2$.
The three relativistic correction factors all make the pressure gradient steeper, so gravity is effectively stronger than in Newton's theory.
This is one reason why neutron stars have a maximum mass.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A star stays in balance because the pressure of its matter pushes outward exactly as hard as gravity pulls inward.
The Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation is the rule for this balance in general relativity.
It says how fast the pressure must fall as one moves outward from the centre.
In general relativity pressure itself also creates gravity, so very dense stars need even more pressure, and beyond some mass no pressure is enough.

### L2 human

The equation is the starting point for models of neutron stars and white dwarfs in the relativistic regime.
Given an equation of state, it yields the mass–radius relation and the maximum mass of the family of stars.
The constant-density solution is the interior Schwarzschild metric, and the general equation leads to Buchdahl's bound $R \ge \frac{9}{4} \frac{GM}{c^2}$ for stars with non-increasing density.
