## Summary

A market is complete if every contingent claim can be replicated by trading in the available assets.
In a complete market without [[arbitrage|Q626SC54P7]], every derivative has a unique price, the cost of its replicating strategy.
By the second [[fundamental theorem of asset pricing|TYVNND6JN0]], an arbitrage-free market is complete exactly when its [[risk-neutral measure|858BHBY2BD]] is unique.
The binomial model and the Black–Scholes model are complete; models with more sources of risk than traded assets are not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A contingent claim is a contract whose payoff depends on what happens in the market, like an option.
The market is complete if you can build any such payoff yourself by buying and selling the traded assets over time.
Then the claim must cost exactly what it costs to build it.
With one stock that goes either up or down, two assets are enough to build any payoff; with three possible outcomes, they are not.
