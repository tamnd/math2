## Summary

Hořava–Lifshitz gravity is a theory of gravity proposed by Petr Hořava in 2009.
It gives up full Lorentz invariance at very short distances and treats time and space differently, with an anisotropic scaling of time against space.
The extra spatial derivatives in its action improve its short-distance behaviour, so that it is power-counting renormalizable in $3+1$ dimensions.
At long distances it is meant to approach [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]], and it is one of the [[alternatives to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In ordinary relativity, space and time are mixed by changes of observer, so they must be treated alike.
Hořava–Lifshitz gravity keeps a preferred way of slicing spacetime into moments of time.
At very short distances, time scales like the cube of length rather than like length.
This is similar to some materials in condensed matter physics, studied by Lifshitz, where the energy of a wave grows like a higher power of its wave number.
The hope is that this change tames the infinities that appear when gravity is quantized.
