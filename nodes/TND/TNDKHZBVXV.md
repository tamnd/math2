## Definition

Let a [[hyperelastic|ZHK2EVRD90]] solid be held in a static, possibly finite, deformation, called the initial or pre-stressed state, with initial stress $\sigma^0$.
Superpose on it a small time-dependent displacement and linearize the equations of motion about the initial state.
The result is a linear [[elastodynamic|T1PYEBKBAJ]] system whose effective coefficients depend on $\sigma^0$.
The *acoustoelastic effect* is the dependence of the speeds of plane waves of this incremental system on the initial stress $\sigma^0$.

## Remarks

For small initial strain the change of each wave speed is, to first order, a linear function of the initial stress.
Its coefficients involve the second-order (Lamé) constants and the third-order elastic constants of the material, for example the Murnaghan constants $l, m, n$ of an isotropic solid.

Example: a steel bar under uniaxial tension carries longitudinal ultrasonic waves along its axis at a speed slightly different from the unstressed value.
For small stress the relative change grows linearly with the stress.

Non-example: in a material described by [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] the wave speeds are fixed by $\lambda$, $\mu$ and $\rho$ alone.
Within that linear model a static load does not change them.
