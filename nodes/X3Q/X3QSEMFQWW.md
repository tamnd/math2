## Summary

Statistical mechanics derives the large-scale behaviour of matter from the laws that govern its many small parts.
It replaces the exact state of all particles, the [[microstate|S1NST0AG71]], by a probability distribution over microstates, a [[statistical ensemble|M0JYTP1C8B]].
Thermodynamic quantities such as energy, pressure and entropy then become averages or functionals of this distribution.
Its mathematics joins probability, measure theory, combinatorics and operator algebras.
It also supplies models, such as the [[Ising model|R0KGM32QN6]], that are studied for their own sake.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A litre of gas holds more molecules than anyone could ever track one by one.
Statistical mechanics gives up on tracking them and asks instead how likely each arrangement is.
Averages over huge numbers of molecules are very stable, so the probabilities give sharp predictions for things like temperature and pressure.
For example, if $N$ coins are tossed, the exact sequence is unpredictable, but the fraction of heads is close to $1/2$ when $N$ is large.
Statistical mechanics turns this kind of reasoning into a theory of heat and matter.
