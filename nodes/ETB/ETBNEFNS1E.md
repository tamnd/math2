## Example

Let $M$ be a finite structure with universe $\set{m_1, \dots, m_k}$, and let $G = \operatorname{Aut}(M)$.
We show that $M$ has the [[small index property|RZAF6NVEXC]].

1. Let $\bar m = (m_1, \dots, m_k)$. The pointwise stabilizer $G_{\bar m}$ is open in the [[topology of pointwise convergence|FVGB8ZP67B]].
2. An [[automorphism|DSQ17TNW1C]] fixing every element is the identity, so $G_{\bar m} = \set{\mathrm{id}}$.
3. So $\set{\mathrm{id}}$ is open, and $G$ is discrete.
4. In a discrete group every subgroup is a union of points, hence [[open|4K9RYSNPG3]].
5. In particular every subgroup of index less than $2^{\aleph_0}$ is open.

## Remarks

The property holds vacuously in a strong form: every subgroup is open, whatever its index.
The same argument works for any countable structure with a finite tuple whose pointwise stabilizer is trivial.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A finite structure has only finitely many symmetries, and fixing all points pins each symmetry down.
So the topology on the symmetry group is discrete, and every subgroup counts as open.

### L2 human

We take $M$ to be the $5$-cycle.

1. $\operatorname{Aut}(M)$ is the dihedral group of order $10$.
2. Fixing all five vertices leaves only the identity.
3. So each of the subgroups of the dihedral group is open, including the subgroup of order $2$ generated by one reflection.

The typical use is as the base case when comparing SIP for different structures.
A trap is to think SIP says anything about finite groups; for them it is automatic.
