## Summary

A photonic crystal is a material whose permittivity varies periodically in space, with a period comparable to the wavelength of light.
Light in it behaves like electrons in a crystal: its allowed frequencies form bands, separated in good cases by photonic band gaps.
In a band gap no light of that frequency can travel through the crystal, in any direction for a complete gap.
Photonic crystals are used to make mirrors, waveguides with sharp bends, low-loss fibers and small optical cavities.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stack thin layers of two kinds of glass, alternating again and again.
Each boundary reflects a little light, and for some colours all these small reflections add up in step, so the stack reflects that colour almost perfectly.
This is how a multilayer mirror works, and it is the simplest photonic crystal.
Butterfly wings and opals get their shimmering colours from natural periodic structures of the same kind.
