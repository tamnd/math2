## Summary

Boundary layer separation is the detachment of a [[boundary layer|YCFPD0GE5G]] from the wall, after which the fluid next to the wall flows backward.
It happens where the outer flow slows down and the pressure rises downstream.
The slow fluid near the wall cannot overcome this adverse pressure gradient.
Separation causes the large wakes behind bluff bodies, much of their drag, and the stall of wings at high angles of attack.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Fluid near a wall has already lost speed to friction.
If the pressure ahead rises, this slow fluid is pushed back, stops, and starts to flow the wrong way.
The main stream then lifts off the surface, leaving a region of swirling, recirculating fluid behind.
You see this behind a car or a ball, and it is why a wing stalls when tilted too far.
In two-dimensional steady flow the point of separation is where the friction at the wall drops to zero.
