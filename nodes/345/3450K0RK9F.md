## Summary

Rational thermodynamics is an axiomatic approach to the thermodynamics of continuous media, developed in the 1960s by Coleman, Noll, Truesdell and others.
It takes temperature and entropy as primitive fields, even far from equilibrium, and does not assume local equilibrium.
The second law enters as the [[Clausius–Duhem inequality|MKXA9E1Z2T]], which every admissible process must satisfy.
This requirement restricts the possible [[constitutive equations|Q4C55V47A8]] of a material, through the [[Coleman–Noll procedure|SSP221A5VG]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Rational thermodynamics treats thermodynamics of materials the way mechanics treats motion: with a few axioms and careful mathematics.
A material is described by laws that give its stress, heat flux, energy and entropy in terms of its deformation and temperature history.
The second law is not a theorem here but a test: a proposed material law is accepted only if it never lets entropy decrease against the rules.
For an elastic solid, this test forces the stress to be the derivative of a free energy.
