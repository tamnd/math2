## Proof

1. Let $n = \dim V$. By [[the dimension of a subspace|0QW084460E]], $W$ is finite-dimensional. By hypothesis, $\dim W = n$.
2. By the definition of [[dimension|05G2G0P224]], $W$ has a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] $w_1, \dots, w_n$ with $n$ vectors.
3. The vectors $w_1, \dots, w_n$ are [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]] in $W$. A linear relation among them in $V$ is the same as one in $W$, so they are linearly independent in $V$.
4. By [[linearly independent set of the right size is a basis|096V7H13H9]], $w_1, \dots, w_n$ form a basis of $V$. In particular, they [[span|0BJKVF90Z6]] $V$.
5. Each $w_i$ lies in $W$, and $W$ is a [[linear subspace|0EPTJA5QV4]]. So every linear combination of $w_1, \dots, w_n$ lies in $W$.
6. By steps 4 and 5, $V \subseteq W$. Since $W \subseteq V$, we get $W = V$.

## Idea

A basis of $W$ is an independent family in $V$ with exactly $\dim V$ vectors. Such a family must already span $V$. Since its span stays inside $W$, the subspace $W$ is all of $V$.
