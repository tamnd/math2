## Proof

We turn both conditions of a basis into statements about the equation $Ax = b$, and then build the inverse column by column.
By [[linear systems as vector equations|E7SAHXQEVC]], for each $x \in F^n$ we have
$$Ax = x_1 v_1 + \dots + x_n v_n.$$
So the columns span $F^n$ exactly when $Ax = b$ has a solution for every $b$, and by the [[column test|76GG9B25A1]] they are independent exactly when $Ax = 0$ only for $x = 0$.

1. Assume that $A$ is [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]], and let $b \in F^n$. Put $x = A^{-1} b$. Then $Ax = A A^{-1} b = b$, so $b$ is a combination of the columns, and the columns span $F^n$.
2. If $Ax = 0$, then $x = A^{-1}(Ax) = A^{-1} 0 = 0$. So the columns are independent.
3. By steps 1 and 2, the columns form a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $F^n$.
4. Conversely, assume that the columns form a basis. For each $j$, the columns span $F^n$, so there is $x_j \in F^n$ with $A x_j = e_j$, the $j$-th standard basis vector.
5. Let $X$ be the $n \times n$ matrix with columns $x_1, \dots, x_n$. Column $j$ of $AX$ is $A x_j = e_j$, so $AX = I$.
6. By associativity, $A(XA - I) = (AX)A - A = A - A = 0$. So every column $y$ of $XA - I$ satisfies $Ay = 0$.
7. The columns of $A$ are independent, so $Ay = 0$ forces $y = 0$. Hence $XA - I = 0$, that is, $XA = I$.
8. By steps 5 and 7, $X$ is a two-sided inverse of $A$, so $A$ is invertible, with $A^{-1} = X$.

## Idea

Spanning lets us solve $Ax = e_j$ for each $j$, and the solutions, side by side, form a right inverse $X$.
Independence then shows that $X$ is also a left inverse: the matrix $XA - I$ is killed by $A$, and only the zero matrix is killed by a matrix with independent columns.
For $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, solving $Ax = e_1$ and $Ax = e_2$ gives $x_1 = (1, -1)$ and $x_2 = (-1, 2)$, which are the columns of $A^{-1}$.

## Variants

A proof by dimension avoids building $X$.
The map $x \mapsto Ax$ is a linear map from $F^n$ to itself; independence of the columns makes it injective, and spanning makes it surjective, so it is a bijective linear map, and its inverse is linear and has a matrix.
A proof by row reduction uses the [[row-reduction criterion|JXTGZPZP47]]: the columns form a basis exactly when the reduced row echelon form of $A$ is $I$, which is also the condition for $A$ to be invertible.
