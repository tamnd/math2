## Summary

The quantum relative entropy $S(\rho \| \sigma)$ is a measure of how distinguishable a quantum state $\rho$ is from a quantum state $\sigma$.
It is the quantum analogue of the [[Kullback–Leibler divergence|PQBW43X5RE]] and reduces to it for commuting states.
It is nonnegative, vanishes only when $\rho = \sigma$, and cannot increase under quantum channels.
Most entropic quantities of quantum information, such as [[von Neumann entropy|H53TSRFWQ4]] and mutual information, are special cases of it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose you must decide whether a source produces the state $\rho$ or the state $\sigma$, using many copies.
The relative entropy is the exponential rate at which your chance of mistaking $\sigma$ for $\rho$ goes to zero as the number of copies grows.
It is not a distance: it is not symmetric, and it can be infinite.
If $\rho = \sigma$, the states cannot be told apart, and the relative entropy is $0$.
