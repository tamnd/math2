## Summary

The thermal diffusivity $\alpha = k / (\rho c_p)$ measures how fast temperature changes spread through a material.
It is the [[thermal conductivity|XBQBT9E7JT]] divided by the heat capacity per unit volume.
It is the coefficient of the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]] $\partial_t T = \alpha \Delta T$, with unit square metres per second.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A material with high conductivity passes heat on quickly, but a material with high heat capacity soaks heat up before it warms.
Diffusivity combines both effects into one number.
It answers the question: how fast does a hot spot spread?
A temperature change travels a distance of about $\sqrt{\alpha t}$ in time $t$.
For steel, $\alpha$ is about $10^{-5}$ square metres per second, so a change spreads about $3$ millimetres in one second.
