## Definition

Let $\varphi \colon B \to \R^3$ be a deformation of an open set $B \subseteq \R^3$, with [[deformation gradient|HTYRR2WDQ6]] $F$ and Jacobian $J = \det F > 0$.
Let $\sigma$ be the [[Cauchy stress tensor|KG2XWEMJ30]] field on $\varphi(B)$.
For $X \in B$, write $\sigma = \sigma(\varphi(X))$, $F = F(X)$ and $F^{-T} = (F^{-1})^T$.

1. The *first Piola–Kirchhoff stress tensor* at $X$ is $P = J\, \sigma\, F^{-T}$.
2. The *second Piola–Kirchhoff stress tensor* at $X$ is $S = F^{-1} P = J\, F^{-1} \sigma\, F^{-T}$.

## Remarks

Nanson's formula $n\, da = J F^{-T} N\, dA$ relates an oriented area element $N\, dA$ of the reference configuration to its image $n\, da$.
It gives $\sigma n\, da = P N\, dA$, so the tensor $P$ gives the current force per unit reference area.
If $\sigma$ is symmetric, then $S$ is symmetric, while $P$ is in general not symmetric.
For $F = I$, both tensors equal $\sigma$.
For a rigid rotation $F = Q$ with $Q$ orthogonal and $\det Q = 1$, one gets $P = \sigma Q$ and $S = Q^T \sigma Q$: here $S$ is the Cauchy stress expressed in the rotated material frame.
The tensor $S$ is work-conjugate to the Green–Lagrange strain, and $P$ is work-conjugate to $F$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Piola–Kirchhoff tensors are versions of stress measured against the undeformed body.
They are the convenient choice when the body changes shape a lot.
