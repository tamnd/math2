## Definition

Let $W$ and $V$ be independent standard [[Wiener processes|22735FGJV6]], and let $X_0$ be independent of them.
Let $b$, $\sigma$, $h$ satisfy standard regularity and growth conditions.
The signal $X_t \in \R^n$ and the observation $Y_t \in \R^d$ are as in the [[filtering problem|T97B6NBPA8]]:
$$dX_t = b(X_t)\, dt + \sigma(X_t)\, dW_t, \qquad dY_t = h(X_t)\, dt + dV_t, \qquad Y_0 = 0.$$
Let $\mathcal{Y}_t$ be the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $Y_s$, $0 \le s \le t$.
Let $L$ be the generator of $X$, acting on twice continuously differentiable functions $\varphi$ by
$$L\varphi = \sum_{i} b_i\, \partial_i \varphi + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i,j} (\sigma \sigma^T)_{ij}\, \partial_i \partial_j \varphi.$$
Let $\mathbb{Q}$ be the probability measure, given by Girsanov's theorem, under which $Y$ is a standard Wiener process independent of $X$.
Put $\Lambda_t = \exp\big(\int_0^t h(X_s)^T dY_s - \frac{1}{2} \int_0^t \norm{h(X_s)}^2 ds\big)$.
The *unnormalized conditional distribution* is $\rho_t(\varphi) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\varphi(X_t)\, \Lambda_t \mid \mathcal{Y}_t\big]$.
The *Zakai equation* is the equation, in the sense of [[Itô calculus|JA95NH206E]],
$$d\rho_t(\varphi) = \rho_t(L\varphi)\, dt + \rho_t(\varphi\, h)^T dY_t,$$
for all bounded $\varphi$ with bounded continuous first and second derivatives.
When $\rho_t$ has a smooth density $p(t, x)$, it reads
$$dp(t, x) = L^* p(t, x)\, dt + p(t, x)\, h(x)^T dY_t,$$
where $L^*$ is the formal adjoint of $L$.

## Remarks

The unnormalized distribution $\rho_t$ satisfies the Zakai equation under the conditions above.
The Kallianpur–Striebel formula $\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_t) \mid \mathcal{Y}_t] = \rho_t(\varphi)/\rho_t(1)$ recovers the [[conditional expectation|0TTDGTEFMN]] of the filtering problem.
Example: if $h = 0$, the observations carry no information, $\Lambda_t = 1$, and $\rho_t(\varphi) = \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_t)]$; the Zakai equation reduces to $\frac{d}{dt} \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_t)] = \mathbb{E}[L\varphi(X_t)]$, and the density form to the Fokker–Planck equation $\partial_t p = L^* p$.
Non-example: the normalized conditional distribution $\pi_t$ does not satisfy the Zakai equation in general; it satisfies the nonlinear [[Kushner equation|FQD9YT0DRK]], $d\pi_t(\varphi) = \pi_t(L\varphi)\, dt + \big(\pi_t(\varphi h) - \pi_t(\varphi)\, \pi_t(h)\big)^T \big(dY_t - \pi_t(h)\, dt\big)$.
The Zakai equation is a linear [[stochastic partial differential equation|PRCQG82QTK]], which is the main reason it is preferred for analysis and numerics.
