## Summary

Conical refraction is the spreading of a narrow beam of light into a hollow cone.
It happens when the beam travels along an optic axis of a biaxial crystal.
It comes from the singular points of [[Fresnel's wave surface|9ZXST6JR11]], where its two sheets meet.
William Rowan Hamilton predicted it in 1832 from the geometry of the wave surface, and Humphrey Lloyd observed it soon after.
It was an early case of a new physical effect found by mathematics before experiment.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Send a thin pencil of light through a biaxial crystal in just the right direction.
Instead of one or two spots on the far side, the light makes a bright ring.
Inside the crystal, the beam has opened into a hollow cone of rays.
This happens because, in that special direction, the crystal has not two but a whole circle of allowed ray directions.
