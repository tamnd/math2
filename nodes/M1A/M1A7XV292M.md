## Statement

Let $G > 0$ be the gravitational constant, let $m_1, m_2 > 0$, and let $M = m_1 + m_2$.
Let $x_1(t), x_2(t) \in \R^3$ be a solution of the [[two-body problem|CSVPJAA75E]] for all $t \in \R$, and let $r = x_2 - x_1$ be the relative position.
Assume that $r \times r' \neq 0$ and that the energy $E = \frac{1}{2} \norm{r'}^2 - G M / \norm{r}$ is negative.
Then:

1. The curve described by $r$ is an [[ellipse|0VKCCEXBJ1]] with one [[focus|0RBKKNH4GV]] at $0$.
2. The segment from $0$ to $r(t)$ sweeps out equal areas in equal times: the area swept between the times $t_0$ and $t_1$ is $\frac{1}{2} \norm{r \times r'} \, (t_1 - t_0)$.
3. The motion is periodic, and if $a$ is the semi-major axis of the ellipse, then its period $T$ satisfies $$T^2 = \frac{4 \pi^2}{G M} a^3.$$

## Remarks

The relative position solves the [[Kepler problem|TYXD1T6FRX]] $r'' = - G M r / \norm{r}^3$.
The second law only needs the force to be [[central|APR7XN00N3]]: then $h = r \times r'$ is constant, since $h' = r \times r'' = 0$, and the swept area grows at the rate $\norm{h}/2$.
This is the conservation of [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]].
The first law comes from the constancy of the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector, or from solving the Binet equation, which gives $\norm{r} = p / (1 + e \cos \theta)$ with $0 \le e < 1$ when $E < 0$.
For the third law, the area $\pi a b$ of the ellipse equals $\frac{1}{2} \norm{h} T$, and $b^2 = a \norm{h}^2 / (G M)$, which gives $T^2 = 4 \pi^2 a^3 / (G M)$.

If $E \ge 0$, the first and third laws fail: the orbit is a parabola or a branch of a hyperbola, and the motion is not periodic.
If $h = 0$, the bodies move on a line and collide in the past or in the future, so such a solution is not defined for all $t \in \R$.
For the Earth around the Sun, $a \approx 1.496 \times 10^{11}$ m and $T \approx 3.156 \times 10^7$ s, which gives $G M \approx 1.33 \times 10^{20}$ m$^3$ s$^{-2}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Kepler found three rules for the planets from observations, and Newton later derived them from his law of gravity.
Each planet moves on an ellipse with the Sun at a focus.
A planet moves faster near the Sun and slower far from it.
It does so in such a way that the line from the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal times.
Planets farther out take longer to go around, and the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the size of the orbit.

### L2 human

In the two-body problem the laws hold exactly for the relative motion, with $G M = G (m_1 + m_2)$ in the third law.
Kepler stated the third law with the mass of the Sun alone, which is accurate because the planets are much lighter.
The second law is valid for every central force, while the first and third are special to the inverse-square law.
The third law is used to weigh celestial bodies: measuring the period and the size of the orbit of a moon gives the mass of its planet.
Inside an orbit, the position as a function of time is found by solving Kepler's equation.
