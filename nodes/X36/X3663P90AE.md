## Summary

A constraint is a condition that the variables of an optimization problem must satisfy.
Typical constraints are equations $g(x) = 0$, inequalities $g(x) \le 0$, and conditions such as $x \in \Z^n$.
The constraints together cut out the [[feasible region|1TTKMRW8PD]], the set of points among which the best one is sought.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A constraint is a rule that a solution has to obey.
If you plan a diet, "at most 2000 calories per day" is a constraint, and "eat a nonnegative amount of each food" is another.
Without constraints, many problems have no answer or a silly answer, such as eating nothing at all.
For example, with variables $x, y$ the inequality $x + y \le 4$ and the equation $x - y = 0$ are two constraints, and together they allow exactly the points $(t, t)$ with $t \le 2$.
