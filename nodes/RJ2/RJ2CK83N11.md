## Statement

Consider steady, laminar, incompressible flow with free-stream speed $U$ along a flat plate at uniform temperature $T_w$, with free-stream temperature $T_\infty \ne T_w$.
Assume constant fluid properties, zero pressure gradient, negligible viscous heating, and boundary layer flow.
Let $\rho$ be the density, $c_p$ the specific heat, $\tau_w(x)$ the wall shear stress and $h(x)$ the local [[heat transfer coefficient|J821AD6YTE]].
Let $C_{f,x} = \tau_w / (\tfrac12 \rho U^2)$ be the local skin friction coefficient and $\mathrm{St}_x = h / (\rho c_p U)$ the local [[Stanton number|6T2FKXGS25]].
If the [[Prandtl number|C4KSBMR413]] is $\Pr = 1$, then
$$\mathrm{St}_x = \frac{C_{f,x}}{2} \quad \text{for every } x > 0.$$

## Remarks

The velocity $u/U$ and the dimensionless temperature $\theta = (T - T_w)/(T_\infty - T_w)$ satisfy the boundary layer equations $u \partial_x f + v \partial_y f = \nu \partial_y^2 f$ and $u \partial_x f + v \partial_y f = \alpha \partial_y^2 f$, with the same boundary values $0$ at the wall and $1$ far away.
When $\Pr = \nu / \alpha = 1$ the two problems coincide, so $\theta = u/U$.
Then $\tau_w = \mu U \, \partial_y \theta(x, 0)$ and $h = k \, \partial_y \theta(x, 0)$, and the ratio gives $\mathrm{St}_x / (C_{f,x}/2) = k / (\mu c_p) = 1/\Pr = 1$.
So a measurement of friction gives the heat transfer, which is the point of the analogy.

Without $\Pr = 1$ the identity fails.
The Blasius and Pohlhausen solutions give $C_{f,x} = 0.664 \, \mathrm{Re}_x^{-1/2}$ and $\mathrm{St}_x = 0.332 \, \mathrm{Re}_x^{-1/2} \Pr^{-2/3}$, where $\mathrm{Re}_x = Ux/\nu$ is the local [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]].
So $\mathrm{St}_x \Pr^{2/3} = C_{f,x}/2$ in general, which is the Chilton–Colburn analogy.
Osborne Reynolds proposed the analogy for turbulent flow, where it holds only approximately.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Friction at a wall and heat flow at a wall are carried by the same thin layer of fluid.
When the fluid spreads momentum and heat equally fast, the two effects follow the same law.
Then one number measured for friction tells you the other for heat.

### L2 human

The analogy is the simplest link between the velocity boundary layer and the [[thermal boundary layer|4VAN0ZE1PB]] in [[forced convection|QFBG7W198Q]].
It is exact for the laminar flat plate with $\Pr = 1$, and the factor $\Pr^{2/3}$ extends it to other fluids.
It does not apply where pressure gradients matter, for example on bluff bodies, because pressure acts on momentum but not on heat.
