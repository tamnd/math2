## Definition

Let a medium in a region $\Omega \subseteq \R^3$ have a piecewise continuous [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] $n \colon \Omega \to (0, \infty)$.
Let $C$ be a piecewise $C^1$ curve in $\Omega$ with arc length $s$.
The *optical path length* of $C$ is
$$\mathrm{OPL}(C) = \int_C n \, ds.$$

## Remarks

If $\gamma \colon [a, b] \to \Omega$ parametrizes $C$, then $\mathrm{OPL}(C) = \int_a^b n(\gamma(t)) \abs{\gamma'(t)} \, dt$.
Since light moves with speed $c / n$, the optical path length equals $c$ times the travel time along $C$.
The optical path difference between two paths is the difference of their optical path lengths; it gives the phase difference $2\pi \, \Delta\mathrm{OPL} / \lambda_0$, where $\lambda_0$ is the vacuum wavelength.

Example: a straight segment of length $5$ cm in air ($n = 1$) followed by $2$ cm in glass ($n = 1.5$).
Check: $\mathrm{OPL} = 1 \cdot 5 + 1.5 \cdot 2 = 8$ cm.

Non-example: the geometric length $\int_C ds$ is not the optical path length in a medium with $n \ne 1$; for the path above it is $7$ cm.
