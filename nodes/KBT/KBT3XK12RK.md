## Definition

*Mathematical biology*, also called theoretical biology or biomathematics, is the field of mathematics whose objects are mathematical models of biological systems.
A model of this kind consists of:

1. a set of state variables that stand for biological quantities, such as population sizes, concentrations or gene frequencies;
2. rules for their change in time or space, given by [[differential equations|003R0V5M7V]], difference equations, [[stochastic processes|0QH5ENKZM1]] or other [[dynamical systems|BBZP1R872N]];
3. parameters that stand for measurable biological rates or constants.

The field studies the existence, behaviour and stability of the solutions of such models, and compares them with data.

## Remarks

Example: the equation $N'(t) = r N(t)$, with $N$ a population size and $r$ a growth rate, is a model of mathematical biology.
It has a state variable $N$, a rule of change, and a parameter $r$.
Non-example: a table of measured population sizes alone is data and not a model, because it has no rule of change.
The main subfields are population dynamics, epidemiology, population genetics, mathematical physiology and the mathematics of phylogenetics.
