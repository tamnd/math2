## Definition

*Quantum field theory in curved spacetime* is [[quantum field theory|W031YNVRE3]] on a fixed, classical, curved spacetime that is not affected by the quantum fields.
Its basic model, the free real scalar field, is defined as follows.
Let $(M, g)$ be a globally hyperbolic, time-oriented [[Lorentzian manifold|BY4QYYS788]] of dimension $4$, let $m \ge 0$ and $\xi \in \R$, and let $R$ be the [[scalar curvature|ZHX2VYQDZ3]] of $g$.
Let $P = \square_g + m^2 + \xi R$ be the [[Klein–Gordon|AYCCDQWCJF]] operator of $g$, with $\square_g$ the d'Alembert operator of $g$.
Let $E = E^- - E^+$ be the causal propagator, the difference of the advanced and retarded fundamental solutions of $P$.
The field algebra is the unital $*$-algebra generated by symbols $\phi(f)$, for $f \in C_c^\infty(M)$ complex-valued, subject to the following relations, for all $f, h \in C_c^\infty(M)$ and $a, b \in \C$.

1. Linearity: $\phi(af + bh) = a\phi(f) + b\phi(h)$.
2. Hermiticity: $\phi(f)^* = \phi(\bar f)$.
3. Field equation: $\phi(Pf) = 0$.
4. Commutation relation: $[\phi(f), \phi(h)] = i E(f, h)\, 1$, where $E(f, h) = \int_M f\, (Eh)\, d\mathrm{vol}_g$.

A state of the theory is a positive normalized linear functional on this algebra.

## Remarks

Sign conventions for $\square_g$ and for $E$ differ between authors.
Example: for $M = \R^{1,3}$ with the Minkowski metric, the relations hold for the usual free field $\phi(f) = \int \phi(x) f(x)\, d^4x$ of mass $m$ on Fock space, with the Minkowski vacuum as a state.
Non-example: let $(M, g)$ be not globally hyperbolic, for instance with closed timelike curves.
Then $P$ need not have unique advanced and retarded fundamental solutions, and clause 4 is not defined.
The physically accepted states are the Hadamard states, whose two-point functions have the same short-distance singularity as the Minkowski vacuum.
The choice $\xi = 1/6$ with $m = 0$ gives the conformally coupled field.
