## Problem

Let $\Omega \subset \R^2$ be a bounded simply connected domain with smooth boundary, and let a straight bar occupy $\Omega \times [0, L]$.
The bar is made of a homogeneous isotropic linear elastic material with [[shear modulus|J2GWW1NPTC]] $G$.
Its lateral surface is free of traction, and there are no body forces.
The end faces carry tractions with zero resultant force, whose resultant moment is a torque $T$ about the axis of the bar.
Find the displacement and the stress in the bar, and the twist per unit length $\theta$ as a function of $T$.

## Remarks

Saint-Venant's semi-inverse method takes the displacement $u = (-\theta y z, \theta x z, \theta \psi(x, y))$, where $\psi$ is the warping function.
The only nonzero stresses are $\sigma_{xz} = G\theta(\partial_x \psi - y)$ and $\sigma_{yz} = G\theta(\partial_y \psi + x)$.
Equilibrium and a free lateral surface hold exactly when $\Delta \psi = 0$ in $\Omega$ and $\partial \psi / \partial n = y n_x - x n_y$ on $\partial \Omega$, where $n$ is the outer normal.
This Neumann problem has a solution, unique up to a constant, because the boundary data has integral zero.
Then $T = G J \theta$, where the torsion constant is $J = \int_\Omega (x^2 + y^2 + x \partial_y \psi - y \partial_x \psi) \, dA$.
The end tractions are those given by this stress field; by Saint-Venant's principle, other end loads with the same resultant give nearly the same field away from the ends.
For a disc of radius $a$, $\psi = 0$ and $J = \pi a^4 / 2$: cross-sections do not warp.
For an ellipse with semi-axes $a$ and $b$, $\psi = -\frac{a^2 - b^2}{a^2 + b^2} x y$ and $J = \pi a^3 b^3 / (a^2 + b^2)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Twist a long bar by its ends and ask how much it turns and where it is most stressed.
For a round bar the answer is simple: each cross-section turns rigidly like a coin.
For any other shape the cross-sections also bend out of their plane, and finding this warping is a problem about the Laplace equation on the cross-section.
