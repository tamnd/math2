## Summary

The great-circle distance between two points of a [[sphere|P9CM4GQ2T0]] is the length of the shortest path between them on the sphere.
This path is an arc of a great circle, a circle whose centre is the centre of the sphere.
The distance equals the radius times the angle between the two position vectors.
On a spherical model of the Earth it is the distance travelled along the surface.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a globe, the shortest route between two cities is not the straight line on a flat map.
Stretch a string tightly between the cities on the globe: it follows a great circle.
That is why many flights from Europe to North America pass over Greenland.
For example, the distance along the surface from the North Pole to the equator is a quarter of a great circle, about $10000$ km.
