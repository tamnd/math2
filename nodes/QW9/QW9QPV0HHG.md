## Summary

Stellar pulsation is the periodic oscillation of a star about its state of [[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]].
Mathematically the small oscillations are the [[normal modes|JVM27J1FTF]] of the linearised equations of a self-gravitating fluid sphere.
The periods depend on the internal structure of the star, so pulsations are a probe of stellar interiors.
Pulsating stars such as Cepheids also serve as distance indicators.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A star is a ball of gas in which gravity pulls inward and pressure pushes outward.
If the balance is disturbed, the star can swell and shrink again and again, like a ringing bell.
Its brightness then changes with a regular period.
For a radial mode the whole star breathes in and out, while for a non-radial mode some regions move outward as others move inward.
The pulsation period of a star is roughly proportional to $1/\sqrt{G \bar\rho}$, where $\bar\rho$ is its mean density, so dense stars pulsate fast.
