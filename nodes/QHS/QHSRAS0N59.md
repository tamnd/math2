## Summary

The Camassa–Holm equation is a nonlinear partial differential equation for shallow [[water waves|R9WC0Z7Z69]] moving in one direction.
Like the [[Korteweg–de Vries equation|KTT75QC72Z]] it is an [[integrable system|N0N7CMRCMH]], with a Lax pair and infinitely many conservation laws.
Unlike KdV it has solitary waves with a corner at the crest, called peakons.
Its smooth solutions can also break in finite time, as real waves do.
It was derived by Roberto Camassa and Darryl Holm in 1993.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Simple model equations for water waves are often either exactly solvable but unable to describe breaking, or able to describe breaking but not solvable.
The Camassa–Holm equation has both features: it is exactly solvable in a deep sense, and its waves can still steepen and break.
Its simplest solitary wave looks like a tent, $c\,e^{-\abs{x - ct}}$, with a sharp peak, moving at a speed equal to its height.
Several such peaks can collide and come out with their shapes and speeds unchanged, as solitons do.
