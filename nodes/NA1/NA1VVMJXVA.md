## Summary

The heat capacity ratio of a substance is the quotient $\gamma = C_p / C_V$ of its [[heat capacity|SYYGZ18FF5]] at constant pressure and at constant volume.
It is also called the adiabatic index.
It governs reversible adiabatic processes of an ideal gas, through $pV^\gamma = \text{const}$, and it enters the speed of sound in a gas.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heating a gas at constant pressure costs more heat than heating it in a rigid box, because the gas also does work as it expands.
The heat capacity ratio says how much more: it is the first cost divided by the second.
For air it is close to $1.4$, and for a gas of single atoms such as helium it is close to $5/3$.
When a gas is squeezed quickly so that no heat escapes, its pressure rises faster than when it is squeezed slowly at constant temperature, and $\gamma$ measures this.
