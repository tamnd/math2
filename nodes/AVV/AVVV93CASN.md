## Summary

A root system is a finite set of vectors in a Euclidean space that is preserved by the reflections in the hyperplanes orthogonal to them.
In addition, an integrality condition restricts the angles and the lengths.
Root systems arise from semisimple Lie algebras over $\C$ and classify them.
They also govern reflection groups, algebraic groups and many finite configurations in geometry.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a finite, very symmetric star of arrows in the plane or in space.
If you reflect the whole star in the line or plane at right angles to any one of its arrows, you get the same star back.
On top of this, the angles between arrows can only be a few special values, such as $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$ or $120^\circ$.
The six vectors pointing to the corners of a regular hexagon centred at the origin form such a star, called $A_2$.
