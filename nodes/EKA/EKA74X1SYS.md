## Summary

Causality conditions are global conditions on a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold that rule out various kinds of causal pathology, such as [[closed timelike curves|DCM7BJ25R8]].
They form a hierarchy, from the chronology condition up to global hyperbolicity, each one implying the weaker ones.
They are stated in terms of the [[causal structure|KZWG5F0TSB]] and are hypotheses of most global theorems of general relativity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In ordinary physics, causes come before their effects, and no one can travel into their own past.
Einstein's equations do not enforce this on their own: some solutions contain loops in time.
Causality conditions are rules one can impose on a spacetime to exclude such behaviour.
The weakest says no observer can return to an earlier moment of their own life.
Stronger ones also forbid near-misses, where an observer comes back arbitrarily close to an earlier event.
