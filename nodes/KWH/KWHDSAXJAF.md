## Example

A course in real analysis has three lectures a week.
In each lecture the teacher states and proves the theorems of the course, for example the theorem that a convergent sequence is bounded.
Each week the students hand in proofs of exercises from the textbook, and a tutor marks them.
We compare the course with the features of the [[Moore method|WKM6535VHG]].

1. A list of theorems for the students to prove: absent, since the teacher proves the theorems.
2. Proofs by students: present only for exercises, which lie off the main line of the theory.
3. Presentation at the board by students: absent.
4. Checking by the class: absent, since a tutor marks the work.
5. The class builds the theory: absent, since the theory comes from the teacher.

Features 1, 3, 4 and 5 fail, so the course is not an instance of the method.

## Remarks

The students of this course do write proofs, so the course trains the writing of proofs.
What it lacks is the core of the method: the students do not produce the theory of the course.
A teacher can move such a course toward the method in small steps, for example by setting one theorem of the main line each week for students to prove and present.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In this course, the teacher proves the main theorems, and the students prove some exercises at home.
In the Moore method, the students prove the main theorems themselves and show them to the class.
So homework with proofs is not enough to make a course a Moore course.

### L2 human

For each feature of the method, ask whether the course has it, as in the numbered check.
Here the course lacks the student-made theory, the presentation and the checking by the class.
Watch out for counting proof homework as "inquiry".
The test is who proves the theorems on which the course is built, and here it is the teacher.
