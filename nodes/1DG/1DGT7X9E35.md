## Example

A [[conic|00HJCMC2CD]] $ax^2 + bxy + cy^2 + dx + ey + f = 0$ has the quadratic part $Q(x, y) = ax^2 + bxy + cy^2$, with matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b/2 \\ b/2 & c \end{pmatrix}$.
The method has three stages.
First, rotate the axes by [[the principal axis theorem|0PR5P9Z4KM]], to remove the $xy$ term.
Second, complete the squares in the new coordinates, to remove the linear terms where possible.
Third, read the type from the signs of the eigenvalues.

**A hyperbola.** Let $x^2 + 4xy + y^2 - 6x - 6y + 3 = 0$.

1. The matrix is $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, with eigenvalue $3$ on $(1, 1)$ and $-1$ on $(-1, 1)$.
2. The centre solves $2A(x, y)^T = (6, 6)^T$, so it is $(1, 1)$.
3. At the centre, the left side has the value $1 + 4 + 1 - 6 - 6 + 3 = -3$.
4. With $x = 1 + X$ and $y = 1 + Y$, the equation becomes $X^2 + 4XY + Y^2 = 3$.
5. The rotation $X = (u - v)/\sqrt 2$, $Y = (u + v)/\sqrt 2$ gives $3u^2 - v^2 = 3$.
6. So the curve is the [[hyperbola|0WN5G117YC]] $u^2 - v^2/3 = 1$, with vertices at $u = \pm 1$, $v = 0$, and asymptotes $v = \pm \sqrt 3\, u$.
7. The vertices are $(x, y) = (1 \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}, 1 \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt 2})$, with the same sign in both places.

**A parabola.** Let $x^2 + 2xy + y^2 + 2x - 6y + 5 = 0$.

1. The matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, with eigenvalue $2$ on $(1, 1)$ and $0$ on $(-1, 1)$.
2. Since $\det A = 0$, there is no unique centre, so we rotate first.
3. The rotation $x = (u - v)/\sqrt 2$, $y = (u + v)/\sqrt 2$ gives $x^2 + 2xy + y^2 = (x + y)^2 = 2u^2$.
4. The linear part becomes $2x - 6y = -2\sqrt 2\, u - 4\sqrt 2\, v$.
5. The equation is $2u^2 - 2\sqrt 2\, u - 4\sqrt 2\, v + 5 = 0$.
6. Completing the square gives $2u^2 - 2\sqrt 2\, u = 2(u - \frac{1}{\sqrt 2})^2 - 1$.
7. So the equation is $2(u - \frac{1}{\sqrt 2})^2 = 4\sqrt 2\, (v - \frac{1}{\sqrt 2})$.
8. This is a [[parabola|04S2GMHX82]] with vertex at $u = v = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}$, which is $(x, y) = (0, 1)$.
9. Check: at $(0, 1)$, the left side is $0 + 0 + 1 + 0 - 6 + 5 = 0$.
10. The axis of the parabola is the line through $(0, 1)$ in the direction $(-1, 1)$, the eigenvector of the eigenvalue $0$.

**An ellipse.** Let $5x^2 + 4xy + 2y^2 - 14x - 8y + 5 = 0$.

1. The matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, with eigenvalue $6$ on $(2, 1)$ and $1$ on $(-1, 2)$.
2. The centre solves $2A(x, y)^T = (14, 8)^T$, so it is $(1, 1)$.
3. At the centre, the left side has the value $5 + 4 + 2 - 14 - 8 + 5 = -6$.
4. With $x = 1 + X$ and $y = 1 + Y$, the equation becomes $5X^2 + 4XY + 2Y^2 = 6$.
5. The rotation of [[the ellipse example|GP4XSY4FNH]] gives $6u^2 + v^2 = 6$.
6. So the curve is the [[ellipse|0VKCCEXBJ1]] $u^2 + v^2/6 = 1$, with semi-axis $1$ along $(2, 1)$ and semi-axis $\sqrt 6$ along $(-1, 2)$.

## Remarks

The signs of the eigenvalues of $A$ give the type when the conic is not degenerate.
Two eigenvalues of one sign give an ellipse, two of opposite signs give a hyperbola, and one zero eigenvalue gives a parabola.
Since $\det A = ac - b^2/4 = -\frac14 (b^2 - 4ac)$, this is [[the classification by the discriminant|0N5NR32NQG]]: here $b^2 - 4ac$ is $12$, $0$ and $-24$.
The same steps also give the degenerate conics:

- $x^2 - y^2 = 0$ is a pair of lines that meet.
- $x^2 + y^2 = 0$ is one point.
- $x^2 = 1$ is a pair of parallel lines.
- $x^2 = 0$ is one line.
- $x^2 + y^2 = -1$ and $x^2 = -1$ have no real points.

The geometry of conics, with foci and eccentricity, is in [[the topic on conic sections|NNJH85SH0H]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A tilted conic has an $xy$ term in its equation.
A rotation removes it, and a shift of the origin removes most of the linear terms.
Then the equation has a standard form that shows the type and the size of the curve.

### L2 human

The usual order is: centre first if $\det A \ne 0$, then rotation.
If $\det A = 0$, rotate first and then complete the square, as for the parabola.
A trap: the value at the centre moves to the right side with the opposite sign; here $-3$ gives the right side $3$.
Another trap: for the ellipse, the semi-axis is $\sqrt{6/6} = 1$ along the eigenvector of $6$, and $\sqrt{6/1}$ along that of $1$.
