## Summary

The Oort constants $A$ and $B$ describe the differential rotation of the Milky Way in the neighbourhood of the Sun.
They are built from the circular speed and its radial derivative at the radius of the Sun.
They can be measured from the motions of nearby stars alone.
They give the local angular velocity and the local slope of the [[rotation curve|V4FYBDSGMT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Milky Way does not rotate like a rigid wheel: the inner parts take less time to go round than the outer parts.
So nearby stars drift relative to the Sun in a regular pattern that depends on their direction in the sky.
Jan Oort showed in 1927 that two numbers, now called $A$ and $B$, describe this pattern.
$A$ measures the shear of the rotation and $B$ measures the local spin.
For a rigid rotation, $A$ would be zero, because nothing would shear.
