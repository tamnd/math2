## Summary

A polhode is the curve traced by the angular velocity vector of a torque-free rigid body, seen in a frame attached to the body.
It lies on [[Poinsot's ellipsoid|FHCJDYE7YF]], where it is cut out by the conservation of energy and of the length of the angular momentum.
The companion curve, traced by the same vector in the fixed invariable plane, is the herpolhode.
The shapes of the polhodes show at a glance which rotations of a body are stable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a body tumbles freely, the axis about which it spins at each moment wanders through the body.
The tip of the angular velocity vector draws a closed loop on an egg-shaped surface fixed in the body; this loop is the polhode.
For a body spun nearly about its longest or shortest principal axis, the loop is a small oval around that axis, so the spin stays close to it.
Near the middle axis there are no such small loops, and the spin axis wanders far away.
