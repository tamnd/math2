## Summary

The Kerr effect is a change of the [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] of a medium proportional to the square of an applied electric field.
In the electro-optic form the field is a static or slowly varying field, and it makes an isotropic medium [[birefringent|8CWCYQJ44T]].
In the optical form the field is that of the light itself, so the index depends on the intensity: $n = n_0 + n_2 I$.
The optical Kerr effect comes from the third-order [[nonlinear susceptibility|7N71T3FKZ6]] and causes self-focusing, self-phase modulation and optical solitons.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a strong electric field, the molecules of a liquid tend to line up, and light polarized along the field then travels at a different speed.
This is the Kerr effect, named after John Kerr.
Light that is intense enough can do the same to the medium by itself.
Then the brighter part of a beam travels more slowly than the dimmer part.
This small effect drives many striking phenomena, such as a beam that focuses itself.
