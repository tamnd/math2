## Summary

The Joule–Thomson effect is the temperature change of a real gas or liquid forced through a valve or porous plug to lower pressure.
No heat is exchanged with the surroundings.
Such a throttling process keeps the [[enthalpy|722RZK72P5]] constant.
The rate of change of temperature with pressure is the Joule–Thomson coefficient.
For an ideal gas it is zero, and for real gases it can have either sign.
The effect is the basis of gas liquefaction and of many refrigerators.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When gas escapes through a narrow valve from a high-pressure tank, it usually comes out cooler.
The reason is that the molecules of a real gas attract each other, and moving them apart as the gas expands costs energy, taken from their motion.
An ideal gas, whose molecules do not interact, would come out at the same temperature.
Above a certain temperature, called the inversion temperature, the gas warms instead of cooling.
Hydrogen and helium have low inversion temperatures, so they must be precooled before throttling can liquefy them.
