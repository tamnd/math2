## Summary

Ambient noise seismic interferometry estimates the seismic waves that would travel between two stations.
It uses the cross-correlation of the ambient noise recorded at both.
When the noise wavefield is diffuse, coming from sources all around, the long-time average cross-correlation is related to the [[Green's function|SH6JY9RN2E]] between the stations.
The method turns every station into a virtual source without any earthquake, and is used mostly with surface waves generated by ocean waves.
The travel times of the recovered waves feed surface-wave tomography of the crust, and their changes over time monitor volcanoes and fault zones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The ground always trembles slightly, mostly because of ocean waves hitting the coasts.
If two seismometers record this trembling for weeks, some of the waves pass first one station and then the other.
Comparing the two records at all time shifts reveals a peak at the time a wave needs to go from one station to the other.
So one learns how fast waves travel between the stations without waiting for an earthquake.
For stations $30$ km apart and waves of speed $3$ km per second, the peak appears at a shift of $10$ seconds.
