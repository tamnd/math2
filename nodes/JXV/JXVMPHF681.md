## Summary

Geomagnetic secular variation is the slow change of the internal part of [[Earth's magnetic field|Z13HPJFV1F]] over years to centuries.
It comes from the flow of liquid iron in the outer core, which carries and stretches the magnetic field lines.
One well-known feature is the westward drift of parts of the field, of about $0.2$ degrees of longitude per year.
Others are the decrease of the dipole strength since the first absolute measurements in the 1830s, and the motion of the magnetic poles.
It is described by the time derivatives of the Gauss coefficients, and it limits how long any [[field model|YKKYJFPXYG]] stays accurate.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The direction in which a compass points slowly changes over the years at every place on Earth.
In London, for example, magnetic north has moved from east of true north to west of it and back over the last few centuries.
These changes come from currents of molten metal in the core, which move the field around like dye in a slowly stirred liquid.
Maps for navigation therefore print the yearly change of the declination next to its value.
