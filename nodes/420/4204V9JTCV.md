## Example

An *LP interpretation* is a pair $(E, A)$ of subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ with $E \cup A = \mathbb{N}$.
Let $n$ be the value of the term $t$.
Then $\|T(t)\|$ is $1$ if $n \in E \setminus A$, $0$ if $n \in A \setminus E$, and $\mathrm{b}$ if $n \in E \cap A$.
Compound sentences get values by the [[LP|WH94DG6D3Q]] tables, which are the strong Kleene tables with $\mathrm{b}$ in the middle.
The jump is $\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{b}}(E, A) := (\{\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner : \|\varphi\| \in \{1, \mathrm{b}\}\}, \{\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner : \|\varphi\| \in \{0, \mathrm{b}\}\} \cup (\mathbb{N} \setminus \mathrm{Sent}))$.
Let $\lambda := \exists y\,(D(\underline{k}, y) \wedge \neg T(y))$ be the liar from the [[proof of the diagonal lemma|SFPSB6JPTC]].
Let $(E, A)$ be an LP interpretation with $\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{b}}(E, A) = (E, A)$.

1. $\|\lambda\| = \|\neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)\|$, because $D(\underline{k}, m)$ is true only for $m = \ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$.
2. By the fixed point condition, $\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \in E$ if and only if $\|\lambda\| \in \{1, \mathrm{b}\}$, and $\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \in A$ if and only if $\|\lambda\| \in \{0, \mathrm{b}\}$.
3. Suppose $\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \in E \setminus A$. Then $\|T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)\| = 1$, so $\|\lambda\| = 0$ by step 1.
4. Then $\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \in A$ by step 2, which contradicts step 3.
5. Suppose $\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \in A \setminus E$. Then $\|T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)\| = 0$, so $\|\lambda\| = 1$ by step 1.
6. Then $\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \in E$ by step 2, which contradicts step 5.
7. Since $E \cup A = \mathbb{N}$, the only case left is $\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \in E \cap A$.
8. So $\|T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)\| = \mathrm{b}$ and $\|\lambda\| = \mathrm{b}$. The liar is a [[glut|WH94DG6D3Q]].
9. Then $\lambda \wedge \neg \lambda$ has the designated value $\mathrm{b}$: the fixed point makes a contradiction true.

*Existence of a fixed point.* Order LP interpretations by inclusion in both components.

10. $\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{b}}$ is monotone. In a larger pair, a value $1$ or $0$ stays the same or becomes $\mathrm{b}$, and $\mathrm{b}$ stays $\mathrm{b}$.
11. Start from $(\mathbb{N}, \mathbb{N})$, apply $\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{b}}$ at successors, and take intersections at limits. Each value is $1$, $\mathrm{b}$ or $0$, so each jump covers $\mathbb{N}$. By [[transfinite induction|0ZHQMQ2C1N]] and step 10, the stages decrease.
12. An intersection of a decreasing chain of LP interpretations covers $\mathbb{N}$. If $n$ leaves $E$ at stage $\beta$, then $n \in A_\gamma$ for all $\gamma$, since $n \in A_\gamma$ for $\gamma \ge \beta$ and $A_\gamma \supseteq A_\beta$ for $\gamma < \beta$.
13. A strictly decreasing sequence of pairs of subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ has countable length. So some stage is a fixed point.

## Remarks

This construction is the dual of [[Kripke's construction|ES71QTSTNV]]: it starts from total glut and removes gluts, where Kripke starts from total gap and fills gaps.
The SEP entry states that Kripke did not give this version, and it names Dowden, Leitgeb, Priest, Visser and Woodruff for variants.
In the fixed point, $\lambda$ and $T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$ have the same value, as the naive truth schema requires in its value form.
The dialetheist reading is that the liar is both true and false, and that this is a fact.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the logic of paradox, a sentence may be on the "true" list and on the "false" list at once.
Every sentence must be on at least one list.
The liar cannot be on only one list, by the usual liar argument.
So the liar is on both lists.

### L2 human

1. The value $v$ of $\lambda$ equals the value of $\neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
2. In a fixed point, the value of $T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$ equals $v$.
3. So $v = \neg v$ in the LP tables.
4. Among $1, \mathrm{b}, 0$, only $\mathrm{b}$ satisfies $v = \neg v$.

The typical use is to show that the naive truth schema, in value form, has a model in $\mathrm{LP}$.
The trap is step 4 in the wrong logic: in $K_3$ the same equation gives $\mathrm{u}$, a gap.
