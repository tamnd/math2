## Example

Problem: the points $A$ and $B$ lie on the same side of a line $\ell$.
Find the point $P$ on $\ell$ for which the length $AP + PB$ is as small as possible.
The two segments $AP$ and $PB$ meet at an angle, so the path is not a straight segment, and we cannot use "the shortest path is straight" directly.
We add an [[auxiliary element|8TKKRAXKFN]]: the reflection $B'$ of $B$ in $\ell$.

1. Let $B'$ be the mirror image of $B$ in $\ell$. Then $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of the segment $BB'$.
2. Each point $P$ of $\ell$ is equally far from $B$ and from $B'$, so $PB = PB'$.
3. So $AP + PB = AP + PB'$ for every $P$ on $\ell$. The problem becomes: minimize $AP + PB'$.
4. By the [[triangle inequality|03HF5PMY5T]], $AP + PB' \geq AB'$, with equality exactly when $P$ lies on the segment $AB'$.
5. The points $A$ and $B'$ lie on opposite sides of $\ell$, so the segment $AB'$ meets $\ell$ in one point $P^*$.
6. So $P^*$ is the unique point that minimizes $AP + PB$, and the least length is $AB'$.

A numerical case: let $\ell$ be the $x$-axis, $A = (0, 3)$ and $B = (4, 1)$.

1. The reflection is $B' = (4, -1)$.
2. The line through $A$ and $B'$ has slope $\frac{-1 - 3}{4 - 0} = -1$, so it is $y = 3 - x$, and it meets the $x$-axis at $P^* = (3, 0)$.
3. The least length is $AB' = \sqrt{4^2 + 4^2} = 4\sqrt{2} \approx 5.657$.
4. Check against another point, $P = (2, 0)$: $AP + PB = \sqrt{4 + 9} + \sqrt{4 + 1} \approx 3.606 + 2.236 = 5.842$, which is larger.

## Remarks

The auxiliary point turns a bent path into a straight one, where the triangle inequality applies.
At $P^*$ the segments $AP^*$ and $P^*B$ make equal angles with $\ell$, because reflection keeps angles.
This is the law of reflection of light, and Heron of Alexandria explained that law by this shortest-path argument.

If $A$ and $B$ lie on opposite sides of $\ell$, no auxiliary point is needed: the segment $AB$ itself meets $\ell$.
If the path must touch two lines, as for a ball that hits two walls of a table, two reflections are needed, one for each line.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A person at $A$ walks to a river, fills a bucket, and walks to a house at $B$, on the same side of the river.
Where should the person reach the river to walk the least?
Imagine the house reflected in the river, as in a mirror.
The walk to the river and on to the real house is as long as the walk to the river and on to the mirror house.
The shortest walk to the mirror house is a straight line, and it crosses the river at the best point.

### L2 human

We find the shortest path from $A = (1, 2)$ to $B = (5, 4)$ that touches the $x$-axis.

1. Reflect $B$ in the $x$-axis: $B' = (5, -4)$.
2. The line through $A$ and $B'$ has slope $\frac{-4 - 2}{5 - 1} = -\frac{3}{2}$, so it is $y = 2 - \frac{3}{2}(x - 1)$.
3. It meets the $x$-axis where $\frac{3}{2}(x - 1) = 2$, so $x = \frac{7}{3}$ and $P^* = \left(\frac{7}{3}, 0\right)$.
4. The least length is $AB' = \sqrt{4^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13} \approx 7.211$.

The typical use: a reflection, rotation or translation of one part of a figure turns a broken path into a segment.

A trap: the point must be reflected in the line $\ell$ that the path touches, since only that reflection keeps $PB = PB'$ for every $P$ on $\ell$.
