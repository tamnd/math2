## Example

The points $A$ and $B$ lie on the same side of a line $\ell$, and we look for the point $P$ on $\ell$ that makes the length $AP + PB$ as small as possible.
The segments $AP$ and $PB$ meet at an angle, so the rule "the shortest path is straight" does not apply as it stands.
We add an [[auxiliary element|8TKKRAXKFN]]: the mirror image $B'$ of $B$ in $\ell$.

1. The line $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of the segment $BB'$, by the definition of a reflection.
2. So each point $P$ of $\ell$ is equally far from $B$ and from $B'$, and $AP + PB = AP + PB'$.
3. The problem becomes: minimize $AP + PB'$ over the points $P$ of $\ell$.
4. By the [[triangle inequality|03HF5PMY5T]], $AP + PB' \ge AB'$, with equality exactly when $P$ lies on the segment $AB'$.
5. The points $A$ and $B'$ lie on opposite sides of $\ell$, so the segment $AB'$ meets $\ell$ in exactly one point $P^*$.
6. So $P^*$ is the unique point that minimizes $AP + PB$, and the least length is $AB'$.

Now a case with numbers: let $\ell$ be the $x$-axis, $A = (0, 3)$ and $B = (4, 1)$.

1. The reflection of $B$ is $B' = (4, -1)$.
2. The line through $A$ and $B'$ has slope $\frac{-1 - 3}{4 - 0} = -1$, so it is $y = 3 - x$, and it meets the $x$-axis at $P^* = (3, 0)$.
3. The least length is $AB' = \sqrt{4^2 + 4^2} = 4\sqrt{2} \approx 5.657$.
4. Check against the point $P = (2, 0)$:
   $$AP + PB = \sqrt{4 + 9} + \sqrt{4 + 1} \approx 3.606 + 2.236 = 5.842,$$
   which is larger.

## Remarks

The auxiliary point turns a bent path into a straight one, where the triangle inequality applies.
At $P^*$ the segments $AP^*$ and $P^*B$ make equal angles with $\ell$, because a reflection keeps angles.
That is the law of reflection of light, and Heron of Alexandria explained the law by this shortest-path argument.

The construction depends on the position of the points.
If $A$ and $B$ lie on opposite sides of $\ell$, no auxiliary point is needed, because the segment $AB$ itself meets $\ell$.
If the path must touch two lines, as a ball that hits two walls of a table, you need two reflections, one for each line.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A person at $A$ walks to a river, fills a bucket, and walks on to a house at $B$, on the same side of the river.
Where should the person reach the river to walk the least?
Imagine the house reflected in the river, as in a mirror.
The walk to the river and on to the real house is as long as the walk to the river and on to the mirror house.
The shortest walk to the mirror house is a straight line, and it crosses the river at the best point.

### L2 human

We find the shortest path from $A = (1, 2)$ to $B = (5, 4)$ that touches the $x$-axis.
Reflect $B$ in the axis to get $B' = (5, -4)$.
The line through $A$ and $B'$ has slope $\frac{-4 - 2}{5 - 1} = -\frac{3}{2}$, so it is $y = 2 - \frac{3}{2}(x - 1)$.
It meets the axis where $\frac{3}{2}(x - 1) = 2$, that is at $x = \frac{7}{3}$, so $P^* = \left(\frac{7}{3}, 0\right)$.
The least length is
$$AB' = \sqrt{4^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13} \approx 7.211.$$

A reflection, a rotation or a translation of one part of a figure often turns a broken path into a segment.
Reflect in the line that the path touches, and in no other line, since only that reflection keeps $PB = PB'$ for every $P$ on the line.
