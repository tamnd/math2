## Definition

A [[rational function|0XDK9CGYKJ]] $G$ of a complex variable $s$, with real coefficients, is *positive real* if:

1. $G$ has no [[pole|1TE3QF8EYC]] in the open right half-plane $\set{s \in \C : \operatorname{Re} s > 0}$, so that it is [[holomorphic|0NAHXR1FRH]] there;
2. $\operatorname{Re} G(s) \ge 0$ for every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$.

Since the coefficients are real, $G(s)$ is real for real $s$ that are not poles.

## Remarks

There is an equivalent test on the imaginary axis.
A real rational $G$ is positive real if and only if:

- it has no pole with $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$;
- its poles on the imaginary axis are simple with real positive residues;
- $\operatorname{Re} G(i\omega) \ge 0$ for every real $\omega$ that is not a pole.
- if the degree of the numerator exceeds that of the denominator, then $G(s)/s$ tends to a positive real number as $\abs{s} \to \infty$.

If $G$ is positive real and not identically $0$, then so is $1/G$, and the degrees of numerator and denominator differ by at most $1$.
The function is *strictly positive real* if $G(s - \varepsilon)$ is positive real for some $\varepsilon > 0$.

Example: $G(s) = 1/(s + 1)$.
Its only pole is $-1$, and for $s = \sigma + i\omega$ with $\sigma > 0$, $\operatorname{Re} G(s) = (\sigma + 1)/\abs{s + 1}^2 > 0$.

Non-example: $G(s) = 1/(s + 1)^2$ satisfies clause 1 but not clause 2.
At $s = 0.1 + 2i$ one has $(s + 1)^2 = (1.1 + 2i)^2 = -2.79 + 4.4i$, whose reciprocal has real part $-2.79/\abs{-2.79 + 4.4i}^2 < 0$.
