## Summary

A gravitational singularity is a place where a spacetime of general relativity breaks down.
Freely falling observers or light rays reach it in finite time and cannot be continued.
Since the singular "points" are not part of the spacetime, the standard definition uses incomplete [[geodesics|NSYMQMHPDN]] of a spacetime that cannot be extended.
Examples are the centre $r = 0$ of the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]] and the big bang of cosmological models.
The [[Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems|WP4YAHJ6V4]] show that singularities form under general physical conditions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In Newton's theory, the gravity of a point mass becomes infinite at the point.
In general relativity something worse can happen: spacetime itself has an edge.
An astronaut falling into a non-rotating black hole reaches the centre after a finite time on her own clock, and the theory says nothing about what happens next.
Such an edge is a singularity.
Often, but not always, curvature quantities such as the [[Kretschmann scalar|62JFWFJ20F]] grow without bound as one approaches it.
