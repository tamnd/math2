## Summary

The mean free path is the average distance that a particle travels between two successive collisions.
For a particle moving among obstacles of number density $n$ and collision cross-section $\sigma$, it equals $1/(n\sigma)$.
It sets the length scale of kinetic theory.
The [[Boltzmann equation|MH6CSWD7F0]] is needed for gases whose mean free path is not small compared with the size of the container.
The ratio of the mean free path to a macroscopic length is the Knudsen number.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A molecule in air flies in a straight line until it bumps into another molecule, then flies off in a new direction.
The mean free path is the average length of these straight pieces.
The more crowded the gas and the bigger the molecules, the shorter it is.
In air at room conditions it is well below a micrometre, while in the thin gas of high altitudes it can reach metres.
