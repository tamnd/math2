## Summary

A statistical ensemble is a probability distribution over the [[microstates|S1NST0AG71]] of a system.
It can be pictured as a very large collection of imagined copies of the system, each in some microstate.
The frequencies of the microstates among the copies are given by the distribution.
The standard equilibrium ensembles are the [[microcanonical|TDGEGWHXCT]], [[canonical|G1P1A0N7NZ]], [[grand canonical|1C5S72A9JX]] and [[isothermal–isobaric|M2EASY53HE]] ensembles.
They differ in which quantities are held fixed.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We never know the exact microstate of a gas, so we describe our knowledge by probabilities.
Imagine a million copies of the same box of gas, prepared in the same way, but each with its molecules in a different arrangement.
The ensemble records which arrangements occur and how often.
For a die that has not been looked at yet, the ensemble is the uniform distribution on the six faces.
Averages over the ensemble are the predictions of the theory.
