## Summary

A holonomic constraint on a mechanical system is an equation between the positions of its parts and possibly the time.
Rigid rods, inextensible strings and surfaces on which a particle must stay give such constraints.
Each independent holonomic constraint removes one coordinate, so a system with holonomic constraints can be described by fewer, independent coordinates.
Constraints that only restrict velocities and cannot be reduced to position equations are called nonholonomic.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bead on a circular wire of radius $1$ in the plane must satisfy $x^2 + y^2 = 1$.
This is an equation between the coordinates only, so it is a holonomic constraint.
It turns the two coordinates $x, y$ into one free coordinate, the angle on the circle.
A pendulum on a rigid rod and two masses joined by a fixed-length string are other examples.
