## Summary

This area studies families of random variables indexed by time or by space as whole random objects, with their laws, their path properties and their long-run behaviour.
A stochastic process is a collection of random variables on one probability space, indexed by a set such as the integers or the half-line.
Stochastic processes model queues, populations, prices, signals and physical diffusion, and Brownian motion is a central object both in probability and in analysis.
Markov introduced the chains that have his name in 1906, and Wiener gave the first rigorous construction of Brownian motion in 1923.
This topic collects the stochastic processes pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Probability theory, stochastic analysis, and random structures and probabilistic models have their own topics.
