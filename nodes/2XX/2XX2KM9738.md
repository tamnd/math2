## Definition

Let a ray of [[electromagnetic radiation|AC6R5A3M1C]] of frequency $\nu$ cross a medium along a path parametrised by arc length $s$.
Let $\alpha_\nu(s) \ge 0$ be the absorption coefficient of the medium at $s$, the fraction of intensity removed per unit length; it may include scattering.
The *optical depth* of the segment of the path from $s_0$ to $s_1$ is
$$\tau_\nu = \int_{s_0}^{s_1} \alpha_\nu(s) \, ds.$$

## Remarks

Example: in a homogeneous slab of thickness $L$ with constant $\alpha_\nu$, $\tau_\nu = \alpha_\nu L$, and with no emission the intensity obeys $dI_\nu/ds = -\alpha_\nu I_\nu$, so $I_\nu(L) = I_\nu(0) \, e^{-\tau_\nu}$.
Non-example: the geometric thickness $L$ itself is not an optical depth; two slabs of equal thickness and different $\alpha_\nu$ have different optical depths.
Often $\alpha_\nu = \kappa_\nu \rho$, with $\kappa_\nu$ the opacity per unit mass and $\rho$ the density.
In a plane-parallel atmosphere $\tau_\nu$ is measured vertically inward from the top, $d\tau_\nu = -\kappa_\nu \rho \, dz$, where $z$ is height.
With emission, the formal solution of the [[radiative transfer equation|EX9YYRMAJA]] along the ray is $I_\nu(\tau) = I_\nu(0) e^{-\tau} + \int_0^\tau S_\nu(t) \, e^{-(\tau - t)} \, dt$, where $S_\nu$ is the source function and $\tau$ is measured along the ray.
