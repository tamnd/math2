## Statement

Over a finite field, "infinitely many solutions" becomes an exact count: each free variable multiplies the number of solutions by the size of the field.
Let $F$ be a [[finite field|0ZXZ82FTX2]] with $q$ elements, let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over $F$, and let $b \in F^m$.
Let $r = \operatorname{rank} A$.
Then the [[system|0GT4WWGWH2]] $Ax = b$ has either no solution or exactly $q^{\,n - r}$ solutions.
The homogeneous system $Ax = 0$ always has exactly $q^{\,n - r}$ solutions.

## Remarks

### Counting by hand

Take $x + y + z = 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_3$.
The matrix $A = (1, 1, 1)$ has rank $r = 1$, and $n = 3$.
The system is consistent, since $(1, 0, 0)$ is a solution, so it has $3^{3 - 1} = 9$ solutions.
Directly: choose $y$ and $z$ in $3 \cdot 3 = 9$ ways, and put $x = 1 - y - z$.
For $y = z = 2$ this gives $x = 1 - 4 = -3 = 0$, and indeed $0 + 2 + 2 = 4 = 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_3$.

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The field must be finite, so that counting makes sense, and over $\mathbb{R}$ the same kind of system has infinitely many solutions when $r < n$.
The coefficients must lie in a field: over the ring $\mathbb{Z}/4$, the equation $2x = 0$ has the $2$ solutions $0$ and $2$, and $2$ is not a power of $4$.

### What follows

The number of solutions of a consistent system over $\mathbb{F}_2$ is a power of $2$.
Exactly $q^r$ of the $q^m$ right sides $b$ give a consistent system, because the column space has dimension $r$.
When $r = m$, every $b$ gives exactly $q^{n - m}$ solutions.

### Which counts occur

For $m \times n$ matrices, every power $q^d$ with $n - \min(m, n) \le d \le n$ occurs as a count.
Take for $A$ the first $n - d$ rows of the identity matrix $I_n$, followed by zero rows, and $b = 0$.

### Beyond matrices

For a linear map $T \colon V \to W$ between finite-dimensional spaces over $\mathbb{F}_q$, every nonempty fiber $T^{-1}(w)$ has $q^{\dim \ker T}$ elements.

### Where the count is used

A binary linear code is the null space of a check matrix $H$ of rank $r$, so it has $2^{n - r}$ codewords.
Linear algebra over $\mathbb{F}_2$ also solves puzzles such as Lights Out, as in [[the Lights Out example|JD8FEPHM6T]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Over a finite field there are only finitely many possible solutions.
Each free variable can take $q$ values, and the other unknowns are then fixed.
So with $k$ free variables there are $q^k$ solutions, if there is any solution at all.

### L2 human

The typical use is to count solutions in coding theory, cryptography and puzzles.
Find the rank by row reduction over $F$, check consistency, and raise $q$ to the number of free variables.
Watch out: the integers modulo $n$ form a field only for $n$ prime, and for other $n$ the count can fail.

### L3 human

The solution set is a coset of the kernel, and the kernel is an $\mathbb{F}_q$-vector space of dimension $n - r$, so it is in bijection with $\mathbb{F}_q^{\,n - r}$.
The count $q^{n-r}$ then follows from $|\mathbb{F}_q^{\,d}| = q^d$.
