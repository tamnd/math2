## Summary

A power-associative algebra is a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] in which the subalgebra generated by any single element is associative.
In such an algebra the powers $x^n$ of an element are well defined, whatever the bracketing, and $x^m x^n = x^{m+n}$.
[[Alternative algebras|N8K9SSVMRN]], [[Jordan algebras|6C2MCVB8C7]] and the [[sedenions|YADT1TBZ5A]] are power-associative.
It is the natural setting in which one can speak of polynomials in one element, idempotents and nilpotent elements.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In ordinary algebra, $x^4$ means $x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x$ and the brackets do not matter.
In a nonassociative algebra, $(xx)(xx)$ and $x(x(xx))$ could differ.
A power-associative algebra is one where they never do, so powers of a single element behave as usual.
The condition only concerns one element at a time; products of different elements may still depend on brackets.
