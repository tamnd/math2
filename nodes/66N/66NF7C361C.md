## Definition

Let $E$ be a finite set and let $\mathcal{F}$ be a family of subsets of $E$, as in a [[combinatorial optimization|FJPER80BJC]] problem.
For $F \subseteq E$ the *incidence vector* $\chi^F \in \R^E$ has $\chi^F_e = 1$ for $e \in F$ and $\chi^F_e = 0$ for $e \notin F$.
The *polytope of $\mathcal{F}$* is the [[convex hull|0FQSDC2EQ2]]
$$P_{\mathcal{F}} = \operatorname{conv} \set{\chi^F : F \in \mathcal{F}} \subseteq \R^E,$$
a [[convex polytope|300K6CB1KH]] whose vertices are exactly the vectors $\chi^F$.
*Polyhedral combinatorics* is the study of such polytopes.
It covers their descriptions by linear inequalities, their facets and other faces, and their use in [[linear programming|VNMXB0J2KA]] methods for the problem.

## Remarks

If $\mathcal{F}$ is nonempty, then for every weight $w \in \R^E$ the maximum of $w(F)$ over $F \in \mathcal{F}$ equals the maximum of $w^T x$ over $P_{\mathcal{F}}$.
The reason is that a linear function on a polytope attains its maximum at a vertex.
So an explicit system of inequalities for $P_{\mathcal{F}}$ turns the problem into a linear program.

Example: let $E$ be the edge set of the complete bipartite graph $K_{n,n}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ the family of perfect matchings.
Then $P_{\mathcal{F}}$, identified with a set of $n \times n$ matrices, is the [[Birkhoff polytope|ADEXY830S8]].
By the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem it is described by $x_{ij} \ge 0$ and all row and column sums equal to $1$.

Non-example: for a graph that is not bipartite, the degree constraints $x \ge 0$ and $\sum_{e \ni v} x_e = 1$ for each vertex $v$ do not describe the perfect matching polytope.
The triangle has no perfect matching, so its polytope is empty, yet $x = (1/2, 1/2, 1/2)$ satisfies the degree constraints.
[[Edmonds' matching polytope theorem|K7JT5SBZFX]] adds the odd set inequalities, which here read $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 \le 1$ and exclude this point.
