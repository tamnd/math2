## Summary

An invertible module over a commutative ring $R$ is a module $M$ that has an inverse for the [[tensor product|F0HQXQ0HCD]]: some module $N$ with $M \otimes_R N \cong R$.
The invertible modules are exactly the [[finitely generated projective modules|HQA9YHDSH5]] of constant [[rank|1WTNGZ5T5A]] $1$.
Their isomorphism classes form the [[Picard group|3Z9DCWYXQZ]] of $R$.
They are the algebraic form of line bundles, and of [[invertible sheaves|7JCF2TE3V5]] on the affine scheme $\mathrm{Spec}\, R$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The ring $R$ itself is the simplest module of rank one.
An invertible module is a module of rank one that may be twisted, like a Möbius band compared with a cylinder.
It is called invertible because tensoring with a suitable partner undoes the twist and gives back $R$.
Over a Dedekind domain, the nonzero ideals are invertible modules, and a non-principal ideal is a twisted one.
