## Summary

Physical geodesy is the part of [[geodesy|WCXMP72NSC]] that studies the gravity field of the Earth.
Its main objects are the [[geopotential|5Y2BWNY6MF]], the [[geoid|999BYZY0ZG]], normal gravity and gravity anomalies.
Its mathematics is potential theory: harmonic functions, spherical harmonic expansions and boundary value problems for [[Laplace's equation|S8RG6N1PRT]].
It provides heights above sea level and the reference surfaces that link geometric positions to the direction of gravity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gravity is not the same everywhere on the Earth: it is stronger at the poles and changes near mountains and dense rocks.
Physical geodesy measures and models these changes.
One of its tasks is to find the shape of the sea surface if the oceans were at rest and extended under the continents.
That surface, the geoid, is the zero level for the heights printed on maps.
