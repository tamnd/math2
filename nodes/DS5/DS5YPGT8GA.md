## Definition

*Product fuzzy logic* $\Pi$ is the Hilbert calculus in the language of [[BL|FQZ04D0HH3]], with modus ponens as the only rule.
Its axioms are those of BL together with the cancellation axiom shown below.

$$\neg \varphi \lor ((\varphi \to \varphi \otimes \psi) \to \psi).$$

A *product algebra* is a BL-algebra in which the cancellation axiom is valid.

The *standard product algebra* $[0, 1]_\Pi$ is the standard BL-algebra of the product [[t-norm|BHGCVPAJW0]] $x \cdot y$.
Its operations are $\min$, $\max$, $x \cdot y$, the residuum, $0$ and $1$, where the residuum is given by [[the residua statement|7R2JBNNMT5]].

$$x \Rightarrow_\Pi y = \begin{cases} 1 & x \le y, \\ y / x & x > y. \end{cases}$$

The negation is $\neg x = x \Rightarrow_\Pi 0$, equal to $1$ if $x = 0$ and to $0$ if $x > 0$.

## Notation

SEP writes the cancellation axiom with $\&$ for $\otimes$.
The letter $\Pi$ names both the logic and the t-norm, and $[0, 1]_\Pi$ is the standard algebra.

## Remarks

The calculus axiomatizes the logic of the product t-norm.
That is, its theorems are exactly the formulas valid in $[0, 1]_\Pi$, a standard completeness theorem.

In $[0, 1]_\Pi$ the axiom has value $1$.
For $x = 0$ the disjunct $\neg x$ is $1$, and for $x > 0$ the second disjunct is $1$; see [[the cancellation example|B6G1P9DBF9]].
The weak connectives are $x \land y = \min(x, y)$ and $x \lor y = \max(x, y)$, by divisibility and the BL definition of $\lor$.

The name comes from the standard algebra, where a positive factor can be cancelled: for $x > 0$, the inequality $x \cdot y \le x \cdot z$ forces $y \le z$.
In residuum form, $x \Rightarrow_\Pi (x \cdot y) = y$ for every $x > 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Product logic is BL with one extra axiom.
The axiom says that, unless $\varphi$ is fully false, conjunction with $\varphi$ loses no information about $\psi$.

### L2 human

To evaluate a formula, read $\otimes$ as multiplication and $\to$ as the residuum above.
Split into cases on whether the antecedent is at most the consequent.
For example, $0.5 \Rightarrow_\Pi 0.35 = 0.35 / 0.5 = 0.7$.
Watch out: $\neg$ is not $1 - x$ here, since it sends every positive value to $0$.

### L3 human

Product algebras are a subvariety of BL-algebras, defined by one extra identity.
Standard completeness says that this subvariety is generated by the single algebra $[0, 1]_\Pi$.
