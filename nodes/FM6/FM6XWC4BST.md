## Summary

Fermi–Dirac statistics describes how identical fermions, such as electrons, fill the single-particle states of a system in thermal equilibrium.
Because of the Pauli exclusion principle, each state holds at most one fermion, and the mean occupation of a state of energy $\varepsilon$ is $1/(e^{(\varepsilon - \mu)/k_B T} + 1)$.
It explains the behaviour of electrons in metals, the stability of white dwarfs, and the small electronic heat capacity of solids.
At high temperature or low density it reduces to [[Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics|B60C1S87XB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a stack of shelves, one per state, where each shelf can hold at most one ball.
At zero temperature the balls fill the lowest shelves up to a sharp top level, the Fermi energy.
Heat blurs this edge: a few balls near the top jump to empty shelves just above.
A shelf exactly at the chemical potential $\mu$ is occupied with probability $1/2$.
Far below $\mu$ shelves are almost surely full, and far above they are almost surely empty.
