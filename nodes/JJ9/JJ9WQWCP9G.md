## Summary

The center of a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] $\mathfrak g$ is the set of elements whose bracket with every element is zero.
It is an ideal of $\mathfrak g$, and it is the kernel of the adjoint representation.
It is the analogue for Lie algebras of the [[centre of a group|0DZ93493VN]] and of the [[center of a ring|MVT64B3B6H]].
A Lie algebra equals its center exactly when it is abelian, and semisimple Lie algebras have zero center.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The center collects the elements that commute with everything.
Here commuting means that the bracket is zero.
In the Lie algebra of all $n \times n$ matrices with $[A, B] = AB - BA$, the center consists of the multiples of the identity matrix.
Those are the only matrices that commute with every other matrix.
