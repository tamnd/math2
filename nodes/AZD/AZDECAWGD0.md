## Summary

The Schur multiplier of a group $G$ is its second integral homology group $H_2(G, \Z)$.
Schur introduced it to study projective representations of finite groups, which are representations up to scalars.
For a perfect group it controls the central extensions: it is the kernel of the universal central extension.
In algebraic K-theory, $K_2$ of a ring is the Schur multiplier of its elementary group.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some groups act on vector spaces only up to scalar factors, and the Schur multiplier measures how far such actions are from honest ones.
It also measures the ways to enlarge a group by a central part that cannot be split off.
For a cyclic group the multiplier is trivial.
For the Klein four-group it has two elements, and for the alternating group $A_5$ it also has two elements.
