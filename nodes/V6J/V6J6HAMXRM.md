## Summary

A classical field theory describes physical quantities that take a value at every point of space and time.
Examples are the electromagnetic field, the gravitational field and the displacement of an elastic body.
The fields obey [[partial differential equations|GC5BDZ6QBC]], the field equations, usually obtained from a [[Lagrangian density|SANYJGZ311]] by a variational principle.
It is classical in the sense that it is not quantized.
Its mathematical tools include the calculus of variations, [[fiber bundles|CX4M8K61AB]], jet bundles and the theory of hyperbolic equations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Mechanics of particles follows finitely many numbers, such as positions, in time.
A field instead assigns a value to every point, like the temperature in a room or the height of a water surface.
A field theory gives the rule for how these values change, which links the value at a point to the values nearby.
A vibrating string is the simplest example: its displacement $\varphi(t, x)$ obeys the [[wave equation|5EHD2XBQDG]] $\varphi_{tt} = c^2 \varphi_{xx}$.
Maxwell's theory of electromagnetism and Einstein's general relativity are classical field theories too.
