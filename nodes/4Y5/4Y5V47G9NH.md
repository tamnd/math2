## Summary

The replica trick computes the average of the logarithm of a random partition function from the averages of its integer powers.
It rests on the identity $\log Z = \lim_{n \to 0} (Z^n - 1)/n$.
For an integer $n$, the power $Z^n$ is the partition function of $n$ independent copies, or replicas, of the system with the same disorder.
It is the main heuristic tool behind the theory of [[spin glasses|EHPA4B5S5W]].
Some of its predictions, such as the [[Parisi formula|32S4Y36QNK]], were later proved by other methods.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Averaging a logarithm over random disorder is hard, but averaging a product is often easy.
The trick replaces $\log Z$ by $(Z^n - 1)/n$ for a small $n$, which is almost the same number.
When $n$ is a whole number, $Z^n$ is the partition function of $n$ copies of the system, and its average can be computed.
Then one pretends that the answer makes sense for any real $n$ and lets $n$ go to $0$.
For example, if $Z = 2$, then $(2^n - 1)/n$ equals $1$, $0.828$ and $0.718$ for $n = 1$, $1/2$ and $1/10$, and tends to $\log 2 \approx 0.693$.
