## Summary

A variable-mass system is a body whose mass changes in time because it ejects or collects matter.
Newton's second law in the form force equals mass times acceleration does not apply to it directly.
One applies conservation of momentum to the body together with the matter it exchanges, which gives an extra thrust term.
Rockets, jets and growing raindrops are the standard examples.
See the [[definition|T4MVWEPAY8]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rocket gets lighter as it burns fuel and throws the gas out of the back.
The gas pushes the rocket forward, much as a skater is pushed back after throwing a heavy ball.
To describe such motion one must keep track of the mass that leaves and the speed at which it leaves.
A raindrop falling through a cloud is the reverse case: it gains mass, and picking up still droplets holds back its fall.
