## Summary

Shear wave splitting happens when a shear wave crosses an anisotropic medium.
The wave separates into two shear waves with perpendicular polarizations and different speeds.
It is the clearest sign of [[seismic anisotropy|XXB2TQX6KW]], much as double refraction is a sign of optical anisotropy in a crystal.
It is described by two numbers: the polarization of the fast wave and the delay time between the two waves.
Splitting of core-crossing phases such as SKS is a main tool for mapping deformation in the upper mantle.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Send a sideways shake into a rock that is stiffer in one direction than another.
The shake breaks into two shakes, one along the stiff direction and one across it, and the first runs ahead.
At the far side the two arrive a little apart in time, often about a second for waves crossing the upper mantle.
The direction of the fast shake tells the alignment of the rock, and the delay tells how strong and how thick the anisotropic layer is.
