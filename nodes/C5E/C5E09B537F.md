## Definition

Let $\R^4$ be Minkowski space with the form $x \cdot y = x^0 y^0 - \sum_{k=1}^3 x^k y^k$, and let $\mathcal{P}$ be the identity component of the [[Poincaré group|8ZQ9TA4Q2Q]], acting by $x \mapsto \Lambda x + a$.
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a separable complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]], and for each Schwartz function $f \in \mathcal{S}(\R^4)$ (see [[Schwartz space|CG9JEATW3Q]]) let $\phi(f)$ be a linear operator on $\mathcal{H}$.
The *Wightman axioms* for a real scalar field $\phi$ are the following.

1. Relativistic quantum mechanics: there is a strongly continuous [[unitary representation|74BPYJNZTM]] $U$ of $\mathcal{P}$ on $\mathcal{H}$.
   The joint spectrum of the generators $P^0, \dots, P^3$ of the translations $U(a, I) = e^{i P \cdot a}$ lies in the cone $\set{p : p^0 \ge \abs{\mathbf{p}}}$.
   There is a unit vector $\Omega$, unique up to a phase, with $U(a, \Lambda)\Omega = \Omega$ for all $(a, \Lambda) \in \mathcal{P}$.
2. Domain and continuity: there is a dense subspace $D \subseteq \mathcal{H}$ with $\Omega \in D$ and $U(a, \Lambda) D \subseteq D$.
   For every $f$, $D$ lies in the domain of the [[unbounded operator|KRJX5RA0PW]] $\phi(f)$, and $\phi(f) D \subseteq D$.
   For all $\chi, \psi \in D$ the map $f \mapsto \ip{\chi}{\phi(f)\psi}$ is a [[tempered distribution|AES2T5JPFP]].
   Moreover $\ip{\chi}{\phi(f)\psi} = \ip{\phi(\bar f)\chi}{\psi}$ for $\chi, \psi \in D$.
3. Covariance: $U(a, \Lambda)\, \phi(f)\, U(a, \Lambda)^{-1} = \phi(f_{(a, \Lambda)})$ on $D$, where $f_{(a, \Lambda)}(x) = f(\Lambda^{-1}(x - a))$.
4. Locality: if the supports of $f$ and $g$ are spacelike separated, that is, $(x - y) \cdot (x - y) < 0$ for all $x \in \operatorname{supp} f$ and $y \in \operatorname{supp} g$, then $\phi(f)\phi(g)\psi = \phi(g)\phi(f)\psi$ for all $\psi \in D$.
5. Cyclicity of the vacuum: the finite linear combinations of vectors $\phi(f_1) \cdots \phi(f_n)\Omega$, $n \ge 0$, are dense in $\mathcal{H}$.

## Remarks

For real-valued $f$, axiom 2 makes $\phi(f)$ a symmetric operator; formally $\phi(f) = \int \phi(x) f(x)\, dx$ for a field $\phi(x)$ that is not itself an operator.
The Wightman functions $\mathcal{W}_n(f_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_n) = \ip{\Omega}{\phi(f_1) \cdots \phi(f_n)\Omega}$ extend to tempered distributions on $\R^{4n}$, and the reconstruction theorem recovers $(\mathcal{H}, U, \Omega, \phi)$ from them up to unitary equivalence.

Example: the free scalar field of mass $m > 0$ satisfies all five axioms.
Its Hilbert space is the bosonic Fock space over $L^2$ of the hyperboloid $\set{p : p \cdot p = m^2,\ p^0 > 0}$ with the invariant measure.
The field is $\phi(f) = a^*(Ef) + a(E\bar f)$, up to normalization, with the creation and annihilation operators $a^*, a$.
Here $(Ef)(p) = \int_{\R^4} f(x)\, e^{i p \cdot x}\, dx$ for $p$ on the hyperboloid.

Non-example: the one-particle space $L^2$ of the same hyperboloid, with its irreducible representation of $\mathcal{P}$, satisfies the spectral condition but contains no nonzero invariant vector, so axiom 1 fails there.

Fields of higher spin are treated with several components that transform under a finite-dimensional representation of $SL(2, \C)$, and then locality allows anticommutation for fermionic fields.
