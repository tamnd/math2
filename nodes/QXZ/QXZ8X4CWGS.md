## Definition

The *theory of computation* is the branch of mathematics and theoretical computer science that studies the following questions about a [[model of computation|YFHEG4GYHW]].

1. Which [[decision problems|YAVW1XHNFH]] and functions can be solved by a machine of the model at all; this is [[computability theory|X14Y880PN5]].
2. How many resources, such as time and memory, a solution needs; this is [[computational complexity theory|2FXXY2HSS7]].
3. What restricted machines, such as finite automata, can recognise; this is [[automata theory|2ZSYVRGQ05]].

## Remarks

Example: the result that no [[Turing machine|758EYCN27B]] solves the [[halting problem|Z7TN2B35B0]] belongs to the theory of computation, in its first part.
Non-example: the design of a faster processor chip is not part of the theory, because it concerns physical hardware and not what an abstract model can compute.
The answers to the first question do not depend on the choice among the usual models, since these are all equivalent in power.
The answers to the second question depend on the model, but only up to polynomial factors among the usual deterministic models.
