## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, with integer entries.
We view $A$ over three fields.

1. $p_A(t) = \det(tI - A) = t^2 + 1$, by the [[definition of the characteristic polynomial|039HF58KQ5]]. Second method: $\tr A = 0$ and $\det A = 1$.
2. Over $\R$: $t^2 + 1 > 0$ for every real $t$. So the [[spectrum|04EHB7YDQS]] of $A$ over $\R$ is empty.
3. Over $\C$: $t^2 + 1 = (t - i)(t + i)$, so the spectrum is $\{i, -i\}$.
4. Check for $i$ with $v = (1, -i)$: $Av = (i, 1)$ and $iv = (i, -i^2) = (i, 1)$.
5. Check for $-i$ with $w = (1, i)$: $Aw = (-i, 1)$ and $-iw = (-i, -i^2) = (-i, 1)$.
6. Over $\mathbb{F}_5$: $2^2 + 1 = 5 = 0$ and $3^2 + 1 = 10 = 0$. So $t^2 + 1 = (t - 2)(t - 3)$, and the spectrum is $\{2, 3\}$.
7. Check for $2$ with $u = (2, 1)$: $Au = (-1, 2) = (4, 2)$ in $\mathbb{F}_5$, and $2u = (4, 2)$.
8. Check for $3$ with $x = (3, 1)$: $Ax = (-1, 3) = (4, 3)$ in $\mathbb{F}_5$, and $3x = (9, 3) = (4, 3)$.

## Remarks

- The [[eigenvalues|0ZND7NQ970]] of a matrix are the roots of $p_A$ in the field of scalars. The polynomial is the same, but its roots depend on the field.
- Over $\mathbb{F}_3$, $t^2 + 1$ has no root, because $0^2 + 1 = 1$, $1^2 + 1 = 2$ and $2^2 + 1 = 2$. So the spectrum over $\mathbb{F}_3$ is empty.
- Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, $t^2 + 1 = (t + 1)^2$, and $1$ is the only eigenvalue.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The same matrix can have no eigenvalues, or two of them, depending on which numbers are allowed.
Over the real numbers it is a quarter turn, with no fixed direction.
Over the complex numbers it has the eigenvalues $i$ and $-i$.

### L2 human

The typical use is to explain why the field must be stated with the spectrum.
Over an algebraically closed field the spectrum is never empty.
A trap is to compute in $\mathbb{F}_5$ and forget to reduce modulo $5$ at the end.
