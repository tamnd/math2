## Proof

We first show that the leading blocks of $LU$ are the products of the leading blocks of $L$ and $U$, which gives part 2, then build the factorization by induction on $n$ with a block formula, and finally combine the two for part 3.

**Part 2.**

1. Let $A = LU$ with $L$ unit lower triangular and $U$ upper triangular. Fix $k$, and write $L_k$ and $U_k$ for the upper left $k \times k$ blocks.
2. For $i, j \le k$, entry $(i, j)$ of $LU$ is $\sum_l \ell_{il} u_{lj}$. The terms with $l > i$ vanish because $L$ is [[lower triangular|00J3KZP8Y0]], so only $l \le i \le k$ occur. So $A_k = L_k U_k$.
3. By the [[multiplicativity of the determinant|04CPJ6AF00]] and the [[determinant of a triangular matrix|0K1KKMFHYH]], $\Delta_k = \det L_k \det U_k = 1 \cdot u_{11} \cdots u_{kk}$.

**Part 1**, by induction on $n$.

4. Base case $n = 1$: $A = (a) = (1)(a)$, with $L = (1)$ and $U = (a)$.
5. Let $n \ge 2$, and assume part 1 for size $n - 1$. Write $A$ as a [[block matrix|0ASDFEQS6P]] $A = \begin{pmatrix} A' & b \\ c^{\mathsf{T}} & d \end{pmatrix}$ with $A' = A_{n-1}$, column vectors $b, c \in F^{n-1}$ and $d \in F$.
6. The leading minors of $A'$ are $\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{n-1}$, and $\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{n-2}$ are nonzero. By the induction hypothesis, $A' = L'U'$ with $L'$ unit lower triangular and $U'$ upper triangular.
7. By part 2 for $A'$, $u'_{11} \cdots u'_{n-1,n-1} = \Delta_{n-1} \ne 0$. So each diagonal entry of $U'$ is nonzero, and $U'$ is invertible by the theorem on [[triangular matrices|7X1B65CTG4]]. The matrix $L'$ has diagonal entries $1$, so it is invertible by the same theorem.
8. Set $L = \begin{pmatrix} L' & 0 \\ c^{\mathsf{T}} U'^{-1} & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $U = \begin{pmatrix} U' & L'^{-1} b \\ 0 & s \end{pmatrix}$ with $s = d - c^{\mathsf{T}} U'^{-1} L'^{-1} b$.
9. $L$ is unit lower triangular, and $U$ is [[upper triangular|0N4XY7Z3NN]], by their block forms.
10. By block multiplication, $LU = \begin{pmatrix} L'U' & L'L'^{-1} b \\ c^{\mathsf{T}} U'^{-1} U' & c^{\mathsf{T}} U'^{-1} L'^{-1} b + s \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} A' & b \\ c^{\mathsf{T}} & d \end{pmatrix} = A$. This completes the induction.

**Part 3.**

11. "If": let $\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_n$ be nonzero. Part 1 gives $A = LU$. By part 2, $u_{11} \cdots u_{nn} = \Delta_n \ne 0$, so each $u_{kk}$ is nonzero, and $U$ is invertible by the theorem on triangular matrices.
12. "Only if": let $A = LU$ be invertible. Then $U = L^{-1} A$ is invertible, as a product of invertible matrices. Here $L$ is invertible by the theorem on triangular matrices, because its diagonal entries are $1$. By the theorem on triangular matrices, each $u_{kk}$ is nonzero.
13. By part 2, $\Delta_k = u_{11} \cdots u_{kk} \ne 0$ for each $k$.
14. By part 2 for $k$ and for $k - 1$, $\Delta_k = \Delta_{k-1} u_{kk}$, and $\Delta_{k-1} \ne 0$. So $u_{kk} = \Delta_k / \Delta_{k-1}$.

## Idea

The upper left corner of a product of a lower and an upper triangular matrix only sees the upper left corners of the factors.
So the leading blocks multiply on their own, and taking determinants gives $\Delta_k = u_{11} \cdots u_{kk}$.
For existence, factor the leading block of size $n - 1$ by induction.
Its factors are invertible because $\Delta_{n-1} \ne 0$, and this is exactly what lets us solve for the last row of $L$ and the last column of $U$.
The last minor $\Delta_n$ is never used in part 1, because the corner entry $s$ of $U$ may be zero.

## Variants

The same result follows from elimination.
At step $k$ the pivot is $\Delta_k/\Delta_{k-1}$, because the row replacements before step $k$ add multiples of higher rows to lower rows and so do not change the leading minors.
The last entry $s$ is the [[Schur complement|6HWFBXYPZP]] $d - c^{\mathsf{T}} A'^{-1} b$, because $A'^{-1} = U'^{-1} L'^{-1}$.
