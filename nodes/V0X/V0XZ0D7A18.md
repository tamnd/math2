## Proof

We write $A_i$ as a product $A X_i$, where $X_i$ has determinant $x_i$.

1. Let $A$ be $n \times n$ over the field $F$ with $\det A \ne 0$, and let $b \in F^n$.
2. By the [[invertibility criterion|FBSAPTP1PR]], $A$ is invertible.
3. Existence: $x = A^{-1} b$ satisfies $Ax = (A A^{-1}) b = b$, by [[associativity|073E4XC016]].
4. Uniqueness: if $Ay = b$, then $y = A^{-1}(Ay) = A^{-1} b = x$.
5. Now let $x$ be any solution of $Ax = b$, and fix $i$.
6. Let $X_i$ be the [[identity matrix|06DW9BAWB1]] $I_n$ with column $i$ replaced by $x$.
7. For $j \ne i$, column $j$ of $A X_i$ is $A e_j$, which is column $j$ of $A$, by the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]].
8. Column $i$ of $A X_i$ is $Ax = b$.
9. By steps 7 and 8, $A X_i = A_i$.
10. Row $i$ of $X_i$ has the single nonzero entry $x_i$, in position $(i, i)$.
11. Deleting row $i$ and column $i$ of $X_i$ gives $I_{n-1}$, with determinant $1$.
12. By the [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]] along row $i$ and steps 10 and 11, $\det X_i = x_i$.
13. By [[multiplicativity|04CPJ6AF00]] and step 9, $\det A_i = \det A \cdot \det X_i = x_i \det A$.
14. Since $\det A \ne 0$, $x_i = \det A_i / \det A$.

## Idea

Replacing column $i$ of the identity by the solution $x$ gives a matrix with determinant $x_i$.
Multiplying it by $A$ replaces column $i$ of $A$ by $b$, and the determinant is multiplicative.

## Variants

Steps 5 to 13 use no division, so they hold over every commutative ring: each solution satisfies $x_i \det A = \det A_i$.
Another version expands $b = \sum_j x_j a_j$ in column $i$ of $A_i$; by multilinearity in columns, all terms with $j \ne i$ have two equal columns and vanish.
