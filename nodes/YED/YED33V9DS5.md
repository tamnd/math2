## Summary

The Richtmyer–Meshkov instability is the growth of perturbations on the interface between two fluids of different density.
It follows a sudden impulse on the interface, typically from a passing [[shock wave|FRVV0NHBXT]].
It can be seen as the impulsive limit of the [[Rayleigh–Taylor instability|3ETBXHDWBM]], where the acceleration acts only for a very short time.
In linear theory the perturbation amplitude grows linearly in time, not exponentially.
It is important in inertial confinement fusion, in supersonic combustion and in supernova remnants.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hit a wavy boundary between a light gas and a heavy gas with a shock wave.
The shock gives the two sides different kicks along the wavy boundary, and the boundary starts to deform.
The crests and troughs keep moving apart at a steady rate, and later they form mushroom-shaped spikes and bubbles.
Unlike the Rayleigh–Taylor case, this happens whichever side the shock comes from.
