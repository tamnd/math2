## Summary

Seismic anisotropy is the dependence of seismic wave speeds on the direction of propagation and on the direction of polarization.
It is the seismological side of [[anisotropic elasticity|NMDDYDXT0V]]: the speeds come from the [[Christoffel equation|EK3A1GMMFM]] for the elastic stiffness tensor of the rock.
It arises from aligned crystals, such as olivine in the upper mantle, from fine layering, and from aligned cracks.
Measuring it reveals the direction of past and present deformation and flow inside the Earth.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Wood splits more easily along the grain than across it; many rocks are also different in different directions.
In such a rock a seismic wave runs faster along some directions than along others.
In the upper mantle, flowing rock lines up its olivine crystals, and waves travelling along the flow are faster.
So by measuring how speed changes with direction, seismologists can map the flow of the mantle.
