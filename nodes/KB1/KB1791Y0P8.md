## Definition

A *formal grammar* is a tuple $G = (N, \Sigma, P, S)$ where:

1. $N$ is a finite set of *nonterminal symbols*.
2. $\Sigma$ is a finite [[alphabet|RABRP2KG81]] of *terminal symbols*, disjoint from $N$.
3. $P$ is a finite set of *production rules* $\alpha \to \beta$, where $\alpha, \beta$ are [[strings|1P6EP01HZF]] in $(N \cup \Sigma)^*$ and $\alpha$ contains at least one nonterminal.
4. $S \in N$ is the *start symbol*.

Here $(N \cup \Sigma)^*$ is the [[Kleene star|ZW1M7XG8V4]] of $N \cup \Sigma$.
For strings $x, y$ over $N \cup \Sigma$, one writes $x \Rightarrow y$ if $x = u \alpha v$ and $y = u \beta v$ for some rule $\alpha \to \beta$ in $P$ and some strings $u, v$.
Let $\Rightarrow^*$ be the reflexive and transitive closure of $\Rightarrow$; a chain $x \Rightarrow \cdots \Rightarrow y$ is a *derivation*.
The *language generated by* $G$ is the [[formal language|5MFZ4VNY6D]]
$$L(G) = \set{w \in \Sigma^* : S \Rightarrow^* w}.$$

## Remarks

Example: $N = \set{S}$, $\Sigma = \set{a, b}$, $P = \set{S \to aSb, \ S \to \varepsilon}$.
Each derivation step either adds one $a$ and one $b$ around $S$ or removes $S$, so $L(G) = \set{a^n b^n : n \ge 0}$.
Non-example: a "rule" $ab \to ba$ over $\Sigma = \set{a, b}$ is not allowed, because its left side has no nonterminal.
Two grammars are *equivalent* if they generate the same language.
Often $N$ is called $V$ and $P$ is called $R$.
