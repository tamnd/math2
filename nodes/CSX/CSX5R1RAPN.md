## Summary

An anyon is a particle, or quasiparticle, in a two-dimensional system whose exchange statistics is neither bosonic nor fermionic.
Exchanging two identical anyons can multiply the state by any phase $e^{i\theta}$, or even act by a non-commuting matrix.
This is possible because in the plane the paths that swap particles form the [[braid group|BNR4H5GCTP]], rather than the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]].
Anyons appear as excitations in the fractional [[quantum Hall effect|11AZ4RGXVN]], and non-abelian anyons are a proposed basis for topological quantum computation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In three dimensions, swapping two identical particles twice is the same as doing nothing, so a swap can only give a sign $+1$ (bosons) or $-1$ (fermions).
In a flat two-dimensional world, swapping twice means one particle walks a full loop around the other, and this loop cannot be shrunk away without passing through the other particle.
So a swap can give any phase, and the particle "remembers" how the others wound around it.
For example, quasiparticles of the quantum Hall state at filling fraction $1/3$ are predicted to pick up the phase $e^{i\pi/3}$ when exchanged.
