## Summary

Which axioms does the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem need?
Reverse mathematics, a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]], answers such questions by proving theorems from axioms and then proving the axioms back from the theorems.
The setting is second-order arithmetic, where natural numbers and sets of natural numbers code reals, continuous functions and countable algebra.
The story starts with the weak base system $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ of computable mathematics, which proves the intermediate value theorem but not weak König's lemma.
Above it stand four stronger systems, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$, and together they are the Big Five.
A large share of classical theorems turns out to be equivalent to one of the five, and Bolzano-Weierstrass is equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$.
Ramsey's theorem for pairs breaks this pattern, and it opens a zoo of principles between the systems.
The subject leads on to measure theory and randomness, to functionals of higher type, and to the philosophical questions of Hilbert's program and predicativity.

1. [[Second-order arithmetic|DC60FYCSPM]]: the language, the axioms, the models and the coding of mathematics.
2. [[Reverse mathematics|7SQS69T17R]]: how a reversal shows that a theorem needs an axiom.
3. [[RCA0|XQENB067R3]]: the base system of computable mathematics and its $\omega$-models.
4. [[WKL0|7W9EG02T79]]: weak König's lemma and the compactness arguments it supports.
5. [[ACA0|NAASTN80QN]]: arithmetical comprehension and sequential compactness.
6. [[ATR0|MKB74TX3Z8]]: transfinite iteration of the jump along well-orderings.
7. [[Π¹₁-CA0|T21WETAHN5]]: the strongest of the Big Five systems.
8. [[β-model|J0Z2GNJZ2W]]: the models that are correct about well-orderings.
9. [[Ramsey's theorem for pairs|ZT5KV6KJ3E]]: a theorem that fits none of the Big Five systems.
10. [[Reverse mathematics zoo|E3X5B175ZR]]: a map of the principles between the Big Five systems.
11. [[Weak weak König's lemma|BJQ77Y2QFX]]: the principle behind measure theory and randomness.
12. [[Higher-order reverse mathematics|NPDRXTBW5W]]: how the program extends to functionals of higher type.
13. [[Partial realizations of Hilbert's program|Z26S516BKH]]: what the conservation results mean for finitism.
14. [[Predicative mathematics|43BSXNWQBG]]: how much mathematics survives without impredicative definitions.
