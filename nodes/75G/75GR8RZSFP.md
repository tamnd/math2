## Summary

A Vickrey auction, or second-price sealed-bid auction, sells one object to the highest bidder at the price of the second-highest bid.
In it, bidding one's true value is a weakly dominant strategy.
So the auction is incentive compatible and gives the object to the bidder who values it most.
William Vickrey analysed it in 1961, and it is the simplest case of the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves mechanism.
It is a basic example of [[auction theory|86ZWXGHNGB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Everyone writes a bid in a sealed envelope.
The highest bid wins, but the winner pays only the second-highest bid.
With bids of $10$, $7$ and $4$, the first bidder wins and pays $7$.
Since your bid only decides whether you win and not what you pay, you can do no better than bidding exactly what the item is worth to you.
