## Proof

We solve the system with the inverse matrix, and then we recognize each coordinate of the solution as a Laplace expansion of $\det A_i$.

1. Let $A = (a_{kl})$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over the [[field|0QA8CHH8MG]] $F$ with $\det A \ne 0$, and let $b = (b_1, \dots, b_n) \in F^n$.
2. First let $n = 1$, so $A = (a_{11})$.
   By the [[Leibniz formula|016PE4VZ8Q]] with one term, $\det A = a_{11}$, so $a_{11} \ne 0$.
   The equation $a_{11} x_1 = b_1$ has the single solution $x_1 = b_1 / a_{11}$.
   Since $A_1 = (b_1)$ has determinant $b_1$, this is $x_1 = \det A_1 / \det A$.
   From now on let $n \ge 2$.
3. In a field the nonzero element $\det A$ has an inverse.
   So the theorem on the [[inverse via the adjugate|0ENVM493VK]] shows that $A$ is invertible, with
$$A^{-1} = (\det A)^{-1} \operatorname{adj}(A).$$
4. Existence: put $x = A^{-1} b$.
   By [[associativity|073E4XC016]], $Ax = (A A^{-1}) b = I_n b = b$, so $x$ solves the [[linear system|0JTRMBQ1SA]] $Ax = b$.
5. Uniqueness: if $Ay = b$, then associativity gives $y = (A^{-1} A) y = A^{-1} (A y) = A^{-1} b = x$.
6. Let $C_{kl}$ be the [[cofactors|0W2QAVJQ1W]] of $A$.
   The [[adjugate|0GMPYM0NSR]] has entry $C_{ji}$ in position $(i, j)$, so the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]] gives
$$x_i = (\det A)^{-1} \sum_{j=1}^{n} C_{ji}\, b_j.$$
7. Fix $i$.
   The matrix $A_i$ agrees with $A$ outside column $i$, and its entry in position $(j, i)$ is $b_j$.
8. The cofactor in position $(j, i)$ is built from the matrix without row $j$ and column $i$.
   That matrix is the same for $A_i$ and for $A$, since the two matrices differ only in column $i$.
   So the cofactor of $A_i$ in position $(j, i)$ is $C_{ji}$.
9. The [[Laplace expansion along column $i$|06AMCJMMVM]] of $A_i$, with steps 7 and 8, gives
$$\det A_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} b_j\, C_{ji}.$$
10. Steps 6 and 9 give $x_i = \det A_i / \det A$ for $1 \le i \le n$, which is Cramer's rule.

## Idea

The solution is $x = A^{-1} b$, and the adjugate writes $A^{-1}$ as the cofactor matrix, transposed and divided by $\det A$.
So coordinate $i$ of $x$ pairs the entries of $b$ with the cofactors of column $i$ of $A$.
Now look at $A_i$, where $b$ sits in column $i$.
Expanding $\det A_i$ along that column pairs the same entries with the same cofactors, because a cofactor of column $i$ never sees column $i$.
The two sums agree, and that is the whole proof.

## Variants

The [[proof by multilinearity|V0XZ0D7A18]] writes $A_i = A X_i$, where $X_i$ is the identity with column $i$ replaced by $x$, and uses multiplicativity.
It needs no adjugate, and it shows that $x_i \det A = \det A_i$ over every commutative ring.
A third route uses only multilinearity in the columns: write $b = \sum_j x_j a_j$ in column $i$ of $A_i$ and expand, so that every term except $x_i \det A$ has two equal columns.
