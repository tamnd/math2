## Summary

Identical particles are particles of the same species, such as two electrons, that no measurement can tell apart.
In quantum mechanics the state of $n$ identical particles is a vector in the $n$-fold [[tensor product|EY0X2NKA3S]] of the one-particle space.
This vector is either symmetric or antisymmetric under all permutations of the factors.
Particles with symmetric states are bosons, and particles with antisymmetric states are fermions.
This choice explains the [[Pauli exclusion principle|ZQGC326NP7]], the structure of the periodic table, and the difference between Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac statistics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two classical billiard balls can be painted different colours, so one can always follow which is which.
Two electrons have no such label, so swapping them must not change anything that can be observed.
Swapping them can still multiply the state by a sign: by $+1$ for bosons such as photons and by $-1$ for fermions such as electrons.
For example, if one particle sits in state $u$ and the other in state $v$, the two-boson state is $u \otimes v + v \otimes u$ and the two-fermion state is $u \otimes v - v \otimes u$, up to normalization.
All states of any number of identical particles together form the [[Fock space|1TJ8TNH331]].
