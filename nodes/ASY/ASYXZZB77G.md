## Summary

Tisserand's parameter is a combination of the semi-major axis, eccentricity and inclination of a small body.
It stays nearly unchanged during a close encounter with a planet.
It comes from the Jacobi integral of the restricted three-body problem.
Astronomers use it to recognise a comet after a planetary encounter and to sort small bodies into families.
See the [[definition|ENHQD5N9D8]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a comet passes close to Jupiter, its orbit can change completely.
Yet one number built from its orbit, Tisserand's parameter relative to Jupiter, stays almost the same.
So if two comets seen years apart have the same value, they may be one and the same comet.
Comets with a value between $2$ and $3$ usually belong to the family controlled by Jupiter.
