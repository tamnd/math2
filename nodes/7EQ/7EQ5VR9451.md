## Definition

Let $\mathcal{H}_A$ and $\mathcal{H}_B$ be complex [[Hilbert spaces|HM2HHE3J51]], and let $\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})$ denote the [[trace class operators|Z2CP530WDE]] on $\mathcal{H}$.
A *quantum channel* from $A$ to $B$ is a linear map $\Phi \colon \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_A) \to \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_B)$ such that:

1. $\Phi$ is [[completely positive|CGY299QMC3]];
2. $\Phi$ preserves the trace: $\tr \Phi(X) = \tr X$ for every $X \in \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_A)$.

Equivalently, a quantum channel is a trace-preserving [[quantum operation|F1K9J81GCT]].
It maps [[density operators|V1JTB52E8C]] to density operators.

## Remarks

Example: the depolarizing channel on $\C^2$ with $0 \le p \le 1$ is $\Phi(X) = (1 - p)X + p\, \tr(X)\, \tfrac12 I$.
It preserves the trace, since $\tr \Phi(X) = (1 - p)\tr X + p \tr X$.
It is completely positive, since $\tfrac12 \tr(X) I = \tfrac14 (X + \sigma_1 X \sigma_1 + \sigma_2 X \sigma_2 + \sigma_3 X \sigma_3)$ with the Pauli matrices $\sigma_i$, so $\Phi$ is a sum of maps $X \mapsto KXK^*$.
Non-example: $X \mapsto \tfrac12 X$ is completely positive but halves the trace, so clause 2 fails; it is a quantum operation but not a channel.
Another non-example is the transpose map, which preserves the trace but is not completely positive.
In the Heisenberg picture a channel is described by its adjoint $\Phi^*$, a unital completely positive map on bounded operators.
