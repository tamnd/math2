## Definition

A *superstring theory* is a [[string theory|GM58M1HFY0]] in which the worldsheet theory has [[supersymmetry|S847N922FY]].
In the Ramond–Neveu–Schwarz formulation, the worldsheet fields are the following.

1. Bosonic fields $X^\mu$, $\mu = 0, \dots, D-1$, which give the embedding of the worldsheet into spacetime, as in the [[Polyakov action|HFEGZQB8SV]].
2. Fermionic fields $\psi^\mu$, two-dimensional Majorana spinors on the worldsheet with one spacetime vector index.
3. A local worldsheet supersymmetry that exchanges $X^\mu$ and $\psi^\mu$.

The quantum theory is consistent, free of anomalies, only for $D = 10$.
A projection of the spectrum, the GSO projection, removes the tachyon and makes the spacetime spectrum supersymmetric.

## Remarks

The five consistent superstring theories in ten dimensions are type I, type IIA, type IIB, heterotic $SO(32)$ and heterotic $E_8 \times E_8$.
Example: the type IIB theory is a superstring theory of closed oriented strings; its massless spectrum is that of type IIB supergravity.
Non-example: the bosonic string theory, with only the fields $X^\mu$, fails clause 2; it is consistent in $D = 26$ and its spectrum has a tachyon and no fermions.
An equivalent formulation, the Green–Schwarz formalism, makes spacetime supersymmetry manifest instead of worldsheet supersymmetry.
