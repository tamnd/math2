## Example

Take $n$ moles of an [[ideal gas|N7R0H2VNDJ]] with constant molar [[heat capacity|SYYGZ18FF5]] $c_p$ at constant pressure and constant [[heat capacity ratio|NA1VVMJXVA]] $\gamma > 1$.
Let $p_2 > p_1$ be two pressures, with pressure ratio $r_p = p_2/p_1$.
The ideal *Brayton cycle* has four steps, with temperatures $T_1, T_2, T_3, T_4$ at their start points:

1. [[isentropic|ECAXF3AWWX]] compression from $p_1$ to $p_2$;
2. [[isobaric|WYAY33ZVYQ]] heat addition at $p_2$, absorbing $Q_H = n c_p (T_3 - T_2)$;
3. isentropic expansion from $p_2$ to $p_1$;
4. isobaric heat rejection at $p_1$, rejecting $Q_C = n c_p (T_4 - T_1)$, back to the initial state.

The gas returns to its initial state, so this is a [[thermodynamic cycle|AGRGCDWQF8]].
On an isentrope of an ideal gas $T p^{(1-\gamma)/\gamma}$ is constant, so $T_2/T_1 = T_3/T_4 = r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$.
Hence $T_3 - T_2 = r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} (T_4 - T_1)$, and the [[thermal efficiency|Z686C32X8S]] is
$$\eta = 1 - \frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = 1 - r_p^{-(\gamma - 1)/\gamma}.$$
For $r_p = 10$ and $\gamma = 1.4$, $\eta = 1 - 10^{-2/7} \approx 0.48$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Brayton cycle models a gas turbine, such as a jet engine or a gas-fired power plant.
A compressor squeezes air, fuel burns in it at steady pressure, the hot gas spins a turbine, and the exhaust leaves and cools at the outside pressure.
Unlike a piston engine, each step happens in a different part of the machine, and the flow is continuous.
As a [[heat engine|S7XFW04T3V]], its ideal efficiency grows with the pressure ratio of the compressor.
