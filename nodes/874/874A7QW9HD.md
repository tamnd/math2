## Example

Let $W = \{w, u, v\}$ with $R = \{(w, u), (u, v), (w, v)\}$.
So $R[w] = \{u, v\}$, $R[u] = \{v\}$ and $R[v] = \emptyset$.
Let $S_w$ be the identity relation on $\{u, v\}$, let $S_u$ be the identity on $\{v\}$, and let $S_v$ be empty.
Let $p$ be true only at $v$.
We check the conditions of a Veltman frame from [[interpretability logic|BGKZTSQN3W]].

1. $W$ is finite and non-empty.
2. $R$ is irreflexive, since no pair $(x, x)$ lies in $R$.
3. $R$ is [[transitive|0GJZW9JFZQ]]: the only chain $xRyRz$ is $wRuRv$, and $wRv$ holds.
4. Each $S_x$ is the identity on $R[x]$, so it is reflexive on $R[x]$ and transitive.
5. The condition "$xRyRz$ implies $y S_x z$" fails: we have $wRuRv$, but $(u, v) \notin S_w$.

So the structure is not a Veltman frame, and it is not a model of $\mathsf{IL}$.
We check that axiom J5, $\lozenge p \rhd p$, fails at $w$.

6. $u \Vdash \lozenge p$, because $uRv$ and $v \Vdash p$.
7. $v \nVdash \lozenge p$, because $v$ has no $R$-successors.
8. So $u$ is the only $R$-successor of $w$ where $\lozenge p$ holds.
9. The only $S_w$-partner of $u$ is $u$ itself, and $u \nVdash p$.
10. So no $x$ with $u S_w x$ satisfies $p$, and $w \nVdash \lozenge p \rhd p$.

## Remarks

The example shows why a Veltman frame must contain the pairs $(y, z)$ with $xRyRz$ in $S_x$: the soundness proof of J5 on the concept page uses exactly this condition.
If we add the pair $(u, v)$ to $S_w$, the structure becomes a Veltman frame, and then $u S_w v$ with $v \Vdash p$ gives $w \Vdash \lozenge p \rhd p$.
Axioms J1 to J4 still hold at every world of this structure under every valuation, because their soundness proofs use only reflexivity and transitivity of $S_w$ and $S_w \subseteq R[w] \times R[w]$.
The axioms of $\mathsf{GL}$ also hold, since $(W, R)$ is a finite strict partial order.
Modus ponens and necessitation preserve this kind of validity, so every theorem of $\mathsf{IL}$ without J5 holds on the structure, while J5 fails at $w$.
This shows that J5 does not follow from the other axioms of $\mathsf{IL}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Here world $w$ sees $u$ and $v$, and $u$ also sees $v$.
At $u$ it is "possible" that $p$, because $u$ sees $v$, where $p$ holds.
J5 says: whenever $p$ is possible, one can jump to a world where $p$ actually holds.
In this structure $u$ is not allowed to jump to $v$, so J5 fails.

### L2 human

We repeat the check of J5 at $w$ after one change.

1. Add $(u, v)$ to $S_w$, so $S_w = \{(u, u), (v, v), (u, v)\}$.
2. This relation is reflexive on $\{u, v\}$ and transitive, and it now contains the pair required by $wRuRv$.
3. The only successor of $w$ with $\lozenge p$ is still $u$.
4. Now $u S_w v$ and $v \Vdash p$, so $w \Vdash \lozenge p \rhd p$.

The typical use of the Veltman condition is in soundness proofs, where it gives the partner for J5.
A trap: the condition is about $S_w$ for the root $w$, not about $S_u$; here $S_u$ already contains $(v, v)$, but that does not help at $w$.
