## Description

The real numbers are the numbers that measure continuous quantities, such as lengths. We picture them as the points of a line.
We write $\mathbb{R}$ for the set of real numbers.
With addition, multiplication and its order, $\mathbb{R}$ is an [[ordered field|~ef6dd8e0]].
This ordered field is complete: each nonempty subset of $\mathbb{R}$ that is bounded above has a [[least upper bound|~2aea0c9b]].
Up to a unique isomorphism, $\mathbb{R}$ is the only [[complete ordered field|~2c2a587d]].

## Remarks

The set $\mathbb{R}$ contains the [[rational numbers|~e25d26db]] and also [[irrational numbers|~9900a69e]] such as $\sqrt{2}$ and $\pi$.
Two common constructions of $\mathbb{R}$ from $\mathbb{Q}$ use [[Dedekind cuts|~881e88b0]] or [[Cauchy sequences|~111471c8]].
The set $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The real numbers fill the number line without gaps.
The rational numbers leave gaps, for example at $\sqrt{2}$, and the real numbers fill these gaps.
Each real number has a [[decimal expansion|~b59fce03]], such as $3.14159\dots$ for $\pi$.
