## Example

Two agents $1$ and $2$ must each press button $0$ or button $1$ at a moment $m$.
There are four histories $h_{ab}$ for $a, b \in \set{0, 1}$, where $a$ is the button of agent 1 and $b$ that of agent 2.
The partitions are
$$\mathrm{Choice}^m_1 = \set{\set{h_{00}, h_{01}}, \set{h_{10}, h_{11}}}, \qquad \mathrm{Choice}^m_2 = \set{\set{h_{00}, h_{10}}, \set{h_{01}, h_{11}}}.$$
The door opens, written $p$, only on $h_{11}$.
We evaluate the operators of [[STIT logic|WYY2JXWMYY]] at $m$, as in [[the one-agent model|~3259f64e]].

1. Independence holds: a cell of agent 1 fixes $a$, a cell of agent 2 fixes $b$, and their intersection is $\set{h_{ab}}$.
2. At $m/h_{11}$, the cell of agent 1 is $\set{h_{10}, h_{11}}$, and $p$ fails at $m/h_{10}$. So $[1]p$ is false.
3. At $m/h_{11}$, the cell of agent 2 is $\set{h_{01}, h_{11}}$, and $p$ fails at $m/h_{01}$. So $[2]p$ is false.
4. The group cell at $m/h_{11}$ is $\set{h_{10}, h_{11}} \cap \set{h_{01}, h_{11}} = \set{h_{11}}$. So $[\set{1, 2}]p$ is true.
5. Let $q$ be true on $h_{1b}$ (agent 1 presses $1$) and $r$ be true on $h_{a1}$. Then $[1]q$ holds at $m/h_{1b}$ and $[2]r$ holds at $m/h_{a1}$.
6. So $\lozenge[1]q \wedge \lozenge[2]r$ is true at $m/h_{00}$.
7. At $m/h_{11}$ both $[1]q$ and $[2]r$ hold, so $\lozenge([1]q \wedge [2]r)$ is true at $m/h_{00}$, as the independence axiom says.
8. At $m/h_{10}$, $\neg p$ is guaranteed by agent 2 alone: the cell $\set{h_{00}, h_{10}}$ has $\neg p$ throughout. So $[2]\neg p$ is true there.

## Remarks

The model shows joint agency: the door opens only by the action of both agents, and neither agent alone sees to it.
Each agent alone can prevent the result, since pressing $0$ guarantees $\neg p$.
If $p$ were true on $h_{10}$ and $h_{11}$, then agent 1 alone would see to $p$ by pressing $1$, and the choice of agent 2 would not matter.
The product form of the histories is the typical way to make choices independent.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A door has two locks, and each of two people holds one key.
The door opens only if both turn their keys.
Neither person alone can make the door open.
Together they can, and either of them alone can keep it shut.

### L2 human

1. List the four histories as pairs of buttons: $00, 01, 10, 11$.
2. A cell of agent 1 is a row of the table, and a cell of agent 2 is a column.
3. A row and a column always meet in one square, which is independence.
4. To test $[G]p$, intersect the cells of the agents in $G$ and check $p$ on the result.

The typical use of such a model is to analyse shared responsibility.
A trap: the group operator is not the conjunction $[1]p \wedge [2]p$, which is false here, while $[\set{1, 2}]p$ is true.
