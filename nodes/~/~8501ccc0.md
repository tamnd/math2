## Proof

Write $\underline{n}$ for the [[numeral|VK6WWSDMXK]] of $n$, and $x \le y$ for $\exists v \, (v + x = y)$.
The axioms of [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]] are numbered as in [[its axiom list|TT8MXRTVBM]]: (1) $Sx \ne 0$, (2) $Sx = Sy \to x = y$, (3) $x \ne 0 \to \exists y \, (x = Sy)$, (4) $x + 0 = x$, (5) $x + Sy = S(x + y)$.

Facts about $\mathsf{Q}$ follow.
Each is a scheme, one sentence for each $n \in \N$.

1. (Q-c) $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \underline{m} + \underline{k} = \underline{m + k}$ for all $m, k$. By induction on $k$: axiom 4 gives $k = 0$, and axiom 5 gives the step.
2. (Q-d) $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \forall u \, (Su + \underline{k} = S(u + \underline{k}))$. For $k = 0$ both sides equal $Su$ by axiom 4. For the step, $Su + S\underline{k} = S(Su + \underline{k}) = S(S(u + \underline{k})) = S(u + S\underline{k})$, by axiom 5, the induction hypothesis and axiom 5.
3. (Q-a) $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \forall x \, (x \le \underline{n} \to x = \underline{0} \vee \cdots \vee x = \underline{n})$. Induction on $n$, steps 4 and 5.
4. Case $n = 0$. Assume $v + x = 0$. If $x \ne 0$, axiom 3 gives $x = Su$, and axiom 5 gives $S(v + u) = 0$. This contradicts axiom 1, so $x = 0$.
5. Case $n + 1$. Assume $v + x = S\underline{n}$ and $x \ne 0$. Axiom 3 gives $x = Su$. Axiom 5 gives $S(v + u) = S\underline{n}$, and axiom 2 gives $v + u = \underline{n}$. By the induction hypothesis $u = \underline{k}$ for some $k \le n$, so $x = \underline{k + 1}$.
6. (Q-b) $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \forall x \, (x \le \underline{n} \vee \underline{n} \le x)$. Induction on $n$, steps 7 to 9.
7. Case $n = 0$. Axiom 4 gives $x + 0 = x$, so $0 \le x$ with $v := x$.
8. Case $n + 1$, first half. By the induction hypothesis, $x \le \underline{n}$ or $\underline{n} \le x$. If $x \le \underline{n}$, then $x = \underline{k}$ with $k \le n$ by Q-a. Then $\underline{n + 1 - k} + x = \underline{n + 1}$ by Q-c, so $x \le \underline{n + 1}$.
9. Case $n + 1$, second half. Assume $v + \underline{n} = x$. If $v = 0$, then $x = \underline{n}$ by Q-c, and $x \le \underline{n + 1}$ by step 8. If $v \ne 0$, then $v = Su$ by axiom 3. By axiom 5 and Q-d, $u + S\underline{n} = S(u + \underline{n}) = Su + \underline{n} = x$, so $\underline{n + 1} \le x$.

Setup.

10. By [[arithmetization of syntax|~f1d9d517]], let $\mathrm{Prf}_T(y, x)$ represent in $\mathsf{Q}$ the relation "$y$ codes a proof in $T$ of the formula with code $x$". Let $\mathrm{neg}$ be the computable function with $\mathrm{neg}(\# \varphi) = \# \neg \varphi$; we use it as a term, through a formula that represents it.
11. Let $\rho(x) := \forall y \, \big(\mathrm{Prf}_T(y, x) \to \exists z \, (z \le y \wedge \mathrm{Prf}_T(z, \mathrm{neg}(x)))\big)$. This is $\neg \mathrm{Prov}^{R}_T(x)$ up to logical equivalence.
12. The [[diagonal lemma|3PRW76471J]] gives $R$ with $\mathsf{Q} \vdash R \leftrightarrow \rho(\ulcorner R \urcorner)$. Write $\underline{r}$ for $\ulcorner R \urcorner$ and $\underline{r'}$ for $\ulcorner \neg R \urcorner$; then $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \mathrm{neg}(\underline{r}) = \underline{r'}$.

Part 1: $T \nvdash R$.

13. Assume $T \vdash R$, and let $p$ code a proof of $R$. By representability, $T \vdash \mathrm{Prf}_T(\underline{p}, \underline{r})$.
14. $T$ is consistent and proves $R$, so no number codes a proof of $\neg R$. By representability, $T \vdash \neg \mathrm{Prf}_T(\underline{k}, \underline{r'})$ for each $k \le p$.
15. By Q-a and step 14, $T \vdash \neg \exists z \, (z \le \underline{p} \wedge \mathrm{Prf}_T(z, \underline{r'}))$.
16. Steps 13 and 15 refute the instance $y := \underline{p}$ of $\rho(\underline{r})$. So $T \vdash \neg \rho(\underline{r})$, and $T \vdash \neg R$ by step 12.
17. Steps 13 and 16 make $T$ inconsistent. So $T \nvdash R$.

Part 2: $T \nvdash \neg R$.

18. Assume $T \vdash \neg R$, and let $q$ code a proof of $\neg R$. By representability, $T \vdash \mathrm{Prf}_T(\underline{q}, \underline{r'})$.
19. $T$ is consistent and proves $\neg R$, so no number codes a proof of $R$. By representability, $T \vdash \neg \mathrm{Prf}_T(\underline{k}, \underline{r})$ for each $k \le q$.
20. We argue in $T$ for an arbitrary $y$ with $\mathrm{Prf}_T(y, \underline{r})$. By Q-b, $y \le \underline{q}$ or $\underline{q} \le y$.
21. If $y \le \underline{q}$, then $y = \underline{k}$ with $k \le q$ by Q-a. This contradicts step 19, so this case is impossible.
22. If $\underline{q} \le y$, then $z := \underline{q}$ satisfies $z \le y \wedge \mathrm{Prf}_T(z, \underline{r'})$ by step 18.
23. So $T \vdash \rho(\underline{r})$, and $T \vdash R$ by step 12. With the assumption of step 18, $T$ is inconsistent. So $T \nvdash \neg R$.

## Idea

The sentence $R$ asks which comes first: a proof of $R$ or a proof of $\neg R$.
A real proof of either sentence has a standard code, and $\mathsf{Q}$ can check all codes below a standard number one by one.
So a proof of $R$ yields a refutation of $R$, and a proof of $\neg R$ yields a proof of $R$.
Only facts Q-a and Q-b about numerals are needed, and no $\omega$-consistency.

## Variants

Facts Q-a and Q-b are also used for the representation of minimization in [[arithmetization of syntax|~f1d9d517]].
The example on the [[Rosser provability predicate|~55c1b89b]] uses the same two facts to show that $T$ proves the Rosser consistency statement.
Another proof uses two c.e. sets that no decidable set separates, and a formula that represents their separation in $\mathsf{Q}$.
