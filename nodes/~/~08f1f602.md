## Proof

We use the notation of the statement: $\mathsf{H}$ is the Hilbert system with the three axiom schemas and modus ponens, and $\mathsf{H} + A$ adds all substitution instances of $A$ as axioms.
A valuation is a function $v$ from the variables to $\set{\mathsf{T}, \mathsf{F}}$, extended to all formulas by the [[truth tables|05RSK4Q657]] of $\lnot$ and $\to$.
We use two known facts about $\mathsf{H}$.

- Soundness: every theorem of $\mathsf{H}$ is a [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]]. This is the [[soundness theorem for the Hilbert system|~4b87d303]].
- Completeness: every tautology is a theorem of $\mathsf{H}$. This is the completeness theorem of [[the propositional calculus|JDK63589SA]], due to Post in 1921 and later proved by Kalmár.

Part 1: $\mathsf{H}$ is Post consistent.

1. The valuation $v$ with $v(p_1) = \mathsf{F}$ makes $p_1$ false, so $p_1$ is not a tautology.
2. By soundness, $p_1$ is not a theorem of $\mathsf{H}$.

Part 2: $\mathsf{H}$ is Post complete.

3. Let $A$ be a formula that is not a theorem of $\mathsf{H}$. By completeness, $A$ is not a tautology.
4. So there is a valuation $v$ with $v(A) = \mathsf{F}$. Let $q_1, \ldots, q_n$ be the variables that occur in $A$.
5. Let $\top$ be the formula $p_1 \to p_1$ and let $\bot'$ be the formula $\lnot(p_1 \to p_1)$. By the truth table of $\to$, every valuation $w$ has $w(\top) = \mathsf{T}$, and then $w(\bot') = \mathsf{F}$.
6. Define a [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]] $\sigma$ by $\sigma(q_i) = \top$ when $v(q_i) = \mathsf{T}$, and $\sigma(q_i) = \bot'$ when $v(q_i) = \mathsf{F}$. Let $A^* = A\sigma$.
7. $A^*$ is a substitution instance of $A$, so $A^*$ is an axiom of $\mathsf{H} + A$.
8. Claim: for every valuation $w$ and every formula $B$ whose variables are among $q_1, \ldots, q_n$, we have $w(B\sigma) = v(B)$. We prove it by [[induction on formulas|EDRV3N0SA3]].
9. Base case: $B = q_i$. Then $B\sigma = \sigma(q_i)$, and by step 5 and the choice in step 6, $w(\sigma(q_i)) = v(q_i)$.
10. Case $B = \lnot C$. Then $B\sigma = \lnot(C\sigma)$, and $w(C\sigma) = v(C)$ by the induction hypothesis. The truth table of $\lnot$ gives $w(\lnot C\sigma) = v(\lnot C)$.
11. Case $B = C \to D$. Then $B\sigma = C\sigma \to D\sigma$, with $w(C\sigma) = v(C)$ and $w(D\sigma) = v(D)$ by the induction hypothesis. The truth table of $\to$ gives $w(B\sigma) = v(B)$.
12. By the claim with $B = A$, every valuation $w$ has $w(A^*) = v(A) = \mathsf{F}$.
13. So every valuation makes $\lnot A^*$ true. Thus $\lnot A^*$ is a tautology, and by completeness it is a theorem of $\mathsf{H}$.
14. Let $B$ be any formula. The formula $\lnot A^* \to (A^* \to B)$ is a tautology: if $w(A^*) = \mathsf{F}$, then $A^* \to B$ is true, and if $w(A^*) = \mathsf{T}$, then $\lnot A^*$ is false. By completeness it is a theorem of $\mathsf{H}$.
15. Every theorem of $\mathsf{H}$ is a theorem of $\mathsf{H} + A$, because $\mathsf{H} + A$ has all axioms of $\mathsf{H}$ and the same rule.
16. In $\mathsf{H} + A$, [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] on steps 13 and 14 gives $A^* \to B$.
17. Modus ponens on step 7 and step 16 gives $B$.
18. Since $B$ was arbitrary, every formula is a theorem of $\mathsf{H} + A$. So $\mathsf{H} + A$ is Post inconsistent, and $\mathsf{H}$ is Post complete.

## Idea

A formula that is not a theorem is not a tautology, so one row of its truth table makes it false.
Replace each variable by a formula that is always true or always false, following that row.
The resulting instance is false in every row, so its negation is a theorem, and together with the instance it gives every formula.
The proof uses both soundness and completeness of $\mathsf{H}$ for truth tables.

## Variants

When the language has a constant $\bot$ for falsity, the substitution can use $\bot$ and $\bot \to \bot$, and the instance $A^*$ has no variables at all.
A proof without the completeness theorem is possible for each fixed $A$: one writes out derivations of $\lnot A^*$ and of $\lnot A^* \to (A^* \to B)$ in $\mathsf{H}$.
These derivations depend on $A$, so a general argument still needs a uniform method such as Kalmár's lemma.
An algebraic proof: if $\mathsf{H} + A$ were Post consistent, its theorems would form a logic whose algebras are Boolean algebras satisfying $A = 1$; the two-element algebra is in every nontrivial such class and refutes $A$.
