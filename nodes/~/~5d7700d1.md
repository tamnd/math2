## Statement

Let $F$ be a field and let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over $F$ of [[rank|0ZC9YTXZRY]] $r \ge 1$.

1. There are an $m \times r$ matrix $C$ of rank $r$ and an $r \times n$ matrix $R$ of rank $r$ with $A = CR$.
2. One choice is the following: $C$ consists of the [[pivot columns|0BCMGDHZGR]] of $A$, and $R$ consists of the $r$ nonzero rows of the [[reduced row echelon form|0NNR68BFRY]] of $A$.
3. $A$ is a sum of $r$ matrices of rank $1$. A sum of $s$ matrices of rank at most $1$ has rank at most $s$.
4. $A$ has rank $1$ if and only if $A = uv^\top$ for some nonzero $u \in F^m$ and nonzero $v \in F^n$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- For $r = 0$, $A = 0$, and the factors would have $0$ columns and $0$ rows. The statement then holds only with empty matrices.
- In part 2, the columns of $C$ must be the pivot columns of $A$ itself. The pivot columns of the reduced form are standard unit vectors, and they do not span $\operatorname{Col}(A)$ in general.

### Consequences

- $\operatorname{Col}(A) = \operatorname{Col}(C)$ and $\operatorname{Row}(A) = \operatorname{Row}(R)$. This gives [[row rank equals column rank|0TZAG60ZYT]].
- The rank is the smallest number of rank-one matrices with sum $A$.
- Storing $C$ and $R$ needs $r(m + n)$ numbers instead of $mn$.

### Converse

The factorization is not unique: $A = (CG)(G^{-1}R)$ for every invertible $r \times r$ matrix $G$.
Every rank factorization of $A$ is of this form.

### Generalizations

- For a linear map $T \colon V \to W$ of rank $r$, $T$ factors as $V \to F^r \to W$, a surjection followed by an injection.
- The singular value decomposition gives a rank factorization with orthogonal columns over $\R$.

## History

Strang uses the name $CR$ factorization for part 2 in his recent textbooks.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every matrix of rank $r$ is a product of a tall matrix with $r$ columns and a wide matrix with $r$ rows.
The tall matrix holds a basis of the column space.
The wide matrix says how to combine the basis to get each column.

### L2 human

The pivot columns of $A$ are a basis of its column space.
The reduced form tells how each column of $A$ is a combination of the pivot columns.
Writing these combinations as a matrix product gives $A = CR$.
Expanding the product column by row gives $A$ as a sum of $r$ rank-one matrices.

### L3 human

A rank factorization is the factorization of $x \mapsto Ax$ through its image $\operatorname{Col}(A) \cong F^r$.
The rank-one matrices $uv^\top$ are the linear maps $x \mapsto (v^\top x) u$, a linear form times a fixed vector.
