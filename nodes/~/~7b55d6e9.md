## Summary

A topological property is a property of topological spaces such that, if a space has it, each [[homeomorphic|~0e10d672]] space also has it.
Examples are [[compactness|~68153d33]], [[connectedness|~711a13dd]], [[separability|~2753463b]], [[metrizability|~db159499]] and the [[Hausdorff property|~a1fe0c25]].

## Remarks

Topological properties are used to show that two spaces are not homeomorphic. For example, $[0, 1]$ is compact and $(0, 1)$ is not, so they are not homeomorphic.
Properties that depend on a metric are often not topological. For example, $(0, 1)$ and $\mathbb{R}$ are homeomorphic, but only $\mathbb{R}$ is [[complete|~aae963c7]] and only $(0, 1)$ is bounded.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A topological property is a property that a space keeps when you deform it by a homeomorphism.
Size and distance are not topological properties, but the number of pieces of a space is one.
