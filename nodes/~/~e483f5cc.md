## Statement

Let $p$ be a prime number, let $G$ be a finite [[p-group|~a63adf2b]], and let $G$ [[act|~0ceb002c]] on a finite set $X$.
Let $X^G$ be the set of the [[fixed points|~2926e5eb]] of the action.
Then $|X| \equiv |X^G| \pmod p$.

## Remarks

The [[orbits|~0d3dfbc7]] partition $X$, and the orbits with one point are exactly the fixed points.
Each other orbit has a size that divides $|G|$ and is greater than $1$, so its size is divisible by $p$.
A consequence: if $p$ does not divide $|X|$, then the action has at least one fixed point.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The orbits of a $p$-group have sizes that are powers of $p$.
The orbits of size $1$ are the fixed points, and all other orbits have a size divisible by $p$.
So counting modulo $p$ sees only the fixed points.
