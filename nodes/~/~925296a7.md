## Proof

We give the full proof when $\mathcal F_1$ and $\mathcal F_2$ use the same set $K$ of connectives.
The case of different sets is cited at the end.
For a formula $\theta$ and a [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]] $\sigma$, write $\theta\sigma$ for the result of replacing each variable $p$ by $\sigma(p)$.

1. *Substitution lemma.* Let $D = \theta_1, \dots, \theta_r$ be an $\mathcal F_2$-derivation from hypotheses $\Gamma$, and let $\sigma$ be a substitution. Then $\theta_1\sigma, \dots, \theta_r\sigma$ is an $\mathcal F_2$-derivation from $\Gamma\sigma$. Reason: if $\theta_i$ is the conclusion of an instance of a rule by a substitution $\rho$, then $\theta_i\sigma$ is the conclusion of the instance of the same rule by the substitution $p \mapsto \rho(p)\sigma$, and its premises are the images of earlier lines.
2. *Fixed derivations.* Let $R = \dfrac{\varphi_1 \;\cdots\; \varphi_k}{\psi}$ be a rule of $\mathcal F_1$. It is sound, so $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k \models \psi$. Since $\mathcal F_2$ is implicationally complete, there is an $\mathcal F_2$-derivation $D_R$ of $\psi$ from $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k$. Fix one such $D_R$ for each rule $R$.
3. *Constants.* $\mathcal F_1$ has finitely many rules. Let $c$ be the maximum number of lines of the $D_R$, and let $c'$ be the maximum size of a formula in some $D_R$.
4. *Translation.* Let $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_m$ be an $\mathcal F_1$-proof. Suppose $\chi_i$ is the conclusion of an instance of $R$ by a substitution $\sigma$, with premises $\varphi_1\sigma, \dots, \varphi_k\sigma$ among $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_{i-1}$. Extend $\sigma$ by $\sigma(q) = q$ for the variables $q$ that occur in $D_R$ but not in $R$. Replace $\chi_i$ by the list $D_R\sigma$, with its hypothesis lines removed.
5. *Correctness.* By step 1, $D_R\sigma$ is an $\mathcal F_2$-derivation of $\psi\sigma = \chi_i$ from $\varphi_1\sigma, \dots, \varphi_k\sigma$. These hypotheses are earlier lines of the new list, by induction on $i$. So the removed hypothesis lines are not needed, and the new list is an $\mathcal F_2$-proof of $\chi_m$.
6. *Lines.* Each $\chi_i$ is replaced by at most $c$ lines. So the new proof has at most $c m$ lines.
7. *Size of one line.* Let $S = \abs{\chi_1} + \cdots + \abs{\chi_m}$. For a variable $p$ of $R$, the formula $\sigma(p)$ is a subformula of one of $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_i$, so $\abs{\sigma(p)} \le S$. A formula $\theta$ of $D_R$ has at most $c'$ symbols, and each variable in it is replaced by at most $S$ symbols. So $\abs{\theta\sigma} \le c' S$.
8. *Total size.* The new proof has at most $c m$ lines, each of size at most $c' S$. Since $m \le S$, its size is at most $c c' S^2$.
9. *Polynomial time.* The translation reads the $\mathcal F_1$-proof, finds for each line a rule and a substitution, and writes $D_R\sigma$. Each part takes time polynomial in $S$, because the rules and the $D_R$ are fixed.
10. *p-simulation.* Let $f_1$ and $f_2$ be the proof systems of $\mathcal F_1$ and $\mathcal F_2$. Let $\tau_0$ be the fixed tautology that $f_1$ outputs on strings that are not $\mathcal F_1$-proofs, and fix one $\mathcal F_2$-proof $w_0$ of $\tau_0$. Define $h(w)$ as the translation of $w$ when $w$ is an $\mathcal F_1$-proof, and as $w_0$ otherwise. Then $h$ is computable in polynomial time by step 9, and $f_2(h(w)) = f_1(w)$ for every string $w$ by step 5.

So $\mathcal F_2$ p-simulates $\mathcal F_1$, and the constant $\max(c, c c')$ works in the statement.
Exchanging the two systems gives p-equivalence.
$\blacksquare$

*Different connectives (cited).* Reckhow's proof first rewrites each line into the connectives of $\mathcal F_2$.
A direct rewriting can be exponentially long, for example when $\leftrightarrow$ is written with $\land$, $\lor$ and $\neg$, since each $\leftrightarrow$ copies both sides.
So each formula is first balanced to depth $O(\log n)$, by Spira's theorem on formula depth, and then translated.
Then the argument above is applied to the translated rules.

## Idea

Each rule of one system is a fixed derived rule of the other, because the other system is implicationally complete.
Replacing each step by a substituted copy of this fixed derivation multiplies the number of lines by a constant.
The size grows at most quadratically, since each substituted formula is at most as long as the whole proof.

## Variants

- The same proof shows that any two [[extended Frege systems|~6925f591]] over the same connectives are p-equivalent. An extension step is copied as it is.
- If $D_R$ uses only formulas of depth at most $d_0$, the translation raises the depth of each line by at most an additive constant. So the proof also works for [[bounded-depth Frege|~2cc592a7]], where the depth of lines is the measure kept bounded.
