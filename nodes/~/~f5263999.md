## Proof

We use the premises (P1), (P2) and C$_1$ to C$_{N-1}$ of the [[statement|~15bcecd7]].

*Part 1.* We use [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $k$, for $0 \le k \le N - 1$.
The claim is that $p_{N - k}$ is derivable with exactly $k$ steps of modus ponens.

1. Base $k = 0$: $p_N$ is the premise (P1). No step of modus ponens is used.
2. Induction step: let $k < N - 1$, and let $p_{N - k}$ be derived with $k$ steps.
3. Put $n := N - k - 1$. Then $1 \le n \le N - 1$, since $0 \le k < N - 1$.
4. The conditional C$_n$ is $p_{n + 1} \to p_n$, that is, $p_{N - k} \to p_{N - k - 1}$.
5. One step of modus ponens on step 2 and step 4 gives $p_{N - (k + 1)}$, with $k + 1$ steps in total.
6. For $k = N - 1$, this gives $p_1$ with exactly $N - 1$ steps. This is part 1.

*Part 2.*

7. Part 1 derives $p_1$, and (P2) is $\neg p_1$. So $\Gamma_N$ derives a formula and its negation.
8. A valuation that makes all premises true would make $p_1$ true and false, by the soundness of modus ponens. So no such valuation exists.
9. Steps 1 to 8 use only modus ponens and the premises. So they are valid in intuitionistic logic.

*Part 3.* We argue by [[contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]] in classical logic.

10. Assume (P1), (P2) and the negation of the disjunction. By De Morgan's law, $\neg (p_{n + 1} \wedge \neg p_n)$ holds for each $n$.
11. In classical logic, $\neg (p_{n + 1} \wedge \neg p_n)$ is equivalent to $p_{n + 1} \to p_n$, by the truth tables. So each C$_n$ holds.
12. Part 2 now gives a contradiction. So (P1) and (P2) refute the negation of the disjunction.
13. By the classical law $\neg \neg A \to A$, the disjunction $\bigvee_{n = 1}^{N - 1} (p_{n + 1} \wedge \neg p_n)$ follows from (P1) and (P2).

## Idea

The chain of tolerance conditionals carries "heap" from $N$ grains down to $1$ grain, one grain at a time.
Each link is one use of modus ponens, so there are $N - 1$ links.
In classical logic, the failure of the chain must happen at some link, which gives the line-drawing form.

## Variants

1. The SEP entry gives a form with one universal premise and mathematical induction in place of $N - 1$ separate conditionals.
2. The line-drawing form also follows from the least number principle. Let $m$ be the least index with $p_m$ true; it exists by (P1).
3. Then $m > 1$ by (P2), and $n := m - 1$ satisfies $p_{n + 1} \wedge \neg p_n$. This uses excluded middle for each $p_m$.
