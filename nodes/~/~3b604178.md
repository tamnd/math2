## Proof

Let $T$ satisfy the hypotheses of the theorem.
If $T$ is given only by a computably enumerable set of theorems, we replace its axioms by a decidable set with the same theorems, by Craig's trick.

1. By [[arithmetization of syntax|~f1d9d517]], the relation "$y$ codes a proof in $T$ of the formula with code $x$" is decidable. Let $\mathrm{Prf}_T(y, x)$ be a $\Sigma_1$ formula that represents it in $\mathsf{Q}$.
2. Let $\mathrm{Prov}_T(x) := \exists y \, \mathrm{Prf}_T(y, x)$, the [[provability predicate|NB2BCZR40F]].
3. Apply the [[diagonal lemma|3PRW76471J]] to $\neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(x)$. It gives a sentence $G$ with $\mathsf{Q} \vdash G \leftrightarrow \neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)$. Since $T \supseteq \mathsf{Q}$, $T$ proves the same equivalence.

Part 1: $T \nvdash G$ when $T$ is consistent.

4. Assume $T \vdash G$, and let $p$ be the code of a proof of $G$ in $T$.
5. Then $\mathrm{Prf}_T(p, \# G)$ is true, so $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \mathrm{Prf}_T(\underline{p}, \ulcorner G \urcorner)$ by representability.
6. By the rule for $\exists$, $T \vdash \mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)$.
7. By step 3, $T \vdash \neg G$. With step 4, $T$ proves $G$ and $\neg G$, so $T$ is inconsistent.
8. So if $T$ is consistent, then $T \nvdash G$, and no number codes a proof of $G$ in $T$.

$G$ is $\Pi_1$ and true.

9. $\mathrm{Prf}_T$ has the form $\exists z \, \delta(y, x, z)$ with $\delta$ in $\Delta_0$. So $\neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)$ is equivalent in logic to $\forall y \, \forall z \, \neg \delta(y, \ulcorner G \urcorner, z)$, a $\Pi_1$ sentence. By step 3, $G$ is $\mathsf{Q}$-equivalent to it.
10. $\N$ is a model of $\mathsf{Q}$, and $\mathrm{Prf}_T$ represents the proof relation, so $\mathrm{Prf}_T$ defines the proof relation in $\N$.
11. By step 8 and step 10, $\N \models \neg \mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)$.
12. The equivalence of step 3 holds in the model $\N$ of $\mathsf{Q}$. With step 11, $\N \models G$.

Part 2: $T \nvdash \neg G$ when $T$ is $\omega$-consistent.

13. Assume that $T$ is [[ω-consistent|~08020cc1]]. Then $T$ is consistent, so step 8 applies.
14. Assume, for a contradiction, that $T \vdash \neg G$. By step 3, $T \vdash \mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)$, that is, $T \vdash \exists y \, \mathrm{Prf}_T(y, \ulcorner G \urcorner)$.
15. Let $n \in \N$. By step 8, $n$ does not code a proof of $G$. By representability, $T \vdash \neg \mathrm{Prf}_T(\underline{n}, \ulcorner G \urcorner)$.
16. Steps 14 and 15 show that the formula $\mathrm{Prf}_T(y, \ulcorner G \urcorner)$ witnesses the $\omega$-inconsistency of $T$. This contradicts step 13.
17. If $T$ is only $1$-consistent, that is $\Sigma_1$-sound, step 14 already gives a contradiction: $\mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)$ is a $\Sigma_1$ sentence that is false by step 11.
18. So $T \nvdash \neg G$. With step 8, $T$ proves neither $G$ nor $\neg G$, and $T$ is not negation complete.

Part 3 of the statement, incompleteness under consistency alone, is proved in [[Rosser's theorem|~71fdf6fd]].

## Idea

The sentence $G$ says "I am not provable in $T$".
If $T$ proved $G$, then $T$ would also prove "$G$ is provable", which is the negation of $G$.
If $T$ proved $\neg G$, then $T$ would claim a proof of $G$ that no standard number codes, which $\omega$-consistency forbids.
So $G$ is undecided, and it is true because it is unprovable.

## Variants

Gödel's 1931 proof worked in a type theory based on Principia Mathematica, and it built the self-referential sentence directly for one formula instead of using a general diagonal lemma.
[[Rosser's theorem|~71fdf6fd]] replaces $\neg \mathrm{Prov}_T$ by the Rosser formula and needs only consistency in part 2.
Other routes avoid self-reference: the [[computability proof|~6463c0bc]] uses the halting problem, and [[Chaitin's proof|~403741ce]] uses Kolmogorov complexity.
