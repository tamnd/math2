## Example

Let PA be [[Peano arithmetic|0QFT7Z76N5]], with a fixed [[Gödel numbering|MFJXPAF0R0]].
Let $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ be the sentence "no number codes a derivation of $0 = 1$ in PA".
It has the form $\forall p \, \lnot\mathrm{Prf}(p, \ulcorner 0 = 1 \urcorner)$ with a $\Delta_0$ formula $\mathrm{Prf}$, so it is a $\Pi_1$ sentence of the [[arithmetical hierarchy|7CVJCQKY41]].
We show that PA does not [[interpret|~95e26594]] the theory $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.

We use two known facts.

- (F1) Orey-Hájek characterization: for a sentence $A$, $\mathsf{PA} \rhd \mathsf{PA} + A$ holds if and only if every $\Pi_1$ sentence provable in $\mathsf{PA} + A$ is provable in PA.
- (F2) [[Gödel's second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]]: a consistent recursively axiomatized extension $U$ of PA does not prove $\mathrm{Con}(U)$.

1. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\mathsf{PA} \rhd \mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.
2. By (F1), every $\Pi_1$ sentence provable in $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is provable in PA.
3. The sentence $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is $\Pi_1$, and $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ proves it, since it is an axiom.
4. By steps 2 and 3, $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.
5. PA is consistent, because the standard model $\N$ satisfies its axioms, and PA is recursively axiomatized.
6. By step 5 and (F2), PA does not prove $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$. This contradicts step 4.

So no interpretation of $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ in PA exists.
The condition of the definition that fails is the last one: no choice of a domain formula and of formulas for $0$, $S$, $+$ and $\cdot$ makes the translations of all axioms of $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ provable in PA.

## Remarks

Step 4 uses only one direction of (F1): an interpretation carries $\Pi_1$ theorems back to PA.
This direction has a short reason.
If $I$ interprets $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ in PA, then PA proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})^I$.
PA can map its own numbers onto an initial segment of the interpreted numbers, by $0 \mapsto 0^I$ and $n + 1 \mapsto S^I(\text{image of } n)$.
A number $p$ that codes a derivation of $0 = 1$ would map to an interpreted number that satisfies $\mathrm{Prf}^I$, because a $\Delta_0$ formula keeps its truth value under an isomorphism onto an initial segment.
So PA proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})^I \to \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, and hence $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.

The result strengthens the second incompleteness theorem for PA: PA not only fails to prove $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, it cannot even interpret a theory that contains it.
The same proof shows $T \not\rhd T + \mathrm{Con}(T)$ for each consistent recursively axiomatized extension $T$ of PA in the language of PA.
By contrast, PA interprets $\mathsf{PA} + \lnot\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, see [[the example of Feferman|~a0b6d664]].
So $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is not an Orey sentence for PA.

The theory $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is still weakly interpretable in PA: it is itself a consistent extension of PA, and it interprets itself.
The non-example also explains why $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is [[not conservative over PA|EQH7XTZ29V]]: it is not even $\Pi_1$-conservative.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Peano arithmetic cannot prove that it is free of contradictions.
Suppose we add this fact as a new rule.
One could hope that the old rules can still imitate the new list, by some clever choice of what the words "number", "plus" and "times" mean.
This example shows that no such choice works.
Any imitation would let the old rules prove the new rule, and Gödel showed that this is impossible.

### L2 human

We compare the two theories $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ and $\mathsf{PA} + \lnot\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.

1. Both theories are consistent, since PA proves neither $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ nor its negation. PA does not prove the negation, because $\N$ is a model of PA in which $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is true.
2. The new axiom $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is $\Pi_1$, and PA does not prove it, so the first theory is not $\Pi_1$-conservative over PA, and PA does not interpret it.
3. The new axiom $\lnot\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is $\Sigma_1$, and the first example shows that the second theory is $\Pi_1$-conservative over PA, so PA interprets it.
4. So PA interprets the false theory and does not interpret the true one.

The typical use is a test: to show that $S$ does not interpret $T$, find a $\Pi_1$ sentence that $T$ proves and $S$ does not.
A trap is to think that a true extension is easier to interpret than a false one; the order of interpretability ignores truth.
