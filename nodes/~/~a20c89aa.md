## Example

Let $T = \set{\overline{0.7} \to \varphi, \ \overline{0.8} \to (\varphi \to \psi)}$ in [[rational Pavelka logic|~4b515603]].
We derive $\overline{0.5} \to \psi$ and show that $0.5$ is the best bound.

Soundness of the bound, in [[Łukasiewicz semantics|~3e406741]]:

1. Let $e$ be a model of $T$, with $x = e(\varphi)$ and $y = e(\psi)$; then $x \ge 0.7$ and $\min(1, 1 - x + y) \ge 0.8$.
2. The [[t-norm|~a4e21349]] $\odot$ is monotone, so $x \odot (x \Rightarrow y) \ge 0.7 \odot 0.8 = \max(0, 1.5 - 1) = 0.5$.
3. By residuation, $x \odot (x \Rightarrow y) \le y$, so $y \ge 0.5$.

Derivation in RPL:

4. From the two premises, $\overline{0.7} \odot \overline{0.8} \to \varphi \odot (\varphi \to \psi)$ is derivable, since $\odot$ is monotone in Łukasiewicz logic.
5. $\varphi \odot (\varphi \to \psi) \to \psi$ is a theorem of Łukasiewicz logic.
6. The bookkeeping axiom gives $\overline{0.5} \to \overline{0.7} \odot \overline{0.8}$.
7. By transitivity of $\to$ and [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]], $T \vdash \overline{0.5} \to \psi$.

Optimality:

8. Put $x = 0.7$ and $y = 0.5$: then $x \Rightarrow y = \min(1, 1 - 0.7 + 0.5) = 0.8$.
9. So this $e$ is a model of $T$ with $e(\psi) = 0.5$, and no bound above $0.5$ is sound.

## Remarks

1. The general rule is: from $\overline{r} \to \varphi$ and $\overline{s} \to (\varphi \to \psi)$ derive $\overline{r \odot s} \to \psi$.
2. Wikipedia describes evaluated syntax as giving each formula an evaluation; in RPL the graded formula "$\varphi$ to degree $r$" is the formula $\overline{r} \to \varphi$.
3. In this theory the truth degree of $\psi$ is $0.5$, by steps 3 and 9.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We know $\varphi$ to degree $0.7$ and "$\varphi$ implies $\psi$" to degree $0.8$.
Then we know $\psi$ to degree $0.5$, and no more.
The losses $0.3$ and $0.2$ add up.

### L2 human

1. Combine the two degrees with the Łukasiewicz t-norm: $\max(0, r + s - 1)$.
2. To check that the bound is best, set $\varphi$ to $r$ and $\psi$ to $r \odot s$.

A trap: if $r + s \le 1$, the rule gives the degree $0$, which carries no information.
