## Proof

We work in [[Heyting arithmetic|RXHJPVMPJH]] with intuitionistic [[natural deduction|PAQ4ZX2TX1]] NJ.
Fix a formula $A$, and let $B \mapsto B^A$ be the [[A-translation|~10a427f4]]: replace each atomic subformula $P$, including $\bot$, by $P \vee A$.
Renaming bound variables and eigenvariables does not change what a derivation proves.
So we assume that no variable free in $A$ is bound in $B$ or used as an eigenvariable.

**Lemma F0 (substitution).**

1. The free variables of $B^A$ are those of $B$ and those of $A$.
2. If $x$ is not free in $A$, then $(B[t/x])^A = B^A[t/x]$, by induction on $B$, since each clause commutes with [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]].

**Lemma F1 (ex falso).** For every formula $C$, $A \vdash_i C^A$.
We use induction on $C$.

1. Atomic $C$: from $A$, the rule ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ gives $C \vee A = C^A$.
2. $C = D \wedge E$: the induction hypothesis gives $D^A$ and $E^A$, and ${\wedge}\mathrm{I}$ combines them.
3. $C = D \vee E$: the induction hypothesis gives $D^A$, and ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ gives $D^A \vee E^A$.
4. $C = D \to E$: the induction hypothesis gives $E^A$, and ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ with no discharged leaf gives $D^A \to E^A$.
5. $C = \forall x\, D$: the induction hypothesis gives $D^A$ from $A$; $x$ is not free in $A$, so ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ applies.
6. $C = \exists x\, D$: the induction hypothesis gives $D^A$, and ${\exists}\mathrm{I}$ with the term $x$ gives $\exists x\, D^A$.

**Lemma F2 (logic).** If NJ derives $B$ from assumptions $\Gamma$, then NJ derives $B^A$ from $\Gamma^A$.
We use induction on the derivation, with one case for its last rule.

1. The rules for $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\to$ and the quantifier rules: the translation commutes with each connective and quantifier, so we apply the same rule.
2. For the quantifier rules, Lemma F0 gives $(D[t/x])^A = D^A[t/x]$, because the variable $x$ is bound and so not free in $A$.
3. The eigenvariable $y$ is not free in $A$. By Lemma F0, $y$ is free in a translated formula only if it is free in the original. So the eigenvariable conditions hold.
4. Rule ${\bot}\mathrm{E}$: we have $\bot^A = \bot \vee A$ and need $C^A$. We use ${\vee}\mathrm{E}$ with two cases.
5. In the case $\bot$, the rule ${\bot}\mathrm{E}$ gives $C^A$. In the case $A$, Lemma F1 gives $C^A$.
6. Rule ${=}\mathrm{I}$: from $t = t$, the rule ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ gives $t = t \vee A$.
7. Rule ${=}\mathrm{E}$: we have $s = t \vee A$ and $\varphi^A[s/x]$, with $x$ not free in $A$. We use ${\vee}\mathrm{E}$.
8. In the case $s = t$, the rule ${=}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\varphi^A[t/x]$. In the case $A$, Lemma F1 gives it.

**Lemma F3 (axioms).** HA proves $B^A$ for every axiom $B$ of HA.

1. An equation axiom $s = t$, such as $x + 0 = x$: ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ gives $s = t \vee A$.
2. The axiom $S x = S y \to x = y$: assume $S x = S y \vee A$. In the first case, the axiom gives $x = y$; in both cases ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ gives $x = y \vee A$.
3. The axiom $S x = 0 \to \bot$: assume $S x = 0 \vee A$. In the first case, the axiom gives $\bot$; in both cases ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ gives $\bot \vee A$.
4. The induction axiom for $\varphi$: its translation is $\varphi^A(0) \wedge \forall x\, (\varphi^A(x) \to \varphi^A(S x)) \to \forall x\, \varphi^A(x)$, by Lemma F0.
5. This is the induction axiom for the formula $\varphi^A$, so it is an axiom of HA.
6. Hence, if $\mathsf{HA} \vdash B$, then $\mathsf{HA} \vdash B^A$: apply Lemma F2 to a derivation from finitely many axioms, and Lemma F3 to the axioms.

**Lemma F4 (decidable formulas).** For every quantifier-free $\varphi$, HA proves $\varphi^A \leftrightarrow \varphi \vee A$.
HA proves $\psi \vee \neg \psi$ for every quantifier-free $\psi$, since it proves this for equations and the connectives preserve it.
We use induction on $\varphi$.

1. Atomic $\varphi$: $\varphi^A$ is $\varphi \vee A$.
2. $\varphi = \psi \wedge \chi$: $(\psi \vee A) \wedge (\chi \vee A) \leftrightarrow (\psi \wedge \chi) \vee A$ holds in NJ, by cases on the two disjunctions.
3. $\varphi = \psi \vee \chi$: $(\psi \vee A) \vee (\chi \vee A) \leftrightarrow (\psi \vee \chi) \vee A$ holds in NJ, by cases.
4. $\varphi = \psi \to \chi$: by induction, $\varphi^A$ is equivalent to $(\psi \vee A) \to (\chi \vee A)$.
5. From $(\psi \to \chi) \vee A$ we get $(\psi \vee A) \to (\chi \vee A)$, by cases on both disjunctions.
6. Conversely, assume $(\psi \vee A) \to (\chi \vee A)$. We use cases on $\psi \vee \neg \psi$.
7. If $\neg \psi$, then $\psi \to \chi$ by ${\bot}\mathrm{E}$, and ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ gives $(\psi \to \chi) \vee A$.
8. If $\psi$, the assumption gives $\chi \vee A$. If $\chi$, then $\psi \to \chi$; if $A$, then $A$. In both cases we get $(\psi \to \chi) \vee A$.

**Lemma F5 (negative translation of decidable formulas).** For quantifier-free $\varphi$, HA proves $\varphi^N \leftrightarrow \varphi$.

1. Atomic $\varphi$: $\bot^N = \bot$, and for an equation HA proves $\neg \neg \varphi \leftrightarrow \varphi$, by cases on $\varphi \vee \neg \varphi$.
2. The clauses for $\wedge$ and $\to$ commute with the translation, so the induction step is direct.
3. For $\psi \vee \chi$: by induction, $(\psi \vee \chi)^N$ is equivalent to $\neg (\neg \psi \wedge \neg \chi)$, which is equivalent to $\neg \neg (\psi \vee \chi)$ in NJ.
4. HA proves $\neg \neg (\psi \vee \chi) \to \psi \vee \chi$, by cases on the decidable formula $\psi \vee \chi$.

**Main proof.**
Let $\varphi(x, y)$ be quantifier-free, and assume $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \forall x\, \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$.

1. The rule ${\forall}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$.
2. The [[Gödel-Gentzen theorem|~c9a9f7c2]], part 2, gives $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \neg \forall y\, \neg \varphi^N(x, y)$.
3. Lemma F5 gives $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \neg \forall y\, \neg \varphi(x, y)$, that is, $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \forall y\, (\varphi(x, y) \to \bot) \to \bot$.
4. Let $A = \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$. The bound variable $y$ is not free in $A$, so the translation is defined.
5. Lemma F3, step 6, gives $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \forall y\, (\varphi^A(x, y) \to \bot \vee A) \to \bot \vee A$.
6. We prove the premise. Fix $y$ and assume $\varphi^A(x, y)$. Lemma F4 gives $\varphi(x, y) \vee A$.
7. In the case $\varphi(x, y)$, the rule ${\exists}\mathrm{I}$ gives $A$. In both cases ${\vee}\mathrm{I}$ gives $\bot \vee A$.
8. The rule ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ discharges $\varphi^A(x, y)$. No open assumption contains $y$, so ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ gives the premise.
9. The rule ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ with step 5 gives $\bot \vee A$ in HA.
10. In the case $\bot$, the rule ${\bot}\mathrm{E}$ gives $A$. So $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$.
11. No assumption is open, so ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ gives $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \forall x\, \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$.

This proves the [[theorem|~af8f99cd]].

## Idea

The negative translation gives an HA proof of "no $y$ fails to exist", that is, of $\neg \forall y\, \neg \varphi$.
This proof treats $\bot$ as an arbitrary formula, apart from ex falso.
The A-translation replaces $\bot$ by the goal $\exists y\, \varphi$ and repairs ex falso by adding $A$ to every atom.
The double negation then turns into "if every $y$ gives the goal, then the goal holds", whose premise is provable.

## Variants

- A route by functional interpretation: the [[Dialectica interpretation|HZ17ES9PDY]] of the negative translation gives a term $t$ of [[system T|~62fef358]] with $\varphi(x, t(x))$. Then $\mathsf{HA}^\omega$ proves $\forall x\, \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$, and $\mathsf{HA}^\omega$ is conservative over HA.
- In minimal logic the negative translation needs no ex falso, and then it suffices to replace only $\bot$ by $A$. This is the refined A-translation used in program extraction.
