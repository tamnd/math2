## Example

In [[linear temporal logic|RMJ56ZPA63]] over $AP = \set{p}$, compare
$$GFp \ (\text{"$p$ infinitely often"}) \qquad\text{and}\qquad FGp \ (\text{"$p$ from some point on"}).$$

1. By [[the definition|8S64VZ6A1S]], $w \models GFp$ if and only if for every $i$ there is $j \ge i$ with $p \in w_j$. This says that the set $\set{j : p \in w_j}$ is infinite.
2. Likewise $w \models FGp$ if and only if there is $i$ with $p \in w_j$ for all $j \ge i$. This says that the complement of that set is finite.
3. Take $w = (\emptyset\,\set{p})^\omega$, with $p$ exactly at the odd positions. The odd numbers are infinite, so $w \models GFp$.
4. The even numbers are infinite too, so $p$ fails at arbitrarily late positions. Hence $w \not\models FGp$.
5. So $GFp \to FGp$ is not valid.
6. Conversely, let $w \models FGp$ with witness $i$. For any $k$, the position $\max(i, k)$ has $p$. So $w \models GFp$.
7. Hence $FGp \to GFp$ is valid, and $FGp$ is strictly stronger.

## Remarks

The duality $\neg GFp \equiv FG\neg p$ follows from $\neg G \equiv F\neg$ and $\neg F \equiv G\neg$.
Recurrence $GFp$ is the acceptance condition of a [[Büchi automaton|92DM43HQZT]]: some accepting state is visited infinitely often.
Persistence $FGp$ is the dual co-Büchi condition.
Fairness assumptions are usually written in these two forms, for example $GF\,\mathit{enabled} \to GF\,\mathit{taken}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"The light blinks forever" makes $GF\,\mathit{on}$ true: the light is on again and again.
It does not make $FG\,\mathit{on}$ true: the light never stays on for good.
A light that flickers a while and then stays on makes both true.

### L2 human

1. On $\emptyset\,\emptyset\,\set{p}^\omega$, both formulas hold, with witness $i = 2$ for $FGp$.
2. On $\set{p}\,\set{p}\,\emptyset^\omega$, both fail: after position $1$ there is no $p$.
3. On $(\emptyset\,\set{p})^\omega$, only $GFp$ holds.

A trap: $GFp$ and $FGp$ differ, but $GGp \equiv Gp$ and $FFp \equiv Fp$ in LTL.
