## Example

The fusion $\mathsf{K} \otimes \mathsf{K}$ is the smallest normal bimodal logic in the boxes $\square_1$ and $\square_2$, see [[multimodal logic|~d172c20d]].
It has the axioms of [[K|~b1c67ad7]] for each box and no axiom that mixes them.
We show that it does not prove the commutativity formula $\square_1\square_2 p \to \square_2\square_1 p$.

1. Take $W = \set{w, v, t}$, $R_1 = \set{(w, t), (v, t)}$, $R_2 = \set{(w, v)}$ and $V(p) = \emptyset$.
2. The world $t$ has no $R_2$-successor, so $\square_2 p$ is true at $t$.
3. The only $R_1$-successor of $w$ is $t$, so $\square_1\square_2 p$ is true at $w$.
4. The world $v$ has the $R_1$-successor $t$, and $p$ is false at $t$, so $\square_1 p$ is false at $v$.
5. The only $R_2$-successor of $w$ is $v$, so $\square_2\square_1 p$ is false at $w$.
6. So the commutativity formula fails at $w$.
7. The proof of [[soundness|~d189ee4b]] works for each box separately, so every theorem of $\mathsf{K} \otimes \mathsf{K}$ is valid on every bimodal frame $(W, R_1, R_2)$.
8. Hence $\mathsf{K} \otimes \mathsf{K}$ does not prove $\square_1\square_2 p \to \square_2\square_1 p$.

## Remarks

The formula is valid on a frame when each path $w \mathrel{R_2} v \mathrel{R_1} t$ has a matching path $w \mathrel{R_1} u \mathrel{R_2} t$.
In step 1, the path $w \mathrel{R_2} v \mathrel{R_1} t$ has no matching path $w \mathrel{R_1} u \mathrel{R_2} t$, since $t$ has no $R_2$-predecessor.
Product frames, where the worlds are pairs and each relation moves one coordinate, satisfy both commutativity directions.
So the product logic $\mathsf{K} \times \mathsf{K}$ is a proper extension of the fusion $\mathsf{K} \otimes \mathsf{K}$, by interaction axioms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two boxes can talk about two kinds of steps, for example moves of two different players.
"First a move of player 1, then of player 2" need not give the same results as the opposite order.
In the three-world model, the two orders lead to different places, and the formula fails.
So the plain combination of two copies of $\mathsf{K}$ does not make the boxes commute.

### L2 human

1. At $w$, go by $R_1$ to $t$; there $\square_2 p$ holds, since $t$ has no $R_2$-successor.
2. At $w$, go by $R_2$ to $v$; there $\square_1 p$ fails, since $t$ lacks $p$.
3. So $\square_1\square_2 p$ holds at $w$ and $\square_2\square_1 p$ fails.

The typical use is to see that interaction between boxes must be added by axioms; the fusion adds none.
A trap: to refute a formula of the fusion, the model may use any two relations, with no link between them.

### L3 human

Fusions preserve many properties, by results of Kracht and Wolter and of Fine and Schurz.
Completeness, the finite model property and decidability transfer from the two components to the fusion.
Products behave very differently; for three or more components they can be undecidable, see [[modal model theory|SPQVNPZDTB]].
