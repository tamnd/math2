## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\R$.

1. Write $A = 3I + J$, where $J$ is the matrix with every entry $1$. Then $tI - A = (t - 3)I - J$.
2. By direct expansion, the [[characteristic polynomial|039HF58KQ5]] is $p_A(t) = t^3 - 12t^2 + 45t - 54$.
3. Second method, by the [[coefficients of the characteristic polynomial|0FRYPZ6D0X]]: $\tr A = 12$. Each principal $2 \times 2$ minor is $16 - 1 = 15$, and their sum is $45$. $\det A = 54$. This gives the same polynomial.
4. $p_A(t) = (t - 3)^2 (t - 6)$. Check: $(t^2 - 6t + 9)(t - 6) = t^3 - 12t^2 + 45t - 54$.
5. So the eigenvalue $3$ has [[algebraic multiplicity|0S7A0EMPF4]] $2$, and the eigenvalue $6$ has algebraic multiplicity $1$.
6. $A - 3I = J$ has rank $1$. By the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]], the [[eigenspace|0AYEPBQMAW]] $E_3 = \{v : v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0\}$ has dimension $2$. So the [[geometric multiplicity|06YSJVR356]] of $3$ is $2$.
7. Check on a basis of $E_3$: $A(1, -1, 0) = (3, -3, 0) = 3(1, -1, 0)$ and $A(1, 0, -1) = (3, 0, -3) = 3(1, 0, -1)$.
8. For $\lambda = 6$: $A(1, 1, 1) = (6, 6, 6) = 6(1, 1, 1)$. So $E_6 = \operatorname{span}\{(1, 1, 1)\}$.
9. $(A - 3I)(A - 6I) = J(J - 3I) = J^2 - 3J = 3J - 3J = 0$, because $J^2 = 3J$.
10. The proper monic divisors of $(t - 3)(t - 6)$ are $1$, $t - 3$ and $t - 6$. None of them annihilates $A$, because $I \ne 0$, $A - 3I = J \ne 0$ and $A - 6I \ne 0$.
11. So the [[minimal polynomial|0V58FPM3EQ]] is $m_A(t) = (t - 3)(t - 6) = t^2 - 9t + 18$, a proper divisor of $p_A$.

## Remarks

- Every eigenvalue has equal algebraic and geometric multiplicity, so $A$ is [[diagonalisable|07SQCNV233]].
- This agrees with the fact that $m_A$ has no repeated root.
- The eigenvectors of $3$ are orthogonal to $(1, 1, 1)$, as for every real symmetric matrix.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The matrix stretches the diagonal line through $(1, 1, 1)$ by $6$.
It stretches the whole plane $v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0$ by $3$.
So one eigenvalue has a plane of eigenvectors.

### L2 human

The typical lesson is that a repeated root of $p_A$ can still give a full eigenspace.
Here the root $3$ is double, and $E_3$ is a plane.
So the minimal polynomial drops the repeated factor.
A trap is to assume that $m_A = p_A$ whenever $p_A$ has a repeated root.
