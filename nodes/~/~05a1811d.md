## Proof

We use the one-sided rules of [[linear logic|H408H4WHBA]] for MLL.
The *degree* $|A|$ of a formula is its size, so $|A^{\perp}| = |A|$ and each immediate subformula of $A$ has a smaller degree.
The height $h(D)$ of a derivation $D$ is the largest number of rule applications on a branch, so an axiom has height $0$.

**Main lemma.**
Let $D_1$ be a cut-free derivation of $\vdash \Gamma, A$ and $D_2$ a cut-free derivation of $\vdash \Delta, A^{\perp}$.
Then $\vdash \Gamma, \Delta$ has a cut-free derivation.
We use induction on the measure $(|A|, h(D_1) + h(D_2))$ in the [[lexicographic order|0G77XFBV00]], a well-order on pairs of natural numbers.
The lemma is symmetric: exchange $D_1$ and $D_2$ and replace $A$ by $A^{\perp}$, and the measure stays the same.
The marked $A$ is *principal* in $D_1$ if the last rule of $D_1$ makes it.

Case 1: $D_1$ or $D_2$ is an axiom with the marked formula in it.

1. By symmetry, let $D_1$ be the axiom $\vdash A^{\perp}, A$, so $\Gamma = A^{\perp}$.
2. The goal $\vdash A^{\perp}, \Delta$ is the conclusion of $D_2$, which is cut-free.

Case 2: $A$ is not principal in $D_1$ (commutative case).

1. $D_1$ is not an axiom, by Case 1.
   It is not $\vdash 1$, since that sequent has one formula.
   So $D_1$ ends with $\otimes$, $\mathbin{\wp}$ or $\bot$ on a formula of $\Gamma$.
2. If the last rule is $\mathbin{\wp}$, then $\Gamma = \Gamma', B \mathbin{\wp} C$ with premise $\vdash \Gamma', B, C, A$.
3. The premise has a smaller height, and $A$ is the same, so the measure decreases.
   The induction hypothesis gives a cut-free derivation of $\vdash \Gamma', B, C, \Delta$.
4. The rule $\mathbin{\wp}$ gives $\vdash \Gamma', B \mathbin{\wp} C, \Delta$, which is the goal.
5. If the last rule is $\bot$, then $\Gamma = \Gamma', \bot$ with premise $\vdash \Gamma', A$.
   The induction hypothesis and the rule $\bot$ give the goal.
6. If the last rule is $\otimes$, then $\Gamma = \Gamma_1, \Gamma_2, B \otimes C$ with premises $\vdash \Gamma_1, B$ and $\vdash \Gamma_2, C$.
   The context split puts $A$ in one premise, say $\vdash \Gamma_1, B, A$.
7. The induction hypothesis on that premise gives $\vdash \Gamma_1, B, \Delta$.
   The rule $\otimes$ with $\vdash \Gamma_2, C$ gives $\vdash \Gamma_1, \Gamma_2, B \otimes C, \Delta$.

Case 3: $A^{\perp}$ is not principal in $D_2$.

1. This is Case 2 after the exchange of $D_1$ and $D_2$, by the symmetry of the lemma.

Case 4: $A$ is principal in $D_1$ and $A^{\perp}$ is principal in $D_2$ (key case).

1. The atoms and their negations are made only by axioms, so by Case 1 the formula $A$ is not atomic.
2. By symmetry, $A$ is $1$ or $A = B \otimes C$, since the duals of these are $\bot$ and $B^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} C^{\perp}$.
3. $A = 1$: $D_1$ is $\vdash 1$, so $\Gamma$ is empty.
   $D_2$ ends with $\bot$ with premise $\vdash \Delta$, which is the goal.
4. $A = B \otimes C$: $D_1$ has premises $\vdash \Gamma_1, B$ and $\vdash \Gamma_2, C$ with $\Gamma = \Gamma_1, \Gamma_2$.
   $D_2$ has premise $\vdash \Delta, B^{\perp}, C^{\perp}$.
5. $|B| < |A|$, so the induction hypothesis applies to $\vdash \Gamma_1, B$ and $\vdash \Delta, C^{\perp}, B^{\perp}$.
   It gives a cut-free derivation of $\vdash \Gamma_1, \Delta, C^{\perp}$.
6. $|C| < |A|$, so the induction hypothesis applies to $\vdash \Gamma_2, C$ and the result of step 5.
   It gives $\vdash \Gamma_1, \Gamma_2, \Delta$, which is the goal.

The cases cover all pairs: if Case 2 and Case 3 do not apply, both formulas are principal, and Case 1 or Case 4 applies.
This proves the main lemma.

**Part 1.**

1. Let $D$ be a derivation with $k$ cuts.
   We use induction on $k$.
2. If $k > 0$, choose a cut with no cut above it.
   Its premises have cut-free derivations.
3. The main lemma replaces this cut and the derivations above it by a cut-free derivation of the same sequent.
4. The result has $k - 1$ cuts, and the induction hypothesis applies.

**Part 2.**

1. Each of the rules $\otimes$, $\mathbin{\wp}$ and $\bot$ makes one occurrence of a connective or unit in its conclusion.
   The premises contain the rest of the conclusion and nothing else.
2. So, in a cut-free derivation, the sum of the sizes of the premises of such a rule is the size of the conclusion minus $1$.
3. By induction on the derivation, a cut-free derivation of a sequent of size $n$ has at most $n$ applications of these rules, since each one uses up one occurrence.
4. A derivation tree in which $r$ rules have at most two premises each has at most $r + 1$ leaves, and the leaves are the axioms.

**Part 3.**

1. In each rule other than cut, every formula of a premise is a formula of the conclusion or an immediate subformula of the principal formula.
2. By induction from the end sequent upward, every formula of a cut-free derivation is a subformula of a formula of the end sequent.

## Idea

A cut on $A$ moves up until both premises make $A$ and $A^{\perp}$ by their last rules.
There a cut on $B \otimes C$ against $B^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} C^{\perp}$ becomes a cut on $B$ and a cut on $C$.
The degree of the cut formula falls in the key case, and the sum of heights falls in the commutative cases.

## Variants

On [[proof nets|~ba2ae7a0]], the same reduction uses an axiom step and a multiplicative step, and it does not need the commutative cases.
A semantic proof uses [[phase semantics|~db29c154]]: completeness for cut-free derivations gives cut admissibility.
