## Proof

This proof gives parts 1 and 2 of the theorem.
We first treat propositional LK, then LJ, and then the extra cases of first-order logic.
We use the rules of LK as in the [[definition of the sequent calculus|PZX9J3VEMT]].

**Conventions.**
The height $h(D)$ of a derivation $D$ is the largest number of rule applications on a branch, so an axiom has height $0$.
The degree $|A|$ and the cut rank are as in the statement.
For a list $\Pi$ and a formula $A$, the list $\Pi_A$ is $\Pi$ with every occurrence of $A$ deleted.
In each logical rule and in cut, the formulas named $A$, $B$ or $C$ in the premises are the *active* formulas, and the other formulas form the *context*.

**A structural fact.**

1. Fact S: let $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$ have a derivation of rank at most $d$. Let each formula of $\Gamma$ occur in $\Gamma'$, and let each formula of $\Delta$ occur in $\Delta'$. Then $\Gamma' \Rightarrow \Delta'$ has a derivation of rank at most $d$.
2. Proof of Fact S: exchanges bring equal formulas together, and contractions leave one occurrence of each formula. Weakenings add the missing occurrences, and exchanges give the order of $\Gamma'$ and $\Delta'$.
3. These steps use no cut, so the rank does not change.

**The mix lemma.**
Let $A$ be a formula with $|A| = d$.
Let $D_0$ derive $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$ and $D_1$ derive $\Pi \Rightarrow \Lambda$, both with rank at most $d$.
Let $A$ occur in $\Delta$ and in $\Pi$.
Then $\Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda$ has a derivation of rank at most $d$.
This conclusion is the *mix* of the two sequents on $A$.
We prove the lemma by induction on $h(D_0) + h(D_1)$, for all such pairs at once, with $A$ fixed.
"The induction hypothesis" means the lemma for a pair with a smaller sum of heights.

*Case 1: $D_0$ is an axiom.*

4. Then $D_0$ is $A \Rightarrow A$, because $A$ occurs in its succedent. The target is $A, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Lambda$.
5. Each formula of $\Pi$ is $A$ or occurs in $\Pi_A$. So Fact S, applied to $D_1$, gives the target.

*Case 2: $D_1$ is an axiom.*

6. Then $D_1$ is $A \Rightarrow A$, and the target is $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta_A, A$. Fact S, applied to $D_0$, gives it.

*Case 3: neither is an axiom, and the last rule of $D_0$ is not a right logical rule with principal formula $A$.*

7. Subcase 3a: the last rule of $D_0$ is a structural rule other than cut. Let $D_0'$ derive its premise $\Gamma' \Rightarrow \Delta'$.
8. For weakening, contraction and exchange, each formula of $\Gamma'$ occurs in $\Gamma$, and each formula of $\Delta'$ occurs in $\Delta$.
9. If $A$ occurs in $\Delta'$, the induction hypothesis for $D_0'$ and $D_1$ gives $\Gamma', \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta'_A, \Lambda$. Each formula of $\Delta'_A$ occurs in $\Delta_A$, so Fact S gives the target.
10. If $A$ does not occur in $\Delta'$, then each formula of $\Delta'$ occurs in $\Delta_A$. Fact S, applied to $D_0'$, gives the target.
11. Subcase 3b: the last rule of $D_0$ is a logical rule or a cut. Let $S_i = \Gamma_i \Rightarrow \Delta_i$ be its premises, with derivations $D_0^i$.
12. For each $i$ we get $T_i = \Gamma_i, \Pi_A \Rightarrow (\Delta_i)_A, \Lambda$ with rank at most $d$. If $A$ occurs in $\Delta_i$, the induction hypothesis for $D_0^i$ and $D_1$ gives it. Otherwise Fact S, applied to $D_0^i$, gives it.
13. Let $U_i$ be $S_i$ with $\Pi_A$ added to the antecedent, $A$ deleted from the succedent context, and $\Lambda$ added to the succedent context. The active formulas stay.
14. Each formula of $T_i$ occurs in $U_i$, so Fact S gives $U_i$ with rank at most $d$.
15. The sequents $U_i$ have the form of premises of the same rule. For ${\wedge}$R and ${\vee}$L, both $U_i$ have the same context, because both $S_i$ have the same context.
16. We apply the same rule to the $U_i$ and get a sequent $V$. If the rule is a cut, its cut formula has degree less than $d$, so $V$ has rank at most $d$.
17. The antecedent of $V$ consists of formulas of $\Gamma$ and of $\Pi_A$. The succedent of $V$ consists of context formulas, which occur in $\Delta_A$ or $\Lambda$, and possibly a principal formula.
18. A principal formula on the right lies in $\Delta$ and is not $A$, by the assumption of Case 3. So it lies in $\Delta_A$.
19. Fact S, applied to $V$, gives the target $\Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda$.

*Case 4: neither is an axiom, the last rule of $D_0$ is a right logical rule with principal formula $A$, and the last rule of $D_1$ is not a left logical rule with principal formula $A$.*

20. We repeat steps 7 to 19 with the two sides exchanged. The induction hypothesis is used for $D_0$ and a premise derivation of $D_1$, so the sum of heights drops.
21. Here $U_i$ is a premise of $D_1$ with $\Gamma$ added to the antecedent context, $A$ deleted from the antecedent context, and $\Delta_A$ added to the succedent.
22. A principal formula on the left lies in $\Pi$ and is not $A$, so it lies in $\Pi_A$. Fact S again gives the target.

*Case 5: the last rule of $D_0$ is a right logical rule, and the last rule of $D_1$ is a left logical rule, both with principal formula $A$.*

23. Subcase $A = B \wedge C$. Then $D_0$ ends with ${\wedge}$R from $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta'', B$ and $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta'', C$, where $\Delta = \Delta'', A$.
24. $D_1$ ends with ${\wedge}$L from $E, \Pi'' \Rightarrow \Lambda$, where $\Pi = A, \Pi''$ and $E$ is $B$ or $C$. So $\Delta_A = \Delta''_A$ and $\Pi_A = \Pi''_A$.
25. We get $\Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda, E$. If $A$ occurs in $\Delta''$, use the induction hypothesis for the premise $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta'', E$ and $D_1$, then Fact S. Otherwise use Fact S on that premise.
26. In step 25 the formula $E$ is not deleted, because $|E| < |A|$ and so $E \neq A$.
27. We get $E, \Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda$. If $A$ occurs in $\Pi''$, use the induction hypothesis for $D_0$ and the premise $E, \Pi'' \Rightarrow \Lambda$, then Fact S. Otherwise use Fact S on that premise.
28. A cut on $E$ joins steps 25 and 27 into $\Gamma, \Pi_A, \Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda, \Delta_A, \Lambda$. Since $|E| < d$, the rank stays at most $d$.
29. Fact S, with contractions, gives the target.
30. Subcase $A = B \vee C$. Now $D_0$ ends with ${\vee}$R from $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta'', E$, with $E$ equal to $B$ or $C$. $D_1$ ends with ${\vee}$L, and we use its premise $E, \Pi'' \Rightarrow \Lambda$. Steps 25 to 29 apply unchanged.
31. Subcase $A = \neg B$. $D_0$ ends with ${\neg}$R from $B, \Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta''$, and $D_1$ ends with ${\neg}$L from $\Pi'' \Rightarrow \Lambda, B$.
32. As in step 25, from the premise of $D_0$ we get $B, \Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda$.
33. As in step 27, from the premise of $D_1$ we get $\Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda, B$.
34. A cut on $B$, of degree less than $d$, and Fact S give the target.
35. Subcase $A = B \to C$. $D_0$ ends with ${\to}$R from $B, \Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta'', C$.
36. $D_1$ ends with ${\to}$L from $\Pi_1 \Rightarrow \Lambda_1, B$ and $C, \Pi_2 \Rightarrow \Lambda_2$. Here $\Pi = A, \Pi_1, \Pi_2$ and $\Lambda = \Lambda_1, \Lambda_2$.
37. As in step 25, from the premise of $D_0$ we get $B, \Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda, C$.
38. As in step 27, from the first premise of $D_1$ we get $\Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda, B$. From the second premise we get $C, \Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda$.
39. A cut on $C$ joins the sequent of step 37 with the second sequent of step 38. Fact S then gives $B, \Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Delta_A, \Lambda$.
40. A cut on $B$ joins the first sequent of step 38 with the sequent of step 39. Fact S gives the target.
41. Both cuts have degree less than $d$, so the rank stays at most $d$.

These cases cover all pairs, so the mix lemma holds.

**From the mix lemma to cut elimination for LK.**

42. Let $D$ have rank $r + 1$, so its cut formulas have degree at most $r$. We show that its end sequent has a derivation of rank at most $r$.
43. We use induction on the number $m$ of cuts in $D$ whose cut formula has degree $r$. If $m = 0$, the rank of $D$ is already at most $r$.
44. If $m \geq 1$, choose such a cut with no cut of degree $r$ above it. It infers $\Gamma, \Pi' \Rightarrow \Delta', \Lambda$ from $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta', C$ and $C, \Pi' \Rightarrow \Lambda$.
45. The derivations of the two premises have rank at most $r$, by the choice of the cut.
46. The mix lemma, with $A = C$ and $d = r$, gives $\Gamma, \Pi'_C \Rightarrow \Delta'_C, \Lambda$ with rank at most $r$.
47. Fact S gives $\Gamma, \Pi' \Rightarrow \Delta', \Lambda$ with rank at most $r$.
48. We replace the subderivation that ends with the chosen cut by this derivation. The new derivation has $m - 1$ cuts of degree $r$ and no cut of larger degree.
49. The induction hypothesis on $m$ gives a derivation of rank at most $r$.
50. By induction on the rank, every derivation can be transformed into a derivation of rank $0$, which has no cut. This is part 1 for propositional LK.

**The changes for LJ.**

51. In LJ the succedent $\Delta$ of $D_0$ contains $A$ and has at most one formula. So $\Delta$ is $A$, $\Delta_A$ is empty, and the target $\Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow \Lambda$ is an LJ sequent.
52. In Case 3, the rule of $D_0$ is a left rule, a structural rule or a cut, because a right rule would have principal formula $A$.
53. For ${\to}$L and for a cut in $D_0$, only the second premise has the succedent of the conclusion. We treat that premise as in steps 12 to 14.
54. For the first premise we use Fact S alone and add only $\Pi_A$ to its antecedent, even when its active formula is $A$.
55. In Case 4 nothing is added to the succedents, because $\Delta_A$ is empty.
56. In Case 5, we omit $\Lambda$ in steps 25, 32 and 37. For example step 37 becomes $B, \Gamma, \Pi_A \Rightarrow C$.
57. With these changes every sequent in the construction has at most one formula on the right. Steps 42 to 50 are unchanged, and they give part 2 for propositional LJ.

**The extra cases of first-order logic.**

58. Substitution lemma: if $D$ derives $S$, and the variable $y$ and the variables of a term $t$ are not eigenvariables of $D$, then $D[t/y]$ derives $S[t/y]$.
59. The derivation $D[t/y]$ has the same height and the same cut rank as $D$, since $|B[t/x]| = |B|$ for every formula $B$.
60. By renaming eigenvariables, every derivation becomes one in which no eigenvariable occurs in a given finite set of terms, with the same height and rank.
61. Generic cases: if the last rule of $D_0$ in Case 3 is ${\forall}$R or ${\exists}$L, then adding $\Pi_A$ and $\Lambda$ could break its eigenvariable condition. We first rename its eigenvariable in $D_0^i$ to a variable that occurs nowhere in $D_0$ and $D_1$.
62. Case 4 treats ${\forall}$R and ${\exists}$L in $D_1$ in the same way.
63. Principal case $A = \forall x\, B$: $D_0$ ends with ${\forall}$R from $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta'', B[y/x]$, and $D_1$ ends with ${\forall}$L from $B[t/x], \Pi'' \Rightarrow \Lambda$.
64. The variable $y$ is not free in $\Gamma$, $\Delta''$ or $\forall x\, B$. So the substitution lemma turns the premise derivation of $D_0$ into a derivation of $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta'', B[t/x]$ of the same height.
65. Steps 25 to 29 then apply with $E = B[t/x]$, and $|E| = |B| < d$.
66. Principal case $A = \exists x\, B$: the same, with the substitution applied to the premise derivation of $D_1$, which ends with ${\exists}$L.

This proves parts 1 and 2 of the theorem.

## Idea

A topmost cut of maximal degree is pushed upward until its cut formula is principal on both sides.
There it is replaced by cuts on the immediate subformulas, which have smaller degree.
The mix rule deletes all copies of the cut formula at once.
This avoids the problem of a contraction above the cut, which would otherwise duplicate the cut without making the derivations shorter.

## Variants

The [[proof by rank reduction|~9c2f7b72]] of Tait works with one-sided sequents of sets, so contraction is built in and mix is not needed.
That proof also gives the bound $2_r(h)$ on the height of the cut-free derivation.
Many textbooks avoid mix in a two-sided calculus with built-in contraction, such as Kleene's G3, and use the [[inversion lemma|~42f9e538]] and the admissibility of contraction instead.
For natural deduction, the analogous procedure is the reduction of detours in the [[normalization theorem|~977456e4]].
