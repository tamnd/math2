## Example

Students first meet tangent lines at circles.
A tangent to a circle meets it at exactly one point, and the circle lies on one side of it.
Many students form the [[concept image|~ce2f3661]]: "a tangent line touches the curve at one point and does not cross it".
The calculus definition of the [[tangent line|01R1HW3Y42]] to $y = f(x)$ at $x = a$ is the line through $(a, f(a))$ with slope $f'(a)$.
We test the image on $f(x) = x^3$.

1. At $a = 0$: $f'(x) = 3x^2$, so $f'(0) = 0$, and the tangent line is $y = 0$.
2. For $x < 0$ we have $x^3 < 0$, and for $x > 0$ we have $x^3 > 0$. So the curve crosses the tangent line at $0$. The image says "does not cross", and this fails.
3. At $a = 1$: $f(1) = 1$ and $f'(1) = 3$, so the tangent line is $y = 1 + 3(x - 1) = 3x - 2$.
4. Intersections: $x^3 = 3x - 2$ gives $x^3 - 3x + 2 = 0$, and $x^3 - 3x + 2 = (x - 1)^2 (x + 2)$.
5. Check the factorization: $(x - 1)^2 (x + 2) = (x^2 - 2x + 1)(x + 2) = x^3 - 3x + 2$.
6. So the tangent at $x = 1$ meets the curve again at $x = -2$, at the point $(-2, -8)$. The image says "touches at one point", and this fails.

The image agrees with the definition for circles and fails for $y = x^3$, so this is a conflict between a concept image and the concept definition.

## Remarks

The conflict also runs the other way.
The vertical line $x = 0$ meets the parabola $y = x^2$ at exactly one point, but it is not the tangent there: the tangent at $0$ is $y = 0$.
So "meets at one point" is neither necessary nor sufficient for tangency of a general curve.
Tall and Vinner (1981) discussed concept images in calculus with examples of this kind.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a circle, a tangent line touches the circle at one point and stays on one side.
Many students think this is what "tangent" means for any curve.
For the curve $y = x^3$, the tangent at $0$ is the flat line $y = 0$, and the curve crosses it.
The tangent at $(1, 1)$ cuts the curve again at $(-2, -8)$.
So the picture from circles does not fit all curves.

### L2 human

1. State the image and the definition side by side.
2. Choose a curve where they could differ: a curve with an inflection point, or a curve that bends back.
3. Compute the tangent by the definition.
4. Test each part of the image: "one point" and "does not cross".

A trap is to show only the picture of $y = x^3$.
A student may then call the line $y = 0$ "not really a tangent" and keep the image.
The computation of $f'(0) = 0$ ties the line to the definition.
