## Proof

We expand both sides in coordinates and show that they are the same polynomial.

1. Let $a = (a_1, a_2, a_3)$ and $b = (b_1, b_2, b_3)$. For $i < j$ write $m_{ij} = a_i b_j - a_j b_i$.
2. By the definition of the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]], $a \times b = (m_{23}, \, -m_{13}, \, m_{12})$.
3. Step 2 uses $a_3 b_1 - a_1 b_3 = -(a_1 b_3 - a_3 b_1) = -m_{13}$.
4. By the definition of the [[length|0XKBKGTCPC]], $\|a \times b\|^2 = m_{23}^2 + m_{13}^2 + m_{12}^2$, since $(-m_{13})^2 = m_{13}^2$.
5. Now the right side. By the definitions, $\|a\|^2 \|b\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{j=1}^{3} a_i^2 b_j^2$.
6. By the definition of the [[dot product|0S52TNNTH9]], $(a \cdot b)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{j=1}^{3} a_i b_i a_j b_j$.
7. The terms with $i = j$ are $a_i^2 b_i^2$ in both sums of steps 5 and 6. They cancel in the difference.
8. The terms with $i \neq j$ come in pairs $\{i, j\}$ with $i < j$. Each pair gives $a_i^2 b_j^2 + a_j^2 b_i^2$ in step 5.
9. Each pair gives $2 a_i b_i a_j b_j$ in step 6, since the terms for $(i, j)$ and $(j, i)$ are equal.
10. So $\|a\|^2 \|b\|^2 - (a \cdot b)^2 = \sum_{i < j} \left(a_i^2 b_j^2 - 2 a_i b_j a_j b_i + a_j^2 b_i^2\right)$.
11. Each bracket is a square: $a_i^2 b_j^2 - 2 a_i b_j a_j b_i + a_j^2 b_i^2 = (a_i b_j - a_j b_i)^2 = m_{ij}^2$.
12. The pairs with $i < j$ are $(1, 2)$, $(1, 3)$ and $(2, 3)$. So the right side is $m_{12}^2 + m_{13}^2 + m_{23}^2$.
13. This equals the left side of step 4. This proves the identity.

## Idea

Both sides are sums of terms $a_i^2 b_j^2$ and $a_i b_i a_j b_j$.
The diagonal terms cancel, and the remaining terms group into three perfect squares.
These squares are the squares of the components of $a \times b$.

## Variants

- The same computation, with the pairs $i < j$ running over all of $\{1, \dots, n\}$, proves Lagrange's identity in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
- With the [[vector triple product|03FTB26QYD]] and the cyclic symmetry of the scalar triple product: $\|a \times b\|^2 = (a \times b) \cdot (a \times b) = a \cdot (b \times (a \times b))$. Then $b \times (a \times b) = (b \cdot b)\, a - (b \cdot a)\, b$, and the dot product with $a$ gives $\|a\|^2 \|b\|^2 - (a \cdot b)^2$.
