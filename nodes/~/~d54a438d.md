## Example

Let $\delta(x, y, \vec{z})$ be a $\Delta_0$ formula, and let $w$ be a variable that does not occur in $\delta$.
We show that the $\Sigma_1$ formula $\exists x\, \exists y\, \delta$ is equivalent in $\mathfrak{N}$ to the formula
$$\exists w\, \exists x \le w\, \exists y \le w\; \delta(x, y, \vec{z}),$$
which has a single unbounded quantifier in front of a $\Delta_0$ formula.

1. The part $\exists x \le w\, \exists y \le w\, \delta$ is $\Delta_0$, by item 1 of the [[definition|~29cc9691]], since $w$ is a variable different from $x$ and $y$.
2. Fix values $\vec{c}$ of $\vec{z}$. Suppose $\mathfrak{N} \models \delta(a, b, \vec{c})$ for some $a, b \in \N$.
3. Put $w = \max(a, b)$. Then $a \le w$ and $b \le w$, so the right-hand formula holds with witnesses $w$, $a$ and $b$.
4. Conversely, witnesses $w$, $x$ and $y$ for the right-hand formula give $\mathfrak{N} \models \delta(x, y, \vec{c})$, so the left-hand formula holds.
5. So the two formulas define the same relation of $\vec{z}$ in $\mathfrak{N}$.

A concrete case is "$x$ is a sum of two squares", $\exists a\, \exists b\,(a \cdot a + b \cdot b = x)$.

6. Here the bound $w$ can even be replaced by $x$: if $a \cdot a + b \cdot b = x$, then $a \le a \cdot a \le x$ and $b \le x$.
7. So the formula is equivalent in $\mathfrak{N}$ to the $\Delta_0$ formula $\exists a \le x\, \exists b \le x\,(a \cdot a + b \cdot b = x)$.
8. For $x = 13$, the witnesses $a = 2$ and $b = 3$ give $4 + 9 = 13$. For $x = 7$, the bounded search over $a, b \le 7$ finds nothing, since $7$ is not a sum of two squares.

## Remarks

The example shows that the number of unbounded existential quantifiers in a $\Sigma_1$ formula does not matter: one suffices.
The same argument merges any block $\exists x_1 \cdots \exists x_k$, with $w = \max(x_1, \ldots, x_k)$.
In a theory $T$, the equivalence needs $T \vdash \forall x\, \forall y\, \exists w\,(x \le w \land y \le w)$; $\mathsf{PA}$ proves this with $w = x + y$.
Step 6 shows that some $\Sigma_1$ formulas are even $\Delta_0$ in $\mathfrak{N}$; the witnesses are bounded by the free variable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"There are $a$ and $b$ with some property" is the same as "there is a bound $w$, and below it there are $a$ and $b$ with the property".
For if $a$ and $b$ exist, the larger of them is such a bound.
So two searches can be done as one search for $w$, followed by a finite check below $w$.

### L2 human

We merge the quantifiers in $\exists x\, \exists y\,(x \cdot y = \overline{12} \land \overline{1} < x \land x < y)$.

1. The matrix is $\Delta_0$, since it has no quantifiers.
2. The merged formula is $\exists w\, \exists x \le w\, \exists y \le w\,(x \cdot y = \overline{12} \land \overline{1} < x \land x < y)$.
3. In $\mathfrak{N}$, the pair $x = 2$, $y = 6$ works, and $w = 6$ bounds it.
4. The pair $x = 3$, $y = 4$ also works, with $w = 4$.

The typical use of merging is to put every $\Sigma_1$ formula in the form $\exists w\, \delta$ with $\delta \in \Delta_0$, as in the [[representability theorem|~d5e5ada1]].
A trap is the theory: in $\mathsf{Q}$ the bound $w$ may not be provably available, so the equivalence is a fact about $\mathfrak{N}$ or $\mathsf{PA}$.
