## Example

In [[natural deduction|PAQ4ZX2TX1]], conjunction has one introduction rule and two elimination rules.

- $\wedge$I: from $A$ and $B$ infer $A \wedge B$.
- $\wedge$E$_1$: from $A \wedge B$ infer $A$.
- $\wedge$E$_2$: from $A \wedge B$ infer $B$.

We check the two conditions of [[harmony|XJSQVK0G71]].

1. Local soundness. Let $D_1$ derive $A$ and $D_2$ derive $B$. Apply $\wedge$I to get $A \wedge B$, and then $\wedge$E$_1$ to get $A$.
2. This derivation reduces to $D_1$ alone. The open assumptions of $D_1$ are among those of the whole derivation, so nothing new is assumed.
3. In the same way, $\wedge$I followed by $\wedge$E$_2$ reduces to $D_2$.
4. Local completeness. Let $D$ derive $A \wedge B$. Apply $\wedge$E$_1$ to $D$ to get $A$, and $\wedge$E$_2$ to a second copy of $D$ to get $B$.
5. Then $\wedge$I gives $A \wedge B$ again, with the same open assumptions as $D$. This is the expansion of $D$.

A concrete instance: from the assumptions $p$ and $q$, the rule $\wedge$I gives $p \wedge q$, and $\wedge$E$_1$ gives $p$.
By step 2 this reduces to the one-line derivation consisting of the assumption $p$.

## Remarks

Step 2 is the conjunction case of the detour reduction in the [[normalization theorem|~977456e4]].
The elimination rules take out exactly the two pieces that the introduction rule puts in.
If $\wedge$E$_2$ were dropped, step 1 would still work, but step 4 would fail, since no derivation could recover $B$ from $A \wedge B$.
Under the [[Curry-Howard correspondence|A77FER1Q5H]], step 2 is the reduction of the first projection of a pair, and step 5 is the eta rule for pairs.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To prove "$A$ and $B$", we prove $A$ and we prove $B$.
From "$A$ and $B$" we may conclude $A$, or $B$.
If we prove "$A$ and $B$" only to take $A$ back out, we could have stopped at the proof of $A$.
That is the balance that harmony asks for.

### L2 human

The check is mechanical: write each introduction followed by each elimination, and find the derivation without the detour.
Then write the eliminations on a proof of the compound formula, and rebuild it with the introduction.
A trap is to forget the second check: it is the one that fails when an elimination rule is missing.
