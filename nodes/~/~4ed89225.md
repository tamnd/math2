## Definition

A unique factorisation domain is an [[integral domain|~6bd4a5ff]] $R$ with these two properties.

1. Every nonzero element $a \in R$ that is not a unit is a product $a = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_n$ of [[irreducible elements|~b61be5cb]], with $n \ge 1$.
2. The factorization is unique up to order and units. If $p_1 \cdots p_n = q_1 \cdots q_m$ with all $p_i, q_j$ irreducible, then $n = m$. Also, there is a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1, \dots, n\}$ such that $p_i$ and $q_{\sigma(i)}$ are [[associates|~b493b42a]] for each $i$.

## Remarks

The integers are a unique factorisation domain: this is the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.
Every [[principal ideal domain is a unique factorisation domain|~11d1b4a3]], and so is every [[polynomial ring over a unique factorisation domain|~0e18a953]].
The ring $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ is [[not a unique factorisation domain|~b70e58ea]].
In a unique factorisation domain, [[irreducible elements are prime|~ba488fa2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the integers, $60 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$, and every other factorization into primes uses the same primes, up to order and signs.
A unique factorisation domain is a ring where this works for every nonzero nonunit.
Units like $-1$ do not count as a different factorization.
