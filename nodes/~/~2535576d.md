## Summary

A function $f$ is continuous at a point $x = a$ when three things hold together. First, [[the limit of the function|0PKRYB8ATM]] $f$ exists as $x \to a$. Second, $f$ has a value at $a$. Third, that limit is equal to the value $f(a)$.

This is the limit form of [[continuity at a point|0KRMJRDRZT]]. It says that the value at $a$ matches what the nearby values approach.
