## Example

Both [[strong Kleene logic|SZHA10A9X7]] K3 and the [[logic of paradox|~655488d6]] LP use the strong Kleene tables on the values $0, \tfrac12, 1$, with $v(\neg A) = 1 - v(A)$.
K3 designates $1$; LP designates $1$ and $\tfrac12$.
In [[multiple-conclusion|3AJTDAP93H]] form, $\Gamma \vDash_{\mathrm{K3}} \Delta$ if every valuation that gives value $1$ to all of $\Gamma$ gives value $1$ to some element of $\Delta$.
$\Gamma \vDash_{\mathrm{LP}} \Delta$ is the same with "value $1$" replaced by "a value in $\set{1, \tfrac12}$".
Write $\neg\Gamma := \set{\neg A : A \in \Gamma}$.

Claim: for all sets $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$, empty or not, $\Gamma \vDash_{\mathrm{K3}} \Delta$ if and only if $\neg\Delta \vDash_{\mathrm{LP}} \neg\Gamma$.

1. For every valuation $v$ and formula $A$: $v(\neg A) \in \set{1, \tfrac12}$ if and only if $v(A) \in \set{0, \tfrac12}$, that is, $v(A) \neq 1$.
2. By 1, $\neg\Delta \vDash_{\mathrm{LP}} \neg\Gamma$ says: for every $v$, if $v(D) \neq 1$ for all $D \in \Delta$, then $v(G) \neq 1$ for some $G \in \Gamma$.
3. The contrapositive of this condition, for each $v$, is: if $v(G) = 1$ for all $G \in \Gamma$, then $v(D) = 1$ for some $D \in \Delta$.
4. This is the definition of $\Gamma \vDash_{\mathrm{K3}} \Delta$.

A check with empty sides.

5. $p, \neg p \vDash_{\mathrm{K3}} \emptyset$: if $v(p) = 1$, then $v(\neg p) = 0$, so no valuation gives $1$ to both.
6. The claim gives $\emptyset \vDash_{\mathrm{LP}} \neg p, \neg\neg p$.
7. Direct check: if $v(p) \neq 1$, then $v(\neg p) \in \set{1, \tfrac12}$; if $v(p) = 1$, then $v(\neg\neg p) = 1$.

## Remarks

The single-conclusion form of this duality in [[Tautologies and duality of LP and K3|~efa1f8f6]] needs a disjunction and at least one premise.
The multiple-conclusion form needs neither, because a set of conclusions already acts as a disjunction.
Step 5 is the multiple-conclusion form of explosion in K3, and step 6 is excluded middle in LP; the duality exchanges them.

## Explanations

### L1 human

K3 and LP use the same three truth values and the same tables.
They differ only in which values count as "good enough".
With several premises and several conclusions, one logic is the mirror image of the other under negation.

### L2 human

The proof uses one fact: $\neg A$ is designated in LP exactly when $A$ is not designated in K3.
Then the validity condition for LP becomes the contrapositive of the condition for K3.
A trap is to forget that the sides swap: premises of K3 become negated conclusions of LP.
