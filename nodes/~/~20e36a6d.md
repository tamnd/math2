## Example

Statement: if $m$ and $n$ are even [[integers|0C4JS9RF5M]], then $m + n$ is even.
Definition used: an integer $x$ is even when $x = 2k$ for some integer $k$.
We give the [[proof|0B94TKFQJX]] and check that each step has a reason.

1. Let $m$ and $n$ be even integers. This is the hypothesis of the statement.
2. Since $m$ is even, there is an integer $a$ with $m = 2a$. The reason is the definition of an even integer.
3. Since $n$ is even, there is an integer $b$ with $n = 2b$. The reason is the same definition, with a new letter, because $n$ need not equal $m$.
4. Then $m + n = 2a + 2b$. The reason is substitution of steps 2 and 3.
5. Then $2a + 2b = 2(a + b)$. The reason is the distributive law of the integers.
6. The number $a + b$ is an integer. The reason is that the sum of two integers is an integer.
7. So $m + n = 2c$ with the integer $c = a + b$, and $m + n$ is even by the definition. This is the conclusion.

## Remarks

The proof is a [[direct proof|0NTF6DMVAP]]: it starts from the hypothesis and reaches the conclusion through definitions and algebra.
It shows the three parts that each proof has: the starting points (the hypothesis and the definition), the steps with their reasons, and the conclusion.
The proof covers every pair of even integers at once, because steps 2 to 7 use only the form $2a$ and $2b$, not particular values.

Small changes give related statements with the same kind of proof.
With $m = 2a + 1$ and $n = 2b + 1$, the sum is $2(a + b + 1)$, so the sum of two odd integers is even.
With $m = 2a$ and $n = 2b + 1$, the sum is $2(a + b) + 1$, so the sum of an even integer and an odd integer is odd.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An even number is a number of the form two times a whole number, such as $8 = 2 \times 4$.
Take two even numbers, say two times $a$ and two times $b$.
Their sum is two times $a$ plus two times $b$, which is two times the number $a + b$.
So the sum is again two times a whole number, and it is even.
The argument does not depend on the values of $a$ and $b$, so it works for all even numbers.

### L2 human

We use the same method to prove that the sum of three even integers is even.

1. Let $m = 2a$, $n = 2b$ and $p = 2c$ with integers $a$, $b$ and $c$.
2. Then $m + n + p = 2a + 2b + 2c = 2(a + b + c)$.
3. The number $a + b + c$ is an integer, so $m + n + p$ is even.

The typical use of the method is to unfold a definition in the hypothesis, compute, and fold the definition back in the conclusion.
A trap is to write $m = 2k$ and $n = 2k$ with the same $k$.
That argument proves the statement only for $m = n$, so it misses almost all cases.
