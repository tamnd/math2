## Example

We compare the fraction $\frac{22}{7}$ with the number [[π|0C1VYVNV2N]] and choose the correct sign.

1. Long division gives the [[decimal expansion|0VM8B2SB4P]] $\frac{22}{7} = 3.142857142857\ldots$, with the block $142857$ repeated.
2. The decimal expansion of $\pi$ begins $3.141592653\ldots$.
3. The two numbers agree in the first three digits $3.14$ and differ in the fourth digit, so $\frac{22}{7} - \pi \approx 0.001264$.
4. So $\frac{22}{7} \ne \pi$, and the true statement is $\frac{22}{7} \approx \pi$, with an error of about $0.04 \%$.
5. A stronger reason: $\pi$ is an [[irrational number|0DZXAXEJQ9]], so no fraction of integers equals $\pi$.
6. A precise statement with only exact signs is $3.141 < \pi < 3.142 < \frac{22}{7}$.

## Remarks

The example shows when the sign $\approx$ is correct and the sign $=$ is wrong.
The sign $\approx$ has no fixed precision, so step 6 gives the information in the form of inequalities, which are exact.
Archimedes proved $3 \tfrac{10}{71} < \pi < 3 \tfrac{1}{7}$, and $3 \tfrac{1}{7} = \frac{22}{7}$ is his upper bound.
A better fraction is $\frac{355}{113} = 3.14159292\ldots$, whose error is less than $3 \times 10^{-7}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Many people use $\frac{22}{7}$ for $\pi$ in school problems.
Divide $22$ by $7$ and you get $3.1428\ldots$, while $\pi$ is $3.1415\ldots$.
They are very close, but they are not the same number.
So we write $\frac{22}{7} \approx \pi$, with the wavy sign that means "about equal".

### L2 human

We find how much error the rounding $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$ causes in the area of a circle of radius $10$.

1. The exact area is $\pi r^2 = 100 \pi \approx 314.159$.
2. With $\frac{22}{7}$ the area is $\frac{2200}{7} \approx 314.286$.
3. The difference is about $0.126$, which is $100$ times the error $0.001264$ of step 3 above.
4. So an error in $\pi$ is multiplied by $r^2$, and the sign $\approx$ in the result has a larger error than the sign $\approx$ in the input.

The typical use of $\frac{22}{7}$ is a quick estimate by hand with three correct digits.
A trap: if a computation starts with $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$, the final answer must use $\approx$ too, and its digits after the third are not reliable.
