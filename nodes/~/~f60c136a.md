## Summary

Interpretability logic extends the modal language of [[provability logic|WJK0YA4GP2]] by a binary operator $\rhd$, where $p \rhd q$ is read as "$T + q$ is interpretable in $T + p$" for a fixed arithmetical theory $T$.
The basic system $\mathsf{IL}$ has as axioms all instances of tautologies, the axioms of $\mathsf{GL}$, and the five schemes J1 to J5 listed below, with the rules modus ponens and necessitation for $\square$.
Its extension $\mathsf{ILM}$ is the interpretability logic of [[Peano arithmetic|HACXBCM7SS]], and its extension $\mathsf{ILP}$ is the interpretability logic of finitely axiomatized theories such as $\mathsf{GB}$.
Interpretability logic matters because its decidable modal systems describe the general laws of relative interpretability between extensions of a theory.

## Motivation

A theory $U$ is interpretable in a theory $V$ when one can translate each symbol of $U$ into a formula of $V$, with a formula for the domain, so that $V$ proves the translation of each axiom of $U$.
An interpretation of $U$ in $V$ shows that $U$ is consistent if $V$ is, and it is the standard way to compare the strength of theories.
For extensions $T + A$ and $T + B$ of one arithmetical theory $T$, the statement "$T + B$ is interpretable in $T + A$" can be written in the language of $T$, just as provability can.
Provability is a special case: $T$ proves $\neg A$ exactly when $T + A$ interprets the inconsistent theory $T + \bot$.
Indeed, an inconsistent theory interprets every theory, and a theory that interprets $T + \bot$ proves the translation of $\bot$, which is $\bot$.
So it is natural to ask which laws the binary relation of interpretability obeys, in the same way as $\mathsf{GL}$ collects the laws of provability.

## Notation

The formulas are built from propositional variables, $\bot$, $\to$, the unary operator $\square$ and the binary operator $\rhd$.
We write $\lozenge \varphi$ for $\neg \square \neg \varphi$.
The operator $\rhd$ binds more weakly than $\neg$, $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\square$ and $\lozenge$, and more strongly than $\to$; so $p \wedge q \rhd r \to s$ means $((p \wedge q) \rhd r) \to s$.
An arithmetical realization $*$ for a theory $T$ sends $(\square \varphi)^*$ to $\mathrm{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi^* \urcorner)$ and $(\varphi \rhd \psi)^*$ to a formalization of "$T + \psi^*$ is interpretable in $T + \varphi^*$".
The axioms of $\mathsf{IL}$ beyond $\mathsf{GL}$ are:

- J1: $\square(p \to q) \to p \rhd q$;
- J2: $(p \rhd q) \wedge (q \rhd r) \to p \rhd r$;
- J3: $(p \rhd r) \wedge (q \rhd r) \to (p \vee q) \rhd r$;
- J4: $p \rhd q \to (\lozenge p \to \lozenge q)$;
- J5: $\lozenge p \rhd p$.

The two extra principles are M: $p \rhd q \to (p \wedge \square r) \rhd (q \wedge \square r)$, and P: $p \rhd q \to \square(p \rhd q)$.
Then $\mathsf{ILM} = \mathsf{IL} + \mathrm{M}$ and $\mathsf{ILP} = \mathsf{IL} + \mathrm{P}$.

## Remarks

### Examples

- $\mathsf{IL} \vdash \square p \leftrightarrow (\neg p \rhd \bot)$, so $\square$ can be defined from $\rhd$; see [[the definition of the box from interpretability|~870e536f]].
- $\mathsf{IL} \vdash p \rhd p$: necessitation gives $\square(p \to p)$ from the tautology $p \to p$, and J1 gives $p \rhd p$.
- A three-world Veltman model has $w \Vdash p \rhd q$ but $w \nVdash \square(p \to q)$, so the converse of J1 is not a theorem; see [[a Veltman model where interpretability is weaker than provable implication|~8ce224d1]].
- For $T = \mathsf{PA}$, the realization of J5 says that $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA} + A)$ interprets $\mathsf{PA} + A$.
- $\mathsf{IL} \vdash p \rhd q \to (p \vee q) \rhd q$, by J3 applied to $p \rhd q$ and $q \rhd q$.

### Non-examples

- A frame $(W, R, \{S_w\})$ in which $wRuRv$ but not $u S_w v$ is not a Veltman frame, and J5 can fail on it; see [[a frame without the Veltman condition|~741c64d3]].
- The formula $p \rhd q \to \square(p \to q)$ is not a theorem of $\mathsf{IL}$, $\mathsf{ILM}$ or $\mathsf{ILP}$, by the three-world model above.
- The principle P is not a theorem of $\mathsf{ILM}$. Take $wRw'Ru$, $wRu$, $wRv$, with $S_w$ the reflexive transitive closure of $\{(w', u), (u, v)\}$ on $R[w]$ and $S_{w'}$ the identity on $\{u\}$. The condition of M holds, but $u S_w v$ and not $u S_{w'} v$, so P fails by the frame condition below.
- The principle M is not a theorem of $\mathsf{ILP}$. Take $wRu$, $wRv$, $wRz$, $vRz$, with $S_w$ the reflexive transitive closure of $\{(u, v), (v, z)\}$ on $R[w]$ and $S_v$ the identity on $\{z\}$. The condition of P holds, but $u S_w v R z$ and not $u R z$.
- For $\mathsf{PA}$ the principle P is not valid, since P is not a theorem of $\mathsf{ILM}$ and $\mathsf{ILM}$ is the interpretability logic of $\mathsf{PA}$.

### Veltman semantics

A *Veltman frame* is a triple $(W, R, \{S_w\}_{w \in W})$ with the following properties.

1. $W$ is a finite non-empty set and $R$ is a transitive irreflexive relation on $W$, so that $(W, R)$ is a frame of $\mathsf{GL}$. Some authors allow infinite $W$ with $R$ transitive and conversely well-founded.
2. For each $w$, $S_w$ is a relation on $R[w] = \{u : wRu\}$ that is reflexive on $R[w]$ and transitive.
3. If $wRu$ and $uRv$, then $u S_w v$.

A *Veltman model* adds a valuation, and truth is defined as in [[Kripke semantics|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]], with one new clause:
$$w \Vdash \varphi \rhd \psi \iff \text{for every } u \text{ with } wRu \text{ and } u \Vdash \varphi \text{ there is } v \text{ with } u S_w v \text{ and } v \Vdash \psi.$$
The idea is that $S_w$ links a world $u$ to the worlds $v$ that "the theory at $w$" can reach from $u$ by an interpretation.

**Soundness.** Every theorem of $\mathsf{IL}$ is true at every world of every Veltman model.

1. The axioms of $\mathsf{GL}$ hold, because $(W, R)$ is a finite transitive irreflexive frame; see [[Kripke completeness of GL|~d95bfe82]].
2. J1: let $w \Vdash \square(p \to q)$, $wRu$ and $u \Vdash p$. Then $u \Vdash q$, and $u S_w u$ by reflexivity, so $v = u$ works.
3. J2: from $u \Vdash p$ we get $v$ with $u S_w v$ and $v \Vdash q$. Since $v \in R[w]$, we get $z$ with $v S_w z$ and $z \Vdash r$, and transitivity gives $u S_w z$.
4. J3: if $wRu$ and $u \Vdash p \vee q$, then $u \Vdash p$ or $u \Vdash q$, and in each case one hypothesis gives the needed $v$.
5. J4: if $w \Vdash p \rhd q$ and $w \Vdash \lozenge p$, take $u \in R[w]$ with $u \Vdash p$, then $v$ with $u S_w v$ and $v \Vdash q$. Since $v \in R[w]$, $w \Vdash \lozenge q$.
6. J5: let $wRu$ and $u \Vdash \lozenge p$. Take $v$ with $uRv$ and $v \Vdash p$. Property 3 gives $u S_w v$.
7. Modus ponens and necessitation preserve truth at all worlds of a model, as in ordinary Kripke semantics.

### Frame conditions of M and P

**M.** A Veltman frame validates M if and only if $u S_w v R z$ implies $u R z$.

1. Assume the condition, and let $w \Vdash p \rhd q$, $wRu$ and $u \Vdash p \wedge \square r$.
2. Take $v$ with $u S_w v$ and $v \Vdash q$. For each $z$ with $vRz$, the condition gives $uRz$, so $z \Vdash r$. So $v \Vdash q \wedge \square r$.
3. Conversely, assume $u S_w v$, $vRz$ and not $uRz$. Make $p$ true only at $u$, $q$ true only at $v$, and $r$ true everywhere except at $z$.
4. Then $w \Vdash p \rhd q$: the only world in $R[w]$ where $p$ holds is $u$, and $u S_w v$ with $v \Vdash q$.
5. Also $u \Vdash p \wedge \square r$, because $z$ is not an $R$-successor of $u$.
6. The only world where $q$ holds is $v$, and $v \nVdash \square r$ since $vRz$. So $w \nVdash (p \wedge \square r) \rhd (q \wedge \square r)$, and M fails at $w$.

**P.** A Veltman frame validates P if and only if $w R w' R u S_w v$ implies $u S_{w'} v$.

1. Assume the condition, and let $w \Vdash p \rhd q$ and $wRw'$. Let $w' R u$ and $u \Vdash p$.
2. Transitivity gives $wRu$, so there is $v$ with $u S_w v$ and $v \Vdash q$. The condition gives $u S_{w'} v$. So $w' \Vdash p \rhd q$.
3. Conversely, assume $w R w' R u S_w v$ and not $u S_{w'} v$. Make $p$ true only at $u$ and $q$ true only at $v$.
4. Then $w \Vdash p \rhd q$, because $u \in R[w]$ by transitivity, $u$ is the only $p$-world, and $u S_w v$.
5. At $w'$, the world $u$ satisfies $p$, but its only possible partner $v$ has not $u S_{w'} v$. So $w' \nVdash p \rhd q$ and $w \nVdash \square(p \rhd q)$.

### Completeness results

The following results are cited; we do not prove them here.

- Modal completeness: $\mathsf{IL}$, $\mathsf{ILM}$ and $\mathsf{ILP}$ are complete for finite Veltman frames, the last two for the frames with the conditions above (de Jongh and Veltman).
- Arithmetical completeness for $\mathsf{PA}$: $\mathsf{ILM}$ is the interpretability logic of $\mathsf{PA}$ (Shavrukov and, independently, Berarducci).
- Arithmetical completeness for finitely axiomatized theories: $\mathsf{ILP}$ is the interpretability logic of finitely axiomatized theories that contain enough arithmetic, such as [[Gödel-Bernays set theory|JTPZ1HZ9KK]] $\mathsf{GB}$ (Visser).

Together with finiteness of the frames, the modal completeness results make $\mathsf{IL}$, $\mathsf{ILM}$ and $\mathsf{ILP}$ decidable.

### The arithmetical meaning of J5

Let $T = \mathsf{PA}$ and let $A$ be a sentence.
The realization of $\lozenge p$ is $\neg \mathrm{Prov}_{\mathsf{PA}}(\ulcorner \neg A \urcorner)$, which is the [[consistency statement|4NBAX3VRQ7]] $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA} + A)$.
So J5 says: $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA} + A)$ interprets $\mathsf{PA} + A$.
This is the formalized [[Henkin construction|RY8KRKR097]]: inside $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA} + A)$ one can define a model of $\mathsf{PA} + A$ by arithmetical formulas, and that model gives the interpretation.
We cite this construction and do not reproduce it here.

### History

De Jongh and Veltman gave the modal semantics now named after Veltman and proved modal completeness for the main systems.
Visser found the logic of finitely axiomatized theories, and Berarducci and Shavrukov found, independently, the logic of $\mathsf{PA}$.
De Jongh and Visser showed that the main interpretability logics have explicit fixed points, as $\mathsf{GL}$ has.

### Common errors

- Reading $p \rhd q$ as "$p$ interprets $q$": the theory with $q$ is interpreted in the theory with $p$, so the direction goes from $q$ into $p$.
- Treating $p \rhd q$ as $\square(p \to q)$: J1 gives only one direction, and the three-world model shows that the converse fails.
- Forgetting the condition "$wRuRv$ implies $u S_w v$" in a Veltman frame: without it J5 can fail.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some theories in mathematics can imitate other theories: one can define, inside the first theory, objects that behave like the objects of the second.
For example, set theory can imitate arithmetic, because one can build numbers from sets.
We then say that the second theory is interpretable in the first.
Interpretability logic writes "$q$ is interpretable in $p$" as $p \rhd q$, and studies the general rules of this relation.
One rule is: if $p$ proves $q$, then $q$ is interpretable in $p$.
Another rule is: anything is interpretable in itself.

### L2 human

We check $p \rhd q$ at a world of a small Veltman model.

1. Let $W = \{w, u, v\}$, $wRu$, $wRv$, and $S_w$ the identity on $\{u, v\}$ together with the pair $(u, v)$.
2. Let $p$ be true only at $u$ and $q$ only at $v$.
3. The $R$-successors of $w$ where $p$ holds: only $u$.
4. For $u$ we need some $x$ with $u S_w x$ and $x \Vdash q$; the world $x = v$ works.
5. So $w \Vdash p \rhd q$.

The typical use is to refute a formula: build a small Veltman model where it fails, and use soundness.
A trap: the witness $v$ is found through $S_w$, the relation that belongs to $w$, and not through $R$; changing the world $w$ changes the relation.

### L3 human

Interpretability logic extends the programme of $\mathsf{GL}$ from one unary operator to a binary one.
Its arithmetical completeness theorems are Solovay-type results: $\mathsf{ILM}$ for $\mathsf{PA}$ uses the Orey-Hájek characterization of interpretability over $\mathsf{PA}$ by $\Pi^0_1$ conservativity, and $\mathsf{ILP}$ for finitely axiomatized theories uses that interpretability between such theories is provable when it holds.
Related systems are Japaridze's logic $\mathsf{TOL}$ of tolerance and the logics of $\Pi^0_1$ conservativity.
A long-standing open question asks for the interpretability logic of all reasonable arithmetical theories, the logic of the principles that hold for every such theory.
On the algebraic side, one adds a binary operation to a [[Magari algebra|~90876855]], in the same way as $\mathsf{IL}$ adds $\rhd$ to $\mathsf{GL}$.
