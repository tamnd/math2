## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring with identity element and let $M$ be a left [[module|~4b7b3b49]] over $R$.
The module $M$ is *finitely generated* when there are $x_1, \dots, x_n \in M$ such that each $x \in M$ is
$$x = r_1 x_1 + \cdots + r_n x_n \qquad \text{for some } r_1, \dots, r_n \in R.$$

## Remarks

Over a field, the finitely generated modules are the finite-dimensional vector spaces.
$\Z^n$ and $\Z / n\Z$ are finitely generated over $\Z$; the group $\Q$ is not.
Over a Noetherian ring, each submodule of a finitely generated module is finitely generated.
The finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain are classified by the structure theorem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A module is finitely generated when finitely many elements produce all others by linear combinations.
This is the module analogue of a finite-dimensional vector space.
Unlike in vector spaces, the generators need not be independent.
