## Statement

Let $m$, $n$ and $p$ be [[natural numbers|0MWKJ44V5A]] (that is, nonnegative integers), and suppose that $m \neq 0$.
Then the power $m^{n+p}$ is the [[product|06J5DE801P]] of the powers $m^n$ and $m^p$:

$$m^{n+p} = m^n \cdot m^p.$$

## Remarks

The condition $m \neq 0$ avoids the case $0^0$, where a convention is needed.
Counting factors gives the idea: $m^{n+p}$ is a product of $n+p$ copies of $m$, and these split into $n$ copies and $p$ copies.
