## Summary

The formula classes $\Delta_0$, $\Sigma_n$ and $\Pi_n$ sort the [[formulas|HYJ10AF6RT]] of the [[language of arithmetic|~f3b9e2e9]] by the shape of their quantifiers.
A $\Delta_0$ formula has only bounded quantifiers $\forall x < t$ and $\exists x < t$.
A $\Sigma_n$ formula is a block of $n$ alternating unbounded quantifiers, starting with $\exists$, in front of a $\Delta_0$ formula; a $\Pi_n$ formula is the same with $\forall$ first.
The [[definition page|~29cc9691]] gives the exact clauses.
The classes are syntactic, and they measure how hard it is to check a formula in $\mathfrak{N}$: $\Delta_0$ formulas define decidable relations, and $\Sigma_1$ formulas define exactly the computably enumerable relations.
They are the formula side of the [[arithmetical hierarchy|7CVJCQKY41]] of sets.

## Motivation

To check a $\Delta_0$ sentence in $\mathfrak{N}$, a finite search suffices, since every quantifier ranges below a known number.
To check a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, a search finds a witness; for a false one, the search never ends.
So $\Sigma_1$ sentences are the ones whose truth can be confirmed by a finite computation, and $\Pi_1$ sentences are the ones whose falsity can.
This matches the strength of weak theories: [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]] proves every true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, by [[Σ1-completeness|~1e1241ee]], but not every true $\Pi_1$ sentence.
The classes also measure induction: the [[fragments|~1d4194bd]] $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n$ of [[Peano arithmetic|~b3b2d1f3]] restrict the induction schema to $\Sigma_n$ formulas.
So a single scale of quantifier complexity organizes computability, provability and induction.

## Notation

$\Sigma_0 = \Pi_0 = \Delta_0$.
Some books write $\Sigma^0_n$ for the class of formulas; in these pages $\Sigma^0_n$ is the class of sets of numbers defined in $\mathfrak{N}$ by a $\Sigma_n$ formula.
For a theory $T$, a formula is $\Sigma_n$ in $T$ when $T$ proves it equivalent to a $\Sigma_n$ formula, and it is $\Delta_n$ in $T$ when it is both $\Sigma_n$ in $T$ and $\Pi_n$ in $T$.
"In $\mathfrak{N}$" means the same with truth in $\mathfrak{N}$ in place of provability in $T$.
Some books bound quantifiers by $\le$ instead of $<$; the two choices give the same class $\Delta_0$ up to logical equivalence.

## Remarks

### Examples

- $\mathrm{Div}(x, y)$, $\mathrm{Even}(x)$ and $\mathrm{Prime}(x)$ are $\Delta_0$ formulas; [[this example|~c39421ef]] writes them out.
- Goldbach's conjecture is a $\Pi_1$ sentence, and the infinitude of primes is a $\Pi_2$ sentence; [[this example|~58655f64]] gives both.
- Two unbounded existential quantifiers can be merged into one, so $\exists x\, \exists y\, \delta$ is equivalent in $\mathfrak{N}$ to a $\Sigma_1$ formula with one quantifier; [[this example|~d54a438d]] checks it.
- The formula $\exists y\,(y \cdot y = x)$, "$x$ is a square", is $\Sigma_1$. It is equivalent in $\mathfrak{N}$ to the $\Delta_0$ formula $\exists y \le x\,(y \cdot y = x)$, because $y \le y \cdot y$ for every $y \in \N$.

### Non-examples

- The formula $\forall u < y\, \exists z\, \theta(u, z)$ with $\theta \in \Delta_0$ is not in the class $\Sigma_1$, since an unbounded quantifier lies inside a bounded one; [[this non-example|~6d00eafb]] shows that it is still $\Sigma_1$ in $\mathfrak{N}$.
- The formula $\exists x\, \delta_1 \land \forall y\, \delta_2$, with $\delta_1, \delta_2 \in \Delta_0$, $y$ not free in $\delta_1$ and $x$ not free in $\delta_2$, lies in no class $\Sigma_n$ or $\Pi_n$ as written, since its quantifiers are not in front. Its prenex form $\exists x\, \forall y\,(\delta_1 \land \delta_2)$ is $\Sigma_2$.

### Basic properties

- Every formula is logically equivalent to a formula in [[prenex normal form|CS82V969DE]], and hence to a $\Sigma_n$ or $\Pi_n$ formula for some $n$, with bounded quantifiers treated as unbounded if needed.
- The negation of a $\Sigma_n$ formula is logically equivalent to a $\Pi_n$ formula, and conversely.
- For $n \ge 1$, in $\mathfrak{N}$, $\Sigma_n$ is closed under $\land$, $\lor$, $\exists x$ and bounded quantifiers, up to equivalence. $\Pi_n$ is closed under $\land$, $\lor$, $\forall x$ and bounded quantifiers.
- $\Sigma_n \cup \Pi_n \subseteq \Delta_{n+1}$ in $\mathfrak{N}$, so the classes increase with $n$.
- A $\Delta_0$ formula defines a decidable relation on $\N$, by a finite search on the bounds.
- A relation on $\N$ is defined in $\mathfrak{N}$ by a $\Sigma_1$ formula exactly when it is [[computably enumerable|TN9G24VABN]].
- The $\Sigma_n$ sets form a strictly increasing chain, by [[Post's theorem|NYJX5QXX1T]].

### History

Kleene (1943) and Mostowski (1947) introduced the classification of arithmetical sets by quantifier prefixes.
The role of bounded quantifiers was made central by Smullyan's rudimentary relations (1961) and by the later study of bounded arithmetic.

### Common errors

- To confuse the formula class $\Sigma_n$ with the set class $\Sigma^0_n$. A formula belongs to $\Sigma_n$ by its shape; a set belongs to $\Sigma^0_n$ when some $\Sigma_n$ formula defines it.
- To read "true $\Sigma_1$ sentence" as "provable". True means true in $\mathfrak{N}$.
- To call a formula $\Sigma_1$ because it is equivalent in $\mathfrak{N}$ to one. Equivalence in $\mathfrak{N}$ may fail in a theory $T$, so "$\Sigma_1$ in $T$" depends on $T$.
- To use a bound $t$ that contains the bound variable, as in $\exists x < x + 1$. Such a quantifier is not bounded.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some statements about numbers can be checked by looking at finitely many cases: "$91$ is prime" only needs a check of the numbers up to $91$.
Formulas of this kind are called $\Delta_0$.
A statement like "there is a number with property $P$" can be confirmed by finding one example, but a failed search never ends.
Such statements are $\Sigma_1$.
A statement like "every number has property $P$" can be refuted by one counterexample, and it is $\Pi_1$.
Putting more "there is" and "for all" in front gives the higher classes $\Sigma_2$, $\Pi_2$ and so on.

### L2 human

We classify the sentence $\forall x\, \exists y\,(x < y \land \mathrm{Prime}(y))$, "there are infinitely many primes".

1. $\mathrm{Prime}(y)$ is $\Delta_0$, since all its quantifiers are bounded by $y$.
2. $x < y$ is atomic, so $x < y \land \mathrm{Prime}(y)$ is $\Delta_0$.
3. $\exists y\,(\ldots)$ puts one unbounded existential quantifier in front, so it is $\Sigma_1$.
4. $\forall x\, \exists y\,(\ldots)$ puts a universal quantifier in front of a $\Sigma_1$ formula, so it is $\Pi_2$.

The typical use of the classification is to know which theorem applies: for example, $\mathsf{Q}$ proves every true $\Sigma_1$ sentence.
A trap is to count bounded quantifiers: $\forall y \le x$ inside $\mathrm{Prime}$ does not raise the class.

### L3 human

The classes interact with theories in two ways.
First, $\Sigma_1$-completeness: every true $\Sigma_1$ sentence is provable in $\mathsf{Q}$, and a theory is $\Sigma_1$-sound when every $\Sigma_1$ sentence it proves is true.
Second, the induction and collection schemas $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n$ and $\mathsf{B}\Sigma_n$ decide which closure properties hold provably: $\mathsf{B}\Sigma_n$ is exactly what is needed to pull an unbounded quantifier out of a bounded one.
By Matiyasevich's theorem, every $\Sigma_1$ formula is equivalent in $\mathfrak{N}$ to one of the form $\exists \vec{y}\,(p(\vec{x}, \vec{y}) = q(\vec{x}, \vec{y}))$ with polynomials $p$ and $q$.
So the $\Delta_0$ matrix can even be reduced to a single equation, at the price of more existential quantifiers.
