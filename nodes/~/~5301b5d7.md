## Definition

Let $G$ be a finite [[graph|~3f82cef4]].
The chromatic number $\chi(G)$ is the smallest natural number $k$ such that $G$ is [[$k$-colourable|~cc8def9d]].

## Remarks

For the [[complete graph|~b29ee1b7]], $\chi(K_n) = n$. For the [[cycle graph|~19143ce5]] $C_n$, $\chi(C_n)$ is $2$ if $n$ is even and $3$ if $n$ is odd.
A [[greedy colouring|~b2a39f97]] shows that $\chi(G) \le \Delta(G) + 1$, where $\Delta(G)$ is the [[maximum degree|~41541976]].
The chromatic number is at least the [[clique number|~c9f5669a]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The chromatic number is the least number of colours that you need to colour a graph properly.
For a map, it is the least number of colours that keeps neighbouring countries in different colours.
