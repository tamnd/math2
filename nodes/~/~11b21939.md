## Definition

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be an $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]] with domain $M$, and let $A \subseteq M$.
A relation $R \subseteq M^n$ is definable in $\mathcal{M}$ with parameters from $A$ when there are an $L$-[[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] $\varphi(x_1, \ldots, x_n, y_1, \ldots, y_m)$ and elements $b_1, \ldots, b_m \in A$ with
$$R = \set{(a_1, \ldots, a_n) \in M^n : \mathcal{M} \models \varphi[a_1, \ldots, a_n, b_1, \ldots, b_m]}.$$
Here $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[\vec{a}, \vec{b}]$ is [[satisfaction|~cf18e0d7]] under an assignment that sends $x_i$ to $a_i$ and $y_j$ to $b_j$.
The relation $R$ is definable when it is definable with parameters from $M$.
It is $\emptyset$-definable, or definable without parameters, when it is definable with parameters from $\emptyset$, that is, by a formula with free variables among $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ only.
A function $f \colon M^n \to M$ is definable when its graph is a definable relation in $M^{n+1}$, and an element $b$ is definable when $\set{b}$ is.

## Notation

The relation defined by $\varphi$ is written $\varphi(\mathcal{M})$, $\varphi(M^n)$ or $\varphi(\mathcal{M}, \vec{b})$ when parameters are used.
For $n = 1$, one says definable set.
In Mathlib, `Set.Definable A L s` says that the set $s$ of tuples is definable in the language $L$ with parameters from $A$.

## Remarks

### Examples

- In the field $(\R, +, \cdot, 0, 1)$, the [[order is definable|~bfc1db09]] without parameters, because the nonnegative reals are the squares.
- In $(\N, +)$, the [[even numbers|~7610ceef]] are $\emptyset$-definable by $\exists y \, (y + y = x)$.
- In $(\Z, <)$, the [[successor relation|~bee1342b]] is $\emptyset$-definable, although the order has no function symbols.
- In every structure, the diagonal $\set{(a, a) : a \in M}$ is $\emptyset$-definable by $x_1 = x_2$, and every finite subset of $M$ is definable with parameters, by a finite disjunction of equations.

### Non-examples

- In $(\Z, +)$, the [[positive integers|~aa6c3623]] are not $\emptyset$-definable, because $x \mapsto -x$ is an automorphism.
- In $(\R, <)$, the set $\set{0}$ is not $\emptyset$-definable, because the shift $x \mapsto x + 1$ is an automorphism; with the parameter $0$ it is definable by $x = y_1$.

### Basic properties

1. The relations definable with parameters from $A$ are closed under finite unions, finite intersections, complements and Cartesian products. The proofs use $\vee$, $\wedge$ and $\neg$.
2. If $R \subseteq M^{n+1}$ is definable, then its projection $\set{\vec{a} : \exists c \, (\vec{a}, c) \in R}$ is definable, by $\exists$.
3. Every [[automorphism|DSQ17TNW1C]] of $\mathcal{M}$ that fixes $A$ pointwise maps each relation definable with parameters from $A$ onto itself. This follows from [[isomorphism invariance of truth|~5166a0b3]].
4. Property 3 is the standard method to prove that a relation is not definable.
5. The converse of property 3 fails in general: a relation can be invariant under all automorphisms without being definable. In a countable structure, there are countably many formulas but possibly uncountably many invariant sets; for example, the rigid structure $(\N, <)$ has every subset invariant.

### History

Tarski studied definable sets of real numbers in a paper of 1931.
The automorphism method is related to Padoa's method of 1900 for showing that a primitive notion is not definable from the others.

### Common errors

- Allowing parameters changes the answer. In $(\R, <)$, the set $\set{0}$ is definable with parameters but not without.
- A definable relation must be defined by one formula. A set that is the union of infinitely many definable sets need not be definable; the set $\N$ is not definable in the field $(\R, +, \cdot, 0, 1)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A relation is definable in a structure when one formula of the language describes exactly the tuples in the relation.
For example, with only addition on the natural numbers, "even" is described by "is $y + y$ for some $y$".
To show that something is not definable, find a symmetry of the structure that moves it.

### L2 human

We check the formula $\varphi(x) = \exists y \, (y + y = x)$ in $(\N, +)$ on two inputs.

1. For $x \mapsto 6$, the assignment $y \mapsto 3$ gives $3 + 3 = 6$, so $(\N, +) \models \varphi[6]$.
2. For $x \mapsto 7$, every $y \in \N$ gives $y + y$ even, so $y + y \neq 7$, and $(\N, +) \not\models \varphi[7]$.

So $\varphi$ defines the even numbers.
The typical use is to transfer a property from a formula to the set it defines.
A trap is to use a symbol that is not in the signature, such as $2 \cdot x$ in $(\N, +)$; it must be written as $x + x$.

### L3 human

Definable relations are the subject of model theory, which studies the family of all definable sets of a structure.
Tame classes of structures are defined by this family: in an o-minimal structure, every definable subset of $M$ is a finite union of points and intervals.
By Tarski's quantifier elimination for real closed fields, the definable sets of $(\R, +, \cdot, 0, 1)$ are exactly the semialgebraic sets.
The concept page [[definable set|CY1TXHF6KQ]] treats these questions.
