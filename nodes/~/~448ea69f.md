## Proof

We define $D_i$ for each $i$ with $C_i|_\rho \ne 1$, by induction on $i$.
Each $D_i$ is either a new clause of $\pi'$, derived from earlier $D_j$ by one step, or equal to an earlier $D_j$.

1. *Axioms.* Let $C_i \in F$ with $C_i|_\rho \ne 1$. Then $C_i|_\rho \in F|_\rho$. Put $D_i = C_i|_\rho$, a new axiom of $\pi'$.
2. *Resolution steps.* Let $C_i = A \cup B$ be the resolvent of $C_j = A \cup \set{x}$ and $C_k = B \cup \set{\neg x}$, with $j, k < i$, and let $C_i|_\rho \ne 1$. Then no literal of $A$ or of $B$ is true under $\rho$.
3. *Case $\rho(x) = 1$.* Then no literal of $C_k = B \cup \set{\neg x}$ is true under $\rho$, so $C_k|_\rho = B|_\rho \ne 1$, and $D_k$ is defined. Put $D_i = D_k$. Then $D_i \subseteq B|_\rho \subseteq C_i|_\rho$.
4. *Case $\rho(x) = 0$.* In the same way $C_j|_\rho = A|_\rho \ne 1$. Put $D_i = D_j \subseteq A|_\rho \subseteq C_i|_\rho$.
5. *Case $x$ not set by $\rho$.* Then $C_j|_\rho = A|_\rho \cup \set{x}$ and $C_k|_\rho = B|_\rho \cup \set{\neg x}$, and both differ from $1$. So $D_j$ and $D_k$ are defined.
6. If $x \notin D_j$, put $D_i = D_j$. Then $D_i \subseteq A|_\rho \subseteq C_i|_\rho$.
7. Otherwise, if $\neg x \notin D_k$, put $D_i = D_k \subseteq B|_\rho \subseteq C_i|_\rho$.
8. Otherwise $x \in D_j$ and $\neg x \in D_k$. Let $D_i$ be the resolvent of $D_j$ and $D_k$ on $x$, a new clause of $\pi'$. It is contained in $A|_\rho \cup B|_\rho = C_i|_\rho$.
9. *Property 1* holds by steps 1 to 8.
10. *Property 2.* Each index $i$ adds at most one new clause, so $\pi'$ has at most $s$ clauses. Each $D_i$ satisfies $\abs{D_i} \le \abs{C_i|_\rho} \le \abs{C_i}$, so the width does not grow.
11. *Property 3.* A clause $D_j$ is a premise of a new step only in step 8, and then $C_j$ is a premise of the step that gives $C_i$. If $\pi$ is tree-like, $C_j$ is a premise of at most one step, so $D_j$ is a premise of at most one new step. Hence $\pi'$ is tree-like.
12. *Refutations.* If $C_s = \square$, then $C_s|_\rho = \square \ne 1$, and $D_s \subseteq \square$. So $D_s = \square$, and $\pi'$ is a refutation of $F|_\rho$. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Follow the old refutation and keep only what survives the restriction.
When $\rho$ sets the pivot, one premise is already as strong as the conclusion, so keep that premise and skip the step.
When the restricted premise has already lost its pivot literal, it is again as strong as the conclusion.

## Variants

- In the [[proof of Haken's theorem|~8dac2cf0]], $\rho$ is a partial matching of pigeons to holes, and $F|_\rho$ is the pigeonhole formula of the remaining pigeons and holes.
- In the tree-like case the proof also shows that $\pi'$ has at most as many leaves as $\pi$.
