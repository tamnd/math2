## Example

Let $m > n \ge 1$.
The [[pigeonhole principle|0HGVCWQGY3]] says that there is no injective map from $m$ pigeons into $n$ holes.
Its negation is the [[CNF|0B3D4C8YF2]] $\mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ in the variables $x_{ij}$, for pigeons $i \in \set{1, \dots, m}$ and holes $j \in \set{1, \dots, n}$, where $x_{ij}$ means "pigeon $i$ sits in hole $j$".

- *Pigeon clauses.* $P_i = \set{x_{i1}, \dots, x_{in}}$ for each $i$: pigeon $i$ sits in some hole.
- *Hole clauses.* $H_{i i' j} = \set{\neg x_{ij}, \neg x_{i'j}}$ for $i < i'$ and each $j$: no two pigeons share hole $j$.

The formula $\mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ is unsatisfiable, and it has $mn$ variables and $m + n \binom{m}{2}$ clauses.
The tautology $\neg \mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ is the propositional form of the pigeonhole principle for this $m$ and $n$.

*The smallest case.* $\mathrm{PHP}^2_1$ has the clauses $\set{x_{11}}$, $\set{x_{21}}$ and $\set{\neg x_{11}, \neg x_{21}}$.
Resolve the last clause with $\set{x_{11}}$ to get $\set{\neg x_{21}}$, and then with $\set{x_{21}}$ to get $\square$.

*Variants.*

- $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ is the usual case, with one extra pigeon.
- The *weak* pigeonhole formulas have many more pigeons, such as $m = 2n$ or $m = n^2$.
- The *functional* version adds the clauses $\set{\neg x_{ij}, \neg x_{ij'}}$ for $j \ne j'$: each pigeon sits in at most one hole.
- The *onto* version adds the clauses $\set{x_{1j}, \dots, x_{mj}}$: every hole is used.
- The *graph* version keeps only the variables $x_{ij}$ for the edges of a bipartite graph between pigeons and holes.

## Remarks

The pigeonhole formulas separate many proof systems.

- *Resolution.* $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ needs [[resolution refutations|~586b5042]] of size $2^{\Omega(n)}$, by [[Haken's theorem|~dd16430c]]. For the weak versions with any $m > n$, Raz proved a bound $2^{n^{\varepsilon}}$ in 2004.
- *Bounded-depth Frege.* For each depth $d$, depth-$d$ Frege proofs of $\neg\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ need size $2^{n^{\varepsilon_d}}$; see [[Ajtai's theorem|~b671fbcc]].
- *Frege.* Polynomial-size Frege proofs exist, by [[Buss's theorem|~d05aa878]].
- *Extended Frege.* Cook and Reckhow gave polynomial-size proofs in [[extended Frege|~6925f591]] in 1979, and Cook gave polynomial-size extended resolution refutations in 1976.
- *Cutting planes.* $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has polynomial-size [[cutting-planes refutations|~d332f763]], because cutting planes can add the inequalities.
- *Weak versions in bounded depth.* Paris, Wilkie and Woods gave quasi-polynomial-size bounded-depth Frege proofs of the weak pigeonhole principle in 1988.
- *Algebraic systems.* The degree of [[polynomial calculus|~57cdb980]] refutations of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ must grow with $n$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If $5$ letters go into $4$ mailboxes, some mailbox gets two letters.
Written as a list of clauses, the opposite claim says: every letter is in some box, and no box has two letters.
The list $\mathrm{PHP}^5_4$ has $20$ variables, $5$ clauses of the first kind and $40$ clauses of the second kind.

### L2 human

We count the clauses of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ for $n = 4$.

1. Pigeon clauses: one for each of the $5$ pigeons, each of width $4$.
2. Hole clauses: for each of the $4$ holes, one clause for each pair of pigeons. There are $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ pairs, so $40$ hole clauses of width $2$.
3. In total $45$ clauses and $20$ variables.

The typical use of the formulas is to test whether a proof system can count.
A trap: the pigeon clauses have width $n$, so the [[size-width relation|~d2afe9c2]] does not apply directly; the graph version on a bounded-degree expander is used instead.

### L3 human

The pigeonhole principle is a basic counting statement, and the formulas measure how much counting a proof system can do.
Systems whose lines can express sums of many bits, such as Frege systems and cutting planes, prove it in polynomial size.
Systems whose lines are clauses or constant-depth formulas cannot, and their lower bounds on these formulas match the circuit lower bounds for the corresponding classes.
In [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]], the weak pigeonhole principle is a central axiom, and its strength depends on the number of pigeons.
