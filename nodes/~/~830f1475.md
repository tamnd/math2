## Statement

A formula is *negation-free*, or *positive*, if it is built from propositional variables with $\wedge$, $\vee$ and $\to$ only, without $\bot$ and so without $\neg$.
The *positive fragment* of a logic is the set of its negation-free theorems.
Let $\mathsf{KC} = \mathsf{IPC} + \neg p \vee \neg \neg p$ be [[the logic of the weak excluded middle|H9Z8BWRN69]].

**Theorem.**

1. The positive fragment of $\mathsf{KC}$ equals the positive fragment of $\mathsf{IPC}$.
2. If $L$ is an [[intermediate logic|~57af80cc]] whose positive fragment equals that of $\mathsf{IPC}$, then $L \subseteq \mathsf{KC}$.

So $\mathsf{KC}$ is the largest intermediate logic with the same positive fragment as $\mathsf{IPC}$.
We prove item 1 in full, and cite item 2 without proof.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

Item 2 compares positive fragments only.
Classical logic $\mathsf{CPC}$ is above $\mathsf{KC}$, and its positive fragment contains Peirce's law $((p \to q) \to p) \to p$, which $\mathsf{IPC}$ does not prove.
Item 1 fails for $\mathsf{LC}$, whose axiom $(p \to q) \vee (q \to p)$ is negation-free.

### Consequences

1. An intermediate logic whose extra axioms are all negation-free and not provable in $\mathsf{IPC}$ is not contained in $\mathsf{KC}$. Examples are [[Dummett's logic LC|~cfc28e48]] and the logics of bounded depth.
2. Every intermediate logic is either contained in $\mathsf{KC}$ or proves a negation-free formula that $\mathsf{IPC}$ does not prove.
3. $\mathsf{KC}$ and $\mathsf{IPC}$ prove the same negation-free formulas, yet $\mathsf{KC}$ proves $\neg p \vee \neg \neg p$, which $\mathsf{IPC}$ does not prove by [[the weak excluded middle theorem|CX032ACTMZ]].

### Converse

Item 2 has the converse: every intermediate logic $L$ with $\mathsf{IPC} \subseteq L \subseteq \mathsf{KC}$ has the positive fragment of $\mathsf{IPC}$.
This follows from item 1, since the positive fragment of $L$ lies between those of $\mathsf{IPC}$ and $\mathsf{KC}$.

### Generalizations

The proof of item 1 shows more: adding a top world to a finite rooted model does not change the truth of negation-free formulas at the old worlds.

## History

Jankov proved the theorem in his early papers on intermediate logics, written around his 1964 doctorate.
For this reason $\mathsf{KC}$ is also called Jankov's logic or Yankov's logic.
The attribution follows the 2025 memoriam by Citkin and Vandoulakis.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some laws of logic talk about "impossible" and some do not.
This theorem says that weak excluded middle adds no new law that avoids the word "impossible".
And it is the strongest logic with this feature.
Any stronger logic proves some new law without negation.

### L2 human

1. Take the formula $p \vee (p \to q)$, which is negation-free.
2. Take the model with worlds $r < a$, $p$ true at $a$ only, and $q$ false everywhere.
3. At $r$, $p$ is false, and $p \to q$ fails because of $a$. So $r \nVdash p \vee (p \to q)$.
4. Add a new top world $t$ above $r$ and $a$, with $p$ and $q$ true at $t$.
5. At $r$, $p$ is still false, and $p \to q$ still fails because of $a$. So the formula still fails at $r$.
6. The new frame is a chain with greatest world $t$, so it validates $\mathsf{KC}$, and $\mathsf{KC} \nvdash p \vee (p \to q)$.

The typical use is to show that a negation-free formula is not in $\mathsf{KC}$ by giving an $\mathsf{IPC}$ countermodel.
A trap: the new top must make every variable true; otherwise implications at old worlds can change.

### L3 human

Algebraically, adding a top world to a finite rooted frame corresponds to adding a new least element below the bottom of the dual Heyting algebra.
Item 2 is a maximality result in the lattice of intermediate logics, like Maksimova's result that [[Medvedev's logic|~6aab2c7f]] is the largest extension of the weak Kreisel-Putnam logic with the disjunction property.
Negation-free formulas are also the language of positive logic, where $\bot$ is absent and every model can be closed off by a top world.
