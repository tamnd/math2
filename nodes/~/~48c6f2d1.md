## Example

Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 & 3 \\ 8 & 7 & 9 \end{pmatrix}$, with the $1 \times 1$ block $A = (2)$, $B = (1, 1)$, $C = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}$ and $D = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 7 & 9 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. $CA^{-1}B = \tfrac12 \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix} (1, 1) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. The [[Schur complement|CA1J41NWGS]] is $M/A = D - CA^{-1}B = \begin{pmatrix} 3 - 2 & 3 - 2 \\ 7 - 4 & 9 - 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. Now do one elimination step on $M$: $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ gives $(0, 1, 1)$, and $R_3 \to R_3 - 4R_1$ gives $(0, 3, 5)$.
4. The lower right $2 \times 2$ block after this step is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, which is $M/A$.
5. By the [[determinant formula|0KA7A4ENVJ]], $\det M = 2 \cdot \det(M/A) = 2 (5 - 3) = 4$.
6. This agrees with the [[worked LU example|~3fa7f06e]], where the pivots of $M$ are $2$, $1$ and $2$, with product $4$.

## Remarks

The general reason: the row operations $R_i \to R_i - (m_{i1}/m_{11}) R_1$ subtract $c_i\, a^{-1}\, b^{\mathsf{T}}$ from row $i$ of the lower block, which is the block $C A^{-1} B$.
So Gaussian elimination is the repeated formation of Schur complements of $1 \times 1$ pivots.
Elimination on $M/A$ continues with the pivot $1$ and leaves the Schur complement $5 - 3 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = 2$, the last pivot.

## Explanations

### L1 human

One step of elimination with the first pivot leaves a smaller matrix in the lower right corner.
That smaller matrix is exactly the Schur complement of the pivot.
So the Schur complement is "what is left to do" after the first pivot.

### L2 human

This explains the quotient notation $M/A$.
Each step of elimination divides out one pivot and keeps the rest.
The determinant is the product of the pivots, which matches $\det M = \det A \det(M/A)$ step by step.
