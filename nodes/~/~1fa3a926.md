## Example

Ann thinks her train leaves at nine, but the timetable changed and it leaves at ten.
Let $p$ say "the train leaves at nine".

1. Let $W = \set{w, v}$ with $p$ true at $v$ only. The actual world is $w$.
2. Let $R = \set{(w, v), (v, v)}$: at both worlds Ann considers only $v$ possible.
3. $R$ is serial, since both worlds see $v$.
4. $R$ is transitive: the only composable pairs are $(w, v), (v, v)$ and $(v, v), (v, v)$, giving $(w, v)$ and $(v, v)$.
5. $R$ is [[Euclidean|JDE7CN1C48]]: every world sees only $v$, and $v R v$ holds.
6. So the frame is a $\mathsf{KD45}$ frame, see [[the completeness of KD45|1CFMP9DEE6]].
7. At $w$ the only world seen is $v$, where $p$ holds. So $\square p$ is true at $w$.
8. $p$ is false at $w$. So $\square p \to p$ is false at $w$.

The box behaves as belief: Ann is sure the train leaves at nine, and she is wrong.
It is not knowledge, because the truth axiom $K\varphi \to \varphi$ fails at $w$.

## Remarks

The truth axiom corresponds to reflexivity, by [[the common modal axioms|FDFQFWPM4X]], and $w R w$ fails here.
The model is a correct model of [[doxastic logic|RTP4HTZYK3]]: Ann's beliefs are consistent, and she is introspective about them.
Adding $(w, w)$ would make Ann consider the true world, so she would no longer believe $p$.
In this sense the gap between belief and knowledge is the gap between seriality and reflexivity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Believing is not knowing.
Ann is certain her train leaves at nine, but it leaves at ten.
A model of her mind has to allow her to be certain of something false.
So the rule "what you know is true" holds for knowledge but not for belief.

### L2 human

To test whether a box can be read as knowledge, look for a world that does not see itself.

1. At such a world $w$, choose a letter $p$ true at all worlds seen from $w$, and false at $w$.
2. Then $\square p$ is true and $p$ is false at $w$.

Here $w$ sees only $v$, and step 1 uses $p$ true exactly at $v$.
The typical use is to sort models of belief from models of knowledge by one frame property.
A trap: the frame satisfies axioms $\mathsf{4}$ and $\mathsf{5}$, so introspection alone does not make a box into knowledge.

### L3 human

$\mathsf{KD45}$ and $\mathsf{S5}$ differ by reflexivity only.
A point-generated $\mathsf{KD45}$ frame is a nonempty cluster of "believed" worlds, possibly with a root outside it that sees the whole cluster.
Here the cluster is $\set{v}$, and $w$ is the root outside it.
Combined logics of knowledge and belief relate the two boxes, for example by $K\varphi \to B\varphi$.
