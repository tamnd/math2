## Example

We show that the logics $\mathsf{T}$ and $\mathsf{K4}$ of [[modal logics T, D and K4|~5580ab65]] are incomparable.
Take the frame
$$W = \set{1, 2, 3}, \quad R = \set{(1, 1), (2, 2), (3, 3), (1, 2), (2, 3)}, \quad V(p) = \set{1, 2}.$$

1. The frame is [[reflexive|0X8NMST0J2]], so it validates $\mathsf{T}$, by [[common modal axiom schemata|FDFQFWPM4X]] and [[soundness|~d189ee4b]].
2. The frame is not [[transitive|0GJZW9JFZQ]]: $1 R 2$ and $2 R 3$ hold, and $1 R 3$ fails.
3. The successor sets are $R[1] = \set{1, 2}$, $R[2] = \set{2, 3}$ and $R[3] = \set{3}$.
4. $\square p$ is true at $1$, since $1$ and $2$ both have $p$.
5. $\square p$ is false at $2$, since the successor $3$ lacks $p$.
6. $\square\square p$ is false at $1$, since the successor $2$ makes $\square p$ false.
7. So $\square p \to \square\square p$ fails at $1$, and $\mathsf{T} \nvdash \mathrm{4}$.
8. Now take one world $w$ with the empty relation and $V(p) = \varnothing$. The empty relation is transitive, so the frame validates $\mathsf{K4}$.
9. At $w$, $\square p$ is true, since $w$ has no successors, and $p$ is false. So $\mathsf{K4} \nvdash \mathrm{T}$.
10. So neither logic contains the other.

## Remarks

The example shows that reflexivity and transitivity are independent, and so are the axioms $\mathrm{T}$ and $\mathrm{4}$.
The smallest normal logic that contains both is the [[modal logic S4|~b4d2786a]], the logic of reflexive transitive frames.
So $\mathsf{T} \subsetneq \mathsf{S4}$ and $\mathsf{K4} \subsetneq \mathsf{S4}$, with steps 7 and 9 as witnesses.
If we add the pair $(1, 3)$, the frame becomes a preorder, and $\square p$ becomes false at $1$, so this valuation no longer refutes $\mathrm{4}$.
The world $3$ is needed: on the two worlds $1, 2$ alone, the relation is a preorder.
The same frame refutes the dual form $\lozenge\lozenge p \to \lozenge p$ at $1$ with $V(p) = \set{3}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture three towns on a road: from each town one sees the town itself and the next town.
From town 1 one sees towns 1 and 2, and from town 2 one sees towns 2 and 3.
If it is sunny in towns 1 and 2, then from town 1 "everywhere I see, it is sunny" is true.
But from town 2 this is false, since town 3 is cloudy.
So town 1 cannot say "everywhere I see, everyone sees sun", and axiom 4 fails.

### L2 human

1. Check the dual on the same frame with $V(p) = \set{3}$.
2. At $2$, the successor $3$ has $p$, so $\lozenge p$ is true at $2$.
3. At $1$, the successor $2$ has $\lozenge p$, so $\lozenge\lozenge p$ is true at $1$.
4. At $1$, the successors are $1$ and $2$, which lack $p$, so $\lozenge p$ is false at $1$.

The typical use of this frame is as the smallest reflexive frame that is not transitive.
A trap: reflexivity gives $\square\square p \to \square p$ in $\mathsf{T}$, the converse of $\mathrm{4}$, but not $\mathrm{4}$ itself.
