## Example

A [[Boolean function|0X05GS525E]] of $n$ variables is a map $f \colon \set{0,1}^n \to \set{0,1}$.
A [[Boolean circuit|SQXNFGVADX]] computes such a function with gates of three types: AND and OR with two inputs, and NOT with one input.
We prove by a [[counting argument|~0ba16916]] that some Boolean function of $10$ variables has no circuit with at most $71$ gates.

1. Count the functions. The set $\set{0,1}^{10}$ has $2^{10} = 1024$ elements, and each element has $2$ possible values. So there are $2^{1024}$ functions.
2. Describe a circuit with exactly $s$ gates $g_1, \ldots, g_s$. The nodes are the $10$ inputs and the $s$ gates, so there are $10 + s$ nodes.
3. For each gate, give its type ($3$ choices) and two input nodes ($(10 + s)^2$ choices). A NOT gate ignores its second input. Then give the output node ($10 + s$ choices).
4. So there are at most $(10 + s) \cdot \left(3 (10 + s)^2\right)^s$ descriptions. Some descriptions are not valid circuits, and this only makes the count larger.
5. A circuit with fewer than $s$ gates computes the same function as a circuit with exactly $s$ gates. Add unused NOT gates on $x_1$ until there are $s$ gates.
6. So the number of functions with a circuit of at most $71$ gates is at most $81 \cdot (3 \cdot 81^2)^{71}$.
7. We take base $2$ logarithms: $\log_2 81 \approx 6.34$ and $\log_2 (3 \cdot 81^2) \approx 14.26$. So the bound is about $2^{6.34 + 71 \cdot 14.26} \approx 2^{1019.1}$.
8. Since $2^{1019.1} < 2^{1024}$, the circuits with at most $71$ gates compute fewer functions than exist. So some function of $10$ variables needs more than $71$ gates.

## Remarks

The example shows that the argument proves more than existence.
The bound $2^{1019.1} < 2^{1020}$ shows that at most $2^{1020}/2^{1024} = 1/16$ of the functions have a circuit with at most $71$ gates.
So more than $15/16$ of the functions of $10$ variables need more than $71$ gates.

The bound stops at $71$.
For $s = 72$, the count of descriptions is about $2^{1036.0}$, which is more than $2^{1024}$, and the argument gives nothing.
The general form, with circuit size about $2^n/n$, is [[Shannon's counting argument|2R30KR3Z5G]].
The proof names no specific function of $10$ variables.
To find one and prove a bound on its [[circuit complexity|4PGX7PHJCK]] is a different and much harder problem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rule that takes $10$ yes-or-no answers and gives one yes-or-no answer is a Boolean function.
There are astronomically many such rules: $2^{1024}$ of them.
A small circuit is a short list of simple steps, and there are not so many short lists.
So the short lists cannot cover all the rules.
Some rule needs a long list of steps, even though we cannot point to it.

### L2 human

We repeat the count for $n = 2$ variables and circuits with at most $s = 1$ gate.

1. There are $2^{2^2} = 2^4 = 16$ functions of $2$ variables.
2. With $s = 1$, there are $2 + 1 = 3$ nodes. The number of descriptions is at most $3 \cdot (3 \cdot 3^2)^1 = 81$.
3. Since $81 > 16$, the count gives nothing here.
4. In fact only $6$ functions have a circuit with at most one gate: $x_1$, $x_2$, $\lnot x_1$, $\lnot x_2$, $x_1 \land x_2$ and $x_1 \lor x_2$. The crude count of $81$ descriptions is far above $6$.

The typical use is for large $n$, where $2^{2^n}$ grows much faster than the number of small circuits.
A trap is to apply the count for small $n$, where the crude count of descriptions is larger than the number of functions.
