## Example

We show $\mathsf{S5} \vdash \square(p \vee \square q) \leftrightarrow (\square p \vee \square q)$ in the [[modal logic S5|VC3A2VWXND]], by a semantic argument.
It suffices to check the formula at every world of every model whose relation is an [[equivalence relation|0G8VEZ3WTK]].
This follows from [[completeness of K, T, D, K4, S4, B and S5|~1d9447ce]].

1. Truth at $w$ depends only on the worlds reachable from $w$. In an equivalence relation these form the class of $w$, on which $R$ is universal.
2. So it suffices to check a model with $R = W \times W$. There $\square\varphi$ is true at one world exactly when $\varphi$ is true at all worlds.
3. In particular, $\square q$ is true at all worlds or at none.
4. Case 1: $\square q$ is true at all worlds. Then $p \vee \square q$ is true everywhere, so $\square(p \vee \square q)$ is true everywhere. Also $\square p \vee \square q$ is true everywhere.
5. Case 2: $\square q$ is false at all worlds. Then $p \vee \square q$ has the same truth set as $p$, so $\square(p \vee \square q)$ and $\square p$ agree at every world.
6. In case 2, $\square p \vee \square q$ also agrees with $\square p$ at every world.
7. In both cases the two sides agree at every world, so the equivalence is valid on universal frames and is a theorem of $\mathsf{S5}$.
8. Now take the [[preorder|0VABYWJWH9]] $W = \set{r, a}$, $R = \set{(r, r), (a, a), (r, a)}$, with $V(p) = \set{r}$ and $V(q) = \set{a}$.
9. $\square q$ is true at $a$, since $a$ sees only $a$. $\square q$ is false at $r$, since $r$ sees $r$.
10. $p \vee \square q$ is true at $r$ by $p$, and at $a$ by $\square q$. So $\square(p \vee \square q)$ is true at $r$.
11. $\square p$ is false at $r$, since $a$ lacks $p$. With step 9, $\square p \vee \square q$ is false at $r$.
12. So $\square(p \vee \square q) \to (\square p \vee \square q)$ fails at $r$, and by [[soundness|~d189ee4b]] it is not a theorem of the [[modal logic S4|~b4d2786a]].

## Remarks

The example shows that in $\mathsf{S5}$ a box distributes over a disjunction when one disjunct is itself boxed.
The direction $(\square p \vee \square q) \to \square(p \vee \square q)$ already holds in $\mathsf{K4}$: RM gives $\square p \to \square(p \vee \square q)$, and $\mathrm{4}$ with RM gives $\square q \to \square\square q \to \square(p \vee \square q)$.
The failing direction needs symmetry: in step 8, $a$ does not see back to $r$, and this is what breaks the argument.
Together with the reduction of iterated modalities, this law lets one rewrite every formula of $\mathsf{S5}$ to modal depth at most 1.
If $\square q$ is replaced by $q$, the equivalence fails even in $\mathsf{S5}$.
Take a universal frame with two worlds, with $p$ at one world and $q$ at the other.
On this frame, $\square(p \vee q)$ is true and $\square p \vee \square q$ is false.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In S5, "box" means "in every world", and all worlds see all worlds.
So "box $q$" is a fixed fact: it is true in every world or in none.
If it is true, both sides of the law are true.
If it is false, both sides say "$p$ in every world".
In a picture where one world cannot look back, this case split breaks.

### L2 human

1. Take a universal frame with $W = \set{1, 2}$, $V(p) = \set{1}$ and $V(q) = \set{2}$.
2. $\square q$ is false at both worlds, since $1$ lacks $q$. So we are in case 2.
3. $\square(p \vee \square q)$ is false, since $2$ lacks $p$, and $\square p \vee \square q$ is false.
4. So both sides are false at both worlds, as the case analysis predicts.

The typical use of the case analysis "a boxed formula is constant" is to prove $\mathsf{S5}$ laws on universal frames.
A trap: this method needs the full completeness theorem for $\mathsf{S5}$; for weaker logics a boxed formula need not have a constant truth value.
