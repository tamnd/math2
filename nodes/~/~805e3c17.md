## Example

Let $\theta \in \R$ not be a multiple of $\pi$.
Let $R_\theta \colon \R^2 \to \R^2$ be the rotation by $\theta$, with matrix
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}.$$

1. By the [[definition of the characteristic polynomial|039HF58KQ5]], $p_A(t) = (t - \cos\theta)^2 + \sin^2\theta = t^2 - 2\cos\theta\, t + 1$.
2. Second method: $\tr A = 2\cos\theta$ and $\det A = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$. This gives the same polynomial.
3. The discriminant is $4\cos^2\theta - 4 = -4\sin^2\theta$. Since $\theta$ is not a multiple of $\pi$, $\sin\theta \ne 0$, so the discriminant is negative.
4. So $p_A$ has no real root. Since the [[eigenvalues are the roots of the characteristic polynomial|053QVHF6N7]], $R_\theta$ has no real [[eigenvalue|0ZND7NQ970]] and no real [[eigenvector|0B5H4208Z9]].
5. Over $\C$, the roots are $\cos\theta \pm i\sin\theta = e^{\pm i\theta}$.
6. Check for $\lambda = e^{i\theta}$ and $v = (1, -i)$: $Av = (\cos\theta + i\sin\theta,\ \sin\theta - i\cos\theta)$.
7. And $\lambda v = (e^{i\theta},\ -i e^{i\theta}) = (\cos\theta + i\sin\theta,\ \sin\theta - i\cos\theta)$. So $Av = \lambda v$.
8. Taking complex conjugates, $(1, i)$ is an eigenvector for $e^{-i\theta}$.

## Remarks

- The [[characteristic polynomial of the linear map|0BZ81AJNWF]] $R_\theta$ is the same in every basis, so no choice of real basis gives a real eigenvalue.
- For $\theta = \pi$, $A = -I$, and every nonzero vector is an eigenvector for $-1$. For $\theta = 0$, $A = I$.
- The existence of eigenvalues over $\C$ is an instance of the [[existence of an eigenvalue over an algebraically closed field|08CJB2NQ92]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rotation by an angle that is not a half turn moves every line through the origin to a different line.
So no real vector keeps its direction.
Over the complex numbers, the rotation has the eigenvalues $e^{i\theta}$ and $e^{-i\theta}$.

### L2 human

The case $\theta = \pi/2$ gives $p_A(t) = t^2 + 1$, with roots $\pm i$.
The typical use of this example is to show that eigenvalues depend on the field.
A trap is to conclude that the rotation has no characteristic polynomial: it has one, but its roots are not real.
