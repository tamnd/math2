## Statement

Let $f \colon A \to B$ be a [[function|~d103e2ff]].
Then $f$ is [[bijective|~14a2ca3d]] if and only if there is a function $g \colon B \to A$ with $g \circ f = \mathrm{id}_A$ and $f \circ g = \mathrm{id}_B$.

## Remarks

The function $g$ is then unique. It is the [[inverse function|~6b98c003]] $f^{-1}$.
This statement does not need the axiom of choice: for a bijection, $g(b)$ is the unique $a$ with $f(a) = b$.
If $f$ has a [[left inverse|~fc763880]] $g$ and a [[right inverse|~d79e9cba]] $h$, then $g = h$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bijection can be undone in both directions.
Going from $A$ to $B$ and back gives the start point, and going from $B$ to $A$ and back gives the start point too.
