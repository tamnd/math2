## Example

Let $L = \set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$ and let $\Phi$ be the conjunction of the field axioms.
For $d \ge 1$ let $\psi_d$ say "every monic polynomial of degree $d$ has a root": $\forall a_0 \cdots \forall a_{d-1} \, \exists x \, (x^d + a_{d-1} x^{d-1} + \cdots + a_0 = 0)$.
The [[algebraically closed fields|0PF6TM8TWT]] are the models of $\mathrm{ACF} = \set{\Phi} \cup \set{\psi_d : d \ge 1}$, so they form an [[elementary class|MEC3ZMC4F1]].
We check that $\mathrm{ACF}$ is not [[finitely axiomatizable|~8205aea8]].
Fix $N \ge 1$ and a prime $p$; we build a field $F$ that satisfies $\Phi, \psi_1, \dots, \psi_N$ and is not algebraically closed.

1. For $m \mid n$ the [[finite field|0ZXZ82FTX2]] $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$, inside a fixed algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$. Let $m_k = (N!)^k$. Then $m_k \mid m_{k+1}$, so the fields $\mathbb{F}_{p^{m_k}}$ form a chain, and their union $F$ is a field.
2. Let $f$ be monic of degree $d \le N$ over $F$. Its coefficients lie in some $\mathbb{F}_{p^{m_k}}$. Let $g$ be an [[irreducible|03FKX7Z5DC]] factor of $f$ over $\mathbb{F}_{p^{m_k}}$, of degree $e \le N$.
3. $g$ has a root in $\mathbb{F}_{p^{m_k e}}$. Since $e \mid N!$, we have $m_k e \mid m_k \cdot N! = m_{k+1}$. So $g$, and hence $f$, has a root in $\mathbb{F}_{p^{m_{k+1}}} \subseteq F$. So $F \models \psi_d$ for $d \le N$.
4. Let $q > N$ be prime, and let $h$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree $q$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$; one exists because $\mathbb{F}_{p^q}$ has a primitive element over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
5. If $h$ had a root $\alpha \in F$, then $\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_{p^{m_k}}$ for some $k$, and $\mathbb{F}_p(\alpha) = \mathbb{F}_{p^q} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^{m_k}}$. So $q \mid (N!)^k$, which is false since the prime $q > N$ does not divide $N!$.
6. So $F$ is not algebraically closed, and $F \models \set{\Phi, \psi_1, \dots, \psi_N}$.
7. Each finite subset of $\mathrm{ACF}$ lies in some $\set{\Phi, \psi_1, \dots, \psi_N}$, so it has a model that is not algebraically closed. By [[the criterion|~c5dccb2a]], part 2, $\mathrm{ACF}$ is not finitely axiomatizable.

## Remarks

The same proof works for $\mathrm{ACF}_p$, the theory of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $p$, since $F$ has characteristic $p$.
For $\mathrm{ACF}_0$, a second reason is that the fields of characteristic $0$ already need infinitely many axioms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A field is algebraically closed when every polynomial equation has a solution.
We can build a field in which every equation of degree at most $N$ has a solution, but some equation of a larger prime degree does not.
So no finite list of axioms can say "algebraically closed", since any finite list only talks about degrees up to some $N$.

### L2 human

We check steps 2 and 3 for $N = 2$, $p = 2$ and $f = x^2 + x + 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

1. Here $m_k = 2^k$, so $F = \bigcup_k \mathbb{F}_{2^{2^k}}$.
2. $f$ has no root in $\mathbb{F}_2$, since $f(0) = f(1) = 1$. So $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$, with $e = 2$.
3. $f$ has a root in $\mathbb{F}_4 = \mathbb{F}_{2^2}$, which lies in $F$.
4. The prime $q = 3 > 2$ gives the irreducible $x^3 + x + 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$, with no root in $F$, since $3$ does not divide any power of $2$.

The typical use of such a field is to separate a schema from each of its finite parts.
A trap is to take $F$ to be a single finite field: every finite field has an irreducible polynomial of degree $2$, so it already fails $\psi_2$.
