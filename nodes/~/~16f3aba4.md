## Example

Let $T$ be the equilateral triangle with centre $0$ whose vertices lie on the unit circle at the angles $90^\circ$, $210^\circ$ and $330^\circ$.
We call these vertices $1$, $2$ and $3$.
We show that the [[symmetry group|0ZG3Z5JZ85]] $\operatorname{Sym}(T)$ has $6$ elements, and that it is isomorphic to the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] $S_3$.
Let $r$ be the [[rotation|0HFREYRSZP]] about $0$ by $120^\circ$, and let $s_i$ be the [[reflection|06BRDF3F0E]] in the line through $0$ and vertex $i$.

1. The rotation $r$ adds $120^\circ$ to the angle of each vertex: $90^\circ \mapsto 210^\circ$, $210^\circ \mapsto 330^\circ$ and $330^\circ \mapsto 450^\circ = 90^\circ$. So $r$ maps the vertices onto the vertices, and $r$ maps $T$ onto $T$. As a [[permutation|05RWFKVTJ7]] of the vertices, $r = (1\ 2\ 3)$.
2. In the same way, $r^2$ is the rotation by $240^\circ$, and $r^2 = (1\ 3\ 2)$ on the vertices. The identity $e = r^0$ is the third rotation.
3. The line of $s_3$ is at the angle $330^\circ$, that is, at $150^\circ$. The vertices $1$ and $2$ are at $60^\circ$ from this line, on opposite sides. So $s_3$ fixes vertex $3$ and swaps $1$ and $2$, and $s_3 = (1\ 2)$.
4. In the same way, $s_1 = (2\ 3)$ and $s_2 = (1\ 3)$.
5. The largest distance between two points of $T$ is the side length, and only pairs of vertices have this distance. So each symmetry maps vertices to vertices, and it gives a permutation of $\{1, 2, 3\}$.
6. A plane isometry is known when we know the images of three points that are not on one line. So different symmetries give different permutations.
7. Steps 1 to 4 give $6$ symmetries with $6$ different permutations. The group $S_3$ has only $6$ elements, so by step 6 there are no other symmetries.
8. The permutation of $f \circ g$ is the permutation of $f$ composed with the permutation of $g$. So the map from $\operatorname{Sym}(T)$ to $S_3$ is a bijective homomorphism, that is, an [[isomorphism|08MG7H8JW5]].

So $\operatorname{Sym}(T) = \{e, r, r^2, s_1, s_2, s_3\}$, and $\operatorname{Sym}(T) \cong S_3$.

## Remarks

The group is not [[abelian|082GHNPJ53]].
With permutations composed from right to left, $s_1 s_2 = (2\ 3)(1\ 3) = (1\ 2\ 3) = r$, but $s_2 s_1 = (1\ 3)(2\ 3) = (1\ 3\ 2) = r^2$.
This agrees with the rule that two reflections compose to a rotation by twice the angle between their lines: the lines of $s_1$ and $s_2$ are at $90^\circ$ and $210^\circ$, and $2(90^\circ - 210^\circ) = -240^\circ$, which is a turn by $120^\circ$.
The group is the [[dihedral group|0JZ27DMJ5D]] of order $6$, the case $n = 3$ of the regular $n$-gon.
For $n = 3$ every permutation of the vertices comes from a symmetry, but for a square only $8$ of the $24$ permutations of the vertices do.
A triangle that is isosceles but not equilateral has only $2$ symmetries: the identity and one reflection.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cut an equilateral triangle out of card and draw its outline on the table.
There are six ways to put the card back into the outline.
We can turn it by a third of a full turn, by two thirds, or not at all.
We can also flip it over in three ways, one for each corner that stays in place.
Number the corners $1$, $2$ and $3$.
Each of the six moves puts the numbers in a different order, and there are exactly six orders of three numbers.

### L2 human

We compute the product $s_1 s_2$ in two ways.

1. On the vertices, $s_2 = (1\ 3)$ acts first and $s_1 = (2\ 3)$ acts second.
2. Vertex $1$ goes to $3$ by $s_2$, and then to $2$ by $s_1$.
3. Vertex $2$ stays at $2$ by $s_2$, and then goes to $3$ by $s_1$.
4. Vertex $3$ goes to $1$ by $s_2$, and then stays at $1$ by $s_1$.
5. So $s_1 s_2 = (1\ 2\ 3) = r$, the turn by $120^\circ$.
6. With the matrix rule $s_\alpha s_\beta = r_{2(\alpha - \beta)}$, we get a turn by $-240^\circ$, which is the same turn as $120^\circ$.

The typical use of this example is as the smallest group that is not abelian, in a form that we can draw.
A trap is the order of composition: $s_1 s_2$ means "first $s_2$, then $s_1$", and the other order gives $r^2$, not $r$.
