## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring, let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $m \times n$ [[matrix|~52f4e417]] over $R$, and let $B = (b_{jk})$ be an $n \times p$ matrix over $R$.
The *product* $AB$ is the $m \times p$ matrix with entries
$$(AB)_{ik} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} b_{jk}$$
for $1 \le i \le m$ and $1 \le k \le p$.

## Remarks

The product $AB$ is defined only when the number of columns of $A$ equals the number of rows of $B$.
Column $k$ of $AB$ is the [[matrix–vector product|~5bc8da49]] of $A$ with column $k$ of $B$.
The product is [[associative|~ca0ef042]] and [[distributive|~5d49d36c]], but it is [[not commutative|~8fd7308e]].
If $A$ and $B$ are the matrices of two linear maps, then $AB$ is the matrix of their composition.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To find the entry in row $i$ and column $k$ of $AB$, go along row $i$ of $A$ and down column $k$ of $B$.
Multiply the pairs of numbers and add the products.
For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 11 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$.
