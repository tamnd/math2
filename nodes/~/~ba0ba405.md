## Example

Let $A$ and $B$ be distinct atoms.
The formula $A \multimap (B \multimap A)$, that is $A^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} (B^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} A)$, has no derivation in the one-sided calculus of [[linear logic|H408H4WHBA]] without exponentials.
By [[cut elimination for multiplicative linear logic|~ac5cd644]], it is enough to show that it has no cut-free derivation.

1. The sequent $\vdash A^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} (B^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} A)$ has one formula, a par.
   It is not an axiom, since an axiom has two formulas.
2. Each rule other than the axiom has a principal formula with its connective.
   So the last rule is ${\mathbin{\wp}}$, with premise $\vdash A^{\perp}, B^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} A$.
3. That sequent is not an axiom: an axiom $\vdash C^{\perp}, C$ would need $B^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} A = A$.
   Its only compound formula is $B^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} A$, so its last rule is ${\mathbin{\wp}}$, with premise $\vdash A^{\perp}, B^{\perp}, A$.
4. The sequent $\vdash A^{\perp}, B^{\perp}, A$ has three atomic formulas.
   It is not an axiom, and no rule has an atomic principal formula.
5. So no cut-free derivation exists, and the formula is not derivable.

## Remarks

In classical and intuitionistic logic, the corresponding formula $A \to (B \to A)$ is derivable [[by weakening|~f76d7f8b]].
The step that fails is step 4: the sequent $\vdash A^{\perp}, A$ is an axiom, and only weakening could add $B^{\perp}$ to it.
With an exponential on $B$, the formula $A \multimap (!B \multimap A)$ is [[derivable|~e9d02b4f]].
In [[affine logic|K67C2N2QAH]], weakening is a rule, and the formula is derivable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Linear logic asks that every hypothesis is used.
The formula says "from $A$, and then from $B$, get $A$".
The hypothesis $B$ is never used, so linear logic rejects the formula.

### L2 human

We confirm the result with the phase space $\mathbb{Z}/2$ of the [[phase semantics example|~d55f75f4]], where $\bot = \set{0}$.

1. Interpret $A$ as $\set{0}$ and $B$ as $\set{1}$.
   Every subset of $\mathbb{Z}/2$ is a fact there.
2. The sequent $\vdash A^{\perp}, B^{\perp}, A$ is valid if $x + y + z \in \bot$ whenever $x$, $y$, $z$ lie in the orthogonals of the facts of $A^{\perp}$, $B^{\perp}$ and $A$.
3. The fact of $A^{\perp}$ is $\set{0}^{\perp} = \set{0}$, with orthogonal $\set{0}$.
   The fact of $B^{\perp}$ is $\set{1}^{\perp} = \set{1}$, with orthogonal $\set{1}$.
   The orthogonal of $A$ is $\set{0}$.
4. So $x = 0$, $y = 1$, $z = 0$, and $x + y + z = 1 \notin \bot$.
5. So the sequent is not valid, and by the soundness of [[phase semantics|~3257a7fb]], it is not derivable.
6. The par rule can be inverted: a cut of $\vdash \Gamma, C \mathbin{\wp} D$ with $\vdash C^{\perp} \otimes D^{\perp}, C, D$ derives $\vdash \Gamma, C, D$.
   So the formula is not derivable either.

The typical use of the syntactic argument is a short proof search.
A trap: the search in steps 1 to 5 is complete only because cut elimination holds; with cut, a derivation could pass through formulas that are not subformulas.
