## Summary

This area studies integral transforms as tools of computation, and the asymptotic methods of analysis.
An integral transform sends a function to a new function by integration against a fixed kernel, as the Laplace transform does with an exponential kernel.
Transforms change linear differential and integral equations into algebraic equations, and asymptotic methods give approximate answers when a parameter is large or small.
Heaviside used an operational calculus to solve the equations of electrical circuits at the end of the nineteenth century, and the Laplace transform later gave this calculus a rigorous base.
This topic collects the transform and asymptotics pages of math2: the Laplace, Mellin, Hilbert, Stieltjes, Hankel, Radon and Z-transforms, operational calculus, moment problems, asymptotic expansions, Laplace's method and steepest descent, the WKB approximation, perturbation methods and boundary layers, and resurgence.
Harmonic analysis, special functions and approximation theory have their own topics.
