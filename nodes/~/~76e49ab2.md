## Proof

Fix $T^*$, $L^*$ and $\Phi$ as in the statement.
For each $n$, $\Phi$ contains, for every $L^*$-formula $\varphi(x_1, \dots, x_n)$, either $\varphi$ or $\neg \varphi$, because it is the type of an indiscernible sequence.

### Step 1: existence in part 1

1. Let $(b_k)_{k \in K}$ be the infinite indiscernible sequence in $\mathcal{M}^* \models T^*$ with type $\Phi$.
2. Add constants $d_i$ for $i \in I$, and let $\Sigma = T^* \cup \set{\varphi(d_{\bar{\imath}}) : \varphi \in \Phi, \ \bar{\imath} \text{ increasing in } I}$.
3. A finite part of $\Sigma$ mentions finitely many $d_i$. Interpret them by $b_k$ with the same order pattern, which exists as $K$ is infinite. Indiscernibility of $(b_k)$ gives the finite part.
4. By the [[compactness theorem|1NR3HAWG9D]], $\Sigma$ has a model $\mathcal{N}$. Put $a_i = d_i^{\mathcal{N}}$; then $(a_i)_{i \in I}$ is indiscernible with type $\Phi$, by the completeness of $\Phi$.
5. Let $H$ be the Skolem hull of $\set{a_i : i \in I}$ in $\mathcal{N}$. As $T^*$ is a Skolem theory, $H$ is an [[elementary substructure|8WZF9SBP39]] of $\mathcal{N}$, by the properties of [[Skolem hulls|NKQY9NB955]].
6. So $H \models T^*$, and $(a_i)$ has type $\Phi$ in $H$. Put $\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi) = H$.

### Step 2: uniqueness in part 1

Let $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{H}'$ be Skolem hulls of sequences $(a_i)$ and $(a'_i)$ indexed by $I$, both with type $\Phi$.

1. Every element of $\mathcal{H}$ has the form $t(a_{\bar{\imath}})$ for an $L^*$-term $t$ and an increasing tuple $\bar{\imath}$, since the hull is the generated substructure.
2. Define $g(t(a_{\bar{\imath}})) = t(a'_{\bar{\imath}})$.
3. Let $\varphi$ be an atomic formula, and let $t_1(a_{\bar{\imath}_1}), \dots, t_r(a_{\bar{\imath}_r})$ be elements. Merge all indices into one increasing tuple $\bar{k}$, so that $\varphi(t_1, \dots, t_r)$ becomes a formula $\chi(a_{\bar{k}})$.
4. Then $\chi(a_{\bar{k}})$ holds exactly when $\chi \in \Phi$, exactly when $\chi(a'_{\bar{k}})$ holds.
5. With $\varphi$ the equality, item 4 shows that $g$ is well defined and injective. With the other atomic formulas, it shows that $g$ is an embedding.
6. The map $g$ is onto because $\mathcal{H}'$ is generated by the $a'_i$. So $g$ is an isomorphism with $g(a_i) = a'_i$.

### Step 3: parts 2 and 3

1. Let $f \colon I \to J$ be an [[order embedding|AZES1C6VMT]]. The family $(a_{f(i)})_{i \in I}$ in $\operatorname{EM}(J, \Phi)$ is indiscernible with type $\Phi$, because $f$ maps increasing tuples to increasing tuples.
2. Its Skolem hull $H'$ is an elementary substructure of $\operatorname{EM}(J, \Phi)$, as in step 1.
3. Step 2 gives an isomorphism $\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi) \to H'$ with $a_i \mapsto a_{f(i)}$. Composed with the inclusion, it is an [[elementary embedding|CGH8C8EA9N]] $\hat{f}$.
4. $\hat{f}$ is unique, since an embedding is fixed by its values on generators.
5. For $f \in \operatorname{Aut}(I)$, the image of $\hat{f}$ contains all generators, so $\hat{f}$ is onto and is an automorphism.
6. By uniqueness, $\widehat{f \circ g} = \hat{f} \circ \hat{g}$, since both extend $a_i \mapsto a_{f(g(i))}$. If $f \ne \id$, then $\hat{f}$ moves some $a_i$, as the $a_i$ are distinct. So the homomorphism is injective.

### Step 4: part 4

1. By step 2, every element is $t(a_{\bar{\imath}})$ with an $L^*$-term $t$ and a finite tuple $\bar{\imath}$ from $I$.
2. There are $\abs{L^*} = \abs{L} + \aleph_0$ terms, and $\abs{I}$ finite tuples, as $I$ is infinite.
3. So $\abs{\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)} \le \abs{I} + \abs{L} + \aleph_0$. The $a_i$ are distinct, since $x_1 \ne x_2$ is in $\Phi$, so $\abs{I} \le \abs{\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)}$.

### Step 5: part 5

1. Let $A$ be a countable subset. Each element of $A$ is a term in finitely many generators, so $A \subseteq \operatorname{Sk}(a_i : i \in I_0)$ for a countable $I_0 \subseteq I$.
2. For $j \in I \setminus I_0$, let $c(j)$ be the least element of $I_0$ above $j$, or $\infty$ when there is none. It exists as $I$ is a [[well-order|01CGSJ0GNQ]].
3. For $\bar{\jmath} = (j_1, \dots, j_n)$, the *pattern* of $\bar{\jmath}$ records the order relations among the $j_l$, and for each $l$ either $j_l \in I_0$ or the value $c(j_l)$. There are countably many patterns.
4. Let $\bar{\jmath}$ and $\bar{k}$ have the same pattern, and let $\bar{\imath}$ be a finite tuple from $I_0$. Then $\bar{\imath}\bar{\jmath}$ and $\bar{\imath}\bar{k}$ have the same order type.
5. By indiscernibility, $a_{\bar{\imath}} a_{\bar{\jmath}}$ and $a_{\bar{\imath}} a_{\bar{k}}$ satisfy the same formulas. So $t(a_{\bar{\jmath}})$ and $t(a_{\bar{k}})$ have the same [[type|9V71Q3SD1G]] over $\operatorname{Sk}(a_i : i \in I_0)$, and over $A$.
6. The type of $t(a_{\bar{\jmath}})$ over $A$ depends only on the countably many choices of $t$ and of the pattern. So countably many types over $A$ are realized.

### Step 6: the reduct to $L$

The [[reduct|EY20BF96SJ]] to $L$ of a model of $T^* \supseteq T$ is a model of $T$.
An elementary $L^*$-embedding is an elementary $L$-embedding, and an $L^*$-automorphism is an $L$-automorphism.
Two elements with the same $L^*$-type over $A$ have the same $L$-type over $A$, so the count in part 5 holds for the reduct.
Part 4 concerns the universe only, so it holds for the reduct as well.

## Idea

The type $\Phi$ is a complete description of the generators, so the Skolem hull of any sequence with type $\Phi$ is determined up to isomorphism.
Each order embedding moves a sequence with type $\Phi$ to another one, and uniqueness turns this into an elementary embedding.
Few types over countable sets come from the few ways in which a finite tuple of a well-order sits over a countable subset.

## Variants

One can also define $\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)$ as a term model: its elements are the terms $t(x_{\bar{\imath}})$, with two terms identified when the corresponding formula is in $\Phi$.
This avoids the choice of an ambient model, and step 2 becomes the proof that the term model is well defined.
In a monster model of $T^*$ the construction is the hull of an indiscernible sequence of the monster model, and step 1 is not needed.
