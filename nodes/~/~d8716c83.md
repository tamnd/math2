## Summary

Computability theory is the study of what an algorithm can compute and of the relative difficulty of the problems that no algorithm solves.
A function on the natural numbers is computable when a Turing machine, or an equivalent model of computation, gives its value for every input.
The theory sets the limits of algorithms, and it supplies the methods that show that problems in logic, algebra and analysis have no algorithmic solution.
Church, Turing, Kleene and Post founded the subject in the 1930s, when they made the notion of an algorithm precise.
This topic collects the computability theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Mathematical logic, set theory, type theory and constructive mathematics, and the theory of computation have their own topics.
