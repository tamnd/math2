## Statement

Let $z$ be a complex number with $z \neq 0$, let $r = |z|$ be its [[modulus|~a255a1b0]] and let $\theta = \operatorname{Arg} z$ be its [[principal argument|~5f7ab665]].
Then
$$z = r (\cos \theta + i \sin \theta) = r e^{i \theta}.$$

## Remarks

The second equality is [[Euler's formula|~573b73e7]].
The pair $(r, \theta)$ gives the polar coordinates of the point $z$ in the [[complex plane|~d5458ec5]].
Polar form makes products easy: $r e^{i \theta} \cdot s e^{i \varphi} = r s \, e^{i (\theta + \varphi)}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

You can describe a point of the plane by its distance from $0$ and its direction.
For example, $1 + i$ has distance $\sqrt{2}$ and direction $\pi/4$, so $1 + i = \sqrt{2} \, e^{i \pi/4}$.
