## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
The [[worked inversion|~7e5fa2f4]] gives $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} -24 & 18 & 5 \\ 20 & -15 & -4 \\ -5 & 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
We solve $Ax = b_1$ and $Ax = b_2$ for $b_1 = (6, 5, 11)^{\mathsf{T}}$ and $b_2 = (1, 0, 0)^{\mathsf{T}}$.

1. Entry 1 of $A^{-1} b_1$ is $-24 \cdot 6 + 18 \cdot 5 + 5 \cdot 11 = -144 + 90 + 55 = 1$.
2. Entry 2 is $20 \cdot 6 - 15 \cdot 5 - 4 \cdot 11 = 120 - 75 - 44 = 1$.
3. Entry 3 is $-5 \cdot 6 + 4 \cdot 5 + 1 \cdot 11 = -30 + 20 + 11 = 1$.
4. So $x = (1, 1, 1)^{\mathsf{T}}$. Check: $A(1, 1, 1)^{\mathsf{T}} = (1 + 2 + 3, \; 0 + 1 + 4, \; 5 + 6 + 0)^{\mathsf{T}} = (6, 5, 11)^{\mathsf{T}}$.
5. For $b_2 = e_1$, the product $A^{-1} e_1$ is column 1 of $A^{-1}$, so $x = (-24, 20, -5)^{\mathsf{T}}$.
6. Check: $A(-24, 20, -5)^{\mathsf{T}} = (-24 + 40 - 15, \; 0 + 20 - 20, \; -120 + 120 + 0)^{\mathsf{T}} = (1, 0, 0)^{\mathsf{T}}$.
7. By the [[solution theorem|3RQX3T70NX]], these are the only solutions.

## Remarks

The two systems together are the matrix equation $AX = B$ with $B = [b_1 \; b_2]$, and $X = A^{-1}B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -24 \\ 1 & 20 \\ 1 & -5 \end{pmatrix}$.
Once $A^{-1}$ is known, each new right-hand side costs $9$ multiplications here.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We computed the inverse once, and then each system needs only one matrix times vector.

### L2 human

The point is reuse: the second system costs no new row reduction.
A trap: a small change in $b$ can cause a large change in $x$, since $A^{-1}$ has large entries such as $-24$.
This is a first sign of the sensitivity that numerical analysis measures.
