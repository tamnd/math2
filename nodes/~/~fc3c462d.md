## Summary

*Product fuzzy logic* $\Pi$ is the logic of the product [[t-norm|~da122496]] $x \cdot y$ and its residuum on $[0, 1]$.
It is the extension of [[BL|~c93e8aa5]] by one axiom of cancellation; see [[the definition|~9e745f02]].
Its algebraic models are the product algebras.
With Łukasiewicz and Gödel logic, it is one of the three basic t-norm fuzzy logics that Hájek studied in his 1998 book.

## Motivation

The product is the third basic continuous t-norm, beside the minimum and the Łukasiewicz t-norm.
Wikipedia states that every strict t-norm, that is, every continuous and strictly monotone t-norm, is isomorphic to the product.
So product logic is the logic of each strict t-norm.
In the Mostert-Shields decomposition, product components are one of the two kinds of Archimedean pieces.

## Notation

Strong conjunction is $\otimes$, read as $x \cdot y$; SEP writes $\&$.
The residuum is $x \Rightarrow_\Pi y = 1$ if $x \le y$, and $y / x$ otherwise.
Negation is $\neg x = x \Rightarrow_\Pi 0$, which is $1$ at $x = 0$ and $0$ elsewhere.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[The cancellation axiom in the product algebra|~4c1a0ee2]] checks the defining axiom at explicit values and in general.
2. [[The residua at 0.7 and 0.4|~5d915ea4]] give $0.7 \Rightarrow_\Pi 0.4 = \tfrac47$.
3. [[Divisibility for the basic t-norms|~9a6f1b0e]] includes $0.7 \cdot \tfrac47 = 0.4$.
4. The two-element Boolean algebra is a product algebra, since $\set{0, 1}$ is closed under the product and its residuum.

### Non-examples

1. [[The Łukasiewicz and Gödel algebras refute cancellation|~dba17db1]], so they are not product algebras.
2. [[The nilpotent minimum|~12981db7]] does not give a BL-algebra, so it gives no product algebra.

### Basic properties

1. Product logic is BL plus $\neg \varphi \lor ((\varphi \to \varphi \otimes \psi) \to \psi)$, as SEP states.
2. Product and Gödel logic have the same negation; so $\neg \neg p \to p$ has value $1 \Rightarrow p = p < 1$ at every $p \in (0, 1)$ in both.
3. The contraction $p \to p \otimes p$ fails: at $p = \tfrac12$ it has value $\tfrac14 / \tfrac12 = \tfrac12$.
4. The three basic logics are pairwise incomparable.
   Gödel logic proves $p \to p \otimes p$, Łukasiewicz logic proves $\neg \neg p \to p$, product logic proves cancellation, and the other two logics refute each of these.
5. SEP reports that the first-order tautologies of product logic over $[0, 1]$ are as complex as true arithmetic (Montagna 2001).
6. Wikipedia states that the Łukasiewicz t-norm is isomorphic to the product t-norm cut at $\tfrac14$: $\max(\tfrac14, x \cdot y)$ on $[\tfrac14, 1]$.

### History

Wikipedia reports that Hájek's 1998 monograph presented the logics of the three basic continuous t-norms, Łukasiewicz, Gödel and product, in propositional and first-order form.
SEP gives the axiomatization of product logic as an extension of BL.

### Common errors

1. Assuming that product logic has an involutive negation. Its negation takes only the values $0$ and $1$.
2. Reading $\Rightarrow_\Pi$ as $y / x$ for all inputs. For $x \le y$ the value is $1$, and $y / x$ is not even defined at $x = 0$.
3. Assuming that every continuous t-norm with no idempotents other than $0$ and $1$ is isomorphic to the product.
   The Łukasiewicz t-norm is such a t-norm, and it is nilpotent, not strict.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Product logic reads "and" as multiplication of degrees of truth.
So "$p$ and $p$" is less true than $p$, unless $p$ is $0$ or $1$.
"Not $p$" is fully true only when $p$ is fully false.

### L2 human

To compute a value, multiply for $\otimes$, divide for $\to$ when the antecedent is larger, and take $\min$ and $\max$ for $\land$ and $\lor$.
In the standard algebra, the cancellation axiom says: if $x > 0$, then $x \Rightarrow_\Pi (x \cdot y) \le y$.
That is, multiplication by $x$ followed by division by $x$ gives $y$ back.
A trap: the rule fails at $x = 0$, and the disjunct $\neg \varphi$ covers that case.

### L3 human

Product algebras are the BL-algebras that satisfy the cancellation axiom.
The standard product algebra is the negative cone $(0, 1]$ of the ordered group $(\mathbb{R}_{> 0}, \cdot)$, with a new bottom $0$ added.
Sub-area 27 treats the algebraic theory of such residuated lattices.
