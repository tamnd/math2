## Example

Let $S_4$ be the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] of the permutations of $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, with identity $e$.
Permutations compose from right to left.
The [[order|0B85S2V2DR]] of $S_4$ is $4! = 24$.
The group $S_4$ is a [[CLT group|~d0e7afc6]], but it is not a [[supersolvable group|AWWM9RWETX]].

Part 1: $S_4$ has a [[subgroup|0X1JXT47GA]] of each order that divides $24$.

1. The divisors of $24$ are $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $12$ and $24$.
2. Order $1$: the subgroup $\{e\}$.
3. Order $2$: the subgroup $\{e, (1\,2)\}$, because $(1\,2)^2 = e$.
4. Order $3$: the subgroup $\{e, (1\,2\,3), (1\,3\,2)\}$ generated by the [[cycle|0Z05VCBQ7Y]] $(1\,2\,3)$ of length $3$.
5. Order $4$: the subgroup $\{e, (1\,2\,3\,4), (1\,3)(2\,4), (1\,4\,3\,2)\}$ generated by the $4$-cycle $(1\,2\,3\,4)$.
6. Order $6$: the permutations that fix the point $4$. They form a copy of $S_3$, with $3! = 6$ elements.
7. Order $8$: the [[dihedral group of order 8|054SBCQ93T]], generated by $r = (1\,2\,3\,4)$ and $s = (1\,3)$.
8. Its elements are $e$, $r$, $r^2 = (1\,3)(2\,4)$, $r^3 = (1\,4\,3\,2)$, $(1\,3)$, $(2\,4)$, $(1\,2)(3\,4)$ and $(1\,4)(2\,3)$.
9. This set is closed under products, because $s r s^{-1} = r^{-1}$. Thus each product has the form $r^i$ or $r^i s$.
10. Order $12$: the [[alternating group|0Z5905Q73D]] $A_4$ of the even permutations.
11. Order $24$: the whole group $S_4$.
12. Thus each divisor of $24$ is the order of a subgroup, and $S_4$ is CLT.

Part 2: $S_4$ is not supersolvable.

13. Assume that $S_4$ is supersolvable. Then there is a series $\{e\} = G_0 \le G_1 \le \cdots \le G_r = S_4$.
14. Each $G_i$ is normal in $S_4$, and each quotient $G_{i+1}/G_i$ is cyclic.
15. Let $i$ be the smallest index with $G_i \ne \{e\}$. Then $G_{i-1} = \{e\}$.
16. Thus $G_i \cong G_i/G_{i-1}$ is cyclic, nontrivial and [[normal|0CB0DX5BVV]] in $S_4$.
17. Let $p$ be a prime that divides $|G_i|$. Let $P$ be the subgroup of order $p$ of $G_i$.
18. The cyclic group $G_i$ has exactly one subgroup of order $p$, [[by this result|0423TXR9AN]].
19. For $g \in S_4$, the conjugate $gPg^{-1}$ lies in $gG_ig^{-1} = G_i$, and it has order $p$.
20. Thus $gPg^{-1} = P$ by step 18. So $P$ is a normal subgroup of $S_4$ of prime order.
21. A normal subgroup is a union of [[conjugacy classes|0DEBXAJ5SA]].
22. The classes of $S_4$ are the [[cycle types|0J438GAC8K]]. Their sizes are $1$, $6$, $3$, $8$ and $6$.
23. The class of size $1$ is $\{e\}$. A union with $e$ has size $1$, or at least $1 + 3 = 4$.
24. Thus no normal subgroup of $S_4$ has order $2$ or $3$. This contradicts step 20.
25. Thus $S_4$ is not supersolvable.

## Remarks

The example shows that the inclusion of the supersolvable groups in the CLT groups is strict.
The normal subgroups of $S_4$ are $\{e\}$, the [[Klein four-group|0PMRQJVTME]] $V_4 = \{e, (1\,2)(3\,4), (1\,3)(2\,4), (1\,4)(2\,3)\}$, $A_4$ and $S_4$.
Their orders are $1$, $4$, $12$ and $24$.
The subgroup $A_4$ of $S_4$ is [[not CLT|0PDBVPP233]], so a subgroup of a CLT group need not be CLT.
For $n = 3$, the group $S_3$ is CLT and also supersolvable, with the series $\{e\} \le A_3 \le S_3$.
For $n \ge 5$, the group $S_n$ is not solvable, so it is not CLT.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The group $S_4$ is the set of the $24$ ways to shuffle four cards.
The numbers that divide $24$ are $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $12$ and $24$.
For each of these numbers, some subgroup of the shuffles has exactly that many elements.
The $8$ symmetries of a square with corners $1$, $2$, $3$ and $4$ give the subgroup of size $8$.

### L2 human

We check the class sizes of step 22.

1. There is $1$ identity element.
2. There are $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ transpositions, such as $(1\,2)$.
3. There are $3$ products of two disjoint transpositions: $(1\,2)(3\,4)$, $(1\,3)(2\,4)$ and $(1\,4)(2\,3)$.
4. There are $4 \cdot 2 = 8$ cycles of length $3$. Each one fixes one of $4$ points and is one of $2$ cycles on the rest.
5. There are $3! = 6$ cycles of length $4$.
6. The sum is $1 + 6 + 3 + 8 + 6 = 24$, as it must be.

The typical use of the example is as a counterexample to "CLT implies supersolvable".
A trap is to think that the subgroup of order $8$ is normal.
It is a Sylow $2$-subgroup, and $S_4$ has $3$ of them, so none of them is normal.
