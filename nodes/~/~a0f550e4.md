## Statement

Consider formulas of [[linear temporal logic|RMJ56ZPA63]], with $X$ and $U$, and finite [[Kripke structures|RFQ8DJZNQT]] $M$ with total transition relation.

1. Satisfiability. The problem "given $\varphi$, is there an infinite word $w$ with $w \models \varphi$?" is [[PSPACE-complete|JWC9CE07Q2]].
2. Model checking. The problem "given $M$, a state $s$ and $\varphi$, does $s$ satisfy $\varphi$?" is PSPACE-complete.
3. Time bound. Model checking can be done in time $\lvert M \rvert \cdot 2^{O(\lvert\varphi\rvert)}$, where $\lvert M \rvert$ is the number of states plus the number of edges.

Here satisfaction by a state is the universal reading of [[the definition|8S64VZ6A1S]]: every infinite path from $s$ satisfies $\varphi$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. The operator $X$ is used in the lower bound proof below, to compare a tape cell with the same cell of the next configuration. For the fragment with $F$ and its dual $G$ only, Sistla and Clarke showed that satisfiability is NP-complete.
2. The transition relation is total so that every state has an infinite path. Otherwise a dead-end state satisfies every formula, and the problem changes.
3. The structure is finite, so that it can be given as an input. Its size enters the time bound of part 3.

### Consequences

1. Validity of LTL formulas is PSPACE-complete, since PSPACE is closed under complement.
2. For a fixed formula, model checking is linear in the size of the structure. This is why LTL model checking is used on large systems with short specifications.
3. An algorithm polynomial in both $\lvert M \rvert$ and $\lvert\varphi\rvert$ would give $\mathrm{P} = \mathrm{PSPACE}$, by part 2.

### Generalizations

1. Adding past operators keeps both problems PSPACE-complete.
2. For [[CTL*|~2b6e0b85]], model checking is still PSPACE-complete, but satisfiability becomes 2EXPTIME-complete.
3. For [[CTL|F6JA5C5BJX]], model checking is in polynomial time, see [[the CTL model checking theorem|~3c680624]].

## History

Sistla and Clarke proved the PSPACE-completeness of satisfiability and model checking in "The complexity of propositional linear temporal logics", Journal of the ACM, 1985.
Lichtenstein and Pnueli stressed in 1985 that the time is linear in the size of the structure.
The automata-theoretic proof of the upper bound is due to Vardi and Wolper (1986).

## Explanations

### L1 human

A computer can decide whether an LTL requirement can be met at all, and whether a finite system meets it.
It needs memory that grows only polynomially with the formula.
Time can grow exponentially with the formula, but only linearly with the system.
So long formulas are costly, while large systems are manageable.

### L2 human

We see where the two sizes enter for $M$ with $10^6$ states and $\varphi = G(\mathit{req} \to F\,\mathit{grant})$.

1. The automaton for $\neg\varphi$ has a few states, independent of $M$.
2. The product of $M$ with this automaton has a few million states.
3. A search for a reachable accepting cycle in the product takes time linear in its size.

A trap: PSPACE-hardness does not mean that every formula is hard; it is a worst-case statement over all formulas.

### L3 human

The upper bound comes from [[the translation into Büchi automata|~6369ab54]] and an on-the-fly search that never stores it.
The lower bound encodes a polynomial-space Turing machine, with $X$ iterated polynomially often to reach the same cell in the next configuration.
For CTL the structure and the formula are processed together bottom-up, which explains its polynomial bound.
