## Summary

Let $T$ be a [[theory|92SWXST1ZM]] in a signature $L$ and let $\Delta$ be a set of $L$-[[formulas|HYJ10AF6RT]].
$T$ has *elimination relative to $\Delta$* if every $L$-formula $\varphi(\bar x)$ is equivalent modulo $T$ to a Boolean combination of formulas $\delta(\bar x)$ in $\Delta$.
[[Quantifier elimination|7FDDXV3JK7]] is the case where $\Delta$ is the set of atomic formulas.
In a many-sorted signature, one says that $T$ eliminates the quantifiers over one sort relative to the other sorts if every formula is equivalent to one whose quantifiers range only over the other sorts.
The method reduces the theory of a structure to the theory of simpler pieces, such as the residue field and the value group of a valued field.

## Motivation

Many natural theories have no quantifier elimination in their usual signature, and adding a predicate for each definable set gives nothing.
Often, though, all quantifiers can be pushed into a small, well understood class of formulas, or into a simpler auxiliary structure.
Then questions about the whole theory, such as completeness, decidability or the form of definable sets, reduce to the same questions for the auxiliary part.
The prime example is the model theory of Henselian valued fields, where the quantifiers over the field are eliminated in favor of the residue field and the value group.

## Notation

One also says that $T$ has *$\Delta$-elimination*, or that $\Delta$ is an *elimination set* for $T$.
For valued fields, "field quantifier elimination" means elimination of the quantifiers over the valued field sort.

## Remarks

### Examples

- [[The integers with order, relative to divisibility formulas|~2cea7063]]: every formula of $(\Z, 0, 1, +, -, <)$ is a Boolean combination of atomic formulas and formulas $\exists y \, (t = n y)$.
- [[Real closed fields in the ring signature, relative to squares|~264c1474]]: every formula is a Boolean combination of equations and formulas $\exists y \, (t = y^2)$.
- Modules: by the Baur-Monk theorem, the theory of a module eliminates quantifiers relative to positive primitive formulas and invariant sentences.
- Henselian valued fields of residue characteristic $0$, in the Denef-Pas signature with an angular component map, eliminate the quantifiers over the field sort relative to the residue field and the value group; this is Pas's theorem.

### Non-examples

- [[Divisibility by 2 alone is not enough|~a2527617]]: the theory of $(\Z, 0, 1, +, -, <)$ has no elimination relative to atomic formulas and $\exists y \, (t = 2 y)$.
- Every theory has elimination relative to the set of all formulas, so this case says nothing; the method is useful only when $\Delta$ is small or simple.

### Basic properties

- Criterion: $\varphi(\bar x)$ is equivalent modulo $T$ to a Boolean combination of formulas of $\Delta$ if and only if any two tuples, in models of $T$, that satisfy the same formulas of $\Delta$ also agree on $\varphi$. The proof uses [[compactness|1NR3HAWG9D]].
- If $T$ has $\Delta$-elimination and every sentence in $\Delta$ is decided by $T$, then $T$ is complete.
- Elimination of the field quantifiers gives the Ax-Kochen-Ershov principle: two [[Henselian|3ACEE4N6NE]] valued fields of residue characteristic $0$ are elementarily equivalent if and only if their residue fields and their value groups are.

### History

Ax and Kochen, and independently Ershov, proved the transfer principle for Henselian valued fields in the 1960s.
It led to the [[Ax-Kochen theorem|PHT3TJCS90]] on zeros of forms over the $p$-adic numbers.

### Common errors

- Confusing relative elimination with quantifier elimination. After field quantifier elimination, quantifiers over the residue field and the value group remain.
- Forgetting that the result depends on the signature. Pas's theorem needs the angular component map, and without it the elimination fails in general.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Sometimes one cannot remove all the "there is" from statements about a structure.
But one can often move them to a simpler place.
For a valued field, every question about the field becomes a question about two simpler structures, the residue field and the value group.
Then what is known about these simpler structures answers the question about the field.

### L2 human

We see elimination relative to squares in the real field $(\R, +, -, \cdot, 0, 1)$.

1. In the ordered signature, $\mathrm{RCF}$ has quantifier elimination.
2. The order is defined by a formula with one quantifier: $x \le y \leftrightarrow \exists z \, (y - x = z^2)$.
3. Replace each $s < t$ in the quantifier-free formula of step 1 by $\neg \exists z \, (s - t = z^2)$.
4. The result is a Boolean combination of equations and formulas $\exists z \, (t = z^2)$.

The typical use is to understand definable sets in a signature where full quantifier elimination fails.
A trap is to think that the formulas of $\Delta$ are quantifier-free: here they have one existential quantifier.

### L3 human

Relative quantifier elimination is the main tool of the model theory of valued fields.
For Henselian fields of residue characteristic $0$ it gives Ax-Kochen-Ershov, and in mixed characteristic and for $p$-adic fields there are versions with extra structure, such as higher residue rings.
Elimination relative to positive primitive formulas plays the same role for modules.
Relative elimination is also a step toward full quantifier elimination: once the formulas of $\Delta$ are understood, one can add a predicate for each of them, as with the predicates $D_n$ of [[Presburger arithmetic|4W0E9G7PPV]].
