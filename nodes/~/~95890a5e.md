## Definition

Let $\mathbf{4} = \set{\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{F}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{N}}$ be the set of subsets of $\set{T, F}$, with $\mathbf{T} = \set{T}$, $\mathbf{F} = \set{F}$, $\mathbf{B} = \set{T, F}$ and $\mathbf{N} = \emptyset$.
Define the operations on $\mathbf{4}$ by the following clauses, for all $x, y \in \mathbf{4}$:

1. $T \in \neg x$ iff $F \in x$, and $F \in \neg x$ iff $T \in x$;
2. $T \in x \land y$ iff $T \in x$ and $T \in y$, and $F \in x \land y$ iff $F \in x$ or $F \in y$;
3. $T \in x \lor y$ iff $T \in x$ or $T \in y$, and $F \in x \lor y$ iff $F \in x$ and $F \in y$.

*First-degree entailment* $\mathsf{FDE}$ is the logic of the [[logical matrix|~a38ba6c1]] $\langle \mathbf{4}, \neg, \land, \lor, \set{\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{B}} \rangle$.
So $\Gamma \models_{\mathsf{FDE}} \varphi$ iff every valuation $v$ with $T \in v(\gamma)$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$ has $T \in v(\varphi)$.

The resulting tables:

| $x$ | $\neg x$ | $x \land \mathbf{N}$ | $x \land \mathbf{F}$ | $x \land \mathbf{T}$ | $x \land \mathbf{B}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathbf{N}$ | $\mathbf{N}$ | $\mathbf{N}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{N}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ |
| $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{T}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ |
| $\mathbf{T}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{N}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{T}$ | $\mathbf{B}$ |
| $\mathbf{B}$ | $\mathbf{B}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{F}$ | $\mathbf{B}$ | $\mathbf{B}$ |

Disjunction is given by De Morgan's law $x \lor y = \neg(\neg x \land \neg y)$.

## Notation

The values are also written $\mathsf{t}, \mathsf{f}, \mathsf{b}, \mathsf{n}$, or as bit pairs.
$\mathsf{FDE}$ is also called $\mathrm{BD}$, for Belnap and Dunn.
In the truth order $\le_t$, $\mathbf{F}$ is the bottom, $\mathbf{T}$ the top, and $\mathbf{N}$, $\mathbf{B}$ are incomparable; $\land$ and $\lor$ are meet and join for $\le_t$.

## Remarks

1. Check of one entry: $\mathbf{N} \land \mathbf{B}$. $T \notin \mathbf{N}$, so $T \notin \mathbf{N} \land \mathbf{B}$. $F \in \mathbf{B}$, so $F \in \mathbf{N} \land \mathbf{B}$. The value is $\mathbf{F}$.
2. Example: $\neg \neg p \models_{\mathsf{FDE}} p$, since $T \in \neg \neg x$ iff $F \in \neg x$ iff $T \in x$.
3. Non-example: $p \land \neg p \models_{\mathsf{FDE}} q$ fails at $p = \mathbf{B}$, $q = \mathbf{F}$.
4. The [[designated values|K6CVH3D7R8]] are the sets that contain $T$. Designating the sets that do not contain $F$ gives the same consequence relation, by SEP.
5. Restricting to $\set{\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{F}, \mathbf{N}}$ gives the [[strong Kleene matrix|~e50e75c5]], and restricting to $\set{\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{F}, \mathbf{B}}$ gives the matrix of $\mathsf{LP}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each sentence carries a set of reports: "true", "false", both or none.
"Not" swaps the two reports.
"And" is reported true when both parts are, and false when one part is.
An argument is valid when "reported true" passes from the premises to the conclusion.

### L2 human

Compute truth and falsity of a formula in two separate passes.
For example, at $p = \mathbf{B}$, $p \land \neg p$ is told true, since both $p$ and $\neg p$ are told true.
It is also told false, so $p \land \neg p = \mathbf{B}$.
A trap: the truth order and the inclusion order on $\mathbf{4}$ are different, and $\land$ is the meet only for the truth order.
