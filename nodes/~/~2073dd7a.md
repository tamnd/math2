## Proof

We prove parts 1, 2 and 3 of the statement.
The construction of part 2 follows Grädel, Kolaitis and Vardi.

Part 1: Scott normal form.

1. Let $\varphi_0 = \varphi$. While $\varphi_j$ contains a quantifier, choose an occurrence of a subformula $Q v \, \theta$ with $Q \in \set{\exists, \forall}$ and $\theta$ quantifier-free. Let $w$ be the other variable, so $\set{v, w} = \set{x, y}$.
2. Take a new unary symbol $P_j$. Let $\varphi_{j+1}$ be $\varphi_j$ with this occurrence replaced by $P_j(w)$, and let $\delta_j$ be $\forall w \, (P_j(w) \leftrightarrow Q v \, \theta)$.
3. The free variables of $Q v \, \theta$ are among $\set{w}$. So in a structure that satisfies $\delta_j$, the formulas $P_j(w)$ and $Q v \, \theta$ have the same truth value under every assignment.
4. Hence, in a structure that satisfies $\delta_j$, $\varphi_j$ and $\varphi_{j+1}$ have the same truth value under every assignment, by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on the context of the occurrence and the clauses of [[satisfaction|1WTCZB4DVB]].
5. Each step removes one quantifier, so the process stops with a quantifier-free $\varphi_k$ after $k$ steps, where $k$ is the number of quantifiers of $\varphi$.
6. In a structure $\mathcal{A}$ that satisfies $\delta_0, \ldots, \delta_{k-1}$, step 4 shows that $\varphi_k$ has the truth value of the sentence $\varphi$ under every assignment. So $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \forall y \, \varphi_k$.
7. For $Q = \exists$, $\delta_j$ is equivalent to $\forall w \, \forall v \, (\theta \to P_j(w)) \wedge \forall w \, \exists v \, (P_j(w) \to \theta)$. The second conjunct uses that the domain is nonempty: if $P_j(w)$ is false, every $v$ works.
8. For $Q = \forall$, $\delta_j$ is equivalent to $\forall w \, \forall v \, (P_j(w) \to \theta) \wedge \forall w \, \exists v \, (\theta \to P_j(w))$. The second conjunct says that $P_j(w)$ holds or some $v$ makes $\theta$ false.
9. If $w$ is $y$, we exchange the names $x$ and $y$ in the conjunct, which gives an equivalent sentence with prefix $\forall x \, \forall y$ or $\forall x \, \exists y$.
10. Let $\chi$ be the conjunction of $\varphi_k$ and of the matrices of the $\forall \forall$ conjuncts. Let $\chi_1, \ldots, \chi_m$ be the matrices of the $\forall \exists$ conjuncts, and add the conjunct $\forall x \, \exists y \, (x = y)$, so that $m \ge 1$.
11. Let $\psi = \forall x \, \forall y \, \chi \wedge \bigwedge_{i=1}^m \forall x \, \exists y \, \chi_i$. Each $\theta$ has length at most $\abs{\varphi}$, so $\psi$ has length $O(\abs{\varphi}^2)$ and is computed in polynomial time.
12. A model of $\psi$ satisfies every $\delta_j$, by steps 7 and 8, and satisfies $\forall x \, \forall y \, \varphi_k$. By step 6, it satisfies $\varphi$.
13. Let $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi$. Interpret $P_0, P_1, \ldots$ in this order by $P_j = \set{a : Q v \, \theta \text{ holds when } w \text{ is } a}$; this uses only the symbols already interpreted. Then each $\delta_j$ holds, and by step 6, $\forall x \, \forall y \, \varphi_k$ holds. So the expansion is a model of $\psi$.

Part 2: a small model of $\psi$.

Let $L'$ be the signature of $\psi$, and let $u$ be the number of its symbols.

14. The 1-type of an element $a$ is the set of atomic formulas $P(x)$ and $R(x, x)$ that hold at $a$. There are at most $2^u$ 1-types.
15. For $a \neq b$, the 2-type of $(a, b)$ is the set of atomic formulas in $x$ and $y$ that hold when $x$ is $a$ and $y$ is $b$. It contains the 1-type of $a$ in $x$, the 1-type of $b$ in $y$, and the facts $R(x, y)$ and $R(y, x)$ that hold.
16. For a quantifier-free $\chi(x, y)$, the truth of $\chi$ at $(a, b)$ with $a \neq b$ depends only on the 2-type of $(a, b)$. The truth of $\chi$ at $(a, a)$ depends only on the 1-type of $a$, since $x = y$ is then true.
17. Conversely, to define an $L'$-structure on a set $D$, it suffices to give a 1-type to each element and a 2-type to each pair $e \neq f$, compatible with the 1-types, with the type of $(f, e)$ obtained from that of $(e, f)$ by exchanging $x$ and $y$. Every atomic fact involves at most two elements.
18. Let $\mathcal{A} \models \psi$. A 1-type is royal if exactly one element of $\mathcal{A}$ has it, and that element is a king. Let $K$ be the set of kings and $\Pi$ the set of non-royal 1-types that occur in $\mathcal{A}$.
19. For each king $a$ and each $i$, choose $w_i(a)$ in $\mathcal{A}$ with $\mathcal{A} \models \chi_i[a, w_i(a)]$. The court $C$ is $K$ together with all $w_i(a)$. Then $\abs{C} \le (m + 1) \abs{K}$.
20. For each $\pi \in \Pi$, choose $r_\pi$ of type $\pi$ in $\mathcal{A}$, and for each $i$ choose $s_{\pi, i}$ with $\mathcal{A} \models \chi_i[r_\pi, s_{\pi, i}]$.
21. Let $D$ be the disjoint union of $C$ and the set of triples $(\pi, j, i)$ with $\pi \in \Pi$, $j \in \set{0, 1, 2}$ and $1 \le i \le m$. Give each $c \in C$ its 1-type in $\mathcal{A}$, and give $(\pi, j, i)$ the 1-type $\pi$.
22. Rule (a). Pairs inside $C$ get their 2-types in $\mathcal{A}$.
23. Rule (b). Let $e = (\pi, j, i')$ and $1 \le i \le m$, with $s = s_{\pi, i}$. If $s = r_\pi$, nothing is assigned. If $s$ is a king, the pair $(e, s)$ gets the 2-type of $(r_\pi, s)$.
24. Otherwise $s$ has a type $\pi' \in \Pi$, and the pair $(e, (\pi', j + 1 \bmod 3, i))$ gets the 2-type of $(r_\pi, s)$.
25. Rule (c). Let $c \in C \setminus K$ and $1 \le i \le m$, and choose $b$ with $\mathcal{A} \models \chi_i[c, b]$. If $b \in C$, nothing is assigned. Otherwise $b$ is not a king and has a type $\pi' \in \Pi$, and the pair $(c, (\pi', 0, i))$ gets the 2-type of $(c, b)$.
26. No pair gets two different 2-types. Rule (a) uses pairs inside $C$; rule (b) uses pairs of a triple with a king or with a triple; rule (c) uses pairs of a non-king of $C$ with a triple.
27. Within rule (b), a pair $(e, s)$ with a king $s$ always gets the type of $(r_\pi, s)$, whatever $i$ is. A triple of level $j$ points only to level $j + 1 \bmod 3$, so two triples never point to each other. Different $i$ give targets with different last coordinates.
28. Within rule (c), different $i$ give different targets $(\pi', 0, i)$.
29. Rule (d). Every other pair $e \neq f$ gets the 2-type of some pair $a \neq b$ in $\mathcal{A}$ with the 1-types of $e$ and $f$. Such a pair exists, because $e$ or $f$ is a triple, whose 1-type is non-royal and so has two elements.
30. Each assigned 2-type is the 2-type of a pair in $\mathcal{A}$ whose 1-types match, so step 17 defines a structure $\mathcal{D}$ on $D$.
31. $\mathcal{D} \models \forall x \, \forall y \, \chi$. A pair $e \neq f$ has the 2-type of a pair $a \neq b$ of $\mathcal{A}$, and $\mathcal{A} \models \chi[a, b]$, so step 16 applies. The pair $(e, e)$ has the 1-type of some $a$, and $\mathcal{A} \models \chi[a, a]$.
32. $\mathcal{D} \models \forall x \, \exists y \, \chi_i$ at a king $a$: the witness $w_i(a)$ lies in $C$, and rule (a) copies the 2-type from $\mathcal{A}$. If $w_i(a) = a$, step 16 applies to the 1-type.
33. At $c \in C \setminus K$: by rule (c), the witness is $b$ itself if $b \in C$, or the triple $(\pi', 0, i)$, which has the 2-type of $(c, b)$ with $c$.
34. At a triple $e = (\pi, j, i')$: by rule (b), the witness is $e$ itself if $s = r_\pi$, the king $s$, or a triple with the 2-type of $(r_\pi, s)$. In each case $\chi_i$ holds, by step 16.
35. So $\mathcal{D} \models \psi$, and by step 12, $\mathcal{D} \models \varphi$. Its size is $\abs{D} \le (m + 1) \abs{K} + 3 m \abs{\Pi} \le (4m + 1) 2^u$, since $\abs{K} + \abs{\Pi} \le 2^u$.
36. Here $u$ is at most the number of symbols of $\varphi$ plus $k$, and $m \le k + 1$, so $u$ and $m$ are at most $2 \abs{\varphi}$. Hence $\abs{D} \le (8 \abs{\varphi} + 1) \cdot 4^{\abs{\varphi}} \le 2^{c \abs{\varphi}}$ for a constant $c$. This proves part 2.

Part 3: decidability and the upper bound.

37. To decide whether $\varphi$ is satisfiable, compute $N = 2^{c \abs{\varphi}}$ and test all structures for the symbols of $\varphi$ with at most $N$ elements. By part 2, this test is correct, and it always stops.
38. A nondeterministic algorithm guesses one structure with at most $N$ elements and checks $\varphi$ in it. The check of a formula with two variables takes time polynomial in $N$ and $\abs{\varphi}$, so satisfiability is in NEXPTIME. This proves part 3.

## Idea

The Scott normal form reduces every two-variable sentence to universal conditions on pairs and one witness demand for each element.
Because only two variables occur, a structure is determined by the 1-types of its elements and the 2-types of its pairs.
A small model keeps the elements whose 1-type is unique, with their witnesses, and adds three copies of a representative of each other 1-type for each witness demand.
The copies in level $j$ take their witnesses from level $j + 1$, so no pair gets two conflicting types.

## Variants

- Mortimer's original proof of 1975 gave the finite model property with a larger bound.
- Scott's reduction of 1962 shows that the satisfiability of $\mathrm{FO}^2$ without equality reduces to the Gödel-Kalmár-Schütte class, which is decidable by the [[decidable prefix classes|~48b4c091]].
- For the counting extension $\mathrm{C}^2$, the small model method fails, since some satisfiable sentences have only infinite models. Pratt-Hartmann's procedure uses integer programming on the numbers of elements of each type.
