## Proof

We use the embedding test of [[the tests for quantifier elimination|~e415d689]]; the language has the constant $0$.
Let $M, N$ be real closed fields, let $A$ be a common substructure, let $\bar a$ be a tuple in $A$, and let $\varphi(\bar x, y)$ be a conjunction of literals with $M \models \varphi(\bar a, b)$ for some $b \in M$.
We show that $N \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$.

### Reduction to a common real closed field

1. $A$ is a subring of $M$ with the induced order, so it is an ordered integral domain. Its [[field of fractions|0F2PTGX1A2]] $F_M \subseteq M$ and its field of fractions $F_N \subseteq N$ are isomorphic over $A$ as [[ordered fields|0P3VCBDEE2]], since $u / v > 0$ if and only if $u v > 0$.
2. Let $R_M$ be the set of elements of $M$ algebraic over $F_M$. It is a real closed field, algebraic over $F_M$, with the order of $M$, so it is a [[real closure|778KFSM09P]] of $F_M$. In the same way $N$ contains a real closure $R_N$ of $F_N$.
3. Real closures are unique up to isomorphism over the base field. So there is an isomorphism of ordered fields $h \colon R_M \to R_N$ that is the identity on $A$.
4. Existential formulas go up from a substructure to a structure, by [[this result|C9494XYEMN]]. So it suffices to find $b' \in R_M$ with $R_M \models \varphi(\bar a, b')$: then $N \models \varphi(\bar a, h(b'))$.

### Finding a witness in $R_M$

1. Each literal of $\varphi(\bar a, y)$ can be written as $p(y) = 0$, $p(y) \neq 0$, $p(y) > 0$ or $p(y) \ge 0$, with $p \in R_M[y]$. Literals with $p = 0$ are either always true or always false, and we drop the true ones; there is no false one, since $b$ satisfies $\varphi$.
2. Case 1: some literal is $p(y) = 0$ with $p \neq 0$. Then $b$ is algebraic over $R_M$, hence over $F_M$, so $b \in R_M$. Take $b' = b$.
3. Case 2: no such literal. Let $c_1 < \cdots < c_m$ be the roots in $R_M$ of the nonzero polynomials that occur. In $R_M[y]$ each of them is a product of a constant, of factors $y - c_i$, and of factors $y^2 + u y + v$ with $u^2 < 4v$.
4. A factor $y^2 + u y + v = (y + \frac u 2)^2 + (v - \frac{u^2}{4})$ with $u^2 < 4v$ is positive at every point of $M$. So the roots of these polynomials in $M$ are the $c_i$, and on each open interval of $M$ between consecutive points of $-\infty, c_1, \dots, c_m, +\infty$, each polynomial has constant sign.
5. If $b = c_i$ for some $i$, take $b' = c_i$. Otherwise $b$ lies in one of these intervals, and we take $b'$ in the same interval and in $R_M$: the midpoint $\frac{c_i + c_{i+1}}{2}$, or $c_1 - 1$, or $c_m + 1$, or $0$ when $m = 0$.
6. Each polynomial has the same sign at $b'$ as at $b$, so $M \models \varphi(\bar a, b')$. The literals have parameters in $R_M$, so $R_M \models \varphi(\bar a, b')$.

So $N \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$, and by the embedding test $\mathrm{RCF}$ has quantifier elimination.

## Idea

Shrink the common substructure to the real closure of its fraction field, which both models contain in the same way, by uniqueness of the real closure.
Over a real closed field, the signs of polynomials in one variable depend only on the position of the variable relative to the finitely many roots, which already lie in that field.
So a witness in a large model can be moved to a witness in the small real closed field.

## Variants

### Sturm sequences

For the one-quantifier test, one counts the roots of $p$ with sign conditions on other polynomials by [[Sturm's theorem|R44CV0KD9T]] and its extension with a second polynomial.
The counts are read from the signs of the leading coefficients of a signed remainder sequence, and these coefficients are rational functions of the parameters.
A case distinction on the vanishing of coefficients turns the count into a quantifier-free formula; this is close to Tarski's original method.

### Cohen-Hörmander

One keeps a finite family of polynomials in $y$ closed under derivatives and remainders, together with its sign table along the real line.
By induction on degrees, the sign table is determined by the signs of the coefficients, and every condition "some $y$ has this sign pattern" is a quantifier-free condition on the coefficients.

### Saturation

One can also use a version of the extension test with a more saturated model $N$.
An element $b \in M$ transcendental over $R_M$ determines a cut in $R_M$, and if $N$ is $\abs{R_M}^+$-saturated, the image of this cut under $h$ is realized in $N$.
This extends the embedding by one element at a time, and the real closure of the new field is handled as in the first part.
