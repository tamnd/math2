## Summary

A counterexample to a universal statement $\forall x \in X, \, P(x)$ is an element $a \in X$ such that $P(a)$ is false.
One counterexample shows that the universal statement is false.

## Remarks

The number $2$ is a counterexample to "each prime number is odd".
This works because of the [[negation of a universal statement|~6e67a95d]]: $\neg \forall x \, P(x)$ is equivalent to $\exists x \, \neg P(x)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To show that a general claim is wrong, one bad case is enough.
"Each continuous function is differentiable" is false: $f(x) = |x|$ is continuous but not differentiable at $0$.
