## Statement

Let $n \ge 0$ be an integer and let $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$.
Then
$$\int_a^b x^n \, dx = \frac{b^{n+1} - a^{n+1}}{n + 1}.$$

## Remarks

By the [[power rule|~df6921ca]], $x^{n+1}/(n + 1)$ is an antiderivative of $x^n$. Apply the [[second fundamental theorem|~be640cd3]].
The same formula holds for each real exponent $r \ne -1$, on intervals inside $(0, \infty)$. The case $r = -1$ gives the [[logarithm|~5a4b36b7]].
Mathlib states it as `integral_pow`.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To integrate $x^n$, raise the power by one and divide by the new power.
For example, $\int_0^1 x^2 \, dx = 1/3$.
