## Example

A box with a weight of $10$ newtons rests on a ramp.
In coordinates with the $y$-axis up, the weight is $F = (0, -10)$, and the ramp rises in the direction $v = (4, 3)$.
We split $F$ into a part along the ramp and a part perpendicular to it with the [[vector projection|0JW13B48YP]].

1. The [[dot products|0S52TNNTH9]] are $F \cdot v = 0 \cdot 4 + (-10) \cdot 3 = -30$ and $v \cdot v = 16 + 9 = 25$.
2. The part along the ramp is $p = \tfrac{-30}{25} v = -\tfrac{6}{5}(4, 3) = (-4.8, -3.6)$.
3. Its [[length|0XKBKGTCPC]] is $\|p\| = \sqrt{23.04 + 12.96} = \sqrt{36} = 6$ newtons. It points down the ramp, since its coefficient $-\tfrac{6}{5}$ is negative.
4. The part perpendicular to the ramp is $q = F - p = (4.8, -6.4)$, with length $\sqrt{23.04 + 40.96} = \sqrt{64} = 8$ newtons.
5. Check: $q \cdot v = 19.2 - 19.2 = 0$, and $6^2 + 8^2 = 100 = \|F\|^2$.

## Remarks

The ramp makes the angle $\alpha$ with $\sin \alpha = \tfrac{3}{5}$ and $\cos \alpha = \tfrac{4}{5}$ with the ground.
So the results agree with the school formulas $10 \sin \alpha = 6$ and $10 \cos \alpha = 8$.
A steeper ramp in the direction $(3, 4)$ gives $8$ newtons along the ramp and $6$ newtons against it.
The part $q$ presses the box onto the ramp, and the ramp pushes back with a normal force $-q$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gravity pulls the box straight down with $10$ newtons.
On a slope, part of this pull drags the box down the slope, and part presses it into the slope.
The projection gives the first part, $6$ newtons, and the rest is $8$ newtons.
The two parts are at a right angle, so $6^2 + 8^2 = 10^2$.

### L2 human

In mechanics, we split a force along the directions that matter for the motion.
The vector method needs no angle, only the direction vector of the ramp.
A trap: the projection along the ramp has the same value for $v = (4, 3)$ and for $v = (-4, -3)$, so the sign of $c$ in step 2 tells the direction only relative to the chosen $v$.
