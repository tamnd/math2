## Definition

Let $V$ be a vector space over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ with a [[norm|~b05fd9e2]] $\|\cdot\|$, and let $d(x, y) = \|x - y\|$.
The space $(V, \|\cdot\|)$ is a Banach space when the metric space $(V, d)$ is [[complete|~aae963c7]].

## Remarks

Examples are $\mathbb{R}^n$ with any norm, the spaces [[ell p|~bd7407b7]] for $1 \le p \le \infty$, and $C_b(X)$ with the [[sup norm|~b55b8305]].
The space of polynomial functions on $[0, 1]$ with the sup norm is not a Banach space. Its completion is $C([0, 1])$.
In a normed space $V$, completeness is the same as: [[each absolutely convergent series converges|~af187288]].
Each [[finite-dimensional normed space is complete|~3636309a]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Banach space is a vector space where you can measure lengths, and where Cauchy sequences always converge.
So you can take limits in it freely, as in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
