## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a [[finite-dimensional|018W3V3TCT]] vector space over $F$, and let $U$ and $W$ be subspaces of $V$.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

1. $V = U \oplus W$.
2. $U \cap W = \{0\}$ and $\dim U + \dim W = \dim V$.
3. $U + W = V$ and $\dim U + \dim W = \dim V$.

Here $\dim$ is the [[dimension|05G2G0P224]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- $V$ must be finite-dimensional. In $F[x]$, let $U$ be spanned by $x^2, x^4, x^6, \dots$ and $W$ by $x, x^3, x^5, \dots$. Then $U \cap W = \{0\}$, and $\dim U + \dim W = \dim F[x]$ as infinite cardinals, but the constant $1$ is not in $U + W$.
- In condition 2, the dimension count is needed. The $x$-axis and the $y$-axis in $\R^3$ meet in $\{0\}$, but $1 + 1 \ne 3$, and they do not fill $\R^3$, see [[the two axes in space|~d559e535]].
- In condition 3, the dimension count is needed. The $xy$-plane and the $yz$-plane have sum $\R^3$, but $2 + 2 \ne 3$, and they meet in the $y$-axis.

### Consequences

- If $V = U \oplus W$, then $\dim W = \dim V - \dim U$. So all [[complements|0M83V17KHX]] of $U$ have the same dimension.
- A line and a plane through $0$ in $\R^3$ form a direct sum decomposition exactly when the line does not lie in the plane.
- Two subspaces of dimension $k$ and $n - k$ in $F^n$ need only one check: zero intersection, or sum equal to $F^n$.

### Converse

The criterion is an equivalence, so no converse is missing.

### Generalizations

- For subspaces $U_1, \dots, U_k$ of a finite-dimensional space, the sum is direct if and only if $\dim(U_1 + \cdots + U_k) = \dim U_1 + \cdots + \dim U_k$.
- The criterion is a special case of [[Grassmann's formula|06HTJNCMPA]] $\dim(U + W) + \dim(U \cap W) = \dim U + \dim W$.

## History

The criterion is a consequence of the dimension formula for a sum of subspaces, which goes back to Grassmann's *Ausdehnungslehre* of 1844 and 1862.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a space of dimension $n$, two subspaces split the space when they share only the zero vector and their dimensions add up to $n$.
Each condition alone is not enough, but the two together are.

### L2 human

We test $U = \operatorname{span}\{(1, 0, 1)\}$ and $W = \{(x, y, z) : x = z\}$ in $\R^3$.

1. $\dim U + \dim W = 1 + 2 = 3 = \dim \R^3$.
2. The vector $(1, 0, 1)$ satisfies $x = z$, so $U \subseteq W$ and $U \cap W = U \ne \{0\}$.
3. So condition 2 fails, and $\R^3 \ne U \oplus W$.

The trap is to check only the dimensions in step 1.
With $U = \operatorname{span}\{(1, 0, 0)\}$ instead, $U \cap W = \{0\}$, and the criterion gives $\R^3 = U \oplus W$.

### L3 human

The criterion is the statement that an injective linear map between spaces of the same finite dimension is bijective.
It is applied to the addition map $U \times W \to V$, whose source has dimension $\dim U + \dim W$.
