## Example

Let $A = (1, 0, 0)$, $B = (0, 2, 0)$ and $C = (0, 0, 3)$, the points where a plane meets the three axes.
We compute the area of the triangle $ABC$ with the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]].

1. Two sides from $A$: $u = B - A = (-1, 2, 0)$ and $v = C - A = (-1, 0, 3)$.
2. First component of $u \times v$: $2 \cdot 3 - 0 \cdot 0 = 6$.
3. Second component: $0 \cdot (-1) - (-1) \cdot 3 = 3$.
4. Third component: $(-1) \cdot 0 - 2 \cdot (-1) = 2$.
5. So $u \times v = (6, 3, 2)$, and its [[length|0XKBKGTCPC]] is $\sqrt{36 + 9 + 4} = \sqrt{49} = 7$.
6. The [[length formula|~bafd22bd]] says that $7$ is the area of the parallelogram on $u$ and $v$.
7. The triangle is half of this parallelogram, so its area is $\tfrac{7}{2}$.

## Remarks

The vector $(6, 3, 2)$ is also a normal vector of the plane through $A$, $B$ and $C$, which is $6x + 3y + 2z = 6$.
The shadows of the triangle on the three coordinate planes have areas $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 3$, $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1 \cdot 3 = \tfrac{3}{2}$ and $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1 \cdot 2 = 1$.
These are half the components $6$, $3$ and $2$, and indeed $3^2 + (\tfrac{3}{2})^2 + 1^2 = \tfrac{49}{4} = (\tfrac{7}{2})^2$.
If we choose the sides from $B$ instead of $A$, the cross product can change its sign, but not its length.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A triangle in space is hard to measure with a ruler.
The cross product of two sides gives an arrow whose length is twice the area of the triangle.
Here the arrow is $(6, 3, 2)$ with length $7$, so the triangle has area $3.5$.

### L2 human

The method works for any three points in space: take two sides from one vertex, compute their cross product, and halve its length.
The remark on the shadows is a three-dimensional Pythagorean theorem for areas.
A trap: the sides must start at the same vertex; $B - A$ and $B - C$ also work, but $B - A$ and $C - B$ need a check of signs.
