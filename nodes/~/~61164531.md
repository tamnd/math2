## Statement

On $V = \set{0, \tfrac12, 1}$ let $\neg x = 1 - x$, $x \land y = \min(x, y)$, $x \lor y = \max(x, y)$, and let $x \to_{Ł} y = \min(1, 1 - x + y)$.
A [[formula|TFDYXS3K1N]] $\varphi(p, q)$ *defines* a function $f \colon V^2 \to V$ when $\varphi$ evaluates to $f(x, y)$ at $p = x$, $q = y$, for all $x, y \in V$.

**Theorem.**

1. No formula built from $p$, $q$ with $\neg$, $\land$, $\lor$ defines $\to_{Ł}$.
2. This stays true when the constants $0$ and $1$ may also be used.
3. Conversely, $\land$ and $\lor$ are definable from $\neg$ and $\to_{Ł}$: $x \lor y = (x \to_{Ł} y) \to_{Ł} y$ and $x \land y = \neg(\neg x \lor \neg y)$.

The proof is in [[the proof by an invariant|~b88595da]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. The third value. On $\set{0, 1}$, implication is definable as $\neg x \lor y$, so the theorem needs the value $\tfrac12$.
2. The specific implication. Kleene's implication $\neg x \lor y$ is definable by its own formula; the theorem is about the entry $\tfrac12 \to_{Ł} \tfrac12 = 1$.
3. No constant $\tfrac12$ is needed in item 2; the obstacle is the order of information, not a missing constant.

### Consequences

1. The language of [[Łukasiewicz's three-valued logic|~d7a9d81e]] is strictly more expressive than that of [[Strong Kleene logic|SZHA10A9X7]] on the same values.
2. $\mathsf{K}_3$ has no tautologies, while $\mathrm{Ł}_3$ has $p \to_{Ł} p$; item 1 explains why no Kleene formula can play its role.

### Converse

Item 3 is the converse direction: the Kleene connectives are definable in $\mathrm{Ł}_3$.

### Generalizations

The same proof shows that no function $f$ with $f(\tfrac12, \dots, \tfrac12) \neq \tfrac12$ is definable from $\neg, \land, \lor$ without constants.
With constants $0$ and $1$, every definable function is monotone for the order of information, in which $\tfrac12$ lies below $0$ and $1$.
Every definable function also maps classical arguments to classical values, so the constant function $\tfrac12$ is monotone but not definable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Kleene's "not", "and" and "or" turn the middle value into the middle value.
A formula built from them alone therefore gives the middle value when all its letters have the middle value.
Łukasiewicz's "if $p$ then $p$" gives full truth there.
So it can not be written with Kleene's connectives.

### L2 human

1. Evaluate a candidate formula, such as $\neg p \lor q$, at $p = q = \tfrac12$.
2. The result is $\tfrac12$, while $\tfrac12 \to_{Ł} \tfrac12 = 1$.
3. So $\neg p \lor q$ does not define $\to_{Ł}$, and the proof shows that no candidate does.

The typical use is to compare the expressive power of two sets of connectives on the same values.
A trap: adding the constants $0$ and $1$ does not help, because of item 2.
