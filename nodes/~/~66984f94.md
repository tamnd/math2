## Example

On $V = \set{0, 1, 2}$ let $w(x, y) = \max(x, y) + 1 \bmod 3$.
Its table, with the row for $x$ and the column for $y$:

| $w$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $0$ |
| $1$ | $2$ | $2$ | $0$ |
| $2$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |

1. The diagonal gives $S$: $w(0, 0) = 1$, $w(1, 1) = 2$, $w(2, 2) = 0$.
2. Post's negation is $S^2$: $\neg x = w(w(x, x), w(x, x))$, with $\neg 0 = 2$, $\neg 1 = 0$, $\neg 2 = 1$.
3. $\max(x, y) = S^2(w(x, y))$. Check at $(1, 0)$: $w(1, 0) = 2$, then $S 2 = 0$ and $S 0 = 1 = \max(1, 0)$.
4. Check at $(2, 1)$: $w(2, 1) = 0$, then $S 0 = 1$ and $S 1 = 2 = \max(2, 1)$.
5. The constant $2$ is $\max(x, S x, S^2 x)$, a term in $w$ by steps 1 and 3.

By [[Webb's theorem|~02b9134b]], every three-valued function is now a term in $w$.

## Remarks

1. The table has no row and no column that is constant on all of $V$ except the row and column of $2$, where $w$ is $0$.
2. The value $w(x, y)$ depends on $x$ and $y$ only through $\max(x, y)$, so $w$ is symmetric.
3. In the same way, Łukasiewicz negation $2 - x$ is a term in $w$, by the construction in [[the three-valued Post example|~f9660d8c]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The table shows Webb's function on three values.
Putting the same value in twice moves it one step around the circle $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 0$.
With this step and the original function, we get "or" and "not" of Post's logic.

### L2 human

1. Read the diagonal of the table to get the shift.
2. Compose the shift with itself to undo the "$+1$".
3. Check the composite on a few pairs.

The typical use is a sanity check of the general proof on a small case.
A trap: the composite $S^2 \circ w$ is $\max$ only because $S^3$ is the identity; with mod $4$ you need $S^3 \circ w$.
