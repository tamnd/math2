## Statement

Let $\gamma \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}^n$ be a piecewise $C^1$ curve with image $C$, and let $\gamma^- \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}^n$, $\gamma^-(t) = \gamma(a + b - t)$, be the reversed curve.
For each continuous vector field $F$ on $C$ and each continuous function $f$ on $C$,
$$\int_{\gamma^-} F \cdot d\mathbf{r} = -\int_\gamma F \cdot d\mathbf{r}, \qquad \int_{\gamma^-} f \, ds = \int_\gamma f \, ds.$$

## Remarks

Proof: by the [[parametric formula|~469b0136]], $(\gamma^-)'(t) = -\gamma'(a + b - t)$. The substitution $u = a + b - t$ then gives both identities.
The [[line integral of a vector field|~e6672d67]] depends on the orientation of the curve. The [[line integral of a scalar field|~4adc4bba]] does not, because it uses only $\|\gamma'\|$.
More generally, under a $C^1$ change of parameter $\varphi$ with $\varphi' > 0$, the vector line integral does not change. With $\varphi' < 0$ it changes sign.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If a force helps you along a path, it works against you on the way back.
So the work, a line integral of a vector field, changes sign when you reverse the path. The length of the path and the mass of a wire do not.
