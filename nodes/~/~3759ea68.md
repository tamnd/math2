## Statement

Let $G$ be a [[graph|~3f82cef4]] and let $u, v$ be vertices of $G$.
Suppose that there is a [[walk|~8945d13d]] $W$ from $u$ to $v$.
Then there is a [[path|~ab8676be]] from $u$ to $v$ whose vertices and edges are vertices and edges of $W$.

## Remarks

Proof idea: if a walk visits a vertex $x$ twice, delete the closed part between the two visits. Each deletion makes the walk shorter, so the process stops with a path.
A consequence: a shortest walk from $u$ to $v$ is a path. Thus the [[distance|~bc6ed22e]] can be defined with paths.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If you can walk from $u$ to $v$, you can also do it without visiting any place twice.
Whenever you come back to a place, cut out the loop that you just made.
