## Statement

Let $(X, d)$ be a [[metric space|~200ea42b]] and let $A \subseteq X$.
If $A$ with the [[subspace metric|~e02a631b]] is [[complete|~aae963c7]], then $A$ is [[closed|~dfeacff3]] in $X$.

## Remarks

Proof: let $x \in \overline{A}$. By [[closure is the set of limits of sequences|~7d164d71]], there is a sequence in $A$ with limit $x$. It is a Cauchy sequence in $A$, so it has a limit in $A$. Limits are unique in $X$, so $x \in A$.
The space $X$ need not be complete here.
For example, $\mathbb{Q}$ is not closed in $\mathbb{R}$, so $\mathbb{Q}$ is not complete.
In a complete space, a subset is complete if and only if it is closed, by [[closed subsets of complete spaces are complete|~ba2f5ba4]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A complete subset contains the limits of its Cauchy sequences, and thus of its convergent sequences.
A set that keeps all its limits is closed.
