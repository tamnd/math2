## Definition

A [[field|~92ce2090]] $K$ is algebraically closed if every polynomial $f \in K[x]$ with $\deg f \ge 1$ has a [[root|~4d191b0b]] in $K$.

## Remarks

Equivalently, every nonzero polynomial over $K$ [[splits|~10d0250b]] over $K$. Also equivalently, the [[irreducible polynomials|~25830f16]] over $K$ are exactly the polynomials of degree one.
Another equivalent condition: $K$ has no [[algebraic extension|~2c8cc802]] other than $K$ itself.
The field $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, by the fundamental theorem of algebra. The field $\mathbb{R}$ is not, because $x^2 + 1$ has no real root.
No finite field is algebraically closed: for a finite field $K$, the polynomial $1 + \prod_{a \in K} (x - a)$ has no root in $K$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an algebraically closed field, every polynomial equation of degree at least one has a solution.
You never need a bigger field to find roots.
