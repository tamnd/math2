## Proof

1. By the [[definition|0PQ2GGY1QV]], $\operatorname{Null}(A)$ is the kernel of the linear map $x \mapsto Ax$. By the [[lemma on the kernel|REWYZ8N7EN]], it is a subspace of $F^n$. This proves part 1.
2. By the [[theorem on row reduction|FCM29NQ6DN]], $A$ is row equivalent to its reduced form $R$. By part 4 of the [[theorem on row equivalence|E4GKK727ZM]], $\operatorname{Null}(A) = \operatorname{Null}(R)$.
3. The augmented matrix $[R \mid 0]$ is in reduced row echelon form, and its last column has no leading entry. So case 2 of the [[theorem on systems in reduced form|P3HMRQN5KT]] applies with $c = 0$.
4. By parts 3 and 4 of that theorem, for each $t \in F^k$ there is exactly one $x \in \operatorname{Null}(R)$ with $x_{f_l} = t_l$ for all $l$. It has $x_{p_i} = -\sum_{l=1}^k R_{i f_l} t_l$ for $i = 1, \dots, r$.
5. Compare with $\sum_{l=1}^k t_l s_l$. In position $f_{l'}$ it has entry $t_{l'}$, since only $s_{l'}$ has a nonzero entry there. In position $p_i$ it has entry $\sum_l t_l (-R_{i f_l})$. So $x = \sum_{l=1}^k t_l s_l$.
6. Each $s_l$ is in $\operatorname{Null}(A)$: it is the vector $x$ of step 4 for $t = e_l$.
7. Spanning. Let $x \in \operatorname{Null}(A)$ and put $t_l = x_{f_l}$. By the uniqueness in step 4 and by step 5, $x = \sum_l t_l s_l$.
8. Independence. Let $\sum_l t_l s_l = 0$. By step 5, its entry in position $f_{l'}$ is $t_{l'}$. So $t_{l'} = 0$ for each $l'$.
9. By steps 6 to 8, $s_1, \dots, s_k$ form a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $\operatorname{Null}(A)$. This proves part 2.
10. By the [[theorem on the pivot basis|11CSKXQNG3]], $\rank A = r$, the number of pivots. By the definition of [[dimension|05G2G0P224]], $\dim \operatorname{Null}(A) = k = n - r = n - \rank A$. This proves part 3.

## Idea

Row reduction does not change the null space.
In the reduced form, each solution is determined by the values of the free variables, and these values are arbitrary.
So the solution set is a copy of $F^k$, and the images of the unit vectors of $F^k$ are a basis.

## Variants

- The [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]] for the map $x \mapsto Ax$ gives part 3 without the explicit basis. The present proof gives the basis as well.
- The [[parametric form|KQT68YPHD1]] of the solution set writes the general solution as $\sum_l t_l s_l$, which is step 5.
