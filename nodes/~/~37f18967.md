## Statement

Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]], and let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over $F$ with $n > m$.
Then the [[homogeneous system|04KGXJG1A2]] $Ax = 0$ has a solution $x \neq 0$.

More precisely, for any $m$ and $n$, the system $Ax = 0$ has a solution $x \neq 0$ if and only if some column of $A$ has no [[pivot|0J0BBCMJ5W]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. The system must be homogeneous: $x + y + z = 1$, $x + y + z = 2$ has $n = 3 > 2 = m$, and it has no solution at all.
2. The inequality $n > m$ must be strict: $x = 0$, $y = 0$ has $n = m = 2$ and only the trivial solution.

### Consequences

1. Any $n$ vectors in $F^m$ with $n > m$ are linearly dependent; this is the key step in the proof that dimension is well defined.
2. A matrix with more columns than rows has a nonzero null space.
3. The [[structure theorem|~a01a76ca]] then says that a consistent system with $n > m$ has more than one solution.

### Converse

The converse of the first part is false: $x + y = 0$, $2x + 2y = 0$ has $n = m = 2$ and the nontrivial solution $(1, -1)$.
The second part is an equivalence and so contains its converse.

### Generalizations

Over an integral domain $D$, the result also holds: solve the system over the field of fractions of $D$, and multiply the solution by a common denominator.

### History

The theorem is a standard step in the proof that two bases of a finite-dimensional space have the same size.
Lay states the criterion with free variables in Section 1.5.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If there are more unknowns than equations, some unknown is free.
We set that unknown to $1$ and solve for the others.
This gives a solution that is not all zeros.

### L2 human

We find a nonzero solution of $x + y + z = 0$, $x - y + 2z = 0$ over $\R$.

1. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - R_1$ gives $(0, -2, 1 \mid 0)$.
2. The pivots are in columns 1 and 2, so $z$ is free; put $z = 2$.
3. Row 2 gives $-2y + 2 = 0$, so $y = 1$.
4. Row 1 gives $x = -1 - 2 = -3$.
5. Check: $-3 + 1 + 2 = 0$ and $-3 - 1 + 4 = 0$.

The typical use is to show that too many vectors are dependent, or that a matrix with more columns than rows is not injective.
A trap: the theorem gives existence only; it does not say how many solutions there are, or that a non-homogeneous system is consistent.

### L3 human

In terms of linear maps, a linear map $F^n \to F^m$ with $n > m$ is not injective.
With the rank-nullity theorem this is $\dim \ker \ge n - m \ge 1$, but the proof by elimination needs no dimension theory.
