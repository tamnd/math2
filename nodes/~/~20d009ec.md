## Statement

For every real $x$,
$$e^x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}.$$
This series is the [[Maclaurin series|~c87f36c5]] of the exponential function. Its partial sums converge to $e^x$ uniformly on each bounded interval.

## Remarks

By the [[derivative rule|~56e3e18f]], every derivative of $\exp$ at $0$ equals $\exp(0) = 1$. So the Maclaurin coefficients are $1/n!$.
With the series as the definition of $\exp$, the first claim holds by definition. Uniform convergence follows from [[the result on power series|~a6ebaeb5]].
The partial sums give good approximations near $0$. For example, $1 + x + x^2/2$ approximates $e^x$ with error $O(x^3)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The exponential function equals the sum of its Taylor polynomials at $0$.
Adding more terms gives better approximations of $e^x$, for every $x$.
