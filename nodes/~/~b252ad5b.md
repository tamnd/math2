## Example

Let $L = \set{a, g}$ with a constant symbol $a$ and a binary function symbol $g$.
The [[Herbrand universe|~bf03becf]] $H(L)$ contains $a$, $g(a, a)$, $g(a, g(a, a))$, $g(g(a, a), a)$, and so on.
Let $N_n$ be the number of closed terms with exactly $n$ occurrences of $g$.
We show that $N_n$ is the $n$-th [[Catalan number|0YWZJEABB2]] $C_n$, defined by $C_0 = 1$ and $C_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} C_k C_{n-1-k}$.

1. A term with $0$ occurrences of $g$ is the constant $a$. So $N_0 = 1$.
2. Let $n \geq 1$. A term with $n$ occurrences of $g$ is not $a$, so it is $g(t_1, t_2)$ for unique closed terms $t_1$ and $t_2$, by unique readability.
3. If $t_1$ has $k$ occurrences of $g$, then $t_2$ has $n - 1 - k$, because the outer $g$ is the remaining one. Here $0 \leq k \leq n - 1$.
4. Conversely, every pair $(t_1, t_2)$ with $k$ and $n - 1 - k$ occurrences gives one term $g(t_1, t_2)$ with $n$ occurrences.
5. So $N_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} N_k N_{n-1-k}$, the same recursion as for $C_n$, with $N_0 = C_0$.
6. By induction on $n$, $N_n = C_n$ for every $n$.

The first values are $N_0 = 1$, $N_1 = 1$, $N_2 = 2$, $N_3 = 5$ and $N_4 = 14$.
For $n = 2$ the two terms are $g(a, g(a, a))$ and $g(g(a, a), a)$.

## Remarks

The example shows that the closed terms of $L$ are the full [[binary trees|0D7EZ10V2R]]: each $g$ is an inner node with two children, and each $a$ is a leaf.
The universe $H(L)$ is countably infinite, and $H_k$ grows very fast: $|H_0| = 1$ and $|H_{k+1}| = 1 + |H_k|^2$, so $|H_1| = 2$, $|H_2| = 5$ and $|H_3| = 26$.
Two different terms, such as $g(a, g(a, a))$ and $g(g(a, a), a)$, are different elements of $H(L)$, even in a structure where $g$ is associative and they get the same value.

## Explanations

### L1 human

With one name $a$ and one way $g$ of joining two names into a new one, we get names such as $g(a, a)$ and $g(g(a, a), a)$.
Each name is a tree that branches in two at each $g$.
The number of names with $n$ joins follows the Catalan numbers $1, 1, 2, 5, 14, \ldots$.

### L2 human

We list the five terms with three occurrences of $g$, by the split $k$ of step 3.

1. $k = 0$: $g(a, t_2)$ with $t_2$ one of the two terms with two $g$: $g(a, g(a, g(a, a)))$ and $g(a, g(g(a, a), a))$.
2. $k = 1$: $g(g(a, a), g(a, a))$.
3. $k = 2$: $g(g(a, g(a, a)), a)$ and $g(g(g(a, a), a), a)$.

This gives $2 + 1 + 2 = 5 = C_3$ terms.
The typical use is to see that a Herbrand universe can be large even for a tiny signature.
A trap is to confuse the count by occurrences of $g$ with the count by levels $H_k$, which grows much faster.
