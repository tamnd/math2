## Statement

Let $p$ be a prime number and let $G$ be a finite [[p-group|~a63adf2b]] with $G \neq \{e\}$.
Then the [[centre|~ff52f9c7]] $Z(G)$ is not $\{e\}$.

## Remarks

In the [[class equation|~1559ca96]] $|G| = |Z(G)| + \sum [G : C_G(a_i)]$, the left side and each term of the sum are divisible by $p$.
So $p$ divides $|Z(G)|$, and $|Z(G)| \geq p$.
Equivalently, apply the [[fixed point lemma|~e483f5cc]] to the action of $G$ on itself by conjugation.
A consequence is that each finite $p$-group is [[nilpotent|~744d1682]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The conjugacy classes of a $p$-group that have more than one element all have sizes divisible by $p$.
The order of the group is also divisible by $p$.
So the classes with one element, which form the centre, cannot be just the identity.
