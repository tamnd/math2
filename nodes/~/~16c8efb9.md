## Proof

Write $a_1, \dots, a_n$ for the columns of $A$.

1. For $x \in F^n$, $Ax = x_1 a_1 + \dots + x_n a_n$, by the definition of the [[matrix-vector product|0PDVQEP0ZC]].
2. So $\{Ax : x \in F^n\}$ is the set of linear combinations of the columns, which is the [[span|0HR573CHNM]] of the columns. By the [[definition|0F0RPPJ2TT]], this is $\operatorname{Col}(A)$. A span is a subspace, which proves part 1.
3. By the [[theorem on row reduction|FCM29NQ6DN]], $A$ is row equivalent to a matrix $R$ in [[reduced row echelon form|0NNR68BFRY]]. Let $r_1, \dots, r_n$ be its columns, and let $p_1 < \dots < p_r$ be its [[pivot columns|0BCMGDHZGR]].
4. By part 4 of the [[theorem on row equivalence|E4GKK727ZM]], $Ax = 0$ if and only if $Rx = 0$. By step 1, $\sum_j x_j a_j = 0$ if and only if $\sum_j x_j r_j = 0$. So the columns of $A$ and of $R$ satisfy the same linear relations.
5. In $R$, the pivot of row $k$ is $1$ and the rest of its column is $0$. So $r_{p_k} = e_k$, the $k$-th standard unit vector of $F^m$, for $1 \le k \le r$.
6. Independence. Let $\sum_{k=1}^r x_{p_k} a_{p_k} = 0$, and put $x_j = 0$ for the other $j$. By step 4, $\sum_{k=1}^r x_{p_k} e_k = 0$. So $x_{p_k} = 0$ for each $k$, and the pivot columns of $A$ are linearly independent.
7. Spanning. In $R$ the rows below row $r$ are zero. So each column of $R$ is $r_j = \sum_{k=1}^r R_{kj} e_k = \sum_{k=1}^r R_{kj}\, r_{p_k}$, by step 5.
8. This is a linear relation among the columns of $R$. By step 4, the same relation holds for $A$: $a_j = \sum_{k=1}^r R_{kj}\, a_{p_k}$ for each $j$.
9. So every column of $A$ is in the span of the pivot columns of $A$. Hence the span of the pivot columns is $\operatorname{Col}(A)$.
10. By steps 6 and 9, the pivot columns of $A$ form a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $\operatorname{Col}(A)$. This proves part 2.
11. The basis of step 10 has $r$ vectors. By the definition of [[dimension|05G2G0P224]], $\dim \operatorname{Col}(A) = r$, the number of pivot positions. This proves part 3.

## Idea

Row operations keep the null space, so they keep every linear relation among the columns.
In the reduced form the relations are visible: the pivot columns are unit vectors, and each other column is a combination of them.
Read the same relations back in $A$.

## Variants

- The [[pivot test for independence|55K7JBE13G]] gives step 6 for the matrix of pivot columns, and the [[pivot test for spanning|D3PN42EP4X]] is a related criterion for the whole space $F^m$.
- In the language of linear maps: the invertible matrix $E$ with $R = EA$ gives the isomorphism $y \mapsto Ey$ of $F^m$, which maps $\operatorname{Col}(A)$ onto $\operatorname{Col}(R)$ and $a_j$ to $r_j$. It sends a basis to a basis, and $\{e_1, \dots, e_r\}$ is a basis of $\operatorname{Col}(R)$.
