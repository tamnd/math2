## Statement

For real $a$ and $b$, let $R(a, b) = \begin{pmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{pmatrix}$.
For $k \ge 1$ and $b > 0$, let $C_k(a, b)$ be the real $2k \times 2k$ matrix with $k$ diagonal blocks $R(a, b)$, the identity $I_2$ in each block position $(s, s + 1)$, and $0$ elsewhere:
$$C_k(a, b) = \begin{pmatrix} R(a,b) & I_2 & & \\ & R(a,b) & \ddots & \\ & & \ddots & I_2 \\ & & & R(a,b) \end{pmatrix}.$$

Let $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$.

1. *Existence.* $A$ is [[similar|027KDW2K72]] over $\mathbb{R}$ to a [[block diagonal matrix|0M0SNKZ482]] whose blocks are [[Jordan blocks|0PBFS92AJP]] $J_k(\lambda)$ with $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ and blocks $C_k(a, b)$ with $a \in \mathbb{R}$ and $b > 0$.
2. *Correspondence.* Each block $C_k(a, b)$ is similar over $\mathbb{C}$ to $J_k(a + ib) \oplus J_k(a - ib)$. So the blocks $J_k(\lambda)$ of the real form are the blocks of the complex [[Jordan normal form|085HQ5G5TE]] with real eigenvalue, and the blocks $C_k(a, b)$ match the pairs of [[complex conjugate|0FTH2E9TAS]] blocks $J_k(a \pm ib)$.
3. *Uniqueness.* The real Jordan form of $A$ is unique up to the order of the blocks.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The condition $b > 0$ makes the form unique: $C_k(a, -b)$ is similar to $C_k(a, b)$, by the change of basis that swaps the two vectors in each pair, so one sign must be fixed.
The matrix must be real; for a complex matrix the nonreal eigenvalues need not come in conjugate pairs, and the ordinary Jordan form over $\mathbb{C}$ is the right form.

### Consequences

A real matrix is similar over $\mathbb{R}$ to a block upper triangular matrix with blocks of size $1$ and $2$.
For a $2 \times 2$ real matrix with nonreal eigenvalues the real Jordan form is one block $R(a, b)$: this is the [[rotation-scaling form|KKJAZTB1EB]].
The real solutions of the [[linear system of differential equations|037ZNY7NAG]] $x' = Ax$ follow from the real form and the [[matrix exponential|020M04FN1H]].
A block $C_k(a, b)$ gives terms $t^j e^{at} \cos bt$ and $t^j e^{at} \sin bt$ with $j < k$.
Two real matrices are similar over $\mathbb{R}$ if and only if they are similar over $\mathbb{C}$.
This is also a case of the theorem that [[similarity does not depend on the field|~a2ce4e28]].

### Generalizations

Over any field $F$ the primary rational canonical form plays the same part: each irreducible factor $p$ of $\chi_A$ gives blocks built from companion matrices of $p$.
For $F = \mathbb{R}$ the irreducible factors have degree $1$ or $2$, and the real Jordan form is a form of this kind with the block $R(a, b)$ in place of $C((x - a)^2 + b^2)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A real matrix can have complex eigenvalues, and then its Jordan form has complex entries.
The real Jordan form avoids complex numbers.
Each pair of complex eigenvalues $a \pm ib$ becomes a small $2 \times 2$ block that rotates and scales the plane.

### L2 human

Take $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, with $\chi_A = x^2 - 4x + 5$ and eigenvalues $2 \pm i$.

1. An eigenvector for $\mu = 2 + i$ is $z = (-1 + i, 1)$. Its real part is $x = (-1, 1)$ and its imaginary part is $y = (1, 0)$.
2. Let $P = (y \mid x) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $R = R(2, 1) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. Then $AP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $PR = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. So $A = P R P^{-1}$.

The trap is the order of $x$ and $y$: with $P = (x \mid y)$ the block becomes $R(2, -1)$.

### L3 human

The theorem is Galois descent for the Jordan form along $\mathbb{C} / \mathbb{R}$.
Complex conjugation maps the generalized eigenspace of $\mu$ onto that of $\bar\mu$.
The real and imaginary parts of a chain for $\mu$ span a real subspace, and its complexification is the sum of the two spaces.
