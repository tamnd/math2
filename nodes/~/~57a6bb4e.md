## Example

Suppose an inhabitant $A$ of the [[island of knights and knaves|MVADXMFV59]] says "I am a knave".
We show that this situation cannot occur on the island, so it is not a knights and knaves puzzle.

1. Let $a$ be "$A$ is a knight". The statement "I am a knave" is $\neg a$.
2. By the rule of the island, the statement gives the [[biconditional|0S6GMNJ62Y]] $a \leftrightarrow \neg a$.
3. [[Truth table|05RSK4Q657]]: if $a$ is T, then $\neg a$ is F and $a \leftrightarrow \neg a$ is F. If $a$ is F, then $\neg a$ is T and $a \leftrightarrow \neg a$ is F.
4. So $a \leftrightarrow \neg a$ is false in both rows, and no type of $A$ is consistent with the statement.
5. In words: a knight cannot say it, because it would be false, and a knave cannot say it, because it would be true.

## Remarks

The sentence "I am a knave" is the island's version of the [[liar sentence|DYAB23WA1X]].
The difference is that the liar sentence produces a paradox, while on the island the sentence produces only a contradiction with the assumption that $A$ said it.
So a puzzle that reports this statement has no solution, and the conclusion is that the report is false.
The same argument shows that no inhabitant can say "I am a knave and $2 + 2 = 4$".
On the other hand, an inhabitant can say "I am a knave and $2 + 2 = 5$": the translation $a \leftrightarrow (\neg a \land \bot)$ is true when $a$ is false, so the speaker is a knave.
In variants of the island with normals, a speaker who says "I am a knave" must be a normal, since a normal may say a true or a false sentence.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A truth-teller who says "I am a liar" would be telling a lie, which a truth-teller never does.
A liar who says "I am a liar" would be telling the truth, which a liar never does.
So nobody on the island can say this sentence.
If a story claims that someone on the island said it, the story is wrong.

### L2 human

The typical use is a quick test: a statement whose biconditional is false in every row cannot be made on the island.
For one speaker, the test is: $a \leftrightarrow S$ must be true for $a$ true or for $a$ false.
A trap is to conclude "so $A$ is a knave" from step 3, by the habit of the earlier puzzles.
Both rows fail, so no type is consistent, and the statement itself is impossible.
