## Statement

Let $T$ be a [[complete theory|655K8Y7VS8]] with infinite models in a countable signature.
A countable model $M$ is *saturated* if for every finite $A \subseteq M$, every complete $1$-[[type|9V71Q3SD1G]] over $A$ is realized in $M$; see [[saturated model|RMTZ5J64FX]].

1. $T$ has a countable saturated model if and only if $T$ is [[small|~166b8a9e]].
2. A countable saturated model $M$ is universal: every countable model of $T$ has an elementary embedding into $M$.
3. Any two countable saturated models of $T$ are isomorphic.

The proof is [[here|~d5009f7f]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Smallness. The theory of [[independent unary predicates|~a949e251]] has $2^{\aleph_0}$ complete $1$-types, and a countable model realizes only countably many, so it has no countable saturated model.
- Completeness of $T$. An incomplete theory has models that are not elementarily equivalent, and no model can embed elementarily into both.
- Countable signature. Saturation over finite sets and the counting of types both use it.

### Consequences

- A small theory has both a prime model and a countable saturated model. These coincide exactly when $T$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical.
- A countable saturated model realizes every type in every $S_n(T)$.
- In the proof of [[Vaught's never-two theorem|~ea319e25]], the prime and the saturated model are two of the countable models.

### Converse

A countable model that is universal for countable models need not be saturated.
For the theory of $(\Z, <)$, the countable saturated model is $\Z \cdot \Q$, a copy of $\Z$ for each rational.
The model $\Z \cdot (1 + \Q)$ contains $\Z \cdot \Q$ as an elementary substructure, so it is universal.
It is not saturated: for $a$ in the first copy, the type "$x < a$ with infinitely many elements between" is omitted.

### Generalizations

The same construction, with types over sets of size less than $\kappa$, gives a saturated model of size $\kappa$ when $\kappa$ is regular, $\kappa > \aleph_0$ and $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$.
In every infinite size, two saturated models of the same size are isomorphic.

## History

The result is part of the basic theory of countable models of complete theories, together with the theory of prime models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A saturated model is a richest model: any consistent wish about a new element, stated with finitely many old elements, is met inside it.
A countable such model exists exactly when there are only countably many kinds of finite lists of elements.
When it exists, every countable model fits inside it, and it is unique.

### L2 human

We check clause 1 for the theory of dense linear orders without endpoints with the model $(\Q, <)$.

1. Let $A = \set{a_1 < \dots < a_k}$ be finite. A $1$-type over $A$ says where $x$ lies: equal to some $a_i$, below $a_1$, above $a_k$, or between $a_i$ and $a_{i+1}$.
2. Each of these positions is filled in $\Q$, since $\Q$ is dense without endpoints.
3. So $\Q$ is saturated. The theory is small, as clause 1 requires.

The typical use is to have one countable model in which every type is realized.
A trap is to require types over infinite subsets of $M$; no infinite model realizes all types over itself.

### L3 human

The prime model and the countable saturated model are the two ends of the countable models of a small theory, in the order of elementary embeddability.
