## Example

Let $U = \R (1, 1, 1)$ and let $W$ be the plane $z = 0$ in $\R^3$, so $p = 1$ and $q = 2$.
There is one [[principal angle|~179768d8]].

1. A unit vector of $U$ is $u = \frac{1}{\sqrt 3} (1, 1, 1)$.
2. The [[orthogonal projection|0Q8HZTJC7G]] onto $W$ sets the last coordinate to $0$, so $P_W u = \frac{1}{\sqrt 3} (1, 1, 0)$.
3. By part 4 of the [[singular value characterization|~339ddff3]], $\cos \theta_1 = \norm{P_W u} = \sqrt{2/3} \approx 0.8165$.
4. So $\theta_1 = \arccos \sqrt{2/3} \approx 35.26°$.
5. The same with matrices: $Q_1 = u$ and $Q_2$ has columns $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 0)$. Then $M = Q_1^{\mathsf{T}} Q_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt 3} (1, 1)$, a $1 \times 2$ matrix with singular value $\norm{M} = \sqrt{2/3}$.
6. Principal vectors: $u_1 = u$ and $w_1 = P_W u / \norm{P_W u} = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2} (1, 1, 0)$, with $u_1 \cdot w_1 = 2 / \sqrt 6 = \sqrt{2/3}$.
7. Check with the normal $n = (0, 0, 1)$ of $W$: the [[angle|0ZEHZY85E6]] between $u$ and $n$ is $\arccos (1/\sqrt 3) \approx 54.74°$, and $90° - 54.74° = 35.26°$.
8. Part 5 of the theorem gives $\norm{P_U - P_W}_F^2 = 1 + 2 - 2 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{3}$.

## Remarks

The angle between a line and a plane in school geometry is the angle between the line and its orthogonal projection onto the plane.
Step 6 shows that this is the principal angle.
Its sine is the length $1/\sqrt 3$ of the component of $u$ along the normal.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The line rises out of the floor at an angle.
The principal angle is the angle between the line and its shadow on the floor, if the light comes from straight above.

### L2 human

For a line and a subspace, no singular value decomposition is needed: the cosine of the angle is the length of the projection of a unit vector of the line.
The trap is to compute the angle with the normal and forget to subtract it from $90°$.
