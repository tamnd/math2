## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, $\lambda \in F$, $k \ge 1$, $J = J_k(\lambda)$ the [[Jordan block|0PBFS92AJP]], and $N = J - \lambda I$.

1. $N e_1 = 0$ and $N e_i = e_{i-1}$ for $2 \le i \le k$. For $0 \le j \le k$, $N^j$ has entry $1$ at each position $(i, i + j)$ and $0$ elsewhere, and $\operatorname{rank} N^j = k - j$. In particular $N^k = 0$.
2. For every $m \ge 0$, $J^m = \sum_{j=0}^{\min(m, k-1)} \binom{m}{j} \lambda^{m-j} N^j$. So the entry $(i, i + j)$ of $J^m$ is $\binom{m}{j} \lambda^{m-j}$.
3. For a polynomial $f = \sum_{j \ge 0} b_j (x - \lambda)^j$ written in powers of $x - \lambda$, $f(J) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} b_j N^j$.
4. The [[characteristic polynomial|039HF58KQ5]] and the [[minimal polynomial|03NDVCSHSB]] of $J$ are both $(x - \lambda)^k$.
5. The only [[eigenvalue|0BQAA2BK3Q]] of $J$ is $\lambda$, and $\dim \ker N^j = \min(j, k)$ for all $j \ge 0$. The eigenspace is $F e_1$.
6. $J$ is cyclic, with cyclic vector $e_k$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The formulas hold over every field.
In part 3, writing $b_j = f^{(j)}(\lambda) / j!$ needs characteristic $0$, or at least $j$ less than the characteristic; the form with the $b_j$ needs no such hypothesis.

### Consequences

$J_k(\lambda)$ is diagonalisable only for $k = 1$, since its minimal polynomial has a repeated root when $k \ge 2$.
$J_k(\lambda)^m \to 0$ as $m \to \infty$ over $\mathbb{C}$ if and only if $|\lambda| < 1$, by part 2 and the limit of $\binom{m}{j} \lambda^{m-j}$.
The numbers $\dim \ker N^j$ in part 5 are the data that the [[normal form of nilpotent endomorphisms|~29b2bf0c]] uses to count blocks.

### Generalizations

For a power series $f$ that converges near $\lambda$, part 3 holds with the Taylor coefficients of $f$ at $\lambda$.
This gives $\exp(t J_k(\lambda)) = e^{\lambda t} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \frac{t^j}{j!} N^j$ over $\mathbb{C}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Jordan block is a number times the identity plus a shift.
The shift moves each basis vector one step down the line and kills the first one.
So its powers are easy to compute with the binomial formula, and they stop after $k$ steps.

### L2 human

For $J = J_3(\lambda)$ and $m = 4$, part 2 gives $J^4 = \lambda^4 I + 4 \lambda^3 N + 6 \lambda^2 N^2$.
So $J^4 = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda^4 & 4\lambda^3 & 6\lambda^2 \\ 0 & \lambda^4 & 4\lambda^3 \\ 0 & 0 & \lambda^4 \end{pmatrix}$.
The ranks of $N, N^2, N^3$ are $2, 1, 0$, so the kernels have dimensions $1, 2, 3$.

### L3 human

$J_k(\lambda)$ is the matrix of multiplication by $x$ on $F[x]/((x - \lambda)^k)$ in the basis $(x - \lambda)^{k-1}, \dots, x - \lambda, 1$.
Parts 3 and 4 then say that $f(x)$ acts as zero on this module exactly when $(x - \lambda)^k$ divides $f$.
