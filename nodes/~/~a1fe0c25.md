## Definition

A [[topological space|~77bfcc55]] $X$ is Hausdorff when for all points $x \neq y$ of $X$ there are [[open sets|~159c5927]] $U$ and $V$ with $x \in U$, $y \in V$ and $U \cap V = \varnothing$.

## Remarks

Each [[metric space is Hausdorff|~ed9b4114]]. Subspaces and products of Hausdorff spaces are Hausdorff.
In a Hausdorff space, [[limits of sequences are unique|~99af3035]] and [[compact subsets are closed|~775d2019]].
Each Hausdorff space is [[T1|~4808a5a2]]. An infinite set with the [[cofinite topology|~9632ad11]] is T1 but not Hausdorff.
The Hausdorff condition is also called T2.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a Hausdorff space, you can put any two distinct points into two open sets that do not meet.
Most spaces of analysis, such as $\mathbb{R}^n$, are Hausdorff.
