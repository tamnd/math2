## Example

Let $\mathrm{HB}$ be the Heine-Borel theorem for countable covers of $[0, 1]$, and $\mathrm{BW}$ the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem, both coded in $L_2$.
We check that $\mathsf{ACA}_0 \vdash \mathrm{HB} \leftrightarrow \mathrm{BW}$, and that this equivalence is not a classification in the sense of [[reverse mathematics|TQTDKMBJ0F]].

1. $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ proves $\mathrm{BW}$, by the [[Bolzano-Weierstrass equivalence|~cd23ea7b]].
2. $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ proves [[König's lemma|~330fcb37]], which gives weak König's lemma, and weak König's lemma proves $\mathrm{HB}$ by the [[Heine-Borel equivalence|~ebb0edca]].
3. A theory that proves two sentences proves their equivalence. So $\mathsf{ACA}_0 \vdash \mathrm{HB} \leftrightarrow \mathrm{BW}$.
4. Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathrm{HB}$ does not imply $\mathrm{BW}$. The [[ω-model of the low sets|~e8a57f1d]] satisfies $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, hence $\mathrm{HB}$.
5. That model does not satisfy $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, and $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{BW}$ proves $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, so $\mathrm{BW}$ fails in it.
6. So the equivalence in step 3 hides a real difference in strength. The base $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ is too strong, and the result is not an equivalence over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.

## Remarks

Every reverse mathematics equivalence needs a base theory that proves neither side.
This is why the base system is weak.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If a theory proves two statements, it also proves that each one implies the other.
So the theory sees them as equivalent, even if one is much stronger than the other.
To compare strength, one must use a weak base theory.

### L2 human

The test is to look for a model of the base theory where one side holds and the other fails.
Over $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ such a model does not exist for $\mathrm{HB}$ and $\mathrm{BW}$.
Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ the low model is one.
