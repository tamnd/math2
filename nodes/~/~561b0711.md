## Statement

Let $L = \set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$ be the signature of rings.
Let $\mathrm{ACF}$ be the $L$-[[theory|92SWXST1ZM]] of [[algebraically closed fields|0PF6TM8TWT]]: the axioms of [[fields|04C5ZBG1WT]], and for each $n \ge 1$ the axiom that every monic polynomial of degree $n$ has a root.
Then $\mathrm{ACF}$ has [[quantifier elimination|RE5TFTDP4D]]: for every $L$-[[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] $\varphi(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ there is a quantifier-free $L$-formula $\psi(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ such that
$$\mathrm{ACF} \vdash \forall \bar x \, \big(\varphi(\bar x) \leftrightarrow \psi(\bar x)\big).$$

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Algebraic closure. The theory of the real field in $L$ has no quantifier elimination: the set of squares $\exists y \, (x = y \cdot y)$ is infinite and coinfinite in $\R$, while a quantifier-free set in one variable is finite or cofinite. See [[this example|~5a627ec0]].
- The axioms for all degrees. $\mathrm{ACF}$ is [[not finitely axiomatizable|2ZTTR9VZ4P]], so a finite part $T_0$ has a model $K$ that is not algebraically closed. Some monic polynomial over $K$ has a root in $\overline K \models T_0$ but not in $K$. Quantifier-free formulas agree in $K$ and $\overline K$, so "this polynomial has a root" has no quantifier-free equivalent modulo $T_0$.

### Consequences

- For $p$ a prime or $0$, the theory $\mathrm{ACF}_p$ of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $p$ is complete: a quantifier-free sentence is a Boolean combination of equations $m = k$ between integers, decided by the characteristic.
- $\mathrm{ACF}$ is model complete, so [[embeddings between algebraically closed fields are elementary|61VW0F3R83]], and it is the [[model completion|~5fc11853]] of the theory of fields.
- The definable subsets of $K^n$ are the constructible sets, the finite Boolean combinations of zero sets of polynomials. This is the logical form of [[Chevalley's theorem|TGN01264C4]].
- Every definable subset of $K$ is finite or cofinite, so $\mathrm{ACF}$ is [[strongly minimal|PNGY60EBDJ]].
- The weak [[Nullstellensatz|D85VZWS9QK]]: a finite system of polynomial equations and inequations over $K$ with a solution in an extension field has a solution in $K$.

### Converse

An infinite field whose complete theory has quantifier elimination in $L$ is algebraically closed.
This is a much deeper result, and it does not follow from the proof of the theorem.

### Generalizations

- Differentially closed fields of characteristic $0$ have quantifier elimination in the signature of differential rings; see [[this concept|KPCN3KHG50]].
- Real closed ordered fields have quantifier elimination in the signature of ordered rings, by [[Tarski's theorem|FSJ9NGRRPA]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an algebraically closed field, like the complex numbers, every question of the form "is there a number $y$ with these polynomial properties?" can be answered by checking polynomial equations in the other numbers.
For example, "there is $y$ with $a y = 1$" is the same as "$a \neq 0$", and "there is $y$ with $y^2 = a$" is always true.
The theorem says that every statement can be simplified in this way, with no "there is" left.

### L2 human

We eliminate the quantifier in $\varphi(a, b) = \exists y \, (y^2 + a y + b = 0 \land y \neq 0)$.

1. The polynomial $y^2 + a y + b$ has degree $2$, so it has a root in an algebraically closed field $K$.
2. If $b \neq 0$, no root is $0$, so $\varphi$ is true.
3. If $b = 0$, the roots are $0$ and $-a$, so $\varphi$ holds if and only if $a \neq 0$.
4. So $\varphi(a, b)$ is equivalent to $b \neq 0 \lor a \neq 0$.

The typical use is to reduce a question about all algebraically closed fields to a question about polynomial equations in the parameters.
A trap is to use the theorem in a field that is not algebraically closed: in $\R$, step 1 fails.

### L3 human

Quantifier elimination for $\mathrm{ACF}$ is the logical side of elimination theory: the projection of a constructible set is constructible.
It makes $\mathrm{ACF}$ the basic example of a strongly minimal theory, with model-theoretic algebraic closure equal to field-theoretic algebraic closure.
The proof by the extension test reduces it to two facts, the existence of roots and of transcendental elements in saturated models.
The [[model theory of algebraically closed fields|MT8TDVPBXB]] builds on it, from the [[Lefschetz principle|SKTMN14X5P]] to the [[Ax-Grothendieck theorem|M2XAN2HGZH]].
