## Example

On the [[real line|08J2M1JYAG]] $\R$, take the topological valuation $V(p) = (0, \infty)$ and $V(q) = (-\infty, 0)$.
We compute four truth sets with the clauses of [[topological semantics|~088e3e8d]].

1. $[\![\neg p]\!] = \mathrm{Int}((-\infty, 0]) = (-\infty, 0)$.
2. $[\![p \vee \neg p]\!] = \R \setminus \set{0}$. So the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]] fails at $0$.
3. $[\![\neg \neg p]\!] = \mathrm{Int}([0, \infty)) = (0, \infty)$. So $[\![\neg p \vee \neg \neg p]\!] = \R \setminus \set{0}$, and the [[law of weak excluded middle|CX032ACTMZ]] fails at $0$.
4. $[\![p \to q]\!] = \mathrm{Int}((-\infty, 0] \cup (-\infty, 0)) = (-\infty, 0)$.
5. $[\![q \to p]\!] = \mathrm{Int}([0, \infty) \cup (0, \infty)) = (0, \infty)$.
6. So $[\![(p \to q) \vee (q \to p)]\!] = \R \setminus \set{0}$, and [[prelinearity|~cfc28e48]] fails at $0$.
7. Now take $V(p) = \R \setminus \set{0}$. Then $[\![\neg p]\!] = \mathrm{Int}\set{0} = \emptyset$ and $[\![\neg \neg p]\!] = \R$.
8. So $[\![\neg \neg p \to p]\!] = \mathrm{Int}(\R \setminus \set{0}) = \R \setminus \set{0}$, and double negation elimination fails at $0$.

## Remarks

By soundness, none of the four formulas is a theorem of $\mathsf{IPC}$.
All four fail only at one boundary point; each holds on the rest of the line.
The value $\R \setminus \set{0}$ in step 7 is dense, so its double negation is the whole line.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of $p$ as "the number is positive", known only with a margin of error.
At $0$, no small interval is all positive or all non-positive.
So at $0$ we can assert neither "$p$" nor "not $p$".
Each of the four classical laws breaks at that single point.

### L2 human

1. Check step 1: the interior of $(-\infty, 0]$ is $(-\infty, 0)$, since every interval around $0$ contains positive numbers.
2. Check step 5: $[0, \infty) \cup (0, \infty) = [0, \infty)$, whose interior is $(0, \infty)$.
3. Check step 7: $\set{0}$ contains no open interval, so its interior is empty.

The typical use is to refute an intuitionistic formula by a short computation with intervals.
A trap: forgetting the interior in step 1 gives $(-\infty, 0]$, and then excluded middle would hold.
