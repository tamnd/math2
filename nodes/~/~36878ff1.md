## Proof

We first prove two facts about forms on a subspace $S$ of a vector space $X$.

Fact E (extension): every linear form $\chi_0 \colon S \to F$ extends to a linear form $\chi \colon X \to F$.

1. Choose a basis $C$ of $S$. It exists because [[every vector space has a basis|00MS9VMQ9Z]].
2. $C$ is [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]] in $X$, so it [[extends to a basis|0M4SXE5WW3]] $D \supseteq C$ of $X$.
3. [[By the existence of linear maps on a basis|0R8X9XA83A]], there is $\chi \in X^*$ with $\chi(c) = \chi_0(c)$ for $c \in C$ and $\chi(d) = 0$ for $d \in D \setminus C$.
4. $\chi|_S$ and $\chi_0$ agree on the basis $C$ of $S$. By the uniqueness part of the same theorem, $\chi|_S = \chi_0$.

Fact S (separation): if $x \in X$ and $x \notin S$, then some $\varphi \in X^*$ has $\varphi|_S = 0$ and $\varphi(x) = 1$.

5. Let $S' = S + Fx$. Each element of $S'$ is $s + ax$ with $s \in S$ and a unique $a \in F$. If $s + ax = t + bx$ with $a \ne b$, then $x = (t - s)/(a - b) \in S$.
6. So $\varphi_0(s + ax) = a$ is a well-defined linear form on $S'$, with $\varphi_0|_S = 0$ and $\varphi_0(x) = 1$.
7. By Fact E, $\varphi_0$ extends to $\varphi \in X^*$, with the same values on $S'$.

Part 1.

8. Let $\varphi \in W^*$. By the definition of the [[dual map|0C2TQMW5A1]], $f^*(\varphi) = 0$ means $\varphi(f(v)) = 0$ for all $v \in V$.
9. The vectors $f(v)$ are exactly the elements of the [[image|0JB228DCWB]] $\im f$.
10. So $f^*(\varphi) = 0$ if and only if $\varphi$ vanishes on $\im f$, that is, $\varphi \in (\im f)^0$ by the definition of the [[annihilator|0R6M158SQ9]].

Part 2, inclusion $\im f^* \subseteq (\ker f)^0$.

11. Let $\psi = f^*(\varphi) = \varphi \circ f$, and let $v \in \ker f$, the [[kernel|02YFME3RGW]].
12. Then $\psi(v) = \varphi(f(v)) = \varphi(0) = 0$. So $\psi \in (\ker f)^0$.

Part 2, inclusion $(\ker f)^0 \subseteq \im f^*$.

13. Let $\psi \in (\ker f)^0$. We define $\chi_0 \colon \im f \to F$ by $\chi_0(f(v)) = \psi(v)$.
14. $\chi_0$ is well defined. If $f(v) = f(v')$, then $v - v' \in \ker f$, so $\psi(v) - \psi(v') = \psi(v - v') = 0$.
15. $\chi_0$ is linear: $\chi_0(a f(v) + b f(v')) = \chi_0(f(av + bv')) = \psi(av + bv') = a\psi(v) + b\psi(v')$.
16. By Fact E with $X = W$ and $S = \im f$, $\chi_0$ extends to some $\chi \in W^*$.
17. For each $v \in V$, $f^*(\chi)(v) = \chi(f(v)) = \chi_0(f(v)) = \psi(v)$. So $\psi = f^*(\chi) \in \im f^*$.

Part 3.

18. Let $S$ be a subspace of $X$. If $S \ne X$, then Fact S gives a nonzero form in $S^0$. So $S^0 = \{0\}$ only if $S = X$.
19. If $S \ne \{0\}$, take $s \ne 0$ in $S$. Fact S with the subspace $\{0\}$ and $x = s$ gives $\varphi \in X^*$ with $\varphi(s) = 1$. So $\varphi \notin S^0$, and $S^0 \ne X^*$.
20. The converses of steps 18 and 19 hold by definition: $X^0 = \{0\}$ and $\{0\}^0 = X^*$. So $S^0 = \{0\}$ if and only if $S = X$, and $S^0 = X^*$ if and only if $S = \{0\}$.
21. $f$ is injective if and only if $\ker f = \{0\}$. By step 20, this holds if and only if $(\ker f)^0 = V^*$. By part 2, this means $\im f^* = V^*$.
22. $f$ is surjective if and only if $\im f = W$. By step 20, this holds if and only if $(\im f)^0 = \{0\}$. By part 1, this means $\ker f^* = \{0\}$.

Part 4.

23. Let $\dim V = n$. By part 2, $\operatorname{rank} f^* = \dim (\ker f)^0$.
24. By the theorem on the [[dimension of the annihilator|06Q1EFVH0Q]], $\dim (\ker f)^0 = n - \dim \ker f$.
25. By the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]], $n - \dim \ker f = \dim \im f = \operatorname{rank} f$.

Part 5.

26. Define $h \colon V \to F^k$ by $h(v) = (f_1(v), \dots, f_k(v))$. It is linear because each $f_i$ is linear.
27. $\ker h = \ker f_1 \cap \dots \cap \ker f_k$. By hypothesis, $g$ vanishes on $\ker h$, so $g \in (\ker h)^0$.
28. By part 2, $g = h^*(\eta)$ for some $\eta \in (F^k)^*$.
29. Let $e_1^*, \dots, e_k^*$ be the [[dual basis|0W6M3PSD08]] of the standard basis. Then $h^*(e_i^*) = e_i^* \circ h = f_i$.
30. Write $\eta = \sum_i c_i e_i^*$. By linearity of $h^*$, $g = \sum_i c_i h^*(e_i^*) = \sum_i c_i f_i$.

## Idea

The kernel of $f^*$ is a direct calculation.
For the image, a form that vanishes on $\ker f$ defines a form on $\im f$, and every form on a subspace extends to the whole space.
The other parts follow by counting dimensions or by applying part 2 to a map into $F^k$.

## Variants

- If $V$ and $W$ are finite-dimensional, part 2 also follows from part 1 by counting dimensions. Part 1 and the annihilator dimension formula give $\operatorname{rank} f^* = \dim W - \dim (\im f)^0 = \operatorname{rank} f$. Step 12 gives $\im f^* \subseteq (\ker f)^0$, and steps 24 and 25 show that both have dimension $\operatorname{rank} f$.
- Part 5 also has a direct proof by induction on $k$, without dual maps.
