## Proof

Call a pair $(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_k; \Phi)$ a *reduction* of $\varphi$ when it has the property of the statement for all $I$, all families and all tuples.
We build a reduction by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $\varphi$, using $\neg$, $\land$ and $\exists$ only.
Write $P = \prod_{i \in I} \mathcal{M}_i$.

1. *Atomic $\varphi$.* In the [[direct product|WJ73XYBNE4]], equations and relations hold exactly when they hold in every coordinate. So $(\varphi; X_1 = 1)$ is a reduction.
2. *Negation.* If $(\bar{\theta}; \Phi)$ reduces $\varphi$, then $(\bar{\theta}; \neg\Phi)$ reduces $\neg\varphi$, since both sides of the equivalence are negated.
3. *Conjunction.* If $(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_k; \Phi)$ reduces $\varphi$ and $(\theta'_1, \dots, \theta'_l; \Psi)$ reduces $\psi$, the joined list with $\Phi(X_1, \dots, X_k) \land \Psi(X_{k+1}, \dots, X_{k+l})$ reduces $\varphi \land \psi$.
4. *Existential quantifier.* Let $(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_k; \Phi)$ reduce $\varphi(\bar{x}, y)$. For $S \subseteq \set{1, \dots, k}$ let $\chi_S$ be $\bigwedge_{j \in S} \theta_j \land \bigwedge_{j \notin S} \neg\theta_j$.
5. Let $\eta_S(\bar{x})$ be $\exists y \, \chi_S(\bar{x}, y)$. Let $\Phi'((Y_S)_S)$ say: there are $(Z_S)_S$ that partition $1$, with $Z_S \le Y_S$ for each $S$, and $\Phi(X_1, \dots, X_k)$ holds for $X_j = \bigvee_{S \ni j} Z_S$.
6. We show that $((\eta_S)_S; \Phi')$ reduces $\exists y \, \varphi$. Fix $\bar{a}$ and write $Y_S = [\![ \eta_S(\bar{a}) ]\!]$.
7. *Forward.* Let $P \models \varphi(\bar{a}, b)$. Put $Z_S = \set{i : \text{the } j \text{ with } \mathcal{M}_i \models \theta_j(\bar{a}(i), b(i)) \text{ form } S}$.
8. The sets $Z_S$ partition $I$, and $Z_S \subseteq Y_S$, with witness $b(i)$. Also $[\![ \theta_j(\bar{a}, b) ]\!] = \bigcup_{S \ni j} Z_S$.
9. By the induction hypothesis $\Phi$ holds of the sets $[\![ \theta_j(\bar{a}, b) ]\!]$. So $\Phi'$ holds of $(Y_S)_S$.
10. *Backward.* Let $(Z_S)_S$ witness $\Phi'$. For $i \in Z_S$ we have $i \in Y_S$, so we can choose $b(i)$ with $\mathcal{M}_i \models \chi_S(\bar{a}(i), b(i))$.
11. This chooses one element in each factor, by the [[axiom of choice|0HTGB13FE2]]; the $Z_S$ cover $I$, so $b$ is defined everywhere.
12. Then $[\![ \theta_j(\bar{a}, b) ]\!] = \bigcup_{S \ni j} Z_S$, so $\Phi$ holds of these sets. The induction hypothesis gives $P \models \varphi(\bar{a}, b)$, so $P \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar{a})$.

Each step is effective, so the reduction is computed from $\varphi$.
This proves the theorem.

## Idea

Truth in a product depends on which formulas hold in which coordinates, and the [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]] $\mathcal{P}(I)$ records this.
A quantifier over the product becomes a quantifier over partitions of $I$: a witness is chosen coordinate by coordinate, and the partition records which type of witness is used where.
Since $\Phi$ only sees sets of indices, it does not depend on the structures.

## Variants

For a reduced product modulo a filter $F$, the same induction works in $\mathcal{P}(I) / F$: a finite partition of $1$ in $\mathcal{P}(I) / F$ lifts to a partition of $I$, and $Z_S \le Y_S$ modulo $F$ can be repaired outside a set in $F$.
For an ultrafilter the quotient has two elements, and the induction becomes the proof of Łoś's theorem.
Feferman and Vaught also gave the proof for generalized products, and Mostowski earlier treated direct products of theories.
