## Example

Problem: given three lengths $b$, $c$ and $m$, construct with [[straightedge and compass|0DAECMZVRT]] a triangle $ABC$ with $CA = b$, $AB = c$ and [[median|0XW8HX8VEV]] $AM = m$, where $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$.
We solve it by Pappus's analysis and synthesis, the classical form of [[working backwards|~4d7ce252]].

Analysis: we assume that the triangle is already drawn, and we work back to a figure that we can construct.

1. Assume that $ABC$ is a solution, and let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$.
2. Extend the median $AM$ beyond $M$ to a point $D$ with $MD = AM$. Then $AD = 2m$.
3. The triangles $AMC$ and $DMB$ are congruent by side-angle-side: $AM = DM$ by step 2, $CM = BM$ since $M$ is the midpoint, and the angles at $M$ are vertical angles.
4. So $BD = CA = b$.
5. The triangle $ABD$ has sides $AB = c$, $BD = b$ and $AD = 2m$. All three are known lengths.
6. A triangle with three given sides can be constructed. So the analysis has reached a known construction.

Synthesis: we reverse the steps and check that the result is a solution.

1. Construct a triangle $ABD$ with $AB = c$, $BD = b$ and $AD = 2m$: draw $AD$, then intersect the circle of radius $c$ about $A$ with the circle of radius $b$ about $D$.
2. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AD$, found with the perpendicular bisector.
3. Extend $BM$ beyond $M$ to a point $C$ with $MC = BM$.
4. The triangles $DMB$ and $AMC$ are congruent by side-angle-side, as in step 3 of the analysis, so $CA = BD = b$.
5. $AB = c$ by step 1, $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$ by step 3, and $AM = \frac{1}{2} AD = m$ by step 2. So $ABC$ is a solution.

When is there a solution?
Step 1 of the synthesis needs a triangle with sides $b$, $c$ and $2m$.
By the [[triangle inequality|03HF5PMY5T]] and its converse, such a triangle exists exactly when $|b - c| < 2m < b + c$.
So the problem has a solution exactly when this condition holds.

A numerical case: for $b = 5$, $c = 7$ and $m = 4$, we get $|5 - 7| = 2 < 8 < 12$, so a solution exists.
The median formula $4m^2 = 2b^2 + 2c^2 - a^2$ gives $64 = 50 + 98 - a^2$, so the third side is $a = \sqrt{84} = 2\sqrt{21} \approx 9.17$.

## Remarks

The analysis does not prove anything by itself.
It assumes the result and finds a necessary condition, the triangle $ABD$.
The synthesis is the proof: each of its steps follows from the one before, and its last step checks every condition of the problem.
The condition $|b - c| < 2m < b + c$ comes from the synthesis, not from the analysis.

The auxiliary point $D$ is the main idea; see [[auxiliary elements|~fbc26a8c]].
A similar reflection solves the problem "construct a triangle from its three medians": the centroid, a vertex and the reflection of the centroid in the midpoint of a side form an auxiliary triangle with sides $\frac{2}{3}m_a$, $\frac{2}{3}m_b$ and $\frac{2}{3}m_c$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We want a triangle with two sides of lengths $5$ and $7$ and a median of length $4$.
We pretend that it is already drawn.
If we double the median, we see a new triangle with sides $5$, $7$ and $8$.
That triangle is easy to draw with a compass.
Then we undo the doubling, and the triangle we wanted appears.
Working backwards found the plan, and working forwards checks it.

### L2 human

We check the analysis and the synthesis on coordinates.

1. Take $A = (0, 0)$ and $D = (8, 0)$, so $AD = 2m = 8$.
2. The point $B = (x, y)$ with $AB = 7$ and $DB = 5$ satisfies $x^2 + y^2 = 49$ and $(x - 8)^2 + y^2 = 25$.
3. Subtracting gives $16x - 64 = 24$, so $x = \frac{11}{2}$, and $y^2 = 49 - \frac{121}{4} = \frac{75}{4}$, so $y = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
4. The midpoint of $AD$ is $M = (4, 0)$, and $C = 2M - B = \left(\frac{5}{2}, -\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$.
5. Then $CA^2 = \frac{25}{4} + \frac{75}{4} = 25$, so $CA = 5 = b$, and $AM = 4 = m$, as required.

The typical use: in a construction problem, the analysis turns the unknown figure into a figure with known parts.

A trap: an analysis that ends at a known figure does not show that a solution exists; the synthesis and its condition $|b - c| < 2m < b + c$ do.
