## Proof

We work over a field $F$, so that the [[linearity of the matrix-vector product|ZE36VEAY80]] holds with scalars on either side.
We first show $(AB)y = A(By)$ for every column $y$, and then we compare columns.

1. Let $A$ be $m \times n$ and $B$ be $n \times p$. Write $b_1, \dots, b_p$ for the columns of $B$.
2. By the [[definition of the product|076JGP6R3T]], column $k$ of $AB$ is the [[matrix-vector product|0CCQV3884B]] $A b_k$.
3. Let $y = (y_1, \dots, y_p)^{\mathsf{T}} \in F^p$. By the column view of the matrix-vector product, $(AB)y = \sum_{k} y_k (A b_k)$, and $By = \sum_k y_k b_k$.
4. By the linearity of $x \mapsto Ax$, $A(By) = A\bigl(\sum_k y_k b_k\bigr) = \sum_k y_k (A b_k)$.
5. By steps 3 and 4, $(AB)y = A(By)$ for all $y \in F^p$.
6. Now let $C$ be $p \times q$, and let $e_l$ be the $l$-th [[standard basis vector|0V7ZD6P7RK]] of $F^q$. For any matrix $M$ with $q$ columns, $Me_l$ is column $l$ of $M$.
7. Column $l$ of $(AB)C$ is $(AB)(Ce_l)$, by step 2 applied to $AB$ and $C$. By step 5 with $y = Ce_l$, this is $A(B(Ce_l))$.
8. Column $l$ of $A(BC)$ is $A((BC)e_l)$, by step 2. By step 5 for $B$ and $C$, $(BC)e_l = B(Ce_l)$. So this column is $A(B(Ce_l))$.
9. By steps 7 and 8, the matrices $(AB)C$ and $A(BC)$ have the same column $l$ for each $l$. Both are $m \times q$, so they are equal.

## Idea

Step 5 says that the linear map of $AB$ is the composite of the maps of $A$ and $B$: first $B$, then $A$.
Composition of functions is associative, so the map of $(AB)C$ equals the map of $A(BC)$.
A matrix is determined by its map, because its columns are the images of the basis vectors.

## Variants

- The [[proof by sums|1QVWFSF796]] works over any ring, without the linear map point of view.
- Over a noncommutative ring $R$, the same proof works if column vectors form a right module, with $A(yc) = (Ay)c$; then step 3 reads $By = \sum_k b_k y_k$.
- In abstract form: the map $A \mapsto (x \mapsto Ax)$ is injective, and it sends products to [[composites|0THBK9V825]], so associativity of composition gives associativity of the product.
