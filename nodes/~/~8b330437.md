## Statement

Let $a < b$ and let $f, g \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$.
Then there is $c \in (a, b)$ with
$$(f(b) - f(a)) \, g'(c) = (g(b) - g(a)) \, f'(c).$$

## Remarks

Apply [[Rolle's theorem|~964e4683]] to $h(x) = (f(b) - f(a)) g(x) - (g(b) - g(a)) f(x)$, which has $h(a) = h(b)$.
With $g(x) = x$ it is the [[mean value theorem|~9bd25708]].
It is the main step in the proof of [[L'Hôpital's rule|~713dab5a]]. Mathlib states it as `exists_ratio_deriv_eq_ratio_slope`.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of $(g(t), f(t))$ as a point moving along a curve in the plane.
At some time, the direction of motion is parallel to the chord joining the start and the end.
