## Summary

The theory of designs, matroids and finite geometries is the study of finite incidence structures, which are sets of points and blocks with a rule that tells which points lie on which blocks.
A balanced incomplete block design is a family of k-element blocks in a set of v points such that every pair of points lies in the same number of blocks.
A matroid is an abstract independence structure that has the common properties of linear independence in vector spaces and of forests in graphs.
Kirkman posed his schoolgirl problem in 1850, Fisher used designs to plan agricultural experiments in the 1920s, and Whitney introduced matroids in 1935.
This topic collects the pages of math2 on the area: block designs, Steiner systems and resolvable designs, Latin squares and orthogonal arrays, difference sets, finite projective planes and Galois geometry, generalized polygons, matroids and their invariants, and polyomino tilings.
Coding theory, group theory, graph theory and convex and discrete geometry have their own topics.
