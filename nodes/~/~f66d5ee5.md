## Summary

Let $\varphi$ be a [[propositional formula|~7139c138]] built with $\neg$, $\land$ and $\lor$.
For each subformula $\psi$ of $\varphi$ that is not a variable, take a new variable $x_\psi$, and for a variable $p$ put $x_p = p$.
For $\psi = \alpha \land \beta$, add the [[clauses|T3SXVH3ZZC]] $\set{\neg x_\psi, x_\alpha}$, $\set{\neg x_\psi, x_\beta}$, $\set{x_\psi, \neg x_\alpha, \neg x_\beta}$, which express $x_\psi \leftrightarrow x_\alpha \land x_\beta$.
For $\psi = \alpha \lor \beta$, add $\set{x_\psi, \neg x_\alpha}$, $\set{x_\psi, \neg x_\beta}$, $\set{\neg x_\psi, x_\alpha, x_\beta}$, which express $x_\psi \leftrightarrow x_\alpha \lor x_\beta$.
For $\psi = \neg \alpha$, add $\set{\neg x_\psi, \neg x_\alpha}$ and $\set{x_\psi, x_\alpha}$, which express $x_\psi \leftrightarrow \neg x_\alpha$.
The Tseitin transformation $T(\varphi)$ is the set of all these defining clauses together with the unit clause $\set{x_\varphi}$.
It is a formula in [[conjunctive normal form|0B3D4C8YF2]] of size linear in $\varphi$, and it is [[equisatisfiable|B37TYWXS74]] with $\varphi$, though not equivalent to it.
It is the standard way to feed an arbitrary formula or circuit to a SAT solver or to [[resolution|~99ab3fe3]].

## Motivation

Every formula has an equivalent CNF, but the conversion by the distributive law can make it exponentially longer.
The formula $(x_1 \land y_1) \lor \dots \lor (x_n \land y_n)$ has $2 n$ literals, and every equivalent CNF has at least $2^n$ clauses; see [[the size comparison|~68219df7]].
A satisfiability test does not need an equivalent formula.
It needs only a formula that is satisfiable exactly when the original one is.
Names for subformulas give such a formula.
Each new variable stands for the value of one subformula, and short clauses force it to take that value.

## Notation

We write $x_\psi$ for the new variable of the subformula $\psi$, and $T(\varphi)$ for the result.
Many texts present the input as a circuit.
Then the new variables are the outputs of the gates, and $T(\varphi)$ joins one small CNF for each gate.
Other connectives get their own defining clauses; for example $x_\psi \leftrightarrow (x_\alpha \leftrightarrow x_\beta)$ needs four clauses with three literals each.
The name is also written Tseytin.

## Remarks

### Examples

- For $\varphi = (p \land q) \lor (r \land s)$ the transformation gives 3 new variables and 10 clauses; see [[the Tseitin transformation of a disjunction of conjunctions|~93d04ed3]].
- For $\varphi = p \land q$ we get $x_\varphi$ with $\set{\neg x_\varphi, p}$, $\set{\neg x_\varphi, q}$, $\set{x_\varphi, \neg p, \neg q}$, $\set{x_\varphi}$. The only satisfying assignment is $p = q = x_\varphi = 1$, which matches the only model $p = q = 1$ of $\varphi$.
- For $\varphi = p \land \neg p$ the defining clauses force $x_\varphi = 0$. If $p = 0$, the clause $\set{\neg x_\varphi, p}$ does it, and if $p = 1$, then $x_{\neg p} = 0$ does it. So $T(\varphi)$ is unsatisfiable, as $\varphi$ is.
- For $(x_1 \land y_1) \lor \dots \lor (x_n \land y_n)$ the transformation has $6 n - 2$ clauses, against $2^n$ clauses for the distributive CNF; see [[the size comparison|~68219df7]].

### Non-examples

- Replacing a subformula by a new variable without the defining clauses is not the Tseitin transformation. It can turn the unsatisfiable $p \land \neg p$ into the satisfiable $p \land x$; see [[renaming without defining clauses|~18084edb]].
- The distributive conversion of $\varphi$ to CNF is not the Tseitin transformation. It adds no variables and gives an equivalent formula, but it can be exponentially longer.
- The set of defining clauses without the unit clause $\set{x_\varphi}$ is always satisfiable, by the values of the subformulas under any assignment. So it is not equisatisfiable with an unsatisfiable $\varphi$.

### Basic properties

Let $\varphi$ have $m$ occurrences of connectives, and let $P$ be the set of its variables.

- Size: $T(\varphi)$ has $m$ new variables and at most $3 m + 1$ clauses, each with at most 3 literals. So $T(\varphi)$ is in 3-CNF, and it can be computed in linear time.
- Correctness: every assignment $v$ to $P$ extends in exactly one way to an assignment that satisfies the defining clauses, namely $x_\psi \mapsto \bar v(\psi)$. So $T(\varphi)$ is satisfiable if and only if $\varphi$ is, and the models of $\varphi$ correspond one to one to the models of $T(\varphi)$.
- $T(\varphi)$ is not [[logically equivalent|0JMR2HM2N2]] to $\varphi$ in general: it mentions new variables. But $T(\varphi) \models \varphi$, and every model of $\varphi$ extends to a model of $T(\varphi)$.
- A formula $\varphi$ is a tautology if and only if $T(\neg \varphi)$ is unsatisfiable. So a [[resolution refutation|~3187381a]] of $T(\neg \varphi)$ proves $\varphi$.

The correctness has a short proof by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]].

1. Let $v$ be an assignment to $P$, and put $w(x_\psi) = \bar v(\psi)$ for each subformula $\psi$, with $w = v$ on $P$. Here $\bar v$ is the [[truth valuation|~41f42e3d]] of $v$.
2. For $\psi = \alpha \land \beta$ the defining clauses say $w(x_\psi) = \min(w(x_\alpha), w(x_\beta))$. This holds, since $\bar v(\alpha \land \beta) = \min(\bar v(\alpha), \bar v(\beta))$ by the definition of $\bar v$.
3. The cases $\lor$ and $\neg$ are the same, with $\max$ and with $1 - w(x_\alpha)$. So $w$ satisfies all defining clauses.
4. Conversely, let $w$ satisfy the defining clauses, and let $v$ be its restriction to $P$. By induction on $\psi$, the clauses of $\psi$ force $w(x_\psi) = \bar v(\psi)$. So $w$ is the extension of step 1.
5. Hence $w$ satisfies $\set{x_\varphi}$ if and only if $\bar v(\varphi) = 1$. This gives both the correspondence of models and the equisatisfiability.

### History

Grigori Tseitin introduced the transformation in the paper "On the complexity of derivation in propositional calculus".
He presented it at the Leningrad Seminar on Mathematical Logic in 1966, and the English translation appeared in 1970.
The same idea of naming subformulas gives the reduction of satisfiability of formulas and circuits to 3-CNF satisfiability in the theory of [[NP-completeness|REKDJ8V504]].

### Common errors

- Forgetting the unit clause $\set{x_\varphi}$. Without it the clauses are always satisfiable.
- Claiming that $T(\varphi)$ is equivalent to $\varphi$. Only satisfiability is kept, so $T(\varphi)$ cannot replace $\varphi$ inside a larger formula without its own new variables.
- Reusing one new variable for two different subformulas. Equal subformulas may share a variable, but different ones may not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a long rule is built from many small parts.
Writing it out in the form that a computer checker wants can make it huge.
Instead we give each part a short name, like "A" for "p and q".
Then we add a few small rules that say what each name means.
The checker now works with many small rules instead of one huge one.
The new rules can all be satisfied exactly when the old rule can.

### L2 human

We transform $\varphi = \neg (p \land q)$, which has 2 connectives.

1. Name the subformulas: $a$ for $p \land q$ and $b$ for $\neg (p \land q)$.
2. The clauses for $a \leftrightarrow p \land q$ are $\set{\neg a, p}$, $\set{\neg a, q}$, $\set{a, \neg p, \neg q}$.
3. The clauses for $b \leftrightarrow \neg a$ are $\set{\neg b, \neg a}$ and $\set{b, a}$.
4. Add the unit clause $\set{b}$. So $T(\varphi)$ has 6 clauses.
5. Check one model: $p = 1$, $q = 0$ makes $\varphi$ true. Then $a = 0$ and $b = 1$, and each of the 6 clauses has a true literal.
6. Check the converse: from $\set{b}$ and $\set{\neg b, \neg a}$ we get $a = 0$. Then $\set{a, \neg p, \neg q}$ gives $p = 0$ or $q = 0$, so $\varphi$ is true.

The typical use is in a SAT solver or a resolution prover.
To prove that $\varphi$ is a tautology, one tests $T(\neg \varphi)$ for unsatisfiability.
A trap is to read the values of the new variables as part of the answer.
Only the values on $P$ of a model of $T(\varphi)$ give a model of $\varphi$.

### L3 human

The transformation is a linear-time reduction from circuit satisfiability to 3-CNF satisfiability.
It gives one route to the [[Cook-Levin theorem|CTK8AH98Z1]]: the computation of a machine is a [[Boolean circuit|SQXNFGVADX]], and the circuit becomes a CNF.
When a subformula occurs only positively, one direction of its definition suffices.
This refinement keeps equisatisfiability, but it loses the one to one correspondence of models.
Tseitin introduced the idea to compare the length of proofs in propositional systems.
His extended resolution allows such definitions inside a refutation, and it is one of the strong systems of [[propositional proof complexity|CBAF7J051K]].
