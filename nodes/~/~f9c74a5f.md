## Proof

We give the structure of the proof of Atserias and Müller.
The step that we cite is their width lower bound, Lemma A below, which they prove by a Prover and Adversary argument.
Let $F$ be a CNF with variables $X_1, \dots, X_n$ and clauses $C_1, \dots, C_m$.
A refutation here is a sequence of non-tautological clauses, each a weakening of a clause of $F$ or of a resolvent of two earlier lines, that ends with $\square$.

*Part 1: the formulas.*

1. For $s \ge 1$, the CNF $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ has the variables $D[u, i, b]$ ("line $u$ contains the literal $X_i^{(b)}$"), $V[u, i]$ ("line $u$ is a resolvent on $X_i$", or an axiom when $i = 0$), $I[u, j]$ ("line $u$ weakens $C_j$", or is a resolvent when $j = 0$), and $L[u, v]$ and $R[u, v]$ (the two earlier lines). Here $u, v \le s$.
2. Its clauses say that these variables describe a refutation of $F$ with $s$ lines. So $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ is satisfiable if and only if $F$ has a refutation of length $s$.
3. Each variable *mentions* one line index, its first index $u$. The *index-width* of a clause is the number of line indices that its variables mention. Each clause of $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ has index-width at most $2$.
4. *Lemma A (cited, Lemma 4 of the paper).* Let $n, w, s \ge 1$ with $2^n \ge s \ge 6nw$, and let $F$ be unsatisfiable with $n$ variables. Then every resolution refutation of $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ has index-width at least $w$. The proof shows that bounded-width inferences cannot tell $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ from $\mathrm{REF}(F, 2^{n+1} - 1)$, which is satisfiable by the full tree refutation of $F$.
5. The *relativized* CNF $\mathrm{RREF}(F, s)$ adds variables $P[u]$, "line $u$ is active", in the style of Dantchev and Riis. It says that the active lines form a refutation of $F$ and that line $s$ is active. The clauses of $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ get extra literals $\neg P[u]$ for the lines they mention, and the indices keep their built-in order $1 < 2 < \dots < s$.

*Part 2: the lower bound when $F$ is unsatisfiable.*

6. *Lemma B.* For $n$ large and $F$ unsatisfiable with $n$ variables, every resolution refutation $\Pi$ of $\mathrm{RREF}(F, 13n^2)$ has more than $2^{2n/5}$ clauses.
7. Suppose $\Pi$ has $\ell \le 2^{2n/5}$ clauses, and let $t = 13n^2$. Choose a random restriction $\rho$. Set each $P[u]$ to $1$ or $0$ with probability $1/2$, and let $A$ be the set of $u$ with $P[u] = 1$. Set $L[u, v] = R[u, v] = 0$ for $u \in A$ and $v \notin A$. Set every other variable that mentions some $u \notin A$ to a random bit.
8. A literal that mentions $u$ is true under $\rho$ with probability at least $1/4$. So a clause of index-width at least $n$ survives with probability at most $(3/4)^n$.
9. By the union bound, some clause of $\Pi$ of index-width at least $n$ survives with probability at most $\ell (3/4)^n \le (2^{2/5} \cdot 3/4)^n$. Since $2^{2/5} \cdot 3/4 < 1$, this is less than $1/4$ for large $n$.
10. $|A|$ is binomial with mean $13n^2/2$. By the [[Chernoff bound|014PEBGDYF]], $|A| < 6n^2$ with probability less than $1/4$ for large $n$. Also $P[t] = 1$ with probability $1/2$.
11. So some $\rho$ has three properties: $\Pi$ restricted by $\rho$ has index-width less than $n$, $|A| \ge 6n^2$, and $t \in A$.
12. For this $\rho$, every clause of $\mathrm{RREF}(F, t)$ becomes true or becomes a clause of a copy of $\mathrm{REF}(F, |A|)$ with index set $A$. So $\Pi$ restricted by $\rho$ is a refutation of that copy with index-width less than $n$.
13. Since $2^n \ge 13n^2 \ge |A| \ge 6n \cdot n$ for large $n$, this contradicts Lemma A with $w = n$.

*Part 3: the upper bound when $F$ is satisfiable.*

14. *Lemma C.* If $F$ is satisfiable, then $\mathrm{RREF}(F, s)$ has a resolution refutation with $O((snm)^2)$ clauses.
15. Fix $\alpha$ that satisfies $F$. For $u = 1, 2, \dots, s$ in order, derive $\mathrm{True}(u) = \neg P[u] \lor \bigvee_{i} D[u, i, \alpha(X_i)]$, "if line $u$ is active, then it has a literal true under $\alpha$".
16. If line $u$ weakens $C_j$, use a literal of $C_j$ true under $\alpha$. If line $u$ is a resolvent on $X_i$, take the earlier premise whose literal on $X_i$ is false under $\alpha$. By $\mathrm{True}$ for that line, which comes first in the built-in order, it has another literal true under $\alpha$, and this literal passes to line $u$.
17. Each $\mathrm{True}(u)$ takes polynomially many resolution steps. Then $\mathrm{True}(s)$, the clauses saying line $s$ is empty, and the clause $P[s]$ give $\square$.

*Part 4: the theorem.*

18. Let $G(F) = \mathrm{RREF}(F, 13n^2)$ for a 3-CNF $F$ with $n$ variables. Then $m \le 8n^3$, so $r(G(F))$ lies between $n^{1/q}$ and $n^q$ for a constant $q$.
19. Part 1 (a) follows from Lemma C, and part 1 (b) follows from Lemma B.
20. For parts 2 to 4, let $t$ be the time bound, non-decreasing and computable within its own bound. Given $F$, compute $G(F)$ and run the automating algorithm for $t(n^q + n^{qc})$ steps. Answer "satisfiable" if and only if it outputs, in time, a sequence of clauses that is a resolution refutation of $G(F)$. This check takes polynomial time.
21. If $F$ is satisfiable, a refutation with at most $n^{qc}$ clauses exists, so the algorithm finishes in time. If $F$ is unsatisfiable, every refutation has more than $2^{r^{1/d}}$ clauses, which exceeds the budget when $t$ is subexponential. So this decides 3-SAT in time $t(\mathrm{poly}(n)) + \mathrm{poly}(n)$. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Encode "$F$ has a short refutation" as a CNF.
If $F$ is satisfiable, the satisfying assignment gives a short refutation of the encoding, line by line.
If $F$ is unsatisfiable, a random restriction turns any short refutation of the relativized encoding into a narrow refutation of the plain encoding, and a width argument rules that out.

## Variants

- Pudlák used $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ and the line by line refutation to show that the canonical pair of resolution is symmetric. Atserias and Müller could not prove a length lower bound for $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ itself, which is why they relativize.
- The built-in order is necessary in Lemma C. If the order were given by new variables with their own axioms, the relativized formula would be hard for resolution whether or not $F$ is satisfiable.
- Taking $\mathrm{RREF}(F, 13n \cdot n^k)$ for a large constant $k$, without two types of clauses that Lemma C does not use, moves the exponents $c$ and $d$ close to $1$ and $2$.
- Alekhnovich and Razborov used a different reduction from a parameterized problem. It gives the weaker conclusion FPT = W[P], but it also applies to tree-like resolution.
