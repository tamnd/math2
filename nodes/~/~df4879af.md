## Proof

1. Let $V_T$ be the [[$F[x]$-module of $T$|~772e5994]]. By part 1 of the [[dictionary theorem|~3b7dc48a]], $V_T$ is finitely generated and torsion.
2. $F[x]$ is a [[Euclidean domain|0CHS7RBD39]], hence a [[principal ideal domain|0QKVE1GD9A]].
3. By the invariant factor form of the [[structure theorem over a principal ideal domain|TXMV0YDACG]], there are $s \ge 0$ and nonzero non-units $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$ of $F[x]$ with an isomorphism $\psi \colon F[x]^s \oplus F[x]/(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus F[x]/(d_r) \to V_T$.
4. The units of $F[x]$ are the nonzero constants. So each $d_i$ is nonconstant, and we may replace $d_i$ by $d_i$ divided by its leading coefficient. This changes neither the ideal $(d_i)$ nor the divisibility. So we take each $d_i$ monic.
5. $s = 0$: the elements $1, x, x^2, \dots$ of $F[x]$ are [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]] over $F$, so $F[x]^s$ has infinite $F$-dimension when $s \ge 1$, while $\psi$ is $F$-linear and injective into the finite-dimensional $V$.
6. Let $u_i$ be the element of the direct sum with the class of $1$ in the summand $F[x]/(d_i)$ and $0$ elsewhere, and put $v_i = \psi(u_i)$.
7. The submodule generated by $u_i$ is the $i$-th summand. Since $\psi$ is an isomorphism, $V$ is the internal direct sum of the submodules $F[x] v_i$.
8. By the definition of the module structure, $F[x] v_i = \set{f(T) v_i : f \in F[x]} = Z(v_i; T)$. With step 7, this proves part 1.
9. For $f \in F[x]$, $f(T) v_i = 0$ if and only if $f \cdot u_i = 0$, that is, $f \in (d_i)$. So the annihilator ideal of $v_i$ is $(d_i)$, and the $T$-annihilator is $m_{v_i} = d_i$, which is nonconstant. In particular $v_i \ne 0$. This proves part 2.
10. Minimal polynomial. Let $f \in F[x]$. Since $f(T)$ commutes with every $g(T)$, $f(T) = 0$ if and only if $f(T) v_i = 0$ for all $i$, by step 7.
11. By step 9 this holds if and only if $d_i \mid f$ for all $i$, that is, if and only if $d_r \mid f$, since $d_i \mid d_r$. So $m_T = d_r$.
12. Characteristic polynomial. By part 5 of the [[lemma on cyclic subspaces|~9c6edc2a]], $T|_{Z(v_i)}$ has the matrix $C(d_i)$ in the basis $v_i, T v_i, \dots, T^{\deg d_i - 1} v_i$.
13. The union of these bases is a basis of $V$ by step 7, and in it $T$ has the block diagonal matrix $C(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus C(d_r)$, since each summand is $T$-invariant.
14. By the [[determinant of a block triangular matrix|0J9TVTNB4K]] and induction on $r$, $\chi_T = \prod_i \det(xI - C(d_i))$. By the [[theorem on companion matrices|~dd227218]], this is $d_1 \cdots d_r$.
15. Uniqueness. Let $V = Z(w_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus Z(w_t)$ with nonconstant monic annihilators $e_1 \mid \cdots \mid e_t$.
16. For each $j$, the map $F[x] \to Z(w_j)$, $f \mapsto f(T) w_j$, is a surjective module map with kernel $(e_j)$. So $Z(w_j) \cong F[x]/(e_j)$, and $V_T \cong F[x]/(e_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus F[x]/(e_t)$.
17. By the uniqueness part of the structure theorem, $t = r$ and $(e_j) = (d_j)$ for each $j$. Two monic generators of the same ideal are equal, so $e_j = d_j$.

## Idea

The module $V_T$ is a finitely generated torsion module over the principal ideal domain $F[x]$.
The structure theorem splits it into cyclic modules $F[x]/(d_i)$ with $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$, and cyclic submodules of $V_T$ are exactly cyclic subspaces.
The minimal and characteristic polynomials are then read off from the summands.

## Variants

Hoffman and Kunze give a proof inside linear algebra, without modules.
They define $T$-admissible subspaces, use the conductor ideals $S(v; W) = \set{f : f(T) v \in W}$.
They build the $v_i$ one at a time, each time with a vector whose conductor into the sum of the earlier summands has maximal degree.
A third proof computes the Smith normal form of $xI - A$; see the [[theorem on the characteristic matrix|~57adc122]].
