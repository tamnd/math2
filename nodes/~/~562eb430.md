## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

1. One row operation on $I_2$ gives one of three kinds of matrices. These are $P_{12} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, a diagonal matrix $D_i(c)$, or a matrix $T_{ij}(c)$ with $1$ on the diagonal.
2. $A$ is not $P_{12}$, because its $(1, 2)$ entry is $2$.
3. $A$ is not diagonal, because its off-diagonal entries are nonzero.
4. $A$ is not of type $T_{ij}(c)$, because its diagonal entries are $0$ and not $1$.
5. So $A$ is not an [[elementary matrix|0M79HRJCRR]].
6. But $A = D_1(2) P_{12}$: $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
7. So $A$ is invertible, with $A^{-1} = P_{12}^{-1} D_1(2)^{-1} = P_{12} D_1(\tfrac{1}{2}) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ \tfrac{1}{2} & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
8. Check: $A A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 + 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 + 0 \end{pmatrix} = I_2$ and $A^{-1} A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \end{pmatrix} = I_2$.

## Remarks

Invertible and elementary are different notions: each elementary matrix is invertible, but an invertible matrix is in general only a product of elementary matrices.
The factorization of step 6 is not unique: $A = P_{12} D_2(2)$ also holds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An elementary matrix records exactly one row step.
This matrix needs two steps from the identity: exchange the rows, and double one of them.

### L2 human

The test for an elementary matrix is to compare it with the three patterns of step 1.
A trap: a matrix that looks simple, with only two nonzero entries, need not be elementary.
