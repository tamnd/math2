## Summary

Mathematical cryptography is the study of methods that keep information secret and authentic in the presence of an adversary, and of the mathematics on which their security rests.
A cryptosystem is a set of algorithms for key generation, encryption and decryption, and it is secure in the computational sense when no efficient adversary can learn useful information about the message from the ciphertext without the key.
Cryptography protects communication, payments and stored data, and its security rests on problems that are believed to be hard, such as integer factorization, discrete logarithms and lattice problems.
Shannon gave a mathematical theory of secrecy systems in 1949, and Diffie and Hellman in 1976 and Rivest, Shamir and Adleman in 1977 started public-key cryptography.
This topic collects the mathematical cryptography pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Coding theory, information theory and the theory of computation have their own topics.
