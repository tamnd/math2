## Proof

We use the notation of the page [[Unprovability of the graph minor theorem|WXNTPQGJ38]].
A sequence of graphs is *bad* if no $i < j$ has $G_i \le G_j$.

**Part 1 and part 3: $\mathrm{GM}$ from the Robertson-Seymour theorem.**

1. Fix $n$, and suppose that no $m$ works. Then for every $m$ there is a bad sequence $G_1, \dots, G_m$ in which $G_i$ has size at most $n + i$.
2. There are finitely many graphs of size at most $n + i$, up to isomorphism. So there are finitely many candidates at each position.
3. Call a bad sequence $(G_1, \dots, G_j)$ *extendable* if it is the start of bad sequences of every length $m \ge j$. The empty sequence is extendable, by step 1.
4. If $(G_1, \dots, G_j)$ is extendable, then one of the finitely many choices of $G_{j+1}$ occurs in bad sequences of every length. So some longer sequence is extendable.
5. Step 4 gives an infinite sequence $G_1, G_2, \dots$ whose finite starts are all bad.
6. So no $i < j$ has $G_i \le G_j$. This contradicts the [[Robertson-Seymour theorem|WP3GDNWJJW]], cited here as a theorem of $\mathsf{ZFC}$.
7. So $m$ exists for every $n$, which is part 1. The argument is a proof in $\mathsf{ZFC}$, which gives part 3.

**Part 2: the structure of the unprovability proof.**

1. **Cited lemma 1** (Friedman, Robertson and Seymour 1987). Over a weak base system, $\mathrm{GM}$ implies a finite form of an extended Kruskal theorem for finite trees with labels.
2. **Cited lemma 2** (Friedman, Robertson and Seymour 1987). The trees of the extended Kruskal theorem code an ordinal notation system up to the [[proof-theoretic ordinal|C72RBH0CDR]] of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$. A slowly descending sequence of notations gives a bad sequence of trees with the size bound.
3. So $\mathrm{GM}$ implies a $\Pi^0_2$ statement: every slowly descending sequence of notations in this system is finite.
4. **Cited lemma 3** (ordinal analysis). $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ does not prove the statement of step 3 for the whole notation system.
5. Suppose that $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ proves $\mathrm{GM}$. Then by steps 1 to 3 it proves the statement of step 3, which contradicts lemma 3.
6. So $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ does not prove $\mathrm{GM}$.

## Idea

If every length had a bad sequence of graphs, the finitely many choices at each position would fit together into an infinite bad sequence, which the Robertson-Seymour theorem forbids.
For the unprovability, graph minors encode labelled trees, and these encode ordinal notations up to the strength of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$.

## Variants

- The same tree argument derives [[Friedman's finite form of Kruskal's theorem|~b0f91a70]] from Kruskal's theorem.
- The structure of Part 2 follows the unprovability proof for the finite Kruskal theorem in $\mathsf{ATR}_0$, with a stronger theory and a larger ordinal.
