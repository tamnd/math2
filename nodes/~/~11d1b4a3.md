## Statement

Let $R$ be a [[principal ideal domain|~91f9e139]].
Then $R$ is a [[unique factorisation domain|~4ed89225]].

## Remarks

Existence of factorisations: a principal ideal domain is Noetherian, so a process of splitting an element into factors must stop.
Uniqueness: in a principal ideal domain, an irreducible element generates a maximal ideal, so it is prime.
Consequences: $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and $K[x]$ for a field $K$ have unique factorisation.
The converse is false: $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is a unique factorisation domain but [[not a principal ideal domain|~88c664a9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a principal ideal domain, every irreducible element behaves like a prime number.
That is the key to unique factorisation.
So every Euclidean domain, such as the integers, has unique factorisation.
