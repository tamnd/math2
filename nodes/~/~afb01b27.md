## Statement

Let $a, b, c$ be real numbers with $a \ne 0$. Consider the equation $a y'' + b y' + c y = 0$.

Suppose that its [[characteristic equation|0QDXMG46QW]] $a r^2 + b r + c = 0$ has the two non-real roots $\alpha + i\beta$ and $\alpha - i\beta$, where $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\beta \ne 0$. This happens exactly when $b^2 - 4ac < 0$.

Then the real solutions of the equation are exactly the functions
$$y(x) = C_1 e^{\alpha x} \cos(\beta x) + C_2 e^{\alpha x} \sin(\beta x),$$
where $C_1, C_2$ are real constants.

## Remarks

The two functions $e^{\alpha x}\cos(\beta x)$ and $e^{\alpha x}\sin(\beta x)$ are the real and imaginary parts of the complex solution $e^{(\alpha + i\beta)x}$. They form a basis of the two dimensional real solution space.

Example: $y'' + 2y' + 5y = 0$ has the roots $-1 \pm 2i$, so $y = e^{-x}(C_1 \cos 2x + C_2 \sin 2x)$.
