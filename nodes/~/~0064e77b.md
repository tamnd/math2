## Summary

Homotopy theory is the study of spaces and maps up to continuous deformation.
Two maps are homotopic when one deforms continuously into the other, and two spaces have the same homotopy type when there are maps in both directions whose composites are homotopic to the identity maps.
Homotopy invariants classify maps and bundles, and the stable methods of the subject compute cobordism rings and solve classical problems about spheres and division algebras.
Hurewicz defined the higher homotopy groups in 1935, and Serre computed many homotopy groups of spheres with spectral sequences in the early 1950s.
This topic collects the homotopy theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Algebraic topology, manifolds and differential topology, category theory and homological algebra and K-theory have their own topics.
