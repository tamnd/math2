## Example

The atoms are $p$ ("Tweety is a bird") and $q$ ("Tweety flies").
Write a valuation as $ab$, with $a$ the value of $p$ and $b$ the value of $q$.
Let $K = \mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge q)$, and take the total preorder
$$11 < 10 \approx 01 < 00.$$
Define $\mathrm{Mod}(K * \varphi) := \min(\mathrm{Mod}(\varphi), \le)$, as in [[the representation theorem|~d81739e6]].
We compute three revisions and check postulates of [[AGM belief revision|~30b6c328]].

1. The preorder is faithful: $\mathrm{Mod}(K) = \set{11}$, and $11$ is strictly below every other valuation.
2. Revision by $\neg q$: $\mathrm{Mod}(\neg q) = \set{10, 00}$, and $10 < 00$. So $K * \neg q = \mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge \neg q)$: Tweety is a bird that does not fly.
3. Revision by $\neg(p \wedge q)$: the models are $10, 01, 00$, and the minimal ones are $10$ and $01$. So $K * \neg(p \wedge q) = \mathrm{Cn}(p \leftrightarrow \neg q)$.
4. Revision by $p \vee q$: this is consistent with $K$, and $11$ is its minimal model. So $K * (p \vee q) = K = K + (p \vee q)$, as postulates 3 and 4 demand.
5. Postulate 2 in step 3: $\neg(p \wedge q)$ holds at $10$ and at $01$.
6. Postulate 7 with $\varphi = \neg(p \wedge q)$ and $\psi = \neg q$: $\varphi \wedge \psi$ has models $10, 00$, so $K * (\varphi \wedge \psi) = \mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge \neg q)$.
7. $(K * \varphi) + \psi$ has models $\set{10, 01} \cap \set{10, 00} = \set{10}$. So both sides equal $\mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge \neg q)$, and postulates 7 and 8 hold here.

## Remarks

The ranking expresses that giving up one belief is more plausible than giving up both.
In step 3 the agent learns only that not both beliefs hold, and it suspends judgement between them, because $10$ and $01$ are tied.
If we change the ranking to $11 < 10 < 01 < 00$, then step 3 gives $\mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge \neg q)$: the agent keeps "Tweety is a bird" and gives up "Tweety flies".
The postulates allow both rankings; they do not say which belief is more entrenched.

## Explanations

### L1 human

You believe that Tweety is a bird and that Tweety flies.
Someone tells you: "That is not all true."
With the first ranking, you cannot tell which belief is wrong, so you keep only "exactly one of them is true".
With the second ranking, you trust "bird" more than "flies", so you drop "flies".

### L2 human

1. List the models of the new information.
2. Keep the lowest ones in the ranking.
3. The revised belief set is everything true in all the kept models.

The typical use is to compute a revision in a small knowledge base by hand.
A trap: the ranking ties $10$ and $01$, so the result of step 3 is a disjunction, not one of the two atoms.
