## Summary

The *Kripke-Feferman theory* $\mathsf{KF}$ is a classical theory in the language $\mathcal{L}_T$ of arithmetic with a truth predicate $T$.
Its axioms are those of [[Peano arithmetic|HACXBCM7SS]], the induction axioms for all formulas of $\mathcal{L}_T$, and thirteen truth axioms.
The truth axioms state, for $T$, the clauses of [[strong Kleene logic|SZHA10A9X7]], separately for a sentence and for its negation.
They include the iteration axiom $T[T\dot A] \leftrightarrow TA$, and they do not include the classical negation axiom $T[\neg A] \leftrightarrow \neg TA$.
The exact list is on the page [[Kripke-Feferman theory (definition)|~a625d303]].
$\mathsf{KF}$ is the standard classical axiomatization of [[Kripke's theory of truth|~1dd87a57]], and it is a type-free truth theory that is consistent and $\omega$-consistent.

## Motivation

Kripke's fixed points are semantic objects: they are sets of codes, built by a transfinite induction.
A theory with axioms for $T$ is needed to reason about truth inside arithmetic.
The typed theory $\mathsf{CT}$ has the negation axiom, but it speaks only about sentences without $T$.
For a type-free predicate, the SEP entry states that the negation axiom and the iteration axiom are inconsistent over weak theories of syntax.
The [[liar paradox|~005ac188]] is the source of this inconsistency.
$\mathsf{KF}$ keeps iteration and gives up the negation axiom.
In its place, it has the clauses by which Kripke's construction puts a sentence or its negation into the extension.

## Notation

1. $A$, $B$ range over codes of sentences of $\mathcal{L}_T$, and $\dot x$ is the numeral of $x$.
2. $T[\neg A]$ abbreviates "$T$ holds of the code of the negation of $A$". Square brackets mark syntactic operations that are expressed in arithmetic.
3. $\mathsf{Cons}$ is the axiom $\forall A\, \neg (TA \wedge T[\neg A])$. $\mathsf{Compl}$ is the axiom $\forall A\, (TA \vee T[\neg A])$.
4. Feferman's own name is $\mathrm{Ref}(\mathsf{PA})$, the weak reflective closure of $\mathsf{PA}$.
5. Some versions have a second predicate $F$ for falsity, with $F A$ in place of $T[\neg A]$.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. For the liar sentence $\lambda$, $\mathsf{KF} \vdash T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \leftrightarrow T\ulcorner \neg \lambda \urcorner$; see [[the liar in KF|~6043fc0b]].
2. Each strong Kleene fixed point $(E, A)$ gives a model $(\mathcal{N}, E)$ of $\mathsf{KF} + \mathsf{Cons}$; see [[this example|~c0100a76]].
3. $\mathsf{KF}$ proves $T\ulcorner T\ulcorner 0 = 0 \urcorner \urcorner$. The axiom for atomic sentences gives $T\ulcorner 0 = 0 \urcorner$, and the iteration axiom adds one $T$.
4. For each arithmetical sentence $\varphi$, $\mathsf{KF} \vdash T\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner \leftrightarrow \varphi$. The proof is by induction on $\varphi$, for $\varphi$ and $\neg \varphi$ together.
5. $\mathsf{KF} + \mathsf{Cons}$ proves $\lambda$ and $\neg T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$. $\mathsf{KF} + \mathsf{Compl}$ proves $\neg \lambda$ and $T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$. Both proofs are in [[the liar in KF|~6043fc0b]].

### Non-examples

1. The [[Friedman-Sheard theory|~c09ae4a3]] is not a version of $\mathsf{KF}$. It has the negation axiom and the rules NEC and CONEC, and it lacks the iteration axiom.
2. The [[compositional theory CT|~5af95d4a]] is not type-free. Its axioms apply only to sentences without $T$, and it has the negation axiom for them.
3. The [[naive truth theory|~34d21c75]] is not $\mathsf{KF}$. $\mathsf{KF}$ is consistent, so it does not prove the T-sentence $T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \leftrightarrow \lambda$.

### Basic properties

1. $\mathsf{KF}$ is consistent: the standard model with the extension of a Kripke fixed point satisfies it, by [[this example|~c0100a76]].
2. $\mathsf{KF}$ does not prove the T-sentence for $\lambda$. With $\lambda \leftrightarrow \neg T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$, that T-sentence gives a contradiction, as in the [[liar paradox|~005ac188]].
3. $\mathsf{KF}$ does not prove $\mathsf{Compl}$. In the model from the least fixed point, neither $\lambda$ nor $\neg \lambda$ is in $E$.
4. The SEP entry states that, over $\mathsf{KF}$, the scheme $T\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner \to \varphi$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{Cons}$. The scheme $\varphi \to T\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{Compl}$.
5. The same entry states that each of the two extensions is consistent and $\omega$-consistent, and that the two together are inconsistent.
6. *Strength.* The SEP entry reports Feferman's result (1991): $\mathsf{KF}$ is proof-theoretically equivalent to ramified analysis through all levels below $\varepsilon_0$.
7. So $\mathsf{KF}$ recovers $\varepsilon_0$ levels of the [[Tarskian hierarchy|Z6MCGB0ZFX]], and it is stronger than $\mathsf{CT}$ and FS.

### History

The SEP entry credits the system to Feferman, on the basis of his lectures of 1979 and 1983.
Feferman published his version as $\mathrm{Ref}(\mathsf{PA})$ in 1991.
Other versions were in print before that, for example by Reinhardt (1986) and Cantini (1989), who both cite Feferman's unpublished work.
The SEP entry on the liar notes that a version that admits both gaps and gluts is now the usual default (Visser 1984, Halbach 2014).

### Common errors

1. Thinking that $\mathsf{KF}$ is a three-valued theory. It is formulated in classical logic; only the notion of truth that it describes is not classical.
2. Thinking that $\mathsf{KF}$ proves that the liar is neither true nor false. It proves only that $\lambda$ and $\neg \lambda$ are both true or both not true.
3. Reading $T[\neg TA] \leftrightarrow T[\neg A]$ as "$\neg TA$ is true if $A$ is not true". It says: $\neg TA$ is true if the negation of $A$ is true.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Kripke built the truth predicate in stages: first the true facts about numbers, then the sentences that call those facts true, and so on.
The Kripke-Feferman theory writes the rules of this construction as axioms.
For example, "it is true that '$2 + 2 = 4$' is true" follows from "'$2 + 2 = 4$' is true".
The theory does not say that a sentence is true exactly when its negation is not true.
That rule fails for the liar, which is never put into either class by the construction.

### L2 human

1. Let $\lambda$ be a liar sentence, so $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$.
2. The truth axioms for $\exists$, $\wedge$ and arithmetic reduce $T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$ to $T\ulcorner \neg T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \urcorner$.
3. The axiom $T[\neg T\dot A] \leftrightarrow T[\neg A]$ turns this into $T\ulcorner \neg \lambda \urcorner$.
4. So $\mathsf{KF} \vdash T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner \leftrightarrow T\ulcorner \neg \lambda \urcorner$.
5. If we add $\mathsf{Cons}$, we get $\neg T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$, and so $\lambda$ by step 1.

The typical use of $\mathsf{KF}$ is to measure, in classical logic, how much of Kripke's theory can be stated as axioms.
The trap is step 5: $\mathsf{KF} + \mathsf{Cons}$ proves $\lambda$, and it also proves that $\lambda$ is not true.

### L3 human

$\mathsf{KF}$ is sound for the closed-off Kripke construction.
This classical model uses the extension of a fixed point and puts the gap into the false part.
The SEP entry on the liar states that this model satisfies $(T\ulcorner A \urcorner \vee T\ulcorner \neg A \urcorner) \to (T\ulcorner A \urcorner \leftrightarrow A)$.
So the T-schema holds for sentences that are true or false in the fixed point.
The SEP entry also reports a result of Horsten and Leigh (2016) on a variant of $\mathsf{KF}$ with a falsity predicate.
Its truth axioms follow by uniform reflection over the positive untyped T-sentences.
The Reinhardt-Bacon observation, as the SEP entry on the liar reports it, applies here.
A classical theory that proves a restricted T-schema $H\ulcorner A \urcorner \to (T\ulcorner A \urcorner \leftrightarrow A)$ proves $C \wedge \neg H\ulcorner C \urcorner$ for some sentence $C$.
For $\mathsf{KF} + \mathsf{Cons}$ and the liar, this is $\lambda \wedge \neg T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$.
The supervaluation analogue of $\mathsf{KF}$ is Cantini's theory $\mathsf{VF}$; see [[axiomatic theories of truth|~67eedfc4]].
