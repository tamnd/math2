## Example

Let $N = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
We follow the [[definition of condensation|F7D849A4RZ]].

1. Step 1: the connected $2 \times 2$ minors give $N^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} -5 & 4 & -2 \\ 3 & -1 & 4 \\ -2 & 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. For example, entry $(1, 1)$ is $1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 3 = -5$, and entry $(3, 3)$ is $1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 1 = -1$.
3. Step 2 divides by the interior of $N$, which is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. Entry $(1, 1)$: $((-5)(-1) - 4 \cdot 3) / 1 = -7$.
5. Entry $(1, 2)$: $(4 \cdot 4 - (-2)(-1)) / 2 = 14/2 = 7$.
6. Entry $(2, 1)$: $(3 \cdot 1 - (-1)(-2)) / 1 = 1$.
7. Entry $(2, 2)$: $((-1)(-1) - 4 \cdot 1) / 1 = -3$.
8. So $N^{(2)} = \begin{pmatrix} -7 & 7 \\ 1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$.
9. Step 3 divides by the interior of $N^{(1)}$, which is $-1$: $((-7)(-3) - 7 \cdot 1) / (-1) = 14 / (-1) = -14$.
10. So condensation gives $\det N = -14$.

## Remarks

The check by another method: the [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]] along row 1 uses the minors $-3, 5, 7, 1$.
It gives $1 \cdot (-3) - 2 \cdot 5 + 0 \cdot 7 - 1 \cdot 1 = -14$.
Step 9 is one use of the [[Desnanot-Jacobi identity|~aa63b68e]] for the whole matrix: the four corner minors of order $3$ are the entries of $N^{(2)}$, and the interior minor is $-1$.
The divisor $2$ in step 5 divides $14$ exactly, because the quotient is a connected minor of $N$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The method works with negative numbers in the same way.
Here the last division is by $-1$, and it changes the sign of the result.

### L2 human

The typical use is to compute and to check a determinant with small integers only.
A trap: the sign of each divisor matters, so write it with its sign before you divide.
