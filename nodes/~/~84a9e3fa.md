## Definition

Let $(W, R)$ be a Kripke frame, as in [[Kripke semantics|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]].
For $X \subseteq W$ let $m_R(X) = \set{w \in W : w R v \text{ for some } v \in X}$.
A *general frame* is a triple $\mathfrak{g} = (W, R, A)$ where $A \subseteq \mathcal{P}(W)$ is closed under finite unions, complements relative to $W$, and $m_R$.
The members of $A$ are the *admissible sets*.
So $(A, \cup, W \setminus \cdot, \emptyset, m_R)$ is a [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]] of sets with an operator, the *complex algebra* $\mathfrak{g}^*$ of $\mathfrak{g}$.

A valuation $V$ is *admissible* when $V(p) \in A$ for every variable $p$.
A formula $\varphi$ is *valid* on $\mathfrak{g}$, written $\mathfrak{g} \models \varphi$, when $\varphi$ is true at every world under every admissible valuation.

A general frame $\mathfrak{g} = (W, R, A)$ is:

1. *differentiated* if for all $u \neq v$ in $W$ some $a \in A$ has $u \in a$ and $v \notin a$;
2. *tight* if, whenever $u R v$ fails, some $a \in A$ has $v \in a$ and $u \notin m_R(a)$;
3. *compact* if every family of admissible sets with the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection;
4. *refined* if it is differentiated and tight;
5. *descriptive* if it is refined and compact.

## Notation

Many texts write $\langle R \rangle X$ or $\lozenge X$ for $m_R(X)$, and $(W, R, A)$ or $\mathfrak{g}$ for a general frame.
The *full* general frame of $(W, R)$ is $(W, R, \mathcal{P}(W))$, and it is identified with the Kripke frame.
For a modal algebra $\mathfrak{B} = (B, \vee, \neg, 0, \lozenge)$, its *dual general frame* $\mathfrak{B}_*$ has as worlds the [[ultrafilters|CQM84EW10G]] of $B$, the relation $u Q v$ iff $\lozenge b \in u$ for every $b \in v$, and the admissible sets $\hat{b} = \set{u : b \in u}$ for $b \in B$.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. Every Kripke frame is a general frame with all subsets admissible; see [[full frames are general frames|~a5e848aa]].
2. A finite frame with all subsets admissible is descriptive; see [[a finite full frame is descriptive|~0ec2588e]].
3. The dual general frame $\mathfrak{B}_*$ of every modal algebra is descriptive. Its admissible sets form a basis of clopen sets of a [[Stone space|6KQ6E3YHKR]].
4. For a normal logic $\Lambda$, the canonical general frame has the maximal $\Lambda$-consistent sets as worlds and the sets $\set{\Gamma : \varphi \in \Gamma}$ as admissible sets. It is the dual of the [[Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra|QC9M3Q8B8Q]] of $\Lambda$, so it is descriptive.

### Non-examples

1. The frame $(\N, <)$ with the finite and the cofinite sets as admissible sets is a refined general frame that is not compact, so it is not descriptive; see [[a refined frame that is not descriptive|~751a44bd]].
2. On $(\N, <)$, the family of finite sets is not a general frame, because it is not closed under complements.

### Basic properties

1. If $V$ is admissible, then the truth set of every formula is admissible, by induction on the formula, since $\neg$, $\vee$ and $\lozenge$ are computed by complement, union and $m_R$.
2. Every normal modal logic $\Lambda$ is the logic of its canonical general frame. So every normal logic is complete for general frames, and even for descriptive frames.
3. Jónsson-Tarski duality: every modal algebra $\mathfrak{B}$ is isomorphic to $(\mathfrak{B}_*)^*$, by $b \mapsto \hat{b}$. A general frame $\mathfrak{g}$ is descriptive if and only if it is isomorphic to $(\mathfrak{g}^*)_*$. We cite both facts.
4. The descriptive frames and the modal algebras form dually equivalent categories, with bounded morphisms that pull admissible sets back to admissible sets as the frame morphisms.
5. If $(W, R)$ validates $\varphi$, then every general frame $(W, R, A)$ validates $\varphi$, because admissible valuations are valuations. The converse fails; see [[Kripke incompleteness|~481a531d]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A general frame is a picture of points and arrows, together with a list of allowed regions.
A statement may only be made true on an allowed region.
With fewer allowed regions, fewer counterexamples exist, so more formulas become valid.
For example, on the numbers with "less than", if the allowed regions are the finite sets and the sets with finitely many gaps, then "true only at $5$" is allowed, and "true only at the even numbers" is not.

### L2 human

We check that the admissible sets of Non-example 1 are closed under $m_<$.

1. Let $X \subseteq \N$ be finite and nonempty, with largest element $m$. Then $w < v$ for some $v \in X$ exactly when $w < m$. So $m_<(X) = \set{0, \ldots, m-1}$, which is finite.
2. $m_<(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, which is finite.
3. Let $X$ be cofinite. For every $w$, some $v > w$ lies in $X$, since $X$ is infinite. So $m_<(X) = \N$, which is cofinite.
4. Finite and cofinite sets are closed under finite unions and complements. So the triple is a general frame.

The typical use of general frames is to give semantics to logics that are not complete for Kripke frames.
A trap: validity on a general frame is weaker than validity on its underlying Kripke frame, so a general frame of a logic need not have a Kripke frame of that logic underneath.

### L3 human

General frames are the geometric side of the algebraic semantics of modal logic.
Descriptive frames are exactly the modal spaces of Jónsson-Tarski duality: Stone spaces with a relation whose images of points are closed and whose $m_R$ preserves clopen sets.
This duality extends Stone's duality for Boolean algebras, and it is the setting of the Goldblatt-Thomason theorem, of Sahlqvist canonicity, and of the Blok-Esakia theorem through Esakia duality for Heyting algebras.
