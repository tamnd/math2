## Description

The Takeuti-Feferman-Buchholz ordinal is the countable ordinal $\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1})$, where $\psi_0$ is [[Buchholz's function|E7WEFRMQTB]] and $\Omega_\omega = \aleph_\omega$.
Here $\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1}$ is the least [[epsilon number|PQSHBKCE7V]] above $\Omega_\omega$, the limit of $\Omega_\omega + 1$, $\omega^{\Omega_\omega + 1}$, $\omega^{\omega^{\Omega_\omega + 1}}$, and so on.
In Feferman's notation the same ordinal is $\theta_{\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1}}(0)$.
It is the order type of the countable part of Buchholz's notation system: the terms of $\mathrm{OT}$ below the term for $\Omega_1$ (Wikipedia, "Buchholz psi functions", section "Ordinal notation").
It is the [[proof-theoretic ordinal|C72RBH0CDR]] of the theory $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA} + \mathrm{BI}$ of [[second-order arithmetic|DC60FYCSPM]], and of the theory $\mathrm{ID}_\omega$ of $\omega$ times [[iterated inductive definitions|5VNT564Y2W]] (Wikipedia, "Ordinal analysis", section "Table of ordinal analyses").
Here $\mathrm{BI}$ is the scheme of bar induction: transfinite induction along every well-founded relation, for every formula.

## Remarks

### The limit of Buchholz's system

The ordinal is the largest value of $\psi_0$.
We give the argument, with the cited fact $\psi_\omega(\xi) = \omega^{\Omega_\omega + \xi}$ for $\xi < \varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1}$ (Wikipedia, "Buchholz psi functions", section "Properties").

1. Let $E = \varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1}$. We show by induction on $n$ that every element of $C^n_0(\alpha)$ is below $E$, for every $\alpha$.
2. The start $\set{0}$ is below $E$, and $E$ is closed under addition.
3. A value $\psi_\mu(\xi)$ with $\mu < \omega$ is below $\Omega_{\mu + 1} < E$.
4. A value $\psi_\omega(\xi)$ has $\xi \in C^n_0(\alpha)$, so $\xi < E$ by the induction hypothesis, and $\psi_\omega(\xi) = \omega^{\Omega_\omega + \xi} < E$.
5. So no argument $\alpha \ge E$ is ever in a closure set, and $C_0(\alpha) = C_0(E)$ for every $\alpha \ge E$. Hence $\psi_0(\alpha) = \psi_0(E)$ for all $\alpha \ge E$.

### Values below it

- $\psi_0(\Omega_\nu)$ for $1 \le \nu < \omega$: the ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_{<\nu}$, the union of the theories $\mathrm{ID}_\mu$ for $\mu < \nu$. In particular $\psi_0(\Omega_2) = \psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega + 1})$ is the [[Bachmann-Howard ordinal|~fcf34cb0]], the ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_1$.
- $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$: the ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ and of [[Π¹₁-comprehension|~9776e851]] $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$. It is smaller than the Takeuti-Feferman-Buchholz ordinal.

The difference between $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA} + \mathrm{BI}$ is the strength of induction: the first theory has induction only for sets, and the second has transfinite induction for all formulas.

### Buchholz hydra

The [[Buchholz hydra|CS9FHV1F3S]] is a game on finite trees whose labels are the indices $\nu \le \omega$ of Buchholz's functions.
Each hydra corresponds to a term of the notation system, and each move of the game lowers the term.
The statement that every hydra game terminates is true, but it is not provable in $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA} + \mathrm{BI}$ (Wikipedia, "Buchholz hydra", lead section).
So the ordinal gives a natural combinatorial statement that the theory cannot prove.

### Name

David Madore named the ordinal after Gaisi Takeuti, Solomon Feferman and Wilfried Buchholz.
Takeuti gave the first analysis of $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension with ordinal diagrams, Feferman introduced the $\theta$-functions, and Buchholz introduced the $\psi$-functions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Proof theorists measure the strength of a theory by a countable ordinal.
For stronger theories they need larger ordinals and better names for them.
Buchholz's functions give names with a ladder of infinities $\Omega_1, \Omega_2, \dots$ and its top $\Omega_\omega$.
The Takeuti-Feferman-Buchholz ordinal is the largest ordinal that these names reach.
It measures a strong part of second-order arithmetic, in which one can form sets defined by one quantifier over all sets.

### L2 human

We place the ordinal in the list of values of $\psi_0$.

1. $\psi_0(\Omega) = \varepsilon_0$, the ordinal of Peano arithmetic.
2. $\psi_0(\Omega_2)$ is the Bachmann-Howard ordinal, the ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_1$.
3. $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$ is the ordinal of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$.
4. The argument $\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1}$ is the end of the system: larger arguments give the same value, by the remark above.
5. $\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1})$ is the Takeuti-Feferman-Buchholz ordinal, the ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_\omega$.

The typical use is as a benchmark: an ordinal analysis of a new theory places its ordinal below, at or above this ordinal.
The trap is to assign this ordinal to $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ or to $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$: their ordinal is the smaller $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$.
