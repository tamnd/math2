## Definition

Let $R$ be a [[commutative ring|~79bc52c4]] and let $n \ge 1$.
A polynomial $f \in R[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ is symmetric if
$$f(x_{\sigma(1)}, \dots, x_{\sigma(n)}) = f(x_1, \dots, x_n)$$
for every permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1, \dots, n\}$.

## Remarks

Examples in two variables: $x_1 + x_2$, $x_1 x_2$ and $x_1^2 + x_2^2$. The polynomial $x_1 - x_2$ is not symmetric.
The symmetric polynomials form a subring of $R[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, the ring of invariants of the symmetric group.
The main examples are the [[elementary symmetric polynomials|~71a32e63]] and the [[power sums|~c5fc6c0d]].
The coefficients of a monic polynomial are symmetric polynomials in its roots, by [[Vieta's formulas|~e76a4223]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A symmetric polynomial treats all its variables in the same way.
Swapping two variables gives the same polynomial.
This is why symmetric expressions in the roots of a polynomial can be computed from the coefficients.
