## Proof

We use the clauses of [[satisfaction|~cf18e0d7]] and [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on terms and formulas.

Part 1, by induction on the term $t$.
Assume $s_1$ and $s_2$ agree on the variables of $t$.

1. If $t$ is a variable $x$, then $x$ occurs in $t$. So $t^{\mathcal{M}}[s_1] = s_1(x) = s_2(x) = t^{\mathcal{M}}[s_2]$.
2. If $t$ is a constant symbol $c$, then both values are $c^{\mathcal{M}}$.
3. Let $t = f(t_1, \ldots, t_n)$. Every variable of $t_i$ occurs in $t$, so $s_1$ and $s_2$ agree on the variables of $t_i$.
4. By the induction hypothesis, $t_i^{\mathcal{M}}[s_1] = t_i^{\mathcal{M}}[s_2]$ for each $i$.
5. Both values of $t$ are $f^{\mathcal{M}}$ applied to the same arguments, so they are equal.

Part 2, by induction on $\varphi$.
The claim for $\varphi$ is: for all assignments $s_1, s_2$ that agree on the free variables of $\varphi$, $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s_1]$ if and only if $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s_2]$.

6. Let $\varphi = R(t_1, \ldots, t_n)$. Every variable of each $t_i$ is free in $\varphi$. By part 1, $t_i^{\mathcal{M}}[s_1] = t_i^{\mathcal{M}}[s_2]$ for each $i$.
7. So the tuple of values is the same under $s_1$ and $s_2$, and it lies in $R^{\mathcal{M}}$ under both or under neither.
8. Let $\varphi$ be $t_1 = t_2$. By part 1, the two values agree under $s_1$ and $s_2$, so the equality holds under both or under neither.
9. Let $\varphi = \neg \psi$. The free variables of $\psi$ and $\varphi$ are the same. By the induction hypothesis, $\psi$ has the same truth value under $s_1$ and $s_2$, so $\neg \psi$ has too.
10. Let $\varphi = \psi \mathbin{\square} \chi$ with $\square \in \set{\wedge, \vee, \to, \leftrightarrow}$. The free variables of $\psi$ and of $\chi$ are free in $\varphi$.
11. By the induction hypothesis, $\psi$ and $\chi$ keep their truth values from $s_1$ to $s_2$. The clause for $\square$ is a function of these two values, so $\varphi$ keeps its value.
12. Let $\varphi = \forall x \, \psi$, and let $a \in M$. We compare the variants $s_1[x \mapsto a]$ and $s_2[x \mapsto a]$.
13. At $x$, both variants take the value $a$.
14. Let $y \neq x$ be free in $\psi$. Then $y$ is free in $\varphi$, so $s_1(y) = s_2(y)$, and both variants take this value at $y$.
15. So the variants agree on the free variables of $\psi$. By the induction hypothesis, $\mathcal{M} \models \psi[s_1[x \mapsto a]]$ if and only if $\mathcal{M} \models \psi[s_2[x \mapsto a]]$.
16. This holds for every $a \in M$. By the clause for $\forall$, $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s_1]$ if and only if $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s_2]$.
17. Let $\varphi = \exists x \, \psi$. Steps 12 to 15 apply without change, and the clause for $\exists$ gives the claim.

Part 3, by induction in the same way.
Let $s$ be any assignment.

18. Each term $t$ of $L$ has the same value in $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{M}'$, because its symbols are in $L$ and get the same interpretations. This is an induction on $t$ as in steps 1 to 5.
19. Atomic formulas of $L$ are satisfied in $\mathcal{M}$ exactly when in $\mathcal{M}'$, by step 18 and the equal interpretations of the relation symbols.
20. The steps for $\neg$ and $\square$ are as in steps 9 to 11.
21. For $\forall x \, \psi$ and $\exists x \, \psi$, the two structures have the same domain. So the variants $s[x \mapsto a]$ range over the same set, and the induction hypothesis gives the claim.

This proves parts 1, 2 and 3 of the coincidence lemma.

## Idea

Satisfaction is defined by recursion on the formula, so we prove the claim by induction along the same recursion.
The only real case is the quantifier: the clause for $\forall x$ resets $x$, so the two variants agree at $x$ as well as on the other free variables.
The claim must be stated for all pairs of assignments at once, because the quantifier step uses it for the variants, not for $s_1$ and $s_2$.

## Variants

For a sentence one can avoid the general lemma by defining satisfaction only for assignments of the free variables, as Mathlib does with tuples indexed by the free variables.
Then part 2 is built into the definition, and the work moves into lemmas about renaming free variables.
