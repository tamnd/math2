## Summary

Take a function $f$, an input value $a$ and a real number $L$.
We say that $f$ has limit $L$ as $x$ approaches $a$, and write $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$, when the outputs $f(x)$ can be kept as near to $L$ as we wish by choosing $x$ close enough to $a$. The input $x$ must differ from $a$.

If the outputs do not settle near one single number as $x$ approaches $a$, then $f$ has no limit at $a$.

This is the intuitive form of the [[limit of a function|0TXTSEMHR5]]. The value of $f$ at $a$ itself does not matter.
