## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
A $2 \times 3$ matrix has no determinant, but it has $\binom{2}{2} \binom{3}{2} = 3$ [[minors|0SD528P0EK]] of order $2$, one for each pair of columns.

1. Columns $\set{1, 2}$: $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 5 \end{pmatrix} = 5 - 8 = -3$.
2. Columns $\set{1, 3}$: $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 6 \end{pmatrix} = 6 - 12 = -6$.
3. Columns $\set{2, 3}$: $\det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix} = 12 - 15 = -3$.
4. A minor of order $2$ is nonzero, so the rank of $A$ is $2$.
5. Check by the [[Cauchy-Binet formula|08WE2W7DYS]]: $A A^{T} = \begin{pmatrix} 14 & 32 \\ 32 & 77 \end{pmatrix}$, with $\det = 1078 - 1024 = 54$.
6. The sum of the squares of the minors is $9 + 36 + 9 = 54$, as Cauchy-Binet predicts.

## Remarks

The minors of order $1$ are the six entries.
The three minors of order $2$ are, up to sign, the coordinates of the cross product $(1, 2, 3) \times (4, 5, 6) = (-3, 6, -3)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A table with $2$ rows and $3$ columns is not square, so it has no determinant.
But each choice of $2$ of its columns gives a square piece, and each piece has a determinant.

### L2 human

The minors of a rectangular matrix measure its rank: the rank is $2$ here, since one minor of order $2$ is nonzero.
The computation of $\det(A A^{T})$ in two ways checks the three minors.
A trap: the minors use the columns in their original order; columns $(3, 1)$ would give $+6$ instead of $-6$.
