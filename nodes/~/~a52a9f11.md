## Statement

Let $V$ be a vector space of finite dimension $n$ over a field $F$, and let $B$ be an [[alternating bilinear form|08ZQAKBKHF]] on $V$.
Let $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. (Symplectic basis.) There are an integer $m \geq 0$ and a basis $(u_1, v_1, \dots, u_m, v_m, w_1, \dots, w_k)$ of $V$, with $2m + k = n$, such that $B(u_i, v_i) = 1$ for each $i$, and $B$ is zero on every other pair of basis vectors taken in the order of the list.
2. In this basis the [[matrix|0P24YW565F]] of $B$ is $J \oplus \dots \oplus J \oplus 0_k$, with $m$ blocks $J$.
3. The rank of $B$ is $2m$; in particular, it is even. The vectors $w_1, \dots, w_k$ are a basis of the radical.
4. Two alternating $n \times n$ matrices over $F$ are [[congruent|NZJDNC6EP6]] if and only if they have the same rank.
5. If $B$ is [[nondegenerate|0KAN330PAH]], then $n$ is even.
6. The determinant of an alternating matrix over $F$ is a square in $F$.

The [[proof|~f2759734]] splits off one hyperbolic plane at a time, by induction on $n$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The form must be alternating, not only skew-symmetric. Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the identity matrix $I_1$ is skew-symmetric, since $-1 = 1$, but it has odd rank $1$.
- Over a commutative ring instead of a field, the statement fails: the form $2 x_1 y_2 - 2 x_2 y_1$ on $\mathbb{Z}^2$ is not congruent to $J$ over $\mathbb{Z}$, since its determinant $4$ is not $(\det P)^2 \cdot 1$ for $\det P = \pm 1$.

### Consequences

- Each [[symplectic vector space|PQ7JJY0VPH]] has even dimension $2m$ and a basis in which the form has the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & I_m \\ -I_m & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
- Two nondegenerate alternating forms on spaces of the same dimension are isometric. So there is only one symplectic vector space of each even dimension, up to isomorphism.
- An alternating matrix of odd size has determinant $0$ in every characteristic, since its rank is even and so smaller than its size.
- The square root of $\det A$ in part 6 is given by a polynomial in the entries, the [[Pfaffian|NH5HWBPWS9]], by [[the theorem on the Pfaffian|473MBZST4D]].

### Converse

The converse of part 5 fails: the zero form on $F^2$ has even dimension and is degenerate.
The converse of part 6 fails: $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has the square determinant $1$, but it is not alternating.
Part 4 is an equivalence.

### Generalizations

Over a principal ideal domain, an alternating matrix is congruent to a block sum of matrices $d_i J$ with $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots$, and zeros; this is a symplectic version of the Smith normal form.
For Hermitian forms over $\C$, the classification uses the rank and the signature instead; for symmetric forms it depends on the field.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Alternating forms have a very simple classification.
In a good basis, every alternating form looks the same: pairs of vectors with value $1$ and $-1$, and some vectors that pair to zero with everything.
So the only invariant is the rank, and it is always even.

### L2 human

We put $B(x, y) = x^T A y$ with $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ on $\Q^2$ in standard form.

1. $B(e_1, e_2) = 2 \neq 0$. Put $u_1 = e_1$ and $v_1 = \frac12 e_2$, so $B(u_1, v_1) = 1$.
2. With $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac12 \end{pmatrix}$, $P^T A P = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = J$.
3. $\det A = 4 = 2^2$, a square, as part 6 says.

The typical use is to compute the rank of an alternating form, or to find coordinates in which a symplectic form is standard.
A trap: the classification ignores the field.
Over $\Q$, $\R$ and $\mathbb{F}_2$ alike, the rank is the only invariant, unlike for symmetric forms.

### L3 human

The theorem is the linear version of the Darboux theorem of symplectic geometry, which gives local coordinates in which a closed nondegenerate $2$-form is standard.
For nondegenerate forms, part 4 says that $\operatorname{GL}_{2m}(F)$ acts transitively on them by $A \mapsto P^T A P$, and the stabilizer of $J \oplus \dots \oplus J$ is the [[symplectic group|X12FS2JAP0]].
The Pfaffian makes part 6 polynomial, and it is a relative invariant: $\operatorname{pf}(P^T A P) = \det P \cdot \operatorname{pf}(A)$.
