## Proof

We prove $1.1 \Rightarrow 1.3 \Rightarrow 1.2 \Rightarrow 1.1$, then parts 2 and 3.

1. Assume that $T$ is diagonalizable, with $T(v_j) = \mu_j v_j$ for a basis $(v_1, \dots, v_n)$.
2. Let $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k$ be the distinct values among the $\mu_j$, and let $f = \prod_{i=1}^k (t - \lambda_i)$.
3. The factors $T - \lambda_i \id_V$ commute, since they are [[polynomials in $T$|0BNEXSKNRV]]. So $f(T) v_j$ has the factor $T - \mu_j \id_V$ last.
4. That factor sends $v_j$ to $0$. So $f(T) v_j = 0$ for every $j$, and $f(T) = 0$.
5. By [[divisibility by the minimal polynomial|0VTAD25A3G]], $m_T$ divides $f$.
6. Each $\lambda_i$ is an eigenvalue, so $m_T(\lambda_i) = 0$ by [[the roots of the minimal polynomial|0QF4ZEFG52]].
7. By the [[factor theorem|0K3MWA7DMC]], $m_T = (t - \lambda_1) h_1$. Since $\lambda_2 \ne \lambda_1$, $h_1(\lambda_2) = 0$.
8. Repeating step 7 for $\lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_k$ shows that $f$ divides $m_T$.
9. Both $f$ and $m_T$ are monic and divide each other. So $m_T = f$, which is condition 1.3.
10. Condition 1.3 implies condition 1.2 with $f = m_T$, since $m_T(T) = 0$.
11. Assume condition 1.2, with $f = \prod_{j=1}^r (t - a_j)$ and distinct $a_j$.
12. For $j \ne l$, $(t - a_j) - (t - a_l) = a_l - a_j$ is a nonzero constant. So the factors are pairwise coprime.
13. By the [[kernels lemma|~4b2aa8fd]], $\ker f(T) = \ker(T - a_1 \id_V) \oplus \cdots \oplus \ker(T - a_r \id_V)$.
14. Since $f(T) = 0$, the left side is $V$. Each nonzero summand is the [[eigenspace|0AYEPBQMAW]] of an eigenvalue $a_j$.
15. Drop the zero summands. Then $V$ is the direct sum of eigenspaces of distinct eigenvalues.
16. Each eigenvalue $\mu$ of $T$ appears among the $a_j$. Indeed, an [[eigenvector|0B5H4208Z9]] $v$ gives $0 = f(T) v = f(\mu) v$, so $f(\mu) = 0$.
17. So $V$ is the direct sum of all eigenspaces of $T$. By condition 3 of the [[characterization by eigenvectors|056SW1E0AC]], $T$ is diagonalizable.
18. For part 2, step 9 shows that the roots of $m_T$ are the distinct values $\mu_j$, which are exactly the eigenvalues.
19. For part 3, let $\pi_1, \dots, \pi_k$ be the projections for the decomposition of step 13 with $f = m_T$.
20. By the kernels lemma, each $\pi_i = g_i(T)$ for a polynomial $g_i$.
21. Let $u \in U$. Since $U$ is $T$-invariant, $g_i(T) u \in U$. So $\pi_i(u) \in U \cap E_{\lambda_i}$.
22. Since $u = \sum_i \pi_i(u)$, $U$ is the sum of the $U \cap E_{\lambda_i}$. The sum is direct, since the sum of the $E_{\lambda_i}$ is direct.
23. A basis of each nonzero $U \cap E_{\lambda_i}$ consists of eigenvectors. Together they form a basis of $U$ of eigenvectors of $T|_U$.
24. So $T|_U$ is diagonalizable. For a matrix $A$, apply steps 1 to 23 to $x \mapsto A x$, which has $m_A$ as minimal polynomial.

## Idea

A diagonal matrix is killed by the product of $t - \mu$ over its distinct diagonal entries.
Conversely, distinct linear factors are pairwise coprime.
The kernels lemma then splits $V$ into the kernels of $T - a_j$, which are eigenspaces.
Its projections are polynomials in $T$, so they preserve every invariant subspace.

## Variants

The [[proof by Lagrange interpolation|E2XSXEVC2Y]] writes the projections explicitly as $\pi_i = \prod_{j \ne i} \frac{T - \lambda_j}{\lambda_i - \lambda_j}$.
This is the kernels lemma with the Bézout coefficients computed by interpolation.
Part 3 also follows from the criterion: $m_T(T|_U) = 0$, so $m_{T|_U}$ divides $m_T$ and has simple roots in $F$.
