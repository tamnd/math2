## Proof

We check that the factors $p_i^{e_i}$ are pairwise coprime, apply the kernels lemma, and then compare minimal polynomials.

1. Let $i \ne j$, and let $d$ be a monic common divisor of $p_i$ and $p_j$. Since $p_i$ is [[irreducible|00X2A9G9W3]], $d = 1$ or $d = p_i$.
2. If $d = p_i$, then $p_i$ divides $p_j$. Since $p_j$ is irreducible and both are monic, $p_i = p_j$, which is false. So $p_i$ and $p_j$ are coprime.
3. By [[Bézout's identity|0VY3WPP9Y3]], $a p_i + b p_j = 1$ for some $a, b \in F[t]$.
4. Raise this identity to the power $e_i + e_j - 1$ and expand by the binomial theorem in $F[t]$.
5. Each term $\binom{e_i + e_j - 1}{l} (a p_i)^l (b p_j)^{e_i + e_j - 1 - l}$ has $l \ge e_i$ or $e_i + e_j - 1 - l \ge e_j$.
6. So each term is a multiple of $p_i^{e_i}$ or of $p_j^{e_j}$. Collecting terms gives $a' p_i^{e_i} + b' p_j^{e_j} = 1$, so the powers are coprime.
7. By the definition of the [[minimal polynomial|0V58FPM3EQ]], $m_T(T) = 0$, and $m_T = p_1^{e_1} \cdots p_k^{e_k}$.
8. By parts 3 and 4 of the [[kernels lemma|~4b2aa8fd]], $V = W_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus W_k$, and the projections are polynomials in $T$. This proves parts 1 and 4.
9. Each $W_i$ is $T$-invariant, by the kernels lemma. Let $T_i = T|_{W_i}$, with minimal polynomial $m_i$.
10. $p_i^{e_i}(T_i) = 0$ by the definition of $W_i$. So $m_i$ divides $p_i^{e_i}$, by [[divisibility by the minimal polynomial|0VTAD25A3G]].
11. $F[t]$ is a [[unique factorization domain|06YMXJMXYS]], since it is a [[Euclidean domain|0DQG39K2NH]]. So the monic divisors of $p_i^{e_i}$ are the $p_i^{f}$ with $0 \le f \le e_i$, and $m_i = p_i^{f_i}$ with $f_i \le e_i$.
12. Let $g = p_1^{f_1} \cdots p_k^{f_k}$. For $w \in W_i$, $g(T) w$ has the factor $p_i^{f_i}(T)$ applied first, since polynomials in $T$ commute. Since $m_i(T_i) = 0$, $g(T) w = 0$.
13. Every $v \in V$ is a sum of vectors of the $W_i$, by step 8. So $g(T) = 0$, and $m_T$ divides $g$, again by divisibility by the minimal polynomial.
14. By unique factorization, the exponent $e_i$ of $p_i$ in $m_T$ is at most $f_i$. With step 11, $f_i = e_i$, so $m_i = p_i^{e_i}$. This proves part 3.
15. If $W_i = 0$, then the constant $1$ kills $T_i$, so $m_i = 1$ and $f_i = 0$. This contradicts $f_i = e_i \ge 1$. So $W_i \ne 0$, which proves part 2.

## Idea

Distinct irreducible polynomials are coprime, and so are their powers.
The kernels lemma then splits $V$ along the factorization of $m_T$, with projections that are polynomials in $T$.
The minimal polynomial of each piece divides $p_i^{e_i}$, and the product of these minimal polynomials kills $T$, so no exponent can drop.

## Variants

Steps 3 to 6 can be replaced by the fact that in a unique factorization domain, coprimality depends only on the irreducible factors.
One can also prove part 1 with the polynomials $q_i = m_T / p_i^{e_i}$: they have no common factor, so $\sum_i c_i q_i = 1$ for some $c_i$, and $\pi_i = (c_i q_i)(T)$ are the projections.
This is the proof in Hoffman and Kunze, Section 6.8.
