## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, a real matrix acting on $\mathbb{R}^2$ with the dot product.
We find an [[orthogonal matrix|0QRBVFJ50X]] $Q$ with $Q^T A Q$ [[upper triangular|0N4XY7Z3NN]].

1. The characteristic polynomial is $(1 - t)(5 - t) + 4 = t^2 - 6t + 9 = (t - 3)^2$. So $3$ is the only eigenvalue, with algebraic multiplicity $2$.
2. The matrix $A - 3I = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 2 \\ -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $1$, and its kernel is the line spanned by $(1, 1)$.
3. Take the unit eigenvector $q_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, 1)$, and the unit vector $q_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-1, 1)$ orthogonal to it.
4. Let $Q = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, with the columns $q_1$ and $q_2$. Its columns are orthonormal, so $Q^T Q = I$.
5. We compute $A q_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, 7)$. Then $q_1^T A q_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + 7) = 4$ and $q_2^T A q_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(-1 + 7) = 3$.
6. Also $A q_1 = 3 q_1$, so $q_1^T A q_1 = 3$ and $q_2^T A q_1 = 0$. Hence
$$Q^T A Q = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

## Remarks

- The diagonal holds the eigenvalue $3$ twice, as the Schur decomposition says.
- The entry $4$ cannot be removed by any orthogonal or unitary change of basis. The sum of the squares of all entries is the same for $A$ and for every Schur form: $1 + 4 + 4 + 25 = 34 = 9 + 9 + 16$. With the diagonal fixed at $3$, $3$, the entry above the diagonal has absolute value $4$.
- So $A$ is not a [[normal matrix|0XYKE8BE7X]]. A direct check: $A A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 8 \\ 8 & 29 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A^T A = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -8 \\ -8 & 29 \end{pmatrix}$.
- With $q_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, -1)$ in place of $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-1, 1)$, the Schur form becomes $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -4 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$. So the Schur form is not unique.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This matrix has only one stretch factor, $3$, and only one special direction, the direction of $(1, 1)$.
So it cannot be made diagonal.
In a rotated pair of perpendicular axes, its table of numbers becomes triangular, with $3$ on the diagonal.
The number $4$ in the corner shows that the matrix also shears space.

### L2 human

The recipe for a $2 \times 2$ real matrix with real eigenvalues:

1. Find one eigenvalue and a unit eigenvector $q_1$.
2. Rotate $q_1$ by a right angle to get $q_2$.
3. Compute the four numbers $q_i^T A q_j$. The entry $q_2^T A q_1$ is $0$, because $A q_1$ is a multiple of $q_1$.

A trap: the Schur form is triangular, not diagonal, and it exists also for matrices that cannot be diagonalized.
