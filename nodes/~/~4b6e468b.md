## Example

Let $F$ be a field, viewed as a vector space over itself of dimension $1$.
Consider the [[algebra of endomorphisms|0E3X2G1ZQB]] $\End(F)$.
For $a \in F$, let $m_a \colon F \to F$ be multiplication by $a$, $m_a(x) = ax$.

1. Each $m_a$ is [[linear|0RJF668P1T]]: $m_a(x + y) = ax + ay$ and $m_a(cx) = c\,(ax)$, since $F$ is commutative.
2. Each linear $T \colon F \to F$ equals $m_a$ with $a = T(1)$. Indeed $T(x) = T(x \cdot 1) = x\,T(1) = ax$.
3. The scalar $a$ is unique, since $m_a(1) = a$. So $a \mapsto m_a$ is a bijection $F \to \End(F)$.
4. Sums: $m_a + m_b = m_{a+b}$. Multiples: $c\,m_a = m_{ca}$.
5. Products: $(m_a m_b)(x) = a(bx) = (ab)x$, so $m_a m_b = m_{ab}$. Also $m_1 = \id_F$.
6. So $a \mapsto m_a$ is an algebra isomorphism $F \cong \End(F)$, and $\End(F)$ is commutative.
7. Numerical check over $\R$: $m_2 m_3 = m_6$, and both sides send $5$ to $30$.

## Remarks

- Step 2 is the [[theorem on bases|0R8X9XA83A]] for the basis $(1)$ of $F$.
- In matrix terms, $\End(F) \cong M_1(F) = F$.
- The automorphisms are the maps $m_a$ with $a \ne 0$, so $GL(F) \cong F^\times$.
- The same holds for every one-dimensional space $V$: $\End(V) \cong F$, with $a \mapsto a \id_V$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A linear map from the number line to itself is multiplication by a fixed number.
Adding or composing such maps adds or multiplies the numbers.
So the endomorphisms of a line behave exactly like the numbers.

### L2 human

The example is the case $n = 1$ of $\End(V) \cong M_n(F)$.
It is the only case in which $\End(V)$ is commutative, apart from $V = \{0\}$.
The trap is to include nonlinear maps such as $x \mapsto x^2$; they are not endomorphisms of the vector space $F$.
