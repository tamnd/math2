## Statement

Let $T$ be a [[theory|92SWXST1ZM]] with infinite models in a signature $L$.
By [[Skolemization|NKQY9NB955]], $T$ extends to a Skolem theory $T^*$ in a signature $L^* \supseteq L$ with $\abs{L^*} = \abs{L} + \aleph_0$.
Let $\Phi$ be the Ehrenfeucht-Mostowski type of an infinite [[indiscernible sequence|~cc1977c7]] in some model of $T^*$; one exists by the [[existence of indiscernibles|~c0b513d4]].
Let $I$ and $J$ be infinite linear orders.

1. There is a model $\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)$ of $T^*$ that is the Skolem hull of an indiscernible sequence $(a_i)_{i \in I}$ with type $\Phi$. It is unique up to an isomorphism that fixes each $a_i$.
2. Every [[order embedding|AZES1C6VMT]] $f \colon I \to J$ extends to a unique [[elementary embedding|CGH8C8EA9N]] $\hat{f} \colon \operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi) \to \operatorname{EM}(J, \Phi)$ with $\hat{f}(a_i) = a_{f(i)}$.
3. The map $f \mapsto \hat{f}$ is an injective group homomorphism from $\operatorname{Aut}(I, <)$ to the [[automorphism group|0K1SCPBNKJ]] of $\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)$.
4. $\abs{I} \le \abs{\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)} \le \abs{I} + \abs{L} + \aleph_0$.
5. If $L$ is countable and $I$ is a [[well-order|01CGSJ0GNQ]], then $\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)$ realizes only countably many [[types|9V71Q3SD1G]] over each countable subset.

Parts 2 to 5 also hold for the [[reduct|EY20BF96SJ]] of $\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)$ to $L$, which is a model of $T$.
The proof is in [[the proof for Ehrenfeucht-Mostowski models|~76e49ab2]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The Skolem theory: in the theory of dense linear orders, the set of an increasing sequence $(a_n)_{n \in \N}$ is a substructure, since $\set{<}$ has no function symbols. It is a copy of $(\N, <)$, which is not a dense order.
- Infinite models: a theory whose models are finite has no infinite indiscernible sequence, so $\Phi$ does not exist.
- The well-order in part 5: for an increasing sequence $\Phi$ in a dense linear order, the generators $a_r$ with $r \in \R$ realize $2^{\aleph_0}$ types over $\set{a_q : q \in \Q}$. Two reals $r < r'$ are separated by the formula $a_q < x$ for a rational $q$ between them.
- Countable $L$ in part 5: with constants $c_r$ for $r \in \R$ and axioms $c_r < c_s$ for $r < s$, every model realizes $2^{\aleph_0}$ types over $\emptyset$, through the formulas $x = c_r$.

### Consequences

- For every $\kappa \ge \abs{L} + \aleph_0$, $T$ has a model of size $\kappa$ with $2^\kappa$ automorphisms. Take $I = \kappa \times \Q$ in the lexicographic order; then $\operatorname{Aut}(I)$ has $2^\kappa$ elements, from independent automorphisms of the copies of $\Q$.
- A countable theory with infinite models has, in each infinite cardinal $\kappa$, a model that realizes only countably many types over each countable set. This is a step in [[Morley's categoricity theorem|V7N84P60SJ]].
- The size bound in part 4 gives $\abs{\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)} = \abs{I}$ when $\abs{I} \ge \abs{L} + \aleph_0$.

### Converse

Part 2 has a converse: an $L^*$-embedding $g$ with $g(a_i) = a_{f(i)}$ for an order embedding $f$ equals $\hat{f}$.
The reason is that $\operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)$ is generated by the $a_i$, and an embedding is fixed by its values on generators.
The map in part 3 is not onto in general: if $\Phi$ is the type of an indiscernible set, then every permutation of $I$ gives an automorphism.

### Generalizations

The same construction works for [[generalized indiscernibles|~9c5e0903]] indexed by other structures, such as trees, when such indiscernibles exist with the required type.
Shelah used Ehrenfeucht-Mostowski models over trees and other index structures in his work on the number of nonisomorphic models.
The construction with Morley's form of the existence theorem gives models that omit types, which bounds the [[Hanf number|~506bb18e]].

## History

Ehrenfeucht and Mostowski introduced these models in 1956, to show that a theory with infinite models has models with many automorphisms.
Morley used Ehrenfeucht-Mostowski models over well-orders in his proof of the categoricity theorem, in 1965.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start with a list of elements that all look alike, such as the numbers $1, 2, 3, \dots$ inside the rational numbers.
Build a model from them by adding the elements that the rules of the theory force to exist.
Now replace the list by any other list, longer or shorter, with any order.
The same rules build a model again, and every way to shift the list, such as $n \mapsto n + 1$ on the integers, gives a symmetry of that model.

### L2 human

We compute the size bound of part 4 for $I = \omega_1$ and a countable $L$.

1. Each element of $\operatorname{EM}(\omega_1, \Phi)$ is a value $t(a_{i_1}, \dots, a_{i_n})$ of an $L^*$-term $t$ at generators, because the model is a Skolem hull.
2. The signature $L^*$ is countable, so there are countably many terms.
3. There are $\aleph_1$ finite tuples of indices from $\omega_1$.
4. So the model has at most $\aleph_0 \cdot \aleph_1 = \aleph_1$ elements, and at least $\aleph_1$ because the generators are distinct.

The typical use is to build a model of a given size with a property inherited from the index order, such as many automorphisms or few types.
The trap is to forget the Skolem functions: without them the generators span a substructure that need not be a model.

### L3 human

The map $I \mapsto \operatorname{EM}(I, \Phi)$ is a functor from linear orders with order embeddings to models of $T^*$ with elementary embeddings.
It preserves directed unions, and it makes the model reflect the combinatorics of the index order.
Well-orders give few types, and orders with many automorphisms give models with many automorphisms.
The same idea, with other index structures, is the source of the many-models theorems of classification theory.
Ehrenfeucht-Mostowski models are used in the [[two-cardinal theorems|~cdfd261d]] and in the computation of the [[Hanf number|~506bb18e]] of infinitary logic.
