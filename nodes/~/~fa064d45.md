## Proof

Parts A and C, and the proof of part 2 in part D, are complete.
Part B cites the theorem of Mostowski on weak direct powers without proof.

Part A: the positive integers under multiplication.

1. Let $P$ be the set of prime numbers. Let $W$ be the set of families $e = (e_p)_{p \in P}$ of natural numbers with $e_p = 0$ for all but finitely many $p$.
2. With pointwise addition, $(W, +)$ is the weak direct power $(\N, +)^{(P)}$ of the structure $(\N, +)$.
3. Define $h : W \to \N_{> 0}$ by $h(e) = \prod_{p \in P} p^{e_p}$. The product is finite, because almost all factors are $p^0 = 1$.
4. The map $h$ is a bijection, by the [[fundamental theorem of arithmetic|084HH1NDCB]]. The number $1$ corresponds to the family of zeros.
5. For $e, e' \in W$, $h(e + e') = h(e) \cdot h(e')$, because $p^{a} \cdot p^{b} = p^{a + b}$ for each prime $p$.
6. So $h$ is an [[isomorphism|PZMGQMDN25]] from $(W, +)$ to $(\N_{> 0}, \cdot)$. Isomorphic structures satisfy the same sentences.

Part B: the theory of the weak power (cited).

7. Theorem of Mostowski (1952), cited: if a structure $\mathcal{A}$ has a decidable theory, then the weak direct power $\mathcal{A}^{(\omega)}$ has a decidable theory.
8. The proof of step 7 computes, from a sentence about $\mathcal{A}^{(\omega)}$, finitely many sentences about $\mathcal{A}$ and a rule that combines their truth values. This is the Feferman-Vaught method.
9. The theory of $(\N, +)$ is decidable by [[Presburger arithmetic|~14d8268c]]: a sentence in $+$ alone is a sentence in $\set{0, 1, +}$.
10. The set $P$ is countably infinite, so $(W, +)$ is isomorphic to $(\N, +)^{(\omega)}$. By steps 6, 7 and 9, $\operatorname{Th}(\N_{> 0}, \cdot)$ is decidable.

Part C: adding the element $0$.

11. Every formula in $\set{\cdot}$ is equivalent to one whose atoms have the forms $x = y$ and $x \cdot y = z$ for variables $x, y, z$.
12. Step 11 holds by unnesting: replace a subterm $s \cdot t$ by a new variable $w$ with $\exists w \, (s \cdot t = w \wedge \ldots)$, and repeat.
13. Let $\top$ be $\forall x \, (x = x)$ and $\bot$ be $\neg \top$. Let $\alpha(x_1, \ldots, x_k)$ be unnested, and let $Z \subseteq \set{1, \ldots, k}$.
14. We define a formula $\alpha_Z$ whose free variables are the $x_i$ with $i \notin Z$. The set $Z$ marks the variables with value $0$.
15. For $\alpha$ of the form $x_i = x_j$: $\alpha_Z$ is $\top$ if $i, j \in Z$, $\bot$ if exactly one is in $Z$, and $\alpha$ otherwise.
16. For $\alpha$ of the form $x_i \cdot x_j = x_l$ with $i \in Z$ or $j \in Z$: $\alpha_Z$ is $\top$ if $l \in Z$ and $\bot$ otherwise.
17. For $\alpha$ of the form $x_i \cdot x_j = x_l$ with $i, j \notin Z$: $\alpha_Z$ is $\bot$ if $l \in Z$ and $\alpha$ otherwise.
18. We set $(\neg \alpha)_Z = \neg \alpha_Z$ and $(\alpha \wedge \beta)_Z = \alpha_Z \wedge \beta_Z$.
19. For a new variable $x_{k + 1}$, we set $(\exists x_{k + 1} \, \alpha)_Z = \alpha_{Z \cup \set{k + 1}} \vee \exists x_{k + 1} \, \alpha_Z$.
20. Claim: let $a_1, \ldots, a_k \in \N$, and let $Z = \set{i : a_i = 0}$. Then $(\N, \cdot) \models \alpha(\vec{a})$ exactly when $(\N_{> 0}, \cdot) \models \alpha_Z(\vec{a})$.
21. We prove the claim by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $\alpha$. Here $\alpha_Z(\vec{a})$ uses only the $a_i$ with $i \notin Z$.
22. Equality: $a_i = a_j$ holds when both are $0$, fails when exactly one is $0$, and otherwise is checked in $\N_{> 0}$.
23. Product with a zero factor: $a_i \cdot a_j = 0$, so the atom holds exactly when $a_l = 0$, that is $l \in Z$.
24. Product of positive factors: $a_i \cdot a_j > 0$, so the atom fails when $a_l = 0$, and otherwise it is checked in $\N_{> 0}$.
25. The cases $\neg$ and $\wedge$ follow from the induction hypothesis.
26. The case $\exists$: a witness $b \in \N$ is either $0$, which is the first disjunct, or positive, which is the second disjunct.
27. For a sentence $\sigma$, step 20 with $k = 0$ gives $(\N, \cdot) \models \sigma$ exactly when $(\N_{> 0}, \cdot) \models \sigma_{\emptyset}$.
28. The map $\sigma \mapsto \sigma_{\emptyset}$ is computable, and step 10 decides $\sigma_{\emptyset}$. This proves part 1.

Part D: successor and order.

29. Let $a, b, c \in \N$ with $c \ne 0$. We show that $a + b = c$ exactly when $(a c + 1)(b c + 1) = c^2 (a b + 1) + 1$.
30. The left side expands to $a b c^2 + a c + b c + 1$. The right side expands to $a b c^2 + c^2 + 1$.
31. So the equation holds exactly when $c (a + b) = c \cdot c$. Since $c \ne 0$, this holds exactly when $a + b = c$.
32. For $c = 0$: $a + b = 0$ holds exactly when $a = 0$ and $b = 0$.
33. Let $\zeta(z)$ be $\forall w \, (z \cdot w = z)$. In $\N$, $\zeta(z)$ holds only for $z = 0$, since $z \cdot 1 = z$ and $z \cdot 2 = z$ force $z = 0$.
34. Let $\pi(x, y, z)$ be $(\zeta(z) \wedge \zeta(x) \wedge \zeta(y)) \vee (\neg \zeta(z) \wedge S(x \cdot z) \cdot S(y \cdot z) = S((z \cdot z) \cdot S(x \cdot y)))$.
35. By steps 29 to 33, $(\N, S, \cdot) \models \pi(a, b, c)$ exactly when $a + b = c$.
36. Let $\sigma$ be a sentence in $\set{0, S, +, \cdot}$. Unnest $\sigma$, replace each atom $x + y = z$ by $\pi(x, y, z)$, and replace $0$ by a variable $u$ with $\zeta(u)$.
37. The result $\sigma^{*}$ is computed from $\sigma$, and $(\N, 0, S, +, \cdot) \models \sigma$ exactly when $(\N, S, \cdot) \models \sigma^{*}$.
38. The theory [[true arithmetic|~0d1ea341]] $\operatorname{Th}(\N, 0, S, +, \cdot)$ is undecidable, because it is a consistent extension of the [[essentially undecidable|~c0b3bb9c]] theory $\mathsf{Q}$.
39. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\operatorname{Th}(\N, S, \cdot)$ is decidable. Then step 37 decides true arithmetic, against step 38.
40. So $\operatorname{Th}(\N, S, \cdot)$ is undecidable.
41. In $(\N, <)$, $S(x) = y$ holds exactly when $x < y$ and $\neg \exists z \, (x < z \wedge z < y)$. Call this formula $\beta(x, y)$.
42. Replacing each unnested atom $S(x) = y$ by $\beta(x, y)$ reduces $\operatorname{Th}(\N, S, \cdot)$ to $\operatorname{Th}(\N, <, \cdot)$ in the same way.
43. So $\operatorname{Th}(\N, <, \cdot)$ is undecidable, and part 2 is proved.

## Idea

Prime factorization turns the positive integers under multiplication into finitely supported sequences of natural numbers under addition.
The theory of such a weak power reduces to the theory of $(\N, +)$, which is Presburger arithmetic, and the number $0$ is handled by a case split.
For part 2, a polynomial identity of Julia Robinson defines addition from successor and multiplication, and then true arithmetic reduces to the richer theory.

## Variants

- The Feferman-Vaught theorem gives a uniform version of step 8 for all generalized products, including full direct products.
- For part 2 with $<$, the route through the successor in steps 41 and 42 avoids a separate definition of addition from $<$ and $\cdot$.
