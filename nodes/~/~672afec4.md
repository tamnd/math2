## Example

Use the predimension $\delta(A) = \lvert A \rvert - \lvert E(A) \rvert$ on finite [[graphs|00C0BR7D7H]].
Let $B$ be the path $a - b - c$, with edges $ab$ and $bc$.
We decide which subsets are self-sufficient in $B$, in the sense of [[Hrushovski amalgamation|~b9f4e93f]].

1. The values are $\delta(\set{x}) = 1$ for each vertex, $\delta(\set{a, b}) = \delta(\set{b, c}) = 1$, $\delta(\set{a, c}) = 2$, and $\delta(B) = 1$.
2. $\set{a} \le B$: the supersets of $\set{a}$ in $B$ have $\delta$ equal to $1$, $2$ and $1$, all at least $\delta(\set{a}) = 1$.
3. $\set{a, c} \not\le B$: its superset $B$ has $\delta(B) = 1 < 2$.
4. $\emptyset \le B$, since every subset has $\delta \ge 0 = \delta(\emptyset)$. So $B \in \mathcal{K}_0$.

## Remarks

The vertex $b$ joins $a$ and $c$ with two edges while adding only one point, so it lowers the predimension of $\set{a, c}$.
The self-sufficient closure of $\set{a, c}$ in $B$ is $B$ itself: the smallest self-sufficient set containing it.
In the generic structure, $\set{a, c}$ has dimension $1$, although it has two points.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The two ends of the path look independent on their own.
But the middle vertex ties them together, and once it is counted the pair has less freedom than it seemed.
So the pair alone is not self-sufficient.

### L2 human

We find the self-sufficient closure of $\set{a, c}$.

1. Among supersets of $\set{a, c}$ in $B$, the smallest value of $\delta$ is $1$, at $B$.
2. The smallest set achieving it is $B$.
3. So the closure is $B$.

The typical use is to compute closures by hand in small cases.
A trap is to compare only with the whole of $B$; all intermediate sets must be checked.
