## Proof

1. Let $u_1, u_2 \in U$. By the [[definition of the composite|05YBKMRVAR]], $(T \circ S)(u_1 + u_2) = T(S(u_1 + u_2))$.
2. $S$ is additive, so $S(u_1 + u_2) = S(u_1) + S(u_2)$.
3. $T$ is additive, so $T(S(u_1) + S(u_2)) = T(S(u_1)) + T(S(u_2))$.
4. Steps 1 to 3 give $(T \circ S)(u_1 + u_2) = (T \circ S)(u_1) + (T \circ S)(u_2)$.
5. Let $a \in F$ and $u \in U$. Homogeneity of $S$ and then of $T$ gives $T(S(au)) = T(a\,S(u)) = a\,T(S(u))$.
6. Steps 4 and 5 are the two conditions of a [[linear map|0RJF668P1T]]. So $T \circ S$ is linear, which is part 1.
7. For part 2, let $x \in F^p$. Then $(T_A \circ T_B)(x) = A(Bx)$.
8. Let $b_1, \dots, b_p$ be the columns of $B$. By the [[matrix–vector product|0CCQV3884B]], $Bx = x_1 b_1 + \dots + x_p b_p$.
9. $T_A$ is linear, so $A(Bx) = x_1 A b_1 + \dots + x_p A b_p$.
10. By the [[definition of the matrix product|076JGP6R3T]], the $k$-th column of $AB$ is $A b_k$.
11. So the right side of step 9 is $(AB)x$, by step 8 applied to $AB$. Thus $T_A \circ T_B = T_{AB}$.

## Idea

Each map passes sums and scalars through itself, so two maps in a row also pass them through.
For matrices, the columns of $AB$ are the images under $A$ of the columns of $B$, which is the composite in coordinates.

## Variants

- Part 1 also follows in one line from the single condition: $T(S(au + v)) = T(aS(u) + S(v)) = aT(S(u)) + T(S(v))$.
- Part 2 can be proved entry by entry: the $i$-th entry of $A(Bx)$ is $\sum_j a_{ij} \sum_k b_{jk} x_k = \sum_k \left(\sum_j a_{ij} b_{jk}\right) x_k$.
