## Example

Let $L = \set{P, Q, R}$ with $P$ and $Q$ unary and $R$ binary.
Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the class of finite [[bipartite graphs|0ZVQKTRKVQ]] with a given bipartition: $P$ and $Q$ partition the vertices, and $R \subseteq P \times Q$.
The class $\mathcal{K}$ has free amalgamation, so it has a [[Fraïssé limit|HJVRBDQSGG]] $M$, the generic bipartite graph.
Let $T = \operatorname{Th}(M)$.
We show that the $\emptyset$-definable set $P$ is not [[stably embedded|~2e4defde]].

1. Extension property. For finite disjoint $A, B \subseteq Q$ and finite $F \subseteq P$, there is $p \in P \setminus F$ adjacent to every vertex of $A$ and to no vertex of $B$. This holds because $M$ is ultrahomogeneous and the one-point extension of $F \cup A \cup B$ is in $\mathcal{K}$.
2. Fix $q \in Q$ and let $X = \set{p \in P : R(p, q)}$. By step 1 with $A = \set{q}$, $X$ has a point outside each finite $F$, so $X$ is infinite.
3. By step 1 with $B = \set{q}$, the set $P \setminus X$ is infinite as well.
4. Quantifier elimination. $M$ is ultrahomogeneous, so $T$ has [[quantifier elimination|7FDDXV3JK7]], by [[this theorem|ZRWJZK78MD]].
5. Traces over $P$. Let $\psi(x, \bar c)$ be quantifier-free with $\bar c$ in $P$. For $x \in P$, the atomic formulas $R(x, c_i)$, $R(c_i, x)$, $R(x, x)$ and $Q(x)$ are false, and $P(x)$ is true. Atoms without $x$ have fixed truth values.
6. So on $P$, the formula $\psi(x, \bar c)$ is a Boolean combination of formulas $x = c_i$, and it defines a finite or cofinite subset of $P$.
7. By steps 2 and 3, $X$ is neither finite nor cofinite. So $X$ is not definable with parameters from $P$, and $P$ is not stably embedded.

## Remarks

The extension property and quantifier elimination are first-order properties of $T$, so the argument also works in the monster model of $T$.

The theory $T$ is not stable, since $R$ has the independence property between $P$ and $Q$.
So the example agrees with the fact that all definable sets are stably embedded in stable theories.
The induced structure on $P$ is a pure infinite set, and the parameter $q$ from outside cuts out a new subset.

If $R$ is replaced by the complete bipartite relation $P \times Q$, every trace on $P$ is $\emptyset$ or $P$, and $P$ is stably embedded.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take two infinite teams, the reds and the blues, and draw lines between some reds and some blues, as randomly as possible.
The reds by themselves have no structure: no two reds are joined.
But a single blue person picks out an infinite group of reds, the reds joined to that person.
Using only reds as reference points, one can only pick out finitely many reds, or all but finitely many.
So the reds are not stably embedded.

### L2 human

We check step 6 on one formula with parameters $c_1, c_2 \in P$.

1. Let $\psi(x, c_1, c_2)$ be $(x = c_1 \vee R(x, c_2)) \wedge \neg Q(x)$.
2. For $x \in P$, the atom $R(x, c_2)$ is false, since $c_2 \in P$ and $R \subseteq P \times Q$.
3. The atom $Q(x)$ is false, so $\neg Q(x)$ is true.
4. So on $P$, $\psi$ becomes $x = c_1$, and it defines the finite set $\set{c_1}$.

The typical use is as the standard test case of a definable set that is not stably embedded.
A trap is to use parameters from $Q$ in step 5: such parameters are exactly the outside parameters that cut out new sets.
