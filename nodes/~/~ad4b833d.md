## Proof

By the full form of the [[singular value decomposition|000NPTMX6J]], extend $u_1, \dots, u_r$ to an orthonormal basis $u_1, \dots, u_m$ of $\C^m$, and $v_1, \dots, v_r$ to an orthonormal basis $v_1, \dots, v_n$ of $\C^n$.
Then $A v_j = \sigma_j u_j$ for $j \le r$ and $A v_j = 0$ for $j > r$, since $v_i^* v_j = 0$ for $i \ne j$.

*Rank of $A_k$.*

1. In the same way $A_k v_j = \sigma_j u_j$ for $j \le k$ and $A_k v_j = 0$ for $j > k$. So the column space of $A_k$ is spanned by $u_1, \dots, u_k$, which are independent. So $\operatorname{rank} A_k = k$.

*A lemma on the Frobenius norm.*

2. Let $M$ be an $m \times n$ matrix and let $\rho_i = M^* e_i \in \C^n$, so the $i$th row of $M$ is $\rho_i^*$. By the [[definition of the Frobenius norm|K33BQX70N0]], $\norm{M}_F^2 = \sum_i \norm{\rho_i}^2$.
3. For each $j$, the $i$th entry of $M v_j$ is $\rho_i^* v_j$. So $\sum_j \norm{M v_j}^2 = \sum_i \sum_j \abs{\rho_i^* v_j}^2$.
4. By [[coordinates in an orthonormal basis|09DZ8CD1AK]] and the [[Pythagorean theorem|0BVTDM872V]], $\sum_j \abs{v_j^* \rho_i}^2 = \norm{\rho_i}^2$. With steps 2 and 3, $\norm{M}_F^2 = \sum_j \norm{M v_j}^2$ for every orthonormal basis $v_1, \dots, v_n$.
5. The entries of $M^*$ have the same absolute values as those of $M$, so $\norm{M^*}_F = \norm{M}_F$.

*Part 1, the operator norm.*

6. Let $x = \sum_j c_j v_j$. Then $(A - A_k) x = \sum_{j = k+1}^r \sigma_j c_j u_j$, by the formulas for $A v_j$ and $A_k v_j$.
7. By the Pythagorean theorem, $\norm{(A - A_k) x}^2 = \sum_{j = k+1}^r \sigma_j^2 \abs{c_j}^2 \le \sigma_{k+1}^2 \sum_j \abs{c_j}^2 = \sigma_{k+1}^2 \norm{x}^2$. Here we use $\sigma_j \le \sigma_{k+1}$ for $j > k$.
8. For $x = v_{k+1}$, step 6 gives $\norm{(A - A_k) x} = \sigma_{k+1}$. By the [[definition of the operator norm|0ZTEH055YF]] and step 7, $\norm{A - A_k}_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$.
9. Let $B$ have rank at most $k$. By the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]], the null space $N$ of $B$ has dimension at least $n - k$.
10. Let $W$ be the span of $v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}$, of dimension $k + 1$. By the [[dimension formula for sums of subspaces|06HTJNCMPA]], $\dim(N \cap W) = \dim N + \dim W - \dim(N + W) \ge (n - k) + (k + 1) - n = 1$.
11. So there is a unit vector $x = \sum_{j \le k+1} c_j v_j$ in $N \cap W$. Then $B x = 0$ and $\sum_{j \le k+1} \abs{c_j}^2 = 1$.
12. So $(A - B) x = A x = \sum_{j \le k+1} \sigma_j c_j u_j$, and $\norm{A x}^2 = \sum_{j \le k+1} \sigma_j^2 \abs{c_j}^2 \ge \sigma_{k+1}^2$, since $\sigma_j \ge \sigma_{k+1}$ for $j \le k+1$.
13. By the definition of the operator norm, $\norm{A - B}_2 \ge \norm{(A - B) x} \ge \sigma_{k+1}$. This proves part 1.

*Part 2, the Frobenius norm.*

14. By step 4 with $M = A - A_k$ and step 6, $\norm{A - A_k}_F^2 = \sum_j \norm{(A - A_k) v_j}^2 = \sum_{j = k+1}^r \sigma_j^2$. In the same way $\norm{A}_F^2 = \sum_{j \le r} \sigma_j^2$.
15. Let $B$ have rank $q \le k$. Let $w_1, \dots, w_q$ be an orthonormal basis of the column space of $B$, and let $P = \sum_{l \le q} w_l w_l^*$. For $y \in \C^m$, $P y = \sum_l (w_l^* y) w_l$ lies in the column space, and $w_l^* (y - P y) = 0$ for every $l$. So $P$ is the [[orthogonal projection|0Q8HZTJC7G]] onto the column space of $B$.
16. Let $a_j$ and $b_j$ be the $j$th columns of $A$ and $B$. Then $P a_j - b_j$ lies in the column space of $B$, and $a_j - P a_j$ is orthogonal to it. By the Pythagorean theorem, $\norm{a_j - b_j}^2 = \norm{a_j - P a_j}^2 + \norm{P a_j - b_j}^2 \ge \norm{a_j - P a_j}^2$.
17. Also $\norm{a_j}^2 = \norm{a_j - P a_j}^2 + \norm{P a_j}^2$. Summing over $j$, with $\norm{M}_F^2 = \sum_j \norm{m_j}^2$ for the columns $m_j$ of $M$ and step 16, gives $\norm{A - B}_F^2 \ge \norm{A}_F^2 - \norm{P A}_F^2$.
18. Extend $w_1, \dots, w_q$ to an orthonormal basis $w_1, \dots, w_m$ of $\C^m$. Then $P w_l = w_l$ for $l \le q$ and $P w_l = 0$ for $l > q$.
19. By steps 5 and 4 with $M = A^* P$ and $P^* = P$, $\norm{P A}_F^2 = \norm{A^* P}_F^2 = \sum_{l \le q} \norm{A^* w_l}^2$.
20. Since $A^* = \sum_{i \le r} \sigma_i v_i u_i^*$ and the $v_i$ are orthonormal, $\norm{A^* w_l}^2 = \sum_{i \le r} \sigma_i^2 \abs{u_i^* w_l}^2$.
21. So $\norm{P A}_F^2 = \sum_{i \le r} \sigma_i^2 c_i$ with $c_i = \sum_{l \le q} \abs{w_l^* u_i}^2$.
22. Since $P u_i = \sum_{l \le q} (w_l^* u_i) w_l$ with orthonormal $w_l$, the Pythagorean theorem gives $c_i = \norm{P u_i}^2$. Since $u_i - P u_i$ is orthogonal to $P u_i$, also $\norm{P u_i}^2 \le \norm{u_i}^2 = 1$. So $0 \le c_i \le 1$.
23. By coordinates in the orthonormal basis $u_1, \dots, u_m$, $\sum_{i \le m} \abs{u_i^* w_l}^2 = \norm{w_l}^2 = 1$. So $\sum_{i \le r} c_i \le \sum_{l \le q} 1 = q \le k$.
24. We have $\sum_{i \le r} \sigma_i^2 c_i - \sum_{i \le k} \sigma_i^2 = \sum_{i \le k} \sigma_i^2 (c_i - 1) + \sum_{k < i \le r} \sigma_i^2 c_i$.
25. In the first sum $c_i - 1 \le 0$ and $\sigma_i^2 \ge \sigma_k^2$. In the second sum $c_i \ge 0$ and $\sigma_i^2 \le \sigma_k^2$. So for $k \ge 1$ the right side of step 24 is at most $\sigma_k^2 \left(\sum_{i \le r} c_i - k\right) \le 0$, by step 23. For $k = 0$ we have $q = 0$, so every $c_i = 0$ and the right side is $0$.
26. By steps 17, 21 and 25, $\norm{A - B}_F^2 \ge \sum_{i \le r} \sigma_i^2 - \sum_{i \le k} \sigma_i^2 = \sum_{i = k+1}^r \sigma_i^2$. With step 14, this proves part 2.

## Idea

In the singular bases, $A$ is diagonal with decreasing entries, and $A_k$ keeps the first $k$ of them.
For the operator norm, a matrix of rank $k$ has a null space of dimension $n - k$, and it must meet the span of $v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}$, where $A$ stretches by at least $\sigma_{k+1}$.
For the Frobenius norm, the best $B$ with a given column space is $PA$, and then the problem is to maximize $\sum_i \sigma_i^2 c_i$ with weights $0 \le c_i \le 1$ of total at most $k$.

## Variants

- The Wikipedia proof of the Frobenius case first derives Weyl's inequality $\sigma_{i+j-1}(A) \le \sigma_i(A - B) + \sigma_j(B)$ from part 1. With $j = k + 1$ it gives $\sigma_i(A - B) \ge \sigma_{k+i}(A)$, and summing squares gives part 2. Weyl's inequalities belong to matrix analysis.
- For real matrices the same proof works with real orthonormal bases and transposes.
