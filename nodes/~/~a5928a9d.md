## Description

Let $n \ge 1$ be an integer.
The Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the set of all $n$-tuples $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ of [[real numbers|08J2M1JYAG]], with the structure below.
As a set, $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the [[Cartesian product|09CEDJYBKP]] of $n$ copies of $\mathbb{R}$.
Addition and multiplication by a scalar $c \in \mathbb{R}$ act on each coordinate: $x + y = (x_1 + y_1, \dots, x_n + y_n)$ and $c x = (c x_1, \dots, c x_n)$.
With these operations, $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a real vector space of dimension $n$.
The dot product of $x$ and $y$ is $x \cdot y = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i$.
The Euclidean norm of $x$ is $\|x\| = \sqrt{x \cdot x}$, and the distance between $x$ and $y$ is $\|x - y\|$.
The open ball with centre $a$ and radius $r > 0$ is $B(a, r) = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x - a\| < r\}$.
A set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is open when each point $a \in U$ has a radius $r > 0$ with $B(a, r) \subseteq U$.

## Remarks

The standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is $e_1, \dots, e_n$, where $e_i$ has $1$ in place $i$ and $0$ in each other place.
For $n = 2$ and $n = 3$, $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the coordinate plane and the coordinate space of school geometry.
The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality $|x \cdot y| \le \|x\| \, \|y\|$ gives the triangle inequality $\|x + y\| \le \|x\| + \|y\|$.
In the synthetic view, a Euclidean space is an affine space with a distance. After a choice of origin and of an orthonormal basis, $\mathbb{R}^n$ is its model.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A point of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a list of three numbers, such as $(1, -2, 5)$, that gives a position in space.
You add two points coordinate by coordinate, as you add arrows tip to tail.
The norm $\|x\|$ is the length of the arrow from the origin to $x$, by the theorem of Pythagoras.
