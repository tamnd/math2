## Summary

Arithmetic geometry is the study of polynomial equations over number fields, local fields and finite fields with the methods of algebraic geometry.
Its central objects are the rational points of varieties that are defined over number fields.
The geometry of a variety controls its arithmetic: for example, a curve of genus at least two over a number field has only finitely many rational points, by the theorem of Faltings.
Mordell proved in 1922 that the rational points of an elliptic curve over the rational numbers form a finitely generated group, and Wiles used the arithmetic of elliptic curves to prove Fermat's Last Theorem in 1995.
This topic collects the arithmetic geometry pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Algebraic geometry, algebraic number theory, Diophantine approximation, and automorphic forms and the Langlands program have their own topics.
