## Proof

Part 1 is proved in full for a universal theory, from three cited facts about $T^i_2$.
For part 2 we give the structure for $i = 0$ and name the cited steps.

**Cited facts.** Let $U_0 := \mathsf{PV}_1$, and for $i \ge 1$ let $U_i := T^i_2(\square^p_{i+1})$.
This is $T^i_2$ with a function symbol and its defining axioms for each $\square^p_{i+1}$ function.

- **K1 (universal axiomatization).** $U_i$ is axiomatized by universal sentences. Every $\Sigma^b_{i+1}$ formula is $U_i$-provably equivalent to a formula $\exists w \, (w \le s \land C)$ with $C$ open. For $i \ge 1$ the induction axioms are replaced by universal axioms that use the symbol for the least failure point.
- **K2 (conservativity).** Each symbol of $U_i$ is $\Sigma^b_{i+1}$-definable in $T^i_2$, by [[Buss's witnessing theorem|~ad7bce8a]] and the $\Sigma^b_{i+1}$-conservativity of $S^{i+1}_2$ over $T^i_2$. A theorem of $U_i$ is a theorem of $T^i_2$, with the symbols read through these definitions.
- **K3 (cases).** For terms $r, r', u, v$ there is a term $D(r, r', u, v)$ with $U_i \vdash (r = r' \to D = u) \land (r \ne r' \to D = v)$. This holds since $\square^p_{i+1}$ is closed under composition and definition by cases.

**Part 1.**

1. Assume $T^i_2 \vdash \forall x \, \exists y \, \forall z \, B(x, y, z)$ with $B \in \Sigma^b_{i+1}$. Then $U_i$ proves the same sentence, since $U_i$ extends $T^i_2$.
2. By K1, $U_i \vdash B \leftrightarrow B'$ with $B'(x, y, z) := \exists w \, (w \le s \land C)$, $C$ open. So $U_i \vdash \forall x \, \exists y \, \forall z \, B'$.
3. Add a new constant $c$ and a new unary function symbol $g$ to the language $L$ of $U_i$. Call the result $L'$.
4. Let $\Sigma := U_i \cup \set{\neg B'(c, t, g(t)) : t \text{ a closed term of } L'}$. We show that $\Sigma$ has no model.
5. Suppose $N \models \Sigma$. Let $N_0$ be the set of values in $N$ of closed $L'$ terms.
6. $N_0$ contains $c^N$ and is closed under every function symbol of $L'$. So $N_0$ is the universe of a [[substructure|676H7EGTKJ]] of $N$.
7. $U_i$ is universal, and universal sentences true in $N$ are true in its substructures. So $N_0 \models U_i$.
8. Let $d \in N_0$, say $d = t^N$. Then $N \models \neg B'(c, d, g(d))$, and $\neg B'$ is universal, so $N_0 \models \neg B'(c, d, g(d))$.
9. So $N_0 \models \forall y \, \exists z \, \neg B'(c, y, z)$, with $z = g(y)$. This contradicts $N_0 \models U_i$ and step 2.
10. By [[compactness|1NR3HAWG9D]], a finite part of $\Sigma$ has no model. So there are closed $L'$ terms $t_1, \dots, t_r$ with $U_i \models \bigvee_{j \le r} B'(c, t_j, g(t_j))$.
11. Concretely: for every model $M$ of $U_i$, every $a \in M$ and every map $G : M \to M$, the disjunction holds in $M$ with $c := a$ and $g := G$.
12. Close the list under arguments of $g$: if $g(s)$ is a subterm of a listed term, add $s$. Only subterms are added, so the list stays finite.
13. Adding disjuncts keeps step 11 true. Remove repetitions, and order the terms as $t_1, \dots, t_k$ by increasing length.
14. If $g(s)$ is a subterm of $t_j$, then $s = t_l$ for some $l$, and $s$ is shorter than $t_j$. So $l < j$.
15. Let $f_j(x, z_1, \dots, z_{j-1})$ be $t_j$ with $c$ replaced by $x$ and each outermost subterm $g(t_l)$ replaced by $z_l$.
16. Every occurrence of $g$ lies inside an outermost $g$-subterm, so $f_j$ is a term of $L$. By step 14 it uses only $z_l$ with $l < j$.
17. Evaluation: in $(M, a, G)$, the value of $t_j$ is $f_j(a, G(t_1^M), \dots, G(t_{j-1}^M))$, where $t_l^M$ is the value of $t_l$.
18. Repair of the answers. The values of different $t_j$ may coincide, so define $L$-terms $F_j(x, z_1, \dots, z_{j-1})$ and $Z_j(x, z_1, \dots, z_j)$ by recursion on $j$.
19. Put $F_j := f_j(x, Z_1, \dots, Z_{j-1})$. Let $Z_j := Z_l$ for the least $l < j$ with $F_j = F_l$, and $Z_j := z_j$ if there is no such $l$.
20. $Z_j$ is a nest of $j - 1$ terms $D$ from K3, testing $F_j = F_1$, then $F_j = F_2$, and so on. So $Z_j$ and $F_j$ are terms of $L$.
21. Claim: $U_i \vdash \bigvee_{j \le k} B'(x, F_j(x, z_1, \dots, z_{j-1}), z_j)$. Let $M \models U_i$ and $a, b_1, \dots, b_k \in M$.
22. Suppose every disjunct fails: $M \models \neg B'(a, v_j, b_j)$, where $v_j$ is the value of $F_j(a, b_1, \dots, b_{j-1})$. Let $d_j$ be the value of $Z_j(a, b_1, \dots, b_j)$.
23. For each $j$, $M \models \neg B'(a, v_j, d_j)$. If $d_j = b_j$ this is step 22. Otherwise $d_j = d_l$ and $v_j = v_l$ for some $l < j$, and induction on $j$ applies.
24. If $v_j = v_l$, then $d_j = d_l$. Both equal $d_{l_0}$, where $l_0$ is the least index with $v_{l_0} = v_j$, by step 19.
25. So $G(v_j) := d_j$ for $j \le k$, and $G(e) := 0$ for other $e$, defines a map $G : M \to M$.
26. By induction on $j$, the value of $t_j$ in $(M, a, G)$ is $v_j$. Step 17 gives $f_j(a, G(v_1), \dots, G(v_{j-1})) = f_j(a, d_1, \dots, d_{j-1})$, which is $v_j$ by step 19.
27. Then the $j$-th disjunct of step 11 is $B'(a, v_j, d_j)$, which fails by step 23. This contradicts step 11.
28. So the disjunction of step 21 holds in every model of $U_i$, and the [[completeness theorem|WQ1ZE255SF]] gives the claim.
29. By step 2, $U_i \vdash \bigvee_{j \le k} B(x, F_j(x, z_1, \dots, z_{j-1}), z_j)$. Each $F_j$ has $j$ arguments.
30. K2 turns the $F_j$ into functions $\Sigma^b_{i+1}$-defined in $T^i_2$, and the disjunction into a theorem of $T^i_2$. This proves part 1.

**Part 2, for $i = 0$.** Assume $\mathsf{PV}_1 = S^1_2$, with $S^1_2$ formulated in the language of $\mathsf{PV}$.

31. Let $\mathrm{Sat}(\varphi, w)$ say that $w \le \varphi$ is a satisfying assignment of the propositional formula $\varphi$. It is a polynomial-time relation.
32. The length-maximization principle $\mathrm{LMAX}$ says: for every sequence $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n$ there are $\ell \le n$ and $w_1, \dots, w_\ell$ with two properties.
33. First, $\mathrm{Sat}(\varphi_j, w_j)$ for all $j \le \ell$. Second, if $\ell < n$, then $\neg \mathrm{Sat}(\varphi_{\ell + 1}, u)$ for every $u$.
34. $\mathrm{LMAX}$ has the form $\forall \exists \forall$ with a matrix that is open in $\mathsf{PV}_1$. Here $\ell$ is read off from the code of $w_1, \dots, w_\ell$, and $j \le \ell$ is sharply bounded.
35. $S^1_2$ proves $\mathrm{LMAX}$, by $\Sigma^b_1$-$\mathrm{LIND}$ on "$\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_\ell$ have satisfying assignments". The property holds at $\ell = 0$, and $n$ is at most the length of the code.
36. If the property holds at $n$, take $\ell = n$. Otherwise LIND gives some $\ell < n$ where it holds at $\ell$ and fails at $\ell + 1$.
37. By the assumption, $\mathsf{PV}_1$ proves $\mathrm{LMAX}$. Part 1 gives $k$ and polynomial-time student functions.
38. **Cited (Buss).** Taking $n = k$, the student functions yield polynomial-time $f_1, \dots, f_k$ with the following property $(\ast)$.
39. $(\ast)$: if $w_1, \dots, w_k$ satisfy $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k$, then for some $j \le k$, $f_j(\bar \varphi, w_1, \dots, w_{j-1})$ satisfies $\varphi_j$. Each $f_j$ sees all of $\bar \varphi$.
40. Fix a bound $a$ on codes of formulas. For $0 \le \ell \le k$ and formulas $\psi_{\ell + 1}, \dots, \psi_k < a$, define $\mathrm{PreAdvice}_a(\psi_{\ell + 1}, \dots, \psi_k)$.
41. It says: for all $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_\ell < a$ and all $w_1, \dots, w_\ell$ that satisfy them, some $j \le \ell$ has $f_j(\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_\ell, \bar \psi, w_1, \dots, w_{j-1})$ satisfying $\varphi_j$.
42. For $\ell = k$ the suffix is empty, and $\mathrm{PreAdvice}_a$ holds by $(\ast)$. For $\ell = 0$ it fails, since no $j \le 0$ exists.
43. So there is a least $\ell \ge 1$ for which some suffix $\bar \psi$ of length $k - \ell$ satisfies $\mathrm{PreAdvice}_a$. Fix such a $\bar \psi$, the advice.
44. Claim: a formula $\varphi < a$ is satisfiable if and only if condition (C) holds.
45. (C): for all $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{\ell - 1} < a$ and $w_1, \dots, w_{\ell - 1}$ satisfying them, some $j \le \ell$ has $f_j(\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{\ell - 1}, \varphi, \bar \psi, w_1, \dots, w_{j-1})$ satisfying the $j$-th formula.
46. If $w_\ell$ satisfies $\varphi$, apply $\mathrm{PreAdvice}_a(\bar \psi)$ with $\varphi_\ell := \varphi$. The functions $f_j$ with $j \le \ell$ do not use $w_\ell$, so (C) holds.
47. Conversely, assume (C) and that $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable. Then $j = \ell$ never succeeds, so (C) holds with $j \le \ell - 1$.
48. That is $\mathrm{PreAdvice}_a(\varphi, \bar \psi)$ with $\ell - 1$ in place of $\ell$, against the minimality of $\ell$. This proves the claim.
49. (C) is a universal statement over polynomially bounded objects followed by a polynomial-time test. So satisfiability of formulas below $a$ is $\mathrm{coNP}$ with the advice $\bar \psi$.
50. The advice has $k - \ell$ formulas below $a$, so its length is polynomial in $\abs a$. Hence $\mathrm{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{coNP}/\mathrm{poly}$, since satisfiability is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete.
51. **Cited.** A [[Karp-Lipton|HM7DNQ2H5D]]-style argument turns $\mathrm{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{coNP}/\mathrm{poly}$ into a collapse of the polynomial hierarchy.
52. **Cited (Buss; Zambella).** For $i \ge 1$ the same argument runs with $U_i$, with quantified Boolean formulas of level $i$ in place of propositional formulas, and with a $\Sigma^p_i$ oracle. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

A universal theory that proves $\forall x \, \exists y \, \forall z \, B$ cannot have a model where every candidate $y$ is refuted by a Skolem function $g$.
Compactness turns this into a finite disjunction over terms in $x$ and $g$.
Reading $g$ as the teacher, the terms become a student strategy with a fixed number of rounds.
For the collapse, the provable length-maximization principle forces such a strategy, and a minimal set of hard formulas serves as polynomial advice.

## Variants

- Steps 3 to 10 can be replaced by [[Herbrand's theorem|VZ21F10WRS]] applied to $U_i$ with its equality axioms. The Herbrand disjunction gives the terms $t_j$ directly.
- Krajíček, Pudlák and Takeuti proved the stronger inclusion $\Sigma^p_{i+1} \subseteq \Delta^p_{i+1}/\mathrm{poly}$. Their argument uses a counting argument on which sets of formulas help others in the student strategy.
- Buss and Zambella formalized the collapse argument in $T^i_2$. Jeřábek formalized the stronger counting version, using approximate counting in bounded arithmetic.
