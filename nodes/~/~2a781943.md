## Example

Let $G = (\mathbb{Z}, +)$ be the [[integers|0C4JS9RF5M]] with addition.
Let $H = 4\mathbb{Z}$ and $K = 6\mathbb{Z}$ be the [[subgroups|0X1JXT47GA]] of multiples of $4$ and of multiples of $6$.
Their [[product|0PT52XQZD7]], written additively, is $H + K = \{4a + 6b : a, b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.
We show that $H + K = 2\mathbb{Z}$.

1. Each element $4a + 6b = 2(2a + 3b)$ is even. Thus $H + K \subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}$.
2. We have $2 = 4 \cdot (-1) + 6 \cdot 1$, so $2 \in H + K$.
3. For each integer $n$, $2n = 4 \cdot (-n) + 6 \cdot n$. Thus $2\mathbb{Z} \subseteq H + K$.
4. By steps 1 and 3, $H + K = 2\mathbb{Z}$.
5. The group $\mathbb{Z}$ is abelian, so $H + K = K + H$. This agrees with the criterion that $H + K$ is a subgroup exactly when $H + K = K + H$.

## Remarks

The intersection is $H \cap K = 12\mathbb{Z}$, the multiples of the least common multiple of $4$ and $6$.
All the sets are infinite, so the product formula does not apply as a quotient of numbers.
The form with an index does apply: $H + K$ is the union of $[H : H \cap K]$ cosets of $K$.
Here $[4\mathbb{Z} : 12\mathbb{Z}] = 3$, and indeed $2\mathbb{Z} = 6\mathbb{Z} \cup (4 + 6\mathbb{Z}) \cup (8 + 6\mathbb{Z})$.
In general, $m\mathbb{Z} + n\mathbb{Z} = d\mathbb{Z}$, where $d$ is the greatest common divisor of $m$ and $n$, by [[Bézout's identity|0DQVHMYAGA]].
For $m = 4$ and $n = 9$, the product is all of $\mathbb{Z}$, because $\gcd(4, 9) = 1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Add a multiple of $4$ to a multiple of $6$.
The result is always even, because both numbers are even.
Each even number appears, for example $2 = 6 - 4$ and $10 = 4 + 6$.
Thus the sums are exactly the even numbers.

### L2 human

We find all sums of a multiple of $10$ and a multiple of $15$.

1. Each sum $10a + 15b = 5(2a + 3b)$ is a multiple of $5$.
2. We have $5 = 10 \cdot (-1) + 15 \cdot 1$.
3. Thus each multiple $5n$ is a sum, and $10\mathbb{Z} + 15\mathbb{Z} = 5\mathbb{Z}$.

The typical use is to find the subgroup generated by two subgroups of an abelian group.
A trap is to confuse the sum $10\mathbb{Z} + 15\mathbb{Z}$ with the union $10\mathbb{Z} \cup 15\mathbb{Z}$, which does not contain $5$.
