## Example

Let $K = \mathbb F_4 = \{0, 1, \omega, \omega^2\}$ be the [[field with four elements|0ZXZ82FTX2]], where $\omega^2 = \omega + 1$ and $1 + 1 = 0$.
View $V = K$ as a vector space of dimension $1$ over $K$.
Let $\sigma \colon K \to K$, $\sigma(a) = a^2$, be the [[Frobenius automorphism|0CGCPX8Z70]], and let $f \colon V \to V$ be $f(x) = x^2$.

1. Additivity: $(x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2 = x^2 + y^2$, since $2 = 0$ in $K$.
2. Twisted homogeneity: $f(\lambda x) = \lambda^2 x^2 = \sigma(\lambda)\, f(x)$.
3. $\sigma$ is an automorphism of $K$: it is additive by step 1, multiplicative, and injective on the finite field $K$.
4. By steps 1 to 3, $f$ is $\sigma$-[[semilinear|~8c967182]].
5. $f$ is not [[linear|0RJF668P1T]] over $K$: $f(\omega \cdot 1) = \omega^2 = \omega + 1$, while $\omega\, f(1) = \omega$.
6. Table of values: $f(0) = 0$, $f(1) = 1$, $f(\omega) = \omega + 1 = \omega^2$, $f(\omega^2) = \omega^4 = \omega$.

## Remarks

- $\sigma \ne \id$, since $\sigma(\omega) = \omega^2 \ne \omega$. So $f$ is semilinear for a nontrivial automorphism.
- $f$ is a bijection with $f \circ f = \id$, since $x^4 = x$ for each $x \in \mathbb F_4$.
- Over the prime field $\mathbb F_2$, the map $f$ is linear, since $\sigma$ fixes $0$ and $1$.
- For $\mathbb F_q$ with $q = p^k$, the map $x \mapsto x^p$ is semilinear on each $\mathbb F_q$-space after a choice of basis, applied to the coordinates.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the field with four elements, $1 + 1 = 0$, so squaring respects sums.
Squaring also turns a scalar $\lambda$ into $\lambda^2$.
So it is linear up to a twist of the scalars, which is what semilinear means.

### L2 human

This example shows a semilinear map that is not antilinear: the twist is the Frobenius map, not complex conjugation.
The check $f(\omega) = \omega^2 \ne \omega = \omega f(1)$ shows that $f$ is not linear.
The trap is to forget that additivity of squaring depends on characteristic $2$.
