## Statement

Let $(X, d)$ be a [[metric space|~200ea42b]], let $x \in X$ and let $r > 0$.
Then the [[open ball|~43faf552]] $B(x, r)$ is an [[open set|~159c5927]] of $X$.

## Remarks

Proof: let $y \in B(x, r)$ and let $\varepsilon = r - d(x, y) > 0$. If $d(y, z) < \varepsilon$, then $d(x, z) \le d(x, y) + d(y, z) < r$ by the [[triangle inequality|~e1357625]]. Thus $B(y, \varepsilon) \subseteq B(x, r)$.
The open sets of $X$ are exactly the unions of open balls.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A point inside an open ball is at some distance from the edge of the ball.
A smaller ball about that point, with radius equal to this distance, stays inside the big ball.
