## Summary

A group is a set $G$ with a [[binary operation|0M418FE7BB]] $*$ under which $G$ is closed. The operation must satisfy three laws.

The operation is [[associative|0K8F7PVDMX]]: $(x*y)*z = x*(y*z)$ for all $x, y, z$ in $G$.

There is an identity $1$ in $G$ with $1*x = x*1 = x$ for all $x$ in $G$.

Each $x$ in $G$ has an [[inverse|0BRHKKP333]] $x^{-1}$ in $G$ with $x*x^{-1} = x^{-1}*x = 1$.

This is the usual notion of [[a group|G5S1M9]]. The group is abelian when it also has commutativity: $x*y = y*x$ for all $x, y$ in $G$.

A subset $K$ of $G$ is a [[subgroup|0N0PXCTYA7]] when $K$ is itself a group under the operation of $G$.

For two groups $G$ and $H$, a map $f \colon G \to H$ is a group homomorphism when $f(a*b) = f(a)*f(b)$ for all $a, b$ in $G$. A homomorphism that is bijective is a group isomorphism.
