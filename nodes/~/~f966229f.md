## Statement

Let $(a_n)$ be a real sequence.
Then $(a_n)$ converges if and only if $(a_n)$ is a [[Cauchy sequence|~7d922c14]].

## Remarks

One direction is [[convergent sequences are Cauchy|~7674ef78]].
For the other direction, a Cauchy sequence is [[bounded|~38ac1401]], so it has a convergent subsequence by the [[Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem|~ade6bb45]]. A Cauchy sequence with a convergent subsequence converges to the same limit.
The criterion says that $\mathbb{R}$ is a complete metric space. It fails in $\mathbb{Q}$.
Its value is that it proves convergence without a candidate for the limit.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To show that a sequence has a limit, you often need to know the limit first.
The Cauchy criterion avoids this. It is enough to show that the terms become close to each other.
