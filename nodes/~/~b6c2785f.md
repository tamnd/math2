## Proof

We use the notation of [[the theorem|~93aa2d58]].
Fix a model $M$ and a world $w$.
If $\varphi$ is false at $w$, then every formula $[!\varphi]\theta$ is true at $w$, and so is every right-hand side of parts 1 to 4, since each has the form $\varphi \to \cdots$.
So for parts 1 to 4 we assume $M, w \models \varphi$, which means $w \in W'$.

Part 1, atoms.

1. $M|\varphi, w \models p$ iff $w \in V'(p) = V(p) \cap W'$.
2. Since $w \in W'$, this holds iff $w \in V(p)$, that is, iff $M, w \models p$.
3. So both sides of part 1 are true at $w$ iff $p$ is true at $w$.

Part 2, negation.

4. The left side holds iff $M|\varphi, w \not\models \psi$.
5. Since $\varphi$ holds at $w$, $M, w \models [!\varphi]\psi$ iff $M|\varphi, w \models \psi$.
6. So the right side, $\neg[!\varphi]\psi$, holds iff $M|\varphi, w \not\models \psi$. The two sides agree.

Part 3, conjunction.

7. $M|\varphi, w \models \psi \wedge \chi$ iff $M|\varphi, w \models \psi$ and $M|\varphi, w \models \chi$.
8. By step 5 for $\psi$ and for $\chi$, this is $M, w \models [!\varphi]\psi \wedge [!\varphi]\chi$.

Part 4, knowledge.

9. $M|\varphi, w \models K_a\psi$ iff $M|\varphi, v \models \psi$ for every $v \in W'$ with $w R_a v$, by the definition of $R'_a$.
10. For any $v$ with $w R_a v$: if $v \notin W'$, then $[!\varphi]\psi$ is true at $v$ in $M$, vacuously.
11. If $v \in W'$, then $M, v \models [!\varphi]\psi$ iff $M|\varphi, v \models \psi$, by the definition of $[!\varphi]$.
12. So the condition of step 9 holds iff $M, v \models [!\varphi]\psi$ for every $v$ with $w R_a v$.
13. That is $M, w \models K_a[!\varphi]\psi$, which is the right side of part 4 given $\varphi$ at $w$.

Part 5, composition.

14. Let $\theta = \varphi \wedge [!\varphi]\psi$. We claim $(M|\varphi)|\psi = M|\theta$.
15. The worlds of $(M|\varphi)|\psi$ are the $v \in W'$ with $M|\varphi, v \models \psi$.
16. For $v \in W'$, $M, v \models [!\varphi]\psi$ iff $M|\varphi, v \models \psi$. So these are the $v$ with $M, v \models \theta$.
17. Both models restrict the relations and the valuation of $M$ to this same set. This proves the claim.
18. Now $M, w \models [!\varphi][!\psi]\chi$ iff: if $w \models \varphi$ and $M|\varphi, w \models \psi$, then $(M|\varphi)|\psi, w \models \chi$.
19. The hypothesis says $M, w \models \theta$, by step 16 at $w$. By step 17 the conclusion is $M|\theta, w \models \chi$.
20. That is the clause for $M, w \models [!\theta]\chi$.

Reduction to epistemic logic.

21. Call two formulas equivalent when they are true at the same worlds of every model.
22. Lemma: if $\varphi \equiv \varphi'$ and $\psi \equiv \psi'$, then $[!\varphi]\psi \equiv [!\varphi']\psi'$. Indeed $M|\varphi = M|\varphi'$, and $\psi, \psi'$ agree in that model.
23. For epistemic formulas $\alpha, \beta$ define $r(\alpha, \beta)$ by recursion on $\beta$:
    $r(\alpha, p) = \alpha \to p$, $r(\alpha, \neg\beta) = \alpha \to \neg r(\alpha, \beta)$,
    $r(\alpha, \beta \wedge \gamma) = r(\alpha, \beta) \wedge r(\alpha, \gamma)$, $r(\alpha, K_a\beta) = \alpha \to K_a r(\alpha, \beta)$.
24. By [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $\beta$, $r(\alpha, \beta) \equiv [!\alpha]\beta$. Each case is one of parts 1 to 4, with the induction hypothesis inside.
25. Define $t$ on all formulas: $t$ commutes with atoms, $\neg$, $\wedge$ and $K_a$, and $t([!\varphi]\psi) = r(t(\varphi), t(\psi))$.
26. By structural induction, $t(\chi)$ has no announcements and $t(\chi) \equiv \chi$.
27. The announcement case uses step 24 and then the lemma of step 22 with $\varphi \equiv t(\varphi)$ and $\psi \equiv t(\psi)$.

## Idea

An announcement deletes worlds and changes nothing else.
So atoms keep their values, Boolean connectives commute with the update, and knowledge after the update is knowledge before, restricted to the surviving worlds.
The only care is the precondition: where $\varphi$ is false, every $[!\varphi]$ statement is vacuously true.

## Variants

1. The same clause-by-clause argument proves the reduction axioms for action models, with the product update in place of restriction.
2. The translation $t$ can blow up exponentially, because $r(\alpha, \beta)$ copies $\alpha$ once for each operator of $\beta$. Lutz showed in 2006 that this blow-up cannot be avoided in general.
