## Proof

We prove the two directions.
For the hard direction, we prove the contrapositive: if $f$ is not surjective, then $f$ is not an epimorphism.
Maps compose from right to left: $\alpha\beta$ means first $\beta$, then $\alpha$.

1. Let $f \colon G \to H$ be a group homomorphism. Assume that $f$ is surjective, and let $u, v \colon H \to K$ be homomorphisms with $u \circ f = v \circ f$. Each $h \in H$ is $f(g)$ for some $g \in G$, so $u(h) = u(f(g)) = v(f(g)) = v(h)$. Thus $u = v$, and $f$ is an [[epimorphism|RVXDSY04KG]].
2. Now assume that $f$ is not surjective. Its [[image|0S21GNW9ZB]] $K = f(G)$ is a subgroup of $H$ with $K \ne H$.
3. Let $H/K$ be the set of [[left cosets|03DEH43F4H]] $xK$ of $K$ in $H$. Let $\infty$ be a new point that is not in $H/K$, and let $Y = (H/K) \cup \{\infty\}$.
4. Let $\operatorname{Sym}(Y)$ be the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] of all bijections $Y \to Y$, with composition as the product.
5. For $h \in H$, define $\lambda_h \colon Y \to Y$ by $\lambda_h(xK) = hxK$ and $\lambda_h(\infty) = \infty$. This is well defined, because $xK = x'K$ gives $hxK = hx'K$.
6. For $h, h' \in H$, we get $\lambda_h \lambda_{h'} = \lambda_{hh'}$, because both maps send $xK$ to $hh'xK$ and fix $\infty$. Also $\lambda_e$ is the identity. So $\lambda_h$ has the inverse $\lambda_{h^{-1}}$, and $\lambda \colon H \to \operatorname{Sym}(Y)$, $h \mapsto \lambda_h$, is a [[group homomorphism|0JYTG5K9Y4]]. This step uses that each $h$ has an inverse in $H$.
7. Let $\sigma \in \operatorname{Sym}(Y)$ swap the two points $K$ and $\infty$, and fix all other points. Then $\sigma^{-1} = \sigma$.
8. Define $\mu \colon H \to \operatorname{Sym}(Y)$ by $\mu_h = \sigma \lambda_h \sigma^{-1}$. It is a homomorphism, because $\mu_h \mu_{h'} = \sigma \lambda_h \sigma^{-1} \sigma \lambda_{h'} \sigma^{-1} = \sigma \lambda_{hh'} \sigma^{-1} = \mu_{hh'}$.
9. Let $k \in K$. We show $\lambda_k \sigma = \sigma \lambda_k$ by cases on the point $y \in Y$.
10. Case $y = \infty$. Then $\lambda_k(\sigma(\infty)) = \lambda_k(K) = kK = K$, and $\sigma(\lambda_k(\infty)) = \sigma(\infty) = K$.
11. Case $y = K$. Then $\lambda_k(\sigma(K)) = \lambda_k(\infty) = \infty$, and $\sigma(\lambda_k(K)) = \sigma(kK) = \sigma(K) = \infty$.
12. Case $y = xK$ with $xK \ne K$. Then $kxK \ne K$, because $kxK = K$ would give $xK = k^{-1}K = K$. So $\sigma$ fixes both $xK$ and $kxK$, and $\lambda_k(\sigma(xK)) = kxK = \sigma(\lambda_k(xK))$.
13. By steps 10 to 12, $\lambda_k \sigma = \sigma \lambda_k$, so $\mu_k = \sigma \lambda_k \sigma^{-1} = \lambda_k$ for each $k \in K$.
14. Each $f(g)$ is in $K$, so step 13 gives $\mu(f(g)) = \lambda(f(g))$ for each $g \in G$. Thus $\mu \circ f = \lambda \circ f$.
15. Now take $h \in H$ with $h \notin K$, which exists by step 2. Then $hK \ne K$, so $\sigma(hK) = hK$. We get $\mu_h(\infty) = \sigma(\lambda_h(\sigma(\infty))) = \sigma(\lambda_h(K)) = \sigma(hK) = hK$.
16. But $\lambda_h(\infty) = \infty$, and $hK \ne \infty$. So $\mu_h \ne \lambda_h$, and $\mu \ne \lambda$.
17. By steps 14 and 16, $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are two different homomorphisms $H \to \operatorname{Sym}(Y)$ with $\lambda \circ f = \mu \circ f$. So $f$ is not an epimorphism.
18. By step 1 and steps 2 to 17, $f$ is an epimorphism if and only if $f$ is [[surjective|01RVCMSN5N]].

This proves the theorem.

## Idea

The group $H$ acts on the cosets of $K = f(G)$, and we add one extra point that $H$ fixes.
Swapping the extra point with the coset $K$ gives a second action, which is the first action conjugated by the swap.
The elements of $K$ fix the coset $K$, so the two actions agree on $K$.
An element outside $K$ moves the coset $K$, so the two actions differ on it.

## Variants

If $K$ is a normal subgroup of $H$, a shorter proof works.
The projection $H \to H/K$ and the trivial homomorphism $H \to H/K$ agree on $K$, and they differ when $K \ne H$.

Another proof uses the [[amalgamated free product|M848AHBRCM]] $P = H *_K H$ of two copies of $H$ over $K$.
The two canonical homomorphisms $H \to P$ agree on $K$.
By the normal form theorem for amalgamated free products, they differ on each $h \notin K$.
