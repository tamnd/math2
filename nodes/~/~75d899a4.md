## Definition

Let $c$ be an [[isolated singularity|~7abd0ee3]] of $f$.
The singularity $c$ is essential if it is neither a [[removable singularity|~88345558]] nor a [[pole|~28935b89]] of $f$.

## Remarks

The singularity $c$ is essential if and only if the [[principal part|~523049a0]] of $f$ at $c$ has infinitely many nonzero terms.
Equivalently, $f(z)$ has no limit in $\mathbb{C}$ as $z \to c$, and $|f(z)|$ does not tend to $\infty$.
The standard example is [[exp(1/z) at 0|~ead0048f]]. The function $\sin(1/z)$ also has an essential singularity at $0$.
Near an essential singularity, $f$ comes close to each complex value, by the [[Casorati–Weierstrass theorem|~66c83564]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An essential singularity is a bad point that is neither a removable hole nor a point where the function simply goes to infinity.
Near such a point, the function behaves wildly: along different paths it can go to $0$, to infinity, or to any other value.
