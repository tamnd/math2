## Example

Let $F$ be a field with $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$, and let $V = F^2$.
For $x, y \in V$ let
$$\omega(x, y) = x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1 = \det \begin{pmatrix} x_1 & y_1 \\ x_2 & y_2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

1. $\omega$ is bilinear, because the [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]] is linear in each column.
2. $\omega$ is [[alternating|08ZQAKBKHF]]: $\omega(x, x) = x_1 x_2 - x_2 x_1 = 0$.
3. The [[matrix|0P24YW565F]] of $\omega$ in the standard basis is $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, since $\omega(e_1, e_2) = 1$ and $\omega(e_2, e_1) = -1$.
4. $\det J = 1 \neq 0$, so $\omega$ is nondegenerate.
5. So $(F^2, \omega)$ is a [[symplectic vector space|PQ7JJY0VPH]], and $(e_1, e_2)$ is a symplectic basis.
6. $\omega$ is not symmetric: $\omega(e_1, e_2) = 1$ and $\omega(e_2, e_1) = -1$, and $1 \neq -1$ because $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$.
7. Over $\R$, $\abs{\omega(x, y)}$ is the area of the parallelogram with sides $x$ and $y$. For $x = (2, 1)$ and $y = (1, 3)$ we get $\omega(x, y) = 6 - 1 = 5$.

## Remarks

The example is the smallest symplectic vector space, and every symplectic space is a sum of copies of it, by the [[classification of alternating forms|~a52a9f11]].
Every line $Fv$ is isotropic: $\omega(v, v) = 0$.
So every line in $F^2$ is a Lagrangian subspace.
If $\operatorname{char} F = 2$, the form is still alternating and nondegenerate, but now $-1 = 1$ and $\omega$ is also symmetric.
On $F^n$ with $n \ge 3$, the form $x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1$ is alternating but degenerate, since $e_3$ is in its radical.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two arrows in the plane span a parallelogram.
The rule here gives its area with a sign: positive if the turn from the first arrow to the second is counterclockwise, negative otherwise.
For $(2, 1)$ and $(1, 3)$ the area is $5$, and in the other order it is $-5$.
An arrow with itself spans no area, so the rule gives $0$.

### L2 human

We compute the values on a second basis to see that $\omega$ keeps its shape.

1. Take $b_1 = (1, 1)$ and $b_2 = (1, 2)$.
2. $\omega(b_1, b_2) = 1 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 1 = 1$.
3. $\omega(b_2, b_1) = -1$, and $\omega(b_1, b_1) = \omega(b_2, b_2) = 0$.
4. So the matrix in the new basis is again $J$, and $(b_1, b_2)$ is also a symplectic basis.

This works because $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = 1$, and in dimension $2$ the symplectic maps are the maps of determinant $1$.
A trap: in characteristic $2$ the form is symmetric too, so "alternating" and "not symmetric" do not go together there.
