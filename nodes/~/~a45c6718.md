## Example

We derive [[Aristotle's theses|4KTD8GB5PV]] AT and AT′ in Wansing's system $\mathbf{C}$ of [[connexive logic|NQFYY76BSM]].
The axioms of $\mathbf{C}$ are positive intuitionistic logic, a2 $\sim\sim A \leftrightarrow A$, the two De Morgan laws, and a5 $\sim(A \to B) \leftrightarrow (A \to \sim B)$.
The rule is [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]].

AT: $\sim(\sim A \to A)$.

1. $\sim A \to \sim A$. Positive intuitionistic logic proves $C \to C$ for every formula $C$; put $C := \sim A$.
2. $\sim(\sim A \to A) \leftrightarrow (\sim A \to \sim A)$. Axiom a5, with $\sim A$ for $A$ and $A$ for $B$.
3. $(\sim A \to \sim A) \to \sim(\sim A \to A)$. From 2, by the elimination of $\land$ in positive intuitionistic logic.
4. $\sim(\sim A \to A)$. Modus ponens on 1 and 3.

AT′: $\sim(A \to \sim A)$.

5. $\sim\sim A \leftrightarrow A$. Axiom a2.
6. $A \to \sim\sim A$. From 5, by the elimination of $\land$.
7. $\sim(A \to \sim A) \leftrightarrow (A \to \sim\sim A)$. Axiom a5, with $\sim A$ for $B$.
8. $(A \to \sim\sim A) \to \sim(A \to \sim A)$. From 7, by the elimination of $\land$.
9. $\sim(A \to \sim A)$. Modus ponens on 6 and 8.

## Remarks

The semantics of $\mathbf{C}$ confirms AT.
A state $t$ supports the truth of $\sim(\sim A \to A)$ if and only if it supports the falsity of $\sim A \to A$.
That holds if and only if every state $u \geq t$ that supports the truth of $\sim A$ also supports the falsity of $A$.
Support of the truth of $\sim A$ is, by definition, support of the falsity of $A$, so the condition always holds.
In classical logic, the same formula AT is false when $A$ is true.

## Explanations

### L1 human

AT says: "it is not the case that not-$A$ implies $A$".
In $\mathbf{C}$, to deny an implication is to say that its antecedent implies the denial of its consequent.
So AT says "not-$A$ implies not-$A$", which is always true.

### L2 human

Axiom a5 turns a negated implication into an implication with a negated consequent.
Each thesis then reduces to an instance of $C \to C$, up to the double negation law a2.
A trap is to use the classical reading $\sim(A \to B) \leftrightarrow (A \land \sim B)$; with it, AT becomes $\sim A \land \sim A$, which is not a theorem.
