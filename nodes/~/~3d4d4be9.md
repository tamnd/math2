## Proof

We prove part 1 for countable $L_1$ and $L_2$; part 2 follows from part 1, as the statement shows.
We write $\varphi(c)$ for $\varphi^x_c$ when $x$ is clear.
By the [[soundness theorem|~331f6430]] and [[Gödel's completeness theorem|WQ1ZE255SF]], $\Delta \vdash \sigma$ iff $\Delta \models \sigma$ for sets of sentences.
We use both forms.

Step (a): inseparable pairs.

1. Let $C = \set{c_0, c_1, \dots}$ be new constants. Put $L_i(C) = L_i \cup C$ for $i = 0, 1, 2$; then $L_1(C) \cap L_2(C) = L_0(C)$.
2. Call a pair $(\Gamma', \Sigma')$ of sets of sentences of $L_1(C)$ and $L_2(C)$ inseparable when no sentence of $L_0(C)$ separates $\Gamma'$ from $\Sigma'$.
3. If $(\Gamma', \Sigma')$ is inseparable, then $\Gamma'$ is consistent. Otherwise $\Gamma' \vdash \neg \forall x \, (x = x)$, and $\Sigma' \vdash \neg \neg \forall x \, (x = x)$ always holds.
4. In the same way, $\Sigma'$ is consistent, with the separating sentence $\forall x \, (x = x)$.

Step (b): the start pair $(\Gamma, \Sigma)$ is inseparable in $L_0(C)$.

5. Suppose an $L_0(C)$-sentence separates $\Gamma$ from $\Sigma$. Write it as $\theta(c_{i_1}, \dots, c_{i_k})$, where $\theta(x_1, \dots, x_k)$ is an $L_0$-formula without symbols of $C$.
6. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an $L_1(C)$-structure that is a model of $\Gamma$, and let $a_1, \dots, a_k$ be elements of $\mathcal{A}$. Change the interpretation of each $c_{i_j}$ to $a_j$. The new structure is still a model of $\Gamma$, since $\Gamma$ has no symbols of $C$.
7. So the new structure satisfies $\theta(c_{i_1}, \dots, c_{i_k})$, and $\mathcal{A} \models \theta[a_1, \dots, a_k]$. Hence $\Gamma \models \forall x_1 \cdots \forall x_k \, \theta$.
8. By completeness, $\Gamma \vdash \forall \bar x \, \theta$. Axiom group 2, used $k$ times with Rule T, gives $\vdash \forall \bar x \, \theta \to \theta(c_{i_1}, \dots, c_{i_k})$. With $\Sigma \vdash \neg \theta(c_{i_1}, \dots, c_{i_k})$ and Rule T, $\Sigma \vdash \neg \forall \bar x \, \theta$.
9. So the $L_0$-sentence $\forall \bar x \, \theta$ separates $\Gamma$ from $\Sigma$, which contradicts the hypothesis.

Step (c): two lemmas.
Let $(\Gamma', \Sigma')$ be inseparable.

10. Lemma A. For each sentence $\alpha$ of $L_1(C)$, one of the pairs $(\Gamma' \cup \set{\alpha}, \Sigma')$ and $(\Gamma' \cup \set{\neg \alpha}, \Sigma')$ is inseparable.
11. Proof. Suppose $\theta_1$ separates the first pair and $\theta_2$ the second. The deduction theorem gives $\Gamma' \vdash \alpha \to \theta_1$ and $\Gamma' \vdash \neg \alpha \to \theta_2$.
12. Rule T gives $\Gamma' \vdash \theta_1 \lor \theta_2$ and $\Sigma' \vdash \neg(\theta_1 \lor \theta_2)$. So $\theta_1 \lor \theta_2$ separates $\Gamma'$ from $\Sigma'$, a contradiction.
13. Lemma B. Let $\neg \forall x \, \varphi \in \Gamma'$, and let $c \in C$ occur neither in $\Gamma'$ nor in $\Sigma'$. Then $(\Gamma' \cup \set{\neg \varphi(c)}, \Sigma')$ is inseparable.
14. Proof. Suppose $\theta(c)$ separates it, where $\theta(y)$ is an $L_0(C)$-formula without $c$. We show that $\exists y \, \theta$ separates $\Gamma'$ from $\Sigma'$.
15. Let $\mathcal{A} \models \Gamma'$. Since $\mathcal{A} \models \neg \forall x \, \varphi$, some $a$ has $\mathcal{A} \models \neg \varphi[a]$. Change the interpretation of $c$ to $a$.
16. The new structure satisfies $\Gamma' \cup \set{\neg \varphi(c)}$, since $c$ is not in $\Gamma'$. So it satisfies $\theta(c)$, and $\mathcal{A} \models \exists y \, \theta$. Hence $\Gamma' \models \exists y \, \theta$.
17. Let $\mathcal{B} \models \Sigma'$ and $b \in \mathcal{B}$. Change the interpretation of $c$ to $b$. The new structure satisfies $\Sigma'$, so it satisfies $\neg \theta(c)$. So $\mathcal{B} \models \neg \exists y \, \theta$.
18. By completeness, $\Gamma' \vdash \exists y \, \theta$ and $\Sigma' \vdash \neg \exists y \, \theta$, a contradiction.
19. Lemmas A and B hold with the roles of $\Gamma'$ and $\Sigma'$ exchanged, by the same proofs.

Step (d): the construction.

20. List the sentences of $L_1(C)$ as $\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \dots$ and those of $L_2(C)$ as $\beta_0, \beta_1, \dots$. They are [[countable|01TQB88H4C]] sets.
21. Put $\Gamma_0 = \Gamma$ and $\Sigma_0 = \Sigma$. At each stage, only finitely many sentences have been added, so only finitely many constants of $C$ occur.
22. Stage $n$, side 1: by Lemma A, add $\alpha_n$ or $\neg \alpha_n$ to $\Gamma_n$ so that the pair stays inseparable. If the added sentence is $\neg \forall x \, \varphi$, also add $\neg \varphi(c)$ for the first $c \in C$ that occurs in no sentence of the current pair. Lemma B keeps the pair inseparable.
23. Stage $n$, side 2: do the same with $\beta_n$ and $\Sigma_n$. The result is the pair $(\Gamma_{n+1}, \Sigma_{n+1})$, which is inseparable.
24. Put $\Gamma^* = \bigcup_n \Gamma_n$ and $\Sigma^* = \bigcup_n \Sigma_n$. A separating sentence would have derivations from finitely many sentences, all in some $(\Gamma_n, \Sigma_n)$. So $(\Gamma^*, \Sigma^*)$ is inseparable.

Step (e): properties of $\Gamma^*$ and $\Sigma^*$.

25. By step 3, $\Gamma^*$ is consistent. By step 22 it contains $\alpha$ or $\neg \alpha$ for each $L_1(C)$-sentence $\alpha$. So $\Gamma^*$ is maximal consistent in $L_1(C)$.
26. Let $\neg \forall x \, \varphi \in \Gamma^*$. It is some $\alpha_n$, and $\alpha_n$ was added at stage $n$, since $\neg \alpha_n \notin \Gamma^*$. So its witness was added too. Hence $\Gamma^*$ has the witness property of the [[Henkin construction|~95e9f5bb]], and it is a Henkin set. In the same way, $\Sigma^*$ is a Henkin set in $L_2(C)$.
27. The two sets agree on $L_0(C)$. Let $\theta$ be an $L_0(C)$-sentence with $\theta \in \Gamma^*$ and $\theta \notin \Sigma^*$. Then $\neg \theta \in \Sigma^*$, and $\theta$ separates them.
28. Symmetrically, $\theta \in \Sigma^*$ and $\theta \notin \Gamma^*$ is impossible. So $\theta \in \Gamma^*$ iff $\theta \in \Sigma^*$.

Step (f): the term models.

29. Let $\mathcal{M}_1$ and $\mathcal{M}_2$ be the term models of $\Gamma^*$ and $\Sigma^*$, built as in Step (f) of the [[proof of model existence|~65534b28]]. By the truth lemma there, $\mathcal{M}_1 \models \Gamma^*$ and $\mathcal{M}_2 \models \Sigma^*$.
30. Every element of $\mathcal{M}_1$ is $[c]_1$ for some $c \in C$. For a closed term $t$, $\vdash \neg \forall x \, \neg (x = t)$, so this sentence is in $\Gamma^*$.
31. Its witness $c$ gives $\neg \neg (c = t) \in \Gamma^*$, so $(c = t) \in \Gamma^*$ by deductive closure, and $[t]_1 = [c]_1$. The same holds for $\mathcal{M}_2$.
32. Define $h([c]_1) = [c]_2$ for $c \in C$. By step 28 with $\theta = (c = d)$, $[c]_1 = [d]_1$ iff $[c]_2 = [d]_2$. So $h$ is well defined and injective.
33. By step 31 for $\mathcal{M}_2$, $h$ is onto. So $h$ is a bijection from $\mathcal{M}_1$ to $\mathcal{M}_2$.
34. Let $R$ be a relation symbol of $L_0$. Then $R^{\mathcal{M}_1}([c_1]_1, \dots)$ iff $R(c_1, \dots) \in \Gamma^*$ iff $R(c_1, \dots) \in \Sigma^*$ iff $R^{\mathcal{M}_2}([c_1]_2, \dots)$, by step 28.
35. Let $f$ be a function symbol of $L_0$ and $f(c_1, \dots, c_n) = d$ be in $\Gamma^*$, for some $d \in C$ (step 31). By step 28 it is also in $\Sigma^*$. So $h$ sends $f^{\mathcal{M}_1}([c_1]_1, \dots)$ to $f^{\mathcal{M}_2}([c_1]_2, \dots)$.
36. Constant symbols of $L_0(C)$ are the case $n = 0$ of step 35. So $h$ is an isomorphism of the reducts of $\mathcal{M}_1$ and $\mathcal{M}_2$ to $L_0(C)$.

Step (g): the joint model.

37. Define a [[structure|983GZBQYT4]] $\mathcal{N}$ for $L_1 \cup L_2 \cup C$ with the universe of $\mathcal{M}_1$. Symbols of $L_1(C)$ are interpreted as in $\mathcal{M}_1$.
38. A symbol of $L_2$ outside $L_0$ is carried over from $\mathcal{M}_2$ by $h$. Put $R^{\mathcal{N}}(\bar a)$ iff $R^{\mathcal{M}_2}(h(\bar a))$, and $f^{\mathcal{N}}(\bar a) = h^{-1}(f^{\mathcal{M}_2}(h(\bar a)))$.
39. For symbols of $L_0(C)$ the two definitions agree, by step 36. So the reduct of $\mathcal{N}$ to $L_1(C)$ is $\mathcal{M}_1$, and $h$ is an isomorphism from the reduct to $L_2(C)$ onto $\mathcal{M}_2$.
40. Hence $\mathcal{N} \models \Gamma^* \supseteq \Gamma$. Isomorphic structures satisfy the same sentences, so $\mathcal{N} \models \Sigma^* \supseteq \Sigma$.
41. The reduct of $\mathcal{N}$ to $L_1 \cup L_2$ is a model of $\Gamma \cup \Sigma$. By soundness, $\Gamma \cup \Sigma$ is consistent.

## Idea

We run the Henkin construction on both sides at once, and we keep one invariant: no sentence in the common language separates the two sides.
At the end, both sides are Henkin sets, and they agree on every sentence in the common language with the new constants.
The new constants name every element on both sides, so this agreement gives an isomorphism of the common reducts.
Then the two term models glue into one structure for the joint language.

## Variants

- For uncountable languages, list the sentences in a well-ordered sequence and use transfinite recursion. Lemma B needs a supply of unused constants at each stage.
- A model-theoretic proof uses compactness and elementary chains: alternate between models of the two sides, with elementary embeddings in $L_0$. The union of the chain is a model of $\Gamma \cup \Sigma$.
- The [[proof from Craig's theorem|~1df8300a]] is short, but it uses interpolation, which is itself proved from Robinson's theorem or from cut elimination.
