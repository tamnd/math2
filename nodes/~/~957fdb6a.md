## Statement

A propositional formula $\varphi$ is *Beth valid* if the root of every [[Beth model|~b3c503cf]] forces $\varphi$.

**Theorem.**

1. Soundness: in every Beth model, every truth set $[\![\varphi]\!]$ is an up-set and is barred. Every theorem of $\mathsf{IPC}$ is forced at every node.
2. Completeness: if $\varphi$ is Beth valid, then $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash \varphi$.
3. More precisely, for every rooted Kripke model $\mathfrak{K}$ with root $w_0$ there is a Beth model $\mathfrak{B}$ and a map $\ell$ from its nodes onto the worlds of $\mathfrak{K}$. For every node $\sigma$ and formula $\chi$, $\sigma \Vdash_{\mathfrak{B}} \chi$ if and only if $\ell(\sigma) \Vdash_{\mathfrak{K}} \chi$.

The proof is in [[the proof by repeated worlds|~1312d263]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. Valuations must be barred. With $V(p)$ the set of nodes other than the root of the binary tree, the root forces $p \vee p$ but not $p$; see [[a valuation that is not barred|~ee92a072]].
2. Valuations must be up-sets, for the same reason as in Kripke semantics: the axiom $p \to (q \to p)$ needs persistence.
3. Infinite trees are needed for completeness: on a finite tree the truth values form a Boolean algebra, so $p \vee \neg p$ is valid there.

### Consequences

1. Beth semantics and [[Kripke semantics|~ac029000]] validate the same propositional formulas.
2. Barred up-sets of a tree correspond to open sets of its space of paths. So every Beth model is a topological model on its space of paths, and topological semantics is complete too.

### Converse

The theorem is a biconditional, so its converse is part of it.
Item 3 can not be reversed: not every Beth model arises by the construction, since the trees of the construction repeat the last world at every node.

### Generalizations

1. Beth semantics extends to intuitionistic predicate logic, with growing domains and bars in the clause for $\exists$.
2. Beth models form a special case of sheaf models over a site, where bars are the covering families.

## History

Beth introduced the semantics in the 1950s, as a semantics for intuitionistic logic with trees of finite stages.
The proof given here reduces Beth completeness to Kripke completeness.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem says that the trees of possible futures capture exactly the laws of intuitionistic logic.
Every law holds at every stage of every tree.
And every formula that is not a law fails at the start of some tree.
For example, "$p$ or not $p$" fails on the tree of all coin toss records, with "$p$" read as "heads has come up".

### L2 human

1. Take the Kripke model $u \leq v$ with $p$ true at $v$, which refutes $p \vee \neg p$ at $u$.
2. The Beth tree has nodes $(u, \ldots, u)$, $(u, \ldots, u, v, \ldots, v)$: sequences of worlds that never go down.
3. The path $(u), (u, u), (u, u, u), \ldots$ never reaches $v$, so no bar above the root consists of nodes that force $p$ or $\neg p$.
4. Hence the root does not force $p \vee \neg p$, as item 3 predicts.

The typical use of the theorem is to transfer results between Kripke and Beth semantics.
A trap: the repeated world in step 3 is needed; without it, the tree for a finite Kripke model is finite and classical.

### L3 human

The proof turns a Kripke model into a tree of histories in which one may stay at a world for ever.
For a finite Kripke frame, each path of the tree eventually stays at one world.
The map that sends a path to this world is continuous and open onto the Alexandrov space of the frame, as in the proof of the [[McKinsey-Tarski theorem|2VF0546VXK]].
