## Statement

Let $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ be distinct [[concyclic points|~444119f0]] of the Euclidean plane $\mathbb{R}^2$. Let $\measuredangle$ be the [[oriented angle|~ac6c32ae]], with values in $\mathbb{R} / 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$.
Then
$$2 \, \measuredangle ACB = 2 \, \measuredangle ADB.$$
For the unoriented angles: if $C$ and $D$ are on the same side of the line $AB$, then $\angle ACB = \angle ADB$. If they are on opposite sides, then $\angle ACB + \angle ADB = \pi$.

## Remarks

The statement follows from the [[inscribed angle theorem|~c2de0e94]]: both sides are equal to the oriented angle $\measuredangle AOB$ at the centre $O$.
The factor $2$ is necessary. It removes the difference of $\pi$ between points on the two arcs.
The case of opposite sides says that the opposite angles of a convex cyclic quadrilateral add up to $\pi$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A segment of a circle is the region between a chord $AB$ and one of its arcs. From every point of that arc, the chord $AB$ is seen under the same angle. From the other arc, it is seen under the supplementary angle.
