## Summary

A non-constructive existence proof shows that an object with a property exists without naming the object or giving a method to find it.
The usual forms are a [[proof by contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]] that assumes no such object exists, a [[proof by cases|0WCP0PVET6]] in which each case has its own witness, and the use of a general theorem such as the [[intermediate value theorem|00KAFBR32E]], the [[pigeonhole principle|0HGVCWQGY3]] or the [[axiom of choice|0HTGB13FE2]].
Such proofs are valid in classical mathematics, which accepts the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]].
They are rejected in intuitionistic and other constructive mathematics, which accept only a [[constructive proof|01XM7JEKHY]] of existence.

## Motivation

A witness is sometimes hard to find and sometimes impossible to describe in finite terms.
The real root of $x^5 - x - 1$ cannot be written with radicals, but the intermediate value theorem shows that it exists.
For a general theory, existence is often all that is needed: a theorem about a root, a maximum or a basis can be applied once the object is known to exist.
Non-constructive proofs are often much shorter than constructive ones.
The price is information: the proof does not say what the object is, and it may not give any way to compute it.

## Notation

There is no special notation; the statement is an ordinary [[existential statement|0JW2FHW7G3]] $\exists x \, P(x)$.
A proof by contradiction of it starts "Suppose that no $x$ satisfies $P(x)$", that is, $\forall x \, \neg P(x)$; see [[negation of an existential statement|05SZQKH75K]].
In Lean, `by_contra h` followed by `push_neg at h` gives this form, and definitions that use the axiom `Classical.choice` must be marked `noncomputable`.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[The proof by cases that some irrational power is rational|~0fe010ee]]: either $\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}$ is rational, or it is irrational and $\left(\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{\sqrt{2}} = 2$; the proof does not decide which.
2. [[A real root of x^5 - x - 1 by the intermediate value theorem|~67129905]]: the polynomial changes sign between $1$ and $2$, so it has a root there.
3. If $n$ real numbers have average $A$, then at least one of them is at least $A$. If all were less than $A$, their sum would be less than $nA$.
4. [[Hilbert's basis theorem|0YKA9J3XEE]]: every ideal of a polynomial ring in finitely many variables over a field is finitely generated. Hilbert's proof of 1890 shows that a finite set of generators exists without computing one.
5. Transcendental numbers exist, because the algebraic numbers are countable and the real numbers are not. This counting form of Cantor's argument names no [[transcendental number|0VTQXTCR2G]].

### Non-examples

1. [[Irrational a and b with a to the power b rational, by construction|~0291138b]]: the pair $\sqrt{2}$ and $\log_2 9$ is named and checked.
2. [[There are n consecutive composite numbers|~ae66fa41]]: the numbers are given by a formula.
3. Liouville's proof that transcendental numbers exist is constructive: it exhibits explicit numbers, such as $\sum_{k \geq 1} 10^{-k!}$, and proves that they are transcendental.

### Basic properties

1. In classical logic, $\neg \forall x \, \neg P(x)$ implies $\exists x \, P(x)$. This step uses the [[double negation law|0NFAGWH84K]], and intuitionistic logic does not accept it. A proof by contradiction of an existence statement is non-constructive for this reason.
2. A proof by cases with a witness in each case is non-constructive when it cannot be decided which case holds.
3. A non-constructive proof can often be turned into a constructive one with more work. The two proofs on irrational powers show this, and bisection turns the root of $x^5 - x - 1$ into a computable sequence of intervals.
4. Some existence results cannot be proved constructively at all. The existence of a subset of the real line that is not Lebesgue measurable is proved with the axiom of choice, and Robert Solovay showed in 1970 that some form of choice is needed, assuming that an inaccessible cardinal is consistent.

### History

In 1888 David Hilbert proved that the invariants of binary forms are generated by finitely many of them, and his paper of 1890 gave the basis theorem in general.
His proof showed that a finite system of generators exists without giving a method to find it.
Paul Gordan, who had found generators for binary forms by explicit computation, is reported to have called the proof "not mathematics" but "theology".
Luitzen Egbertus Jan Brouwer's intuitionism, from the early twentieth century, rejected such proofs in principle; see [[constructivism|G958EQ8CWW]].

### Common errors

1. Reading a witness into a non-constructive proof. The proof of example 1 does not show that $\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}$ is irrational.
2. Treating a non-constructive proof as invalid. In classical mathematics it proves existence fully; it only gives less information.
3. Negating the existence statement wrongly at the start. The negation of "some $x$ satisfies $P$" is "every $x$ fails $P$", not "some $x$ fails $P$".

## Explanations

### L1 human

Thirteen people are in a room.
There are only twelve months, so two of them must have a birthday in the same month.
This argument proves that such a pair exists.
It does not say who the two people are.
To find them, one would have to ask everybody.
A proof that shows "something must exist" without pointing to it is a non-constructive proof.

### L2 human

We prove: if $a_1, \ldots, a_n$ are real numbers with average $A = \frac{a_1 + \cdots + a_n}{n}$, then $a_i \geq A$ for some $i$.

1. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $a_i < A$ for every $i$.
2. Adding the $n$ inequalities gives $a_1 + \cdots + a_n < nA$.
3. But $a_1 + \cdots + a_n = nA$ by the definition of $A$. This is a contradiction.
4. So some $a_i$ satisfies $a_i \geq A$.

The typical use: assume that every candidate fails, and add up or count the failures to reach a contradiction.

A trap: the proof does not tell which $a_i$ works.
Here a constructive proof is easy, since the largest $a_i$ always works, but in other problems no such choice is known.

### L3 human

In intuitionistic logic, a proof of $\exists x \, P(x)$ must contain a witness, so the classical step from $\neg \forall x \, \neg P(x)$ to $\exists x \, P(x)$ is not valid; the Gödel-Gentzen negative translation still embeds classical arithmetic into intuitionistic arithmetic, with $\exists$ read as $\neg \forall \neg$.
Markov's principle is the special case of this step for decidable properties of natural numbers, and it is accepted by some constructive schools and not by others.
Reverse mathematics measures how non-constructive a theorem is by the set existence axioms it needs, for example weak König's lemma for the theorem that a continuous function on $[0, 1]$ attains a maximum.
In Lean and other proof assistants, non-constructive proofs of propositions are accepted, but definitions that depend on them cannot be compiled to code.
The broader theory is treated in the topic [[type theory and constructive mathematics|K6P3NSQEZ3]].
