## Summary

Reducibility says that a set cut out by holomorphic equations splits into two genuinely different pieces. It is defined for a [[germ of an analytic set|Q9B74BYZ8C]] at a point, and also for a [[subvariety|4YSCK1DF26]] of an open set.

Let $(X,p)$ be a germ of a subvariety at a point $p \in \mathbb{C}^n$. It is reducible at $p$ if it is a union $(X,p) = (X_1,p) \cup (X_2,p)$ of two germs of subvarieties such that neither germ is contained in the other. If no such union exists, the germ is irreducible at $p$.

The global notion is the same. A subvariety $X$ of an open set $U$ is reducible in $U$ if $X = X_1 \cup X_2$ for two subvarieties of $U$, neither contained in the other. Otherwise $X$ is irreducible in $U$.
