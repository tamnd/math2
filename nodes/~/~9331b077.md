## Definition

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be an $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]] with domain $M$, and let $\kappa$ be an infinite [[cardinal|0EBACQFAZW]].
A [[partial elementary map|XTNYY0WRQ5]] on $\mathcal{M}$ is a map $f : A \to M$ with $A \subseteq M$ such that $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi(\bar{a}) \leftrightarrow \varphi(f(\bar{a}))$ for every $L$-formula $\varphi$ and every tuple $\bar{a}$ of $A$.

1. $\mathcal{M}$ is *$\kappa$-homogeneous* if for every partial elementary map $f : A \to M$ with $\abs{A} < \kappa$ and every $b \in M$ there is a partial elementary map $g : A \cup \set{b} \to M$ that extends $f$.
2. $\mathcal{M}$ is *strongly $\kappa$-homogeneous* if every partial elementary map $f : A \to M$ with $\abs{A} < \kappa$ extends to an [[automorphism|DSQ17TNW1C]] of $\mathcal{M}$.
3. $\mathcal{M}$ is *homogeneous* if it is $\abs{M}$-homogeneous, and *strongly homogeneous* if it is strongly $\abs{M}$-homogeneous.

## Notation

The automorphisms of $\mathcal{M}$ that fix $A$ pointwise form the group $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{M}/A)$.
Some sources call strongly $\kappa$-homogeneous structures $\kappa$-homogeneous.

## Remarks

The main page of the idea is [[homogeneous model|~ee122c2b]].

Example: in the order $(\Q, <)$, an order-preserving map between finite sets is partial elementary by quantifier elimination, and it extends to a piecewise linear automorphism of $\Q$.
So $(\Q, <)$ is strongly $\aleph_0$-homogeneous, and it is strongly homogeneous since it is countable.

Non-example: in $(\Q, <, c_n)_{n \in \N}$ with $c_n = 1 - 1/(n+1)$, the map $2 \mapsto 1$ is partial elementary, and no extension of it has $3/2$ in its domain; see [[an order whose types are not orbits|~eb3dc704]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Homogeneity asks that every partial matching of elements that respects all first-order properties can be continued.
The plain version continues the matching one element at a time, and the strong version continues it to a symmetry of the whole structure.

### L2 human

We show that a strongly $\kappa$-homogeneous structure is $\kappa$-homogeneous.

1. Let $f : A \to M$ be partial elementary with $\abs{A} < \kappa$, and let $b \in M$.
2. By strong homogeneity, $f$ extends to an automorphism $\sigma$.
3. An automorphism preserves every formula, so the restriction of $\sigma$ to $A \cup \set{b}$ is partial elementary and extends $f$.

The trap is the converse: its proof is a back-and-forth construction of length $\abs{M}$, which works only when $\abs{M} \le \kappa$.
