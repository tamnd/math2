## Statement

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, with a norm $\|\cdot\|$.
Then $V$ is a [[Banach space|~44d094ec]].

## Remarks

Proof: by [[equivalence of norms in finite dimension|~7c2c8f85]], the norm is equivalent to the sup norm of the coordinates in a basis. Equivalent norms have the same Cauchy sequences and the same convergent sequences. With the sup norm, completeness follows from the completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ in each coordinate.
A consequence: each finite-dimensional subspace of a normed space is complete, and thus [[closed|~71d47767]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In finite dimensions, a Cauchy sequence is Cauchy in each coordinate, so each coordinate converges.
This works for each norm, because all norms are equivalent there.
