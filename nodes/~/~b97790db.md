## Example

[[Intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]] uses the connectives $\neg, \land, \lor, \to$ of classical logic, so it has the same formulas.
We show that it is a [[deviation|~b095f207]]: the classical tautology $p \lor \neg p$ is not intuitionistically valid.
We use [[Kripke semantics for intuitionistic logic|35VKJY77TY]].

Take the frame with two worlds $w_0 \le w_1$, where $\le$ is reflexive.
Let $p$ be forced at $w_1$ only.

1. The forcing of $p$ is persistent: if $p$ is forced at a world, it is forced at every later world. The only world where $p$ is forced is $w_1$, which has no later world except itself. So this is a Kripke model.
2. At $w_0$, $p$ is not forced, by the choice of the model.
3. A world forces $\neg p$ when no later world forces $p$. The world $w_1 \ge w_0$ forces $p$, so $w_0$ does not force $\neg p$.
4. A world forces $p \lor \neg p$ when it forces $p$ or forces $\neg p$. By steps 2 and 3, $w_0$ forces neither.
5. So $p \lor \neg p$ is not forced at $w_0$, and by soundness of Kripke semantics it is not provable in intuitionistic logic.
6. In classical logic, $p \lor \neg p$ is the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]], a tautology.

So the two logics have the same formulas and different valid formulas.

## Remarks

Intuitionistic logic proves no formula that classical logic does not prove.
So it is a deviation that is weaker than classical logic.
This is the most common kind of deviation, but not the only one: connexive logics prove some classical non-theorems.
If we add a third world, nothing changes for $p \lor \neg p$; the failure needs only one world where $p$ is undecided.
If the frame has one world only, intuitionistic forcing becomes classical truth, and $p \lor \neg p$ holds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In intuitionistic logic, "true" means "proved so far".
Think of two moments: today, when we have no proof of a statement $p$, and tomorrow, when we have one.
Today we cannot assert $p$, since it is not yet proved.
We also cannot assert "not $p$", because tomorrow $p$ will be proved.
So today we cannot assert "$p$ or not $p$", although classical logic always allows it.

### L2 human

We repeat the check for $\neg\neg p \to p$ in the same model.

1. A world forces $\neg\neg p$ when no later world forces $\neg p$.
2. At $w_1$, $p$ is forced, so $w_1$ does not force $\neg p$. At $w_0$, $\neg p$ is not forced by step 3 above.
3. So no world forces $\neg p$, and $w_0$ forces $\neg\neg p$.
4. But $w_0$ does not force $p$. So $w_0$ does not force $\neg\neg p \to p$.

The typical use of such a model is to show that a classical law is not intuitionistic.
A trap is to read "not forced" as "false": at $w_0$ the formula $p$ is not forced, but $\neg p$ is not forced either.
