## Example

Let $G = S_3$ be the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] on $\{1, 2, 3\}$.
We compose permutations from right to left, so $\sigma\tau$ means first $\tau$, then $\sigma$.
The subgroups of order $2$ of $S_3$ are
$$H_1 = \{e, (2\,3)\}, \qquad H_2 = \{e, (1\,3)\}, \qquad H_3 = \{e, (1\,2)\}.$$
Each of them is generated by one [[transposition|0FWVRHBRWM]].
We show that they are [[conjugate subgroups|0H11ZPX4ND]], and we count the conjugates of $H_3$.

1. For each $g$, the conjugate $gH_3g^{-1}$ is $\{geg^{-1}, g(1\,2)g^{-1}\} = \{e, g(1\,2)g^{-1}\}$.
2. For $g = (2\,3)$, we get $g(1\,2)g^{-1} = (1\,3)$. So $(2\,3)H_3(2\,3)^{-1} = H_2$.
3. For $g = (1\,3)$, we get $g(1\,2)g^{-1} = (2\,3)$. So $(1\,3)H_3(1\,3)^{-1} = H_1$.
4. So $H_1$, $H_2$ and $H_3$ are conjugate, and $H_3$ has exactly $3$ conjugate subgroups.
5. The [[normaliser|0YGN3VAA0D]] $N_G(H_3)$ is the set of $g$ with $gH_3g^{-1} = H_3$, that is, with $g(1\,2)g^{-1} = (1\,2)$.
6. The table of conjugates of $(1\,2)$ in [[the transpositions of S3 are conjugate|~50e0bc16]] gives $N_G(H_3) = \{e, (1\,2)\} = H_3$.
7. So the [[index|0DTXBQPA2S]] $[G : N_G(H_3)]$ is $6 / 2 = 3$.
8. This index is equal to the number $3$ of conjugates from step 4.

## Remarks

Step 8 is an instance of the theorem that [[the number of conjugates of a subgroup is the index of its normaliser|~10708e3f]].
The three subgroups are isomorphic, as the theory of conjugate subgroups predicts: each one is a group of order $2$.
None of them is normal in $S_3$, because a normal subgroup has only one conjugate, itself.
In contrast, the subgroup $A_3$ of order $3$ has normaliser $S_3$, so it has $[S_3 : S_3] = 1$ conjugate, and it is normal.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each subgroup of order two in $S_3$ holds one swap of two points.
A renaming of the points turns one swap into another swap.
So it also turns one of these subgroups into another one.
This is what "conjugate subgroups" means: subgroups that differ only by a renaming.

### L2 human

The count in steps 5 to 8 is the general method to count conjugates.
Find the normaliser, which is the set of $g$ that move the subgroup to itself.
Then the number of conjugates is the index of the normaliser.
A common error is to divide by the order of the subgroup instead.
Here the two numbers agree, because $N_G(H_3) = H_3$.
For $A_3$ they do not agree: $[S_3 : A_3] = 2$, but $A_3$ has only $1$ conjugate.
