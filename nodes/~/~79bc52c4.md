## Definition

A commutative ring is a [[ring|~447242bf]] $R$ such that $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$ for all $a, b \in R$.

## Remarks

The integers $\mathbb{Z}$, the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$ and the polynomials in one variable over $\mathbb{Q}$ are commutative rings.
The ring of $2 \times 2$ real matrices is not commutative.
Every [[field|~92ce2090]] is a commutative ring.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a commutative ring, the order of the factors in a product does not matter.
The integers are the model: $3 \cdot 5 = 5 \cdot 3$.
