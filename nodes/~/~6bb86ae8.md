## Statement

Let $X$ be a [[topological space|~77bfcc55]] and let $K \subseteq X$.
Then $K$ is [[compact|~56dabfb9]] if and only if each family $(U_i)_{i \in I}$ of open subsets of $X$ with $K \subseteq \bigcup_{i \in I} U_i$ has a finite subfamily $U_{i_1}, \ldots, U_{i_n}$ with $K \subseteq U_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup U_{i_n}$.

## Remarks

Proof: the open sets of the [[subspace topology|~74712f5b]] on $K$ are the sets $U \cap K$ with $U$ open in $X$. So covers of $K$ by open sets of $K$ and covers by open sets of $X$ match each other.
For $K = X$, the statement is the definition of a [[compact space|~68153d33]].
Thus compactness of a subset does not depend on the space around it. If $K \subseteq Y \subseteq X$, then $K$ is compact in $Y$ if and only if it is compact in $X$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To test if a subset is compact, you can use open sets of the big space.
Each time you cover the subset with open sets, finitely many of them must already be enough.
