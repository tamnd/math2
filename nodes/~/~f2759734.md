## Proof

Parts 1 and 2, by induction on $n = \dim V$.

1. If $B = 0$, which includes the case $n = 0$, take $m = 0$ and any basis $(w_1, \dots, w_n)$. Every pair gives $0$, and the matrix is $0_n$.
2. Let $B \neq 0$, and pick $u, z \in V$ with $B(u, z) \neq 0$. Put $u_1 = u$ and $v_1 = z / B(u, z)$. Then $B(u_1, v_1) = 1$ by linearity.
3. $B(v_1, u_1) = -1$, since $B$ is skew-symmetric by the [[relation of alternating and skew-symmetric forms|~205a4a20]]. Also $B(u_1, u_1) = B(v_1, v_1) = 0$, since $B$ is [[alternating|08ZQAKBKHF]].
4. $u_1, v_1$ are independent: if $v_1 = c u_1$, then $B(u_1, v_1) = c B(u_1, u_1) = 0 \neq 1$; and $u_1 \neq 0$ since $B(u_1, v_1) \neq 0$.
5. So $H = \operatorname{span}(u_1, v_1)$ has dimension $2$, and step 3 gives the matrix $J$ of $B$ on $H$ in the basis $(u_1, v_1)$.
6. $\det J = 1 \neq 0$, so $B$ is nondegenerate on $H$ by the [[criteria for nondegeneracy|~acb831c2]].
7. By part 3 of the [[dimension statement for orthogonal complements|~fb9287d6]], $V = H \oplus H^\perp$, and $\dim H^\perp = n - 2$.
8. The restriction of $B$ to $H^\perp$ is alternating. By the induction hypothesis, $H^\perp$ has a basis $(u_2, v_2, \dots, u_m, v_m, w_1, \dots, w_k)$ with the properties of part 1.
9. For $x \in H$ and $y \in H^\perp$, $B(x, y) = 0$ by the definition of $H^\perp$, and $B(y, x) = -B(x, y) = 0$ by skew-symmetry.
10. Since $V = H \oplus H^\perp$, the list $(u_1, v_1, u_2, v_2, \dots, w_k)$ is a basis of $V$. By steps 3, 8 and 9, it has the properties of part 1.
11. In this basis, the [[matrix|0P24YW565F]] has the blocks $J$ for each pair $(u_i, v_i)$ and zeros elsewhere, which is part 2.

Part 3.

12. The matrix $J \oplus \dots \oplus J \oplus 0_k$ has $2m$ nonzero rows, which are independent. So its rank is $2m$.
13. The rank of a form does not depend on the basis, by the [[change of basis rule|0SPYAGE7T0]]. So $\rank B = 2m$, which is even.
14. Each $w_j$ pairs to zero with each basis vector, so $w_j$ is in the radical. The radical has dimension $n - \rank B = k$, by the criteria for nondegeneracy, so $(w_1, \dots, w_k)$ is a basis of it.

Part 4.

15. Let $A$ be an alternating $n \times n$ matrix of rank $2m$. The form $x^T A y$ on $F^n$ is alternating, by the matrix criterion in the relation of the two notions.
16. Parts 2 and 3 give an invertible $P$, with the new basis as its columns, such that $P^T A P = S$, where $S = J \oplus \dots \oplus J \oplus 0$ with $m$ blocks. So $A$ is [[congruent|NZJDNC6EP6]] to $S$.
17. Two alternating matrices of the same rank $2m$ are both congruent to the same $S$. [[Congruence is an equivalence relation|~12295b2d]], so they are congruent to each other.
18. Conversely, congruent matrices have the same rank, by the properties of congruence.

Part 5.

19. If $B$ is [[nondegenerate|0KAN330PAH]], then $\rank B = n$ by the criteria for nondegeneracy. By part 3, $n = 2m$ is even.

Part 6.

20. Let $A$ be alternating, and $P^T A P = S$ as in step 16. Then $\det S = (\det P)^2 \det A$, by the properties of congruence.
21. If $\rank A < n$, then $\det A = 0 = 0^2$.
22. If $\rank A = n$, then $S = J \oplus \dots \oplus J$ and $\det S = (\det J)^m = 1$. So $\det A = (\det P)^{-2} = \bigl((\det P)^{-1}\bigr)^2$, a square.

This proves all six parts.

## Idea

A nonzero alternating form has a pair $u, v$ with $B(u, v) = 1$.
This pair spans a plane on which the form is nondegenerate, so the plane splits off with its orthogonal complement.
Induction on the dimension then gives the whole basis, and the shape of the matrix gives the rank and the determinant.

## Variants

One can phrase the proof as symmetric row and column operations on the matrix: use a nonzero entry $a_{ij}$ to clear row $i$ and column $j$, then move the pair to the top left.
This is the alternating version of Gaussian elimination, and it gives an algorithm.
A second proof of part 6 uses the [[Pfaffian|C9JJD2AVJR]] and the identity $\operatorname{pf}(A)^2 = \det A$.
