## Example

Let $\varphi_1(x) = x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + x_4$ and $\varphi_2(x) = x_1 - x_2$ be [[linear forms|0RT96J4D3H]] on $\R^4$, and let $U = \ker \varphi_1$ and $W = \ker \varphi_2$ be their [[kernels|02YFME3RGW]].
We compute the codimensions of $U$, $W$ and $U \cap W$.

1. $\varphi_1$ is surjective onto $\R$, since $\varphi_1(t, 0, 0, 0) = t$. So $\R^4 / U \cong \R$, and $\operatorname{codim} U = 1$. The same holds for $W$.
2. $U \ne W$: the vector $(1, -1, 0, 0)$ lies in $U$, but $\varphi_2(1, -1, 0, 0) = 2 \ne 0$.
3. So $U + W$ strictly contains the hyperplane $U$. Its dimension is more than $3$, so $U + W = \R^4$.
4. By the [[codimension of an intersection|~6fd4efc3]], $\operatorname{codim}(U \cap W) = 1 + 1 = 2$.
5. Direct check: the map $x \mapsto (\varphi_1(x), \varphi_2(x))$ is surjective onto $\R^2$, since $(a, b)$ is the image of $\big(\tfrac{a + b}{2}, \tfrac{a - b}{2}, 0, 0\big)$. Its kernel is $U \cap W$. So $\R^4 / (U \cap W) \cong \R^2$.
6. A basis of $U \cap W$ is $(1, 1, -2, 0)$, $(1, 1, 0, -2)$, so $\dim(U \cap W) = 2 = 4 - 2$.

## Remarks

The [[quotient space|0SVR29SV1V]] $\R^4 / U$ is identified with $\R$ by $\varphi_1$, so each coset of $U$ is a parallel hyperplane $\varphi_1 = c$.
If we replace $\varphi_2$ by $2 \varphi_1$, then $W = U$ and the intersection has codimension $1$, not $2$.
In the infinite-dimensional space $\R[x]$, the forms $p \mapsto p(0)$ and $p \mapsto p(1)$ give two hyperplanes whose intersection $\{p : p(0) = p(1) = 0\}$ has codimension $2$, with the same proof.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each hyperplane is one linear equation.
Two independent equations cut out a subspace that misses two directions.

### L2 human

The test in step 2 checks that the two forms are not proportional.
For two forms, being non-proportional is the same as being independent, and independent forms always add their codimensions.
