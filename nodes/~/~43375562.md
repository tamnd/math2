## Example

Fix $n$ and $2 \leq k \leq n$.
The shared variables are $p_{uv}$ for $1 \leq u < v \leq n$, and we write $p_{vu} = p_{uv}$.
An assignment $a$ of them is the graph $G_a$ on $\set{1, \dots, n}$ with the edges $\set{u, v}$ for which $a_{uv} = 1$.

The CNF $\mathrm{Clique}(p, q)$ has the variables $q_{i,u}$ for $i \leq k$ and $u \leq n$, read as "the $i$-th vertex of the clique is $u$", and these clauses.

1. $q_{i,1} \vee \cdots \vee q_{i,n}$ for each $i \leq k$.
2. $\neg q_{i,u} \vee \neg q_{j,u}$ for all $i < j$ and all $u$.
3. $\neg q_{i,u} \vee \neg q_{j,v} \vee p_{uv}$ for all $i \neq j$ and all $u \neq v$.

The CNF $\mathrm{Colour}(p, r)$ has the variables $r_{u,c}$ for $u \leq n$ and $c \leq k - 1$, read as "vertex $u$ has colour $c$", and these clauses.

4. $r_{u,1} \vee \cdots \vee r_{u,k-1}$ for each $u$.
5. $\neg r_{u,c} \vee \neg r_{v,c} \vee \neg p_{uv}$ for all $u < v$ and all $c$.

We check the conditions of the [[interpolation theorem for resolution|~902cba69]], part 2, and draw the conclusion.

1. **Disjoint private variables.** The $q_{i,u}$ occur only in $\mathrm{Clique}$, and the $r_{u,c}$ only in $\mathrm{Colour}$.
2. **Sign condition.** The variables $p_{uv}$ occur in $\mathrm{Clique}$ only in the clauses of type 3, and there they are positive.
3. **Meaning of the first side.** $\mathrm{Clique}(a, q)$ is satisfiable exactly when $G_a$ has a [[clique|0BYX1D7C2E]] with $k$ vertices. Given a clique $\set{u_1, \dots, u_k}$, set $q_{i, u_i} = 1$ and all other $q$ to $0$. Conversely, choose for each $i$ one $u_i$ with $q_{i, u_i} = 1$. Clauses 2 make the $u_i$ distinct, and clauses 3 make them pairwise adjacent.
4. **Meaning of the second side.** $\mathrm{Colour}(a, r)$ is satisfiable exactly when $G_a$ has a proper [[colouring|0NCPXH194Y]] with $k - 1$ colours: give $u$ one colour $c$ with $r_{u,c} = 1$.
5. **Unsatisfiability.** A graph with a $k$-clique needs $k$ colours, so $\mathrm{Clique} \wedge \mathrm{Colour}$ is unsatisfiable.
6. **The interpolant.** Let $\pi$ be a [[resolution refutation|~586b5042]] with $L$ clauses. The theorem gives a monotone circuit $I$ with at most $4L$ gates. If $G_a$ has a $k$-clique, $\mathrm{Clique}(a, q)$ is satisfiable, so $I(a) = 1$. If $G_a$ is $(k-1)$-colourable, $\mathrm{Colour}(a, r)$ is satisfiable, so $I(a) = 0$.
7. **A circuit lower bound.** A monotone Boolean circuit is a monotone real circuit, with AND as the minimum and OR as the maximum, and constant inputs can be absorbed into the gates. Take $k = \floor{(n / \log_2 n)^{2/3} / 8}$, with $n$ large. Part 1 of [[Pudlák's theorem|~8976ef2c]] gives that $I$ has $2^{\Omega((n / \log n)^{1/3})}$ gates.
8. **Conclusion.** So $4L \geq 2^{\Omega((n / \log n)^{1/3})}$, and every resolution refutation of $\mathrm{Clique} \wedge \mathrm{Colour}$ has $2^{\Omega((n / \log n)^{1/3})}$ clauses.

## Remarks

The measure is the number of clauses of a resolution refutation.
The CNF has $N$ variables with $n(n-1)/2 \leq N \leq 3n^2$, so the bound is $2^{\Omega((N^{1/2} / \log N)^{1/3})}$ in terms of $N$.

The interpolant need not compute the clique function itself.
It only has to answer $1$ on graphs with a $k$-clique and $0$ on $(k-1)$-colourable graphs, and it may answer either way on other graphs.
So the lower bound needs a monotone bound for every such separating function, as in Pudlák's part 1.
The form of [[Razborov's lower bound|2XH5VDJ426]] stated for the clique function does not apply directly, while Pudlák's part 1 covers every separating function.

For [[cutting planes|~f9bde1dc]], part 2 of Pudlák's theorem gives the same lower bound for a system of inequalities with the same meaning.
The route above needs only Boolean monotone circuits, since the interpolant of a resolution refutation is Boolean.

## Explanations

### L1 human

One side says "this graph has a group of $k$ vertices that are all joined", and the other side says "this graph can be coloured with $k - 1$ colours".
Both cannot be true, and a resolution proof shows this.
From the proof one can build a small machine with AND and OR gates that tells which claim fails for a given graph.
Such machines are known to be huge, so the proof must be huge too.

### L2 human

The argument has three steps.

1. The theorem turns a refutation with $L$ clauses into a monotone circuit with at most $4L$ gates.
2. The circuit separates the graphs with a $k$-clique from the $(k-1)$-colourable graphs.
3. Every monotone circuit that separates them has $2^{\Omega((n / \log n)^{1/3})}$ gates, for the value of $k$ above.

So $L$ is at least $2^{\Omega((n / \log n)^{1/3})}$.
The same pattern works for every system with monotone feasible interpolation.
A trap is to put the edge variables negatively on the clique side: then the sign condition fails, and the theorem gives no monotone circuit.
