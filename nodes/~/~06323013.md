## Example

The [[inequality of arithmetic and geometric means|0DRF0F3XC9]] for two numbers states:
$$\sqrt{ab} \le \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b) \quad \text{for all real } a, b \ge 0.$$
We show that the constant $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is [[sharp|~07c20fd3]], and we find all equality cases.

1. The bound holds. For $a, b \ge 0$ the square roots $\sqrt{a}$ and $\sqrt{b}$ are real, and $(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2 \ge 0$. Expanding gives $a - 2\sqrt{ab} + b \ge 0$, which is the inequality.
2. The bound is attained. At $a = b = 1$, the left side is $\sqrt{1} = 1$ and the right side is $\tfrac{1}{2}(1 + 1) = 1$.
3. No smaller constant works. Let $c < \tfrac{1}{2}$. At $a = b = 1$, the bound $\sqrt{ab} \le c(a + b)$ would say $1 \le 2c$, but $2c < 1$.
4. The equality cases. Equality in step 1 holds exactly when $(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2 = 0$, that is, when $\sqrt{a} = \sqrt{b}$, that is, when $a = b$.

So the constant $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is best possible, and the extremal cases are the pairs $(a, a)$ with $a \ge 0$.

## Remarks

The example shows the usual pattern of a sharpness proof: a proof of the bound, then one equality case that blocks every smaller constant.
The equality case $a = b = 0$ also blocks an additive improvement: the bound $\sqrt{ab} \le \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b)$ cannot be replaced by $\sqrt{ab} \le \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b) - \varepsilon$ for any fixed $\varepsilon > 0$, because it would give $0 \le -\varepsilon$.
For $n$ numbers, the constant $\tfrac{1}{n}$ in $\sqrt[n]{x_1 \cdots x_n} \le \tfrac{1}{n}(x_1 + \dots + x_n)$ is sharp for the same reason: all $x_i = 1$ gives equality.
If we restrict the class to pairs with $a \ge 2b > 0$, the equality case $a = b$ is no longer allowed, and the constant $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is no longer attained on that class.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The average of two numbers is never smaller than their geometric mean.
For $4$ and $9$, the average is $6.5$ and the geometric mean is $\sqrt{36} = 6$.
For $5$ and $5$, both means equal $5$.
Because the two sides can be equal, no better general bound of this kind exists.

### L2 human

A rectangle with sides $a$ and $b$ has area $ab$ and perimeter $2(a + b)$.

1. Fix the perimeter $P = 2(a + b)$. Then $a + b = P/2$.
2. By the inequality, $ab \le \tfrac{1}{4}(a + b)^2 = P^2/16$.
3. The square with $a = b = P/4$ has area $P^2/16$, so the bound is attained.

The typical use is in optimization: a sharp inequality with an equality case gives the maximum, and the equality case gives the point where the maximum is reached.
A trap: the equality case must lie in the class; here it must satisfy $a, b \ge 0$, and the square with side $P/4$ does.
