## Statement

Let $K$ be a [[field|~92ce2090]] and let $f \in K[x]$ with $\deg f \ge 1$.
Then there is a [[field extension|~05511c4e]] $L/K$ with $[L : K] \le \deg f$ such that $f$ has a [[root|~4d191b0b]] in $L$.

## Remarks

The proof: let $g$ be an irreducible factor of $f$ in $K[x]$. [[Adjoining a root of $g$|~533a9b84]] gives the field $L = K[x]/(g)$ of degree $\deg g$ over $K$.
The class of $x$ in $L$ is a root of $g$, and so of $f$.
The irreducible factor $g$ exists because $K[x]$ is a [[unique factorisation domain|~0e18a953]].
Repeating the step gives a [[splitting field|~8d6926f1]] of $f$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A polynomial with no root in a field gets a root in a bigger field.
The bigger field is built from the polynomial itself, without any reference to the complex numbers.
