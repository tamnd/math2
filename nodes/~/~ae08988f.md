## Proof

We prove part 1; part 2 is the case without the constants $e_m$, see step 12.

1. The language. Let $L^*$ be $L$ with new constants $e_m$ for $m \in M$, $c_a$ for $a \in N_1 \setminus f_1(M)$, and $d_b$ for $b \in N_2 \setminus f_2(M)$, all distinct.
2. Names. In $\mathcal{N}_1$ the name of $f_1(m)$ is $e_m$ and the name of any other $a$ is $c_a$. In $\mathcal{N}_2$ the name of $f_2(m)$ is $e_m$ and the name of any other $b$ is $d_b$.
3. If $f_1(m) = f_1(m')$, then $m = m'$, because an elementary embedding is injective. So each element of $\mathcal{N}_1$ has one name, and the same holds for $\mathcal{N}_2$.
4. Let $\Delta_1$ be the [[elementary diagram|~4a49d7ae]] of $\mathcal{N}_1$ written with these names, and $\Delta_2$ that of $\mathcal{N}_2$. Put $\Gamma = \Delta_1 \cup \Delta_2$.
5. Reduction. $\Delta_2$ is closed under conjunction. So by the [[compactness theorem|1NR3HAWG9D]] it is enough to satisfy $\Delta_1 \cup \set{\psi}$ for each $\psi \in \Delta_2$.
6. Write $\psi = \psi(\bar e, \bar d)$, where $\bar e$ are constants $e_m$ for a tuple $\bar m$ of $M$ and $\bar d$ are distinct constants $d_b$ for a tuple $\bar b$. Then $\mathcal{N}_2 \models \psi(f_2(\bar m), \bar b)$.
7. So $\mathcal{N}_2 \models \exists \bar y \, \psi(f_2(\bar m), \bar y)$. The map $f_2$ is [[elementary|CGH8C8EA9N]], so $\mathcal{M} \models \exists \bar y \, \psi(\bar m, \bar y)$.
8. The map $f_1$ is elementary, so $\mathcal{N}_1 \models \exists \bar y \, \psi(f_1(\bar m), \bar y)$. Choose a witness tuple $\bar a'$ in $N_1$.
9. Expand $\mathcal{N}_1$ to $L^*$: names of step 2 for $e_m$ and $c_a$, the entries of $\bar a'$ for $\bar d$, and any element for the other $d_b$. This expansion satisfies $\Delta_1$ by the definition of the diagram, and $\psi$ by step 8.
10. So $\Gamma$ has a model $\mathcal{N}^*$, and by compactness we can take $\abs{N^*} \le \abs{L^*} + \aleph_0 \le \abs{N_1} + \abs{N_2} + \abs{L} + \aleph_0$.
11. Let $\mathcal{N}$ be the $L$-reduct of $\mathcal{N}^*$. Let $g_1$ send each element of $N_1$ to the value of its name, and $g_2$ the same for $N_2$. By the [[diagram lemma|~4a49d7ae]], part 2, $g_1$ and $g_2$ are elementary embeddings. Both send $f_1(m)$ and $f_2(m)$ to the value of $e_m$, so $g_1 \circ f_1 = g_2 \circ f_2$.
12. Part 2. Use no constants $e_m$. In step 7, $\psi(\bar d)$ is a sentence with constants only from $N_2$, so $\mathcal{N}_2 \models \exists \bar y \, \psi(\bar y)$, and $\mathcal{N}_1 \models \exists \bar y \, \psi(\bar y)$ because $\mathcal{N}_1 \equiv \mathcal{N}_2$. Steps 9 to 11 are unchanged.
13. Inclusion. Replace each $g_1(a)$ by $a$, and the other elements of $N$ by new objects. The result is [[isomorphic|PZMGQMDN25]] to $\mathcal{N}$, and $g_1$ becomes the inclusion.

## Idea

The elementary diagrams of $\mathcal{N}_1$ and $\mathcal{N}_2$ describe elementary extensions of each, and the shared constants $e_m$ force the two copies of $\mathcal{M}$ to coincide.
To see that the two diagrams fit together, compactness reduces to one sentence $\psi$ about $\mathcal{N}_2$.
Its existential closure $\exists \bar y \, \psi$ is a statement about $\mathcal{M}$, which moves to $\mathcal{N}_1$ by elementarity and gives witnesses there.

## Variants

The same proof amalgamates any family of elementary extensions of $\mathcal{M}$: take one diagram for each member, with the constants $e_m$ shared.
A second proof uses saturation: if $\mathcal{N}_1$ is replaced by a $\abs{N_2}^+$-saturated elementary extension, then $\mathcal{N}_2$ embeds into it over $\mathcal{M}$ by extending [[partial elementary maps|~7e54c18a]] one element at a time.
