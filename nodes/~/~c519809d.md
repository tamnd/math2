## Proof

We prove the theorem assuming the generalized [[continuum hypothesis|0MN2MER9WN]], $2^\lambda = \lambda^+$ for every infinite cardinal $\lambda$, as Keisler did.
The "if" direction holds in ZFC: by the theorem that the [[diagonal embedding is elementary|~50eaa729]], $\mathcal{M} \equiv \mathcal{M}^I / \mathcal{U} \cong \mathcal{N}^I / \mathcal{U} \equiv \mathcal{N}$.
For the "only if" direction, let $\mathcal{M} \equiv \mathcal{N}$.

1. *Finite case.* Let $M$ be finite with $k$ elements. The sentence "exactly $k$ elements" holds in $\mathcal{M}$, so it holds in $\mathcal{N}$.
2. For a finite part $L_0$ of $L$, one sentence describes the $L_0$-reduct of $\mathcal{M}$ up to isomorphism. It holds in $\mathcal{N}$, so some bijection $M \to N$ respects $L_0$.
3. There are finitely many bijections $M \to N$, and the set of those that respect $L_0$ shrinks as $L_0$ grows. So one bijection respects all of $L$, and $\mathcal{N} \cong \mathcal{M}$.
4. So in the finite case any $I$ and $\mathcal{U}$ work.
5. *Infinite case.* Now $M$ and $N$ are infinite. Choose an infinite cardinal $\lambda \ge \abs{L} + \abs{M} + \abs{N}$.
6. Choose a regular, hence countably incomplete, $\lambda^+$-good ultrafilter $\mathcal{D}$ on $\lambda$. Keisler built one under $2^\lambda = \lambda^+$, and Kunen built one in ZFC.
7. By [[saturation of ultraproducts|~bf3451a3]], part 2, both $\mathcal{M}^\lambda / \mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{N}^\lambda / \mathcal{D}$ are $\lambda^+$-saturated.
8. *Upper bound on size.* $\abs{M^\lambda / \mathcal{D}} \le \abs{M}^\lambda \le \lambda^\lambda = 2^\lambda = \lambda^+$, by the hypothesis. The same holds for $N$.
9. *Lower bound on size.* Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an infinite $\lambda^+$-saturated structure, and suppose $\abs{A} \le \lambda$.
10. The set of formulas $\set{x \neq a : a \in A}$ uses at most $\lambda$ parameters, and each finite part is realized, since $A$ is infinite.
11. By saturation it is realized by some element of $A$, which then differs from itself. So $\abs{A} \ge \lambda^+$.
12. By steps 8 to 11, both ultrapowers have size exactly $\lambda^+$, so they are [[saturated|RMTZ5J64FX]] structures of size $\lambda^+$.
13. By [[Łoś's theorem|AGYBSTD7BG]] and $\mathcal{M} \equiv \mathcal{N}$, the two ultrapowers are [[elementarily equivalent|P3B1V182N4]].
14. Two elementarily equivalent saturated structures of the same size are isomorphic, by a back-and-forth construction of length $\lambda^+$.
15. So $\mathcal{M}^\lambda / \mathcal{D} \cong \mathcal{N}^\lambda / \mathcal{D}$, with $I = \lambda$ and $\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{D}$.

This proves the theorem under the generalized continuum hypothesis.

## Idea

Good ultrafilters make ultrapowers as saturated as their size allows.
Under $2^\lambda = \lambda^+$, the ultrapowers of $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{N}$ are saturated of the same size $\lambda^+$, and saturated models are determined by their theory and their size.
The continuum hypothesis enters only through the size computation in step 8.

## Variants

Shelah's proof in ZFC, "Every two elementarily equivalent models have isomorphic ultrapowers" (1971), uses an ultrafilter on $2^\lambda$ and a long transfinite construction.
It does not pass through saturated models of size $\lambda^+$, since $2^\lambda$ may be larger than $\lambda^+$.
Keisler's first proof of 1961 also used the generalized continuum hypothesis, with a different construction.
