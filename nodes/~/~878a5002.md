## Example

The [[field of fractions|~38dbd12e]] of the [[ring of integers|~afe0a38f]] $\mathbb{Z}$ is the field $\mathbb{Q}$ of rational numbers.
Each element of $\operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{Z})$ is a class $a/b$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $b \neq 0$.
Two pairs give the same class, $a/b = c/d$, if and only if $a d = b c$.
The map $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}$, $n \mapsto n/1$, is the usual inclusion of the integers in the rationals.

## Remarks

Every rational number has a unique form $a/b$ with $b > 0$ and $\gcd(a, b) = 1$.
Every subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ contains $\mathbb{Z}$, so it contains $\mathbb{Q}$, by the universal property of the field of fractions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rational numbers are exactly the fractions of integers.
The field of fractions makes this precise for any integral domain.
The fractions $1/2$, $2/4$ and $-3/(-6)$ all name the same rational number.
