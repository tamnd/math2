## Summary

Thermodynamics and statistical mechanics study the bulk properties of matter and their relation to heat, work and temperature.
Thermodynamics gives laws for macroscopic quantities such as energy, entropy and temperature, and statistical mechanics derives these quantities from a probability distribution on the microscopic states of a system.
The theory explains the behaviour of gases, liquids, solids and magnets, the phase transitions between them, and the approach of a system to equilibrium.
Thermodynamics grew from the work of Carnot, Clausius and Kelvin on heat engines in the nineteenth century, and statistical mechanics started with the kinetic theory of Maxwell and Boltzmann and the ensembles of Gibbs.
This topic collects the thermodynamics and statistical mechanics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Random structures such as percolation and spin glasses, ergodic theory, the partial differential equations of heat and of kinetic theory, and quantum theory have their own topics.
