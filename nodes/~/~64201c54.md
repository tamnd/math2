## Summary

Real analysis is the rigorous theory of real numbers, real sequences and real functions, with full proofs of the results that calculus uses.
Its starting point is the completeness of the real numbers: every nonempty set of real numbers that has an upper bound has a least upper bound.
Real analysis gives proofs for the results of calculus, and its counterexamples show why each hypothesis of a theorem is necessary.
Bolzano and Cauchy started the rigorous theory of limits and continuity in the early 19th century, and Weierstrass gave it the epsilon-delta form that is in use today.
This topic collects the real analysis pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Calculus, measure and integration, inequalities and sequences, series and summability have their own topics.
