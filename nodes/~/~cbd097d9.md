## Proof

We prove part (a) in full from two cited facts about $\mathrm{ID}_n$, and we give parts (b) and (c) as a chain of cited lemmas from Buchholz's method of the $\Omega$-rule.
Write $o(a)$ for the ordinal of a term $a \in \mathrm{OT}$, and $\tau$ for the term of $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$.

Part (a): the lower bound.

1. Interpretation of the operator sets. Let $A(X, x)$ be an $X$-positive arithmetical formula, possibly with set parameters. Define $I = \set{x : \forall X \, (\forall y \, (A(X, y) \to y \in X) \to x \in X)}$. The defining formula is $\Pi^1_1$, so $I$ is a set by $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension.
2. $I$ is closed under $A$. Let $A(I, x)$ hold, and let $X$ be closed under $A$. Then $I \subseteq X$ by the definition of $I$, so $A(X, x)$ holds by [[monotonicity|0A7KV2WB7A]] of positive formulas. So $x \in X$, and hence $x \in I$.
3. $I$ is contained in every closed set given by a formula. A formula of $\mathrm{ID}_n$ becomes, after the interpretation, an arithmetical formula with set parameters. So it defines a set by arithmetical comprehension, which $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ contains, and the definition of $I$ applies to this set.
4. Iteration. A proof in $\mathrm{ID}_n$ uses finitely many predicates $P_A$. For each of them and each stage $0, 1, \dots, n - 1$, apply step 1, with the sets of the earlier stages as parameters. The induction axioms of $\mathrm{ID}_n$ hold, because each formula defines a set and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ has induction for sets. The scheme of transfinite induction along a finite order holds by ordinary induction.
5. So $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ proves the interpretation of each theorem of [[ID_n|5VNT564Y2W]], for each $n \ge 1$.
6. Cited fact 1 (Buchholz, Pohlers): $|\mathrm{ID}_n| = \psi_0(\Omega_{n+1})$, and the well-ordering proof gives, for each term $a$ with $o(a) < \psi_0(\Omega_{n+1})$, a proof in $\mathrm{ID}_n$ that $a$ lies in the [[accessible part|~dbbd338b]] $\mathrm{Acc}$ of $\prec$.
7. Cited fact 2: $|\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}| = \psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$. Since $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ is the union of the theories $\mathrm{ID}_n$, the ordinal $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$ is the supremum of the ordinals $\psi_0(\Omega_{n+1})$.
8. Let $a \prec \tau$. Then $o(a) < \psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$, so $o(a) < \psi_0(\Omega_{n+1})$ for some $n \ge 1$, by step 7.
9. By steps 5 and 6, $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ proves $a \in \mathrm{Acc}^*$, where $\mathrm{Acc}^*$ is the set of step 1 for $A(X, x) = \forall y \, (y \prec x \to y \in X)$.
10. A set $X$ is closed under this operator exactly when it is progressive, as in $\mathrm{WO}(a)$. Steps 2 and 3 make $\mathrm{Acc}^*$ a fixed point, as in [[ID₁|5VNT564Y2W]], so $y \in \mathrm{Acc}^*$ for all $y \prec a$. For progressive $X$, step 1 gives $\mathrm{Acc}^* \subseteq X$.
11. So $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ proves $\forall y \, (y \prec a \to y \in X)$ for every progressive $X$, which is $\mathrm{WO}(a)$.

Parts (b) and (c): the upper bound.
The lemmas of steps 12 to 16 are Buchholz's, from Lecture Notes in Mathematics 897 (1981) and later papers; we state them without proof.

12. Embedding lemma (cited). Each derivation in $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ of a $\Pi^1_1$ sentence translates into a derivation in an infinitary sequent calculus. The calculus has the [[ω-rule|~e0034cfd]] and, for each $\mu < \omega$, an $\Omega_{\mu+1}$-rule that handles the instances of $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension. The height and the cut rank of the translation are bounded by terms of $\mathrm{OT}$ below $\Omega_\omega$.
13. Predicative cut elimination (cited). Cuts of a fixed rank can be removed from such a derivation, with a height that grows from $\alpha$ to $\omega^\alpha$, as in [[cut elimination|AYRZ98Y0NW]] for the ω-rule.
14. Collapsing lemma (cited). A derivation of height $\alpha$ that uses the $\Omega_{\mu+1}$-rule, and whose end sequent has low complexity, becomes a derivation of height $\psi_\mu(\alpha)$ without that rule. This is where [[Buchholz's functions|E7WEFRMQTB]] enter.
15. Applying steps 13 and 14 finitely often, from the highest level $\mu$ down to $\mu = 0$, gives: every $\Pi^1_1$ theorem of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ has a cut-free derivation with the ω-rule alone, of height $o(b)$ for some term $b \prec \tau$.
16. Boundedness lemma (cited). If $\mathrm{WO}(a)$ has a cut-free ω-derivation of height $\beta$, then $o(a)$ is at most an ordinal computed from $\beta$ by a fixed function that maps ordinals below $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$ to ordinals below $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$.
17. Proof of (b), by contradiction. Assume that $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ proves $\mathrm{WO}(\tau)$. By step 15 there is a cut-free derivation of height $o(b) < \psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$. By step 16, $o(\tau) < \psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$. But $o(\tau) = \psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$, a contradiction.
18. Proof of (c), first part. The infinitary derivations of steps 12 to 15 have primitive recursive codes. The reduction steps act primitive recursively on the codes.
19. A derivation of $0 = 1$ in $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ would reduce to a cut-free derivation of the false atomic sentence $0 = 1$. No rule of the cut-free calculus ends in a false atomic sentence.
20. The termination of this reduction is an instance of quantifier-free transfinite induction along $\prec$ below $\tau$. So primitive recursive arithmetic with this induction proves $\mathrm{Con}(\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0)$.
21. Steps 11, 17, 19 and 20 give parts (a), (b) and (c) of the [[statement|~9776e851]].

## Idea

The lower bound reduces $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension to finitely iterated inductive definitions: each inductively defined set is the intersection of all closed sets, which is a $\Pi^1_1$ definition.
The upper bound translates proofs into infinitary derivations, where impredicative comprehension is handled by rules that quantify over all derivations of a lower level.
Cut elimination removes ordinary cuts, and the collapsing functions $\psi_\mu$ remove the impredicative rules one level at a time.
The resulting heights are below $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$, and a derivation of small height cannot prove the well-foundedness of a long ordering.

## Variants

- Takeuti's original proof (1967) reduces derivations of a finite Gentzen-style calculus directly and assigns ordinal diagrams to them. It gives part (c) with ordinal diagrams in place of Buchholz's notation.
- Pohlers's method of [[local predicativity|~af6dfa84]] replaces the $\Omega_{\mu+1}$-rules by a semantic hierarchy of levels and gives the same ordinals.
- Rathjen and Buchholz later used operator-controlled derivations, in which an operator on sets of ordinals records which ordinals may appear. This makes the collapsing lemma shorter and works for stronger theories.
