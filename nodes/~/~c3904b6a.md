## Proof

We write $a, b, c$ for $T(A), T(B), T(C)$.
We use the Hilbert system for $\mathsf{IPC}$ with modus ponens and the axiom schemes

- (I1) $A \to (B \to A)$, (I2) $(A \to (B \to C)) \to ((A \to B) \to (A \to C))$;
- (I3) $A \wedge B \to A$, (I4) $A \wedge B \to B$, (I5) $A \to (B \to A \wedge B)$;
- (I6) $A \to A \vee B$, (I7) $B \to A \vee B$, (I8) $(A \to C) \to ((B \to C) \to (A \vee B \to C))$;
- (I9) $\bot \to A$.

In $\mathsf{S4}$ we use the axioms K, T: $\square p \to p$ and 4: $\square p \to \square \square p$, [[necessitation|G989VP2NGP]], and the derived rule (Mon): from $\vdash X \to Y$ infer $\vdash \square X \to \square Y$.
Two results are cited without proof: completeness of $\mathsf{IPC}$ for intuitionistic Kripke models, and soundness of $\mathsf{S4}$ for reflexive and transitive frames.

Part A: soundness.
Assume $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash A$; we show $\mathsf{S4} \vdash T(A)$.

1. Lemma: $\mathsf{S4} \vdash a \to \square a$ for every $A$. By induction on $A$: for $p$ this is axiom 4, and for $\bot$ it is a tautology.
2. For $\wedge$: from $b \to \square b$ and $c \to \square c$ we get $b \wedge c \to \square b \wedge \square c$, and $\square b \wedge \square c \to \square(b \wedge c)$ holds in every normal logic.
3. For $\vee$: $\square b \to \square(b \vee c)$ and $\square c \to \square(b \vee c)$ by (Mon). For $\to$: $T(B \to C) = \square(b \to c)$, and axiom 4 applies.
4. (I3), (I4), (I6), (I7), (I9): the translation is $\square X$ where $X$ is a tautology, such as $\square(a \wedge b \to a)$. Apply necessitation.
5. (I1): the translation is $\square(a \to \square(b \to a))$. By step 1, $a \to \square a$. By (Mon) on the tautology $a \to (b \to a)$, $\square a \to \square(b \to a)$. Chain the two and apply necessitation.
6. (I5): the translation is $\square(a \to \square(b \to a \wedge b))$. As in step 5, use $a \to \square a$ and (Mon) on $a \to (b \to a \wedge b)$.
7. (I2): let $X = \square(a \to \square(b \to c))$. The translation is $\square(X \to \square(\square(a \to b) \to \square(a \to c)))$.
8. By K, $X \wedge \square(a \to b) \to \square((a \to \square(b \to c)) \wedge (a \to b))$. By axiom T inside, $(a \to \square(b \to c)) \wedge (a \to b) \to (a \to c)$ is a theorem. So by (Mon), $X \wedge \square(a \to b) \to \square(a \to c)$.
9. So $\vdash X \to (\square(a \to b) \to \square(a \to c))$. By (Mon), $\square X \to \square(\square(a \to b) \to \square(a \to c))$. With $X \to \square X$ from axiom 4, and necessitation, the translation of (I2) is a theorem.
10. (I8): let $Y = \square(a \to c)$ and $Z = \square(b \to c)$. The translation is $\square(Y \to \square(Z \to \square(a \vee b \to c)))$.
11. By K and the tautology $(a \to c) \wedge (b \to c) \to (a \vee b \to c)$, $\vdash Y \wedge Z \to \square(a \vee b \to c)$. So $\vdash Y \to (Z \to \square(a \vee b \to c))$.
12. By (Mon) and $Y \to \square Y$ from axiom 4, $\vdash Y \to \square(Z \to \square(a \vee b \to c))$. Necessitation gives the translation of (I8).
13. Modus ponens: assume $\mathsf{S4} \vdash a$ and $\mathsf{S4} \vdash \square(a \to b)$. Axiom T gives $a \to b$, so $\mathsf{S4} \vdash b$.
14. Since $T$ is compositional, the translation of an axiom instance is the formula of steps 4 to 12, with $a, b, c$ the translations of the formulas in the instance. By induction on the length of an $\mathsf{IPC}$ derivation, $\mathsf{S4} \vdash T(A)$.

Part B: faithfulness.
Assume $\mathsf{IPC} \nvdash A$; we show $\mathsf{S4} \nvdash T(A)$.

15. By completeness of $\mathsf{IPC}$, there is an intuitionistic Kripke model $(W, \leq, U)$ and $w \in W$ with $w \nVdash A$. Here $\leq$ is a [[preorder|0VABYWJWH9]], and each $U(p)$ is an up-set.
16. Recall the forcing clauses: $v \Vdash p$ iff $v \in U(p)$; $\bot$ is never forced; $\wedge$ and $\vee$ are pointwise; $v \Vdash B \to C$ iff every $u \geq v$ with $u \Vdash B$ has $u \Vdash C$.
17. Read $(W, \leq, U)$ as a modal Kripke model with $V(p) = U(p)$. Claim: for every $B$ and $v$, $v \Vdash B$ iff $v \models T(B)$.
18. Case $p$: $v \models \square p$ iff every $u \geq v$ is in $U(p)$. Since $U(p)$ is an up-set and $v \geq v$, this holds iff $v \in U(p)$.
19. Cases $\bot$, $\wedge$, $\vee$: the clauses are the same on both sides, and the induction hypothesis applies.
20. Case $B \to C$: $v \models \square(T(B) \to T(C))$ iff every $u \geq v$ with $u \models T(B)$ has $u \models T(C)$. By the induction hypothesis, this is the forcing clause of $B \to C$.
21. So $w \not\models T(A)$ in a model on a reflexive and transitive frame. By soundness of $\mathsf{S4}$ for such frames, $\mathsf{S4} \nvdash T(A)$.

## Idea

Soundness checks that the translation of each intuitionistic axiom is an $\mathsf{S4}$ theorem; the key fact is that translations are stable, $T(A) \to \square T(A)$.
Faithfulness compares two semantics on the same preorder: intuitionistic forcing with up-set valuations is exactly modal truth of the translation.
A countermodel for $A$ is then a countermodel for $T(A)$.

## Variants

McKinsey and Tarski proved faithfulness with topological and algebraic semantics: the open sets of a space form a Heyting algebra, and the translation computes the value of $A$ in this algebra.
Gödel's original translation boxes every subformula; it is equivalent in $\mathsf{S4}$ to the translation used here.
A proof theoretic version uses cut-free sequent calculi for $\mathsf{IPC}$ and $\mathsf{S4}$ and translates derivations in both directions.
