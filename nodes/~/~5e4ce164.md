## Example

Let $R$ be a binary relation symbol.
We show that three common properties of $R$ are expressed by sentences of the [[guarded fragment|~cca08292]].

$$\sigma_{\mathrm{sym}} = \forall x \, \forall y \, (R(x, y) \to R(y, x)),$$
$$\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}} = \forall x \, (x = x \to R(x, x)),$$
$$\sigma_{\mathrm{ser}} = \forall x \, (x = x \to \exists y \, (R(x, y) \wedge y = y)).$$

1. Symmetry. The block $\forall x \, \forall y$ quantifies the two variables $x$ and $y$ at once, with the guard $R(x, y)$. The guard is atomic and contains both quantified variables. The body $R(y, x)$ is atomic, and its free variables $x$ and $y$ occur in the guard.
2. Reflexivity. The quantifier $\forall x$ has the equality guard $x = x$, which contains $x$. The body $R(x, x)$ is atomic with the free variable $x$.
3. Seriality, part 1. The inner formula $\exists y \, (R(x, y) \wedge y = y)$ has guard $R(x, y)$, which contains $y$. Its body $y = y$ has the free variable $y$.
4. Seriality, part 2. The inner formula has the free variable $x$ only. The outer quantifier $\forall x$ has guard $x = x$, which contains $x$.

So $\sigma_{\mathrm{sym}}$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{ser}}$ are guarded sentences.
In each of them, the guard is written out; the plain forms $\forall x \, R(x, x)$ and $\forall x \, \exists y \, R(x, y)$ are equivalent to them, and they are guarded up to this rewriting.

## Remarks

The example shows that symmetry, reflexivity and seriality are guarded frame properties.
So the satisfiability of a modal formula on symmetric, reflexive or serial frames reduces to the satisfiability of a guarded sentence: the translation of the formula, in conjunction with the frame property.
The decidability of the guarded fragment then decides these modal logics, the logics $\mathsf{B}$, $\mathsf{T}$ and $\mathsf{D}$ in the usual names.
Transitivity behaves differently: it needs three variables in one clause, and it is [[not guarded|~d976c997]].
Symmetry of a ternary relation $S$, for example $\forall x \, \forall y \, \forall z \, (S(x, y, z) \to S(y, x, z))$, is also guarded, by the atom $S(x, y, z)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"If Anna is a friend of Ben, then Ben is a friend of Anna" talks about a pair of people that is already linked by friendship.
The link "Anna is a friend of Ben" is the guard: the statement only looks at pairs of friends.
So the property "friendship goes both ways" belongs to the guarded fragment.

### L2 human

We decide whether a symmetric relation can have a point with no successor while some point has one.

1. The sentence is $\sigma_{\mathrm{sym}} \wedge \exists x \, \exists y \, R(x, y) \wedge \exists x \, \forall y \, \neg R(x, y)$.
2. We write each conjunct in guarded form: $\exists x \, \exists y \, (R(x, y) \wedge x = x)$ and $\exists x \, (x = x \wedge \forall y \, (R(x, y) \to \neg (y = y)))$.
3. The model with elements $a, b, c$ and $R = \set{(a, b), (b, a)}$ is symmetric, $a$ has the successor $b$, and $c$ has no successor.
4. So the sentence is satisfiable, and the model is finite, as the finite model property of the guarded fragment predicts.

The typical use is to add frame conditions to a modal translation and stay inside a decidable fragment.
A trap is to think that every universal sentence is guarded; the guard must cover all variables of the body.
