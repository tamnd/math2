## Summary

*Basic fuzzy logic* BL is the propositional logic of all continuous [[t-norms|~da122496]].
Hájek introduced it in his 1998 book with a Hilbert calculus of seven axiom schemes and modus ponens; see [[the definition|~82f3a17c]].
A formula is provable in BL iff it has value $1$ under every evaluation in $[0, 1]$, for every continuous t-norm and its residuum.
BL extends [[MTL|~bb574db9]] by the divisibility axiom.
Łukasiewicz, Gödel and product logic are the extensions of BL by one further axiom each.

## Motivation

Each continuous t-norm gives a logic: its tautologies are the formulas with value $1$ under all evaluations.
BL collects the formulas that these logics have in common.
By the Mostert-Shields theorem, every continuous t-norm is an ordinal sum of copies of the three basic t-norms.
So BL studies the three basic fuzzy logics and their combinations together.

## Notation

The primitive connectives are strong conjunction, implication and $\bot$.
This sub-area writes strong conjunction as $\odot$ in the Łukasiewicz case and $\otimes$ in general BL formulas; Wikipedia writes $\otimes$, and SEP writes $\&$.
Weak conjunction is $A \land B := A \otimes (A \to B)$, and negation is $\neg A := A \to \bot$.
Weak disjunction is $A \lor B := ((A \to B) \to B) \land ((B \to A) \to A)$.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[Divisibility for the three basic t-norms|~9a6f1b0e]]: the standard algebras of the Łukasiewicz, minimum and product t-norms are BL-algebras.
2. The [[ordinal sum of Łukasiewicz and product|~82ec51e1]] is a continuous t-norm, so it gives a standard BL-algebra.
3. Every Boolean algebra is a BL-algebra, with $x \otimes y = x \land y$ and $x \to y = \neg x \lor y$.
4. [[Prelinearity holds for every left-continuous t-norm|~2988a2f8]], so the axiom BL6 is valid in every standard BL-algebra.

### Non-examples

1. [[The nilpotent minimum refutes divisibility|~12981db7]]: it gives an MTL-algebra that is not a BL-algebra.
2. [[The fork refutes prelinearity|6S7TSV01WB]]: the Heyting algebra of a branching Kripke frame is not a model of BL.
3. The drastic t-norm gives no BL-algebra, since it has no residuum; see [[the drastic non-example|~87d02c6c]].

### Basic properties

1. BL is MTL plus the divisibility axiom $A \otimes (A \to B) \to B \otimes (B \to A)$, as SEP states.
2. Łukasiewicz logic is BL plus double negation, Gödel logic is BL plus idempotence of $\otimes$, and [[product logic|~fc3c462d]] is BL plus a cancellation axiom.
3. BL is sound and complete for three classes: all BL-algebras, all linearly ordered BL-algebras, and the standard BL-algebras on $[0, 1]$.
4. The standard BL-algebras are fixed by their strong conjunction, which can be any continuous t-norm.
5. The law of excluded middle $A \lor \neg A$ is not provable: in Gödel logic it has value $\tfrac12$ at $A = \tfrac12$.
6. Weak conjunction is definable in BL, while in MTL it must be a primitive connective.

### History

Wikipedia reports that Hájek introduced BL in his 1998 monograph *Metamathematics of Fuzzy Logic*, with the axioms BL1 to BL7.
That book also treated the three basic t-norm logics, in propositional and first-order form.
Cintula (2005) and Chvalovský (2012) proved that BL2 and BL3 are redundant; Chvalovský proved the other axioms independent.
Esteva and Godo introduced the weaker logic MTL in 2001.

### Common errors

1. Reading $\land$ as the strong conjunction. In BL, $\land$ is the minimum and $\otimes$ is the t-norm; they agree only in Gödel logic.
2. Taking BL to be the logic of one t-norm. BL is the intersection of the logics of all continuous t-norms, and it is strictly weaker than each basic logic.
3. Assuming that BL proves $\neg \neg A \to A$. In Gödel logic this formula has value $\tfrac12$ at $A = \tfrac12$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

There are many ways to compute "and" on degrees of truth, and each way gives a fuzzy logic.
BL collects the laws that hold for every continuous way.
It is a common base: each of the three main fuzzy logics adds one law to it.

### L2 human

To test whether a formula is provable in BL, it is sufficient to test it for every continuous t-norm on $[0, 1]$.
A formula that fails for one basic t-norm is not provable.
For example, $A \to A \otimes A$ holds for the minimum, and it fails for the product at $A = \tfrac12$, where it has value $\tfrac12$.
A trap: a formula valid for one t-norm, such as $\neg \neg A \to A$ for the Łukasiewicz t-norm, need not be in BL.

### L3 human

BL-algebras are the MTL-algebras that satisfy divisibility $x \land y = x \otimes (x \to y)$.
They form a variety, and every BL-algebra is a subdirect product of BL-chains.
Standard completeness reduces validity in all BL-chains to validity in the chains on $[0, 1]$ given by continuous t-norms.
Sub-area 27 treats [[residuated lattices|E337A6WVH3]] and MV-algebras as algebras.
