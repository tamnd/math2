## Example

Let $L = \set{\le}$.
The class of [[total orders|0FRXBVVR03]] is a [[universal class|ZSTEZ3PRT9]], axiomatized by the partial order axioms and totality $\forall x \forall y \, (x \le y \vee y \le x)$.
It is not a [[universal Horn class|9A4CYWXPEA]].

1. Let $\mathbf{2} = \set{0 < 1}$ and form $\mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$ with $(a, b) \le (a', b')$ when $a \le a'$ and $b \le b'$.
2. This is the direct product, the reduced product for the filter $\set{I}$ on $I = \set{1, 2}$.
3. In $\mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$, neither $(0, 1) \le (1, 0)$ nor $(1, 0) \le (0, 1)$ holds.
4. So $\mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$ is not total, and the class is not closed under reduced products.
5. By [[the characterization of universal Horn classes|~b2077580]], no set of universal Horn sentences axiomatizes total orders.

## Remarks

The failure sits in the totality axiom, a clause with two unnegated atoms.
The class is closed under ultraproducts, since an ultrafilter picks one of the two disjuncts on a large set.
The universal Horn sentences true in all total orders axiomatize exactly the partial orders, by part 2 of the characterization.
Indeed, a partial order is the intersection of its linear extensions, by the [[Szpilrajn extension theorem|T9CJHV6JW3]].
So it embeds in the direct product of these chains by the diagonal map.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a total order any two things can be compared, like numbers on a line.
Compare pairs of numbers by "both coordinates are smaller", as for heights and weights of people.
Then a tall light person and a short heavy person cannot be compared.
So "any two can be compared" is lost when one combines orders side by side, and rules of the "if, then" kind can never force it.

### L2 human

We see the role of the ultrafilter by comparing a direct product and an ultraproduct of the same chains.

1. Let $I = \mathbb{N}$, each factor the chain $\mathbf{2}$, and $a = (0, 1, 0, 1, \ldots)$, $b = (1, 0, 1, 0, \ldots)$.
2. In the direct product, $a \le b$ fails at odd places and $b \le a$ fails at even places.
3. In an ultraproduct by an ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$, exactly one of the even and odd places is in $\mathcal{U}$, so $[a]$ and $[b]$ are comparable.

A trap is to order $\mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$ lexicographically; that order is total, but it is not the product structure, whose relation is defined coordinatewise.
