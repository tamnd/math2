## Example

The basic tense logic $\mathsf{K}_t$ is a [[multimodal logic|~d172c20d]] with two boxes.
$G\varphi$ says "$\varphi$ will always be true", and $H\varphi$ says "$\varphi$ has always been true".
Their duals are $F\varphi = \neg G \neg\varphi$, "at some future time", and $P\varphi = \neg H \neg\varphi$, "at some past time".
A bimodal frame $(W, R_G, R_H)$ interprets $G$ by $R_G$ and $H$ by $R_H$.
A tense frame is one where $R_H$ is the converse of $R_G$: $v \mathrel{R_H} w$ iff $w \mathrel{R_G} v$.

1. Claim: on a tense frame, $p \to G P p$ is true at every world under every valuation.
2. Let $w \models p$, and let $w \mathrel{R_G} v$.
3. Then $v \mathrel{R_H} w$, since $R_H$ is the converse of $R_G$.
4. So $v$ has a past world, $w$, where $p$ is true, and $v \models P p$.
5. As $v$ was any future world of $w$, we get $w \models G P p$.
6. The same argument with $G$ and $H$ exchanged shows that $p \to H F p$ is valid on tense frames.

Now drop the converse condition.

7. Take $W = \set{a, b}$, $R_G = \set{(a, b)}$ and $R_H = \emptyset$, and $V(p) = \set{a}$.
8. $p$ is true at $a$.
9. The only $R_G$-successor of $a$ is $b$, and $b$ has no $R_H$-successor, so $P p$ is false at $b$.
10. So $G P p$ is false at $a$, and $p \to G P p$ fails at $a$.

Steps 2 to 4 also give the exact frame condition.

11. Suppose $w \mathrel{R_G} v$ but not $v \mathrel{R_H} w$, and set $V(p) = \set{w}$.
12. Then $P p$ at $v$ needs a world $u$ with $v \mathrel{R_H} u$ and $u = w$, which does not exist.
13. So $p \to G P p$ fails at $w$; it is valid on a frame iff $R_G$ is contained in the converse of $R_H$.

## Remarks

$\mathsf{K}_t$ is the smallest bimodal normal logic that contains $p \to G P p$ and $p \to H F p$.
By steps 1 to 6 and [[soundness|~d189ee4b]], extended to two boxes with the same proof, its theorems are valid on every tense frame.
The two axioms are interaction axioms: each mentions both boxes, so $\mathsf{K}_t$ is larger than the fusion $\mathsf{K} \otimes \mathsf{K}$.
The model of step 7 is a model of $\mathsf{K} \otimes \mathsf{K}$, so step 10 shows that the fusion does not prove $p \to G P p$.
The relation $R_G$ is a [[binary relation|05AVARPXA2]] on times; adding transitivity or linearity gives the logics of [[temporal modal logic|8XB117FKKV]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

"If it rains now, then at every later time it will be true that it rained once."
This holds when "later" and "earlier" are two views of one time line.
If the past relation forgets the present, the law can fail: a later time may not look back to now.
The two-world model shows such a failure.

### L2 human

1. Assume $w \models p$ and $w \mathrel{R_G} v$.
2. Use $v \mathrel{R_H} w$ to get $v \models P p$.
3. Conclude $w \models G P p$.

The typical use is to see that interaction axioms express a link between the relations of different boxes.
A trap: on a tense frame, $R_H$ is determined by $R_G$, so the frame is really one relation used in two directions.

### L3 human

Prior introduced the operators $F$, $P$, $G$ and $H$ in the 1950s and 1960s.
In [[multimodal logic|~d172c20d]], tense logic is the basic case of a logic with a converse modality.
Its frames are one relation read forward and backward.
