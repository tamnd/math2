## Example

We solve the equation
$$x^4 + x^3 - 4x^2 + x + 1 = 0$$
over the real numbers by a [[symmetry argument|~dfa60ea2]].
The coefficients $1, 1, -4, 1, 1$ read the same forwards and backwards, so the polynomial is palindromic.

1. The number $0$ is not a solution, because the left side at $x = 0$ is $1$. So each solution is nonzero.
2. Let $x \neq 0$. Replacing $x$ by $1/x$ and multiplying by $x^4$ gives $1 + x - 4x^2 + x^3 + x^4$, the same polynomial. So $x$ is a solution exactly when $1/x$ is a solution: the map $x \mapsto 1/x$ is a symmetry of the equation on $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$.
3. A quantity that the symmetry does not change is $t = x + \frac{1}{x}$. We rewrite the equation in $t$.
4. Divide the equation by $x^2$, which is allowed since $x \neq 0$: $x^2 + x - 4 + \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{x^2} = 0$.
5. Group the symmetric pairs: $\left(x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}\right) + \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right) - 4 = 0$.
6. From $t^2 = x^2 + 2 + \frac{1}{x^2}$, we get $x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} = t^2 - 2$. So the equation becomes $t^2 - 2 + t - 4 = 0$, that is, $t^2 + t - 6 = 0$.
7. This factors as $(t - 2)(t + 3) = 0$, so $t = 2$ or $t = -3$.
8. Case $t = 2$: $x + \frac{1}{x} = 2$ gives $x^2 - 2x + 1 = 0$, that is, $(x - 1)^2 = 0$, so $x = 1$.
9. Case $t = -3$: $x + \frac{1}{x} = -3$ gives $x^2 + 3x + 1 = 0$, so $x = \frac{-3 + \sqrt 5}{2}$ or $x = \frac{-3 - \sqrt 5}{2}$, by the quadratic formula.
10. Each step from step 4 on is reversible for $x \neq 0$, so the solutions are $1$, $\frac{-3 + \sqrt 5}{2}$ and $\frac{-3 - \sqrt 5}{2}$.

A check by expansion: $(x - 1)^2 = x^2 - 2x + 1$, and
$$(x^2 - 2x + 1)(x^2 + 3x + 1) = x^4 + 3x^3 + x^2 - 2x^3 - 6x^2 - 2x + x^2 + 3x + 1 = x^4 + x^3 - 4x^2 + x + 1.$$
So the [[roots|019MA1VTR2]] are $1$ (twice) and the two roots of $x^2 + 3x + 1$.

## Remarks

1. The symmetry halves the degree: a palindromic polynomial of degree $4$ becomes a polynomial of degree $2$ in $t$.
2. The roots come in pairs $\{x, 1/x\}$, as the symmetry predicts. The two roots of step 9 are such a pair: their product is $1$, by the constant term of $x^2 + 3x + 1$. The root $1$ is fixed by the symmetry, since $1/1 = 1$.
3. The variable $t = x + 1/x$ plays the role of an elementary [[symmetric polynomial|0VHSHB9B6J]] of the pair $x$ and $1/x$, whose product is always $1$.
4. A palindromic polynomial of odd degree has the root $-1$. After division by $x + 1$ the quotient is palindromic of even degree, and the method applies.
5. If one coefficient is changed, for example to $x^4 + 2x^3 - 4x^2 + x + 1$, the symmetry is lost: $x \mapsto 1/x$ gives $x^4 + x^3 - 4x^2 + 2x + 1$, a different polynomial, and the substitution of step 6 no longer works.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The equation has a mirror property: its coefficients read the same from left to right and from right to left.
Because of this, if a number solves it, then one divided by that number also solves it.
So the solutions come in pairs, like $2$ and $\tfrac12$.
We can then look for the sum of a pair instead of the numbers themselves, and the sum solves a simpler equation.

### L2 human

We apply the same steps to $2x^4 - 5x^3 + 6x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0$.

1. The coefficients $2, -5, 6, -5, 2$ are palindromic, and $x = 0$ is not a solution.
2. Divide by $x^2$: $2\left(x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}\right) - 5\left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right) + 6 = 0$.
3. With $t = x + \frac1x$: $2(t^2 - 2) - 5t + 6 = 0$, that is, $2t^2 - 5t + 2 = 0$, so $t = 2$ or $t = \tfrac12$.
4. For $t = 2$: $x = 1$ (double root).
5. For $t = \tfrac12$: $x^2 - \tfrac12 x + 1 = 0$ has discriminant $\tfrac14 - 4 < 0$, so it has no real solution.
6. So the only real solution is $x = 1$.

The typical use is a polynomial or a system whose form does not change under an exchange or an inversion of the unknowns.
A trap is to divide by $x^2$ without first checking that $x = 0$ is not a solution.
