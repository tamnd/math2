## Statement

Let $a < b$ and let $f \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$, with $f(a) = f(b)$.
Then there is $c \in (a, b)$ with $f'(c) = 0$.

## Remarks

By the [[extreme value theorem|~8ce35667]], $f$ has a maximum and a minimum on $[a, b]$. If both occur at the endpoints, $f$ is constant. Otherwise one occurs at an interior point $c$, and [[Fermat's theorem|~f405b82b]] gives $f'(c) = 0$.
Differentiability inside the interval is needed. The function $|x|$ on $[-1, 1]$ has no point with derivative $0$.
Mathlib states it as `exists_deriv_eq_zero`.

## History

Michel Rolle proved a version for polynomials in 1691. Cauchy gave a proof of the general theorem in 1823.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A smooth graph that starts and ends at the same height must turn around somewhere.
At the turning point, the tangent is horizontal.
