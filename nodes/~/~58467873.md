## Statement

Let $f$ and $g$ be arithmetic functions.
Then $g(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} f(d)$ for all positive integers $n$ if and only if
$$f(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d) \, g\!\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)$$
for all positive integers $n$. Here $\mu$ is the [[Möbius function|~23b71668]].

## Remarks

In terms of the [[Dirichlet convolution|~fd7304b0]], the statement is: $g = f * \mathbf{1}$ if and only if $f = g * \mu$.
The proof uses [[$\mu * \mathbf{1} = \varepsilon$|~eaa3206d]] and the associativity of $*$.
For example, the [[sum of the totient over the divisors|~9b3d392a]] gives $\sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d) = n$. So $\varphi(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d) \, n/d$.

## History

August Ferdinand Möbius introduced the formula into number theory in 1832.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If you know all the divisor sums of $f$, the formula recovers $f$ itself.
It undoes the operation "sum over the divisors", as subtraction undoes addition.
