## Definition

$\mathsf{PV}$ is an [[equational theory|1AKKDC17M2]] whose terms name exactly the polynomial-time functions on $\N$.
We follow Cook and write numbers in dyadic notation: every $n \ge 1$ has exactly one form $s_1(m) = 2m + 1$ or $s_2(m) = 2m + 2$ with $m < n$.
The function symbols and their defining equations are introduced in stages, called ranks.

1. **Initial functions.** A fixed finite set of initial functions, among them the constant $0$, the successors $s_1$ and $s_2$, and $\mathrm{Tr}(x)$, which deletes the last dyadic digit of $x$.
2. **Composition.** If $f$ and $g_1, \dots, g_k$ are symbols, then there is a symbol $h$ with the defining equation $h(\bar x) = f(g_1(\bar x), \dots, g_k(\bar x))$.
3. **Limited recursion on notation.** Let $g$, $h_1$, $h_2$, $l_1$, $l_2$ be symbols of rank at most $k$. Assume that $\mathsf{PV}$ derives, at rank $k$, an equation that bounds $h_i(\bar x, y, z)$ by the concatenation of the dyadic strings $z$ and $l_i(\bar x, y)$, for $i = 1, 2$. Then there is a symbol $f$ of rank $k + 1$ with the defining equations
$$f(\bar x, 0) = g(\bar x), \qquad f(\bar x, s_i(y)) = h_i(\bar x, y, f(\bar x, y)) \quad (i = 1, 2).$$

The theorems of $\mathsf{PV}$ are the equations $t = u$ between terms that follow from the defining equations by these rules:

- the equality rules: $t = t$, from $t = u$ infer $u = t$, and from $t = u$ and $u = v$ infer $t = v$;
- substitution: from $t(x) = u(x)$ infer $t(r) = u(r)$ for every term $r$;
- congruence: from $t = u$ infer $f(\dots, t, \dots) = f(\dots, u, \dots)$;
- **induction on notation (uniqueness rule):** if $f_1$ and $f_2$ both satisfy the recursion equations of item 3 for the same $g$, $h_1$ and $h_2$, as theorems of $\mathsf{PV}$, then infer $f_1(\bar x, y) = f_2(\bar x, y)$.

A [[feasibly constructive proof|~3d1b2721]], in Cook's sense, is a derivation in $\mathsf{PV}$.

## Notation

- Later presentations use binary notation, with $s_0(x) = 2x$, $s_1(x) = 2x + 1$ and recursion on $\floor{y/2}$. The resulting theories are equivalent in strength.
- $\mathsf{PV}_1$ is the theory $T^0_2$ with function symbols and defining axioms for all polynomial-time functions. Its induction axioms can be replaced by universal axioms, so it is a universal first-order theory. It was introduced by Krajíček, Pudlák and Takeuti.
- $\mathsf{PV}$ also denotes the set of its function symbols, as in $\mathrm{dWPHP}(\mathsf{PV})$.
- $\mathsf{VPV}$ is the two-sorted theory of Cook and Nguyen built on $\mathsf{PV}$.

## Remarks

### Examples

- The successor $y \mapsto y + 1$ is a $\mathsf{PV}$ function by limited recursion on notation; [[this example|~5525d13f]] gives its equations and checks the bound.
- The copying function $c(0) = 0$, $c(s_i(y)) = s_i(c(y))$ satisfies $c(y) = y$ in $\mathsf{PV}$ by the uniqueness rule; [[this example|~2ad74a78]] gives the derivation.
- The function $x \mapsto 2^x$ has no $\mathsf{PV}$ symbol, since its output is too long; [[this non-example|~58dd17e2]] gives the count.

### Basic properties

- Every function symbol of $\mathsf{PV}$ is computed in polynomial time, by induction on its definition. The bounding condition keeps the length of each value polynomial.
- Every polynomial-time function has a $\mathsf{PV}$ symbol. This is Cobham's theorem, which characterizes the [[polynomial-time|GVT2NKN37K]] functions by composition and limited recursion on notation.
- Each equation of $\mathsf{PV}$ translates into a family of propositional tautologies with polynomial-size proofs in the extended Frege system, which can be built in polynomial time. This is Cook's translation into [[proof complexity|1Q046J0AW2]].
- $\mathsf{PV}_1$ is conservative over $\mathsf{PV}$ for equations.
- $\mathsf{PV}_1$ is a universal theory, so [[Herbrand's theorem|VZ21F10WRS]] applies to it. This is the start of the [[Krajíček-Pudlák-Takeuti theorem|~2d7d49f7]].
- $S^1_2$ is conservative over $\mathsf{PV}_1$ for $\forall \Sigma^b_1$ sentences, when the language of $S^1_2$ is extended by the $\mathsf{PV}$ symbols. This follows from [[Buss's witnessing theorem|~ad7bce8a]].

### History

Cobham characterized the polynomial-time functions by limited recursion on notation in 1965.
Cook defined $\mathsf{PV}$ in *Feasibly constructive proofs and the propositional calculus*, at STOC 1975, and proved the translation into extended Frege proofs there.
Krajíček, Pudlák and Takeuti introduced $\mathsf{PV}_1$ in 1991.

### Common errors

- To allow recursion on notation without the bounding condition. Without it, repeated squaring along the digits gives functions such as $y \mapsto 2^{2^{\abs{y}}}$, which are not polynomial-time.
- To confuse recursion on notation with ordinary recursion on $y$. Ordinary recursion runs $y$ times, which is exponential in the length of $y$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

$\mathsf{PV}$ is a language of rules for computer programs that are fast, together with rules for proving that two programs give the same answers.
Each program is built from simple pieces, for example "add a digit at the end of a number".
A program may repeat a step once for each digit of its input, but not once for each unit of its value.
For the number $1000$, which has $10$ binary digits, this means about $10$ repetitions, not $1000$.
The only way to prove two programs equal is to show that both follow the same step-by-step recipe.

### L2 human

We compute with the successor function of the [[example|~5525d13f]] in dyadic notation.

1. The defining equations are $\mathrm{succ}(0) = s_1(0)$, $\mathrm{succ}(s_1(y)) = s_2(y)$ and $\mathrm{succ}(s_2(y)) = s_1(\mathrm{succ}(y))$.
2. The number $5$ is $s_1(2)$, since $5 = 2 \cdot 2 + 1$, and $2 = s_2(0)$.
3. So $\mathrm{succ}(5) = s_2(2) = 6$, by the second equation.
4. The number $6$ is $s_2(2)$, so $\mathrm{succ}(6) = s_1(\mathrm{succ}(2))$.
5. $\mathrm{succ}(2) = \mathrm{succ}(s_2(0)) = s_1(\mathrm{succ}(0)) = s_1(1) = 3$, so $\mathrm{succ}(6) = s_1(3) = 7$.

The typical use of $\mathsf{PV}$ is as a base theory: a combinatorial statement is formalized as an equation, and a $\mathsf{PV}$ proof gives short extended Frege proofs of its propositional translations.
A trap is to forget the bounding derivation in item 3: it is a part of the definition of the language, not a side remark.

### L3 human

$\mathsf{PV}$ is to polynomial time what primitive recursive arithmetic is to the primitive recursive functions: a quantifier-free theory with a symbol for each function of the class and induction as a rule.
Its first-order extension $\mathsf{PV}_1$ is the bottom of the hierarchy of theories $\mathsf{PV}_i$ used in the proof of the Krajíček-Pudlák-Takeuti theorem.
In the two-sorted world of Cook and Nguyen the same theory reappears as $\mathsf{VPV}$.

The link with proof complexity is the main reason for the interest in $\mathsf{PV}$.
$\mathsf{PV}_1$ proves that extended Frege is sound, and extended Frege p-simulates every proof system that $\mathsf{PV}_1$ proves sound.
So the strength of $\mathsf{PV}$ and the strength of extended Frege are two views of one object.
Extensions such as $\mathsf{PV}_1 + \mathrm{dWPHP}(\mathsf{PV})$ give the [[approximate counting|~a1e5c7e9]] theories for randomized algorithms.
