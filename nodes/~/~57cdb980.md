## Definition

Fix a field $\mathbb{F}$ and variables $x_1, \dots, x_n$, and work in the polynomial ring $\mathbb{F}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$.
The third system, sum of squares, is defined in the Remarks.
We read the value $1$ as true and $0$ as false.

**Translation of clauses.**
A [[clause|T3SXVH3ZZC]] $C$ with positive literals $C^+$ and negative literals $C^-$ translates to the polynomial
$$P_C = \prod_{x \in C^+} (1 - x) \cdot \prod_{\neg x \in C^-} x.$$
At a $0$-$1$ point, $P_C$ is $0$ exactly when some factor is $0$, that is, when the point satisfies $C$.
A [[CNF formula|0B3D4C8YF2]] $F$ translates to the set of the $P_C$ for its clauses.
The *Boolean axioms* are the polynomials $x_j^2 - x_j$ for $j = 1, \dots, n$, whose common zeros in $\mathbb{F}^n$ are the $0$-$1$ points.

Below, $f_1, \dots, f_m$ are polynomials, such as the translation of a CNF, and a refutation shows that they have no common $0$-$1$ zero.

**Nullstellensatz.**
A *Nullstellensatz refutation* of $f_1, \dots, f_m$ is a list of polynomials $g_1, \dots, g_m, h_1, \dots, h_n$ such that
$$\sum_{i=1}^m g_i f_i + \sum_{j=1}^n h_j \, (x_j^2 - x_j) = 1$$
holds as an identity in $\mathbb{F}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$.
Its *degree* is the largest degree of the products $g_i f_i$ and $h_j (x_j^2 - x_j)$.
Its *size* is the total number of monomials of these products.

**Polynomial calculus.**
A *polynomial calculus derivation* from $f_1, \dots, f_m$ is a sequence of polynomials, the *lines*, in which each line is obtained in one of the following ways.

1. **Axiom.** It is one of $f_1, \dots, f_m$ or a Boolean axiom.
2. **Linear combination.** It is $a f + b g$ for earlier lines $f, g$ and $a, b \in \mathbb{F}$.
3. **Multiplication.** It is $x_j f$ for an earlier line $f$ and a variable $x_j$.

A *polynomial calculus refutation* is a derivation whose last line is the constant polynomial $1$.
Its *degree* is the largest degree of a line, its *size* is the number of monomials of all lines, and its *length* is the number of lines.

## Notation

We write NS for the Nullstellensatz system, PC for polynomial calculus and SOS for sum of squares.
Many authors use the opposite convention, with $0$ for true, and then the translation of $x$ is $x$ instead of $1 - x$.
PCR is the variant of PC with a second variable $\bar x_j$ for each $x_j$ and the axioms $x_j + \bar x_j - 1$, so that every clause translates to a single monomial.
The Positivstellensatz system is the general form of SOS, in which the certificate may use products of the inequalities of the problem, as in the [[Positivstellensatz|FN734RQYM6]].

## Remarks

### Sum of squares

Let $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{R}$.
A *sum-of-squares refutation* of $f_1, \dots, f_m$ is a list $g_1, \dots, g_m, h_1, \dots, h_n$ together with polynomials $s_1, \dots, s_t$ such that
$$\sum_{i=1}^m g_i f_i + \sum_{j=1}^n h_j \, (x_j^2 - x_j) + \sum_{l=1}^t s_l^2 = -1$$
holds as a polynomial identity.
Its degree is the largest degree of the terms $g_i f_i$, $h_j (x_j^2 - x_j)$ and $s_l^2$.

### Soundness and completeness

All three systems are sound.
At a common $0$-$1$ zero of $f_1, \dots, f_m$, every line of a PC derivation vanishes, by induction on the lines, and $1$ does not vanish.
For NS, the left side of the identity vanishes at such a point, and the right side is $1$.
For SOS over $\mathbb{R}$, the left side is at least $0$ at such a point, and the right side is $-1$.

NS, and hence PC, is complete for unsatisfiable CNFs.
Let $F$ be unsatisfiable, and for each $a \in \set{0,1}^n$ choose a clause $C_a$ of $F$ that $a$ falsifies.
Let $\delta_a = \prod_{a_j = 1} x_j \prod_{a_j = 0} (1 - x_j)$, so that $\sum_a \delta_a = \prod_j (x_j + (1 - x_j)) = 1$.
Since $P_{C_a}(a) = 1$, the polynomials $\delta_a P_{C_a}$ and $\delta_a$ have the same value at every $0$-$1$ point.
So their difference vanishes on $\set{0,1}^n$ and has degree at most $2n$.
A polynomial $g$ that vanishes on $\set{0,1}^n$ is a sum $\sum_j h_j (x_j^2 - x_j)$ with terms of degree at most $\deg g$.
To see this, replace $x_j^2$ by $x_j$ step by step, where each step subtracts a term $m (x_j^2 - x_j)$.
The multilinear result vanishes on $\set{0,1}^n$, so it is $0$.
Summing over $a$ gives a Nullstellensatz refutation of degree at most $2n$.
[[Hilbert's Nullstellensatz|D85VZWS9QK]] gives the general form: polynomials over an algebraically closed field have no common zero exactly when $1$ is in the ideal they generate.

### Basic properties

- **PC simulates NS in degree.** A term $c \, m f_i$ of an NS refutation of degree $d$, with a monomial $m$, is derived from $f_i$ by multiplying by the variables of $m$ one at a time, and every intermediate line has degree at most $d$. A final linear combination gives $1$.
- **PC simulates resolution in degree.** From $P_C (1 - x)$ and $P_D \, x$, multiplication by the missing factors and linear combination give $P_E (1 - x)$ and $P_E \, x$ for $E = C \cup D$, and their sum is $P_E$. So a resolution refutation of width $w$ gives a PC refutation of degree at most $w + 1$. The size can grow, since $P_E$ can have $2^{\abs{E}}$ monomials.
- **SOS simulates NS in degree.** An NS refutation multiplied by $-1$ is an SOS refutation with no squares.
- **Existence of SOS refutations.** If $f_1, \dots, f_m$ have no common real zero, then part 1 of the [[Positivstellensatz|FN734RQYM6]], applied to the inequalities $f_i \geq 0$ and $-f_i \geq 0$, gives a certificate of the form above.
- **Search in bounded degree.** Clegg, Edmonds and Impagliazzo showed that if a set of polynomials in $n$ variables has a PC refutation of degree $d$, then a refutation can be found in time $n^{O(d)}$, by a version of the Gröbner basis algorithm that keeps only polynomials of degree at most $d$. This is a link with [[Gröbner bases|C83KPT47R0]] and with [[automatizability|~64ace3b8]].

### Lower bounds by duality

A degree lower bound for NS comes from a linear functional.
Let $V_d$ be the vector space of polynomials of degree at most $d$, and let $\Lambda \colon V_d \to \mathbb{F}$ be linear.
Assume that $\Lambda(1) = 1$, and that $\Lambda(m f) = 0$ for every axiom $f$, Boolean axioms included, and every monomial $m$ with $\deg(m f) \leq d$.
Then $f_1, \dots, f_m$ have no NS refutation of degree at most $d$.

Proof.

1. Assume that $\sum_i g_i f_i + \sum_j h_j (x_j^2 - x_j) = 1$ is a refutation of degree at most $d$.
2. Write each $g_i$ as a sum $\sum_m c_{i,m} m$ of monomials with coefficients in $\mathbb{F}$.
3. Over a field, $\deg(g f) = \deg g + \deg f$ for non-zero $g$ and $f$. So each monomial $m$ of $g_i$ has $\deg(m f_i) \leq \deg(g_i f_i) \leq d$. The same holds for the $h_j$.
4. Apply $\Lambda$ to the identity. By linearity, the left side becomes a sum of terms $c_{i,m} \Lambda(m f_i)$ and similar terms for the $h_j$.
5. Each term is $0$ by the assumption on $\Lambda$, so the left side gives $0$.
6. The right side gives $\Lambda(1) = 1$, and $0 \neq 1$ is a contradiction.

Such a $\Lambda$ is called a *design* in the literature on NS lower bounds.

### Main results

Each result names its measure and its system.

- The Nullstellensatz system was introduced by Beame, Impagliazzo, Krajíček, Pitassi and Pudlák, who proved degree lower bounds for it.
- Polynomial calculus was introduced by Clegg, Edmonds and Impagliazzo in 1996.
- **Pigeonhole, degree in PC.** Razborov (1998) proved that, over every field and for every $m > n$, every PC refutation of the pigeonhole principle $\mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ has degree at least $n/2 + 1$. Here the pigeon axioms are $1 - \sum_{j} x_{ij}$ and the hole axioms are $x_{ij} x_{kj}$.
- **Size and degree in PC.** Impagliazzo, Pudlák and Sgall proved that if polynomials of degree at most $d_0$ in $n$ variables have a PC refutation of size $S$, then they have one of degree $O(d_0 + \sqrt{n \log S})$. So there is a constant $c$ such that, if every refutation has degree at least $d$, then every refutation has size $\exp(\Omega((d - c d_0)^2 / n))$.
- **Tseitin formulas.** Over a field of characteristic other than $2$, the [[Tseitin formulas|~4386fc47]] of suitable expander graphs of bounded degree need PC degree linear in the number $n$ of variables (Buss, Grigoriev, Impagliazzo and Pitassi). By the size-degree relation, with $d_0$ bounded by the degree of the graph, they then need PC size $\exp(\Omega(n))$.
- **Tseitin over $\mathbb{F}_2$.** Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ the same formulas are easy. The constraint of a vertex $v$ with charge $\chi(v)$ is the linear polynomial $\sum_{e \ni v} x_e + \chi(v)$, which PC derives from the clauses of $v$ in a degree that depends only on the degree of the graph. Every edge lies on two vertices, so the sum of these polynomials over all $v$ is $\sum_v \chi(v)$, which is $1$ because the total charge is odd.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Write "true" as $1$ and "false" as $0$, and turn each clause into a polynomial that is $0$ exactly when the clause is true.
If all clauses are true, all these polynomials are $0$.
An algebraic proof combines the polynomials, multiplying them by other polynomials and adding, until it reaches the constant $1$.
Since a combination of zeros is zero, and $1$ is not zero, the clauses cannot all be true.
For the clauses "$x$" and "not $x$", the polynomials are $1 - x$ and $x$, and their sum is $1$.

### L2 human

We refute the clauses $x \vee y$, $\neg x$ and $\neg y$ with Nullstellensatz.

1. The polynomials are $(1 - x)(1 - y)$, $x$ and $y$.
2. The certificate is $(1 - x)(1 - y) + (1 - y) \cdot x + 1 \cdot y$.
3. Expand: $1 - x - y + xy + x - xy + y = 1$.
4. The degree is $2$, since $(1 - x)(1 - y)$ and $(1 - y) x$ have degree $2$.

The typical use of these systems is to measure degree: a lower bound on the degree often gives a lower bound on the size, by the size-degree relation.
A trap is to forget the Boolean axioms: an identity that holds only at $0$-$1$ points is not a certificate unless the Boolean axioms appear in it.

### L3 human

Algebraic proof systems connect proof complexity with commutative algebra.
An NS refutation of degree $d$ is a witness that $1$ lies in the ideal of the axioms with a representation of degree $d$, and PC of degree $d$ is close to the [[Gröbner basis|C83KPT47R0]] algorithm restricted to degree $d$.
SOS adds real positivity, through sums of squares, and its refutations of bounded degree are dual to the relaxations of the hierarchy of [[sum-of-squares optimization|AM0BYNK5YJ]].
The field matters: parity is easy over $\mathbb{F}_2$ and hard over other fields, while the pigeonhole principle is hard in degree over every field.
As for every system, a lower bound for these systems says nothing about [[Frege systems|~3f0577bc]], and the [[cutting-planes system|~f9bde1dc]] refutes the pigeonhole formulas in polynomial size.
