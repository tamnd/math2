## Proof

We use one fact in both parts.
If $x \cdot y = 0$, then $\|x + y\|^2 = \|x\|^2 + 2 x \cdot y + \|y\|^2 = \|x\|^2 + \|y\|^2$, by expanding the [[dot product|0S52TNNTH9]].

**Part 1: point and line.**

1. Let $u = q - a$. For each $t$, $\|q - (a + t d)\| = \|u - t d\|$, by algebra.
2. By the [[orthogonal decomposition|~16119a5a]], the least value of $\|u - t d\|$ is reached at $t = \dfrac{u \cdot d}{d \cdot d}$.
3. By the same theorem, the least value is $\|w\|$ with $w = u - \dfrac{u \cdot d}{d \cdot d} d$, and $w \cdot d = 0$.
4. By the fact above, $\|u\|^2 = \|w\|^2 + \dfrac{(u \cdot d)^2}{\|d\|^2}$. So $\|w\|^2 = \dfrac{\|u\|^2 \|d\|^2 - (u \cdot d)^2}{\|d\|^2}$.
5. By [[Lagrange's identity|~cf895c55]], the numerator is $\|u \times d\|^2$. So $\|w\| = \dfrac{\|(q - a) \times d\|}{\|d\|}$.

**Part 2: two non-parallel lines.**

6. For points $a + t d$ and $b + s e$, let $x(t, s) = (b + s e) - (a + t d) = (b - a) + s e - t d$.
7. $m \cdot d = m \cdot e = 0$, by the [[perpendicularity of the cross product|0T4QHJXSD4]]. So $m \cdot x(t, s) = m \cdot (b - a)$.
8. The vectors $d$, $e$, $m$ are independent, as in the [[coplanarity proof|~979ecca8]], steps 9 to 11. So they form a [[basis|096V7H13H9]].
9. Write $b - a = \alpha d + \beta e + \gamma m$. Step 7 gives $m \cdot (b - a) = \gamma \|m\|^2$.
10. Then $x(t, s) = (\alpha - t) d + (\beta + s) e + \gamma m$. Let $y = (\alpha - t) d + (\beta + s) e$.
11. $y \cdot m = 0$ by step 7. By the fact above, $\|x(t, s)\|^2 = \|y\|^2 + \gamma^2 \|m\|^2 \geq \gamma^2 \|m\|^2$.
12. For $t = \alpha$ and $s = -\beta$, $y = 0$ and equality holds.
13. So the least distance is $|\gamma| \, \|m\| = \dfrac{|m \cdot (b - a)|}{\|m\|}$, by step 9. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

In each case the connecting vector splits into a part that the free points can change and a part that they cannot.
The fixed part is perpendicular to the given sets, so by Pythagoras it is a lower bound, and a choice of the free points removes the other part.

## Variants

- Part 1 with areas: the parallelogram on $q - a$ and $d$ has the area $\|(q - a) \times d\|$, by the [[length theorem|~bafd22bd]]. Its height over the base $d$ is the distance.
- Part 2 with calculus: minimize $\|x(t, s)\|^2$, a quadratic in $t$ and $s$. The two partial derivatives give $x \cdot d = 0$ and $x \cdot e = 0$.
