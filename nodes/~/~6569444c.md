## Summary

Enumerative combinatorics is the study of the number of ways to build a finite structure of a given kind.
Its central object is a counting sequence, the number of structures of each size, and a generating function often records this sequence.
Counting problems occur in probability, in the analysis of algorithms, in statistical physics and in every part of discrete mathematics.
Pascal and Fermat used binomial coefficients to solve problems of probability in the 17th century, and Euler used generating functions to count partitions in the 18th century.
This topic collects the enumerative combinatorics pages of math2: counting principles, binomial coefficients, inclusion-exclusion, generating functions and recurrences, lattice paths, Stirling and Bell numbers, permutation statistics and patterns, q-analogues, Pólya counting, species, analytic combinatorics, combinatorial identities and combinatorics on words.
Algebraic combinatorics, additive and combinatorial number theory, graph theory and special functions have their own topics.
