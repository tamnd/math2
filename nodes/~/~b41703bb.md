## Example

Let $F$ be a field, let $V = M_n(F)$ with $n \ge 1$, and let $U = \set{X \in V : \tr X = 0}$ be the subspace of trace-zero matrices, where $\tr$ is the [[trace|07PTPSZPME]].
We show that the [[annihilator|0R6M158SQ9]] $U^0$ is the line $F \cdot \tr$.

1. $\tr$ vanishes on $U$ by definition, so $\tr \in U^0$, and then $F \cdot \tr \subseteq U^0$.
2. $\tr$ is a nonzero form, since $\tr E_{11} = 1$ for the [[matrix unit|4H93XF4RVH]] $E_{11}$. So $U = \ker(\tr)$ is a hyperplane, and $\dim U = n^2 - 1$, by the theorem on [[hyperplanes and linear forms|~577d8228]].
3. By the [[dimension of the annihilator|06Q1EFVH0Q]], $\dim U^0 = n^2 - (n^2 - 1) = 1$.
4. A line contained in a line is equal to it. So $U^0 = F \cdot \tr$.

A direct check for $n = 2$, with the [[trace pairing|~cf01dd68]]: each form is $\tau_A(X) = \tr(AX)$ for one $A = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{pmatrix}$.

5. The matrices $E_{12}$, $E_{21}$ and $E_{11} - E_{22}$ have trace $0$, so they lie in $U$.
6. $\tau_A(E_{12}) = a_{21}$ and $\tau_A(E_{21}) = a_{12}$. So $\tau_A \in U^0$ forces $a_{12} = a_{21} = 0$.
7. $\tau_A(E_{11} - E_{22}) = a_{11} - a_{22}$. So $\tau_A \in U^0$ forces $a_{11} = a_{22}$.
8. Hence $A = aI$ for some $a \in F$, and $\tau_A(X) = a \tr X$. So $U^0 = F \cdot \tr$, as in step 4.

## Remarks

- In words: a linear form on $M_n(F)$ that vanishes on all trace-zero matrices is a multiple of the trace.
- Under the trace pairing, $U^0$ corresponds to the scalar matrices $F \cdot I$.
- The three matrices of step 5 are a basis of $U$ for $n = 2$, which agrees with $\dim U = 3$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The trace-zero matrices are the matrices whose diagonal entries add up to $0$.
The only linear rules that give $0$ on all of them are the multiples of the trace itself.
This is because the trace-zero matrices form a hyperplane, and a hyperplane has one equation up to a factor.

### L2 human

The method is a dimension count.

1. Find one nonzero form in the annihilator, here the trace.
2. Compute the dimension of the annihilator, here $n^2 - (n^2 - 1) = 1$.
3. Conclude that the form from step 1 spans the annihilator.

The typical use is to recognize the trace as the essentially unique form with a given kernel.
A trap: $U^0$ is a set of forms on matrices, not a set of matrices; the scalar matrices appear only after the trace pairing.
