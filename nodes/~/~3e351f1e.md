## Counterexample

The claim "$\rank(A^\top A) = \rank A$ for every matrix over every field" is false.

1. Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{pmatrix}$, a $2 \times 1$ matrix over $\C$. Its column is nonzero, so its [[rank|0EWQEYRVA8]] is $1$.
2. The [[transpose|0W809K6AW0]] is $A^\top = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \end{pmatrix}$.
3. By the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]], $A^\top A = (1 \cdot 1 + i \cdot i) = (1 + i^2) = (1 - 1) = (0)$.
4. So $\rank(A^\top A) = 0 \ne 1 = \rank A$.
5. Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, let $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Then $\rank B = 1$, and $B^\top B = (1 + 1) = (0)$ in $\mathbb{F}_2$. So $\rank(B^\top B) = 0 \ne 1$.
6. With the conjugate transpose over $\C$: $A^* = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -i \end{pmatrix}$, and $A^* A = (1 + (-i)(i)) = (1 + 1) = (2)$. So $\rank(A^* A) = 1 = \rank A$.

## Remarks

- In each case the column $y$ of the matrix is a nonzero vector with $y^\top y = 0$, an isotropic vector.
- The [[statement over the real numbers|~9758208b]] uses that a real vector with $y^\top y = 0$ is zero.
- Step 6 shows the correct complex form, with $A^*$ in place of $A^\top$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Over the complex numbers, the vector $(1, i)$ has "squared length" $1 + i^2 = 0$ when no conjugate is used.
So $A^\top A$ can be zero although $A$ is not.

### L2 human

The real proof uses that $x^\top A^\top A x = \lVert Ax \rVert^2$ is zero only when $Ax = 0$.
Without conjugation, $y^\top y$ is not a norm over $\C$, and over $\mathbb{F}_2$ it is not a norm either.
