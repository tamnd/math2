## Summary

*Post's logics* $\mathrm{P}_m$ use $m \ge 2$ linearly ordered values, $\max$ as disjunction, $\min$ as conjunction and a *cyclic negation* that moves each value one step down and moves the bottom to the top; see [[the definition|~fbe0fcc9]].
Their main property is [[functional completeness|~a7498925]]: every function on the $m$ values is given by a formula in $\neg$ and $\lor$.
So the $m$-valued Post algebra plays the role for $m$ values that the two-element Boolean algebra plays for two.
Post introduced them in 1921, at the same time as Łukasiewicz's three-valued logic, and they are one of the two oldest families of [[many-valued logics|DWYCKAKXZD]].

## Motivation

In classical logic, every truth function is a formula in $\neg$ and $\lor$, by [[functional completeness|TDZVCWYJ9F]].
Post asked for a set of $m$-valued connectives with the same property.
Łukasiewicz's connectives fail: they cannot produce the constant $\tfrac12$, since they map $\set{0, 1}$ into itself.
Post's negation breaks this obstacle: it moves every value, so no proper subset of values is closed under it except the empty set and the whole set.

## Notation

Wikipedia writes the values as $0, \tfrac{1}{m-1}, \dots, 1$; we often relabel them as $0, 1, \dots, m-1$.
Then Post's negation is $\neg x = x - 1 \bmod m$, and the shift $S x = x + 1 \bmod m$ is $\neg$ applied $m - 1$ times.
SEP writes $\mathcal{P}_m^\lambda$ for the matrix with $\lambda$ designated values.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[The three-valued Post logic|~f9660d8c]]: its tables and a formula for Łukasiewicz negation.
2. $\mathrm{P}_2$ is classical logic: $\neg$ swaps $0$ and $1$, and $\max$ is $\lor$.
3. In $\mathrm{P}_m$, the term $x \lor \neg x \lor \neg\neg x \lor \dots$ with $m$ disjuncts is the constant $m - 1$, since the disjuncts run through all values.

### Non-examples

1. [[Łukasiewicz's three-valued connectives are not functionally complete|~a1c5cb0e]].
2. Kleene's connectives on three values preserve $\set{\tfrac12}$, so they define no constant $0$ or $1$ and are not functionally complete.

### Basic properties

1. Every function $V^n \to V$, $n \ge 1$, is a term function of $\langle V, \neg, \lor \rangle$; see [[the proof|~a9389619]].
2. In particular $\land = \min$ is definable from $\neg$ and $\lor$.
3. The algebra $\langle V, \neg, \lor \rangle$ is therefore a [[primal algebra|XD01Y7B6XW]].
4. The algebras of the Post logics form the variety of [[Post algebras|RD2A5Z7E2B]], treated with the algebras of logic.

### History

Post introduced the systems $\mathrm{P}_m$ in his 1921 paper "Introduction to a general theory of elementary propositions".
SEP reports that Post solved the problem of a functionally complete $m$-element algebra there.
Słupecki gave general criteria for functional completeness of $m$-valued operations in 1939.

### Common errors

1. Taking $\neg$ to be an involution. Post's negation has order $m$: $\neg^m x = x$, and $\neg\neg x \ne x$ for $m \ge 3$.
2. Reading the values as degrees of truth. In $\mathrm{P}_m$ the order matters for $\lor$, but negation does not reverse it.
3. Confusing Post algebras with Post's lattice, the lattice of two-valued clones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Post's logic has $m$ values on a line, from $0$ up to $m - 1$.
"Or" picks the larger value.
"Not" steps one place down, and $0$ jumps to the top.
With these two connectives, you can build every possible truth table on the $m$ values.

### L2 human

The proof of completeness builds, for each value $a$, a formula that is the top exactly at $a$.
It then combines such formulas with $\lor$, as disjunctive normal form does in classical logic.
The typical use is to show that a set of connectives is complete by defining $\neg$ and $\lor$ of $\mathrm{P}_m$ from it.
A trap: completeness is about functions of at least one variable; constants are obtained as constant functions of one variable.

### L3 human

$\langle \set{0, \dots, m-1}, \neg, \lor \rangle$ is primal, so the variety it generates is equivalent to Boolean algebras by Foster's theorem.
Post algebras, studied with the other algebras of logic, are the algebraic counterpart of the Post logics.
The lattice of $m$-valued clones is uncountable for $m \ge 3$, by SEP; the maximal clones are described by [[Rosenberg's classification|GF1RK91VED]].
