## Proof

### The easy direction

1. Let $h \colon A \to B$ be a surjective [[homomorphism|KBXSZC6BV1]]. We show by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]]: if $\psi(\bar x)$ is positive and $A \models \psi(\bar a)$, then $B \models \psi(h \bar a)$.
2. Atomic formulas: $h$ preserves constants, functions and relations, so it preserves the values of terms, equations and relations. The case $\top$ holds, and $\bot$ is never true.
3. The steps for $\wedge$, $\vee$ and $\exists$ follow from the induction hypothesis, with the witness $h(a')$ for $\exists$.
4. For $\forall y \, \psi$: each $b \in B$ is $h(a')$ for some $a' \in A$, since $h$ is onto, and $A \models \psi(\bar a, a')$ gives $B \models \psi(h \bar a, b)$.

### The hard direction: set-up

5. Let the models of $T$ be closed under surjective homomorphic images. Let $\Delta$ be the set of positive consequences of $T$, and let $B \models \Delta$. We show $B \models T$; part 1 is the case $T = \set{\varphi}$, see step 22.
6. For structures $A, B$, call a set $P \subseteq A \times B$ *positive* if for every finite list of pairs $(a_i, b_i)$ in $P$ and every positive $\psi(\bar x)$, $A \models \psi(\bar a)$ implies $B \models \psi(\bar b)$.
7. Lemma A: there is $A \models T$ such that $\emptyset$ is positive for $(A, B)$. Let $\Sigma = T \cup \set{\neg\psi : \psi \text{ positive}, B \models \neg\psi}$.
8. If $\Sigma$ had no model, [[compactness|1NR3HAWG9D]] would give $T \models \psi_1 \vee \dots \vee \psi_k$ with each $\psi_j$ false in $B$; we may include $\bot$, so $k \ge 1$. The disjunction is positive, so it lies in $\Delta$ and holds in $B$, a contradiction.
9. A model $A$ of $\Sigma$ satisfies $T$, and a positive sentence true in $A$ cannot be false in $B$, because its negation is in $\Sigma$.

### Forth and back

10. Forth lemma: if $P$ is positive for $(A, B)$, there are $B' \succeq B$ and a positive $P' \supseteq P$ for $(A, B')$ whose first coordinates cover $A$.
11. Add constants $d_a$ for $a \in A$. Let $\Gamma$ be $\mathrm{ElDiag}(B)$ with all $\psi(\bar b, d_{a_1}, \dots, d_{a_m})$ such that $\psi$ is positive, $(\bar a, \bar b)$ comes from $P$ and $A \models \psi(\bar a, a_1, \dots, a_m)$.
12. A finite part of $\Gamma$ beyond $\mathrm{ElDiag}(B)$ has a conjunction $\psi(\bar b, \bar d)$ of this kind. Then $A \models \exists \bar z \, \psi(\bar a, \bar z)$, a positive formula, so $B \models \exists \bar z \, \psi(\bar b, \bar z)$, and $B$ satisfies the finite part.
13. Let $B'$ be a model of $\Gamma$; by the [[diagram lemmas|EP5E317TNW]] we may take $B \preceq B'$. Put $P' = P \cup \set{(a, d_a^{B'}) : a \in A}$, which is positive by the choice of $\Gamma$.
14. Back lemma: if $P$ is positive for $(A, B)$, there are $A' \succeq A$ and a positive $P' \supseteq P$ for $(A', B)$ whose second coordinates cover $B$.
15. Add constants $e_b$ for $b \in B$. Let $\Gamma$ be $\mathrm{ElDiag}(A)$ with all $\neg\psi(\bar a, e_{b_1}, \dots, e_{b_m})$ such that $\psi$ is positive, $(\bar a, \bar b)$ comes from $P$ and $B \models \neg\psi(\bar b, b_1, \dots, b_m)$.
16. If a finite part with $\neg\psi_1, \dots, \neg\psi_k$ failed in $A$, then $A \models \forall \bar z \, (\psi_1 \vee \dots \vee \psi_k)(\bar a, \bar z)$. This formula is positive, so it holds at $\bar b$ in $B$, and some $\psi_j$ is true at the chosen $b$'s, a contradiction.
17. A model $A' \succeq A$ of $\Gamma$ with $P' = P \cup \set{(e_b^{A'}, b) : b \in B}$ works: a positive formula true in $A'$ at the new elements cannot be false in $B$, by the choice of $\Gamma$.

### The chain and the homomorphism

18. Start with $A_0 = A$ from lemma A, $B_0 = B$ and $P_0 = \emptyset$. Apply the forth lemma and then the back lemma, and repeat by [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]]. This gives elementary chains $(A_n)$ and $(B_n)$ and positive sets $P_0 \subseteq P_1 \subseteq \cdots$.
19. Let $A_\omega$, $B_\omega$ be the unions and $P = \bigcup_n P_n$. By the [[elementary chain theorem|NHCRZ74JHH]], $A_n \preceq A_\omega$ and $B_n \preceq B_\omega$, so $P$ is positive for $(A_\omega, B_\omega)$.
20. Every element of $A_\omega$ lies in some $A_n$ and is covered by the next forth step; every element of $B_\omega$ is covered by a back step. The formula $x_1 = x_2$ is positive, so $(a, b), (a, b') \in P$ gives $b = b'$. So $P$ is the graph of a map $h \colon A_\omega \to B_\omega$, and it is onto.
21. Atomic formulas are positive, so $h$ is a homomorphism. Now $A \preceq A_\omega$ gives $A_\omega \models T$, closure gives $B_\omega \models T$, and $B \preceq B_\omega$ gives $B \models T$.
22. For one sentence $\varphi$, compactness gives finitely many positive consequences whose conjunction implies $\varphi$. The conjunction is positive and equivalent to $\varphi$.

## Idea

A model $B$ of the positive consequences of $T$ receives "positive information" from some model $A$ of $T$.
A back-and-forth construction, which enlarges $A$ and $B$ by elementary extensions at each step, turns this information into a surjective homomorphism between elementary extensions.
Closure under surjective images moves $T$ from the extension of $A$ to the extension of $B$, and elementarity brings it down to $B$.

## Variants

- Lyndon's original proof uses the [[Lyndon interpolation theorem|BRDT1BT7WW]].
- With saturated models one can avoid the chains: if $A$ and $B$ are saturated of the same size and positive information passes from $A$ to $B$, a back-and-forth argument of length $\abs{A}$ gives the homomorphism at once.
