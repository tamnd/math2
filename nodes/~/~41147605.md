## Statement

Let $p$ be a prime number and let $G$ be a [[group|G5S1M9]] of order $p^2$.
Then $G$ is [[abelian|~b6df27fe]].

## Remarks

By the [[nontrivial centre theorem|~438b0555]] and [[Lagrange's theorem|3C53TD]], $|Z(G)|$ is $p$ or $p^2$.
If $|Z(G)| = p$, then $G/Z(G)$ has order $p$, so it is cyclic, and [[this criterion|~508b55bc]] gives a contradiction.
Thus $Z(G) = G$, and $G$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}$ or to $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.
For $p^3$ the result is false: the [[dihedral group|~42ac19c1]] $D_4$ and the [[quaternion group|~f03a0e7d]] have order $8$ and are not abelian.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A group of order $p^2$ has a centre with more than one element.
If the centre were not the whole group, the quotient by the centre would be cyclic, and that is impossible.
So all elements commute.
