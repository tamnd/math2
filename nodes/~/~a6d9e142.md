## Proof

1. Let $a_1, \dots, a_n \in \R^m$ be the columns of $A$. By the [[definition of the column space|0F0RPPJ2TT]], $\operatorname{Col}(A)$ is their span.
2. By [[transpose times a vector|GNPP8QK9EM]], entry $j$ of $A^\top y$ is the [[dot product|05HQYM0R4G]] $a_j \cdot y$.
3. Let $y \in \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$. By the [[definition of the orthogonal complement|079D68S3AW]], $a_j \cdot y = 0$ for each $j$, since $a_j \in \operatorname{Col}(A)$.
4. So every entry of $A^\top y$ is $0$ by step 2, and $y$ lies in the [[null space|0PQ2GGY1QV]] of $A^\top$.
5. Conversely, let $A^\top y = 0$. By step 2, $a_j \cdot y = 0$ for each $j$.
6. Each $b \in \operatorname{Col}(A)$ is $b = c_1 a_1 + \dots + c_n a_n$. By linearity of the dot product, $b \cdot y = \sum_j c_j (a_j \cdot y) = 0$.
7. So $y \in \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$. Steps 3 to 7 give $\operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp = \operatorname{Null}(A^\top)$.

## Idea

The entries of $A^\top y$ are the dot products of $y$ with the columns of $A$.
So $A^\top y = 0$ says that $y$ is orthogonal to each column, and so to their span.

## Variants

The same argument for $A^\top$ gives $\operatorname{Row}(A)^\perp = \operatorname{Null}(A)$.
Over $\C$, the same proof with the Hermitian product gives $\operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp = \operatorname{Null}(A^*)$.
