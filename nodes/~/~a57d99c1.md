## Summary

This area is the study of infinite series and of the methods that give a value to a series or a sequence that does not converge.
A series converges when the sequence of its partial sums has a limit, and a summation method is a rule that gives a value to a wider class of series and keeps the sum of each convergent series.
Series give expansions of functions and values of constants, and summation methods are tools in Fourier analysis, number theory and physics.
Euler worked freely with divergent series in the 18th century, and Hardy wrote the standard account of their theory in his book Divergent Series of 1949.
This topic collects the sequences, series and summability pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Calculus, real analysis, harmonic analysis and analytic number theory have their own topics.
