## Proof

Let $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{N}$ be saturated, elementarily equivalent, of size $\kappa$, and let $f_0$ be a partial elementary map from $\mathcal{M}$ to $\mathcal{N}$ with $\abs{\operatorname{dom} f_0} < \kappa$.
For part 2, take $f_0 = \emptyset$; it is partial elementary because $\mathcal{M} \equiv \mathcal{N}$.

### The forth step

1. Let $f$ be a [[partial elementary map|XTNYY0WRQ5]] with domain $A$, $\abs{A} < \kappa$, and let $m \in M$.
2. Let $p(x) = \operatorname{tp}^{\mathcal{M}}(m/A)$, and let $q(x) = \set{\varphi(x, f(\bar{a})) : \varphi(x, \bar{a}) \in p}$, a set of formulas over $f(A)$.
3. For finitely many formulas $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k$ of $p$, the sentence $\exists x \bigwedge_i \varphi_i(x, \bar{a})$ holds in $\mathcal{M}$, witnessed by $m$.
4. Since $f$ is partial elementary, $\exists x \bigwedge_i \varphi_i(x, f(\bar{a}))$ holds in $\mathcal{N}$. So $q$ is finitely satisfiable in $\mathcal{N}$.
5. The set $q$ is complete, since $p$ is complete, so $q$ is a complete type over $f(A)$ in $\mathcal{N}$.
6. Since $\abs{f(A)} < \kappa$ and $\mathcal{N}$ is saturated of size $\kappa$, some $n \in N$ realizes $q$.
7. Then for every formula $\varphi(x, \bar{a})$ over $A$: $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi(m, \bar{a})$ iff $\varphi \in p$ iff $\varphi(x, f(\bar{a})) \in q$ iff $\mathcal{N} \models \varphi(n, f(\bar{a}))$.
8. So $f \cup \set{(m, n)}$ is partial elementary.

### The back step

9. The inverse of a partial elementary map is partial elementary, from $\mathcal{N}$ to $\mathcal{M}$.
10. Apply steps 1 to 8 to $f^{-1}$, with the roles of $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{N}$ exchanged and $\mathcal{M}$ saturated. This adds any given $n \in N$ to the range.

### The chain

11. List $M = \set{m_\alpha : \alpha < \kappa}$ and $N = \set{n_\alpha : \alpha < \kappa}$.
12. By [[transfinite recursion|0ZHQMQ2C1N]] on $\alpha < \kappa$, build partial elementary maps $f_0 \subseteq f_1 \subseteq \dots \subseteq f_\alpha \subseteq \dots$.
13. At a successor $\alpha + 1$, add $m_\alpha$ to the domain by the forth step and then $n_\alpha$ to the range by the back step.
14. At a limit $\alpha$, put $f_\alpha = \bigcup_{\beta < \alpha} f_\beta$. It is partial elementary, since each formula mentions finitely many points, which all lie in the domain of one $f_\beta$.
15. By construction, $\operatorname{dom} f_\alpha$ is $\operatorname{dom} f_0$ together with the points $m_\beta$ and the preimages of the points $n_\beta$ for $\beta < \alpha$. So it has at most $\abs{\operatorname{dom} f_0} + 2\abs{\alpha}$ elements.
16. This cardinal is less than $\kappa$, since $\abs{\operatorname{dom} f_0} < \kappa$, $\abs{\alpha} < \kappa$ and $\kappa$ is infinite.
17. So the forth and back steps apply at every stage. Let $F = \bigcup_{\alpha < \kappa} f_\alpha$.
18. $F$ is partial elementary by step 14, its domain contains every $m_\alpha$, and its range contains every $n_\alpha$.
19. A partial elementary map preserves all atomic formulas and their negations, including $x = y$, so $F$ is an injective embedding of $\mathcal{M}$ into $\mathcal{N}$.
20. Its range is all of $N$, so $F : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{N}$ is an isomorphism that extends $f_0$.

## Idea

This is the [[back-and-forth method|6F2DB4HTJR]] of Cantor's proof for countable dense orders, run for $\kappa$ steps.
At each step a type over fewer than $\kappa$ points is copied to the other side, and saturation realizes it.
The union of the maps is an isomorphism.

## Variants

Using only the forth step gives an elementary embedding of any $\mathcal{M}' \equiv \mathcal{N}$ with $\abs{M'} \le \kappa$ into $\mathcal{N}$, the universality of saturated models.
An Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game of length $\kappa$ gives the same proof in the language of games.
