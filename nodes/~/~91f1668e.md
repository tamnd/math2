## Example

Let $R, S \colon \R^2 \to \R^2$ be the rotation by $90°$ and the scaling by $2$:
$$R(x, y) = (-y,\ x), \qquad S(x, y) = (2x,\ 2y).$$
Both are [[linear maps|0RJF668P1T]], with matrices $A_R = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A_S = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. By the [[definition of the operations|0VPCJWGMP9]], $(R + S)(x, y) = (-y, x) + (2x, 2y) = (2x - y,\ x + 2y)$.
2. $R + S$ is linear: it is the map $v \mapsto Bv$ with $B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. $B = A_R + A_S$, so the [[matrix|0YQSGNYE3K]] of the sum is the sum of the matrices.
4. $(3R)(x, y) = (-3y,\ 3x)$, with matrix $3A_R$.
5. $R - R$ is the zero map, the zero vector of $\Hom(\R^2, \R^2)$.
6. Numerical check at $(1, 1)$: $R(1, 1) = (-1, 1)$ and $S(1, 1) = (2, 2)$, with sum $(1, 3)$. Also $B(1, 1)^{\mathsf T} = (2 - 1,\ 1 + 2) = (1, 3)$.

## Remarks

- The sum $R + S$ rotates by the angle $\arctan(1/2)$ and scales by $\sqrt 5$, since $\det B = 5$ and $B$ is of the form $\begin{pmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{pmatrix}$.
- The composite $R \circ S$ is a different map: $(R \circ S)(x, y) = (-2y,\ 2x)$. Its matrix is $A_R A_S$, not $A_R + A_S$.
- Replacing $S$ by $-S$ gives $(R - S)(x, y) = (-2x - y,\ x - 2y)$, again linear.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Rotate a vector by a quarter turn, and separately double it.
Adding the two results gives a new map of the plane.
It is again linear, and its matrix is the sum of the two matrices.

### L2 human

The example shows the vector space operations of $\Hom(\R^2, \R^2)$ in coordinates.
Sums of maps correspond to sums of matrices, and multiples to multiples.
The trap is to add matrices when the question asks for the composite; the composite multiplies them.
