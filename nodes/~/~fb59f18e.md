## Description

The *Rieger-Nishimura lattice* is the set of formulas in one variable $p$, taken modulo equivalence in $\mathsf{IPC}$ and ordered by provable implication.
It is the [[Lindenbaum algebra|MGCVQXXQJH]] of $\mathsf{IPC}$ in one variable, and so the [[free Heyting algebra|GTS4DRRMZR]] on one generator $[p]$.
Its elements are the classes of Nishimura's formulas:
$$F_0 = p \wedge \neg p, \quad F_1 = p, \quad F_2 = \neg p, \quad F_{2n+3} = F_{2n+1} \vee F_{2n+2}, \quad F_{2n+4} = F_{2n+3} \to F_{2n+1},$$
for $n \geq 0$, together with $F_\infty = p \to p$.
By [[the Rieger-Nishimura theorem|~791fc2d0]], every one-variable formula is equivalent to exactly one $F_m$ with $m \in \mathbb{N} \cup \set{\infty}$.

The first elements are $F_3 = p \vee \neg p$, $F_4 = (p \vee \neg p) \to p$, equivalent to $\neg \neg p$, and $F_5 = p \vee \neg p \vee F_4$, equivalent to $\neg p \vee \neg \neg p$.
Then $F_6 = F_5 \to F_3$ is equivalent to $\neg \neg p \to p$.

The *Rieger-Nishimura ladder* is the [[Kripke model|~11b532e9]] with worlds $0, 1, 2, \dots$, where $n \leq m$ means $m = n$ or $m \leq n - 2$, and $p$ is true at $0$ only.
Its up-sets are $\emptyset = I_0$, the segments $I_k = \set{0, \dots, k - 1}$, the sets $J_k = I_k \cup \set{k + 1}$ for $k \geq 0$, and $\mathbb{N}$.
The truth set of $F_{2n+1}$ is $I_{n+1}$, that of $F_{2n+2}$ is $J_n$, that of $F_0$ is $I_0$, and that of $F_\infty$ is $\mathbb{N}$.
So the lattice is isomorphic to the Heyting algebra of all up-sets of the ladder.

The order is: $I_a \subseteq I_b$ iff $a \leq b$; $I_a \subseteq J_b$ iff $a \leq b$; $J_a \subseteq I_b$ iff $a + 2 \leq b$; and $J_a \subseteq J_b$ iff $a = b$ or $a + 2 \leq b$.

## Remarks

1. In terms of the formulas: $F_{2n+1}$ and $F_{2n+2}$ are incomparable, $F_{2n}$ implies $F_{2n+1}$, and $F_{2n+1}$ implies both $F_{2n+3}$ and $F_{2n+4}$.
2. The lattice is infinite, so the free Heyting algebra on one generator is infinite. The free Boolean algebra on one generator has four elements: the classes of $\bot$, $p$, $\neg p$ and $\top$.
3. Every element other than $[F_\infty]$ has a finite truth set, so a descending chain of such elements is eventually constant. Hence every filter is principal.
4. The quotient by the filter generated by $[F_m]$, with $m$ finite, is isomorphic to the finite algebra of elements below $[F_m]$. So the finite Heyting algebras generated by one element are exactly these quotients.
5. Bezhanishvili and de Jongh use the formulas $g_n$ and $f_n$, with another indexing; the lattice is the same.
6. The logic of the ladder as a frame, with all valuations, is studied in Section 5.5 of the lecture notes of Bezhanishvili and de Jongh.

## Explanations

### L1 human

With classical logic and one letter $p$, there are only four different statements: always false, $p$, not $p$, and always true.
In intuitionistic logic there are infinitely many.
For example, "not not $p$" and "$p$" are different, and so are "$p$ or not $p$" and "always true".
They line up in a ladder with two rails, and every statement about $p$ sits on one rung.

### L2 human

1. Compute the truth set of $\neg p$ in the ladder. A world $n$ sees $0$ iff $n = 0$ or $n \geq 2$. So $\neg p$ holds only at $1$, and its truth set is $J_0 = \set{1}$.
2. Compute $\neg \neg p$. A world $n$ sees $1$ iff $n = 1$ or $n \geq 3$. So $\neg \neg p$ holds at $0$ and $2$, the set $J_1$.
3. Compute $\neg \neg p \to p$. A world $n$ sees $2$ iff $n = 2$ or $n \geq 4$. So the formula holds at $0$, $1$ and $3$, the set $J_2$.
4. These are the truth sets of $F_2$, $F_4$ and $F_6$, as the description says.

The typical use is to decide whether one one-variable formula implies another: compare their places in the lattice.
A trap: equality of truth sets in the ladder gives equivalence in $\mathsf{IPC}$ only because the ladder is universal for one variable, which needs a proof.

### L3 human

The lattice is the free algebra on one generator in the [[variety of Heyting algebras|NG943XNQWH]], and the ladder is its dual: the frame of the 1-universal model.
For two or more generators the free Heyting algebras are much more complicated, and their duals are the $n$-universal models of Bezhanishvili and de Jongh.
In the algebra of logic, the lattice is a basic example of a Heyting algebra that is not a Boolean algebra and not finite.
