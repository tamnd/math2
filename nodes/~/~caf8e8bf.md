## Statement

Let $F$ be a field with [[characteristic|0F6ES3BRYP]] not $2$.
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$ with a [[nondegenerate|0KAN330PAH]] [[symmetric bilinear form|0AXZNE5XNH]] $B$.
An isometry of $V$ is a linear bijection $\tau \colon V \to V$ with $B(\tau x, \tau y) = B(x, y)$ for all $x, y$.
For $w \in V$ with $B(w, w) \neq 0$, the reflection in $w$ is $\tau_w(x) = x - \frac{2 B(x, w)}{B(w, w)} w$.
Write $\langle a \rangle$ for the line $F$ with the form $B(x, y) = a x y$, and $V_1 \perp V_2$ for the orthogonal direct sum.

1. Each reflection $\tau_w$ is an isometry of $V$, with $\tau_w(w) = -w$ and $\tau_w(x) = x$ for $x \in w^\perp$.
2. If $x, y \in V$ and $B(x, x) = B(y, y) \neq 0$, then there is an isometry $\tau$ of $V$ with $\tau(x) = y$. It is one reflection or a product of two reflections.
3. Let $U, U'$ be subspaces on which $B$ is nondegenerate, and let $\sigma \colon U \to U'$ be a linear bijection with $B(\sigma x, \sigma y) = B(x, y)$. Then $\sigma$ extends to an isometry of $V$.
4. (Witt cancellation.) Let $V_1, V_2, V_3$ be nondegenerate symmetric spaces over $F$. If $V_1 \perp V_2$ is isometric to $V_1 \perp V_3$, then $V_2$ is isometric to $V_3$.

The [[proof|~dc5db1b8]] uses induction on $\dim U$ in part 3.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$ is needed to write the reflection, which divides by $2$.
- Part 4 also fails in characteristic $2$. Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, $\langle 1 \rangle \perp \langle 1 \rangle \perp \langle 1 \rangle$ is isometric to $\langle 1 \rangle \perp H$, where $H$ is the plane with matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. But $\langle 1 \rangle \perp \langle 1 \rangle$ is not isometric to $H$, since $B(v, v) = 0$ for all $v$ in $H$.
- $B(w, w) \neq 0$ is needed: a reflection in an [[isotropic vector|~0d389e21]] is not defined.
- In part 3, nondegeneracy on $U$ is a hypothesis of this lemma only. [[Witt's theorem|CCB8VNQCV0]] removes it.

### Consequences

- The isometry class of the anisotropic part in the [[Witt decomposition|~1d0d0be2]] is unique.
- Over $\R$, cancellation gives the uniqueness part of Sylvester's law of inertia: $p \langle 1 \rangle \perp q \langle -1 \rangle \cong p' \langle 1 \rangle \perp q' \langle -1 \rangle$ with $p + q = p' + q'$ forces $p = p'$. Cancel $\min(p, p')$ copies of $\langle 1 \rangle$; one side is then negative definite and the other contains a vector with $B(v, v) = 1$, unless $p = p'$.

### Converse

Part 2 has a converse: if $\tau(x) = y$ for an isometry $\tau$, then $B(y, y) = B(x, x)$.
So nonzero values $B(x, x)$ are the only invariant of a single anisotropic vector under isometries.

### Generalizations

The Cartan-Dieudonné theorem says that every isometry of $V$ is a product of at most $\dim V$ reflections.
[[Witt's theorem|CCB8VNQCV0]] extends part 3 to all subspaces $U$, degenerate or not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A reflection flips one vector and keeps every vector orthogonal to it.
Two vectors with the same nonzero "length" $B(x, x)$ can be moved onto each other by one or two reflections.
From this, a symmetry between two nondegenerate subspaces extends to the whole space.
So isometric pieces can be cancelled from both sides of an isometry.

### L2 human

On $\Q^2$ with $B(x, y) = x_1 y_1 - x_2 y_2$, we move $x = (1, 0)$ onto $y = (\frac53, \frac43)$.

1. $B(x, x) = 1$ and $B(y, y) = \frac{25}{9} - \frac{16}{9} = 1$. So part 2 applies.
2. Put $w = x - y = (-\frac23, -\frac43)$. Then $B(w, w) = \frac49 - \frac{16}{9} = -\frac43 \neq 0$.
3. $B(x, w) = -\frac23$, so $\frac{2 B(x, w)}{B(w, w)} = \frac{-4/3}{-4/3} = 1$.
4. So $\tau_w(x) = x - w = y$.
5. A check that $\tau_w$ is an isometry: $\tau_w(y) = y - \frac{2 B(y, w)}{B(w, w)} w$ with $B(y, w) = -\frac{10}{9} + \frac{16}{9} = \frac23$. So $\tau_w(y) = y + w = x$, and $\tau_w$ swaps $x$ and $y$.

The typical use is the induction step of Witt's theorem: move one anisotropic vector to its image, then work in its orthogonal complement.
A trap: when $x - y$ is isotropic, the reflection $\tau_{x - y}$ does not exist.
Then use $\tau_y \circ \tau_{x + y}$, which also sends $x$ to $y$.

### L3 human

The reflections generate the orthogonal group $O(V)$, by the Cartan-Dieudonné theorem.
Witt cancellation makes the isometry classes of nondegenerate forms a cancellative monoid under $\perp$.
Its group of differences, divided by the subgroup generated by the hyperbolic plane, is the [[Witt group|KFVY0RPGF2]] of $F$.
