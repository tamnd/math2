## Statement

Let $\sigma$ be a [[signature|WDVVEYD6AT]].
Assume that the symbols of $\sigma$, the variables, the connectives, the quantifiers, $=$, the two brackets and the comma are pairwise distinct single symbols.
Take [[terms|6D3WA7N2W4]] and [[formulas|HYJ10AF6RT]] as finite [[strings|1P6EP01HZF]] built by the clauses of their definitions, with $f(t_1, \dots, t_n)$ and $R(t_1, \dots, t_n)$ for applications.

1. Every term is exactly one of the following: a variable; a constant symbol; or $f(t_1, \dots, t_n)$ for a function symbol $f$ of arity $n \ge 1$. In the last case $f$ and the terms $t_1, \dots, t_n$ are unique.
2. Every formula is exactly one of the following, and in each case the displayed parts are unique:
   - $t_1 = t_2$ with terms $t_1, t_2$;
   - $R(t_1, \dots, t_n)$ with a relation symbol $R$ of arity $n$ and terms $t_1, \dots, t_n$;
   - $\neg \psi$ with a formula $\psi$;
   - $(\psi \circ \chi)$ with $\circ \in \set{\land, \lor, \to, \leftrightarrow}$ and formulas $\psi, \chi$;
   - $\forall x \, \psi$ or $\exists x \, \psi$ with a variable $x$ and a formula $\psi$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. The symbols must be distinct. If one minus sign had arity $1$ and $2$, the string $- - x \, y$ in prefix notation would have [[two readings|~c53e6eb1]].
2. The brackets around binary connectives are needed. Without them the string $p \land q \lor r$, for atomic formulas $p, q, r$, comes from $(p \land q) \lor r$ and from $p \land (q \lor r)$.
3. Each symbol must have one arity. Otherwise $f(x)$ and $f(x, y)$ could both start a term with the same symbol $f$, and the number of arguments would not be fixed.

### Consequences

1. Functions on terms and formulas can be defined by recursion on the clauses: the value of a term, the set of [[free variables|~2fd0cd75]], [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]] and satisfaction. Unique readability makes each such definition give exactly one value.
2. Each formula has exactly one formation tree, and its main connective or quantifier is well defined.
3. The [[subformulas|HYJ10AF6RT]] of a formula and the scope of each quantifier occurrence are well defined.

### Converse

The converse direction holds by definition: every string of one of the listed forms, with well-formed parts, is a term or a formula.
So the listed forms give a bijection between the formulas and their immediate decompositions.

### Generalizations

1. In Polish (prefix) notation, as in Enderton, terms and formulas need no brackets at all, and unique readability still holds.
2. The set of terms over $\sigma$ is the free algebra over the variables, the [[term algebra|3YYKGWJJND]]. Unique readability says that its operations are injective with disjoint images.
3. In proof assistants such as Lean, terms and formulas are inductive types, and unique readability holds by construction: the constructors are injective and have disjoint images.

## History

Łukasiewicz introduced prefix notation in the 1920s, and it needs no brackets because of a unique readability property.
Textbooks of mathematical logic state unique readability as a lemma before the recursive definitions of truth and substitution.
Enderton proves it for terms and formulas in prefix notation by a counting argument.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A formula is a string of symbols, and the rules say how to build it from smaller pieces.
Unique readability says that a formula can be taken apart in only one way.
For example, $(x = 0 \lor x = 1)$ splits only into $x = 0$, the symbol $\lor$, and $x = 1$.
This is why a formula has one meaning, and not two.

### L2 human

We take apart the term $+(\cdot(x, y), S(0))$ of the signature $\set{0, S, +, \cdot}$.

1. The first symbol is $+$, of arity $2$, so the term is $+(t_1, t_2)$.
2. The term $t_1$ starts after "$+($". The string $\cdot(x, y)$ is a term, and no shorter or longer term starts there, by the prefix lemma of the proof.
3. So $t_1 = \cdot(x, y)$, and the next symbol is the comma.
4. The term $t_2$ is $S(0)$, and the closing bracket ends the term.

The typical use is to justify a recursive definition, such as "the value of $+(t_1, t_2)$ is the sum of the values of $t_1$ and $t_2$".
A trap: the comma inside $\cdot(x, y)$ is not the comma that separates $t_1$ from $t_2$, so splitting at the first comma fails.

### L3 human

Unique readability says that the term algebra and the formula algebra are free: each element has one decomposition into a constructor and its arguments.
This freeness gives the recursion theorem for terms and formulas, as for the natural numbers.
In type theory the same fact is the injectivity and disjointness of the constructors of an inductive type.
For a context-free grammar, unique readability is the statement that the grammar is unambiguous.
Mathlib needs no lemma of this kind, because `FirstOrder.Language.Term` and `FirstOrder.Language.BoundedFormula` are inductive types.
