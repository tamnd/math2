## Proof

This proof gives items 1 and 2 with a version of the [[Kleene slash|~0d19f928]], and item 3 by cut elimination.
For a set $\Gamma$ of formulas, write $\Gamma \vdash$ for $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{i}}$, and define $\Gamma \mid \varphi$ by induction on $\varphi$.

- $\Gamma \mid p$ if $\Gamma \vdash p$, and $\Gamma \mid \bot$ if $\Gamma \vdash \bot$.
- $\Gamma \mid \varphi \wedge \psi$ if $\Gamma \mid \varphi$ and $\Gamma \mid \psi$.
- $\Gamma \mid \varphi \vee \psi$ if $\Gamma \mid \varphi$ or $\Gamma \mid \psi$.
- $\Gamma \mid \varphi \to \psi$ if $\Gamma \vdash \varphi \to \psi$, and $\Gamma \mid \varphi$ implies $\Gamma \mid \psi$.

Part A: the slash implies provability.

1. Claim: $\Gamma \mid \varphi$ implies $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$. We use induction on $\varphi$.
2. Atoms, $\bot$ and implications have the provability built in.
3. For $\wedge$ and $\vee$, the claim follows from the induction hypothesis and axioms 4 and 5 of [[the Hilbert system|52XCYMK0WW]].

Part B: soundness of the slash.

4. Call $\Gamma$ *self-slashing* if $\Gamma \mid \gamma$ for every $\gamma \in \Gamma$.
5. Claim: if $\Gamma$ is self-slashing and $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$, then $\Gamma \mid \varphi$. We use induction on the derivation.
6. Members of $\Gamma$ are slashed by assumption. Modus ponens is the second half of the clause for $\to$.
7. Each axiom is an implication derivable from $\Gamma$. So the provability half of its clause holds, and also for its inner implications, by Part A and modus ponens.
8. Axiom 1, $\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)$: if $\Gamma \mid \varphi$, then $\Gamma \mid \psi$ implies $\Gamma \mid \varphi$.
9. Axiom 2: let $\Gamma \mid \varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)$ and $\Gamma \mid \varphi \to \psi$ and $\Gamma \mid \varphi$. Then $\Gamma \mid \psi$ and $\Gamma \mid \psi \to \chi$, so $\Gamma \mid \chi$.
10. Axioms 3, 4 and 5 follow from the clauses for $\wedge$ and $\vee$.
11. Axiom 6: let $\Gamma \mid \varphi \to \chi$, $\Gamma \mid \psi \to \chi$ and $\Gamma \mid \varphi \vee \psi$. Then $\Gamma \mid \varphi$ or $\Gamma \mid \psi$, and in both cases $\Gamma \mid \chi$.
12. Axiom 7: if $\Gamma \mid \bot$, then $\Gamma \vdash \bot$. By induction on $\varphi$, an inconsistent $\Gamma$ slashes every $\varphi$.

Part C: items 1 and 2.

13. The empty set is self-slashing. If $\vdash \varphi \vee \psi$, then $\emptyset \mid \varphi \vee \psi$ by step 5.
14. So $\emptyset \mid \varphi$ or $\emptyset \mid \psi$. By step 1, $\vdash \varphi$ or $\vdash \psi$. This proves item 1.
15. Let $\Gamma$ consist of Harrop formulas. Claim: $\Gamma \vdash \eta$ implies $\Gamma \mid \eta$, for every Harrop $\eta$. We use induction on $\eta$.
16. For atoms, $\bot$ and $\wedge$, the claim follows from the clauses and the induction hypothesis.
17. For $\eta = \chi \to \theta$ with $\theta$ Harrop: if $\Gamma \mid \chi$, then $\Gamma \vdash \chi$ by step 1. So $\Gamma \vdash \theta$, and $\Gamma \mid \theta$ by the induction hypothesis.
18. So $\Gamma$ is self-slashing. Steps 13 and 14 with $\Gamma$ in place of $\emptyset$ prove item 2.

Part D: item 3 by cut elimination.

19. We cite, without proof, [[cut elimination|AYRZ98Y0NW]] for the [[sequent calculus|PZX9J3VEMT]] $\mathrm{LJ}$ with quantifiers.
20. Let $\mathsf{IQC} \vdash \exists x\, \varphi(x)$, closed. Then the sequent $\Rightarrow \exists x\, \varphi(x)$ has a cut-free derivation.
21. Its last rule has a conclusion with empty antecedent. So it is not a left rule, a left contraction or a left weakening.
22. It is not a right weakening: the premise would be the empty sequent $\Rightarrow$, which is not derivable, since it is not valid.
23. So the last rule is the right rule for $\exists$, with premise $\Rightarrow \varphi(t)$ for a term $t$. This proves the existence property.
24. The same argument with the right rules for $\vee$ proves the disjunction property of $\mathsf{IQC}$.

## Idea

The slash is a predicate that mixes truth and provability: a slashed disjunction has a slashed disjunct.
Every formula derived from a self-slashing set is slashed, by a check of each axiom.
Then a derivable disjunction has a slashed disjunct, which is derivable.
Harrop formulas carry no hidden disjunction, so sets of them are self-slashing.

## Variants

1. The provability condition in the clause for implication was added by Aczel, in order to prove Part A. The version here is therefore the Aczel slash, a variant of Kleene's original slash.
2. With a clause for $\exists$ that asks for a closed term $t$ with $\Gamma \mid \varphi(t)$, the slash gives the existence property for $\mathsf{HA}$.
3. The proof [[by gluing Kripke models|~f1b3fa0e]] gives item 1 semantically.
