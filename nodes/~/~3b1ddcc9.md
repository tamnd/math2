## Summary

When the [[implication|~06eaf3d7]] $p \to q$ is true, $p$ is a sufficient condition for $q$, and $q$ is a necessary condition for $p$.
A condition $p$ is necessary and sufficient for $q$ when $p \to q$ and $q \to p$ are both true, that is, when $p \leftrightarrow q$ is true.

## Remarks

"$p$ only if $q$" means $p \to q$, and "$p$ if $q$" means $q \to p$.
"Necessary and sufficient" is another way to say "if and only if". See [[biconditional|~3bc14f64]].
For an integer $n$, "$n$ is divisible by $4$" is sufficient but not necessary for "$n$ is even".

## Explanations

### L1 human

A sufficient condition is enough to get the result.
A necessary condition is one that you cannot do without.
To have a driving licence is necessary to drive legally, but it is not sufficient: you also need a car.
