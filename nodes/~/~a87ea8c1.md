## Example

Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
We follow the [[definition of condensation|F7D849A4RZ]].

1. Step 1 takes the $2 \times 2$ determinant of each block of neighbouring entries.
2. For example, the top left block gives $2 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 1 = 3$, and the next block gives $1 \cdot 1 - 3 \cdot 2 = -5$.
3. So $M^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -5 & 8 \\ -5 & 3 & -5 \\ 8 & -5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. Step 2 takes the $2 \times 2$ determinants of $M^{(1)}$ and divides them by the interior of $M$, which is $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
5. Entry $(1, 1)$: $(3 \cdot 3 - (-5)(-5)) / 2 = -16/2 = -8$.
6. Entry $(1, 2)$: $((-5)(-5) - 8 \cdot 3) / 1 = 1$.
7. Entry $(2, 1)$: $((-5)(-5) - 3 \cdot 8) / 1 = 1$.
8. Entry $(2, 2)$: $(3 \cdot 3 - (-5)(-5)) / 2 = -8$.
9. So $M^{(2)} = \begin{pmatrix} -8 & 1 \\ 1 & -8 \end{pmatrix}$.
10. Step 3 divides by the interior of $M^{(1)}$, which is the single entry $3$: $((-8)(-8) - 1 \cdot 1) / 3 = 63/3 = 21$.
11. So condensation gives $\det M = 21$.

## Remarks

The check by another method: the [[generalized Laplace expansion|~69866df1]] along rows 1 and 2 has six terms.
Their values are $9, -1, -25, -25, -1, 64$, and their sum is $21$.
Every division in steps 5 to 10 is exact, as the [[Desnanot-Jacobi identity|~aa63b68e]] predicts for an integer matrix.
The entries of $M^{(2)}$ are the connected $3 \times 3$ minors of $M$; for example, the top left $3 \times 3$ minor of $M$ is $-8$.
If the entry $2$ in position $(2, 2)$ of $M$ is changed to $0$, then step 5 divides by $0$, and the method stops; see the [[non-example|~93e1d396]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Condensation turns a $4 \times 4$ matrix into a $3 \times 3$, then a $2 \times 2$, then a single number.
Each new number is a small $2 \times 2$ determinant, divided by a number from two steps back.

### L2 human

The typical use is a hand computation for an integer matrix with nonzero interior entries.
The divisions are exact, so a fraction in a step signals an arithmetic error.
A trap: in step 2 the divisor is the interior of the matrix from two steps back, not from the step before.
