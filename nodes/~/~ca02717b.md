## Statement

Let $L$ be a relational signature, and let $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ be $L$-[[structures|983GZBQYT4]].
The following are equivalent.

1. $\mathcal{A} \cong_p \mathcal{B}$: there is a [[back-and-forth system|~95b1c1d3]] from $\mathcal{A}$ to $\mathcal{B}$.
2. $\mathcal{A} \equiv_{\infty\omega} \mathcal{B}$: $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ satisfy the same sentences of the [[infinitary logic|PQG7VAZ1R2]] $L_{\infty\omega}$, which allows conjunctions and disjunctions of any set of formulas, with finitely many free variables in total, and finite strings of quantifiers.
3. Duplicator has a winning strategy in the infinite [[Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game|2RE7YAZK8R]] $G_\omega(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B})$.

If $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ are also countable, these conditions hold if and only if $\mathcal{A} \cong \mathcal{B}$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Countability is needed for the last sentence. The orders $(\Q, <)$ and $(\R, <)$ satisfy conditions 1 to 3, by [[this example|~9ed0f11a]], and they are not [[isomorphic|PZMGQMDN25]].
- Countability of one structure is not enough. Two infinite sets in the empty signature satisfy 1 to 3 by [[this example|~8f3c4221]], and $\N$ is not isomorphic to $\R$.
- The relational hypothesis is only a convention: with function and constant symbols, condition 1 is read with partial maps that are isomorphisms between finitely generated substructures, and the theorem holds as stated.

### Consequences

- Countable structures that satisfy the same $L_{\infty\omega}$ sentences are isomorphic.
- Scott's isomorphism theorem follows; see the outline below.
- First-order logic cannot express completeness of an order, and even $L_{\infty\omega}$ cannot, since $(\Q, <) \cong_p (\R, <)$.

### Scott's isomorphism theorem

The following is an outline only; see [[infinitary logic|VBYC17XY83]].
Let $L$ be countable and $\mathcal{A}$ countable.

1. For tuples $\bar a$ and countable ordinals $\alpha$, define formulas $\varphi^\alpha_{\bar a}$ of $L_{\omega_1\omega}$ by the recursion of the Hintikka formulas, with a conjunction at limit stages.
2. $\mathcal{A}$ has countably many tuples, so the partition of tuples by these formulas stops changing at some countable $\alpha$.
3. Let $\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}$ be the conjunction of $\varphi^\alpha_{\emptyset}$ and of $\forall \bar x \, (\varphi^\alpha_{\bar a} \to \varphi^{\alpha+1}_{\bar a})$ over all tuples $\bar a$.
4. In a model $\mathcal{B}$ of $\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}$, the maps $\bar a \mapsto \bar b$ with $\mathcal{B} \models \varphi^\alpha_{\bar a}[\bar b]$ form a back-and-forth system.
5. So a countable $\mathcal{B}$ satisfies $\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}$ if and only if $\mathcal{B} \cong \mathcal{A}$.

### Converse

The theorem is an equivalence.
Elementary equivalence is strictly weaker than conditions 1 to 3: $(\Z, <) \equiv (\Z + \Z, <)$, but they are [[not partially isomorphic|~c299ea70]].

### Generalizations

- The bounded systems $(I_m)_{m \le n}$ of the Notation of [[back-and-forth systems|~95b1c1d3]] match the sentences of $L_{\infty\omega}$ of quantifier rank at most $n$.
- With systems indexed by ordinals one gets the same match for every ordinal rank.
- For the logic $L_{\infty\omega}^k$ with $k$ variables, the game is played with $k$ pebbles.

## History

The theorem is named after Carol Karp, whose 1964 book *Languages with Expressions of Infinite Length* developed the infinitary logics.
The isomorphism theorem in the outline is named after Dana Scott.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The infinitary logic $L_{\infty\omega}$ allows sentences with infinitely long "and" and "or", but each sentence still looks at finitely many elements at a time through its quantifiers.
Karp's theorem says that such sentences see exactly as much as a back-and-forth system: two structures agree on them exactly when finite matchings can always be extended.
For countable structures this is the same as being isomorphic.

### L2 human

We see the theorem on two infinite sets $A$ and $B$ in the empty signature.

1. The finite injective maps form a back-and-forth system, so condition 1 holds.
2. Condition 2 then says that every $L_{\infty\omega}$ sentence true in $A$ is true in $B$.
3. The sentence $\bigvee_{n \ge 1} \exists x_1 \cdots \exists x_n \forall y \bigvee_i y = x_i$ says "finite", and it is false in both.
4. If $A = \N$ and $B = \Z$, both countable, the theorem gives $A \cong B$: a bijection.

A trap is to forget the bound on free variables: a conjunction of formulas with infinitely many free variables is not a formula of $L_{\infty\omega}$, and the proof of condition 1 from condition 2 uses this bound.

### L3 human

Karp's theorem shows that back-and-forth equivalence is the right notion of sameness for $L_{\infty\omega}$, as elementary equivalence is for first-order logic.
In descriptive set theory, the Scott sentence makes each isomorphism class of countable structures a Borel set.
In infinitary logic the Scott rank of a structure measures how many back-and-forth steps are needed before the partition of tuples stabilizes.
