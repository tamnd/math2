## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & -1 & 4 \\ 5 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Z}$.
We compute $\det A$ by the [[rule of Sarrus|~ca424b6f]] and check the value by a [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]].

1. Copy the first two columns to the right. The rows of the array are $2, 1, 3, 2, 1$, then $0, -1, 4, 0, -1$, then $5, 2, 1, 5, 2$.
2. Down diagonals: $2 \cdot (-1) \cdot 1 = -2$, $1 \cdot 4 \cdot 5 = 20$ and $3 \cdot 0 \cdot 2 = 0$. Their sum is $18$.
3. Up diagonals: $3 \cdot (-1) \cdot 5 = -15$, $2 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 16$ and $1 \cdot 0 \cdot 1 = 0$. Their sum is $1$.
4. The rule gives $\det A = 18 - 1 = 17$.
5. Check by expansion along column $1$, whose entries are $2, 0, 5$: $\det A = 2 \det \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 4 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} - 0 + 5 \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ -1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$.
6. The two $2 \times 2$ determinants are $-1 - 8 = -9$ and $4 + 3 = 7$.
7. So $\det A = 2 \cdot (-9) + 5 \cdot 7 = -18 + 35 = 17$, the same value.

## Remarks

The example shows that negative entries need care: the product of an up diagonal is subtracted with its own sign.
Here $-15$ is subtracted, so it adds $15$ to the total.
If the entry $a_{22} = -1$ changes to $a_{22} = t$, then $\det A = 2t + 20 - 15t - 16 = 4 - 13t$, which is linear in $t$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We add three products going down and subtract three products going up.
The down products are $-2$, $20$ and $0$; the up products are $-15$, $16$ and $0$.
So the answer is $18 - 1 = 17$.

### L2 human

The check by a column expansion uses a column with a zero, so it needs only two $2 \times 2$ determinants.
A trap: in step 3 the product $3 \cdot (-1) \cdot 5$ is negative, and forgetting its sign gives $18 - 31 = -13$, which is wrong.
