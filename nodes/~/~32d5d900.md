## Example

Let $H = \set{0, a, b, d, 1}$ with $a$ and $b$ incomparable, $a \land b = 0$, $a \lor b = d$ and $d < 1$.
This is the four-element Boolean lattice with a new top added.
It is a [[Heyting algebra|BND6A96S3D]], with $\otimes = \land$ and $x \to y$ the largest $z$ with $z \land x \le y$.
It is the algebra of open sets of [[the V-shaped poset|~faaa65ad]].

| $x \to y$ | $0$ | $a$ | $b$ | $d$ | $1$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $a$ | $b$ | $1$ | $b$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $b$ | $a$ | $a$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $d$ | $0$ | $a$ | $b$ | $1$ | $1$ |

1. Clauses 1 to 3 of [[the definition|~aa47af8f]] hold, since every Heyting algebra is a residuated lattice with $\otimes = \land$.
2. Divisibility holds: $x \land (x \to y) = x \land y$ in every Heyting algebra.
3. Prelinearity fails: $(a \to b) \lor (b \to a) = b \lor a = d \ne 1$.

So $H$ is not a BL-algebra.

## Remarks

1. The failure needs incomparable elements: every Heyting chain is prelinear.
2. Prelinearity is what makes BL-algebras subdirect products of chains; $H$ is subdirectly irreducible and not a chain.
3. Checking all $25$ pairs confirms divisibility, and one pair shows the failure of prelinearity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In fuzzy logic, of two statements, one always implies the other fully.
Here $a$ and $b$ are two unrelated statements, and neither implies the other.
So this algebra breaks a basic rule of fuzzy logic, though it is a fine model of intuitionistic logic.

### L2 human

1. Compute $a \to b$: the largest $z$ with $z \land a \le b$ is $b$, since $d \land a = a \not\le b$.
2. Compute $b \to a = a$ in the same way.
3. Their join is $d$, the element just below the top.

The typical use: it shows that intuitionistic logic does not prove $(p \to q) \lor (q \to p)$.
The trap: adding a top is essential; the four-element Boolean algebra is prelinear, since there $a \to b = b \lor \neg a = b$, and $b \to a = a$, with join $1$.
