## Statement

Let $R$ be an [[integral domain|~6bd4a5ff]] and let $p \in R$ be a [[prime element|~363b219f]].
Then $p$ is [[irreducible|~b61be5cb]].

## Remarks

The proof: let $p = a b$. Then $p \mid a b$, so $p \mid a$, say. Write $a = p c$. Then $p = p c b$, and [[cancellation|~1ab69f02]] gives $c b = 1$, so $b$ is a unit.
The converse is false in general: in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, [[$2$ is irreducible but not prime|~eb44d6b3]].
In a [[unique factorization domain|~4ed89225]], [[irreducible elements are prime|~ba488fa2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose that a prime $p$ factors as $a b$.
Then $p$ divides one factor, so that factor is at least as large as $p$ for divisibility.
The other factor must then be a unit.
