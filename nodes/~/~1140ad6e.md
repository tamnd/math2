## Proof

1. Let $b_1, \dots, b_n$ be a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $V$. By [[isomorphism with the coordinate space|059THXBRBB]], $v \mapsto [v]_B$ is a bijection $V \to \mathbb{F}_q^n$.
2. A vector of $\mathbb{F}_q^n$ is a list of $n$ entries, each with $q$ choices. By the product rule, $\abs{\mathbb{F}_q^n} = q^n$. So $\abs{V} = q^n$, by step 1. This proves part 1.
3. For part 2, an ordered basis is a list $(c_1, \dots, c_n)$. We count the lists $(c_1, \dots, c_k)$ that are [[independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]], for $k = 0, 1, \dots, n$, by induction on $k$.
4. Claim: there are $N_k = \prod_{i = 0}^{k - 1} (q^n - q^i)$ such lists. For $k = 0$, the empty list is the one independent list, and $N_0 = 1$.
5. Let $(c_1, \dots, c_k)$ be independent with $k < n$. Its span $S_k$ has the basis $c_1, \dots, c_k$, so $\abs{S_k} = q^k$ by part 1.
6. If $c \notin S_k$, then $(c_1, \dots, c_k, c)$ is independent, by part 4 of the [[dependence lemma|~ddec9473]].
7. If $c \in S_k$, then $c$ is a combination of $c_1, \dots, c_k$, so $(c_1, \dots, c_k, c)$ is dependent.
8. By steps 6 and 7, each independent list of length $k$ has exactly $q^n - q^k$ independent extensions of length $k + 1$. So $N_{k + 1} = N_k (q^n - q^k)$, which proves the claim.
9. By the [[right-size criterion|096V7H13H9]], an independent list of $n$ vectors is a basis, and each basis is independent. So the number of ordered bases is $N_n = \prod_{i = 0}^{n - 1} (q^n - q^i)$.

## Idea

Coordinates turn vectors into lists of $n$ entries from $\mathbb{F}_q$.
To build a basis, choose each new vector outside the span of the earlier ones; that span has $q^k$ elements.
