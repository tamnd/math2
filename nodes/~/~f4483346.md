## Definition

A [[topological space|~77bfcc55]] $X$ is normal when the property that follows holds.
For all disjoint [[closed sets|~dfeacff3]] $A, B \subseteq X$, there are [[open sets|~159c5927]] $U$ and $V$ with $A \subseteq U$, $B \subseteq V$ and $U \cap V = \varnothing$.

## Remarks

Names vary. Some authors call a space T4 when it is normal and [[T1|~4808a5a2]]. Such a space is [[regular|~9f103e07]] and [[Hausdorff|~a1fe0c25]].
Each [[metrizable space is normal|~07c6c39a]], and each [[compact Hausdorff space is normal|~244d957a]].
A product of two normal spaces need not be normal. The Sorgenfrey plane is an example.
[[Urysohn's lemma|~e88e4f6a]] characterises normal spaces by continuous functions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a normal space, two closed sets that do not meet can be put into two open sets that do not meet.
This is a strong condition, but it holds for all metric spaces.
