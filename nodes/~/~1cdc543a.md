## Summary

Category theory is the study of categories, functors and natural transformations.
A category consists of objects and arrows between them, with an associative composition of arrows and an identity arrow for each object.
Many constructions in algebra, topology and logic are cases of a few categorical notions, such as limits, adjoint functors and monads, so category theory gives these fields a common language.
Eilenberg and Mac Lane introduced categories in 1945 in their work on algebraic topology, and Grothendieck, Kan and Lawvere extended the theory in the next decades.
This topic collects the category theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Homological algebra and K-theory, homotopy theory, type theory and constructive mathematics, and order, lattices and universal algebra have their own topics.
