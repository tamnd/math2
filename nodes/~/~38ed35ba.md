## Statement

Let $M$ and $N$ be countably infinite [[$\omega$-categorical|A22K1X8GKD]] structures in countable signatures.
Give their [[automorphism groups|0K1SCPBNKJ]] the [[topology of pointwise convergence|FVGB8ZP67B]].
Then $M$ and $N$ are $\emptyset$-[[bi-interpretable|~80d07c0a]] if and only if $\operatorname{Aut}(M)$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(N)$ are isomorphic as [[topological groups|92700QAB5Y]].

More precisely, a $\emptyset$-bi-interpretation $(\Gamma, \Delta)$ induces a topological isomorphism $\sigma \mapsto \hat\sigma$, where $\hat\sigma(f_\Gamma \bar a) = f_\Gamma(\sigma \bar a)$.
Every topological isomorphism $\operatorname{Aut}(M) \to \operatorname{Aut}(N)$ is induced in this way by some $\emptyset$-bi-interpretation.

The proof is [[here|~06565b16]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- $\omega$-categoricity. The structures $(\mathbb{N}, <)$ and $(\mathbb{N}, +, \cdot)$ both have the trivial automorphism group, and neither is $\omega$-categorical. A $\emptyset$-[[interpretation|~0acf8019]] of the second in the first would translate its sentences computably into sentences of the [[decidable|HWC687ES0Q]] theory of $(\mathbb{N}, <)$. But the theory of $(\mathbb{N}, +, \cdot)$ is undecidable.
- Infinity. A one-element structure and a two-element structure with a unary predicate for one element both have the trivial group. Every interpretation in a one-element structure has one element, so they are not bi-interpretable.
- The topology. There are countable $\omega$-categorical structures whose automorphism groups are isomorphic as abstract groups but not as topological groups. For them, an abstract isomorphism does not come from a bi-interpretation.
- Parameters. The theorem is about interpretations without parameters. An interpretation over a tuple $\bar c$ only gives a homomorphism defined on the stabilizer $\operatorname{Aut}(M)_{\bar c}$.

### Consequences

- Every property of $\operatorname{Th}(M)$ preserved by $\emptyset$-bi-interpretation, such as [[stability|T7JW5QY6KR]], is a property of the topological group $\operatorname{Aut}(M)$.
- If $M$ has the [[small index property|RZAF6NVEXC]], every abstract isomorphism $\alpha : \operatorname{Aut}(M) \to \operatorname{Aut}(N)$ is continuous. A continuous bijective homomorphism between [[Polish groups|9C1WSBNQX9]] is a homeomorphism, so abstract isomorphism then implies bi-interpretability.
- The orbits of $G = \operatorname{Aut}(M)$ on $M^{\mathrm{eq}}$ are, up to $G$-isomorphism, the coset spaces $G / H$ of the [[open subgroups|QYCFH68RD1]] $H$; see [[imaginary elements|~25317890]].

### Converse

The direction from bi-interpretation to topological isomorphism holds for all structures, with no countability or $\omega$-categoricity.
The direction from topological isomorphism to bi-interpretation needs both, as the first hypothesis shows.

### Generalizations

The proof of the hard direction gives more.
Let $\alpha : \operatorname{Aut}(M) \to \operatorname{Aut}(N)$ be a continuous homomorphism whose image has finitely many orbits on $N$.
Then $N$ has a $\emptyset$-interpretation in $M$ that induces $\alpha$.
So, for $\omega$-categorical $M$, interpretations of other structures in $M$ without parameters are described by the topological group $\operatorname{Aut}(M)$ alone.

## History

Ahlbrandt and Ziegler proved the theorem in 1986, in their work on totally categorical theories.
The question of when the topology can be recovered from the abstract group leads to the small index property.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some infinite structures are so uniform that they are determined by their symmetries.
For these structures, if two of them have the same symmetry group, with the same notion of "close" symmetries, then each can be built from the other by formulas.
For example, the rational numbers with their order can be recovered from the group of order-preserving bijections of the rationals.
Symmetry groups are easier to compare than structures, so this turns a logic question into a group question.

### L2 human

We check the easy direction on the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ interpreted in $\mathbb{N}$, without topologies.

1. $\mathbb{N}$ and $\mathbb{Z}$ are $\emptyset$-bi-interpretable; see [[this example|~8f4d8f93]].
2. An automorphism of $\mathbb{N}$ is the identity, since each $n$ is $\emptyset$-definable.
3. So $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{N})$ is trivial, and the easy direction gives that $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z})$ is trivial as well.
4. Directly: an automorphism of the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ fixes $1$, so it fixes every integer.

The typical use is to prove that two $\omega$-categorical structures are not bi-interpretable, by finding a difference between their automorphism groups.
A trap is to apply the theorem to $\mathbb{N}$ and $\mathbb{Z}$: they are not $\omega$-categorical, and trivial groups say little there.

### L3 human

The theorem places $\omega$-categorical model theory inside the theory of oligomorphic permutation groups.
Closed subgroups of $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{N})$ with finitely many orbits on each $\mathbb{N}^n$ are, up to topological isomorphism, exactly the automorphism groups of $\omega$-categorical structures up to $\emptyset$-bi-interpretation.
The open subgroups play the role of the sorts of $M^{\mathrm{eq}}$, and continuous homomorphisms play the role of interpretations.
Questions about $M^{\mathrm{eq}}$, such as weak elimination of imaginaries, become questions about open subgroups: for example, whether each open subgroup contains the stabilizer of a finite real tuple with finite index.
