## Example

Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 & 1 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 & 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$
over $\R$, so $m = 3$ and $n = 4$.
Its reduced row echelon form has the nonzero rows $(1, 2, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 0, 1, 2)$, so $r = 2$, by the [[column space example|~e2144491]].

1. [[Column space|0F0RPPJ2TT]]: the pivot columns $(1, 2, 3)$ and $(0, 1, 1)$ of $A$ are a basis. So $\dim \operatorname{Col}(A) = 2 = r$.
2. [[Null space|0PQ2GGY1QV]]: the basis is $(-2, 1, 0, 0)$ and $(-1, 0, -2, 1)$, by the [[null space example|~9b3c620b]]. So $\dim \operatorname{Null}(A) = 2 = n - r$.
3. [[Row space|0EP73HK4C4]]: the basis is $(1, 2, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 0, 1, 2)$. So $\dim \operatorname{Row}(A) = 2 = r$.
4. Left null space: the basis is $(-1, -1, 1)$, by the [[left null space example|~b0b75fbe]]. So $\dim \operatorname{Null}(A^\top) = 1 = m - r$.
5. Row space against null space, with the [[dot product|05HQYM0R4G]]: $(1, 2, 0, 1) \cdot (-2, 1, 0, 0) = -2 + 2 = 0$ and $(1, 2, 0, 1) \cdot (-1, 0, -2, 1) = -1 + 1 = 0$.
6. Also $(0, 0, 1, 2) \cdot (-2, 1, 0, 0) = 0$ and $(0, 0, 1, 2) \cdot (-1, 0, -2, 1) = -2 + 2 = 0$. So $\operatorname{Null}(A) \perp \operatorname{Row}(A)$.
7. Column space against left null space: $(1, 2, 3) \cdot (-1, -1, 1) = -1 - 2 + 3 = 0$ and $(0, 1, 1) \cdot (-1, -1, 1) = -1 + 1 = 0$.
8. Dimensions add up: $2 + 2 = 4 = n$ in $\R^4$, and $2 + 1 = 3 = m$ in $\R^3$.
9. Row space to column space: for $u = (1, 2, 0, 1)$, $Au = (1 + 4 + 0 + 1,\ 2 + 8 + 0 + 4,\ 3 + 12 + 0 + 5) = (6, 14, 20)$.
10. The column space is the plane $b_3 = b_1 + b_2$, and $6 + 14 = 20$. So $Au$ lies in $\operatorname{Col}(A)$, as part 4 of the theorem says.
11. Adding a null vector does not change the image: $A(u + (-2, 1, 0, 0)) = Au + 0 = (6, 14, 20)$.

## Remarks

- The two bases of $\operatorname{Row}(A)$ and $\operatorname{Null}(A)$ together give four independent vectors in $\R^4$, so they form a basis of $\R^4$.
- The left null space vector $(-1, -1, 1)$ gives the condition $b_3 = b_1 + b_2$ for $Ax = b$ to have a solution.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This matrix has rank $2$, so its row space and its column space are planes.
The null space is a plane in $\R^4$, and the left null space is a line in $\R^3$.
Each pair of subspaces in the same space is orthogonal.

### L2 human

In $\R^4$ the row space and the null space are orthogonal planes that together span $\R^4$.
In $\R^3$ the column space is a plane and the left null space is its normal line.
The matrix sends the row space onto the column space, one-to-one.
