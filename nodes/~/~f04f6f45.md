## Example

Let $J$ be the $3 \times 3$ matrix with all entries $1$, and let $A = I + J = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the dot product.
The matrix is [[symmetric|0N28QN4CYA]].

1. $J (1, 1, 1) = (3, 3, 3)$, so $A (1, 1, 1) = 4 \cdot (1, 1, 1)$.
2. If $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0$, then $J x = 0$ and $A x = x$. So the plane $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0$ lies in the [[eigenspace|0AYEPBQMAW]] $E_2$ of $1$.
3. Steps 1 and 2 give a basis of eigenvectors. So the eigenvalues are $4$ and $1$, and $1$ has multiplicity $2$. The trace check is $4 + 1 + 1 = 6 = 2 + 2 + 2$.
4. The [[orthogonal projection|0Q8HZTJC7G]] onto the line $E_1$ spanned by $(1, 1, 1)$ is $P_1 = \tfrac{1}{3} J$, because $P_1 x = \tfrac{1}{3}(x_1 + x_2 + x_3)(1, 1, 1)$.
5. The projection onto the plane $E_2$ is $P_2 = I - \tfrac{1}{3} J$, because $E_2$ is the orthogonal complement of $E_1$.
6. Then $P_1 + P_2 = I$, and $4 P_1 + P_2 = \tfrac{4}{3} J + I - \tfrac{1}{3} J = I + J = A$.
7. Since $J^2 = 3J$, we get $P_1^2 = \tfrac{1}{9} \cdot 3J = P_1$ and $P_1 P_2 = \tfrac{1}{3} J - \tfrac{1}{9} \cdot 3J = 0$.

## Remarks

- The product formula gives the same projections: $\tfrac{A - I}{4 - 1} = \tfrac{1}{3} J$ and $\tfrac{A - 4I}{1 - 4} = I - \tfrac{1}{3} J$.
- The inverse is $A^{-1} = \tfrac{1}{4} P_1 + P_2 = I - \tfrac{1}{4} J$. Check: $(I + J)(I - \tfrac{1}{4} J) = I + \tfrac{3}{4} J - \tfrac{1}{4} \cdot 3J = I$.
- The square root is $\sqrt{A} = 2 P_1 + P_2 = I + \tfrac{1}{3} J$. Its square is $I + \tfrac{2}{3} J + \tfrac{1}{9} \cdot 3J = I + J$.
- $P_2$ does not depend on a choice of basis in $E_2$. With the orthonormal basis $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, -1, 0)$ and $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(1, 1, -2)$, the sum $u u^T + u' u'^T$ is again $I - \tfrac{1}{3} J$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This matrix stretches the diagonal direction $(1, 1, 1)$ by $4$.
It keeps every vector in the perpendicular plane.
So it is $4$ times the projection onto the line plus $1$ times the projection onto the plane.

### L2 human

The recipe for the spectral decomposition:

1. Find the distinct eigenvalues and their eigenspaces.
2. Find the orthogonal projection onto each eigenspace. Here the plane is the complement of the line, so $P_2 = I - P_1$.
3. Write $A = \sum_i \lambda_i P_i$, and compute a function of $A$ as $\sum_i f(\lambda_i) P_i$.

A trap: for the repeated eigenvalue $1$, there is one projection of rank $2$, not two separate terms with a forced choice of eigenvectors.
