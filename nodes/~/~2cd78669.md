## Proof

We reuse the parts of [[the proof of Kripke completeness for IPC|~1962f700]], cited below as "the IPC proof".

Part A: frame correspondence.

1. Let $F$ be locally linear, let $\mathfrak{M}$ be a model on $F$, let $w$ be a world, and let $\varphi, \psi$ be formulas.
2. Suppose $w \nVdash \varphi \to \psi$ and $w \nVdash \psi \to \varphi$. Then some $u \geq w$ forces $\varphi$ and not $\psi$, and some $v \geq w$ forces $\psi$ and not $\varphi$.
3. Both $u$ and $v$ lie in $\mathord{\uparrow} w$, so they are comparable. If $u \leq v$, persistence gives $v \Vdash \varphi$. If $v \leq u$, persistence gives $u \Vdash \psi$. Both contradict step 2.
4. So $w \Vdash (\varphi \to \psi) \vee (\psi \to \varphi)$, and every instance of the schema is valid on $F$.
5. Conversely, let $F$ not be locally linear. Choose $w \leq u$ and $w \leq v$ with $u, v$ incomparable. Put $V(p) = \mathord{\uparrow} u$ and $V(q) = \mathord{\uparrow} v$; both are up-sets.
6. Then $u \Vdash p$, and $u \nVdash q$ because $v \not\leq u$. So $w \nVdash p \to q$. Symmetrically $w \nVdash q \to p$, and prelinearity fails at $w$. This proves item 1.

Part B: soundness.

7. Let $F$ be linear, hence locally linear. The axioms of $\mathsf{IPC}$ are valid on $F$ by [[Kripke soundness|~ac029000]], and every instance of prelinearity is valid by step 4.
8. Modus ponens preserves validity in a model, by step 9 of the IPC proof. By the schema form of [[the definition|~694d0fb1]], every theorem of $\mathsf{LC}$ is valid on $F$. This proves (a) implies (b).
9. Every finite linear frame is linear, so (b) implies (c).

Part C: the canonical model of $\mathsf{LC}$.

10. Write $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{LC}} \chi$ if $\chi$ follows from $\Gamma$ and theorems of $\mathsf{LC}$ by modus ponens. The [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] holds for $\vdash_{\mathsf{LC}}$, since $\mathsf{LC}$ contains the axioms of $\mathsf{IPC}$.
11. An *$\mathsf{LC}$-theory* is a set closed under $\vdash_{\mathsf{LC}}$. Prime theories are defined as in step 11 of the IPC proof.
12. Prime extension: if $\Gamma \nvdash_{\mathsf{LC}} \chi$, there is a prime $\mathsf{LC}$-theory $T \supseteq \Gamma$ with $\chi \notin T$. Steps 12 to 19 of the IPC proof give it with $\vdash_{\mathsf{LC}}$ in place of $\vdash_{\mathsf{i}}$, since they use only modus ponens, axioms 6 and 7 and the deduction theorem.
13. Let $\mathfrak{M}_c$ be the [[canonical model|TVE4F04KME]]: prime $\mathsf{LC}$-theories, ordered by $\subseteq$, with $V_c(p) = \set{T : p \in T}$.
14. Truth lemma: $T \Vdash \chi$ iff $\chi \in T$. Steps 22 to 27 of the IPC proof apply verbatim, with step 12 in place of step 19.
15. Local linearity: let $T \subseteq U_1$ and $T \subseteq U_2$ be prime, and suppose neither of $U_1, U_2$ contains the other. Choose $\chi \in U_1 \setminus U_2$ and $\psi \in U_2 \setminus U_1$.
16. The instance $(\chi \to \psi) \vee (\psi \to \chi)$ is in $\mathsf{LC} \subseteq T$. Since $T$ is prime, $\chi \to \psi \in T$ or $\psi \to \chi \in T$.
17. If $\chi \to \psi \in T \subseteq U_1$, then $\psi \in U_1$ by modus ponens. If $\psi \to \chi \in T \subseteq U_2$, then $\chi \in U_2$. Both contradict step 15, so $\mathfrak{M}_c$ is locally linear.

Part D: a finite chain.

18. Let $\mathsf{LC} \nvdash \varphi$. Step 12 with $\Gamma = \emptyset$ gives a prime $T_0$ with $\varphi \notin T_0$, and $T_0 \nVdash \varphi$ by step 14.
19. Let $\mathfrak{M}_0$ be the restriction of $\mathfrak{M}_c$ to $W_0 = \mathord{\uparrow} T_0$. The clauses of forcing at $x$ only look at worlds above $x$, so by induction on formulas each $x \in W_0$ forces the same formulas in $\mathfrak{M}_0$ as in $\mathfrak{M}_c$.
20. So $T_0 \nVdash \varphi$ in $\mathfrak{M}_0$, and $W_0$ is linear by step 17.
21. Apply steps 30 to 36 of the IPC proof to $\mathfrak{M}_0$ and $w = T_0$. This [[filtration|6ZZSHAKG5F]] gives a finite model on $W' = \set{\Sigma(x) : x \in W_0}$, ordered by inclusion, in which $\varphi$ fails at $\Sigma(T_0)$.
22. $W'$ is linear: for $x, y \in W_0$, we have $x \subseteq y$ or $y \subseteq x$, and persistence gives $\Sigma(x) \subseteq \Sigma(y)$ or $\Sigma(y) \subseteq \Sigma(x)$.
23. A strictly increasing chain of subsets of an $n$-element set has at most $n + 1$ members. So $W'$ is a chain with at most $n + 1$ worlds, which proves item 3.
24. By contraposition, (c) implies (a). With steps 8 and 9, this proves item 2.

## Idea

Prelinearity says that the worlds above any world can not branch.
In the canonical model this is forced by primeness: one disjunct of each instance of prelinearity is already in the theory.
The part of the canonical model above a refuting theory is then a chain, and merging worlds that agree on subformulas keeps it a chain.

## Variants

1. Algebraic proof: every subdirectly irreducible Heyting algebra satisfying $(x \to y) \vee (y \to x) = 1$ is a chain, and a finite chain is a subalgebra of any longer chain. Then [[algebraic completeness|~32a20f35]] gives Dummett's form for the infinite chain.
2. Steps 15 to 17 show that the canonical model of every logic that contains $\mathsf{LC}$ is locally linear.
