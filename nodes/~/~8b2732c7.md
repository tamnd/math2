## Statement

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space over $\R$ or $\C$, and let $T \colon V \to V$ be a [[self-adjoint|~1968c73d]] linear map.
Then $V$ has an [[orthonormal basis|~0dcc6ec5]] that consists of [[eigenvectors|~606eab95]] of $T$.
The corresponding eigenvalues are real.

## Remarks

In this basis, the matrix of $T$ is a real diagonal matrix.
Equivalently, $V$ is the orthogonal direct sum of the eigenspaces of $T$.
Over $\C$, the same conclusion holds, with complex eigenvalues, for each [[normal operator|~e8cfc0a2]].
The matrix form is the [[spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices|~4421b7ac]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A self-adjoint map is a pure stretch along perpendicular axes.
The axes are the eigenvectors, and the stretch factors are the real eigenvalues.
Every symmetric real matrix is diagonal in a suitable orthonormal basis.
