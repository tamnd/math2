## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{R}$.

1. $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 0 & 1 \cdot 5 + 2 \cdot 3 \\ 0 \cdot 3 + 1 \cdot 0 & 0 \cdot 5 + 1 \cdot 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 11 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. $BA = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \cdot 1 + 5 \cdot 0 & 3 \cdot 2 + 5 \cdot 1 \\ 0 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 0 & 0 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 11 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. So $AB = BA$, and $A$ and $B$ [[commute|MX26N71ZD3]].
4. Check the [[binomial theorem|~af4d44e0]] for $m = 2$. $A + B = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 7 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, and $(A + B)^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 16 & 4 \cdot 7 + 7 \cdot 4 \\ 0 & 16 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 16 & 56 \\ 0 & 16 \end{pmatrix}$.
5. $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $2AB = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 22 \\ 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 9 & 30 \\ 0 & 9 \end{pmatrix}$.
6. $A^2 + 2AB + B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + 6 + 9 & 4 + 22 + 30 \\ 0 & 1 + 6 + 9 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 16 & 56 \\ 0 & 16 \end{pmatrix}$, which agrees with step 4.

## Remarks

The reason for the commuting is that $A = I + 2E_{12}$ and $B = 3I + 5E_{12}$ are both polynomials in the one [[matrix unit|~709aa34e]] $E_{12}$.
Compare [[this non-example|60W7PZXBVB]], where the matrices do not commute and the formula fails.

## Explanations

### L1 human

These two matrices can be multiplied in either order with the same result.
So the usual square formula works for them.

### L2 human

Both matrices have the form $aI + bE_{12}$.
Two such matrices commute, because $I$ commutes with everything and $E_{12}$ commutes with itself.
