## Example

Let $T > 0$, $A > 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ with $4 \varepsilon A < 1$.
Let $u \colon [0, T] \to [0, \infty)$ be [[continuous|0X3WGX95FE]], with
$$u(0) \le A \qquad \text{and} \qquad u(t) \le A + \varepsilon\, u(t)^2 \quad \text{for each } t \in [0, T].$$
We prove that $u(t) \le 2A$ for each $t \in [0, T]$, by [[bootstrapping|~57b93b38]].
The bootstrap hypothesis is $u(t) \le 2A$, and the bootstrap conclusion is $u(t) < 2A$.

1. Let $S = \{ t \in [0, T] : u(t) \le 2A \}$.
2. Non-empty: $u(0) \le A < 2A$, so $0 \in S$.
3. Closed: $S$ is the preimage of the closed set $(-\infty, 2A]$ under the continuous map $u$, so $S$ is closed in $[0, T]$.
4. The bootstrap step: let $t \in S$. Since $0 \le u(t) \le 2A$, we have $u(t)^2 \le 4A^2$.
5. The hypothesis then gives $u(t) \le A + \varepsilon \cdot 4A^2 = A(1 + 4\varepsilon A)$.
6. Since $4 \varepsilon A < 1$, we have $1 + 4 \varepsilon A < 2$, so $u(t) \le A(1 + 4 \varepsilon A) < 2A$.
7. Open: by step 6 and the continuity of $u$, there is $\delta > 0$ with $u(s) < 2A$ for all $s \in [0, T]$ with $\lvert s - t \rvert < \delta$. So a neighbourhood of $t$ lies in $S$.
8. $S$ is non-empty, open and closed in the [[connected|0V032VWTHD]] interval $[0, T]$, so $S = [0, T]$, by the [[open-closed argument|~d440d524]].
9. Hence $u(t) \le 2A$ for each $t$, and step 5 even gives $u(t) \le A(1 + 4\varepsilon A)$ for each $t$.

A numerical check: for $A = 1$ and $\varepsilon = \tfrac18$, we get $4 \varepsilon A = \tfrac12$, and the final bound is $u \le 1 \cdot (1 + \tfrac12) = \tfrac32$.

## Remarks

1. The inequality $u \le A + \varepsilon u^2$ alone allows two ranges. Let $r_\pm = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 4\varepsilon A}}{2\varepsilon}$ be the roots of $\varepsilon r^2 - r + A = 0$. Then $u \le A + \varepsilon u^2$ holds exactly when $u \le r_-$ or $u \ge r_+$.
2. We have $r_- = \frac{2A}{1 + \sqrt{1 - 4\varepsilon A}} \le 2A$ and $r_+ > \frac{1}{2\varepsilon} > 2A$. So the forbidden zone $(r_-, r_+)$ contains the value $2A$, and continuity stops $u$ from crossing it.
3. Iteration sharpens the bound. Put $b_0 = 2A$ and $b_{k+1} = A + \varepsilon b_k^2$. Then $u \le b_k$ for each $k$, the sequence $(b_k)$ decreases, and its limit is $r_-$.
4. For $A = 1$ and $\varepsilon = \tfrac18$: $r_- = 4 - 2\sqrt2 \approx 1.172$ and $r_+ = 4 + 2\sqrt2 \approx 6.828$. The iteration gives $b_0 = 2$, $b_1 = 1.5$, $b_2 = 1.28125$, and the values decrease towards $1.172$.
5. Without continuity the result fails. See [[the non-example with a jump|~2a6e91c7]].
6. If $4\varepsilon A \ge 1$, the quadratic $\varepsilon r^2 - r + A$ has no sign change, so the hypothesis holds for every value of $u$. The forbidden zone disappears, and the argument proves nothing.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a quantity changes smoothly over time and starts at $1$.
A rule says: whenever the quantity is at most $2$, it is in fact at most $1.5$.
Then it can never reach $2$, because just before reaching $2$ it would be at most $1.5$, far from $2$.
Since it cannot jump, it stays at most $2$, and so at most $1.5$, for all time.

### L2 human

We check the iteration of remark 3 for $A = 1$ and $\varepsilon = \tfrac18$.

1. $b_0 = 2$.
2. $b_1 = 1 + \tfrac18 \cdot 4 = 1.5$.
3. $b_2 = 1 + \tfrac18 \cdot 2.25 = 1.28125$.
4. $b_3 = 1 + \tfrac18 \cdot 1.28125^2 = 1 + \tfrac18 \cdot 1.6416015625 \approx 1.2052$.
5. The sequence decreases, because $b \mapsto 1 + b^2/8$ is increasing on $[0, \infty)$ and $b_1 < b_0$.
6. It is bounded below by $r_- = 4 - 2\sqrt2$, because $b \ge r_-$ gives $1 + b^2/8 \ge 1 + r_-^2/8 = r_-$.
7. So it converges, and its limit $L$ satisfies $L = 1 + L^2/8$ and $r_- \le L \le 2 < r_+$. Hence $L = r_- \approx 1.1716$.

The typical use is to turn a crude bootstrap bound into the sharp bound of the inequality.
A trap is to start the iteration without the continuity argument: the iteration needs the first bound $u \le b_0$, and only continuity provides it.
