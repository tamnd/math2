## Example

Problem: compute $S = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{k}{2^k} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{4} + \frac{3}{8} + \cdots$.
We use [[generalization|~478f3b5e]]: the number $\frac{1}{2}$ becomes a variable $x$.

1. Generalize: let $g(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k x^k$, a [[power series|0MZB06ST2B]]. Then $S = g\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)$.
2. Find a known relative. The geometric series gives $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} x^k = \frac{1}{1 - x}$ for $|x| < 1$.
3. Use the new tool. By [[term-by-term differentiation|0TJ8QBJX5K]], which holds inside the radius of convergence $1$, $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k x^{k-1} = \frac{1}{(1 - x)^2}$ for $|x| < 1$.
4. Multiply by $x$: $g(x) = \frac{x}{(1 - x)^2}$ for $|x| < 1$.
5. Specialize to $x = \frac{1}{2}$: $S = \frac{1/2}{1/4} = 2$.

Check with partial sums: $\frac{1}{2} = 0.5$, then $1$, $1.375$, $1.625$, $1.78125$, and the sum of the first $10$ terms is $\frac{509}{256} \approx 1.988$.

## Remarks

The given series has no variable, so differentiation could not be applied to it.
The general series $g(x)$ is a function, and its relation to the geometric series is a derivative.
The same family gives $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{k}{3^k} = g\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{3}{4}$, and a second differentiation gives $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k^2 x^k = \frac{x(1 + x)}{(1 - x)^3}$.

A direct proof also exists: the sum of the first $n$ terms is $2 - \frac{n + 2}{2^n}$, which can be proved by induction and tends to $2$.
The generalization found the value without guessing this formula.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Write the sum as a staircase: one copy of $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8} + \cdots$, plus one copy of $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8} + \cdots$, plus one copy of $\frac{1}{8} + \cdots$, and so on.
The term $\frac{3}{8}$ appears as three copies of $\frac{1}{8}$, one in each of the first three rows.
The rows add to $1$, $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{1}{4}$, and so on, which add to $2$.
The power series method gives the same $2$ by a calculation that works for every base.

### L2 human

We use the generalization once more to compute $T = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{k^2}{2^k}$.

1. Differentiate $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k x^k = \frac{x}{(1 - x)^2}$ term by term: $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k^2 x^{k-1} = \frac{1 + x}{(1 - x)^3}$.
2. Multiply by $x$ and put $x = \frac{1}{2}$: $T = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2}}{\frac{1}{8}} = 6$.
3. Check with partial sums: the first $20$ terms add to about $5.9996$.

The typical use: a factor $k$ in the terms is the trace of a derivative of $x^k$.

A trap: the formula $\frac{x}{(1 - x)^2}$ holds only for $|x| < 1$; at $x = 2$ it gives $2$, but the series $\sum k 2^k$ diverges.
