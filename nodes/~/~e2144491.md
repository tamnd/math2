## Example

Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 & 1 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 & 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$
over $\R$, with columns $a_1 = (1, 2, 3)$, $a_2 = (2, 4, 6)$, $a_3 = (0, 1, 1)$ and $a_4 = (1, 4, 5)$.

1. Subtract $2$ times row 1 from row 2, and $3$ times row 1 from row 3. The rows become $(1, 2, 0, 1)$, $(0, 0, 1, 2)$ and $(0, 0, 1, 2)$.
2. Subtract row 2 from row 3. The [[reduced row echelon form|0NNR68BFRY]] is
$$R = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
3. The [[pivots|0BCMGDHZGR]] are in columns 1 and 3. So $\{a_1, a_3\} = \{(1, 2, 3), (0, 1, 1)\}$ is a basis of the [[column space|0F0RPPJ2TT]] of $A$, and $\rank A = 2$.
4. Column 2 of $R$ is $2$ times column 1. So $a_2 = 2a_1$. Check: $2(1, 2, 3) = (2, 4, 6)$.
5. Column 4 of $R$ is $1$ times column 1 plus $2$ times column 3. So $a_4 = a_1 + 2a_3$. Check: $(1, 2, 3) + (0, 2, 2) = (1, 4, 5)$.
6. The vectors $a_1$ and $a_3$ are not multiples of each other, since the first entry of $a_3$ is $0$ and that of $a_1$ is not. So they are independent.
7. Each vector $s a_1 + t a_3 = (s,\ 2s + t,\ 3s + t)$ has third entry equal to the sum of the first two. So $\operatorname{Col}(A) = \{(u, v, w) : w = u + v\}$, a plane in $\R^3$.

## Remarks

- The vector $b = (1, 1, 1)$ is not in $\operatorname{Col}(A)$, since $1 \ne 1 + 1$. So $Ax = (1, 1, 1)$ has no solution.
- The pivot columns of $R$ are $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 0)$. Neither is in $\operatorname{Col}(A)$: for $(1, 0, 0)$, $0 \ne 1 + 0$. This is the trap of the [[example on the reduced form|~41f359cb]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The column space of this matrix is a plane, spanned by the first and third columns.
The second and fourth columns are combinations of these two, so they add nothing.

### L2 human

Row reduction finds the pivot columns and also the relations $a_2 = 2a_1$ and $a_4 = a_1 + 2a_3$.
The relations come from the non-pivot columns of $R$.
The basis must be taken from $A$, not from $R$: row operations change the column space but keep the relations among the columns.
