## Example

Statement: the number $\log_2 3$ is [[irrational|080T2TXVQV]].
Here $\log_2 3$ is the real number $x$ with $2^x = 3$; it is about $1.585$.
We give a [[proof by contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]].

1. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\log_2 3$ is rational. Then $\log_2 3 = a / b$ for integers $a$ and $b$ with $b \neq 0$.
2. Since $2^1 = 2 < 3 = 2^{x}$ and $t \mapsto 2^t$ is increasing, $\log_2 3 > 1 > 0$. So we can choose $a > 0$ and $b > 0$, by changing the signs of both if necessary.
3. By the definition of the logarithm, $2^{a/b} = 3$.
4. Raise both sides to the power $b$: $2^a = 3^b$.
5. Since $a \geq 1$, the left side $2^a$ is even.
6. The right side $3^b$ is a product of odd factors, so it is odd.
7. An integer cannot be both even and odd. This is a contradiction.
8. So the assumption in step 1 is false, and $\log_2 3$ is irrational.

## Remarks

The assumption in step 1 gives integers $a$ and $b$, and the whole proof works with them.
Without the assumption there would be nothing to compute with: the statement "$\log_2 3$ is not a fraction" is negative.

The same proof shows that $\log_m n$ is irrational whenever $m, n \geq 2$ and some prime divides one of $m$ and $n$ but not the other.
Then $m^a = n^b$ with $a, b \geq 1$ is impossible, by unique factorization.
The condition is needed: $\log_4 8 = 3/2$ is rational, because $4^{3/2} = 8$.
Here $4$ and $8$ have the same prime factor $2$, and $4^3 = 8^2 = 64$.

This number is used in the example of [[irrational a and b with a^b = 3|~0291138b]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The question is: to what power must we raise $2$ to get $3$?
The answer is a number between $1$ and $2$, because $2^1 = 2$ and $2^2 = 4$.
Suppose the answer were a fraction $a / b$.
Then $2$ multiplied by itself $a$ times would equal $3$ multiplied by itself $b$ times.
But the first product is even and the second is odd, so they cannot be equal.
So the answer is not a fraction.

### L2 human

We apply the same method to $\log_{10} 2$, a number used in counting the digits of powers of $2$.

1. Suppose $\log_{10} 2 = a / b$ with $a, b > 0$; the number is positive, because $10^0 = 1 < 2$.
2. Then $10^{a/b} = 2$, so $10^a = 2^b$.
3. The left side $10^a = 2^a 5^a$ is divisible by $5$, since $a \geq 1$.
4. The right side $2^b$ is not divisible by $5$, because its only prime factor is $2$.
5. This is a contradiction, so $\log_{10} 2$ is irrational.

The typical use: an equation between powers of different primes has no solution in positive exponents.

A trap: step 2 of the main proof needs $a, b > 0$.
If $a = 0$ were allowed, then $2^0 = 1$ is odd, and the parity argument in step 5 would fail.
