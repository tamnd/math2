## Statement

Let $S = \mathbb{C} \setminus (-\infty, 0]$ be the slit plane, the complement of the standard [[branch cut|~4a4e1678]].
Let $\operatorname{Log}$ be the [[principal branch|~30c54f34]] of the logarithm.
Then $\operatorname{Log}$ is [[holomorphic|~1a187203]] on $S$, and
$$\operatorname{Log}'(z) = \frac{1}{z}$$
for each $z \in S$.

## Remarks

Proof idea: on $S$, $\operatorname{Log}$ is continuous and it is the inverse of the [[complex exponential|~aa66b863]] on the strip $\{w : -\pi < \operatorname{Im} w < \pi\}$. The inverse function rule gives $\operatorname{Log}'(z) = 1 / e^{\operatorname{Log} z} = 1/z$.
$\operatorname{Log}$ is not continuous at the points of $(-\infty, 0)$. For $x < 0$, its values tend to $\ln|x| + i\pi$ from above the axis and to $\ln|x| - i\pi$ from below.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Away from the cut along the negative real axis, the principal logarithm behaves like the real logarithm: its derivative is $1/z$.
On the cut itself, the angle jumps from about $\pi$ to about $-\pi$, so the function is not even continuous there.
