## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ 4 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{R}$.

1. $3E_{11} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $-E_{12} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $4E_{21} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 4 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $7E_{22} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. Add the four matrices place by place: $3E_{11} - E_{12} + 4E_{21} + 7E_{22} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ 4 & 7 \end{pmatrix} = A$.
3. If $aE_{11} + bE_{12} + cE_{21} + dE_{22} = 0$, then $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = 0$, so $a = b = c = d = 0$.
4. By steps 2 and 3, the four [[matrix units|~2c685e2c]] span the space of $2 \times 2$ matrices and are linearly independent.
5. So they form a basis, the [[standard basis|0V7ZD6P7RK]] of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$, and $\dim M_2(\mathbb{R}) = 4$.

## Remarks

The coordinates of a matrix in this basis are its entries.
If the entries are listed row by row, this basis identifies $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ with $\mathbb{R}^4$.
The [[trace|07PTPSZPME]] of $A$ is the sum of the coordinates on $E_{11}$ and $E_{22}$, here $3 + 7 = 10$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each entry of a matrix tells how much of one matrix unit the matrix contains.

### L2 human

The same argument shows that the $mn$ matrix units of size $m \times n$ form a basis, for each field $F$.
