## Example

On $\Q^3$ let $B(x, y) = x^T A y$ with
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}.$$
$A$ is symmetric, so $B$ is [[symmetric|0744CN4MDA]], and the left and right radicals are equal.

1. The third row of $A$ is the sum of the first two rows: $(1, 1, 2) + (1, 2, 3) = (2, 3, 5)$.
2. So $\det A = 0$, and by the [[criteria for nondegeneracy|~acb831c2]] the form is degenerate.
3. The first two rows are not proportional, so $\rank A = 2$.
4. By part 1 of the criteria, the radical has dimension $3 - 2 = 1$.
5. The radical is the null space of $A$. Row reduction gives $x_1 + x_2 + 2x_3 = 0$ and $x_2 + x_3 = 0$.
6. With $x_3 = -1$ we get $x_2 = 1$ and $x_1 = 1$. So the radical is the line through $r = (1, 1, -1)$.
7. A check: $Ar = (1 + 1 - 2, 1 + 2 - 3, 2 + 3 - 5) = (0, 0, 0)$.
8. So $B(r, y) = (Ar)^T y = 0$ for every $y$, although $r \neq 0$.
9. On the plane $U = \operatorname{span}(e_1, e_2)$ the form has the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, with determinant $1$. So $B$ restricted to $U$ is nondegenerate.
10. $U$ meets the radical only in $0$, because $r$ has a nonzero third coordinate. So $\Q^3 = U \oplus \operatorname{rad} B$.

## Remarks

The rank $2$ of the form is the dimension of $\Q^3 / \operatorname{rad} B$.
The induced form on this quotient is nondegenerate, and step 9 gives its matrix in the basis of the classes of $e_1$ and $e_2$.
Over $\R$ the form is positive semidefinite, since $U$ has a positive definite matrix.
So here $B(v, v) = 0$ only for $v$ in the radical.
For an indefinite form this fails: $x_1 y_1 - x_2 y_2$ has $B(v, v) = 0$ for $v = (1, 1)$, but its radical is $\{0\}$.
A degenerate form can still be nonzero on many pairs: $B(e_1, e_1) = 1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This form looks harmless, but one direction is invisible to it.
The vector $(1, 1, -1)$ gives the value $0$ with every vector.
We find it as the solution of the equations given by the matrix.
Without this direction, the form on the remaining plane is nondegenerate.

### L2 human

The general recipe to find the radical of a form $x^T A y$ is the following.

1. Compute $\rank A$, or $\det A$ for a quick test.
2. Solve $A^T x = 0$ for the left radical and $A y = 0$ for the right radical.
3. Check each solution by multiplying with $A$.

The typical use is to remove the radical and to work with the nondegenerate form on the quotient.
A trap: $B(e_1, e_1) \neq 0$ and $B(e_2, e_2) \neq 0$ do not show that $B$ is nondegenerate.
Only the determinant or the radical decides.
