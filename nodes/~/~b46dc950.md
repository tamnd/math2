## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} i & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
We show that $\abs{\det A} = 1$, but $A$ is not in the [[unitary group|~4244c18b]] $U(2)$.

1. $A$ is upper triangular, so $\det A = i \cdot 1 = i$, and $\abs{\det A} = 1$.
2. The eigenvalues are the diagonal entries $i$ and $1$. Both lie on the unit circle.
3. $A^* = \begin{pmatrix} -i & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. $A^*A = \begin{pmatrix} (-i)(i) & -i \\ i & 1 + 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -i \\ i & 2 \end{pmatrix} \neq I_2$.
5. So $A$ is not [[unitary|0D6RMJ10M9]]. Its second column $(1, 1)$ has length $\sqrt{2}$.

## Remarks

- So neither the determinant nor the eigenvalues detect unitary matrices.
- The eigenvectors $(1, 0)$ for $i$ and $(1, 1 - i)$ for $1$ are not orthogonal. For a unitary matrix, they would be orthogonal.
- $A$ is not normal: $AA^* = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \neq A^*A$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The determinant of this matrix has size $1$, so the matrix keeps areas in a complex sense.
But it stretches some vectors, for example $(0, 1)$, which goes to $(1, 1)$.
So it does not keep lengths, and it is not unitary.

### L2 human

The worked check is in the Example.
The typical use: to show that a matrix is not unitary, find one column whose length is not $1$.
A trap: the test $\abs{\det A} = 1$ is necessary for a unitary matrix, but it is not sufficient.
