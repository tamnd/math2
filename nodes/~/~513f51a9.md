## Example

Let $Q(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2$ on $\R^2$, with symmetric matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \frac12 \\ \frac12 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
We show in three ways that $Q$ is [[positive definite|00QN1N6MNT]].

1. [[Completing the square|XNCV97HTZ8]]: $Q = \left( x + \frac y2 \right)^2 + \frac34 y^2$.
2. Both terms are $\ge 0$. If $Q(x, y) = 0$, then $y = 0$ and $x + \frac y2 = 0$, so $(x, y) = (0, 0)$. So $Q(v) > 0$ for $v \ne 0$.
3. Eigenvalues: $A (1, 1)^T = \frac32 (1, 1)^T$ and $A (1, -1)^T = \frac12 (1, -1)^T$. Both eigenvalues are positive, so [[the eigenvalue test|0HYC2RHD3M]] applies.
4. Leading minors: $1 > 0$ and $\det A = 1 - \frac14 = \frac34 > 0$. So [[Sylvester's criterion|0WKEF78PMV]] applies.

## Remarks

The level set $Q = 1$ is an ellipse.
Its axes lie along the eigenvectors $(1, 1)$ and $(1, -1)$, with semi-axes $\sqrt{2/3}$ and $\sqrt 2$, the values $1/\sqrt{\lambda}$.
The smallest value of $Q$ on the unit circle is $\frac12$, at $\pm (1, -1)/\sqrt 2$, and the largest is $\frac32$, at $\pm (1, 1)/\sqrt 2$.
The form $x^2 - xy + y^2$ is positive definite for the same reasons, but $x^2 + 3xy + y^2$ is not: its determinant is $1 - \frac94 < 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The cross term $xy$ can be negative, but it is small compared with $x^2 + y^2$.
For example, at $(1, -1)$ the form is $1 - 1 + 1 = 1 > 0$.
Completing the square shows that the form is never $0$ or negative except at the origin.

### L2 human

The three tests agree, as they must.
For a $2 \times 2$ form, the minors test is the fastest: check the top left entry and the determinant.
A trap: the matrix has $\frac12$ off the diagonal, not $1$.
With $1$, the determinant would be $0$, and the test would give the wrong answer.
