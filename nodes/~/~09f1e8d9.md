## Definition

*Łukasiewicz's three-valued logic* $\mathrm{Ł}_3$ is the logic of the [[logical matrix|~a38ba6c1]] $\langle \set{0, \tfrac12, 1}, \neg, \to, \set{1} \rangle$ with
$$\neg x = 1 - x, \qquad x \to y = \min(1, 1 - x + y).$$
The other connectives are abbreviations: $\varphi \lor \psi = (\varphi \to \psi) \to \psi$ and $\varphi \land \psi = \neg(\neg \varphi \lor \neg \psi)$.
So $\Gamma \models_{\mathrm{Ł}_3} \varphi$ iff every valuation that gives all members of $\Gamma$ the value $1$ gives $\varphi$ the value $1$.

The tables of $\neg$ and $\to$, with the row for $x$ and the column for $y$:

| $x$ | $\neg x$ | $x \to 0$ | $x \to \tfrac12$ | $x \to 1$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $\tfrac12$ | $\tfrac12$ | $\tfrac12$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $1$ | $0$ | $0$ | $\tfrac12$ | $1$ |

## Notation

The derived connectives have the values $x \lor y = \max(x, y)$ and $x \land y = \min(x, y)$.
We also use $x \oplus y = \neg x \to y = \min(1, x + y)$ and $x \odot y = \neg(x \to \neg y) = \max(0, x + y - 1)$.
SEP writes the axioms in Polish notation: $CpCqp$, $CCpqCCqrCpr$, $CCNpNqCqp$, $CCCpNppp$.

## Remarks

1. The derived values. If $x \le y$, then $x \to y = 1$ and $1 \to y = y = \max(x, y)$. If $x > y$, then $x \to y = 1 - x + y$ and $(1 - x + y) \to y = \min(1, x) = x = \max(x, y)$. So $(x \to y) \to y = \max(x, y)$, and De Morgan gives $\min$.
2. Wajsberg's axioms. With modus ponens as the only rule, the tautologies of $\mathrm{Ł}_3$ are the theorems of the schemas W1 $\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)$, W2 $(\varphi \to \psi) \to ((\psi \to \chi) \to (\varphi \to \chi))$, W3 $(\neg \varphi \to \neg \psi) \to (\psi \to \varphi)$ and W4 $((\varphi \to \neg \varphi) \to \varphi) \to \varphi$.
3. Check of W4. At $x = 0$: $x \to \neg x = 1$, then $1 \to 0 = 0$ and $0 \to 0 = 1$. At $x = \tfrac12$: $\tfrac12 \to \tfrac12 = 1$, then $1 \to \tfrac12 = \tfrac12$ and $\tfrac12 \to \tfrac12 = 1$. At $x = 1$: $1 \to 0 = 0$, then $0 \to 1 = 1$ and $1 \to 1 = 1$.
4. W1 to W3 are tautologies of $[0, 1]$ with the same operations, but W4 is not; see [[the four-valued counter-model|~c33691b0]].
5. Example: $p \to p$ is a tautology. Non-example: $p \lor \neg p$ is not, by [[the evaluation at $\tfrac12$|6S872VHF67]].
6. [[Strong Kleene logic|SZHA10A9X7]] has the same $\neg$, $\land$, $\lor$, and its implication $\neg x \lor y$ differs from the table above only at $x = y = \tfrac12$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The three values are false ($0$), possible ($\tfrac12$) and true ($1$).
"Not" swaps false and true and keeps possible.
"If $x$ then $y$" is fully true when $x$ is at most $y$; otherwise it loses as much truth as $x$ exceeds $y$.
Only the value $1$ counts as true.

### L2 human

We evaluate $\neg p \to (p \to q)$ at $p = \tfrac12$, $q = 0$.

1. $\neg p = \tfrac12$ and $p \to q = \tfrac12 \to 0 = \tfrac12$.
2. $\neg p \to (p \to q) = \tfrac12 \to \tfrac12 = 1$.
3. In general, $\neg x \to (x \to y) = \min(1, x + \min(1, 1 - x + y)) = \min(1, 1 + x, 1 + y) = 1$.

So this classical law is a tautology of $\mathrm{Ł}_3$.
The typical use of the tables is such a row-by-row check.
A trap: $\lor$ is not $\neg \varphi \to \psi$; that is $\oplus$.
