## Summary

Numerical analysis studies algorithms that compute approximate answers to problems of continuous mathematics, with their accuracy, their stability and their cost.
An algorithm is backward stable when each answer that it computes is the exact answer for data that differ from the given data by a small relative amount.
Numerical methods are the base of scientific computation in physics, engineering, statistics and data analysis, and their error theory tells when a computed answer can be trusted.
Von Neumann, Goldstine and Turing analysed rounding errors in matrix computations in the 1940s, and Wilkinson developed backward error analysis in the 1950s and 1960s.
This topic collects the numerical analysis pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Numerical methods for differential equations, approximation theory and optimization have their own topics.
