## Example

We derive in the [[normal modal logic|~2cd45d93]] $\mathsf{K}$ the equivalence
$$\vdash \square(p \wedge q) \leftrightarrow (\square p \wedge \square q).$$
The rules are [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]] instances, axiom $\mathrm{K}$, [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] (MP) and [[necessitation|G989VP2NGP]] (Nec).

1. $(p \wedge q) \to p$, a tautology.
2. $\square((p \wedge q) \to p)$, by Nec from 1.
3. $\square((p \wedge q) \to p) \to (\square(p \wedge q) \to \square p)$, an instance of $\mathrm{K}$.
4. $\square(p \wedge q) \to \square p$, by MP from 2 and 3.
5. $\square(p \wedge q) \to \square q$, by lines 1 to 4 with the tautology $(p \wedge q) \to q$.
6. $\square(p \wedge q) \to (\square p \wedge \square q)$, from 4 and 5 by the tautology $(\alpha \to \beta) \to ((\alpha \to \gamma) \to (\alpha \to \beta \wedge \gamma))$ and MP twice.
7. $p \to (q \to (p \wedge q))$, a tautology.
8. $\square(p \to (q \to (p \wedge q)))$, by Nec from 7.
9. $\square p \to \square(q \to (p \wedge q))$, by the $\mathrm{K}$ instance for 8 and MP.
10. $\square(q \to (p \wedge q)) \to (\square q \to \square(p \wedge q))$, an instance of $\mathrm{K}$.
11. $\square p \to (\square q \to \square(p \wedge q))$, from 9 and 10 by the tautology of the chain rule and MP.
12. $(\square p \wedge \square q) \to \square(p \wedge q)$, from 11 by the tautology $(\alpha \to (\beta \to \gamma)) \to (\alpha \wedge \beta \to \gamma)$ and MP.
13. Lines 6 and 12 give the equivalence.
14. The dual: substitute $\neg p, \neg q$ for $p, q$, and use RE of [[normal modal logic K|~b1c67ad7]] with the tautology $\neg(p \vee q) \leftrightarrow (\neg p \wedge \neg q)$. This gives $\vdash \square\neg(p \vee q) \leftrightarrow (\square\neg p \wedge \square\neg q)$.
15. Negate both sides and use $\lozenge\varphi := \neg\square\neg\varphi$: $\vdash \lozenge(p \vee q) \leftrightarrow (\lozenge p \vee \lozenge q)$.

## Remarks

The example shows that the box commutes with conjunction and the diamond commutes with disjunction in every normal modal logic.
The box does not commute with disjunction: $\square(p \vee q) \to (\square p \vee \square q)$ is not a theorem of $\mathsf{K}$.
It fails at world $1$ of [[truth sets in a three-world Kripke model|~dc32167f]], and soundness turns this into non-derivability.
For the diamond and conjunction, only one direction holds: $\vdash \lozenge(p \wedge q) \to (\lozenge p \wedge \lozenge q)$, by RM for the diamond.
The converse fails on $W = \set{1, 2, 3}$, $R = \set{(1, 2), (1, 3)}$, $V(p) = \set{2}$, $V(q) = \set{3}$.
There $\lozenge p$ and $\lozenge q$ are true at $1$, but no successor of $1$ has both $p$ and $q$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"Necessarily, it rains and it is cold" means the same as "necessarily it rains, and necessarily it is cold".
This works because "necessarily" means "in every possible situation", and "every" passes through "and".
It does not pass through "or": in each situation it may rain or be cold, with rain in one situation and cold in another.
Then neither "necessarily it rains" nor "necessarily it is cold" holds.

### L2 human

1. In a [[Kripke model|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]], $\square(p \wedge q)$ is true at $w$ when every successor has $p$ and $q$.
2. This is the same as: every successor has $p$, and every successor has $q$.
3. So the semantic check agrees with lines 6 and 12.
4. For disjunction, "every successor has $p$ or $q$" does not give "every successor has $p$" or "every successor has $q$".

The typical use is to move boxes in and out of conjunctions during derivations, for example in the proof of the [[truth lemma|~f856785f]].
A trap: the dual for the diamond is disjunction, not conjunction, so $\lozenge p \wedge \lozenge q \to \lozenge(p \wedge q)$ is not a theorem.
