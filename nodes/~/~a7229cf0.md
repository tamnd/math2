## Proof

Let $V$ be an inner product space over $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, and let $x, y \in V$.
For a complex number $z$, write $\operatorname{Re} z$ for its real part.

1. Expand by linearity in the first argument and conjugate-linearity in the second: $\norm{x + y}^2 = \ip{x}{x} + \ip{x}{y} + \ip{y}{x} + \ip{y}{y}$.
2. By conjugate symmetry, $\ip{y}{x} = \overline{\ip{x}{y}}$, and $z + \overline{z} = 2\operatorname{Re} z$. So $\norm{x + y}^2 = \norm{x}^2 + 2\operatorname{Re}\ip{x}{y} + \norm{y}^2$.
3. For $z = a + bi$ with $a, b$ real, $\operatorname{Re} z = a \leq \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = |z|$, where $|z|$ is the [[modulus|0KW2X0TFBX]]. Equality holds exactly when $b = 0$ and $a \geq 0$, that is, when $z$ is a real number $\geq 0$.
4. The [[Cauchy-Schwarz inequality|0RFWW56Y22]] gives $|\ip{x}{y}| \leq \norm{x}\,\norm{y}$.
5. By steps 3 and 4, $\operatorname{Re}\ip{x}{y} \leq |\ip{x}{y}| \leq \norm{x}\,\norm{y}$.
6. By steps 2 and 5, $\norm{x + y}^2 \leq \norm{x}^2 + 2\norm{x}\,\norm{y} + \norm{y}^2 = (\norm{x} + \norm{y})^2$.
7. Both $\norm{x + y}$ and $\norm{x} + \norm{y}$ are $\geq 0$, and the square root is increasing on $[0, \infty)$. So $\norm{x + y} \leq \norm{x} + \norm{y}$. This proves part 1.
8. Equality, first direction. If $\norm{x + y} = \norm{x} + \norm{y}$, then the squares are equal, so step 2 gives $\operatorname{Re}\ip{x}{y} = \norm{x}\,\norm{y}$.
9. Then both inequalities of step 5 are equalities. So $|\ip{x}{y}| = \norm{x}\,\norm{y}$, and $\ip{x}{y}$ is a real number $\geq 0$, by step 3.
10. By the equality case of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, $x$ and $y$ are [[linearly dependent|0EVHJFTG9Z]].
11. If $y = 0$, we are done. If $y \neq 0$, then $x = cy$ for a scalar $c$, because in a relation $\alpha x + \beta y = 0$ with $\alpha = 0$ we would get $y = 0$.
12. Then $\ip{x}{y} = c\norm{y}^2$. By step 9 this is a real number $\geq 0$, and $\norm{y}^2 > 0$, so $c$ is a real number $\geq 0$.
13. Equality, second direction: if $y = 0$, both sides equal $\norm{x}$. If $x = cy$ with $c \geq 0$ real, then $\norm{x + y} = (c + 1)\norm{y} = c\norm{y} + \norm{y} = \norm{x} + \norm{y}$, by homogeneity. This proves part 2.
14. Part 3, the norm axioms of the [[definition of a norm|08NYZ2FS6B]]. The triangle inequality is part 1.
15. Homogeneity: $\norm{ax}^2 = \ip{ax}{ax} = a\overline{a}\ip{x}{x} = |a|^2\norm{x}^2$, and both $\norm{ax}$ and $|a|\,\norm{x}$ are $\geq 0$. So $\norm{ax} = |a|\,\norm{x}$.
16. Definiteness: if $\norm{x} = 0$, then $\ip{x}{x} = 0$, so $x = 0$ by positive definiteness. So the [[induced norm|0KG4D8AP6G]] is a norm.
17. The [[metric|063W5JMY85]] axioms for $d(x, y) = \norm{x - y}$ follow. We have $d(x, y) = 0$ only for $x = y$, by step 16. Also $d(y, x) = \norm{-(x - y)} = d(x, y)$, by step 15 with $a = -1$.
18. Finally $d(x, z) = \norm{(x - y) + (y - z)} \leq d(x, y) + d(y, z)$, by part 1. This proves part 3.

## Idea

Expand $\norm{x + y}^2$, and bound the cross term $2\operatorname{Re}\ip{x}{y}$ by $2\norm{x}\,\norm{y}$ with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
The result is $(\norm{x} + \norm{y})^2$, and a square root finishes the proof.
Equality forces equality in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, so the vectors are dependent, and the real part condition makes the factor real and nonnegative.

## Variants

- In $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $\mathbb{R}^3$ the inequality is a fact of plane geometry. One side of a triangle is shorter than the sum of the other two. The proof above does not use this picture.
- For a general norm the triangle inequality is an axiom, and nothing is to prove. The [[triangle inequality for norms|0FJZMBX0ZZ]] then derives the reverse form.
