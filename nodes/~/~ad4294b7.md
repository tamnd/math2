## Statement

Let $(X, d)$ be a [[metric space|~200ea42b]] and let $K \subseteq X$.
Then $K$ is [[compact|~56dabfb9]] if and only if $K$ is [[sequentially compact|~67eb1a63]].

## Remarks

One proof of the hard direction uses the [[Lebesgue number lemma|~78ff67a3]] and the fact that a sequentially compact metric space is [[totally bounded|~4737b673]].
Both conditions are also equivalent to: $K$ is complete and totally bounded. This is the statement [[compact iff complete and totally bounded|~816043e4]].
Without a metric, the two notions differ. For example, the product $\{0, 1\}^{[0, 1]}$ is compact but not sequentially compact.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a metric space, you can test compactness with sequences. A set is compact exactly when each sequence in it has a subsequence with a limit in the set.
This test is often easier than the test with open covers.
