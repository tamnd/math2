## Statement

There are no positive integers $a$, $b$ and $c$ with $a^4 + b^4 = c^4$.

## Remarks

A stronger statement holds: $x^4 + y^4 = z^2$ has no solution in positive integers.
The theorem also follows from [[Fermat's right triangle theorem|~57f60386]], in the form that $x^4 - y^4 = z^2$ has no solution in positive integers.
The proof uses [[infinite descent|~f7936b61]] and the [[classification of Pythagorean triples|~2deb3bcd]].
Since $4$ divides every power of $2$ above $2$, the case $n = 4$ settles [[Fermat's Last Theorem|~72fd40f9]] for all such exponents.

## History

Fermat's proof of his right triangle theorem, by infinite descent, gives this case.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The equation $a^4 + b^4 = c^4$ has no whole-number solutions.
The proof assumes a solution and builds a smaller one from it, which leads to a contradiction.
