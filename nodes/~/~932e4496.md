## Summary

Inquisitive logic is the logic of a semantics in which formulas can express questions as well as statements.
Let $W$ be the set of valuations of a set of atoms of [[propositional logic|09K9MWD2QQ]].
An information state is a set $s \subseteq W$, the worlds that are still possible.
Formulas are evaluated by support, not by truth:

1. $s \models p$ iff $w(p) = 1$ for all $w \in s$;
2. $s \models \bot$ iff $s = \emptyset$;
3. $s \models \varphi \wedge \psi$ iff $s \models \varphi$ and $s \models \psi$;
4. $s \models \varphi \vee \psi$ iff $s \models \varphi$ or $s \models \psi$;
5. $s \models \varphi \to \psi$ iff for all $t \subseteq s$, $t \models \varphi$ implies $t \models \psi$.

Negation is $\neg\varphi := \varphi \to \bot$.
A formula is valid when every state supports it, and the set of valid formulas is the basic inquisitive logic InqB.
The polar question "whether $p$" is $?p := p \vee \neg p$.
The logic matters because it treats questions and their dependencies with the same tools as statements and their consequences.

## Motivation

In classical logic every formula denotes a set of worlds, the information it conveys.
A question such as "is it raining?" conveys no information, yet it is not trivial: it asks which of two sets of worlds holds.
Support semantics captures this by assigning to a formula the set of states that settle it.
A state settles $?p$ when it decides $p$ one way, so the full state $W$ does not settle $?p$, although $?p$ is true at every world.
Entailment between questions then expresses dependency: knowing the answer to one question determines the answer to another.

## Notation

1. $[\varphi] = \set{s \subseteq W : s \models \varphi}$ is the proposition expressed by $\varphi$.
2. $\mathrm{info}(\varphi) = \bigcup [\varphi]$ is the informative content of $\varphi$.
3. The alternatives of $\varphi$ are the maximal elements of $[\varphi]$.
4. $w \models \varphi$, truth at a world, means $\set{w} \models \varphi$.
5. $!\varphi := \neg\neg\varphi$ is the non-inquisitive projection of $\varphi$.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. The polar question $?p$ has the two alternatives $|p|$ and $|\neg p|$, and its informative content is $W$, see [[the polar question|~b44a00c4]].
2. The disjunction $p \vee q$ has two alternatives, $|p|$ and $|q|$; the state $|p| \cup |q|$ makes $p \vee q$ true at each of its worlds but does not support it, see [[inquisitive disjunction|~95e8120c]].
3. Every atom $p$ and every negation $\neg\varphi$ is a statement: it has exactly one alternative.
4. The dependency "whether $q$ is determined by whether $p$" is the formula $?p \to ?q$, which a state $s$ supports iff, among the worlds of $s$, the value of $q$ is a function of the value of $p$.

### Non-examples

1. The double negation law $\neg\neg\varphi \to \varphi$ is not valid: it fails for $\varphi = ?p$, see [[double negation and substitution|~0e17dbfd]].
2. The classical truth set $\set{w : w \models p \vee q}$ is not the meaning of $p \vee q$ in InqB; the meaning is the set of states included in $|p|$ or in $|q|$.

### Basic properties

1. Persistence: if $s \models \varphi$ and $t \subseteq s$, then $t \models \varphi$.
2. Empty state: $\emptyset \models \varphi$ for every $\varphi$.
3. Truth at a world is classical: $w \models \varphi$ iff $\varphi$ is true at $w$ in classical propositional logic.
4. $s \models \neg\varphi$ iff no $w \in s$ makes $\varphi$ true. So $s \models \neg\neg\varphi$ iff every $w \in s$ makes $\varphi$ true.
5. Ciardelli and Roelofsen axiomatized InqB as [[intuitionistic propositional logic|Q11N8X99FK]] plus the Kreisel-Putnam schema $(\neg\varphi \to \psi \vee \chi) \to (\neg\varphi \to \psi) \vee (\neg\varphi \to \chi)$ plus $\neg\neg p \to p$ for atoms $p$.
6. InqB is not closed under uniform substitution. The set of formulas all of whose substitution instances are valid is Medvedev's logic of finite problems.
7. Every formula is equivalent to a finite disjunction of negations, $\varphi \equiv \neg\alpha_1 \vee \dots \vee \neg\alpha_n$.

### History

Inquisitive semantics was developed by Ciardelli, Groenendijk, Mascarenhas and Roelofsen.
Ciardelli and Roelofsen published the axiomatization and the link with Medvedev's logic in "Inquisitive logic" (Journal of Philosophical Logic, 2011).
Ciardelli, Groenendijk and Roelofsen gave a book-length account in Inquisitive Semantics (Oxford University Press, 2019).
Earlier logics of questions, erotetic logics, treated questions as sets of answers; inquisitive logic instead makes questions formulas of the same language as statements.

### Common errors

1. Reading $\vee$ as classical disjunction. Truth at a world is classical, but support is not: $W \not\models p \vee \neg p$.
2. Expecting substitution instances of valid formulas to be valid. The atomic double negation law $\neg\neg p \to p$ is valid, but $\neg\neg ?p \to ?p$ is not.
3. Identifying support in a state with truth at all its worlds. The two agree exactly for statements.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A statement gives information; a question asks for it.
Inquisitive logic gives both a meaning of the same kind.
The meaning of a sentence is the set of bodies of information that settle it.
"It rains" is settled by knowing that it rains.
"Does it rain?" is settled by knowing that it rains or by knowing that it does not.

### L2 human

Take one atom $p$ and the two worlds $w_1$ with $p$ true and $w_0$ with $p$ false.

1. The states are $\emptyset$, $\set{w_1}$, $\set{w_0}$ and $W = \set{w_0, w_1}$.
2. $p$ is supported by $\emptyset$ and $\set{w_1}$.
3. $\neg p$ is supported by $\emptyset$ and $\set{w_0}$.
4. $?p = p \vee \neg p$ is supported by $\emptyset$, $\set{w_1}$ and $\set{w_0}$, but not by $W$.
5. So $?p$ is true at both worlds but is not valid: it is a question.

The typical use is to model what a question asks and which questions determine which.
A trap: the implication clause quantifies over all substates, as in the Kripke semantics of intuitionistic logic, so it is not the material conditional.

### L3 human

The implication clause is the intuitionistic Kripke clause on states ordered by reverse inclusion, and the posets of nonempty subsets of a finite set are the Medvedev frames; this explains why InqB lies between intuitionistic and classical logic.
The statements, the formulas with one alternative, form a classical fragment, and questions are disjunctions of statements.
Entailment between questions is a form of dependency, related to dependence logic, and inquisitive modal and first-order logics extend the approach to knowledge of answers and to "which" questions.
