## Example

On $\C^2$ let $h(x, y) = x^T M \bar y$ with $M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \\ -i & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, so
$$h(x, y) = x_1 \bar y_1 + i x_1 \bar y_2 - i x_2 \bar y_1 + 2 x_2 \bar y_2.$$

1. $M^* = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \overline{-i} \\ \bar i & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \\ -i & 2 \end{pmatrix} = M$. By the [[characterization|~debbafcd]], $h$ is Hermitian.
2. Let $x = (1, i)$, so $\bar x = (1, -i)$. Then $M \bar x = (1 + i(-i), -i + 2(-i)) = (2, -3i)$.
3. So $h(x, x) = 1 \cdot 2 + i \cdot (-3i) = 2 + 3 = 5$, a real number.
4. Let $y = (0, 1)$. Then $M \bar y = (i, 2)$, and $h(x, y) = 1 \cdot i + i \cdot 2 = 3i$.
5. $h(y, x) = y^T M \bar x = -3i = \overline{3i}$, as the Hermitian condition requires.
6. For $v = (v_1, v_2)$, $h(v, v) = \abs{v_1}^2 + i v_1 \bar v_2 - i v_2 \bar v_1 + 2\abs{v_2}^2 = \abs{v_1 - i v_2}^2 + \abs{v_2}^2$.
7. A check of step 6: $\abs{v_1 - i v_2}^2 = (v_1 - i v_2)(\bar v_1 + i \bar v_2) = \abs{v_1}^2 + i v_1 \bar v_2 - i v_2 \bar v_1 + \abs{v_2}^2$.
8. So $h(v, v) \geq 0$, with equality only when $v_2 = 0$ and $v_1 = i v_2 = 0$. Hence $h$ is positive definite, and it is a complex inner product.

## Remarks

Step 6 is the Hermitian version of completing the square.
For $x = (1, i)$, step 6 gives $\abs{1 - i \cdot i}^2 + \abs{i}^2 = 4 + 1 = 5$, as in step 3.
The leading principal minors of $M$ are $1$ and $\det M = 2 - i \cdot (-i) = 2 - 1 = 1$, both positive, which gives a second test of positivity.
The off-diagonal entries are not real, but they are conjugates of each other, as for every [[Hermitian matrix|0EKZCX4K3P]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

This form has complex numbers off the diagonal, but it is still Hermitian.
Its value on each nonzero vector is a positive real number.
So it is an inner product on $\C^2$, different from the standard one.

### L2 human

The recipe to test a Hermitian form for positivity is the following.

1. Check $M^* = M$.
2. Complete the square: write $h(v, v)$ as a sum of terms $c\abs{\ell(v)}^2$ with linear forms $\ell$.
3. The form is positive definite when all $c > 0$ and the $\ell$ are independent.

The typical use is to build an inner product on $\C^n$ from a matrix.
A trap: $h(x, y) = 3i$ is not real, and this is fine; only the diagonal values $h(v, v)$ must be real.
