## Example

Consider the following tree, which claims to derive $\exists x\, P(x) \to \forall y\, P(y)$ with no open assumptions.

1. $[\exists x\, P(x)]^1$. Assumption with label 1.
2. $[P(y)]^2$. Assumption with label 2, of the form $P(x)[y/x]$.
3. $P(y)$. Claimed ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ from lines 1 and 2, with eigenvariable $y$ and $\chi = P(y)$, discharging label 2.
4. $\forall y\, P(y)$. ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ from line 3. The only open assumption is line 1, and $y$ is not free in it.
5. $\exists x\, P(x) \to \forall y\, P(y)$. ${\to}\mathrm{I}$, discharging label 1.

We check that the tree is not a derivation, and that its conclusion is not valid.

6. The eigenvariable condition of ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ in the [[definition|PAQ4ZX2TX1]] requires that $y$ is not free in the conclusion $\chi$.
7. Here $\chi = P(y)$, and $y$ is free in it. So line 3 breaks the condition, and the tree is not a derivation.
8. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be the [[structure|983GZBQYT4]] with domain $\{0, 1\}$ where $P$ holds of $0$ only.
9. $\mathcal{M} \models \exists x\, P(x)$, with the witness $0$.
10. $\mathcal{M} \not\models \forall y\, P(y)$, because $P$ does not hold of $1$.
11. So $\mathcal{M}$ does not satisfy the conclusion. By [[soundness|4A84VHDTHV]], no correct derivation has this conclusion.

## Remarks

Line 3 lets the name of the witness escape from the box of ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$.
Inside the box, $y$ names one object with $P$; outside, ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ treats $y$ as an arbitrary object.
The correct use of ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ with $\chi = \exists z\, P(z)$ derives only $\exists x\, P(x) \to \exists z\, P(z)$, which is valid.
In a structure with one element the conclusion is true, so the countermodel needs two elements.
The same error with ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ instead of ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ derives $P(y) \to \forall x\, P(x)$; there $y$ is free in an open assumption.

## Explanations

### L1 human

From "somebody in the room is a doctor" we may say "call this doctor Sam".
We may then argue about Sam, but the final conclusion must not mention Sam.
The faulty tree concludes "Sam is a doctor" outside the argument, and then treats Sam as an arbitrary person.
So it ends with "everybody is a doctor", which does not follow.

### L2 human

To check a step of ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ with eigenvariable $y$, do three checks.

1. Check that $y$ is not free in the major premise $\exists x\, \varphi$. Here this holds.
2. Check that $y$ is not free in the conclusion $\chi$. Here this fails, so the step is wrong.
3. Check that $y$ is not free in an open assumption other than $\varphi[y/x]$. Here this holds.

A trap is to check only the open assumptions, as for ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$, and to forget the conclusion.
