## Example

We show that in the [[modal logic S5|VC3A2VWXND]], $\mathsf{S5} = \mathsf{KT5}$, only the last operator of a string of boxes and diamonds counts.
We use RE of [[normal modal logic K|~b1c67ad7]] and the equivalence $\lozenge\neg p \leftrightarrow \neg\square p$, which RE gives from $\neg\neg p \leftrightarrow p$.

1. The instance of $\mathrm{5}$ for $\neg p$ is $\lozenge\neg p \to \square\lozenge\neg p$.
2. By RE, $\lozenge\neg p \leftrightarrow \neg\square p$ and $\square\lozenge\neg p \leftrightarrow \square\neg\square p$. So step 1 becomes $\neg\square p \to \square\neg\square p$.
3. Contraposition gives $\neg\square\neg\square p \to \square p$, that is $\lozenge\square p \to \square p$.
4. The dual of $\mathrm{T}$ for $\square p$ is $\square p \to \lozenge\square p$. So $\mathsf{S5} \vdash \lozenge\square p \leftrightarrow \square p$.
5. Axiom $\mathrm{T}$ for $\lozenge p$ gives $\square\lozenge p \to \lozenge p$, and axiom $\mathrm{5}$ gives $\lozenge p \to \square\lozenge p$. So $\mathsf{S5} \vdash \square\lozenge p \leftrightarrow \lozenge p$.
6. By [[the axioms of S5|ECYRK9C2ZT]], $\mathsf{S5} \vdash \square p \to \square\square p$, and $\mathrm{T}$ for $\square p$ gives the converse. So $\mathsf{S5} \vdash \square\square p \leftrightarrow \square p$.
7. Dually, $\mathsf{S5} \vdash \lozenge\lozenge p \leftrightarrow \lozenge p$.
8. So $M N p \leftrightarrow N p$ for all $M, N \in \set{\square, \lozenge}$, and the same holds with any formula in place of $p$, by substitution.
9. Claim: every string $M_1 \cdots M_k p$ with $k \geq 1$ is $\mathsf{S5}$-equivalent to $M_k p$. We use [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $k$.
10. For $k = 1$ there is nothing to show. For $k > 1$, the hypothesis gives $M_2 \cdots M_k p \leftrightarrow M_k p$, and RE puts $M_1$ in front of both sides.
11. Step 8 gives $M_1 M_k p \leftrightarrow M_k p$, which completes the induction.
12. With negations, duality moves each $\neg$ to the front, so each modality of $\mathsf{S5}$ is equivalent to one of $p$, $\neg p$, $\square p$, $\lozenge p$, $\neg\square p$, $\neg\lozenge p$.
13. These six are distinct. On the frame $W = \set{1, 2}$ with $R = W \times W$, the valuation $V(p) = \set{1}$ gives truth sets $\set{1}$, $\set{2}$, $\varnothing$, $W$, $W$, $\varnothing$.
14. This separates all pairs except $\square p, \neg\lozenge p$ and $\lozenge p, \neg\square p$. The valuation $V(p) = W$ gives $\square p$ and $\lozenge p$ the set $W$, and $\neg\lozenge p$ and $\neg\square p$ the set $\varnothing$.

## Remarks

The example shows that $\mathsf{S5}$ has exactly six modalities, against fourteen in $\mathsf{S4}$.
Step 14 is needed on this frame.
Under one valuation of a universal frame, $\square p$ and $\neg\lozenge p$ are both empty unless $p$ holds everywhere or nowhere.
One valuation suffices on a frame with two classes, where $p$ holds on all of one class and on part of the other.
The universal frame validates $\mathsf{S5}$, since $W \times W$ is an equivalence relation, so [[soundness|~d189ee4b]] turns different truth sets into non-equivalence.
In $\mathsf{B}$ and in $\mathsf{S4}$, the formula $\lozenge\square p \to \square p$ of step 3 is not a theorem, and the reduction stops earlier.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In S5, "box" means "in every possible world" and "diamond" means "in some possible world".
Every world can see every other world, so the starting world does not matter.
"Possibly necessarily $p$" then says "in some world, $p$ holds in all worlds", which is just "$p$ holds in all worlds".
So any chain of boxes and diamonds is decided by its last word.

### L2 human

1. Reduce $\lozenge\square\lozenge\lozenge\square p$.
2. The last operator is $\square$, so the claim gives $\lozenge\square\lozenge\lozenge\square p \leftrightarrow \square p$.
3. Check on a universal frame: $\square p$ is true everywhere or nowhere, so every operator in front of it keeps the same value.
4. So the long formula and $\square p$ have the same truth set under every valuation.

The typical use of this reduction is to simplify formulas of $\mathsf{S5}$.
This is a first step toward a normal form of modal depth at most 1.
A trap: the reduction concerns strings of operators in front of one formula.
So $\square(p \vee \square q)$ needs a separate argument; see [[in S5, □(p ∨ □q) is equivalent to □p ∨ □q|~8b657ee8]].
