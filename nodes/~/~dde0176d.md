## Statement

Fix $n \ge 1$.
Each formula $\varphi(p_1, \dots, p_n)$ of [[infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic|~3e406741]] defines a function $f_\varphi \colon [0, 1]^n \to [0, 1]$.
Its value at $x$ is the value of $\varphi$ under the valuation $p_i \mapsto x_i$.

A *McNaughton function* is a map $f \colon [0, 1]^n \to [0, 1]$ with two properties:

1. $f$ is continuous;
2. there are finitely many affine functions $g_j(x) = m_{j0} + m_{j1} x_1 + \dots + m_{jn} x_n$, with all $m_{ji} \in \mathbb{Z}$, such that for each $x$ we have $f(x) = g_j(x)$ for some $j$.

**Theorem.**
A function $f \colon [0, 1]^n \to [0, 1]$ is $f_\varphi$ for some formula $\varphi$ iff $f$ is a McNaughton function.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. Continuity is needed. The function that is $0$ on $[0, \tfrac12]$ and $1$ on $(\tfrac12, 1]$ has integer pieces but is not definable, since every $f_\varphi$ is continuous.
2. Integer coefficients are needed. The constant $\tfrac12$ is continuous and affine, but $f_\varphi(0) \in \set{0, 1}$ for every $\varphi$, since $\set{0, 1}$ is closed under the connectives.
3. The codomain matters. The function $2 x$ has integer coefficients, but it leaves $[0, 1]$; its truncation $\min(1, 2x) = x \oplus x$ is definable.

### Consequences

1. The truth function of a formula is determined by finitely many linear pieces. So Łukasiewicz logic, unlike Gödel logic, sees the actual values and not only their order.
2. Every McNaughton function maps rational points to rational points, and maps $\set{0, 1}^n$ into $\set{0, 1}$.
3. The functions $f_\varphi$, with pointwise operations, form the free MV-algebra on $n$ generators; this uses [[Chang's completeness theorem|~e7bd3b21]].

### Converse

The theorem is an equivalence.
The direction "definable implies McNaughton" is the easy part and is proved by induction on formulas.

### Generalizations

1. The proof gives an algorithm: from the pieces of $f$ it builds a formula for $f$.
2. The constant $\tfrac12$ becomes definable if one adds truth constants, as in [[rational Pavelka logic|~4b515603]].

## History

SEP attributes the theorem to McNaughton (1951) and states it as above.
The book of Cignoli, D'Ottaviano and Mundici presents it as a main tool for free MV-algebras.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A formula of Łukasiewicz logic is a recipe that turns input values into an output value.
McNaughton's theorem lists all recipes that formulas can produce.
They are the continuous functions that are made of finitely many flat pieces, each with integer slopes and integer offset.

### L2 human

1. To show that a function is not definable, find a point where it jumps, or a piece with a non-integer coefficient.
2. To show that a function is definable, check continuity and list its pieces.
3. Then the proof gives a formula.

Example: $|2x - 1|$ on $[0, 1]$ is continuous with pieces $2x - 1$ and $1 - 2x$, so it is definable.
A trap: the coefficient $m_{j0}$ must also be an integer, so $x + \tfrac12$ is not allowed even on a small region.

### L3 human

The easy direction is an induction: $1 - f$ and $\min(1, f + g)$ are McNaughton when $f$ and $g$ are.
The hard direction has two parts.
First, the truncation $\max(0, \min(1, g))$ of each integer affine $g$ is definable, by induction on the coefficients.
Second, every continuous piecewise affine function on a convex set is a max of mins of its pieces.
Truncation commutes with max and min, and max and min are definable, so $f$ is definable.
