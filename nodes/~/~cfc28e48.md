## Summary

*Gödel-Dummett logic* $\mathsf{LC}$ is the [[intermediate logic|~57af80cc]] $\mathsf{IPC} + (p \to q) \vee (q \to p)$; see [[the definition|~694d0fb1]].
The added formula is the [[prelinearity axiom|P4T18C5736]].
Its Kripke frames are the frames in which the worlds above any world form a [[total order|0FRXBVVR03]].
By [[the completeness theorem|~2b41c6c2]], $\mathsf{LC}$ is exactly the logic of the finite linear frames.
Algebraically, it is the logic of the real interval $[0,1]$ with $x \to y = 1$ for $x \leq y$ and $x \to y = y$ otherwise.
It is the base of the Gödel logics, the fuzzy logics of comparison of truth degrees.

## Motivation

In a linear Kripke frame, knowledge grows along a single line, with no branching into alternative futures.
Such a frame validates a formula that intuitionistic logic rejects: of two propositions, one is established no later than the other.
This is the formula $(p \to q) \vee (q \to p)$.
Gödel used the finite linear algebras to show that $\mathsf{IPC}$ has no finite characteristic matrix.
Dummett then found the axiom that captures all of them at once.
So $\mathsf{LC}$ is the natural logic of truth values that are only compared, not measured.

## Notation

$\mathsf{LC}$ is also written $\mathsf{G}$ or $\mathsf{G}_\infty$.
For $n \geq 1$, we write $C_n$ for the chain with $n$ worlds, as a Kripke frame.
Its logic $\mathrm{Log}(C_n)$ is the logic of the Heyting algebra that is a chain with $n + 1$ elements.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[Weak excluded middle is a theorem of LC|~65d4934d]], so $\mathsf{KC} \subseteq \mathsf{LC}$.
2. [[The Gödel chain on the unit interval|~e93ef865]] validates prelinearity and refutes $p \vee \neg p$.
3. Each finite chain $C_n$ is a frame of $\mathsf{LC}$, and its logic is a proper extension of $\mathsf{LC}$.

### Non-examples

1. [[The fork refutes prelinearity|~84f5b36b]], so it is not a frame of $\mathsf{LC}$.
2. $\mathsf{IPC}$ itself is not $\mathsf{LC}$: the fork shows $\mathsf{IPC} \nvdash (p \to q) \vee (q \to p)$.

### Basic properties

1. [[Completeness|~2b41c6c2]]: $\mathsf{LC} \vdash \varphi$ iff $\varphi$ is valid on all finite linear frames.
2. $\mathsf{LC}$ is decidable, since a non-theorem fails on a chain with at most $n + 1$ worlds, where $n$ is the number of subformulas.
3. For a rooted frame, prelinearity is valid iff the frame is linear.
4. By SEP, adding $(A_1 \to A_2) \vee \dots \vee (A_1 \wedge \dots \wedge A_n \to A_{n+1})$ gives the logic of the linear frames with at most $n$ worlds.
5. Two equivalent axioms over $\mathsf{IPC}$ are $(p \to q \vee r) \to (p \to q) \vee (p \to r)$ and $((p \to q) \to q) \to p \vee q$.
6. $\mathsf{LC}$ is hereditarily structurally complete; see [[admissible rules|~41ee4c18]].
7. $\mathsf{LC}$ has the Craig interpolation property; it is one of [[Maksimova's seven logics|~a89c6574]].
8. $\mathsf{LC}$ lacks the disjunction property, since it proves $(p \to q) \vee (q \to p)$ and neither disjunct.

### History

Gödel introduced the finite linear matrices in his 1932 note on the intuitionistic propositional calculus.
Dummett axiomatized the logic of the infinite chain in 1959, in "A propositional calculus with denumerable matrix".
Avron later gave a cut-free [[hypersequent calculus|73J7F0FN4A]] for $\mathsf{LC}$; see [[the hypersequent derivation of prelinearity|BNCS8H51B2]].

### Common errors

1. Reading prelinearity as "$p$ implies $q$, or $q$ implies $p$, classically". It is a classical tautology, so it says nothing new in $\mathsf{CPC}$.
2. Claiming that the frames of $\mathsf{LC}$ are exactly the linear frames. A disjoint union of two chains validates $\mathsf{LC}$ and is not linear.
3. Confusing $\mathrm{Log}(C_n)$ with the $n$-valued Gödel logic. The chain $C_n$ with $n$ worlds corresponds to the algebra with $n + 1$ truth values.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine that facts become known one after another, along a single timeline.
Then, for any two facts, one of them becomes known no later than the other.
Gödel-Dummett logic is intuitionistic logic plus this one principle.
It is still weaker than classical logic: "$p$ or not $p$" can fail when $p$ becomes known only later.

### L2 human

1. To test $\varphi$ in $\mathsf{LC}$, search for a refuting model on a finite chain.
2. If there is none of size at most $n + 1$, then $\mathsf{LC} \vdash \varphi$.
3. Equivalently, evaluate $\varphi$ in the chain $\set{0, 1/n, \dots, 1}$ with the Gödel operations.

The typical use is as the simplest intermediate logic with a complete semantics by chains.
A trap: the logic of all linear frames is the same as the logic of the finite ones, but not the same as the logic of one finite chain.

### L3 human

The variety of Heyting algebras that satisfy $(x \to y) \vee (y \to x) = 1$ is the variety of Gödel algebras.
Its subdirectly irreducible members are the chains with a unique coatom, which explains completeness for chains.
Through the Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation, $\mathsf{S4.3}$ is a modal companion of $\mathsf{LC}$; compare the [[Blok-Esakia theorem|BB9TA5Q9PN]].
