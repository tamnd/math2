## Example

We prove that
$$a^3 + b^3 \ge a^2 b + a b^2 \quad \text{for all real } a \ge 0,\ b \ge 0$$
by a [[reduction to a special case|~472d0870]]: the case $b = 1$.

1. Both sides are [[homogeneous polynomials|2C3PAQ7H50]] of degree $3$. For $s > 0$, replacing $(a, b)$ by $(sa, sb)$ multiplies both sides by $s^3 > 0$, so the inequality for $(sa, sb)$ is equivalent to the inequality for $(a, b)$.
2. Case $b = 0$: the claim reads $a^3 \ge 0$, which holds since $a \ge 0$. This case is checked first, because the scaling in step 3 needs $b > 0$.
3. Case $b > 0$: take $s = 1/b$ in step 1, and let $t = a / b \ge 0$. The claim is equivalent to $t^3 + 1 \ge t^2 + t$, the case $b = 1$.
4. Move all terms to one side: $t^3 - t^2 - t + 1 = t^2 (t - 1) - (t - 1) = (t - 1)(t^2 - 1) = (t - 1)^2 (t + 1)$.
5. Since $(t - 1)^2 \ge 0$ and $t + 1 > 0$ for $t \ge 0$, the product is not negative. So $t^3 + 1 \ge t^2 + t$.
6. By step 3, the claim holds for all $a \ge 0$ and $b > 0$, and by step 2 also for $b = 0$.

A check of the factorization at $t = 2$: $t^3 - t^2 - t + 1 = 8 - 4 - 2 + 1 = 3$, and $(t - 1)^2 (t + 1) = 1 \cdot 3 = 3$.
A check of the claim at $a = 2$, $b = 1$: $a^3 + b^3 = 9$ and $a^2 b + a b^2 = 4 + 2 = 6$.

## Remarks

1. Equality holds exactly when $t = 1$ or $b = 0 = a$, that is, when $a = b$.
2. The reduction removes one variable: an inequality in $a$ and $b$ becomes an inequality in $t$ alone. One scaling fixes one quantity, here $b$, and $a$ stays free as $t$.
3. The hypothesis $a, b \ge 0$ is needed. For $a = -2$ and $b = 1$, we get $a^3 + b^3 = -7$ and $a^2 b + a b^2 = 4 - 2 = 2$, so the inequality fails. In step 5, $t = -2$ gives $t + 1 < 0$.
4. A direct proof without the reduction: $a^3 + b^3 - a^2 b - a b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)^2$. This is the factorization of step 4, multiplied by $b^3$.
5. The case split of steps 2 and 3 is a [[proof by cases|0WCP0PVET6]]. The scaling cannot reach $b = 0$ from $b > 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If we multiply $a$ and $b$ by the same positive number, both sides of the inequality are multiplied by its cube.
So only the ratio of $a$ to $b$ matters.
We can choose units so that $b$ is $1$, and then only one number, $t$, is left.
For one number, the claim becomes $(t - 1)^2 (t + 1) \ge 0$, which is a square times a positive number.

### L2 human

We use the same reduction for $a^4 + b^4 \ge a^3 b + a b^3$ with $a, b \ge 0$.

1. Both sides are homogeneous of degree $4$, and the case $b = 0$ reads $a^4 \ge 0$.
2. For $b > 0$, with $t = a / b$, the claim is $t^4 + 1 \ge t^3 + t$.
3. We have $t^4 - t^3 - t + 1 = t^3 (t - 1) - (t - 1) = (t - 1)(t^3 - 1)$.
4. The two factors $t - 1$ and $t^3 - 1$ have the same sign for $t \ge 0$, since $t^3 - 1 = (t - 1)(t^2 + t + 1)$ and $t^2 + t + 1 > 0$. So the product is not negative.
5. A check at $t = 2$: $16 + 1 = 17$ and $8 + 2 = 10$.

The typical use is a symmetric polynomial inequality with all terms of the same degree.
A trap is to set both $a = 1$ and $b = 1$: this proves only the case $a = b$.
