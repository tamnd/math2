## Proof

Let $G$ be a group of order $n = |G|$ with $1 \leq n \leq 5$, and let $e$ be its identity.
We treat the possible values of $n$ as separate [[cases|0WCP0PVET6]].

1. Case $n = 1$. Then $G = \{e\}$, so $a = b = e$ for all $a, b \in G$, and $ab = ba$.
2. Case $n = 2$, $3$ or $5$. Then $n$ is prime, so $G$ is cyclic, because [[each group of prime order is cyclic|05MM4EK4SA]].
3. In the case of step 2, $G$ is abelian, because [[cyclic groups are abelian|04RG02S6BA]].
4. Case $n = 4$. By the result that the [[order of an element divides the order of the group|08V7JHS3QZ]], each $g \in G$ has [[order|0WE2NHZJ7B]] $1$, $2$ or $4$.
5. Subcase: some $g \in G$ has order $4$. Then $e, g, g^2, g^3$ are four different elements, because $g$ has order $4$.
6. In the subcase of step 5, these four elements are all of $G$, since $|G| = 4$. So $G = \langle g \rangle$ is cyclic, and it is abelian by [[the same result|04RG02S6BA]].
7. Subcase: no element of $G$ has order $4$. By step 4, each $g \in G$ has order $1$ or $2$, so $g^2 = e$.
8. In the subcase of step 7, $G$ is abelian, because [[a group of exponent 2 is abelian|~2dc226b7]].

The cases cover each $n$ from $1$ to $5$, so each group of order at most $5$ is abelian.

## Idea

Small orders are prime or equal to $4$.
A group of prime order is cyclic.
A group of order $4$ is cyclic or has exponent $2$.

## Variants

Step 2 can avoid the result on prime order, with only the [[order of an element|0WE2NHZJ7B]].
For $n$ prime, take $g \neq e$.
Its order divides $n$ and is not $1$, so it is $n$, and $G = \langle g \rangle$.
