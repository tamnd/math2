## Proof

We write the difference of the squared norms as a quadratic form, and we apply the quadratic form statement to a self-adjoint operator.

1. Let $v \in V$. By the [[definition of the adjoint|0ST8QF9T3T]], $\norm{Tv}^2 = \ip{Tv}{Tv} = \ip{T^* T v}{v}$.
2. By [[the algebraic properties|~2b450581]], $(T^*)^* = T$. So the definition for $T^*$ gives $\norm{T^* v}^2 = \ip{T^* v}{T^* v} = \ip{T T^* v}{v}$.
3. Subtracting step 2 from step 1 gives $\norm{Tv}^2 - \norm{T^* v}^2 = \ip{S v}{v}$, where $S = T^* T - T T^*$.
4. Part 1, first direction. If $T$ is [[normal|0ENZN5G4CV]], then $S = 0$. Step 3 gives $\norm{Tv}^2 = \norm{T^* v}^2$, and both norms are nonnegative, so $\norm{Tv} = \norm{T^* v}$.
5. Part 1, second direction. The algebraic properties give $(T^* T)^* = T^* T^{**} = T^* T$ and $(T T^*)^* = T^{**} T^* = T T^*$. So $S$ is [[self-adjoint|0MB8HQW495]].
6. If $\norm{Tv} = \norm{T^* v}$ for all $v$, then step 3 gives $\ip{Sv}{v} = 0$ for all $v$.
7. By part 3 of [[the quadratic form statement|~4dc472c7]], a self-adjoint operator with zero quadratic form is $0$. So $S = 0$, and $T$ is normal.
8. Part 2. Let $T$ be normal. By positivity of the [[inner product|0VGSYBWHV2]], $Tv = 0$ if and only if $\norm{Tv} = 0$. By part 1, this holds if and only if $\norm{T^* v} = 0$, that is, $T^* v = 0$.
9. Part 3. By the algebraic properties, $(T - \lambda I)^* = T^* - \overline{\lambda} I$.
10. Expanding gives $(T - \lambda I)^* (T - \lambda I) = T^* T - \lambda T^* - \overline{\lambda} T + \abs{\lambda}^2 I$.
11. Expanding gives $(T - \lambda I)(T - \lambda I)^* = T T^* - \overline{\lambda} T - \lambda T^* + \abs{\lambda}^2 I$.
12. Since $T^* T = T T^*$, the right sides of steps 10 and 11 are equal. So $T - \lambda I$ is normal.
13. Apply part 2 to the normal operator $T - \lambda I$. Its adjoint is $T^* - \overline{\lambda} I$ by step 9. So $(T - \lambda I) v = 0$ if and only if $(T^* - \overline{\lambda} I) v = 0$. This is part 3.

## Idea

The identity $\norm{Tv}^2 - \norm{T^* v}^2 = \ip{(T^* T - T T^*) v}{v}$ turns the condition on norms into a condition on a quadratic form.
The operator $T^* T - T T^*$ is self-adjoint, so it is $0$ exactly when its quadratic form is $0$.
Parts 2 and 3 follow by looking at the vectors with norm $0$.

## Variants

Over $\mathbb{C}$, step 7 can use part 1 of the quadratic form statement, which needs no self-adjointness.
Over $\mathbb{R}$, the self-adjointness of $S$ in step 5 is necessary.
