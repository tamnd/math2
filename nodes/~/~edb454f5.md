## Summary

A Nelson algebra is a Kleene algebra with a weak implication $\to$ that satisfies a residuation law relative to the negation $\sim$; see [[the definition|~407b91d8]].
Nelson algebras are the algebras of Nelson's [[constructive logic|TJ345K2153]] with strong negation, where $\sim x$ means "$x$ is refuted".
Each [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]] $H$ gives a Nelson algebra on the pairs $(a, b)$ with $a \land b = 0$, called a twist structure.
Every Nelson algebra embeds in a twist structure, so Nelson algebras are Heyting algebras "with a second coordinate for refutation".

## Motivation

In intuitionistic logic, $\neg x$ means that $x$ leads to a contradiction, which is an indirect notion.
Nelson proposed a negation with direct evidence: a refutation of $x \land y$ is a refutation of $x$ or of $y$, and so on.
So a proposition carries two pieces of evidence, one for and one against it.
The twist structure makes this literal: an element is a pair (verification, refutation) in a Heyting algebra.

## Notation

We write $(A, \land, \lor, \to, \sim, 0, 1)$, and $x \Rightarrow y := (x \to y) \land (\sim y \to \sim x)$ for the strong implication.
For a Heyting algebra $H$, the twist structure is $H^{\bowtie} = \set{(a, b) \in H \times H : a \land b = 0}$.
Its operations are $(a, b) \land (c, d) = (a \land c, b \lor d)$, $(a, b) \lor (c, d) = (a \lor c, b \land d)$, $\sim(a, b) = (b, a)$ and $(a, b) \to (c, d) = (a \to c, a \land d)$.
Its constants are $1 = (1, 0)$ and $0 = (0, 1)$.
Rasiowa called these algebras N-lattices, and some texts say quasi-pseudo-Boolean algebras.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[The three-element Nelson chain|~e8311f8f]], the twist structure over the two-element Heyting algebra.
2. [[The five-element Nelson chain|~0e98da42]], the twist structure over the three-element Heyting chain.
3. Every twist structure $H^{\bowtie}$ over a Heyting algebra $H$.
4. Every Boolean algebra, with $\sim x = \neg x$ and $x \to y = \neg x \lor y$; it is the twist structure over itself restricted to pairs $(a, \neg a)$.

### Non-examples

1. [[The four-element De Morgan algebra with two fixed points|~43aa03ed]]: it fails the Kleene condition, so no implication makes it a Nelson algebra.
2. The three-element Heyting chain $0 < h < 1$, with its pseudo-complement $\neg$ as $\sim$, fails $\sim \sim x = x$: $\neg \neg h = 1$.

### Basic properties

1. $x \to x = 1$ and $1 \to x = x$.
2. The order is recovered by the strong implication: $x \le y$ iff $x \Rightarrow y = 1$.
3. The weak implication does not determine the order: $x \to y = 1$ does not imply $x \le y$.
4. The relation $x \equiv y$ iff $x \to y = y \to x = 1$ is a lattice congruence, and $A / {\equiv}$ is a Heyting algebra.
5. The map $x \mapsto ([x], [\sim x])$ embeds $A$ in the twist structure over $A / {\equiv}$.
6. Nelson algebras form a variety, and they are the equivalent algebraic semantics of Nelson's logic $\mathbf{N3}$.

### History

Nelson introduced constructive logic with strong negation in his 1949 paper "Constructible falsity".
Rasiowa developed the algebraic theory of N-lattices in the 1950s.
Vakarelov and Fidel found the representation by twist structures over Heyting algebras.
Spinks and Veroff later showed that Nelson algebras are term-equivalent to a class of residuated lattices.

### Common errors

1. Reading $x \to y = 1$ as $x \le y$. In a Nelson algebra this needs the strong implication.
2. Expecting $x \land \sim x = 0$. The Kleene condition only gives $x \land \sim x \le y \lor \sim y$.
3. Confusing $\sim$ with the intuitionistic negation $x \to 0$. In twist structures, $(a, b) \to 0 = (\neg a, a)$, while $\sim(a, b) = (b, a)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In everyday reasoning, we can have evidence for a claim and evidence against it.
A Nelson algebra keeps track of both.
The strong negation swaps the two kinds of evidence.
No claim has both kinds at once, but a claim can have neither.

### L2 human

1. Take $H = \set{0, 1}$. The pairs with $a \land b = 0$ are $(1, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, $(0, 1)$.
2. Read them as "true", "unknown" and "false".
3. Strong negation swaps true and false and fixes unknown.
4. Compute $(0, 0) \to (0, 1) = (0 \to 0, 0 \land 1) = (1, 0)$: from "unknown", anything follows weakly.
5. Compute $(0, 0) \Rightarrow (0, 1)$: it is $(1, 0) \land ((1, 0) \to (0, 0)) = (1, 0) \land (0, 0) = (0, 0)$.

The typical use: questions about Nelson algebras reduce to questions about pairs in Heyting algebras.
The trap: the weak implication behaves like intuitionistic implication only on the first coordinate.

### L3 human

In Nelson's logic the strong negation commutes with the other connectives by De Morgan laws, and on the first coordinate of a twist structure the logic behaves as intuitionistic logic.
The twist construction is a functor from Heyting algebras to Nelson algebras, and the quotient by $\equiv$ is a functor back.
Dropping the condition $a \land b = 0$ gives the twist structures of the [[paraconsistent|C7YWCR87QJ]] Nelson logic $\mathbf{N4}$.
As residuated lattices, Nelson algebras make $\mathbf{N3}$ an axiomatic extension of a substructural logic.
