## Statement

Let $R$ be a [[commutative ring|~79bc52c4]] and let $M$ be an ideal of $R$.
Then the [[quotient ring|~fcc6d7c6]] $R/M$ is a [[field|~92ce2090]] if and only if $M$ is a [[maximal ideal|~3f9701c1]].

## Remarks

The proof uses the [[correspondence theorem|~3efc94e9]]: the ideals of $R/M$ are the images of the ideals of $R$ that contain $M$.
So $M$ is maximal if and only if $R/M$ is nonzero and has only the two ideals $\{0\}$ and $R/M$.
A nonzero commutative ring with only these two ideals is a field, by the [[converse for fields|~0ebcb6d4]].
For example, $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is a field because $p\mathbb{Z}$ is maximal for $p$ prime.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The quotient by an ideal is a field exactly when the ideal is as large as possible.
In the integers, $(5)$ is maximal, and $\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$ is a field.
The ideal $(6)$ is not maximal, and $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ is not a field.
