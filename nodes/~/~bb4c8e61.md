## Summary

The calculus of variations studies the minimization of functionals, which assign numbers to functions or to geometric objects, and optimal control studies how to choose the input of a dynamical system so that a cost becomes minimal.
The Euler-Lagrange equation is the differential equation that each smooth minimizer of an integral functional satisfies.
Variational principles express many laws of physics, and the methods of the area give existence theorems for nonlinear partial differential equations, minimal surfaces and optimal transport, with uses in engineering and economics.
Johann Bernoulli posed the brachistochrone problem in 1696, and Pontryagin and his coworkers proved the maximum principle of optimal control in the 1950s.
This topic collects the calculus of variations and optimal control pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Optimization, partial differential equations, measure and integration, and control and systems theory have their own topics.
