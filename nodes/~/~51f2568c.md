## Example

Let $L = \set{<, c_0, c_1, c_2, \dots}$ with constants $c_i$.
Let $T$ be the [[theory of dense linear orders without endpoints|2W4S2H4VYA]] together with the axioms $c_i < c_{i+1}$ for all $i$.
We show that $T$ is complete and has exactly three countable models.

1. Let $T_k$ be $\mathrm{DLO}$ with $c_0 < \dots < c_k$, in the signature with $c_0, \dots, c_k$. Two countable models of $T_k$ split into $k + 2$ open intervals around the constants, each a countable dense order without endpoints. Matching them gives an isomorphism, so $T_k$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical.
2. By the [[Łoś-Vaught test|ZR7Y5C235P]], each $T_k$ is complete. A sentence of $L$ mentions finitely many constants, so it is decided by some $T_k \subseteq T$. So $T$ is complete.
3. Let $M$ be a countable model. Exactly one case holds. (a) The $c_i$ have no upper bound. (b) They have a least upper bound $s$. (c) They are bounded, and the set $U$ of upper bounds has no least element.
4. Case (a): $M$ is the union of $(-\infty, c_0)$, the points $c_i$, and the intervals $(c_i, c_{i+1})$. Each interval is a countable dense order without endpoints, so two models in case (a) are isomorphic interval by interval.
5. Case (b): $M$ adds the point $s$ and the interval $(s, \infty)$; nothing lies between all $c_i$ and $s$, since such a point would be a smaller upper bound. Case (c): $M$ adds the set $U$, a countable dense order without endpoints. In each case, two models are isomorphic piece by piece.
6. All three cases occur in $\Q$: take $c_i = i$ for (a), $c_i = -\frac{1}{i+1}$ for (b), and increasing rationals with real limit $\sqrt{2}$ for (c).
7. An isomorphism maps each $c_i$ to $c_i$, so it keeps boundedness and the existence of a least upper bound. The three models are pairwise non-isomorphic.

## Remarks

The model in case (a) is the [[prime model|S8W40PYNHX]]: each element lies below $c_0$, at some $c_i$ or between $c_i$ and $c_{i+1}$, and its type is isolated.
The model in case (c) is the countable [[saturated model|RMTZ5J64FX]].
The type $p(x) = \set{c_i < x : i \in \N}$ is not isolated: it is omitted in case (a) and realized in cases (b) and (c).
Case (b) is the third model in the proof of [[Vaught's never-two theorem|~ea319e25]]: the prime model after naming an element $d$ that realizes $p$.
$T$ is [[small|~166b8a9e]], since three countable models realize only countably many types.
With $m$ unary predicates that split the order into $m$ dense colors, and the constants all of the first color, case (b) splits by the color of $s$, and the theory has exactly $m + 2$ countable models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the fractions with their order and mark an increasing sequence of points.
The marks can run off to infinity, they can creep up to a last point, or they can creep up to a gap.
These three pictures are the only countable possibilities, and they are different.
So the rules have exactly three countable worlds.

### L2 human

We check the claim in step 7 for models (b) and (c) built in step 6.

1. In model (b), $0$ is above all $c_i = -\frac{1}{i+1}$, and every rational $q < 0$ is below some $c_i$.
2. So $0$ is the least upper bound.
3. In model (c), an upper bound is a rational $q > \sqrt{2}$. There is a rational $q'$ with $\sqrt{2} < q' < q$, which is a smaller upper bound.
4. An isomorphism would carry the least upper bound of (b) to one of (c), which does not exist.

The typical use is as the standard example of a theory with finitely many, but more than one, countable models.
A trap is to call model (b) saturated; over the parameter $s$, the type of a point between all $c_i$ and $s$ is consistent and omitted.
