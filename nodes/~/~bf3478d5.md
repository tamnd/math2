## Statement

Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]], or more generally a division ring, and let $n \ge 1$.
The only [[two-sided ideals|0CD87SFM2N]] of $M_n(F)$ are $\{0\}$ and $M_n(F)$.
So $M_n(F)$ is a [[simple ring|62M0NC3E2T]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The field is needed to divide by a nonzero entry. Over $\mathbb{Z}$, the set $M_n(2\mathbb{Z})$ of matrices with even entries is a proper nonzero two-sided ideal of $M_n(\mathbb{Z})$.
- The ideal must be two-sided. The matrices whose second column is zero form a nonzero proper left ideal of $M_2(F)$.

### Consequences

- Every nonzero ring homomorphism from $M_n(F)$ to another ring is injective, since its kernel is a two-sided ideal.
- For $n \ge 2$, there is no ring homomorphism with $1 \mapsto 1$ from $M_n(F)$ to a nonzero commutative ring. Its kernel would be a proper ideal, hence $\{0\}$, and then $M_n(F)$ would be commutative.
- In particular, the matrices of [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]] zero do not form a two-sided ideal: $E_{12}$ has trace $0$, but $E_{21} E_{12} = E_{22}$ has trace $1$.

### Generalizations

- For any ring $R$ with identity, the two-sided ideals of $M_n(R)$ are the sets $M_n(I)$ for the ideals $I$ of $R$.
- So $M_n(R)$ is simple when $R$ is [[simple|J41Y7CCE6B]].
- By the Wedderburn-Artin theorem, every simple ring with the descending chain condition on left ideals is some $M_n(D)$ with $D$ a division ring.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An ideal is a set of matrices that is closed under addition and under multiplication by any matrix on either side.
The theorem says: if such a set contains one nonzero matrix, it contains every matrix.

### L2 human

The [[proof|~a8e6a3b7]] uses the [[matrix units|~2c685e2c]] as tools.
Multiplying a nonzero matrix on the left and on the right by matrix units isolates one nonzero entry.
Dividing by that entry gives a matrix unit, and the matrix units add up to the identity.

### L3 human

The left ideals of $M_n(F)$ are the sets $\{A : \ker A \supseteq W\}$ for subspaces $W$ of $F^n$, so there are many left ideals.
The simplicity of $M_n(F)$ expresses that $F^n$ is, up to isomorphism, the only simple module.
