## Summary

$\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ is the subsystem of [[second-order arithmetic|DC60FYCSPM]] with comprehension for $\Pi^1_1$ formulas of the [[analytical hierarchy|M6JGF4TPM6]], as in its [[definition|~999ea337]].
It is the strongest of the big five systems of reverse mathematics, and it is impredicative.
Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ it is equivalent to the Cantor-Bendixson theorem for closed sets of reals.

## Motivation

The Cantor-Bendixson theorem writes a closed set as the union of a perfect set and a countable set.
The perfect part is defined by a statement about all paths through a tree, which is a $\Pi^1_1$ property.
[[ATR0|~ff80e550]] can iterate the removal of isolated points, but it cannot in general form the perfect kernel, and $\Pi^1_1$ comprehension is exactly the strength needed.

## Notation

$\mathrm{HJ}(X)$ denotes the hyperjump of $X$: the set of indices $e$ such that $\Phi^X_e$ is the characteristic function of a tree on $\N$ with no infinite path.

## Remarks

### Examples

- $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ proves that the [[set of indices of computable well-orderings|~02091eff]] exists.
- The [[full structure on the natural numbers|~9a3df793]] is a model of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$.
- Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ is equivalent to each of these: every closed subset of $\R$ is the union of a perfect set and a countable set; every tree on $\N$ has a perfect kernel; every countable abelian group is the direct sum of a divisible group and a reduced group.

### Non-examples

- The [[arithmetical sets|~46cb5771]] form a model of $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, but not of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$.
- The [[computable sets|~f2c0a555]] do not form a model of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$, because they do not form a model of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.

### Basic properties

- $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ is equivalent to $\Sigma^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$, because a set exists if and only if its complement exists.
- Over $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ is equivalent to the statement that $\mathrm{HJ}(X)$ exists for every set $X$.
- $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ proves every axiom of $\mathsf{ATR}_0$, and it proves the consistency of $\mathsf{ATR}_0$, so it is strictly stronger.
- The [[proof-theoretic ordinal|C72RBH0CDR]] of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ is $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$ in Buchholz's notation.
- $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ is equivalent over $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ to the statement that for every set $X$ there is a countable coded [[β-model|J0Z2GNJZ2W]] that contains $X$.

### Common errors

- $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ does not prove $\Pi^1_2$ comprehension.
- The definition of $\mathrm{HJ}(X)$ uses trees on $\N$, which may be infinitely branching. For finitely branching trees, "no infinite path" is arithmetical by Kőnig's lemma, and the hyperjump would collapse.

## Explanations

### L1 human

$\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ forms sets defined by one quantifier over all sets.
It is the strength needed to split a closed set into its perfect part and its countable part.

### L2 human

A proof in $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ usually forms the set of nodes of a tree above which there is no infinite path, or the hyperjump of a parameter.
A reversal to $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ usually shows that the theorem gives $\mathrm{HJ}(X)$, or the set of nodes of a tree with an infinite path above them.

### L3 human

$\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ is impredicative: the set formed by the scheme is defined by a quantifier over all sets, including the set being formed.
For this reason it lies beyond the predicative systems such as [[ATR0|~ff80e550]].
