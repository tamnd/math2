## Example

On $\Q^3$ let $B(x, y) = x^T A y$ with
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
so $B(x, y) = x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1 + x_2 y_3 - x_3 y_2$.

1. $A^T = -A$ and the diagonal is zero, so $B$ is [[alternating|08ZQAKBKHF]].
2. The radical is the null space of $A$: $x_2 = 0$, $-x_1 + x_3 = 0$ and $-x_2 = 0$. So it is the line through $w = (1, 0, 1)$.
3. A check: $Aw = (0, -1 + 1, 0) = (0, 0, 0)$.
4. By the [[criteria for nondegeneracy|~acb831c2]], $\rank A = 3 - 1 = 2$, and $B$ is degenerate.
5. $B(e_1, e_2) = 1$, so $(u_1, v_1) = (e_1, e_2)$ is a symplectic pair.
6. In the basis $(e_1, e_2, w)$ the matrix is $J \oplus 0$, since $B(e_1, w) = 0$, $B(e_2, w) = -1 + 1 = 0$ and $B(w, w) = 0$.

## Remarks

The rank $2$ is even, as the [[classification|~a52a9f11]] says.
Every alternating form on a space of odd dimension is degenerate, since its rank is even and so smaller than the dimension.
Here $\det A = 0$, which is $0^2$, a square as part 6 of the classification says.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An alternating form on a space of odd dimension always has a vector that pairs to zero with everything.
Here that vector is $(1, 0, 1)$.
On the plane of $e_1$ and $e_2$ the form is the standard symplectic form.

### L2 human

To find the radical of an alternating form $x^T A y$, solve $Ax = 0$.
Since $A^T = -A$, the left and the right radical are the same.
A trap: an alternating $3 \times 3$ matrix can have rank $2$ or $0$, never $1$ or $3$.
