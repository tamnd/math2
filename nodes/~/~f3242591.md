## Summary

[[Hilbert's program|P1RW6JWX73]] asked for a finitistic consistency proof for all of mathematics.
By Gödel's [[second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]], this is not possible in full.
A *partial realization* of the program is a formal system $T$ that proves a significant part of ordinary mathematics, together with a finitistic proof that $T$ is conservative over a finitistic system for a class of finitistically meaningful sentences.
The main example is [[WKL0|~e7c6fa0e]]: it is conservative over [[primitive recursive arithmetic|WCXH72T8D8]] for $\Pi^0_2$ sentences, and [[reverse mathematics|7SQS69T17R]] shows how much mathematics it proves.
Stephen Simpson called this approach finitistic reductionism.

## Motivation

Hilbert divided mathematics into a finitistic part, with concrete statements about finite objects, and an ideal part, with infinite sets.
The ideal part was to be justified by a finitistic proof that it proves no false finitistic statement.
Gödel's theorems show that no finitistic proof can do this for all of mathematics.
Reverse mathematics gives a precise measure of which ideal axioms a theorem needs, so one can ask which theorems can still be reduced to finitism.

## Remarks

### Examples

- The [[Π⁰₂-conservativity of WKL0 over PRA|~de3d00df]] has a proof that can be formalized in $\mathsf{PRA}$. So $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, which proves the [[Heine-Borel theorem for [0,1]|~ebb0edca]], is a partial realization.
- A [[Π⁰₂ theorem of WKL0 has a primitive recursive witness|~497f54b6]]: the ideal proof yields a finitistic computation.
- Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ proves the Brouwer fixed point theorem, the Hahn-Banach theorem for separable Banach spaces, and Gödel's completeness theorem for countable languages. So their $\Pi^0_2$ consequences have finitistic proofs.

### Non-examples

- The [[Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem is not finitistically reducible|~6c9f5ed6]]: with it, $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves the consistency of $\mathsf{PRA}$.
- $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ and every stronger system of the big five prove $\Pi^0_1$ sentences that $\mathsf{PRA}$ does not prove, so they are not partial realizations over $\mathsf{PRA}$.

### Predicative reductionism

There is a similar program one level higher.
[[ATR0|~ff80e550]] proves the same $\Pi^1_1$ sentences as Feferman's predicative system $\mathsf{IR}$, whose proof-theoretic ordinal is $\Gamma_0$.
So theorems of $\mathsf{ATR}_0$, such as the comparability of countable well-orderings, are reducible to [[predicative mathematics|~531abfcc]].
This view is called predicative reductionism, and it is due to Friedman and Simpson.

### Common errors

- A partial realization does not prove the consistency of all of mathematics. It reduces only the theorems of one system, and only for one class of sentences.
- The conservativity result must have a finitistic proof. A model-theoretic proof in a strong set theory does not by itself give a finitistic reduction.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hilbert hoped to justify the use of infinite sets by finite methods.
That is not possible for all of mathematics.
But for a large part of analysis, the part provable in $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, the hope comes true: every concrete consequence of it has a finite proof.

### L2 human

To check that a theorem is finitistically reducible in this sense, one shows that $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves it from weak König's lemma.
One way to show that it is not is to show that it implies arithmetical comprehension over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, because then it proves the consistency of $\mathsf{PRA}$.

### L3 human

Simpson presented finitistic reductionism in a paper of 1988 on partial realizations of Hilbert's program.
The approach changes the question from "is mathematics consistent" to "which part of mathematics is conservative over finitism", and reverse mathematics answers the second question theorem by theorem.
