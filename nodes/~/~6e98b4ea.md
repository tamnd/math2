## Proof

The signature $L$ has the constant symbol $0$, so we may use the extension test of [[the tests for quantifier elimination|~e415d689]].
Let $M, N$ be algebraically closed fields with $N$ $\omega$-[[saturated|RMTZ5J64FX]].
Let $A \subseteq M$ be a finitely generated substructure, let $f \colon A \to N$ be an embedding and let $b \in M$.
We must extend $f$ to an embedding of the subring $A[b]$ generated by $A$ and $b$.

### Reduction to fields

1. $A$ is a subring of the field $M$, so it is an integral domain. Let $F \subseteq M$ be its [[field of fractions|0F2PTGX1A2]].
2. The map $a / c \mapsto f(a) / f(c)$, for $a, c \in A$ and $c \neq 0$, is well defined and is an embedding of fields $F \to N$ that extends $f$. We call it $f$ again.
3. It suffices to extend $f$ to the field $F(b)$, since $A[b] \subseteq F(b)$.

### Case 1: $b$ is algebraic over $F$

1. Let $q \in F[x]$ be the [[minimal polynomial|0GK1WB9R98]] of $b$ over $F$. Its image $q^f$, with $f$ applied to the coefficients, is irreducible over $f(F)$, since $f$ is an isomorphism $F \to f(F)$.
2. $N$ is algebraically closed, so $q^f$ has a root $c \in N$.
3. $F(b) \cong F[x]/(q)$ and $f(F)(c) \cong f(F)[x]/(q^f)$, by the theory of [[simple extensions|0P6SBR7YM2]]. So there is an isomorphism $g \colon F(b) \to f(F)(c)$ that extends $f$, with $g(b) = c$.

### Case 2: $b$ is transcendental over $F$

1. Let $\bar a$ be a finite tuple that generates $A$ as a ring. Every polynomial over $A$ in one variable has the form $p(\bar a, x)$ with $p \in \Z[\bar y, x]$.
2. Let $\Sigma(x)$ be the set of formulas $p(f(\bar a), x) \neq 0$, for all $p \in \Z[\bar y, x]$ such that $p(\bar a, x)$ is a nonzero polynomial in $x$. Its parameters form the finite tuple $f(\bar a)$.
3. $\Sigma$ is finitely satisfiable in $N$: finitely many nonzero polynomials have finitely many roots in total, and $N$ is infinite, as every algebraically closed field is.
4. By $\omega$-saturation, some $c \in N$ satisfies $\Sigma$. Then $c$ is not a root of any nonzero polynomial over $f(A)$, and after clearing denominators, over $f(F)$. So $c$ is [[transcendental|HMF8G4EBDX]] over $f(F)$.
5. $F(b)$ and $f(F)(c)$ are both fields of rational functions in one variable. So $f$ extends to an isomorphism $g \colon F(b) \to f(F)(c)$ with $g(b) = c$.

### Conclusion

In both cases the restriction of $g$ to $A[b]$ is an embedding into $N$ that extends $f$.
By the extension test, $\mathrm{ACF}$ has quantifier elimination.

## Idea

To embed one more element, it is enough to find in $N$ an element with the same algebraic relations over the image of $A$.
If $b$ is algebraic, a root of the conjugate minimal polynomial works, and $N$ has one since it is algebraically closed.
If $b$ is transcendental, any transcendental element works, and $\omega$-saturation provides one.

## Variants

A syntactic proof eliminates $\exists y$ from a conjunction of equations and inequations by the Euclidean algorithm on polynomials in $y$, or by resultants, with a case distinction on which leading coefficients vanish.
A third proof first shows model completeness, by Robinson's test and the Nullstellensatz.
Then $\mathrm{ACF}$ is the model companion of the universal theory of integral domains, which has the amalgamation property, and so $\mathrm{ACF}$ has quantifier elimination by the [[properties of model companions|~7269c0e3]].
