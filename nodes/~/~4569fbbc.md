## Summary

An $n$-valued Łukasiewicz-Moisil algebra is a [[De Morgan algebra|5Q53XWV0QN]] with $n - 1$ unary operations $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{n - 1}$, the Moisil operators; see [[the definition|~a5b71690]].
Each $\varphi_i x$ is a complemented element, and $\varphi_i x$ answers the question "is the value of $x$ at least level $n - i$?".
The standard example is the chain $L_n = \set{0, \tfrac{1}{n - 1}, \dots, 1}$ with $\sim x = 1 - x$.
Moisil introduced these algebras for Łukasiewicz's $n$-valued logic, but for $n \ge 5$ they do not define the Łukasiewicz implication.

## Motivation

In Łukasiewicz's three-valued logic, one can say "$x$ is true" and "$x$ is not false" with two-valued connectives.
These connectives map each value to $0$ or $1$, and together they determine the value.
Moisil took such operations as the primitive structure, on top of a lattice with an involutive negation.
The result is an algebra whose elements are coded by increasing chains of Boolean elements, as in [[Post algebras|RD2A5Z7E2B]].

## Notation

We write $\mathbf{LM}_n$ for the class of $n$-valued Łukasiewicz-Moisil algebras, and $(A, \lor, \land, \sim, \varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{n - 1}, 0, 1)$ for one of them.
On $L_n$, $\varphi_i(\tfrac{j}{n - 1}) = 1$ if $i + j \ge n$, and $0$ otherwise.
Some texts index the operators in the opposite order, so that $\varphi_1$ is the largest; check the convention before using a formula.
The set $C(A)$ of complemented elements is a Boolean algebra, the center of $A$.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[The three-element chain $L_3$|~3359e128]], where $\varphi_1$ is "necessarily" and $\varphi_2$ is "possibly".
2. Every [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]], as an algebra in $\mathbf{LM}_2$ with $\varphi_1 x = x$.
3. The chain $L_n$, and every power $L_n^X$ with the pointwise operations.
4. For a Boolean algebra $B$, the increasing chains $b_1 \le \dots \le b_{n - 1}$ in $B$, with $(\sim b)_i = \neg b_{n - i}$ and $\varphi_i b = (b_i, \dots, b_i)$.
5. [[The subalgebra $\set{0, \tfrac14, \tfrac34, 1}$ of $L_5$|~7fd4c499]].

### Non-examples

1. [[The four-element De Morgan algebra with two fixed points|~3ec227c4]]: no Moisil operators satisfy the axioms on it, for any $n$.
2. The chain $L_3$ with the two operators exchanged, so that $\varphi_1$ is "possibly" and $\varphi_2$ is "necessarily", fails clause 5 at $x = \tfrac12$.
3. The chain $L_3$ with $\varphi_1 = \varphi_2 = $ "necessarily" fails clause 4 at $x = \tfrac12$: $\varphi_1 \sim \tfrac12 = 0$, but $\sim \varphi_2 \tfrac12 = 1$.

### Basic properties

1. Each $\varphi_i$ is a lattice homomorphism from $A$ onto $C(A)$, and it is the identity on $C(A)$.
2. The map $x \mapsto (\varphi_1 x, \dots, \varphi_{n - 1} x)$ embeds $A$ into the algebra of increasing chains over $C(A)$ of example 4.
3. Each ultrafilter $U$ of $C(A)$ gives a homomorphism $A \to L_n$ that sends $x$ to $\tfrac{k}{n - 1}$, where $k$ is the number of $i$ with $\varphi_i x \in U$. So $A$ embeds in a power of $L_n$.
4. For $n = 3$ and $n = 4$, the Łukasiewicz implication is a term, and $\mathbf{LM}_n$ is term-equivalent to the $n$-valued [[MV-algebras|8T4M7RN9AC]].
5. For $n \ge 5$, the Łukasiewicz implication is not a term, by [[the subalgebra example|~7fd4c499]].

### History

Moisil introduced these algebras around 1940 as an algebraic counterpart of [[Łukasiewicz's three-valued logic|1CE2TGM6MQ]], and later for $n$ values.
It was then found that for $n \ge 5$ they do not capture Łukasiewicz's implication.
Today the $n$-valued MV-algebras play the role of the algebras of the $n$-valued Łukasiewicz logic.

### Common errors

1. Calling every algebra in $\mathbf{LM}_n$ an algebra of Łukasiewicz's $n$-valued logic. This holds only for $n \le 4$.
2. Forgetting the determination principle. Without it, the axioms allow two different elements with the same $\varphi$ values.
3. Mixing the two indexing conventions for the $\varphi_i$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A three-valued truth value is false, half or true.
Two yes or no questions determine it: "is it true?" and "is it at least half?".
A Moisil algebra has these questions as operations.
With $n$ values, $n - 1$ such questions are needed.

### L2 human

1. In $L_5 = \set{0, \tfrac14, \tfrac12, \tfrac34, 1}$, take $x = \tfrac12$, so $j = 2$.
2. Then $\varphi_i x = 1$ iff $i + 2 \ge 5$ iff $i \ge 3$.
3. So $(\varphi_1 x, \varphi_2 x, \varphi_3 x, \varphi_4 x) = (0, 0, 1, 1)$.
4. For $\sim x = \tfrac12$, the axiom $\varphi_i \sim x = \sim \varphi_{5 - i} x$ gives $(\sim 1, \sim 1, \sim 0, \sim 0) = (0, 0, 1, 1)$ again.

The typical use: the Moisil operators reduce questions about $\mathbf{LM}_n$ to Boolean questions about the center.
The trap: the chain of values $\varphi_i x$ increases with $i$, so the first operator is the strongest test.

### L3 human

The class $\mathbf{LM}_n$ is the variety generated by $L_n$, and its subdirectly irreducible members are the subalgebras of $L_n$.
The algebra of increasing chains over a Boolean algebra $B$ has the same lattice as the [[Post algebra|RD2A5Z7E2B]] of order $n$ over $B$.
A term operation of $L_n$ takes at each point one of the values $0$, $1$, $x$, $y$, $\sim x$, $\sim y$ of its arguments, and every such operation is a term; this criterion decides which connectives are definable.
The gap between $\mathbf{LM}_n$ and the $n$-valued MV-algebras for $n \ge 5$ is a standard example of two natural algebraizations of one logic that do not agree.
