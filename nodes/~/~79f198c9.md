## Proof

Let $V$ be an [[inner product space|01EK923SKP]] over $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, and let $x, y \in V$.
Over $\mathbb{R}$ every conjugate in the proof does nothing, so the proof covers both cases.

1. Case $y = 0$. By conjugate-linearity in the second argument, $\ip{x}{0} = 0$, and $\norm{0} = 0$. So both sides are $0$, and equality holds.
2. In this case $x$ and $y$ are [[linearly dependent|0EVHJFTG9Z]], because $0 \cdot x + 1 \cdot y = 0$. So the theorem holds when $y = 0$.
3. From now on let $y \neq 0$. Then $\norm{y}^2 = \ip{y}{y} > 0$, by positive definiteness of the [[inner product|0VGSYBWHV2]].
4. Choose a scalar $\lambda$: let $\lambda = \ip{x}{y} / |\ip{x}{y}|$ if $\ip{x}{y} \neq 0$, and $\lambda = 1$ otherwise. Over $\mathbb{R}$, $\lambda$ is $1$ or $-1$.
5. In both cases $|\lambda| = 1$ and $\overline{\lambda}\ip{x}{y} = |\ip{x}{y}|$, because $\overline{\ip{x}{y}}\ip{x}{y} = |\ip{x}{y}|^2$.
6. Put $w = \lambda y$ and $m = |\ip{x}{y}|$. Conjugate-linearity in the second argument and step 5 give $\ip{x}{w} = \overline{\lambda}\ip{x}{y} = m$. By conjugate symmetry, $\ip{w}{x} = \overline{m} = m$, since $m$ is real.
7. Also $\norm{w}^2 = \lambda\overline{\lambda}\norm{y}^2 = \norm{y}^2$, because $\lambda \overline{\lambda} = |\lambda|^2 = 1$.
8. For a real number $t$ let $f(t) = \norm{x - tw}^2$. Expand with linearity in the first argument and conjugate-linearity in the second, using $\overline{t} = t$: $f(t) = \norm{x}^2 - t\ip{w}{x} - t\ip{x}{w} + t^2\norm{w}^2$.
9. By steps 6 and 7, $f(t) = a t^2 - 2 m t + c$ with $a = \norm{y}^2 > 0$ and $c = \norm{x}^2$. This is a real quadratic polynomial in $t$.
10. By positive definiteness, $f(t) \geq 0$ for every real $t$.
11. [[Completing the square|XNCV97HTZ8]] gives $f(t) = a\left(t - \frac{m}{a}\right)^2 + \frac{ac - m^2}{a}$, by expanding the right side.
12. Put $t_0 = m/a$. Then $f(t_0) = \frac{ac - m^2}{a}$, and step 10 gives $ac - m^2 \geq 0$, since $a > 0$. In the language of the [[discriminant|0VVK64Q4JK]], $(2m)^2 - 4ac \leq 0$.
13. So $|\ip{x}{y}|^2 = m^2 \leq ac = \norm{x}^2\norm{y}^2$. Both $|\ip{x}{y}|$ and $\norm{x}\norm{y}$ are $\geq 0$, so taking square roots gives $|\ip{x}{y}| \leq \norm{x}\norm{y}$.
14. Equality, first direction. Suppose $|\ip{x}{y}| = \norm{x}\norm{y}$. Then $m^2 = ac$, so $f(t_0) = 0$ by step 12.
15. By the definition of $f$, $\norm{x - t_0 w} = 0$, so $x = t_0 w = t_0\lambda y$, by positive definiteness. Hence $x$ and $y$ are linearly dependent.
16. Equality, second direction: suppose $\alpha x + \beta y = 0$ with $\alpha, \beta$ not both $0$. If $\alpha = 0$, then $\beta y = 0$ with $\beta \neq 0$, so $y = 0$, which is excluded. So $\alpha \neq 0$, and $x = d y$ with $d = -\beta/\alpha$.
17. Then $\ip{x}{y} = d\norm{y}^2$, so $|\ip{x}{y}| = |d|\,\norm{y}^2$, by the [[multiplicativity of the absolute value|0GMV7GP8W0]].
18. Also $\norm{x} = |d|\,\norm{y}$, by step 7 with $d$ in place of $\lambda$. So $\norm{x}\norm{y} = |d|\,\norm{y}^2 = |\ip{x}{y}|$, and equality holds.
19. Steps 2, 13, 15 and 18 prove the inequality and show that equality holds exactly for dependent $x$ and $y$.

## Idea

Turn $y$ by a scalar $\lambda$ of modulus $1$ so that the inner product with $x$ becomes a nonnegative real number $m$.
Then $\norm{x - tw}^2$ is a real quadratic in $t$ that is never negative, so its discriminant is not positive, and this is the inequality.
Equality means that the quadratic has a real root $t_0$, so $x = t_0 w$ is a multiple of $y$.

## Variants

- Over $\mathbb{R}$ we can take $w = y$ and use the quadratic $\norm{x - ty}^2 = \norm{x}^2 - 2t\ip{x}{y} + t^2\norm{y}^2$ directly. The discriminant gives $\ip{x}{y}^2 \leq \norm{x}^2\norm{y}^2$.
- Over $\mathbb{C}$ one can also use a complex parameter: $\norm{x - sy}^2 \geq 0$ with $s = \ip{x}{y}/\norm{y}^2$ gives the inequality in one step. This is the [[proof by orthogonal projection|6NB1CYDTNK]].
- Without the rotation by $\lambda$, the quadratic over $\mathbb{C}$ has the middle term $-2t\operatorname{Re}\ip{x}{y}$. Then the discriminant gives only $(\operatorname{Re}\ip{x}{y})^2 \leq \norm{x}^2\norm{y}^2$, which is weaker.
