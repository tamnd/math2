## Example

We compute the subformulas and the modal depth of the [[modal formula|G8VGTW9P4F]]
$$\varphi = \square(p \to \lozenge q) \to \lozenge\square p.$$
For this count we treat $\neg$ and $\lozenge$ as primitive symbols, so $\lozenge\psi$ has the single immediate subformula $\psi$.
The modal depth $\mathrm{md}$ is defined by recursion on formulas:
$$\mathrm{md}(p) = \mathrm{md}(\bot) = 0, \quad \mathrm{md}(\alpha \to \beta) = \max(\mathrm{md}(\alpha), \mathrm{md}(\beta)), \quad \mathrm{md}(\square\alpha) = \mathrm{md}(\lozenge\alpha) = \mathrm{md}(\alpha) + 1.$$

1. The main connective of $\varphi$ is $\to$, with left part $\square(p \to \lozenge q)$ and right part $\lozenge\square p$.
2. The subformulas of the left part are $p$, $q$, $\lozenge q$, $p \to \lozenge q$ and $\square(p \to \lozenge q)$.
3. The subformulas of the right part are $p$, $\square p$ and $\lozenge\square p$.
4. With $\varphi$ itself, the distinct subformulas are $p, q, \lozenge q, p \to \lozenge q, \square(p \to \lozenge q), \square p, \lozenge\square p, \varphi$. There are 8 of them.
5. The variables give $\mathrm{md}(p) = \mathrm{md}(q) = 0$.
6. One modal operator gives $\mathrm{md}(\lozenge q) = 1$ and $\mathrm{md}(\square p) = 1$.
7. The implication gives $\mathrm{md}(p \to \lozenge q) = \max(0, 1) = 1$.
8. The boxes give $\mathrm{md}(\square(p \to \lozenge q)) = 2$ and $\mathrm{md}(\lozenge\square p) = 2$.
9. So $\mathrm{md}(\varphi) = \max(2, 2) = 2$.

## Remarks

The example shows that the modal depth counts the largest nesting of modal operators, not the number of operators.
The formula $\varphi$ has 4 modal operators, but its depth is 2.
With the official primitives of [[modal logic|C3RZY20ETK]], $\lozenge q$ is the formula $\neg\square\neg q$, that is $\square(q \to \bot) \to \bot$.
Then $\lozenge q$ adds the subformulas $\bot$, $\neg q$ and $\square\neg q$, and $\lozenge\square p$ adds $\neg\square p$ and $\square\neg\square p$.
The official count is 13 subformulas, but the modal depth of each formula is the same, since $\neg$ adds no box.
The depth has a semantic meaning.
The truth of a formula of depth $d$ at a world $w$ depends only on the worlds reachable from $w$ in at most $d$ steps.
The reason is a [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]].
The truth of $\square\psi$ at $w$ depends on the truth of $\psi$ at the successors of $w$, and $\psi$ has depth $d - 1$.
If we replace $p$ by $\square p$ in $\varphi$, the depth becomes 3, and the formula looks one step further from $w$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A modal formula is built from letters with "and", "if then", "necessarily" and "possibly".
Its subformulas are the pieces that appear during the building.
The modal depth is the largest number of "necessarily" and "possibly" that are nested inside each other.
In "necessarily (if $p$ then possibly $q$)", the word "possibly" is inside "necessarily", so the depth is 2.
The formula "necessarily $p$ and necessarily $q$" has two boxes side by side, so its depth is only 1.

### L2 human

1. Take $\psi = \square p \to \square\square q$.
2. The subformulas are $p$, $q$, $\square p$, $\square q$, $\square\square q$ and $\psi$.
3. The depths are 0, 0, 1, 1, 2 and $\max(1, 2) = 2$.

The typical use is in induction proofs and in [[filtration|~9960fa2c]] arguments, which work with the finite set of subformulas of one formula.
A trap: a count of subformulas depends on the chosen primitive symbols, so a book that takes $\lozenge$ as primitive gives a smaller number.
