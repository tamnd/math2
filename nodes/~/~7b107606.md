## Statement

Let $L$ be a signature, let $\mathcal{M}$ be an $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]] with domain $M$, and let $s_1, s_2 \colon \mathrm{Var} \to M$ be assignments.

1. Let $t$ be an $L$-[[term|6D3WA7N2W4]]. If $s_1(x) = s_2(x)$ for every variable $x$ that occurs in $t$, then $t^{\mathcal{M}}[s_1] = t^{\mathcal{M}}[s_2]$.
2. Let $\varphi$ be an $L$-[[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]]. If $s_1(x) = s_2(x)$ for every free variable $x$ of $\varphi$, then $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s_1]$ if and only if $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s_2]$.
3. Let $L \subseteq L'$, let $\mathcal{M}'$ be an $L'$-structure, and let $\mathcal{M}$ be its reduct to $L$: the same domain, with the interpretations of the symbols of $L$ only. Then for every $L$-formula $\varphi$ and every assignment $s$, $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s]$ if and only if $\mathcal{M}' \models \varphi[s]$.

Here $\models$ is [[satisfaction|~cf18e0d7]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Agreement on the free variables is needed. In $(\N, <)$ with a constant $1$, the formula $x < 1$ is satisfied when $s_1(x) = 0$ and not when $s_2(x) = 5$.
- Agreement on bound variables is not needed. The sentence $\exists x \, (x < 1)$ is satisfied by $s_1$ and by $s_2$ above, although they differ at $x$.
- In part 3, $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{M}'$ must agree on the symbols of $\varphi$. If $\mathcal{N}$ has domain $\set{0, 1}$ and $P^{\mathcal{N}} = \set{1}$, while $\mathcal{M}$ has the same domain and $P^{\mathcal{M}} = \set{0}$, then $s(x) = 0$ satisfies $P(x)$ in $\mathcal{M}$ only.

### Consequences

1. For a [[sentence|DKFGQTRYZE]] $\sigma$, all assignments agree on $\sigma$. So $\sigma$ is [[true or false|K7XDVK5854]] in $\mathcal{M}$, with no reference to an assignment.
2. If the free variables of $\varphi$ are among $x_1, \ldots, x_n$, then $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s]$ depends only on $a_i = s(x_i)$. This justifies the notation $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[a_1, \ldots, a_n]$.
3. Truth of a sentence and [[validity|5SDS9T8E7J]] do not depend on the signature in which the sentence is read, by part 3. Adding new symbols to a language does not change the truth of old sentences.

### Converse

The converse of part 2 is false.
In $(\Z, <)$, the assignments $x \mapsto 0$ and $x \mapsto 1$ satisfy the same formulas whose only free variable is $x$, because $n \mapsto n + 1$ is an automorphism; see [[isomorphism invariance of truth|~5166a0b3]].
Yet the two assignments differ at the free variable $x$.

### Generalizations

The same lemma, with the same proof, holds for many-sorted logic, for second-order logic with assignments to second-order variables, and for infinitary logics.

## History

Tarski's 1933 definition of truth uses satisfaction by infinite sequences of objects.
He observed that satisfaction by a sequence depends only on the terms of the sequence that correspond to the free variables of the formula; truth is then defined as satisfaction by every sequence.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Whether a formula holds depends only on the letters that actually appear free in it.
If two ways of filling in letters agree on those letters, they give the same answer.
A complete sentence, with no free letters, has one answer in a given structure: true or false.

### L2 human

We check part 2 on $\varphi = \exists y \, (x < y)$ in $(\set{0, 1, 2}, <)$.
The only free variable of $\varphi$ is $x$.

1. Let $s_1 = (x \mapsto 1, y \mapsto 0)$ and $s_2 = (x \mapsto 1, y \mapsto 2)$. They agree at $x$.
2. Under $s_1$, the variant $y \mapsto 2$ gives $1 < 2$, so $\varphi$ is satisfied.
3. Under $s_2$, the same variant $y \mapsto 2$ works, so $\varphi$ is satisfied.

The value of $y$ in $s_1$ and $s_2$ plays no role, because the clause for $\exists y$ replaces it.
The typical use is to drop the assignment when we talk about sentences.
A trap is to forget that a variable can occur both free and bound, as in $P(x) \wedge \exists x \, Q(x)$; here $x$ is free, and the assignment matters.

### L3 human

The lemma says that the satisfaction relation factors through the restriction of the assignment to the free variables.
So the meaning of $\varphi(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ in $\mathcal{M}$ is a subset of $M^n$, the set that $\varphi$ defines.
Part 3 says that this meaning is also stable under reducts and expansions, which makes the semantics compositional in the signature.
In Mathlib, formulas use de Bruijn style bound variables, and the corresponding facts are lemmas about `BoundedFormula.Realize` under changes of the free-variable map.
