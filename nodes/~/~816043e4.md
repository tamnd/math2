## Statement

Let $(X, d)$ be a [[metric space|~200ea42b]] and let $K \subseteq X$.
Then $K$ is [[compact|~56dabfb9]] if and only if $K$, with the [[subspace metric|~e02a631b]], is [[complete|~aae963c7]] and $K$ is [[totally bounded|~4737b673]].

## Remarks

Proof idea of the converse: in a totally bounded set, a diagonal argument gives a Cauchy subsequence of each sequence. Completeness gives a limit. Thus $K$ is sequentially compact, and so [[compact|~ad4294b7]].
In a complete space $X$, a subset is compact if and only if it is closed and totally bounded.
The [[Heine–Borel theorem|~207cb5fd]] follows, because bounded subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ are totally bounded.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Compactness in a metric space splits into two parts: no holes, and finitely many small pieces for each size.
The interval $(0, 1)$ is totally bounded but has holes at its ends. The real line has no holes but is not totally bounded.
