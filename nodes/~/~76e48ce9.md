## Summary

*Light linear logics* are variants of [[linear logic|2P2184B6HE]] with weaker rules for the exponentials $!$ and $?$, chosen so that cut elimination has bounded complexity.
Elementary linear logic (ELL) captures the [[elementary recursive functions|RJ3MCAAZSV]].
Light linear logic (LLL), due to Girard, and soft linear logic (SLL), due to Lafont, capture the functions computable in [[polynomial time|GVT2NKN37K]].
They belong to *implicit computational complexity*, which describes complexity classes by logical systems without explicit bounds.

## Motivation

In full linear logic, the rules for $!$ allow a proof to copy a subproof without bound.
The SEP entry states that cut elimination can make proofs grow to enormous size, and it traces this to contraction.
If the rules for $!$ are weaker, each copy is controlled, and the cost of cut elimination is bounded.
A program written as a proof in such a logic then runs within the bound, by its type alone.

## Notation

We use the one-sided rules of [[linear logic|H408H4WHBA]].
*Functorial promotion* is the rule: from $\vdash \Gamma, A$ infer $\vdash ?\Gamma, !A$, where $?\Gamma$ puts $?$ before each formula of $\Gamma$.
*Dereliction* $?D$ and *digging*, the derivability of $!A \multimap !!A$, are the two principles that light logics drop.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. ELL is linear logic with functorial promotion in place of promotion and dereliction.
   It keeps weakening and contraction for $?$.
   The SEP entry describes it as one rule that introduces $!$ and $?$ at the same time.
   [[Functorial promotion|~eb708ff1]] derives $!(A \multimap B) \multimap (!A \multimap !B)$.
2. SLL replaces the rules for $?$ by functorial promotion and *multiplexing*: from $\vdash \Gamma, A, \ldots, A$, with $k \geq 0$ copies of $A$, infer $\vdash \Gamma, ?A$.
   [[Multiplexing|~5bfff439]] derives $!A \multimap A \otimes A$.
3. LLL restricts promotion to contexts with at most one formula and adds a second modality, written $\S$, with its own promotion rule.

### Non-examples

Full linear logic is not a light logic: it derives [[digging|~109a5c30]], $!A \multimap !!A$, which ELL does not derive.

### Basic properties

1. In ELL, functorial promotion raises the *depth* of every formula of the sequent by one, where the depth counts the $!$ and $?$ around it.
   An invariant of this kind proves [[the failure of digging|~109a5c30]].
2. The SEP entry states that ELL can encode all and only the Kalmár elementary functions.
3. The SEP entry states that LLL and SLL both characterize the functions computable in polynomial time.

### History

The SEP entry attributes ELL and LLL to Girard (1998) and SLL to Lafont (2004), the papers cited in this node.

### Common errors

ELL is not a fragment of linear logic obtained by dropping a connective; it has all the connectives, with different rules for $!$ and $?$.
Every ELL derivation is a derivation of linear logic, after each functorial promotion is replaced by derelictions and a promotion, but the converse fails.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In linear logic, "!A" means "as many copies of $A$ as you like".
If copying is free, a program can grow very fast.
Light linear logics allow copying in a more careful way, and so every program in them stays inside a fixed time bound.

### L2 human

We see why functorial promotion is weaker than promotion with dereliction.

1. In linear logic, $\vdash A^{\perp}, A$ gives $\vdash ?A^{\perp}, A$ by dereliction, and then $\vdash ?A^{\perp}, !A$ by promotion.
2. Promotion again gives $\vdash ?A^{\perp}, !!A$, since the context $?A^{\perp}$ has the form $?\Gamma$.
3. In ELL, functorial promotion on $\vdash A^{\perp}, A$ gives $\vdash ?A^{\perp}, !A$ directly.
4. Functorial promotion again gives $\vdash ??A^{\perp}, !!A$, which adds $?$ on both sides.
5. So the depth of $A$ goes up by one on each side, and ELL never reaches $\vdash ?A^{\perp}, !!A$.

The typical use of these logics is to type programs so that the type guarantees a complexity bound.
A trap: SLL has no contraction rule for $?$ and no separate weakening; multiplexing with $k = 0$ gives weakening, and with $k = 2$ it gives a fixed number of copies.

### L3 human

The common idea is stratification: each $!$ adds a level, and the rules never move a formula between levels.
Cut elimination then works level by level, and the number of levels in a proof bounds its cost.
ELL bounds the cost by a tower of exponentials whose height depends on the depth, and LLL and SLL refine this to a polynomial.
