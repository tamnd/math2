## Example

Let $G = (\mathbb{Q}^\times, \cdot)$ be the nonzero [[rational numbers|0NCJ5WXN6Z]] under multiplication, with identity $1$.
We compute the [[cyclic subgroup|08ABT85339]] $\langle 2 \rangle = \{2^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.

1. Some powers: $2^0 = 1$, $2^1 = 2$, $2^2 = 4$, $2^{-1} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and $2^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4}$.
2. The map $k \mapsto 2^k$ from $\mathbb{Z}$ to $\mathbb{Q}^\times$ is strictly increasing: $2^{k+1} = 2 \cdot 2^k > 2^k$, because $2^k > 0$.
3. So the map is injective: if $j < k$, then $2^j < 2^k$, and thus $2^j \neq 2^k$.
4. In particular, $2^k \neq 1$ for each $k \geq 1$. So $2$ has infinite [[order|0WE2NHZJ7B]].
5. Thus $\langle 2 \rangle = \{\ldots, \tfrac{1}{4}, \tfrac{1}{2}, 1, 2, 4, \ldots\}$ is infinite, and its elements $2^k$ are distinct for distinct $k$.

This is the infinite case of [[the theorem on the order of $\langle g \rangle$|031M8HX7DA]].

## Remarks

The [[laws of exponents|00DRK90Z6K]] give $2^a \cdot 2^b = 2^{a+b}$.
So the bijection $k \mapsto 2^k$ is an isomorphism from $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ onto $\langle 2 \rangle$, and $\langle 2 \rangle$ is an [[infinite cyclic group|02B1T50JD1]].
The subgroup $\langle 2 \rangle$ is not all of $\mathbb{Q}^\times$: for example $3 \notin \langle 2 \rangle$ and $-1 \notin \langle 2 \rangle$.
In fact $\mathbb{Q}^\times$ is not cyclic: it contains $-1$, of order $2$, and $2$, of infinite order.
A finite cyclic group has no element of infinite order, and $\mathbb{Z}$ has no element of order $2$.
The set of nonnegative powers $\{1, 2, 4, \ldots\}$ is [[not this subgroup|~e9eb00ea]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start at $1$ and double again and again: $2, 4, 8, 16, \ldots$.
Also halve again and again: $\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{4}, \tfrac{1}{8}, \ldots$.
The numbers never come back to $1$.
So the powers of $2$ form an infinite list without repeats, and the list is a group under multiplication.

### L2 human

We check that a given number is or is not in $\langle 2 \rangle$.

1. The number $\tfrac{1}{32}$ is $2^{-5}$, so it is in $\langle 2 \rangle$.
2. The number $6$ is not in $\langle 2 \rangle$. The powers $2^k$ with $k \leq 2$ are at most $4$, and those with $k \geq 3$ are at least $8$.

The typical use: an element of infinite order gives a copy of $\mathbb{Z}$ inside the group.
A trap: a group with an element of infinite order is not always cyclic, and $\mathbb{Q}^\times$ is an example.
