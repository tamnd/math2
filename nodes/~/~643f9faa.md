## Example

Let $T$ be $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, the theory [[Peano arithmetic|~b3b2d1f3]] with the consistency statement added as an axiom.
We show that $T$ is not a [[fragment of PA|~1d4194bd]], that is, some theorem of $T$ is not a theorem of $\mathsf{PA}$.

1. $\mathsf{PA}$ is consistent, because $\mathfrak{N}$ is a model of it and [[soundness|4A84VHDTHV]] holds.
2. $\mathsf{PA}$ is recursively axiomatized and contains $\mathsf{PA}$.
3. By the [[second incompleteness theorem|X9WYNYE29C]], $\mathsf{PA} \nvdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.
4. $T \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, since it is an axiom of $T$.
5. So $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is a theorem of $T$ that is not a theorem of $\mathsf{PA}$.

So $T$ is not a fragment of $\mathsf{PA}$, although it is consistent and true in $\mathfrak{N}$.

## Remarks

The contrast is with the fragments $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n$.
For $n \ge 1$, $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n)$ is a fragment, because $\mathsf{PA}$, and even $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_{n+1}$, proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n)$.
Adding the consistency of a theory lifts it strictly above itself; for a fragment this stays inside $\mathsf{PA}$, for $\mathsf{PA}$ it leaves $\mathsf{PA}$.
[[This example|EQH7XTZ29V]] shows more: $T$ is not even conservative over $\mathsf{PA}$ for $\Pi_1$ sentences.
In the same way, $\mathsf{PA} + \sigma$ is not a fragment for any sentence $\sigma$ that $\mathsf{PA}$ does not prove; the [[Goodstein sentence|XQMNJF241J]] is a true example.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A fragment of Peano arithmetic proves only things that Peano arithmetic proves.
The statement "Peano arithmetic is consistent" is true, but Gödel showed that Peano arithmetic cannot prove it.
So a theory that has this statement as an axiom proves something new.
It is therefore stronger than Peano arithmetic, not a part of it.

### L2 human

We compare the two theories $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1 + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1)$ and $\mathsf{PA} + \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.

1. $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1)$, so every theorem of the first theory is a theorem of $\mathsf{PA}$.
2. $\mathsf{PA} \nvdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, so the second theory has a theorem outside $\mathsf{PA}$.
3. So the first theory is a fragment and the second is not.

A trap is to think that the consistency of a true theory is "already true" and therefore adds nothing; truth in $\mathfrak{N}$ and provability in $\mathsf{PA}$ are different.
