## Statement

Let $r$ and $s$ be positive integers.
Then there is a positive integer $n$ with this property.
Colour each edge of the [[complete graph|~b29ee1b7]] $K_n$ blue or red, in any way.
Then there is a blue [[clique|~d6405616]] with $r$ vertices or a red clique with $s$ vertices.

## Remarks

The least such $n$ is the [[Ramsey number|~88785faa]] $R(r, s)$.
The proof gives the bound $R(r, s) \le R(r - 1, s) + R(r, s - 1)$, by induction on $r + s$.
The theorem also holds for any finite number of colours.

## History

Frank Ramsey proved the first version of the theorem in a 1930 paper on formal logic.
This result started the field now called Ramsey theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Complete disorder is impossible in a large enough structure.
If you colour the edges of a large complete graph with two colours, a large clique of one colour always appears.
