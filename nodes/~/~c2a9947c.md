## Summary

Mathematical economics uses mathematical models to study the choices of agents, markets, prices and the allocation of resources, and the mathematical social sciences use such models for collective decisions, measurement and social structure.
The central object of economic theory is an economy, which is a set of agents with preferences and endowments or technologies, together with the rules by which they trade or decide.
The models give exact forms to economic and social claims, such as the existence of a competitive equilibrium and the impossibility theorems of social choice.
Cournot and Walras started the mathematical theory of markets in the nineteenth century, and Arrow, Debreu and McKenzie proved the existence of general equilibrium in the 1950s.
This topic collects the mathematical economics and social sciences pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Game theory, mathematical finance and insurance, operations research and mathematical biology have their own topics.
