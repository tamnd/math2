## Proof

We use the calculus $\mathrm{LK}^2$ and the abstracts of the [[statement|~fb73bfd1]].
We take $\neg$, $\to$, $\forall x$ and $\forall X$ as the logical symbols; the other symbols are abbreviations.
Formulas may contain free variables, and "formula" means a formula with free variables allowed.
We prove the contrapositive: if $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ has no cut-free derivation, then it has no derivation.

Definitions.
A *semi-valuation* is a partial map $V$ from formulas to $\set{\mathrm{t}, \mathrm{f}}$ with these conditions.

- If $V(\neg A) = \mathrm{t}$, then $V(A) = \mathrm{f}$. If $V(\neg A) = \mathrm{f}$, then $V(A) = \mathrm{t}$.
- If $V(A \to B) = \mathrm{t}$, then $V(A) = \mathrm{f}$ or $V(B) = \mathrm{t}$. If $V(A \to B) = \mathrm{f}$, then $V(A) = \mathrm{t}$ and $V(B) = \mathrm{f}$.
- If $V(\forall x \, A) = \mathrm{t}$, then $V(A[s/x]) = \mathrm{t}$ for every term $s$. If $V(\forall x \, A) = \mathrm{f}$, then $V(A[y/x]) = \mathrm{f}$ for some variable $y$.
- If $V(\forall X \, A) = \mathrm{t}$, then $V(A[T/X]) = \mathrm{t}$ for every abstract $T$. If $V(\forall X \, A) = \mathrm{f}$, then $V(A[Y/X]) = \mathrm{f}$ for some variable $Y$.

A *valuation* is a semi-valuation that is defined on every formula, like a [[truth valuation|H31CS3HK5P]] of propositional logic.
A sequent $\Gamma' \vdash \Delta'$ is *true under* $W$ if $W(C) = \mathrm{f}$ for some $C$ in $\Gamma'$ or $W(D) = \mathrm{t}$ for some $D$ in $\Delta'$.
A *substitution* $\sigma$ replaces the free first-order variables by terms and the free second-order variables by abstracts.

1. Assume that $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ has no cut-free derivation.
2. Build the search tree of $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$, as for [[analytic tableaux|WGWBBVE8GX]]. Its root is $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$. Above each sequent, apply one rule backwards, in a fixed fair order. Keep the principal formula in the premise, so that each formula stays in all later sequents of the branch.
3. Fairness means: each formula on a branch is treated infinitely often. Each time a formula $\forall X \, A$ on the left is treated, the next abstract in a fixed list of all abstracts is used. Each first-order term is used in the same way. On the right, a new variable is used.
4. A branch stops when its last sequent has a formula on both sides. Such a sequent has a cut-free derivation, from an axiom $A \vdash A$ by weakening.
5. If every branch stops, the tree is finite, by König's lemma, because each sequent has at most two premises. Then the tree is a cut-free derivation of $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$, against step 1.
6. So some branch $B$ never stops. Define $V(A) = \mathrm{t}$ when $A$ is on the left of a sequent of $B$, and $V(A) = \mathrm{f}$ when $A$ is on the right of a sequent of $B$.
7. $V$ is well defined. If $A$ were on the left of one sequent and on the right of another, both occurrences would be in the later sequent, by step 2. Then $B$ would stop, by step 4.
8. $V$ is a semi-valuation, by fairness (step 3). For example, if $\forall X \, A$ is on the left of $B$, then for each abstract $T$ the formula $A[T/X]$ appears on the left of a later sequent of $B$.
9. Extension lemma (cited: Tait 1966 for second-order logic, after Schütte; Takahashi 1967 and Prawitz 1968 for higher-order logic). Every semi-valuation $V$ extends to a valuation $W$. We do not reprove this lemma; it is the non-constructive part of the proof.
10. Claim: for every derivable sequent $\Gamma' \vdash \Delta'$ of $\mathrm{LK}^2$, with or without cut, and every substitution $\sigma$, the sequent $\sigma\Gamma' \vdash \sigma\Delta'$ is true under $W$. We prove it by induction on the derivation, in steps 11 to 15.
11. Axioms $A \vdash A$: $W(\sigma A)$ is $\mathrm{t}$ or $\mathrm{f}$, and in both cases the sequent is true. The structural rules keep truth, because they only add, copy or move formulas.
12. Cut. The premises are $\Gamma_1 \vdash \Delta_1, A$ and $A, \Gamma_2 \vdash \Delta_2$. If $W(\sigma A) = \mathrm{t}$, then the second premise is true through a formula of $\sigma\Gamma_2$ or $\sigma\Delta_2$. If $W(\sigma A) = \mathrm{f}$, the first premise is true through a formula of $\sigma\Gamma_1$ or $\sigma\Delta_1$. In both cases the conclusion is true.
13. The propositional rules and the rules for $\forall x$ follow from the conditions on $W$, as in the [[soundness theorem|AVD7T7J063]]. Since $W$ is total, each condition is an equivalence: for example, if all $W(A[T/X])$ are $\mathrm{t}$, then $W(\forall X \, A) = \mathrm{f}$ is impossible, so $W(\forall X \, A) = \mathrm{t}$.
14. Rule $(\forall^2 \mathrm{L})$. If the premise is true through $\sigma(A[T/X]) = (\sigma A)[\sigma T/X]$, this formula is false. Then $W(\sigma \forall X \, A) = \mathrm{f}$ by the condition for $\forall X$, and the conclusion is true.
15. Rule $(\forall^2 \mathrm{R})$, with the variable $Y$. Assume that the conclusion is false under $\sigma$. Let $T$ be any abstract, and let $\sigma'$ be $\sigma$ with $Y \mapsto T$. Since $Y$ is not free in $\Gamma'$ or $\Delta'$, the premise under $\sigma'$ is true only through $(\sigma A)[T/X]$. So $W((\sigma A)[T/X]) = \mathrm{t}$ for every $T$, and $W(\sigma \forall X \, A) = \mathrm{t}$ by step 13. This contradicts the assumption.
16. Apply the claim of step 10 to $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ with the identity substitution. Every formula of $\Gamma$ has $V$-value $\mathrm{t}$ and every formula of $\Delta$ has $V$-value $\mathrm{f}$, by step 6, because $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ is the first sequent of $B$. Since $W$ extends $V$, the sequent $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ is not true under $W$.
17. So $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ has no derivation in $\mathrm{LK}^2$, by step 10. This proves the contrapositive of the theorem, so every derivable sequent has a cut-free derivation.

## Idea

A failed search for a cut-free proof leaves an infinite branch, and the branch describes a partial counter-model.
The cited extension lemma completes it to a total valuation that respects all instances of comprehension.
Every rule of $\mathrm{LK}^2$, including cut, is sound for such a valuation, so the sequent cannot have any derivation.
All the strength of the theorem lies in the extension lemma.

## Variants

- Girard's proof uses strong normalization of [[System F|SVNYKTGQ48]] with reducibility candidates. It works on natural deduction for second-order intuitionistic logic, and the classical case follows by a translation.
- Takeuti proved cut elimination for fragments of his calculus by syntactic reductions with ordinal diagrams; this is the method of his consistency proof for $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension. Such proofs work only for fragments, because a full proof with an ordinal bound would need an ordinal analysis of second-order arithmetic.
