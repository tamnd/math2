## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $(V_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of vector spaces over $F$, and let $\pi_j \colon \prod_{i \in I} V_i \to V_j$ be the projections of the [[direct product|0MM085RX00]].
Let $X$ be a [[vector space|05EGHEQZAW]] over $F$, and for each $i \in I$ let $g_i \colon X \to V_i$ be a [[linear map|0RJF668P1T]].
Then there is exactly one linear map $g \colon X \to \prod_{i \in I} V_i$ with $\pi_i \circ g = g_i$ for every $i \in I$, namely
$$g(x) = \big(g_i(x)\big)_{i \in I}.$$

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The maps $g_i$ must be linear. If $g_1(t) = t^2$, then $\pi_1 \circ g$ cannot equal $g_1$ for a linear $g$, because $\pi_1 \circ g$ is linear.
- All $g_i$ must start from one space $X$. The formula evaluates every $g_i$ at the same vector $x$.
- The target must be the full product. In the [[external direct sum|0S7W6EG32V]] $\R^{(\N)}$, the maps $g_i(t) = t$ from $\R$ would need $g(1) = (1, 1, 1, \dots)$, which is not in the direct sum.

### Consequences

- The map $g \mapsto (\pi_i \circ g)_i$ is an isomorphism $\mathcal{L}\big(X, \prod_i V_i\big) \cong \prod_i \mathcal{L}(X, V_i)$.
- A linear map into $V_1 \times V_2$ is a pair of maps. In matrix form, its matrix is the block column $\begin{pmatrix} B_1 \\ B_2 \end{pmatrix}$.
- The product is determined up to a unique isomorphism by this property. It is the [[categorical product|S5744QZV4N]] of vector spaces.

### Converse

If a space $P$ with linear maps $p_i \colon P \to V_i$ has the same property, then the induced map $P \to \prod_i V_i$ is an isomorphism.

## History

The characterization of products by maps into them goes back to the work of Mac Lane in the late 1940s and to Samuel's universal mappings of 1948.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To give a linear map into a product, give a linear map into each factor.
The combined map lists the results.
There is no other choice, because a vector of the product is known once all its entries are known.

### L2 human

Let $X = \R$, $V_1 = \R^2$ and $V_2 = \R$, with $g_1(t) = (t, -t)$ and $g_2(t) = 3t$.

1. The formula gives $g(t) = ((t, -t), 3t)$.
2. Then $\pi_1(g(t)) = (t, -t) = g_1(t)$ and $\pi_2(g(t)) = 3t = g_2(t)$.
3. In coordinates of $\R^3$, the matrix of $g$ is the column $(1, -1, 3)^T$, the block column of $g_1$ and $g_2$.

The trap is to use the direct sum as target for infinite families: the combined map can leave the direct sum.

### L3 human

The product represents the functor $X \mapsto \prod_i \mathcal{L}(X, V_i)$.
This is dual to the [[universal property of the direct sum|~2662e26c]], which describes maps out of $\bigoplus_i V_i$.
Mathlib builds the induced map as `LinearMap.pi`.
