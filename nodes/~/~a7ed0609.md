## Example

We work in the [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]] $H$ for propositional logic with the axiom schemas A1 to A3 and the single rule modus ponens (MP).

- A1: $\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)$.
- A2: $(\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)) \to ((\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi))$.
- A3: $(\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi) \to ((\neg \psi \to \varphi) \to \psi)$.

[[Hypothetical syllogism|7MT8CPCEM4]] is the rule "from $\varphi \to \psi$ and $\psi \to \chi$ infer $\varphi \to \chi$".
We show that it is a derived [[rule of inference|QPD3X471Q1]] of $H$: for all formulas $\varphi, \psi, \chi$, there is a derivation of $\varphi \to \chi$ from the two premises.

1. $\psi \to \chi$, premise.
2. $(\psi \to \chi) \to (\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi))$, instance of A1 with $\psi \to \chi$ in place of $\varphi$ and $\varphi$ in place of $\psi$.
3. $\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)$, by MP from lines 1 and 2.
4. $(\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)) \to ((\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi))$, instance of A2.
5. $(\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi)$, by MP from lines 3 and 4.
6. $\varphi \to \psi$, premise.
7. $\varphi \to \chi$, by MP from lines 6 and 5.

Each line is a premise, an instance of an axiom schema or follows by MP from two earlier lines.
So $\set{\varphi \to \psi, \psi \to \chi} \vdash_H \varphi \to \chi$ for all $\varphi, \psi, \chi$, and the rule is derivable.

The rule also preserves truth.
Let $v$ be a valuation that makes both premises true.
If $v(\varphi)$ is false, then $\varphi \to \chi$ is true.
If $v(\varphi)$ is true, then $v(\psi)$ is true by the first premise, $v(\chi)$ is true by the second, and $\varphi \to \chi$ is true.

## Remarks

Because the rule is derivable, it may be used inside any derivation from hypotheses, as an abbreviation of lines 1 to 7.
This is the difference from a rule that is only admissible, such as [[uniform substitution|~759059cd]].
Applying the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] twice to the derivation above gives the theorem $\vdash_H (\varphi \to \psi) \to ((\psi \to \chi) \to (\varphi \to \chi))$.
The derivation uses only A1, A2 and MP, so hypothetical syllogism is derivable in every Hilbert system that contains these two schemas, including the systems for intuitionistic logic.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hypothetical syllogism chains two implications: if rain makes the street wet, and a wet street makes it slippery, then rain makes the street slippery.
The Hilbert system does not have this rule among its basic rules.
The derivation shows that the system can reach the same conclusion in seven small steps, using only its axioms and modus ponens.
So we may add the chaining rule as a shortcut without changing what the system can prove.

### L2 human

We use the derived rule to derive $p \to r$ from the hypotheses $p \to q$ and $q \to r$, in a concrete instance.

1. Put $\varphi = p$, $\psi = q$ and $\chi = r$ in the seven lines above.
2. Line 2 becomes $(q \to r) \to (p \to (q \to r))$, which is an instance of A1.
3. Line 4 becomes $(p \to (q \to r)) \to ((p \to q) \to (p \to r))$, which is an instance of A2.
4. Line 7 becomes $p \to r$.

The typical use of a derived rule is to shorten later derivations, for example to chain $p \to q$, $q \to r$ and $r \to s$ into $p \to s$ with two uses.
A trap is the order of the premises in line 7: modus ponens needs $\varphi \to \psi$ and $(\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi)$, and line 5 is the second of these.
