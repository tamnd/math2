## Proof

*The "if" direction.* If $h : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{N}^I / \mathcal{U}$ is elementary, then $\mathcal{M} \equiv \mathcal{N}^I / \mathcal{U}$, and $\mathcal{N}^I / \mathcal{U} \equiv \mathcal{N}$ by [[Łoś's theorem|AGYBSTD7BG]].

*The "only if" direction.* Let $\mathcal{M} \equiv \mathcal{N}$, and fix $n_0 \in N$.

1. Add a new constant $c_a$ for each $a \in M$, and let $\Delta$ be the elementary diagram: the set of sentences $\varphi(c_{a_1}, \dots, c_{a_k})$ with $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi(a_1, \dots, a_k)$.
2. Let $I$ be the set of finite subsets of $\Delta$. Fix $\delta \in I$, and let $\psi(c_{a_1}, \dots, c_{a_k})$ be the conjunction of $\delta$, with distinct $a_j$.
3. $\mathcal{M} \models \exists x_1 \dots \exists x_k \, \psi(x_1, \dots, x_k)$, witnessed by $a_1, \dots, a_k$. This is an $L$-sentence, and $\mathcal{M} \equiv \mathcal{N}$, so it holds in $\mathcal{N}$.
4. Choose witnesses $b_1, \dots, b_k \in N$. Let $\mathcal{N}_\delta$ be $\mathcal{N}$ with $c_{a_j}$ interpreted as $b_j$, and every other $c_a$ as $n_0$. Then $\mathcal{N}_\delta \models \delta$.
5. For $\varphi \in \Delta$ let $A_\varphi = \set{\delta \in I : \varphi \in \delta}$. The finite intersection $A_{\varphi_1} \cap \dots \cap A_{\varphi_m}$ contains $\set{\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_m}$.
6. By the [[ultrafilter lemma|3MKZST79MK]], some ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$ on $I$ contains every $A_\varphi$.
7. Let $\mathcal{P} = \prod_{\delta \in I} \mathcal{N}_\delta / \mathcal{U}$. For $\varphi \in \Delta$, the truth set of $\varphi$ contains $A_\varphi$ by step 4, so $\mathcal{P} \models \varphi$ by Łoś's theorem.
8. Every $\mathcal{N}_\delta$ has the $L$-reduct $\mathcal{N}$. The $L$-symbols are interpreted coordinatewise, so the $L$-reduct of $\mathcal{P}$ is the [[ultrapower|~93a646cd]] $\mathcal{N}^I / \mathcal{U}$.
9. Define $h : M \to N^I / \mathcal{U}$ by $h(a) = c_a^{\mathcal{P}}$, the class of $\delta \mapsto c_a^{\mathcal{N}_\delta}$.
10. Let $\varphi(x_1, \dots, x_k)$ be an $L$-formula and $a_1, \dots, a_k \in M$. If $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi(a_1, \dots, a_k)$, then $\varphi(c_{a_1}, \dots, c_{a_k}) \in \Delta$, so $\mathcal{P}$ satisfies it by step 7.
11. If $\mathcal{M} \not\models \varphi(a_1, \dots, a_k)$, then $\neg\varphi(c_{a_1}, \dots, c_{a_k}) \in \Delta$, so $\mathcal{P}$ satisfies the negation.
12. By steps 8, 10 and 11, $\mathcal{N}^I / \mathcal{U} \models \varphi(h(a_1), \dots, h(a_k))$ if and only if $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi(a_1, \dots, a_k)$.

So $h$ is an [[elementary embedding|CGH8C8EA9N]]; injectivity is the case of the formula $x_1 = x_2$.
This proves the theorem.

## Idea

Each finite part of the elementary diagram of $\mathcal{M}$ can be copied into $\mathcal{N}$, since $\mathcal{N}$ satisfies the same sentences.
The [[ultraproduct method|~0aa1297f]] glues these partial copies into one copy of $\mathcal{M}$.
The glued structure is an expansion of an ultrapower of $\mathcal{N}$, because each partial copy only adds constants to $\mathcal{N}$.

## Variants

The compactness theorem gives an elementary extension of $\mathcal{N}$ into which $\mathcal{M}$ embeds elementarily, with the elementary diagrams of both; the ultrapower proof makes this extension an ultrapower.
The corollary on elementary classes follows: if $\mathcal{K}$ is closed under the three operations and $\mathcal{M} \models \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{K})$, then $\mathcal{M}$ is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct $\mathcal{N}$ of members of $\mathcal{K}$, as in the [[proof of the characterization of elementary classes|~de161ae8]].
Then $\mathcal{M}$ is isomorphic to an elementary substructure of an ultrapower of $\mathcal{N}$, which lies in $\mathcal{K}$, so $\mathcal{M} \in \mathcal{K}$.
