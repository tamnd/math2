## Example

In the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] $S_3$, compose permutations from right to left.
Let $S = \{(1\,2)\}$, and let $H = \langle S \rangle = \{e, (1\,2)\}$ be the [[subgroup generated|0RP7VWQ0YN]] by $S$.
Then $H$ contains $S$, but $H$ is not the [[normal closure|~99dbea13]] of $S$.

1. The normal closure of $S$ is a [[normal subgroup|0CB0DX5BVV]] of $S_3$.
2. $H$ is not normal in $S_3$. Conjugation by $(1\,3)$ gives $(1\,3)(1\,2)(1\,3)^{-1} = (2\,3)$, and $(2\,3) \notin H$.
3. By steps 1 and 2, $H$ is not the normal closure of $S$.
4. In fact the normal closure of $S$ is $S_3$, which has $6$ elements ([[the normal closure of a transposition in S3|~bb953b24]]).

## Remarks

$H$ is the smallest subgroup that contains $S$.
It is not the smallest normal subgroup that contains $S$.
The two notions agree exactly when $\langle S \rangle$ is normal.
For example, in an abelian group they always agree.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The subgroup $\{e, (1\,2)\}$ is the smallest group that contains the swap $(1\,2)$.
But it is not normal: a renaming of the numbers turns $(1\,2)$ into the swap $(2\,3)$, which is outside.
So it cannot be the normal closure.

### L2 human

The test for the normal closure has two parts: the subgroup must contain $S$, and it must be normal.
$H$ passes the first part and fails the second part.
One conjugate outside $H$ is enough to show the failure.
A common error is to read $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle$ as $\langle S \rangle$.
The double brackets mean that all conjugates of $S$ are included before we generate a subgroup.
