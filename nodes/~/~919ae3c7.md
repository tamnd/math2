## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ over a field $F$.
It sends $(x, y)$ to $(x + y, y)$.

1. $tI - A = \begin{pmatrix} t - 1 & -1 \\ 0 & t - 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is upper triangular. By the [[determinant of a triangular matrix|0K1KKMFHYH]], $p_A(t) = (t - 1)^2$.
2. Second method: $\tr A = 2$ and $\det A = 1$, so $p_A(t) = t^2 - 2t + 1 = (t - 1)^2$.
3. So $1$ is the only eigenvalue, with [[algebraic multiplicity|0S7A0EMPF4]] $2$.
4. $A - I = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $1$. Its kernel is $\{(x, 0)\}$.
5. Check: $A(1, 0) = (1, 0) = 1 \cdot (1, 0)$. And $A(0, 1) = (1, 1)$, which is not a multiple of $(0, 1)$.
6. So the [[eigenspace|0AYEPBQMAW]] $E_1$ is the line spanned by $(1, 0)$, and the [[geometric multiplicity|06YSJVR356]] of $1$ is $1$.
7. $(A - I)^2 = 0$, since $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^2 = 0$.
8. The only proper monic divisors of $(t - 1)^2$ are $1$ and $t - 1$. Neither annihilates $A$, because $I \ne 0$ and $A - I \ne 0$.
9. So the [[minimal polynomial|0V58FPM3EQ]] is $m_A(t) = (t - 1)^2 = p_A(t)$.

## Remarks

- The geometric multiplicity $1$ is strictly less than the algebraic multiplicity $2$.
- So $A$ is not [[diagonalisable|07SQCNV233]]: it has no basis of eigenvectors.
- The repeated root of $m_A$ gives the same conclusion.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A shear slides each horizontal line sideways, by an amount equal to its height.
Only the horizontal axis keeps its direction.
So the shear has just one line of eigenvectors, although its eigenvalue is a double root.

### L2 human

The shear is the standard example of a defective matrix.
The algebraic multiplicity counts the root twice, and the geometric multiplicity sees only one line.
A trap is to count eigenvectors by counting roots with multiplicity.
