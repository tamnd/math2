## Summary

Elementary number theory is the study of the integers with methods that use no complex analysis and little algebra beyond congruences.
Its central objects are the primes, and a prime is an integer greater than 1 whose only positive divisors are 1 and itself.
Congruences and primality tests are the base of public-key cryptography, of check digits and of many algorithms on integers.
Euclid proved that there are infinitely many primes, and Gauss gave the subject its modern form in the Disquisitiones Arithmeticae of 1801.
This topic collects the elementary number theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Analytic number theory, additive number theory, algebraic number theory and arithmetic geometry have their own topics.
