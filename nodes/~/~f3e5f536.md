## Example

Floyd Burton Jones, a student of Robert Lee Moore, described Moore's graduate course in topology at the University of Texas.
We check the description against the steps of the [[Moore method|~492bc465]].

1. Selection: Moore chose the members of the class, and he excluded students who had already studied topology. He told the class not to read about topology.
2. Axioms: he stated the undefined terms, such as "point" and "region", and the axioms that control their meaning, in the style of the [[axiomatic method|GD2JBXJV5Z]].
3. List: he read definitions and statements of theorems, and the students copied them. He gave no proofs.
4. Examples: he asked the students to construct examples showing that the hypotheses of the theorems could not be weakened or omitted.
5. Presentation: in the next meeting he asked a student to prove Theorem 1 at the board. Once he knew the class, he called on the weaker students first.
6. Checking: the other students, especially those without a proof, checked that the proof was correct and convincing.
7. Gaps: when a flaw appeared, the class waited for the presenter to repair it. If the presenter could not, the theorem stayed open, or the next student tried.

Each of the steps of the method is present, so the course is an instance of the method.

## Remarks

This is the original form of the method, with two features that later forms often drop: the selection of the class and the ban on reading.
Moore's subject was [[point-set topology|DQ77PZJBHV]], a term that he coined, in which a few axioms about points and regions generate many theorems.
The subject suits the method because its first theorems need few tools.
Modern forms keep steps 3 to 7 and allow group work and reading.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Moore taught topology without a textbook and without proofs on the board.
He gave the students the basic rules and a list of theorems.
The students had to find the proofs alone and show them to the class.
The class checked each proof, and a theorem stayed open until someone proved it.
Several of his students became well-known topologists.

### L2 human

1. List the steps of the method: list of theorems, proofs by students, presentation, checking by the class, guidance by the teacher.
2. Match each step with a part of Jones's description.
3. Note the extra features of the original: selection of students and the ban on reading.
4. Conclude that the course is an instance, and that later forms of the method relax step 3.

A trap is to treat the ban on reading as a part of the method itself.
Most modern courses in the Moore style allow reading after a theorem is proved, and they are still instances of the method.
