## Example

Let $L$ have one constant symbol $a$ and one unary function symbol $f$.
Let $\Gamma = \set{\forall x \, \forall y \, (x = y)}$, which says that the universe has exactly one element.
We follow the [[Henkin construction|~95e9f5bb]] and look at the term model.

1. Add the new constants $c_0, c_1, \dots$ and the witness axioms, and let $\Delta$ be a maximal consistent extension, as in the [[proof of model existence|~65534b28]].
2. The closed [[terms|6D3WA7N2W4]] of $L(C)$ are $f^n(a)$ and $f^n(c_i)$ for $n, i \ge 0$. They are infinitely many different strings.
3. For closed terms $t$ and $u$, instantiate $\forall x \, \forall y \, (x = y)$ twice with axiom group 2. This gives $\Gamma \vdash t = u$, so $(t = u) \in \Delta$ by property 1 of [[Lindenbaum's lemma|~8ecc8dc7]].
4. So $t \sim u$ for all closed terms, and the quotient $M$ of the set of closed terms has exactly one element $[a]$.
5. The function $f^{\mathcal{M}}$ sends $[a]$ to $[f(a)] = [a]$, and every constant denotes $[a]$.
6. The one-element structure satisfies $\forall x \, \forall y \, (x = y)$. So the term model is a model of $\Gamma$, as the truth lemma predicts.

## Remarks

The quotient is necessary.
Suppose the universe were the set of closed terms, with $=$ read as identity of strings.
Then $a$ and $f(a)$ would be two different elements, and $\forall x \, \forall y \, (x = y)$ would be false.
The relation $t \sim u \iff (t = u) \in \Delta$ is an [[equivalence relation|RGNF33GVHX]] and a congruence, by axiom groups 5 and 6, so the quotient structure is well defined.
In a language without $=$, no quotient is needed and the universe is the set of closed terms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The facts say "there is only one thing".
We have infinitely many names, such as $a$, $f(a)$ and $c_0$.
All of these names must be names of the same thing.
So the model has one element with many names.

### L2 human

1. In a term model, an element is a class of closed terms that the theory proves equal.
2. Here every pair of closed terms is provably equal, so there is one class.
3. The typical use is the step "take the quotient by provable equality" in the proof of the model existence theorem.
4. The trap is to count the closed terms and conclude that the term model is infinite.

### L3 human

The term model is the quotient of the term algebra by the congruence that $\Delta$ defines.
The term algebra is the free algebra on the constants, and the quotient is the initial model of the equations in $\Delta$.
This example shows that the size of a Henkin model is at most the number of closed terms, and can be much smaller.
