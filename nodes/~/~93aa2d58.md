## Statement

Let $M = (W, (R_a)_{a \in \mathcal{A}}, V)$ be any multi-agent Kripke model, and let $\varphi$ be a formula of [[public announcement logic|~0c910f5d]].
Let $M|\varphi = (W', (R'_a)_a, V')$ with $W' = \set{w \in W : M, w \models \varphi}$, $R'_a = R_a \cap (W' \times W')$ and $V'(p) = V(p) \cap W'$.
Define $M, w \models [!\varphi]\psi$ iff $M, w \models \varphi$ implies $M|\varphi, w \models \psi$.
Then the following formulas are true at every world of every model:

1. $[!\varphi]p \leftrightarrow (\varphi \to p)$, for atoms $p$;
2. $[!\varphi]\neg\psi \leftrightarrow (\varphi \to \neg[!\varphi]\psi)$;
3. $[!\varphi](\psi \wedge \chi) \leftrightarrow ([!\varphi]\psi \wedge [!\varphi]\chi)$;
4. $[!\varphi]K_a\psi \leftrightarrow (\varphi \to K_a[!\varphi]\psi)$;
5. $[!\varphi][!\psi]\chi \leftrightarrow [!(\varphi \wedge [!\varphi]\psi)]\chi$.

Every formula of public announcement logic without common knowledge is equivalent to a formula of epistemic logic without announcements.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. No condition on the relations is used, so the axioms are valid in every Kripke model.
   The updated model $M|\varphi$ may leave an intended class of models, as item 2 shows.
2. $\mathsf{S5}_n$ models stay $\mathsf{S5}_n$ models after restriction, since the restriction of an equivalence relation is an equivalence relation. Seriality is not preserved, so $\mathsf{KD45}_n$ models can turn into models with dead ends.
3. The atom clause needs the valuation to be restricted, not changed. Events that change facts need other axioms.

### Consequences

1. Completeness. Add parts 1 to 5 as axioms to $\mathsf{S5}_n$, with the rule "from $\psi$ infer $[!\varphi]\psi$". This is a complete axiomatization of $\mathsf{PAL}$ over $\mathsf{S5}_n$ models. A $\mathsf{PAL}$-validity translates into an $\mathsf{S5}_n$-validity, which is provable.
2. Decidability. $\mathsf{PAL}$ validity is decidable, by translation into $\mathsf{S5}_n$.
3. Two announcements in a row equal one announcement, by part 5.

### Converse

There is no reduction axiom of this form for common knowledge.
Some formulas $[!\varphi]C_G\psi$ have no equivalent in epistemic logic with $C_G$, and $\mathsf{PAL}$ with common knowledge is strictly more expressive than epistemic logic with common knowledge.

### Generalizations

1. For an action model $\mathsf{E}$ and event $e$, $[\mathsf{E}, e]K_a\psi \leftrightarrow (\mathrm{pre}(e) \to \bigwedge_{e \to_a f} K_a[\mathsf{E}, f]\psi)$.
2. With relativized common knowledge $C_G(\varphi, \psi)$, the reduction extends to announcements and common knowledge.

## History

Plaza gave the reduction axioms in "Logics of public communications" (1989).
The reduction axiom for action models is due to Baltag, Moss and Solecki (1998).

## Explanations

### L1 human

After a public announcement, every question about what agents know can be answered before the announcement.
"After $\varphi$ is announced, Ann knows $\psi$" means "if $\varphi$ is true, Ann knows that $\psi$ would hold after the announcement".
So announcements do not let us say anything new; they only let us say it more conveniently.

### L2 human

We read axiom 4 at a world $w$ where $\varphi$ is true.

1. After the announcement, Ann considers possible the worlds she saw before and where $\varphi$ holds.
2. So "Ann knows $\psi$ after" means: at every world she saw before, if $\varphi$ holds there then $\psi$ holds there after the announcement.
3. That is $K_a[!\varphi]\psi$, since $[!\varphi]\psi$ is automatically true where $\varphi$ is false.

The typical use is to eliminate announcements from a formula, innermost first.
A trap: in axiom 2 the antecedent $\varphi$ is needed.
Without it the axiom fails at worlds where $\varphi$ is false: there $[!\varphi]\neg\psi$ and $[!\varphi]\psi$ are both true.

### L3 human

The axioms describe how the model transformer $M \mapsto M|\varphi$ acts on truth sets, clause by clause.
They are recursion equations for the weakest precondition of $\psi$ under $!\varphi$, in the sense of program semantics.
