## Summary

A BL-algebra is a bounded commutative integral [[residuated lattice|E337A6WVH3]] that is prelinear and divisible; see [[the definition|~aa47af8f]].
BL-algebras are the algebras of [[Hájek's basic logic BL|3AQ5GHTXA4]], the logic of all continuous t-norms.
[[MV-algebras|8T4M7RN9AC]], Gödel algebras and product algebras are the three main subvarieties.

## Motivation

A continuous [[t-norm|BHGCVPAJW0]] on $[0, 1]$ has a residuum, and the pair satisfies prelinearity and divisibility.
BL is the logic of all continuous t-norms.
BL-algebras abstract the laws common to these standard algebras, as Boolean algebras abstract the truth tables.
BL is complete for three semantics: all BL-algebras, all BL-chains, and the standard algebras of continuous t-norms.

## Notation

We write $\otimes$ for strong conjunction, $\to$ for its residuum and $\neg x := x \to 0$.
Some texts write $\ast$ or $\&$ for $\otimes$.
An ordinal sum $A_1 \oplus A_2$ places a copy of $A_2$ above $A_1$, with a common top; the same sign $\oplus$ is also used for MV addition.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. The standard algebras of the Łukasiewicz, minimum and product t-norms; see [[divisibility for the three basic t-norms|E1M4RTRAVS]].
2. [[The Gödel three-element chain|~6b09f453]], with $\otimes = \min$.
3. [[An ordinal sum of a three-element MV-chain and a two-element chain|~90ad434d]], which is neither MV nor Gödel.
4. Every MV-algebra, with $x \otimes y = x \odot y$ and $x \to y = \neg x \oplus y$.
5. [[An ordinal sum of the Łukasiewicz and product t-norms|Z6H5WJYJ4H]], a standard BL-algebra.

### Non-examples

1. [[A five-element Heyting algebra|~32d5d900]]: it is divisible, but prelinearity fails.
2. [[The nilpotent minimum|ZJX7P6TVG9]]: it is a prelinear residuated lattice, but divisibility fails.

### Basic properties

1. BL-algebras form a variety, and every BL-algebra is a subdirect product of BL-chains.
   The same decomposition holds for all MTL-algebras.
2. Every continuous t-norm is an ordinal sum of copies of the Łukasiewicz, minimum and product t-norms.
3. Every BL-chain is an ordinal sum of totally ordered Wajsberg hoops; we state this without proof.
   A Wajsberg hoop is a hoop with $(x \to y) \to y = (y \to x) \to x$, and a bounded one is an MV-algebra.
4. A BL-algebra is an [[MV-algebra|8T4M7RN9AC]] iff $x \lor y = (x \to y) \to y$, which is equivalent to $\neg \neg x = x$.
5. A BL-algebra is a Gödel algebra iff $x \otimes x = x$; Gödel algebras are the prelinear [[Heyting algebras|NG943XNQWH]].

### History

Hájek introduced BL and BL-algebras in his monograph of 1998, which made fuzzy logic a part of mathematical logic.
The description of continuous t-norms as ordinal sums goes back to Mostert and Shields (1957) and Ling (1965).
Cignoli, Esteva, Godo and Torrens proved in 2000 that BL is complete for the standard algebras of continuous t-norms.

### Common errors

1. Assuming every residuated chain is BL. The nilpotent minimum is a prelinear chain without divisibility.
2. Assuming every BL-chain is MV or Gödel. [[The ordinal sum example|~90ad434d]] is neither.
3. Confusing the ordinal sum $\oplus$ with MV addition $\oplus$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Fuzzy logic gives truth values in $[0, 1]$, and there are many ways to define "and".
Each reasonable smooth choice of "and" comes with its own "if then".
BL-algebras collect the rules that all these choices share.

### L2 human

1. Take the product t-norm $x \otimes y = xy$ on $[0, 1]$.
2. Its residuum is $x \to y = 1$ if $x \le y$, and $y/x$ otherwise.
3. Check divisibility at $x = 0.5$, $y = 0.2$: $0.5 \cdot 0.4 = 0.2 = \min(0.5, 0.2)$.
4. Check prelinearity: $0.2 \to 0.5 = 1$, so the join of the two implications is $1$.

The typical use: one proves a law of BL by checking it on all BL-chains, by the subdirect representation.
The trap: $\neg \neg x = x$ can fail; in [[the ordinal sum example|~90ad434d]], $\neg \neg c = 1 \ne c$.

### L3 human

BL-algebras sit between MTL-algebras and the three classical fuzzy varieties.
The ordinal sum decomposition reduces the study of BL-chains to totally ordered Wajsberg hoops.
The bounded ones are MV-chains, which [[Mundici's equivalence|~41795275]] relates to ordered groups.
In the hierarchy of substructural logics, BL is the extension of MTL by the divisibility axiom.
