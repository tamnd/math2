## Statement

Let $L$ be the language of [[propositional logic|09K9MWD2QQ]] over finitely many atoms, and let $\Omega$ be the finite set of its valuations.
For a set $\Gamma$ of formulas, $\mathrm{Mod}(\Gamma) \subseteq \Omega$ is the set of valuations that satisfy every formula of $\Gamma$.
Fix a belief set $K$, and let $*$ map each formula $\varphi$ to a belief set $K * \varphi$.

A total [[preorder|0VABYWJWH9]] $\le$ on $\Omega$ is faithful to $K$ when, for all $x \in \mathrm{Mod}(K)$ and all $y \in \Omega$:

- if $y \in \mathrm{Mod}(K)$, then $x \le y$ and $y \le x$;
- if $y \notin \mathrm{Mod}(K)$, then $x < y$, that is, $x \le y$ and not $y \le x$.

For $A \subseteq \Omega$, put $\min(A, \le) := \set{x \in A : x \le y \text{ for all } y \in A}$.

The operator $*$ satisfies the eight [[AGM postulates|~30b6c328]] for $K$ if and only if there is a total preorder $\le$ on $\Omega$, faithful to $K$, such that
$$\mathrm{Mod}(K * \varphi) = \min(\mathrm{Mod}(\varphi), \le) \quad \text{for every formula } \varphi.$$

The proof is in [[the proof of the representation theorem|~96984d3b]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. Finiteness of the language makes $\Omega$ finite, so every non-empty set has minimal elements, and every set of valuations is $\mathrm{Mod}$ of one formula. For infinite languages the statement needs Grove's spheres of maximal consistent sets with a limit assumption, see the generalizations.
2. Totality of $\le$ is needed for postulate 8. Take a partial order with $a < b$ and $c$ incomparable to both, and select the minimal elements. Then revision by $\mathrm{form}(\set{a, b, c})$ keeps $\set{a, c}$, and adding $\mathrm{form}(\set{b, c})$ leaves $\set{c}$, while revision by $\mathrm{form}(\set{b, c})$ keeps $\set{b, c} \not\subseteq \set{c}$, against postulate 8.
3. Faithfulness is needed for postulates 3 and 4. Without it, revising by a formula consistent with $K$ could select models outside $\mathrm{Mod}(K)$.

### Consequences

1. Every AGM revision of $K$ is given by a ranking of the valuations in levels, with the models of $K$ at the bottom.
2. Revision by $\varphi$ consistent with $K$ is expansion, since the minimal models of $\varphi$ are then $\mathrm{Mod}(K) \cap \mathrm{Mod}(\varphi)$.
3. The postulates 1 to 6 alone correspond to selection functions that need not come from a single preorder.

### Converse

The theorem is an equivalence.
The preorder is unique: $x \le y$ holds exactly when $x \in \mathrm{Mod}(K * \varphi)$ for a formula $\varphi$ with $\mathrm{Mod}(\varphi) = \set{x, y}$.

### Generalizations

1. Grove's theorem: for an arbitrary propositional language, AGM revisions correspond to systems of spheres on the maximal consistent sets, centered on those that contain $K$.
2. The same preorders represent AGM contraction through the Harper identity: $\mathrm{Mod}(K \div \varphi) = \mathrm{Mod}(K) \cup \min(\mathrm{Mod}(\neg\varphi), \le)$.
3. Katsuno and Mendelzon gave the analogous representation of update by a family of preorders, one for each valuation.

## History

Grove gave the representation by systems of spheres in 1988.
Katsuno and Mendelzon stated the finite version with faithful total preorders in 1991.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine that you rank all the ways the world could be, from "most believable" to "least believable".
Your current beliefs are what is true in the top-ranked ways.
When you learn something new, you look at the top-ranked ways in which the news is true, and believe what holds there.
The theorem says that every rational way of revising beliefs works like this, for some ranking.

### L2 human

We read off the preorder from a revision operator on two atoms.

1. Valuations of $(p, q)$ are $11, 10, 01, 00$; let $K = \mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge q)$.
2. Suppose $K * \neg p = \mathrm{Cn}(\neg p \wedge q)$. The formula $\neg p$ has the models $01, 00$, and the result keeps $01$, so $01 < 00$.
3. Suppose $K * \neg q = \mathrm{Cn}(p \wedge \neg q)$. In the same way $10 < 00$.
4. Suppose $K * (p \leftrightarrow \neg q) = \mathrm{Cn}(p \leftrightarrow \neg q)$. Both models $10, 01$ are kept, so $10 \approx 01$.
5. Faithfulness puts $11$ at the bottom: $11 < 10 \approx 01 < 00$.

The typical use is to build revision operators for knowledge bases by assigning ranks to valuations.
A trap: the preorder belongs to one belief set $K$; a new belief set after revision needs a new preorder, which the postulates do not supply.

### L3 human

The theorem is a completeness theorem: the postulates are the logic of minimal-model selection with respect to total preorders.
The same structure appears in Lewis's sphere semantics for counterfactuals and in the ranked models of rational nonmonotonic consequence, and the three representation theorems are close relatives.
In dynamic epistemic logic, plausibility models put such a preorder at every world, and revision becomes an operation on models.
