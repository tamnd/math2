## Example

Let $A$ and $B$ be formulas.
The sequent $A \Rightarrow B \backslash A$ is derivable in $\mathsf{FL}_{ew}$, an extension of the [[full Lambek calculus|~783dffb5]].

1. $A \Rightarrow A$ is an axiom.
2. Left [[weakening|~20ee74f2]] adds $B$ at the left end: $B, A \Rightarrow A$.
3. The rule ${\backslash}\mathrm{R}$, from $C, \Gamma \Rightarrow D$ infer $\Gamma \Rightarrow C \backslash D$, gives $A \Rightarrow B \backslash A$.

## Remarks

The sequent is the paradox of weakening $A \to (B \to A)$ in the notation of $\mathsf{FL}$.
For atoms $p$ and $q$, the sequent $p \Rightarrow q \backslash p$ is not derivable in $\mathsf{FL}$ or $\mathsf{FL}_e$.

1. By [[cut elimination|~1050a703]], we search for a cut-free derivation.
2. The antecedent is an atom, so no left logical rule applies, and only ${\backslash}\mathrm{R}$ can end the derivation, with premise $q, p \Rightarrow p$.
3. In $\mathsf{FL}_e$, exchange gives also $p, q \Rightarrow p$, which has the same formulas.
4. No axiom has two formulas on the left, and no logical rule ends in a sequent of atoms.
   So neither premise is derivable.

The example also works in $\mathsf{FL}_w$ without exchange, since step 2 puts $B$ at the left end, where ${\backslash}\mathrm{R}$ needs it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

With weakening we may add an unused hypothesis.
So if we have $A$, then "$B$ implies $A$" holds for any $B$.
For example, if it is Monday, then "if it rains, it is Monday" is true.
Without weakening this step is not allowed, since the hypothesis "it rains" is not used.

### L2 human

We check the sequent in the $\mathsf{FL}_{ew}$-algebra on $[0, 1]$ with fusion the real product $x \cdot y$.

1. The residual is $b \backslash a = \min(1, a / b)$ for $b > 0$, and $1$ for $b = 0$.
2. Take $a = 0.3$ and $b = 0.6$.
   Then $b \backslash a = 0.5$.
3. The sequent says $a \leq b \backslash a$, that is, $0.3 \leq 0.5$, which is true.
4. In general $a \leq a / b$ for $0 < b \leq 1$, and $a \leq 1$, so the sequent is valid.

The typical use is to recognize the weakening axiom in algebraic form: $a \leq b \backslash a$ is equivalent to $b \cdot a \leq a$.
A trap: the sequent fails in the residuated lattice $(\mathbb{Z}, \leq, +, 0)$, since $b \backslash a = a - b$ and $a \leq a - b$ fails for $b = 1$.
