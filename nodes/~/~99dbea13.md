## Summary

Let $G$ be a [[group|G5S1M9]] and let $S$ be a subset of $G$.
The normal closure of $S$ in $G$ is the smallest [[normal subgroup|0CB0DX5BVV]] of $G$ that contains $S$.
It is the [[intersection|0ZTGMDNVTB]] of all normal subgroups of $G$ that contain $S$.
It is also the [[subgroup generated|0RP7VWQ0YN]] by all the [[conjugates|0DXD4QSZ8A]] $gsg^{-1}$ with $g \in G$ and $s \in S$:
$$\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle = \langle gsg^{-1} : g \in G, s \in S \rangle.$$

## Motivation

Suppose that we want to make the elements of $S$ trivial and change $G$ as little as possible.
We look for a quotient $G/N$ in which each $s \in S$ becomes the identity, that is, $s \in N$.
The subgroup $N$ must be normal, and it must contain $S$.
The smallest such $N$ gives the largest such quotient.
This smallest $N$ is the normal closure of $S$.
The subgroup $\langle S \rangle$ generated by $S$ is not enough, because it need not be normal.
The normal closure is the tool that turns a list of relations into a group presentation.

## Notation

We write $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle$, or $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle_G$ when the group $G$ must be named.
Some books write $S^G$ or $\operatorname{ncl}_G(S)$.
For one element $s$ we write $\langle\langle s \rangle\rangle$.
Conjugation is $gsg^{-1}$; some books use $g^{-1}sg$, and this gives the same subgroup.
The name normal closure also has a different meaning in field theory, for [[field extensions|6XM4CCVTKE]].

## Remarks

### Examples

- In $S_3$, the normal closure of $\{(1\,2)\}$ is all of $S_3$ ([[the normal closure of a transposition in S3|~bb953b24]]).
- In the alternating group $A_4$, the normal closure of $\{(1\,2)(3\,4)\}$ is the [[Klein four-group|0PMRQJVTME]] $V_4$ of double transpositions ([[the normal closure of a double transposition in A4|~1ed3833b]]).
- In an [[abelian group|082GHNPJ53]], $gsg^{-1} = s$ for all $g$ and $s$. So $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle = \langle S \rangle$. For example, in $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ the normal closure of $\{n\}$ is $n\mathbb{Z}$.
- In the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] $S_n$ with $n \ge 3$, the normal closure of a $3$-cycle is $A_n$. Its conjugates are all the $3$-cycles, and the $3$-cycles [[generate $A_n$|0KFZ0QX83G]].
- In $S_n$ with $n \ge 2$, the normal closure of a [[transposition|0FWVRHBRWM]] is $S_n$. Its conjugates are all the transpositions, and the transpositions [[generate $S_n$|0AQNX7G443]].
- If $S$ is contained in a normal subgroup $N$, then $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle \subseteq N$. If $\langle S \rangle$ is already normal, then $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle = \langle S \rangle$.

### Non-examples

- In $S_3$, the subgroup $\langle (1\,2) \rangle = \{e, (1\,2)\}$ contains $(1\,2)$, but it is not the normal closure, because it is not normal ([[a subgroup that is not the normal closure|~2a2389dc]]).
- The set of conjugates $\{gsg^{-1} : g \in G, s \in S\}$ is often not a subgroup. In $S_3$, the three transpositions do not form a subgroup, because $(1\,2)(1\,3) = (1\,3\,2)$.
- The normal closure is not the [[normal core|0GETX79CJN]]. For $H = \{e, (1\,2)\}$ in $S_3$, the normal closure is $S_3$, and the normal core is $\{e\}$.

### Basic properties

- An intersection of normal subgroups is normal. If $gNg^{-1} \subseteq N$ for each $N$ in a family, then $g\left(\bigcap N\right)g^{-1} \subseteq \bigcap N$. So the intersection in the definition is a normal subgroup.
- The two descriptions agree. Let $M$ be the subgroup generated by the conjugates $gsg^{-1}$. Each normal subgroup that contains $S$ contains every $gsg^{-1}$, and so it contains $M$.
- $M$ is normal. An element of $M$ is a product $x_1 x_2 \cdots x_k$, where each $x_i$ is $g_i s_i g_i^{-1}$ or its inverse $g_i s_i^{-1} g_i^{-1}$.
- For $a \in G$, we get $a x_1 \cdots x_k a^{-1} = (a x_1 a^{-1}) \cdots (a x_k a^{-1})$, and $a g_i s_i^{\pm 1} g_i^{-1} a^{-1} = (a g_i) s_i^{\pm 1} (a g_i)^{-1}$. So $aMa^{-1} \subseteq M$. With the previous item, $M = \langle\langle S \rangle\rangle$.
- $S \subseteq \langle S \rangle \subseteq \langle\langle S \rangle\rangle$.
- If $\varphi \colon G \to K$ is a [[homomorphism|0JYTG5K9Y4]] with $\varphi(s) = e$ for each $s \in S$, then $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle \subseteq \ker \varphi$. So $\varphi$ factors through $G/\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle$ by the [[universal property of the quotient group|0GJ83E78A5]].
- If $S \subseteq T$, then $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle \subseteq \langle\langle T \rangle\rangle$.
- A nontrivial group $G$ is [[simple|0YMYMNQ0QZ]] if and only if $\langle\langle g \rangle\rangle = G$ for each $g \ne e$.

### History

Groups given by generators and relations go back to Walther von Dyck in 1882.
In modern terms, such a group is the quotient of a free group by the normal closure of the relations.

### Common errors

- The normal closure is not the subgroup $\langle S \rangle$. They are equal only when $\langle S \rangle$ is normal.
- The normal closure depends on the group $G$. In $S_4$ and in $A_4$, the normal closure of $(1\,2)(3\,4)$ is $V_4$. In $V_4$ itself, which is abelian, it is $\{e, (1\,2)(3\,4)\}$.
- The field theory normal closure does not match the group normal closure under the Galois correspondence. It matches the [[normal core|0GETX79CJN]] of the subgroup.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start with some elements of a group.
We want the smallest normal subgroup that contains them.
First, collect each element together with all its conjugates, that is, all the elements $gsg^{-1}$.
Then multiply these elements together in all possible ways, with inverses.
The result is the normal closure.
For example, in the group of permutations of $\{1, 2, 3\}$, the swap of $1$ and $2$ has the three swaps as conjugates.
Products of swaps give every permutation, so the normal closure is the whole group.

### L2 human

We compute a normal closure in the [[dihedral group|0JZ27DMJ5D]] $D_4$ of order $8$.
Let $r$ be a rotation of order $4$ and $s$ a reflection, with $srs^{-1} = r^{-1}$.
The elements are $r^k$ and $r^k s$ for $k = 0, 1, 2, 3$.

1. From $srs^{-1} = r^{-1}$ we get $sr^{-1} = rs$.
2. Then $rsr^{-1} = r(sr^{-1}) = r(rs) = r^2 s$.
3. More generally, $r^k s r^{-k} = r^{2k} s$, and $(r^k s) s (r^k s)^{-1} = r^k s r^{-k} = r^{2k} s$.
4. So the conjugates of $s$ are $s$ and $r^2 s$ only.
5. The subgroup generated by $s$ and $r^2 s$ contains $(r^2 s)s = r^2$. So it is $\{e, r^2, s, r^2 s\}$.
6. This subgroup has index $2$, so it is normal by [[the index two theorem|0V547YQRMV]].
7. Thus $\langle\langle s \rangle\rangle = \{e, r^2, s, r^2 s\}$. It is larger than $\langle s \rangle = \{e, s\}$ and smaller than $D_4$.

The typical use is to describe a group by generators and relations.
The quotient $G/\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle$ is the largest quotient of $G$ in which every element of $S$ becomes $e$.
A trap is to take only the conjugates and stop.
In step 5 the element $r^2$ is not a conjugate of $s$, but it is in the normal closure.

### L3 human

A [[presentation|0JGEQXQ4AC]] $\langle X \mid R \rangle$ is the quotient $F(X)/\langle\langle R \rangle\rangle$ of the [[free group|0KTW26C80J]] on $X$.
For example, $F(\{a\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and $\langle a \mid a^n \rangle = \mathbb{Z}/\langle\langle n \rangle\rangle = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.
The normal closure of a finite set can fail to be finitely generated.
In the free group on $a$ and $b$, the normal closure of $\{a\}$ is the kernel of the map onto $\langle b \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$ that sends $a$ to $e$, and it is free on the infinitely many elements $b^k a b^{-k}$.
A subset $S$ normally generates $G$ when $\langle\langle S \rangle\rangle = G$.
The least size of such a set is the weight of $G$.
The normal closure is dual to the [[normal core|0GETX79CJN]]: one is the smallest normal subgroup above a subgroup $H$, and the other is the largest normal subgroup below $H$.
In the [[lattice of subgroups|0B8BW4YWTF]], the map $H \mapsto \langle\langle H \rangle\rangle$ is a closure operator, and its closed elements are the normal subgroups.
