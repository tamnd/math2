## Summary

The theory of special functions studies the named functions of classical analysis, such as the gamma function, the hypergeometric functions, the Bessel functions and the orthogonal polynomials.
A special function is known through its series and integral representations, its differential equation, its identities, its zeros and its asymptotic behaviour.
Special functions appear when the equations of physics are solved by separation of variables, and they are also used in probability, combinatorics and number theory.
Euler defined the gamma function, and Gauss gave the first systematic study of the hypergeometric series.
This topic collects the special functions pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Complex analysis, integral transforms and asymptotic methods, and automorphic forms and the Langlands program have their own topics.
