## Example

On $[0, 1]$, put $x \oplus y = \max(x, y)$ and $\neg x = 1 - x$.
These are the min-max operations of Zadeh's fuzzy sets.

1. $([0, 1], \max, 0)$ is a commutative monoid, and $\neg \neg x = x$.
2. $x \oplus \neg 0 = \max(x, 1) = 1 = \neg 0$.
3. The last axiom fails at $x = 0.6$, $y = 0.5$.
   The left side is $\max(1 - \max(0.4, 0.5), 0.5) = 0.5$.
   The right side is $\max(1 - \max(0.5, 0.6), 0.6) = 0.6$.

So $([0, 1], \max, 1 - x, 0)$ is not an [[MV-algebra|QVWHWDJ5X1]].

## Remarks

1. Only the last axiom fails; the two values $0.5$ and $0.6$ show it.
2. With $\min$ and $\max$, this structure is a [[De Morgan algebra|5Q53XWV0QN]] with $x \land \neg x \le y \lor \neg y$.
   Such an algebra is called a Kleene algebra.
3. The name "Kleene algebra" has a second meaning: the [[algebra of regular expressions|BK4DDS24XC]], an idempotent semiring with a star.
   The two notions are not the same.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Here "or" takes the larger of two truth values, as in many fuzzy systems.
This looks like the MV rules, but the last rule breaks.
In an MV-algebra, the strong "or" adds values; here nothing adds.

### L2 human

1. In an MV-algebra, $x \oplus x = x$ for all $x$ forces a Boolean algebra.
2. Here $x \oplus x = \max(x, x) = x$ for all $x$.
3. But $[0, 1]$ with $1 - x$ is not Boolean, since $0.5 \land \neg 0.5 = 0.5 \ne 0$.
4. So the structure cannot be an MV-algebra, which agrees with the direct check.

The typical use: it separates Łukasiewicz logic from the min-max fuzzy logic.
The trap: the word "Kleene algebra" here is not the Kleene algebra of automata theory.
