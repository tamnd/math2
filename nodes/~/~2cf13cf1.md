## Proof

This is an outline of Willard's construction, with the main step cited.
Steps 1 to 4 describe the construction, and steps 5 and 6 are proved here in full.
Step 7, the consistency of $W$, is the hard part of Willard's work; we cite it and do not outline it.
Steps 1 to 4 follow Willard (2001), "Self-verifying axiom systems, the incompleteness theorem and related reflection principles", Journal of Symbolic Logic 66(2), as summarized on Wikipedia; we omit the details of the axioms.

1. Language (outlined). Take a language of arithmetic with constants for small numbers and with subtraction and division as function symbols, and define addition and multiplication as predicates $\mathrm{Add}(x, y, z)$ and $\mathrm{Mult}(x, y, z)$ from them; we omit Willard's exact definitions. There is no function symbol for addition or multiplication, so the totality of these operations is not a law of logic.
2. Base axioms (outlined). Take a set $A$ of axioms, all true in $\N$, that describe the basic properties of the operations. According to the Wikipedia summary of Willard's work, any true $\Pi_1$ sentence can be added while keeping consistency; this is property 5 of the statement.
3. Proofs as tableaux (outlined). Code formulas and analytic tableaux by numbers, as in the [[arithmetization of syntax|~f1d9d517]], and write a formula $\mathrm{Tab}(y, x)$ that says "$y$ codes a closed tableau that refutes the sentence with code $x$ from the axioms". The coding is chosen so that the formula makes sense in the weak language of step 1, without the totality of multiplication.
4. The self-referential axiom (outlined). Let $\theta$ be a sentence that says "there is no tableau proof of $0 = 1$ from $A$ together with $\theta$". Such a sentence is obtained by a diagonal construction in the metatheory, as in the [[diagonal lemma|3PRW76471J]]: the construction is a computation on codes and is not carried out inside the theory. Let $W = A \cup \set{\theta}$.
5. $W$ proves its own tableau consistency (proved). The tableau consistency statement of $W$ is the sentence "there is no tableau proof of $0 = 1$ from $A \cup \set{\theta}$", which is $\theta$. Since $\theta$ is an axiom of $W$, $W \vdash \theta$ by a one-line proof.
6. $W$ is consistent if and only if $\theta$ is true in $\N$ (proved).
   Tableau proofs are sound and complete for first-order logic, by the [[soundness|4A84VHDTHV]] and [[completeness|WQ1ZE255SF]] theorems for tableaux.
   If $W$ is consistent, then no tableau proof of $0 = 1$ from $W$ exists, which is what $\theta$ says, so $\theta$ is true.
   Conversely, if $\theta$ is true, there is no tableau proof of $0 = 1$ from $W$, so by completeness $W$ has a model and is consistent.
7. $W$ is consistent (cited, not outlined).
   Willard proves this by a relative consistency argument with respect to ordinary arithmetic.
   By step 6 this is the same as the truth of $\theta$, and the content of the theorem is in this step.
   If the base $A$ contained $\mathsf{PA}$, the [[second incompleteness theorem|~c79a35b5]] would make $W$ inconsistent.
   The reason is that $\mathsf{PA}$ proves that tableau consistency and Hilbert-style consistency are equivalent.
   The argument uses that $A$ does not prove the totality of multiplication.
8. Properties 1 to 3 of the statement follow from steps 1, 5 and 7. Property 4 is part of Willard's analysis (cited): $W$ does not prove the $\Pi_2$ sentence that multiplication is total; then $W$ does not contain $\mathsf{Q}$, since in $\mathsf{Q}$ the totality of multiplication holds by logic. Property 5 is part of the same cited analysis, see step 2.

## Idea

Since the theory is too weak to construct a self-referential sentence internally, the self-reference is put into an axiom from outside.
That axiom says "I am consistent", so the theory proves its own consistency for free.
The whole difficulty moves to the question whether the theory with this axiom is consistent, which is the same as whether the axiom is true.
The axiom is true because the base is too weak for the argument of Gödel's second theorem.

## Variants

1. The same pattern works with many choices of base axioms and of the notion of proof; Willard studied a family of such systems and the boundary where self-verification fails.
2. With Hilbert-style proofs in place of tableaux, the corresponding statement is harder to obtain, because a Hilbert-style proof can be much shorter than any tableau proof of the same sentence, and the translation between them uses arithmetic that the weak base does not have.
