## Problem

For a formula $\varphi(u, v)$, possibly with parameters, write $\varphi : a \hookrightarrow b$ if $\varphi$ defines an injective map from $\set{0, \dots, a - 1}$ into $\set{0, \dots, b - 1}$.
That is, every $u < a$ has exactly one $v < b$ with $\varphi(u, v)$, and different $u$ have different $v$.
The [[$\Delta_0$|GE43CSZTS9]] pigeonhole principle $\Delta_0\text{-}\mathrm{PHP}$ is the schema: for every $\Delta_0$ formula $\varphi$ and every $z$, not $\varphi : z + 1 \hookrightarrow z$.

**Problem 1 (open).** Does [[$\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$|~38fa6f6e]] prove $\Delta_0\text{-}\mathrm{PHP}$?

**Problem 2 (open).** Does $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ prove the weak pigeonhole principle for $\Delta_0$ formulas: for every $\Delta_0$ formula $\varphi$ and every $z > 1$, not $\varphi : z^2 \hookrightarrow z$?

**Problem 3 (open).** Let $R$ be a new binary relation symbol.
Does $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0(R)$ prove the weak pigeonhole principle for $R$, with $2z$ or $z^2$ pigeons and $z$ holes?

Problem 3 is tied to [[proof complexity|1Q046J0AW2]] through the [[Paris-Wilkie translation|~c9c38d6d]].
A positive answer gives constant-depth Frege proofs of polynomial size for the propositional weak pigeonhole formulas.
All three problems are open.

## Remarks

### What is known

- For a new relation symbol and $z + 1$ pigeons the answer is negative. Ajtai proved that the pigeonhole principle for $R$ is unprovable in $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0(R)$, and even in $T_2(R)$. The propositional formulas $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ have no constant-depth Frege proofs of polynomial size, and later work made the lower bound exponential.
- Ajtai's theorem does not answer Problem 1, because a $\Delta_0$ formula without $R$ is not a new symbol: its Paris-Wilkie translation has no variables.
- Paris, Wilkie and Woods showed that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \Omega_1$ proves the weak principle for $\Delta_0$ formulas with $(1 + \varepsilon) z$ pigeons, for each rational $\varepsilon > 0$.
- They also showed that if $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ is finitely axiomatizable, then it proves the weak principle with $z^2$ pigeons for $\Delta_0$ formulas.
- In [[bounded arithmetic|~55a002f3]] with a new function symbol $f$, the theory $T^2_2(f)$ proves the surjective and the injective weak pigeonhole principles for $f$. No variant of the weak principle is provable in $S^2_2(f)$ (Krajíček; Riis).
- The propositional formulas $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ have Frege proofs of polynomial size (Buss), so the lower bounds depend on the restriction to constant depth.
- The propositional weak pigeonhole formulas have constant-depth Frege proofs of quasipolynomial size (Paris, Wilkie and Woods; Maciel, Pitassi and Woods). Whether polynomial size suffices is open.

### Why the problems matter

A positive answer to Problem 2 would give much of elementary number theory in $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$.
Paris, Wilkie and Woods showed that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ plus the weak principle for $\Delta_0$ formulas proves that there are infinitely many primes.
Whether $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ alone proves the infinitude of primes is an open question of Wilkie, and it is Problem 1 of the same paper.
The dual weak principle for polynomial-time functions is the basis of [[approximate counting|~a1e5c7e9]] in bounded arithmetic.

### Common errors

- To read Ajtai's theorem as a solution of Problem 1. It concerns a new symbol $R$, and Problem 1 concerns formulas of the language of arithmetic only.
- To think that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ can copy the usual induction proof of the pigeonhole principle. That proof quantifies over maps, and a map on $z$ points has a code of size about $z^z$, which $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ cannot prove to exist.

## History

Wilkie asked in 1978 whether $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ proves that there are infinitely many primes.
Paris, Wilkie and Woods stated the $\Delta_0$ pigeonhole problem in their 1988 paper, and proved the partial results above with a diagonalization argument.
Ajtai proved the unprovability of the pigeonhole principle for a new relation symbol in 1988.
Exponential lower bounds for $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ in constant-depth Frege systems were proved by Krajíček, Pudlák and Woods, and by Pitassi, Beame and Impagliazzo.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If $4$ pigeons sit in $3$ holes, some hole has two pigeons.
The question is whether a very weak system of axioms for numbers can prove this fact for all numbers of pigeons.
The weak system may only use statements in which every "for all" and every "there is" is limited to numbers below a given bound.
The usual proof looks at all possible seatings at once, and the number of seatings is far too large for the weak system.
Nobody knows whether a cleverer proof exists in the weak system.

### L2 human

We count why the usual proof does not work in $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$.

1. The usual proof is by induction on $z$: if $F : z + 1 \hookrightarrow z$, a modified map gives $z \hookrightarrow z - 1$.
2. The induction statement is "no injective map from $z + 1$ into $z$ exists", which quantifies over all maps.
3. A map from $\set{0, \dots, z}$ to $\set{0, \dots, z - 1}$ is a list of $z + 1$ values below $z$. Its code as a number is about $z^{z + 1}$.
4. For $z = 1000$, the input has $10$ binary digits, and the code has about $1001 \cdot 10 = 10010$ binary digits.
5. In general the code has about $z \cdot \abs{z}$ digits, which is exponential in $\abs{z}$. By [[Parikh's theorem|~5b376b4b]], $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ does not prove that such numbers exist.
6. So the induction formula is not $\Delta_0$, and induction on it is not available.

The typical use of the weak principle is as an extra axiom, for example to prove the infinitude of primes in $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ plus the weak principle.
A trap is to fix the map $F$ and induct on $z$: the modified maps change at every step, and a single $\Delta_0$ formula cannot describe all of them.

### L3 human

The problems sit at the meeting point of weak arithmetic, counting and proof complexity.
For theories with a new symbol, they become lower bound questions for constant-depth Frege systems, and the switching lemma of circuit complexity gives the known results.
For the language of arithmetic alone, no translation to proof complexity is available, and the problems are as hard as other questions about $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$, such as its finite axiomatizability.

The weak pigeonhole principles also measure the strength of counting.
Added as axioms to weak theories, they give [[approximate counting|~a1e5c7e9]], and Jeřábek's theories show that much of probabilistic reasoning follows from them.
The unprovability of weak pigeonhole principles in $S^2_2(f)$ separates it from $T^2_2(f)$, a relativized case of the [[separation problem|~4ddef1ef]].
