## Proof

We prove [[the finite model property theorem|~4d501751]].
Let $\Lambda$ be one of the seven logics, $\mathsf{C}$ its frame class, and $\Lambda \nvdash \varphi$.
Put $\Sigma = \operatorname{Sub}(\varphi)$, a finite set closed under subformulas with $|\Sigma| = n$.

1. Countermodel. By [[the completeness theorem|~1d9447ce]], there are a model $\mathfrak{M} = (W, R, V)$ on a frame of $\mathsf{C}$ and a world $w$ with $\mathfrak{M}, w \not\models \varphi$.
2. Choice of the filtration. For $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{T}$, $\mathsf{D}$ and $\mathsf{B}$, take the smallest filtration $R^s$. For $\mathsf{K4}$ and $\mathsf{S4}$, take the transitive filtration $R^t$. For $\mathsf{S5}$, take $R^e$.
3. Each choice is a filtration. For $R^s$ this is part 2 of [[the filtration theorem|~889ee92a]]. For $R^t$ it is part 4, which uses that $R$ is transitive. For $R^e$ it is part 6, which uses that $R$ is an equivalence relation.
4. The finite frame lies in $\mathsf{C}$. For $\mathsf{T}$ and $\mathsf{D}$, (min) carries reflexivity and seriality from $R$ to $R^s$, as in [[filtration|~9960fa2c]].
5. For $\mathsf{B}$, $R^s$ is reflexive by (min) and symmetric by part 5. For $\mathsf{K4}$, $R^t$ is transitive by part 4, and for $\mathsf{S4}$ it is also reflexive by part 4.
6. For $\mathsf{S5}$, $R^e$ is an equivalence relation by part 6. For $\mathsf{K}$, every frame is in $\mathsf{C}$.
7. Size and truth. By part 3, $\mathfrak{M}^f$ has at most $2^n$ worlds. By part 1, the filtration lemma, $\mathfrak{M}^f, [w] \not\models \varphi$, since $\varphi \in \Sigma$. This is part 1 of the theorem.
8. The logic of the finite frames. The frame of $\mathfrak{M}^f$ is in $\mathsf{C}$, so it validates $\Lambda$ by [[soundness|~d189ee4b]] and [[common modal axiom schemata|FDFQFWPM4X]]. So every frame of $\mathsf{C}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ validates $\Lambda$, and $\Lambda \subseteq \operatorname{Log}(\mathsf{C}_{\mathrm{fin}})$.
9. By step 7, every non-theorem of $\Lambda$ fails on a frame of $\mathsf{C}_{\mathrm{fin}}$. So $\operatorname{Log}(\mathsf{C}_{\mathrm{fin}}) \subseteq \Lambda$. This is part 2.
10. Decision. Up to isomorphism, there are finitely many frames with at most $2^n$ worlds, and for each one we can check the frame condition of $\mathsf{C}$.
11. On each such frame, the truth of $\varphi$ depends only on the values of the variables of $\varphi$. So there are finitely many valuations to test.
12. By steps 7 and 8, $\Lambda \vdash \varphi$ if and only if all these tests succeed. This is part 3.

## Idea

Completeness gives a countermodel on a frame of the class, and filtration makes it finite.
The one real choice is the relation on the quotient: it must keep the frame property of the logic.
For transitive logics, only the transitive filtration does so.

## Variants

1. One can filter the canonical model directly; then the classes are the maximal consistent subsets of $\Sigma \cup \set{\neg\psi : \psi \in \Sigma}$.
2. Tableau methods give the same result with countermodels built from the formula, and they also give the better space bounds of [[the complexity theorem|~c5e1a419]].
