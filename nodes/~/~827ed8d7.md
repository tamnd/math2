## Problem

Given an arbitrary angle, construct with [[straightedge and compass|~7930e158]] alone an angle one third as large.

## Remarks

Some angles can be trisected. For example, a right angle can be trisected, because an angle of $30^\circ$ is constructible.
The general problem asks for one method that works for every angle.
The answer is no: [[the angle of $60^\circ$ cannot be trisected|~917e6b6b]].
The problem has solutions with other tools, such as a marked ruler or origami.

## History

The problem comes from ancient Greek geometry.
Pierre Wantzel proved in 1837 that it has no solution for arbitrary angles.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Bisecting an angle is easy with a compass.
Trisecting it, that is, dividing it into three equal parts, is not possible in general with straightedge and compass alone.
