## Definition

Let $(X, d)$ be a [[metric space|~200ea42b]].
The metric topology of $(X, d)$ is the set $\tau_d$ of all $U \subseteq X$ such that for each $x \in U$ there is a real $\varepsilon > 0$ with $B(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$.
Here $B(x, \varepsilon)$ is the [[open ball|~43faf552]] with centre $x$ and radius $\varepsilon$.

## Remarks

The set $\tau_d$ is a [[topology|~77bfcc55]] on $X$: it contains $\varnothing$ and $X$, and it is closed under [[unions|~f9048772]] and [[finite intersections|~5728a40c]].
Each open ball is in $\tau_d$, by [[open balls are open|~054efe4e]]. The open balls form a [[basis|~b57c1608]] of $\tau_d$.
For $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual metric, $\tau_d$ is the usual topology, generated by the open intervals.
Different metrics can give the same topology. Such metrics are [[equivalent|~a4d651af]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The metric topology is how a metric space becomes a topological space.
A set is open when each of its points has a small ball about it inside the set.
