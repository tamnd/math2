## Statement

Let $(X, d)$ be a [[metric space|~200ea42b]], with the [[metric topology|~2852bf47]].
Then $X$ is a [[Hausdorff space|~a1fe0c25]].

## Remarks

Proof: for $x \neq y$, let $r = d(x, y) / 2 > 0$. The [[open balls|~43faf552]] $B(x, r)$ and $B(y, r)$ are open. They are disjoint: a point $z$ in both would give $d(x, y) \le d(x, z) + d(z, y) < 2r$, by the [[triangle inequality|~e1357625]].
Thus each [[metrizable space|~db159499]] is Hausdorff. A non-Hausdorff space, such as an infinite set with the cofinite topology, is not metrizable.
For a pseudometric, the statement fails: points at distance $0$ cannot be separated.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two distinct points of a metric space are at a positive distance. Balls of half that radius about them do not meet.
