## Proof

We prove part 1 of the [[Paris-Harrington theorem|509ZWA0R26]] from one cited lemma, and we cite parts 2 and 3.
Write $\mathrm{PH}$ for the [[strengthened finite Ramsey theorem|4P2BAT6YC3]], and $\mathsf{PA}$ for [[Peano arithmetic|MPSCREYC3M]].
A finite set $Y$ of positive integers is *large* if $\abs{Y} \ge \min Y$.

**Definition of the indicator.**
For $1 \le a \le b$, let $Y(a, b)$ be the greatest $e \le b$ with the following property.
Every $e$-colouring of $[\set{a, \dots, b}]^e$ has a large homogeneous set $Z \subseteq \set{a, \dots, b}$ with $\abs{Z} \ge e$.
The property is decided by a search over finitely many colourings, with a bound given by iterated exponentials of $b$.
So the relation $Y(a, b) \ge c$ is defined by a formula whose quantifiers are bounded by terms built with $+$, $\cdot$ and $x \mapsto 2^x$.

**Lemma 1: $\mathrm{PH}$ gives large values of $Y$.**
$\mathsf{PA}$ proves: if $\mathrm{PH}$ holds, then for all $a, e \ge 1$ there is $b$ with $Y(a, b) \ge e$.

1. Apply $\mathrm{PH}$ with exponent $e$, $e + 1$ colours and $m = a + e$. Let $N$ be the number that it gives, and let $b = \max(N, a)$.
2. Let $c$ be an $e$-colouring of $[\set{a, \dots, b}]^e$. Extend it to $[\set{1, \dots, b}]^e$: a set with least element below $a$ gets the colour $e + 1$.
3. Apply step 1 to the restriction to $[\set{1, \dots, N}]^e$. This gives a large homogeneous set $Z$ for the extended colouring with $\abs{Z} \ge a + e$.
4. Suppose $Z$ has the colour $e + 1$. Then $Z$ has at most $e - 1$ elements $\ge a$, because $e$ of them would form a set of colour at most $e$.
5. Then $\abs{Z} \le (a - 1) + (e - 1) < a + e$, which contradicts step 3. So the colour of $Z$ is at most $e$.
6. If $Z$ had an element $z < a$, then an $e$-subset of $Z$ containing $z$ would have the colour $e + 1$, since $\abs{Z} \ge e$. So $Z \subseteq \set{a, \dots, b}$.
7. So $Z$ is a large homogeneous set for $c$ with $\abs{Z} \ge e$. Also $e \le \abs{Z} \le b$.
8. So $e$ has the property in the definition of $Y(a, b)$, and $Y(a, b) \ge e$.
9. Every step is a finite combinatorial argument, so it is carried out in $\mathsf{PA}$.

**Cited lemma 2 (indicator lemma, Paris and Harrington).**
Let $M$ be a countable [[non-standard model|1QWN5CV4CG]] of $\mathsf{PA}$, and let $a < b$ be in $M$.
If $Y(a, b)$ is non-standard in $M$, then there is an initial segment $I$ of $M$ with $a \in I$, $b \notin I$ and $I \models \mathsf{PA}$.
This lemma is cited without proof.
Its proof builds $I$ from a sequence of nested homogeneous sets that behave like indiscernibles, and it uses the condition $\abs{Z} \ge \min Z$ at every stage.
In the terms of the [[indicators|~1d28aa25]] page, it says that $Y$ is an indicator for initial segments that are models of $\mathsf{PA}$.

**Lemma 3: absoluteness.**
Let $I$ be an initial segment of $M$ with $I \models \mathsf{PA}$, and let $a, b', c \in I$.
Then $I \models Y(a, b') \ge c$ if and only if $M \models Y(a, b') \ge c$.

1. $I$ satisfies "$2^x$ exists" for every $x \in I$. The graph of $x \mapsto 2^x$ has a bounded definition, so $2^x$ is the same element in $I$ and in $M$.
2. So $I$ is closed under the terms that bound the quantifiers in the definition of $Y(a, b') \ge c$.
3. A formula with bounded quantifiers has the same truth value in an initial segment and in the full model, when its parameters and bounds are in the segment. This is proved by induction on the formula.

**Proof of part 1.**

1. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\mathsf{PA}$ proves $\mathrm{PH}$. Then $\mathsf{PA}$ proves $\forall a\, \exists b\; Y(a, b) \ge a$, by Lemma 1 with $e = a$.
2. By the [[compactness theorem|1NR3HAWG9D]] and the downward Löwenheim-Skolem theorem, there is a countable non-standard model $M$ of $\mathsf{PA}$. Let $a \in M$ be non-standard.
3. By step 1, $M \models \exists b\; Y(a, b) \ge a$. By the least number principle in $M$, there is a least such $b$ in $M$.
4. Then $Y(a, b) \ge a$ is non-standard, so cited lemma 2 gives an initial segment $I$ with $a \in I$, $b \notin I$ and $I \models \mathsf{PA}$.
5. By step 1, $I \models \exists b'\; Y(a, b') \ge a$, because $I$ is a model of $\mathsf{PA}$. Take such a $b' \in I$.
6. By Lemma 3, $M \models Y(a, b') \ge a$.
7. Also $b' < b$, because $b' \in I$, $b \notin I$ and $I$ is an initial segment.
8. This contradicts the choice of $b$ as the least such element. So $\mathsf{PA}$ does not prove $\mathrm{PH}$.

**Parts 2 and 3: cited results.**

1. **Cited lemma 4** (Paris and Harrington). $\mathsf{PA}$ proves that $\mathrm{PH}$ is equivalent to $1\text{-}\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, which says that every [[$\Sigma_1$|CV2E6JZHVP]] sentence provable in $\mathsf{PA}$ is true.
2. Part 2 is lemma 4. So $\mathsf{PA} + 1\text{-}\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ proves $\mathrm{PH}$.
3. The [[compactness proof|~35cec6ef]] of $\mathrm{PH}$ uses the infinite Ramsey theorem and an infinite tree argument. Both are proved in second-order arithmetic and in $\mathsf{ZFC}$, which gives the rest of part 3.
4. **Cited lemma 5** (Ketonen and Solovay 1981). The least witness $\sigma(x, x, x)$ of $\mathrm{PH}$ eventually dominates every function that $\mathsf{PA}$ proves total. This is the growth form of part 1.

## Idea

If $\mathsf{PA}$ proved $\mathrm{PH}$, then every non-standard model would contain an interval $[a, b]$ with a non-standard indicator value, and a least such $b$.
The indicator lemma turns this interval into an initial segment that is itself a model of $\mathsf{PA}$ and ends below $b$.
Inside that segment $\mathrm{PH}$ holds again, and it gives a smaller $b$, which is a contradiction.

## Variants

- Lemma 4 gives a second proof of part 1. If $\mathsf{PA}$ proved $\mathrm{PH}$, it would prove $1\text{-}\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ and so $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, against Gödel's second incompleteness theorem.
- Ketonen and Solovay gave a direct combinatorial proof through growth rates, with no non-standard models.
- The same scheme with another indicator proves the [[Kanamori-McAloon theorem|~53c52c9d]].
