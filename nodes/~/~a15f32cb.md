## Example

We use the focused system for MALL from the page on [[focusing|~bc719aec]], with atoms $a, b$ positive and $a^{\perp}, b^{\perp}$ negative.
We find a focused proof of the sequent
$$\vdash a^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} b^{\perp}, \ a \otimes b ,$$
which is the one-sided form of $a \otimes b \vdash a \otimes b$ in [[linear logic|H408H4WHBA]].
The focused goal is $\vdash \cdot \Uparrow a^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} b^{\perp}, a \otimes b$.
We list the steps from the goal upward, as a proof search.

1. The first formula of the list is negative, with main connective $\mathbin{\wp}$. Rule $[\mathbin{\wp}]$ gives the premise $\vdash \cdot \Uparrow a^{\perp}, b^{\perp}, a \otimes b$.
2. $a^{\perp}$ is a negated atom, so Store gives $\vdash a^{\perp} \Uparrow b^{\perp}, a \otimes b$.
3. $b^{\perp}$ is a negated atom, so Store gives $\vdash a^{\perp}, b^{\perp} \Uparrow a \otimes b$.
4. $a \otimes b$ is positive, so Store gives $\vdash a^{\perp}, b^{\perp}, a \otimes b \Uparrow \cdot$.
5. The list is empty, so the inversion phase ends. The only positive formula in the context is $a \otimes b$, so Decide gives $\vdash a^{\perp}, b^{\perp} \Downarrow a \otimes b$.
6. Rule $[\otimes]$ needs a split of $a^{\perp}, b^{\perp}$ into $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$. We take $\Gamma = a^{\perp}$ and $\Delta = b^{\perp}$.
7. The premises are $\vdash a^{\perp} \Downarrow a$ and $\vdash b^{\perp} \Downarrow b$.
8. Both are instances of $[\mathrm{init}]$, so the proof is complete.

We check that this is the only focused proof.

9. Steps 1 to 4 are forced: in an inversion sequent, the first formula of the list determines the rule.
10. In step 5 the negated atoms cannot be decided, because Decide applies only to positive formulas.
11. In step 6 there are four splits. A premise $\vdash \Gamma \Downarrow a$ with $a$ a positive atom needs $[\mathrm{init}]$, so $\Gamma$ must be exactly $a^{\perp}$.
12. The same argument forces $\Delta = b^{\perp}$. So the split of step 6 is the only one that works.

## Remarks

The proof has one inversion phase (steps 1 to 4) and one focus phase (steps 5 to 8).
The focus phase decomposes $a \otimes b$ completely, because both $a$ and $b$ are positive.
Erasing the arrows gives the MALL proof: ${\otimes}$ on two initial sequents $\vdash a^{\perp}, a$ and $\vdash b^{\perp}, b$, then ${\mathbin{\wp}}$.

If we replace $a \otimes b$ by $a \oplus b$, the search fails.
After the same inversion phase, Decide gives $\vdash a^{\perp}, b^{\perp} \Downarrow a \oplus b$, and $[\oplus_1]$ gives $\vdash a^{\perp}, b^{\perp} \Downarrow a$.
This is not an instance of $[\mathrm{init}]$, because the context has two formulas, and $[\oplus_2]$ fails in the same way.
This matches MALL, where $a \otimes b \vdash a \oplus b$ is not provable because there is no weakening.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The goal says, in linear logic, that the resource "$a$ and $b$" gives the resource "$a$ and $b$".
The search first does all the safe steps: it unpacks the left side into $a^{\perp}$ and $b^{\perp}$ and stores them.
Then it picks the only formula with a real choice, $a \otimes b$, and must decide how to share the stored resources.
The only sharing that works gives $a^{\perp}$ to $a$ and $b^{\perp}$ to $b$.

### L2 human

The proof search runs in two phases.

1. Inversion: apply $[\mathbin{\wp}]$ and Store until the list is empty.
2. Focus: decide on $a \otimes b$, split the context, and close both branches with $[\mathrm{init}]$.

The typical use of this example is to show that inversion is deterministic and that all real choices are in the focus phase.
A trap is to decide on $a \otimes b$ before the inversion phase ends; the focused system does not allow Decide while the list is nonempty.
