## Summary

Fix an integer $k \ge 1$ or $k = \infty$. A subset $M \subset \R^n$ is a *$C^k$ hypersurface* if it is locally the zero set of a function with nonzero derivative.

Precisely, for every $p \in M$ there are an open neighbourhood $V$ of $p$ and a function $r \colon V \to \R$ of class $C^k$ (see [[smoothness classes|CRJSW18XVM]]) such that the [[derivative|0FNKFT8H6E]] of $r$ is nonzero at every point of $V$, and $M \cap V = \{x \in V : r(x) = 0\}$. Such a function $r$ is a *defining function* of $M$ at $p$.

An open set $U \subset \R^n$ has *$C^k$ boundary* if $\partial U$ is a $C^k$ hypersurface and, at each $p \in \partial U$, some defining function $r$ satisfies $r < 0$ on $U$ and $r > 0$ outside the closure of $U$ near $p$.

The word *smooth* alone means $C^\infty$, so the defining functions are infinitely differentiable.
