## Proof

We use three general facts about derivations in the [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]] L.
(F1) If $\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'$ and $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$, then $\Gamma' \vdash \varphi$, because a derivation from $\Gamma$ is also a derivation from $\Gamma'$.
(F2) If $\vdash \varphi$, then $\varphi$ can be written as a line of any derivation, since a derivation of $\varphi$ can be inserted before that line.
(F3) The [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]]: $\Gamma \cup \set{\varphi} \vdash \psi$ holds if and only if $\Gamma \vdash \varphi \to \psi$.
We write MP $i, j$ for [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] applied to line $i$ (the formula $\varphi$) and line $j$ (the formula $\varphi \to \psi$).
"Hyp" marks a hypothesis.
Each axiom instance is given with the substitution for the letters of the schema.

**T1.** The following five lines derive $\varphi \to \varphi$ from no hypotheses.

1. $\varphi \to ((\varphi \to \varphi) \to \varphi)$, by A1 with $\psi := \varphi \to \varphi$.
2. $(\varphi \to ((\varphi \to \varphi) \to \varphi)) \to ((\varphi \to (\varphi \to \varphi)) \to (\varphi \to \varphi))$, by A2 with $\psi := \varphi \to \varphi$ and $\chi := \varphi$.
3. $(\varphi \to (\varphi \to \varphi)) \to (\varphi \to \varphi)$, by MP 1, 2.
4. $\varphi \to (\varphi \to \varphi)$, by A1 with $\psi := \varphi$.
5. $\varphi \to \varphi$, by MP 4, 3.

**T2.** We derive $\varphi$ from $\set{\neg \neg \varphi}$.

1. $\neg \neg \varphi$, Hyp.
2. $\neg \neg \varphi \to (\neg \varphi \to \neg \neg \varphi)$, by A1 with $\varphi := \neg \neg \varphi$ and $\psi := \neg \varphi$.
3. $\neg \varphi \to \neg \neg \varphi$, by MP 1, 2.
4. $(\neg \varphi \to \neg \neg \varphi) \to ((\neg \varphi \to \neg \varphi) \to \varphi)$, by A3 with $\psi := \varphi$ and $\varphi := \neg \varphi$.
5. $(\neg \varphi \to \neg \varphi) \to \varphi$, by MP 3, 4.
6. $\neg \varphi \to \neg \varphi$, by T1 with $\varphi := \neg \varphi$ and (F2).
7. $\varphi$, by MP 6, 5.
8. So $\set{\neg \neg \varphi} \vdash \varphi$, and (F3) with $\Gamma = \emptyset$ gives $\vdash \neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi$.

**T3.** We derive $\neg \neg \varphi$ from $\set{\varphi}$.

1. $\varphi$, Hyp.
2. $\varphi \to (\neg \neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi)$, by A1 with $\psi := \neg \neg \neg \varphi$.
3. $\neg \neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi$, by MP 1, 2.
4. $\neg \neg \neg \varphi \to \neg \varphi$, by T2 with $\varphi := \neg \varphi$ and (F2).
5. $(\neg \neg \neg \varphi \to \neg \varphi) \to ((\neg \neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi) \to \neg \neg \varphi)$, by A3 with $\psi := \neg \neg \varphi$.
6. $(\neg \neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi) \to \neg \neg \varphi$, by MP 4, 5.
7. $\neg \neg \varphi$, by MP 3, 6.
8. So $\set{\varphi} \vdash \neg \neg \varphi$, and (F3) gives $\vdash \varphi \to \neg \neg \varphi$.

**T4.** We derive $\psi$ from $\set{\neg \varphi, \varphi}$.

1. $\neg \varphi$, Hyp.
2. $\varphi$, Hyp.
3. $\neg \varphi \to (\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi)$, by A1 with $\varphi := \neg \varphi$ and $\psi := \neg \psi$.
4. $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$, by MP 1, 3.
5. $\varphi \to (\neg \psi \to \varphi)$, by A1 with $\psi := \neg \psi$.
6. $\neg \psi \to \varphi$, by MP 2, 5.
7. $(\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi) \to ((\neg \psi \to \varphi) \to \psi)$, by A3.
8. $(\neg \psi \to \varphi) \to \psi$, by MP 4, 7.
9. $\psi$, by MP 6, 8.
10. So $\set{\neg \varphi, \varphi} \vdash \psi$. Then (F3) gives $\set{\neg \varphi} \vdash \varphi \to \psi$, and (F3) again gives $\vdash \neg \varphi \to (\varphi \to \psi)$.

**T5.** We derive $\psi$ from $\set{\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi, \varphi}$.

1. $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$, Hyp.
2. $\varphi$, Hyp.
3. $(\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi) \to ((\neg \psi \to \varphi) \to \psi)$, by A3.
4. $(\neg \psi \to \varphi) \to \psi$, by MP 1, 3.
5. $\varphi \to (\neg \psi \to \varphi)$, by A1 with $\psi := \neg \psi$.
6. $\neg \psi \to \varphi$, by MP 2, 5.
7. $\psi$, by MP 6, 4.
8. So $\set{\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi, \varphi} \vdash \psi$, and (F3) used twice gives $\vdash (\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi) \to (\varphi \to \psi)$.

**T6.** We derive $\neg \neg \psi$ from $\set{\varphi \to \psi, \neg \neg \varphi}$, and then use T5.

1. $\varphi \to \psi$, Hyp.
2. $\neg \neg \varphi$, Hyp.
3. $\neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi$, by T2 and (F2).
4. $\varphi$, by MP 2, 3.
5. $\psi$, by MP 4, 1.
6. $\psi \to \neg \neg \psi$, by T3 with $\varphi := \psi$ and (F2).
7. $\neg \neg \psi$, by MP 5, 6.
8. So $\set{\varphi \to \psi, \neg \neg \varphi} \vdash \neg \neg \psi$, and (F3) gives $\set{\varphi \to \psi} \vdash \neg \neg \varphi \to \neg \neg \psi$.
9. T5 with $\varphi := \neg \psi$ and $\psi := \neg \varphi$ gives $\vdash (\neg \neg \varphi \to \neg \neg \psi) \to (\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi)$.
10. By (F1) and MP with steps 8 and 9, $\set{\varphi \to \psi} \vdash \neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$.
11. Then (F3) gives $\vdash (\varphi \to \psi) \to (\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi)$.

**T7.** We derive $\psi$ from $\set{\varphi, \varphi \to \psi}$, and then use T6.

1. $\varphi$, Hyp.
2. $\varphi \to \psi$, Hyp.
3. $\psi$, by MP 1, 2.
4. So $\set{\varphi, \varphi \to \psi} \vdash \psi$, and (F3) gives $\set{\varphi} \vdash (\varphi \to \psi) \to \psi$.
5. T6 with $\varphi := \varphi \to \psi$ gives $\vdash ((\varphi \to \psi) \to \psi) \to (\neg \psi \to \neg (\varphi \to \psi))$.
6. By (F1) and MP with steps 4 and 5, $\set{\varphi} \vdash \neg \psi \to \neg (\varphi \to \psi)$.
7. Then (F3) gives $\vdash \varphi \to (\neg \psi \to \neg (\varphi \to \psi))$.

**T8.** We derive $\psi$ from $\set{\varphi \to \psi, \neg \varphi \to \psi}$.

1. $\varphi \to \psi$, Hyp.
2. $\neg \varphi \to \psi$, Hyp.
3. $(\varphi \to \psi) \to (\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi)$, by T6 and (F2).
4. $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$, by MP 1, 3.
5. $(\neg \varphi \to \psi) \to (\neg \psi \to \neg \neg \varphi)$, by T6 with $\varphi := \neg \varphi$ and (F2).
6. $\neg \psi \to \neg \neg \varphi$, by MP 2, 5.
7. $(\neg \psi \to \neg \neg \varphi) \to ((\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi) \to \psi)$, by A3 with $\varphi := \neg \varphi$.
8. $(\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi) \to \psi$, by MP 6, 7.
9. $\psi$, by MP 4, 8.
10. So $\set{\varphi \to \psi, \neg \varphi \to \psi} \vdash \psi$, and (F3) used twice gives $\vdash (\varphi \to \psi) \to ((\neg \varphi \to \psi) \to \psi)$.

**HS.** Assume $\Gamma \vdash \varphi \to \psi$ and $\Gamma \vdash \psi \to \chi$.

1. By (F1), $\Gamma \cup \set{\varphi} \vdash \varphi \to \psi$ and $\Gamma \cup \set{\varphi} \vdash \psi \to \chi$.
2. The formula $\varphi$ is a hypothesis, so $\Gamma \cup \set{\varphi} \vdash \varphi$.
3. By MP with steps 2 and 1, $\Gamma \cup \set{\varphi} \vdash \psi$.
4. By MP again, $\Gamma \cup \set{\varphi} \vdash \chi$.
5. Then (F3) gives $\Gamma \vdash \varphi \to \chi$.

This proves T1 to T8 and HS for all formulas $\varphi$, $\psi$ and $\chi$.

## Idea

T1 is a direct derivation from A1 and A2, and it is the only part that does not use the deduction theorem.
For each other theorem we assume the antecedents as hypotheses, derive the consequent, and then remove the hypotheses with the deduction theorem.
The axiom A3 is the only source of the negation laws: T2 and T3 come first, and the contraposition laws T5 and T6 are built from them.
T7 and T8 then follow from T6 and A3.

## Variants

The same theorems can be derived without the deduction theorem, but the derivations are much longer; the deduction theorem only shortens the writing.
With Łukasiewicz's third axiom $(\neg \varphi \to \neg \psi) \to (\psi \to \varphi)$ in place of A3, T5 is an axiom instance, and the order of the derivations changes.
Another route uses the [[completeness theorem for propositional logic|~e697b103]]: each of T1 to T8 is a [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]], so it is derivable.
That route is circular here, because the standard proofs of completeness for L use T1 to T8.
