## Proof

We show that $\Hom(V, W)$ is a subspace of the space of all maps $V \to W$.

1. Let $W^V$ be the set of all maps $V \to W$. With pointwise operations, $W^V$ is a vector space, by the [[function space example|0GZB2SN0PH]].
2. The operations of $\Hom(V, W)$ in its [[definition|0VPCJWGMP9]] are the operations of $W^V$. So it is enough to show that $\Hom(V, W)$ is a [[subspace|0EPTJA5QV4]] of $W^V$.
3. The zero map is [[linear|0RJF668P1T]]: $0(u + v) = 0 = 0(u) + 0(v)$ and $0(av) = 0 = a \cdot 0(v)$.
4. Let $S, T \in \Hom(V, W)$, $u, v \in V$ and $a \in F$. Additivity of $S$ and $T$ gives the next equality.
5. $(S + T)(u + v) = S(u) + S(v) + T(u) + T(v) = (S + T)(u) + (S + T)(v)$.
6. Homogeneity of $S$ and $T$ gives $(S + T)(av) = a\,S(v) + a\,T(v) = a\,(S + T)(v)$. So $S + T$ is linear.
7. Let $c \in F$. Then $(cT)(u + v) = c\,(T(u) + T(v)) = (cT)(u) + (cT)(v)$.
8. Also $(cT)(av) = c\,a\,T(v) = a\,c\,T(v) = a\,(cT)(v)$. This step uses $ca = ac$ in $F$.
9. So $cT$ is linear. By steps 3, 6 and 9, $\Hom(V, W)$ contains $0$ and is closed under both operations.
10. So $\Hom(V, W)$ is a subspace of $W^V$, and hence a vector space over $F$.
11. The zero vector of $W^V$ is the zero map. The negative of $T$ is $(-1)T$, which sends $v$ to $-T(v)$.

## Idea

All the vector space axioms already hold for arbitrary maps $V \to W$, because they hold in $W$ value by value.
The only work is to check that sums and scalar multiples of linear maps stay linear.

## Variants

- One can check the eight vector space axioms directly in $\Hom(V, W)$. Each axiom reduces to the same axiom in $W$, applied to the values at $v$.
- For finite-dimensional spaces with bases, $\Hom(V, W)$ corresponds to the matrices $M_{m \times n}(F)$. The result then follows from the vector space structure of matrices, but this proof depends on bases.
