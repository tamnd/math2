## Definition

A [[group|G5S1M9]] $G$ is nilpotent when it has a central series. This is a finite sequence $\{e\} = N_0 \subseteq N_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq N_n = G$ of [[normal subgroups|~ebf813cb]] of $G$ with $N_{i+1}/N_i \subseteq Z(G/N_i)$ for each $i < n$.
Here $Z(G/N_i)$ is the [[centre|~ff52f9c7]] of the [[quotient group|~592bfb26]] $G/N_i$.

## Remarks

The condition $N_{i+1}/N_i \subseteq Z(G/N_i)$ means $[N_{i+1}, G] \subseteq N_i$.
Equivalently, the [[lower central series|~573765d2]] reaches $\{e\}$, or the [[upper central series|~e94622c4]] reaches $G$; the least such length is the nilpotency class.
The nilpotent groups of class at most $1$ are exactly the [[abelian groups|~b6df27fe]].
The quaternion group $Q_8$ is nilpotent of class $2$, but $S_3$ is not nilpotent.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A nilpotent group is close to abelian in a strong sense.
Each layer of a central series commutes with the whole group, up to the lower layers.
Each nilpotent group is solvable, but the converse is false, for example for $S_3$.
