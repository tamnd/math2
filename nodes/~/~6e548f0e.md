## Statement

Let $A$ be a finite [[subdirectly irreducible|TYYS649FAJ]] Heyting algebra with unique coatom $s_A$.
Let $\chi(A) = \delta(A) \to p_{s_A}$ be its [[Jankov formula|~be90dd8c]].
Write $\mathbf{SH}(B)$ for the class of algebras isomorphic to a subalgebra of a homomorphic image of $B$.

A *p-morphism* $f \colon H \to F$ of intuitionistic [[Kripke frames|~11b532e9]] is a monotone map such that $f(y) \leq x$ implies $x = f(y')$ for some $y' \geq y$.
A *generated subframe* of $G$ is an up-set of $G$ with the induced order.
Write $F \leq G$ if $F$ is the image of a surjective p-morphism from a generated subframe of $G$.

**Theorem.**

1. Algebraic form: for every [[Heyting algebra|BND6A96S3D]] $B$, $\chi(A)$ is not valid in $B$ iff $A \in \mathbf{SH}(B)$.
2. Frame form: for every finite rooted frame $F$ and every frame $G$, $G \nvDash \chi(F)$ iff $F \leq G$.
3. Splitting: for every superintuitionistic logic $L$, exactly one of $\chi(A) \in L$ and $L \subseteq \mathrm{Log}(A)$ holds.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The unique coatom gives the formula its conclusion $p_{s_A}$; for a product such as [[the four-element Boolean algebra|~243f13a8]] no formula has property 1.
Finiteness makes $\delta(A)$ a finite conjunction; Citkin's version replaces it by finite presentability.
In part 2 the frame $G$ may be infinite, and only $F$ must be finite and rooted.
In part 3, $L$ may be the inconsistent logic; then $\chi(A) \in L$ holds.

### Consequences

1. $A \nvDash \chi(A)$, since $A \in \mathbf{SH}(A)$.
2. For finite rooted frames $F$ and $G$, $G \vDash \chi(F)$ iff $F \not\leq G$. So an antichain of frames gives independent formulas, the key step of [[Jankov's continuum theorem|~7c8927a2]].
3. [[The Jankov formula of the two-element algebra|~7da3b272]] has $\bot$ as an instance. So every consistent superintuitionistic logic lies inside $\mathsf{CPC}$.
4. [[The two-element chain frame|~c76f17e6]] shows that $\neg \neg p \to p$ is refuted exactly on the frames $G$ with $C_2 \leq G$.
5. In the lattice of [[varieties|AJNZJD8442]] of Heyting algebras, $A$ is a splitting algebra and $\chi(A)$ axiomatizes the largest variety omitting $A$.

### Converse

Part 3 says that each finite subdirectly irreducible algebra is a splitting algebra.
Conversely, the splitting algebras of the variety of Heyting algebras are exactly the finite subdirectly irreducible ones.
We cite this converse without proof from Section 1.5 of Citkin's survey.

### Generalizations

1. Citkin proves part 1 for every finitely presented subdirectly irreducible $A$.
2. For closure algebras the same construction gives the Fine formulas; see [[the lattice of normal modal logics|EZ011FT3GT]].
3. Zakharyaschev's canonical formulas extend part 2 so that every intermediate logic is axiomatizable.

## History

Jankov proved the algebraic form in 1963 and used it for intermediate logics.
De Jongh gave the frame form, with formulas in fewer variables.
The lecture notes of Bezhanishvili and de Jongh prove part 2 with de Jongh formulas and universal models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pick a small finite structure of truth values.
Its Jankov formula is true in a structure exactly when the small one does not "occur" in it.
Occur means: after collapsing some values and keeping only some of the rest, you see a copy of the small structure.
So one formula captures one forbidden pattern.

### L2 human

1. Let $F = C_2$, a root $r$ below a top $t$.
2. Let $G$ be the three-element chain $a < b < c$.
3. The generated subframe $\set{b, c}$ maps onto $C_2$ by $b \mapsto r$ and $c \mapsto t$.
4. So $C_2 \leq G$ and $G \nvDash \chi(C_2)$.
5. Also $a \mapsto r$, $b \mapsto t$, $c \mapsto t$ is a surjective p-morphism from all of $G$.

The typical use is to turn "this logic proves $\chi(F)$" into "no frame of this logic contains $F$ in the order $\leq$".
A trap: part 3 is algebraic.
For a logic that is not Kripke complete, $L \subseteq \mathrm{Log}(F)$ cannot be checked on frames of $L$ alone.

### L3 human

The theorem shows that every finite subdirectly irreducible Heyting algebra splits the lattice of superintuitionistic logics into the filter above $\chi(A)$ and the ideal below $\mathrm{Log}(A)$.
The proof of part 1 uses that congruences of a Heyting algebra are determined by filters, and that the filter generated by one element $d$ gives the quotient $\mathord{\downarrow} d$.
So a refutation of $\delta(A) \to p_{s_A}$ in $B$ becomes, after one quotient, a copy of $A$.
The frame form is the dual statement: generated subframes are dual to quotients, and p-morphic images to subalgebras.
For infinite $G$ the two forms are linked through descriptive frames, but the direct proof below avoids duality.
