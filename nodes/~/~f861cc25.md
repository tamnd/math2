## Example

In [[propositional dynamic logic|VJ78A0Q22R]] the program $\mathbf{while}\ p\ \mathbf{do}\ a$ is the regular program
$$\omega = (p ? ; a)^* ; \neg p ? .$$
Let $\mathfrak{M} = (W, R_a, V)$ with $W = \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, $R_a = \{(0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 2), (3, 3)\}$ and $V(p) = \{0, 1, 3\}$.
We compute $R_\omega$.

1. $R_{p ?} = \{(w, w) : w \in V(p)\} = \{(0, 0), (1, 1), (3, 3)\}$.
2. $R_{p ? ; a}$ joins a $p ?$-step with an $a$-step. From $(0, 0)$ and $0 R_a 1$ we get $(0, 1)$. From $(1, 1)$ and $1 R_a 2$ we get $(1, 2)$. From $(3, 3)$ and $3 R_a 3$ we get $(3, 3)$. So $R_{p ? ; a} = \{(0, 1), (1, 2), (3, 3)\}$.
3. The reflexive transitive closure adds the identity and the chain $(0, 1), (1, 2)$, which gives $(0, 2)$. So $R_{(p ? ; a)^*} = \{(0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 2), (3, 3), (0, 1), (1, 2), (0, 2)\}$.
4. $R_{\neg p ?} = \{(2, 2)\}$, since $2$ is the only world where $p$ is false.
5. $R_\omega$ contains $(w, u)$ when $w R_{(p ? ; a)^*} v$ and $v R_{\neg p ?} u$. The second step forces $v = u = 2$. The pairs of step 3 that end in $2$ are $(0, 2)$, $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 2)$.
6. So $R_\omega = \{(0, 2), (1, 2), (2, 2)\}$.

## Remarks

The relation $R_\omega$ matches the runs of the loop: from $0$ the loop runs twice and stops at $2$, from $1$ it runs once, and from $2$ it does not run.
World $3$ has no $\omega$-successor, because $p$ stays true along $3, 3, 3, \dots$ and the loop never stops.
So $[\omega] \bot$ holds at $3$, and $\langle \omega \rangle \top$ fails at $3$: the formula $\langle \omega \rangle \top$ expresses termination.
The formula $[\omega] \neg p$ is valid in every model, since every pair of $R_\omega$ ends with a $\neg p ?$-step.
In the language of [[Hoare logic|H1M6RSBJ5H]] this is the triple $\{\top\} \, \mathbf{while}\ p\ \mathbf{do}\ a \, \{\neg p\}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A while loop repeats a step as long as a condition holds.
Here the condition is $p$ and the step is $a$.
Starting in world $0$, the condition holds, so we step to $1$; it still holds, so we step to $2$; now it fails, and the loop stops at $2$.
In world $3$ the step leads back to $3$, where the condition still holds, so the loop runs forever.

### L2 human

We check that $[\omega] \neg p$ holds at every world of the model.

1. At $0$, the only $\omega$-successor is $2$, and $2 \notin V(p)$. True.
2. At $1$, the only $\omega$-successor is $2$. True.
3. At $2$, the only $\omega$-successor is $2$. True.
4. At $3$, there is no $\omega$-successor, so $[\omega] \neg p$ holds because there is nothing to check.

The typical use of this translation is to prove loop properties with the induction axiom of $\mathsf{PDL}$.
A trap: $[\omega] \neg p$ does not say that the loop stops; at world $3$ it holds although the loop runs forever.
