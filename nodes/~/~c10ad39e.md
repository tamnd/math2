## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over $F$, and let $T \colon V \to W$ be a [[linear map|0RJF668P1T]].
Then the following hold.

1. $T(0) = 0$, where the first $0$ is the zero vector of $V$ and the second is the zero vector of $W$.
2. $T(-v) = -T(v)$ for each $v \in V$.
3. For each integer $k \ge 1$, all $v_1, \dots, v_k \in V$ and all $a_1, \dots, a_k \in F$, the [[linear combination|0V8F3E48E6]] is preserved:
$$T(a_1 v_1 + \dots + a_k v_k) = a_1 T(v_1) + \dots + a_k T(v_k).$$

Also, a function $T \colon V \to W$ is linear if and only if $T(au + v) = a\,T(u) + T(v)$ for all $u, v \in V$ and $a \in F$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Parts 1 and 2 use only additivity. Homogeneity alone also gives part 1, since $T(0) = T(0 \cdot 0) = 0 \cdot T(0) = 0$.
- Part 3 needs both conditions. The map $(x, y) \mapsto \sqrt[3]{x^3 + y^3}$ on $\R^2$ is homogeneous, but it sends $(1, 0) + (0, 1)$ to $\sqrt[3]{2}$, not to $1 + 1$.
- Complex conjugation on $\C$ is additive, but it sends $i \cdot 1$ to $-i$, not to $i \cdot 1$. So part 3 fails for it with $k = 1$ and $a_1 = i$.

### Consequences

- Part 1 is a quick test of non-linearity: a [[translation|~866bb5bd]] by $w_0 \ne 0$ sends $0$ to $w_0$, so it is not linear.
- Part 3 says that a linear map is fixed by its values on a [[spanning set|088RYKAWK9]]; this is the first half of the theorem that a [[linear map is determined by a basis|0R8X9XA83A]].
- Part 3 also says that the image of a [[subspace|00YC1D652K]] is closed under linear combinations.

### Converse

The converse of part 1 is false.
The [[squaring map|~1498930f]] $x \mapsto x^2$ on $\R$ sends $0$ to $0$, but it is not linear.
The converse of part 3 is true: if part 3 holds for $k = 2$, then $T$ is linear, by the case $a_1 = a_2 = 1$ and the case $a_2 = 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A linear map always sends the zero vector to the zero vector.
It sends the opposite of a vector to the opposite of its image.
If a vector is built from other vectors by scaling and adding, its image is built from their images in the same way.
For example, if $T(v_1) = 5$ and $T(v_2) = 1$, then $T(2v_1 + 3v_2) = 2 \cdot 5 + 3 \cdot 1 = 13$.

### L2 human

Let $T \colon \R^2 \to \R^2$ be linear with $T(1, 0) = (2, 1)$ and $T(0, 1) = (0, 3)$.

1. The vector $(4, -2)$ is $4 \cdot (1, 0) + (-2) \cdot (0, 1)$.
2. Part 3 gives $T(4, -2) = 4\,(2, 1) - 2\,(0, 3) = (8, 4 - 6) = (8, -2)$.
3. Part 2 gives $T(-4, 2) = -(8, -2) = (-8, 2)$.

The typical use is to compute a linear map from a few values, or to reject a map as non-linear because $T(0) \ne 0$.
The trap is the converse of part 1: a map with $T(0) = 0$ can still fail to be linear.

### L3 human

Part 3 says that a linear map is a homomorphism for all finite linear combinations, and that is the structure of a vector space.
For infinite sums, part 3 needs a topology and continuity, which is the setting of [[continuous linear maps|03TM135G0N]].
The equivalent single condition $T(au + v) = aT(u) + T(v)$ is the usual form of the definition in proofs and in proof assistants.
