## Statement

A Kripke frame $(W, \leq)$ is *linear* if any two worlds are comparable.
It is *locally linear* if, for every $w$, the set $\mathord{\uparrow} w = \set{v : w \leq v}$ is linear.

**Theorem.**

1. Frame correspondence: $(p \to q) \vee (q \to p)$ is valid on a frame $F$ iff $F$ is locally linear.
2. Completeness: for every formula $\varphi$, the following are equivalent.
   - (a) $\mathsf{LC} \vdash \varphi$.
   - (b) $\varphi$ is valid on every linear frame.
   - (c) $\varphi$ is valid on every finite linear frame.
3. Bound: if $\mathsf{LC} \nvdash \varphi$, then $\varphi$ fails at the root of a model on a chain with at most $n + 1$ worlds, where $n$ is the number of subformulas of $\varphi$.

The proof is in [[the proof by canonical chains|~2cd78669]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. Linearity can not be dropped: the fork refutes prelinearity; see [[the fork|~84f5b36b]].
2. In item 1, linearity of the whole frame is too strong. The disjoint union of two one-world frames is locally linear, not linear, and validates $\mathsf{LC}$.
3. In item 2, finiteness of the frames costs nothing, but one finite chain does not suffice. By SEP, $C_n$ validates the extra axiom of $\mathsf{G}_n$, which fails on $C_{n+1}$.

### Consequences

1. $\mathsf{LC}$ is decidable: check $\varphi$ on all models on chains with at most $n + 1$ worlds.
2. $\mathsf{LC} = \bigcap_n \mathrm{Log}(C_n)$, where $C_n$ is the chain with $n$ worlds.
3. Each $C_n$ validates $\mathsf{LC}$, so $\mathsf{LC} \subseteq \mathrm{Log}(C_n)$ for every $n$.
4. $\neg p \vee \neg \neg p$ is in $\mathsf{LC}$, since it is valid on every linear frame; see [[the derivation|~65d4934d]] for a syntactic proof.

### Converse

Item 1 is an equivalence.
The converse of item 2 is soundness, the implication from (a) to (b), and it is part of the theorem.

### Generalizations

1. The algebraic form: $\mathsf{LC}$ is the logic of the [[Gödel chain on the unit interval|~e93ef865]], and of any infinite linearly ordered Heyting algebra.
2. Strong completeness also holds: $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{LC}} \varphi$ iff every world of a locally linear model that forces $\Gamma$ forces $\varphi$. This follows from steps 10 to 17 of the proof.
3. By SEP, $\mathsf{G}_n = \mathsf{LC} + (A_1 \to A_2) \vee \dots \vee (A_1 \wedge \dots \wedge A_n \to A_{n+1})$ is the logic of the linear frames with at most $n$ worlds.

## History

Gödel used the finite linear matrices in 1932 to show that $\mathsf{IPC}$ has no finite characteristic matrix.
Dummett proved in 1959 that $\mathsf{IPC}$ plus prelinearity is complete for the infinite linear matrix.
The Kripke form followed after Kripke's semantics of 1965.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem says that Gödel-Dummett logic is exactly the logic of linear time.
Every theorem holds whenever knowledge grows along a single line.
Every non-theorem fails on some finite line of states.
So one can decide the logic by checking short lines.

### L2 human

1. To show $\mathsf{LC} \nvdash p \vee \neg p$, take the chain $a < b$ with $p$ true only at $b$. The root forces neither $p$ nor $\neg p$.
2. To show $\mathsf{LC} \vdash ((p \to q) \to q) \to p \vee q$, check it on the chains with at most $7$ worlds, since it has $6$ subformulas.
3. Hence the second formula axiomatizes a logic contained in $\mathsf{LC}$.

The typical use is to decide membership in $\mathsf{LC}$ and to compare it with other logics.
A trap: the canonical model of $\mathsf{LC}$ is not linear, only locally linear, so the proof passes to a generated submodel.
