## Proof

This proof works over a field $F$; the [[proof by uniqueness|86J5B0YFNK]] covers every commutative ring.

1. Let $E$ be an [[elementary matrix|0M79HRJCRR]] and $M$ any $n \times n$ matrix. By [[part 1 of the row operation lemma|F7F6AA3XQ7]], $EM$ arises from $M$ by one row operation.
2. By the [[effect of row operations|09YB3WQG38]], $\det(EM) = \kappa \det M$, with $\kappa = -1$ for a swap, $c$ for a scaling by $c$, and $1$ for an addition.
3. With $M = I_n$, step 2 and $\det I_n = 1$ give $\det E = \kappa$. So $\det(EM) = \det E \det M$ for every $M$.
4. By the [[existence of the reduced row echelon form|FCM29NQ6DN]], there are elementary matrices with $E_k \cdots E_1 A = R$, and $R$ in [[reduced row echelon form|0WF5AQYTW6]].
5. By part 2 of the row operation lemma, each $E_r$ has an elementary inverse. So $A = E_1^{-1} \cdots E_k^{-1} R = G_1 \cdots G_k R$ with elementary $G_r$.
6. Applying step 3 $k$ times gives $\det(AB) = \det G_1 \cdots \det G_k \det(RB)$, and with $B = I_n$, $\det A = \det G_1 \cdots \det G_k \det R$.
7. Case $R = I_n$. Then $\det(RB) = \det B$ and $\det R = 1$, so step 6 gives $\det(AB) = \det A \det B$.
8. Case $R \neq I_n$. A square matrix in reduced row echelon form with $n$ pivots has its pivots on the diagonal, so it equals $I_n$.
9. So $R$ has fewer than $n$ pivots, and its last row is zero.
10. Row $n$ of $RB$ is (row $n$ of $R$) times $B$, which is zero.
11. By the rule for a [[zero row|0K4VQB7HWR]], $\det R = 0$ and $\det(RB) = 0$.
12. By step 6, $\det A = 0$ and $\det(AB) = 0$. So $\det(AB) = 0 = \det A \det B$.

## Idea

For one elementary matrix the product rule is the effect of one row operation.
Row reduction writes $A$ as a product of elementary matrices times its reduced echelon form $R$.
Either $R = I_n$, or $R$ has a zero row, and then both sides are $0$.

## Variants

This proof does not use the [[invertibility criterion|FBSAPTP1PR]]; it shows directly that $\det A = 0$ when $R \neq I_n$.
Over an integral domain one can pass to the field of fractions, and over any commutative ring one can use the generic matrix argument: the identity holds in $\mathbb{Q}(x_{ij}, y_{ij})$, hence in $\mathbb{Z}[x_{ij}, y_{ij}]$, hence everywhere.
