## Example

Let $B$ be the [[power set Boolean algebra|004J7ZEFAT]] of $W = \set{1, 2}$, with $0 = \emptyset$, $1 = W$, $\neg a = W \setminus a$.
A valuation $v$ sends each variable to a subset of $W$, and $v(\varphi \to \psi) = (W \setminus v(\varphi)) \cup v(\psi)$.
We check that [[Peirce's law|GMSZ75D5WE]] $((p \to q) \to p) \to p$ has the value $W$ under all $16$ valuations, and that $p \to q$ does not.

1. For $w \in W$, put $v_w(\varphi) = 1$ if $w \in v(\varphi)$, and $0$ otherwise.
2. Each $v_w$ is a valuation in $\mathbf{2}$, because $w \in W \setminus a$ iff $w \notin a$, and $w \in a \cup b$ iff $w \in a$ or $w \in b$.
3. So $w \in v(\varphi)$ iff the truth table of $\varphi$ gives $1$ at the row $(v_w(p), v_w(q))$.
4. Peirce's law is a tautology: if $p$ is $1$, the final implication is true; if $p$ is $0$, then $p \to q = 1$ and $(p \to q) \to p = 0$.
5. So $v_w$ of Peirce's law is $1$ for $w = 1, 2$, and $v$ of it is $W$, for each of the $4 \times 4 = 16$ valuations.
6. For $p \to q$, take $v(p) = \set{1}$ and $v(q) = \emptyset$.
7. Then $v(p \to q) = \set{2} \cup \emptyset = \set{2} \ne W$.

So in $B$ Peirce's law is valid and $p \to q$ is not, as in $\mathbf{2}$.

## Remarks

1. Steps 1 to 3 show that $B \cong \mathbf{2} \times \mathbf{2}$, and a valuation in $B$ is a pair of rows of the truth table.
2. For $W$ with $n$ points, a valuation in the power set is an $n$-tuple of rows, and the same argument works.
3. In a [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]] that is not Boolean, Peirce's law can fail; see [[the three-element chain|~6ca00a91]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of two possible worlds, world 1 and world 2.
A sentence gets the set of worlds where it is true.
"$p$ is true in world 1 only, $q$ is true nowhere" is one choice.
Then "if $p$ then $q$" is true in world 2 only, since in world 1 $p$ holds and $q$ fails.
Peirce's law comes out true in both worlds for every choice.

### L2 human

1. Take $v(p) = \set{2}$ and $v(q) = \set{1}$.
2. $v(p \to q) = \set{1} \cup \set{1} = \set{1}$.
3. $v((p \to q) \to p) = \set{2} \cup \set{2} = \set{2}$.
4. $v(((p \to q) \to p) \to p) = \set{1} \cup \set{2} = W$.

The typical use: a power set algebra shows several truth-table rows at once.
The trap: the value $\set{2}$ is not "half true"; it means "true in world 2".
