## Example

Fix a characteristic $p$, zero or prime, and an uncountable cardinal $\bar{\kappa}$.
Let $\mathbb{K}$ be an [[algebraically closed field|0PF6TM8TWT]] of characteristic $p$ and [[transcendence degree|D7TR0YWR9H]] $\bar{\kappa}$ over its prime field.

1. $\mathbb{K}$ is saturated of size $\bar{\kappa}$, by [[saturated algebraically closed fields|~b53b2a60]], so it is $\bar{\kappa}$-saturated.
2. By [[uniqueness of saturated models|~a2cbf168]] with both models equal to $\mathbb{K}$, every partial elementary map of size less than $\bar{\kappa}$ extends to an automorphism, so $\mathbb{K}$ is strongly $\bar{\kappa}$-homogeneous.
3. So $\mathbb{K}$ is a [[monster model|~84f3dbc1]] of $\mathrm{ACF}_p$ for the small sets, the sets of size less than $\bar{\kappa}$.
4. Every algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$ and size less than $\bar{\kappa}$ embeds elementarily into $\mathbb{K}$, since $\mathbb{K}$ is [[universal|~40536de0]].
5. For a small set $A$ with generated subfield $k$, two elements transcendental over $k$ have the same type over $A$. Step 2 gives an automorphism over $A$ that maps one to the other, as [[types as orbits|~3d38bf9d]] predicts.
6. In this monster model, $\operatorname{acl}(A)$ is the [[algebraic closure|0X6AB5P3G1]] of $k$, and $\operatorname{dcl}(A)$ is the [[perfect closure|PWNZQVQ175]] of $k$; see [[algebraic and definable closure|~07008141]].

## Remarks

Unlike most theories, $\mathrm{ACF}_p$ has a saturated model in every infinite cardinal, so no set-theoretic hypothesis is needed for its monster model.
The reason is that types over $A$ are fixed by the ideal of polynomials over $k$ that they contain, and there are at most $\abs{A} + \aleph_0$ such types.
This is the model-theoretic form of a universal domain in algebraic geometry, and $\C$ plays this role for countable parameter sets.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take an algebraically closed field with as many independent transcendental elements as it has elements.
Every smaller algebraically closed field of the same characteristic sits inside it, and any two configurations with the same algebraic description are moved onto each other by a field automorphism.
This field can serve as the single universe for all computations with algebraically closed fields.

### L2 human

We see step 5 at work for $A = \set{\sqrt{2}}$ in characteristic $0$.

1. The field generated by $A$ is $k = \Q(\sqrt{2})$.
2. Two elements $s, t \in \mathbb{K}$ transcendental over $k$ satisfy the same polynomial equations over $k$, namely none.
3. By quantifier elimination they have the same type over $A$, and an automorphism of $\mathbb{K}$ that fixes $\sqrt{2}$ maps $s$ to $t$.

The typical use is to replace an arbitrary field extension by a subfield of $\mathbb{K}$.
The trap is to take $\bar{\kappa}$ countable: then the small sets are finite, and no algebraically closed field is small.
