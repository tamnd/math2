## Example

Consider the system over $\R$
$$2y + x = 5, \qquad 3z - y = 1, \qquad x = 4z.$$
We find its [[coefficient matrix|0P7A2MJD24]] and solve it.

1. Order the unknowns as $x, y, z$ and move all of them to the left: $x + 2y + 0z = 5$, $0x - y + 3z = 1$, $x + 0y - 4z = 0$.
2. So $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 3 \\ 1 & 0 & -4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $b = (5, 1, 0)^T$.
3. The [[augmented matrix|0YCDZ7N8VD]] is $\left[\begin{array}{rrr|r} 1 & 2 & 0 & 5 \\ 0 & -1 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & -4 & 0 \end{array}\right]$.
4. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_1$ gives $(0, -2, -4 \mid -5)$.
5. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 2R_2$ gives $(0, 0, -10 \mid -7)$.
6. So $z = \tfrac{7}{10}$, then $-y + 3z = 1$ gives $y = 3z - 1 = \tfrac{11}{10}$, then $x = 5 - 2y = \tfrac{14}{5}$.
7. Check: $2 \cdot \tfrac{11}{10} + \tfrac{14}{5} = \tfrac{22}{10} + \tfrac{28}{10} = 5$.
8. Check: $3 \cdot \tfrac{7}{10} - \tfrac{11}{10} = \tfrac{10}{10} = 1$, and $4 \cdot \tfrac{7}{10} = \tfrac{14}{5}$.

## Remarks

The zeros in $A$ come from the unknowns that are missing in each equation.
If we forgot them and wrote the rows as $(2, 1)$, $(3, -1)$, $(1, -4)$, the columns would mix different unknowns and the answer would be wrong.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Before making the table, write every equation in the same pattern: first $x$, then $y$, then $z$.
When an unknown is missing, write it with the number $0$ in front.
Then each column of the table belongs to one unknown.

### L2 human

The typical use is to bring a word problem into a standard form.

1. Fix the order of the unknowns.
2. Move all unknowns to the left side.
3. Fill in zeros for the missing unknowns.

A trap: in $x = 4z$ the coefficient of $z$ is $-4$, not $4$, after moving $4z$ to the left.
