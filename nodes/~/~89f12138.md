## Example

Let $\mathfrak{N}$ be the [[standard model|WP27CABPA4]] of the language of arithmetic, with domain $\N$.
We check that $\mathfrak{N}$ satisfies each axiom of [[Robinson arithmetic|TT8MXRTVBM]], so $\mathfrak{N}$ is a [[model|MVCH6N9SNW]] of $\mathsf{Q}$.
Let $m, n \in \N$ be arbitrary values of the variables.

1. $\mathsf{Q}1$: $S^{\mathfrak{N}}(m) = m + 1 \ge 1$, so $m + 1 \ne 0$.
2. $\mathsf{Q}2$: if $m + 1 = n + 1$, then $m = n$, by cancelling $1$.
3. $\mathsf{Q}3$: if $m \ne 0$, then $m \ge 1$, and $k = m - 1 \in \N$ satisfies $m = k + 1$.
4. $\mathsf{Q}4$: $m + 0 = m$.
5. $\mathsf{Q}5$: $m + (n + 1) = (m + n) + 1$, by associativity of addition.
6. $\mathsf{Q}6$: $m \cdot 0 = 0$.
7. $\mathsf{Q}7$: $m \cdot (n + 1) = m n + m$, by distributivity.
8. $\mathsf{Q}8$: if $m < n$, then $v = n - m - 1 \in \N$ satisfies $v + (m + 1) = n$. Conversely, $v + (m + 1) = n$ gives $n \ge m + 1 > m$.

So every axiom of $\mathsf{Q}$ holds in $\mathfrak{N}$ for all values of its variables.

## Remarks

The example gives the [[consistency|VM8TQ70CVJ]] of $\mathsf{Q}$: a theory with a model proves no contradiction, by soundness.
It also gives an upper bound on what $\mathsf{Q}$ proves: every theorem of $\mathsf{Q}$ is true in $\mathfrak{N}$.
Steps 5 and 7 use laws of $\N$, associativity and distributivity, that $\mathsf{Q}$ itself does not prove.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The eight rules of Robinson arithmetic are true for the ordinary numbers $0, 1, 2, \ldots$.
For example, rule 7 says $m \cdot (n + 1) = m \cdot n + m$, and this is how multiplication works.
So the ordinary numbers are one world in which all the rules hold.

### L2 human

We test $\mathsf{Q}7$ and $\mathsf{Q}8$ on specific numbers.

1. For $\mathsf{Q}7$ with $m = 3$ and $n = 4$: $3 \cdot 5 = 15$ and $3 \cdot 4 + 3 = 15$.
2. For $\mathsf{Q}8$ with $m = 2$ and $n = 7$: $2 < 7$, and $v = 4$ gives $4 + 3 = 7$.
3. For $\mathsf{Q}8$ with $m = 7$ and $n = 2$: $7 < 2$ is false, and $v + 8 = 2$ has no solution in $\N$.

The typical use of this example is to prove that $\mathsf{Q}$ is consistent.
A trap is to conclude that $\mathsf{Q}$ proves everything true in $\mathfrak{N}$; [[this model|~1e15aa46]] shows that it does not.
