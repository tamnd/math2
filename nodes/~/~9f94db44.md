## Statement

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $I$ be an interval.
Let $p_0, \dots, p_{n-1}$ and $f$ be [[continuous functions|0P27W9RW0G]] from $I$ to $\mathbb{R}$.
Let $a \in I$ and let $b_0, \dots, b_{n-1}$ be real constants.
Consider the [[linear differential equation of order n|0QQRP17Z7S]]
$$y^{(n)} + p_{n-1}(x)\, y^{(n-1)} + \dots + p_1(x)\, y' + p_0(x)\, y = f(x).$$
Then the [[initial value problem|075VBY4K4X]] given by this equation together with the conditions $y(a) = b_0, y'(a) = b_1, \dots, y^{(n-1)}(a) = b_{n-1}$ has one and only one [[solution|07VX34QXAV]] $y$ defined on all of $I$.

## Remarks

The solution lives on the whole interval $I$, not only near $a$. This is the difference from the general local theorem for nonlinear equations.
For $n = 1$ the conditions reduce to one value $y(a) = b_0$.
The case $f = 0$ is what makes the solution space of the homogeneous equation have dimension $n$.
