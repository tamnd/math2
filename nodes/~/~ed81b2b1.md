## Summary

A divisibility rule is a short method to decide whether a fixed integer $d$ divides an integer $n$.
It uses the digits of $n$, and it does not need a full division.
Most rules for base $10$ come from the [[congruences|~e79e2431]] of the powers $10^k$ modulo $d$.
For example, $10 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, so $n$ and the sum of its digits have the same remainder modulo $3$.

## Remarks

For example, $2$ divides $n$ if and only if the last digit of $n$ is even. Also $5$ divides $n$ if and only if the last digit is $0$ or $5$.
See [[divisibility by 3|~4be63842]], [[divisibility by 9|~cd6db790]] and [[divisibility by 11|~f0f01669]].
[[Casting out nines|~02d85b9b]] uses the rule for $9$ to check computations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

You can see at once that $1{,}234{,}570$ is a multiple of $10$, because it ends with $0$.
Divisibility rules give similar quick tests for other divisors, for example $3$, $4$, $9$ and $11$.
