## Example

Let $G = (\mathbb{C}^\times, \cdot)$ be the nonzero [[complex numbers|09FTAET5Z4]] under multiplication, with identity $1$.
We compute the [[cyclic subgroup|08ABT85339]] $\langle i \rangle = \{i^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.

1. The nonnegative powers: $i^0 = 1$, $i^1 = i$, $i^2 = -1$, $i^3 = i^2 \cdot i = -i$, and $i^4 = (i^2)^2 = (-1)^2 = 1$.
2. The [[order|0WE2NHZJ7B]] of $i$ is $4$: the powers $i^1, i^2, i^3$ are not $1$, and $i^4 = 1$.
3. Let $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, and divide by $4$: $k = 4q + r$ with $0 \leq r \leq 3$. By the [[laws of exponents|00DRK90Z6K]], $i^k = (i^4)^q i^r = i^r$.
4. So each power of $i$ is one of $i^0, i^1, i^2, i^3$. For example, $i^{-1} = i^3 = -i$, and indeed $i \cdot (-i) = -i^2 = 1$.
5. The four numbers $1, i, -1, -i$ are distinct. So $\langle i \rangle = \{1, i, -1, -i\}$, a subgroup with $4$ elements.

The list $g^0, g^1, \ldots, g^{n-1}$ with $n = 4$ is the whole subgroup, as in [[the theorem on the order of $\langle g \rangle$|031M8HX7DA]].

## Remarks

The subgroup $\langle i \rangle$ is the group of fourth roots of unity: it is the set of solutions of $z^4 = 1$ in $\mathbb{C}$.
In the plane, multiplication by $i$ is the rotation by a quarter turn about $0$, so $\langle i \rangle$ is the group of rotations of a square about its center.
Other elements give the same subgroup: $\langle -i \rangle = \langle i \rangle$, since $-i = i^{-1}$.
The element $-1 = i^2$ gives a smaller subgroup, $\langle -1 \rangle = \{1, -1\}$, of order $2$.
The element $2 \in \mathbb{C}^\times$ has infinite order, and $\langle 2 \rangle$ is infinite.
It is the same set as $\langle 2 \rangle$ in [[the group $\mathbb{Q}^\times$|~5a3994de]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Multiply $1$ by $i$ again and again.
We get $i$, then $-1$, then $-i$, then $1$ again.
After four steps we are back at the start.
So the powers of $i$ go around a cycle of four numbers, and these four numbers form a small group.

### L2 human

We check the method on a large exponent.

1. Take $k = 2026$. Then $2026 = 4 \cdot 506 + 2$, so $i^{2026} = i^2 = -1$.
2. Take $k = -7$. Then $-7 = 4 \cdot (-2) + 1$, so $i^{-7} = i^1 = i$.

The typical use: to compute a power $g^k$ of an element of finite order $n$, reduce $k$ modulo $n$.
A trap: the remainder must be in $\{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, also for negative $k$.
The remainder of $-7$ is $1$, not $-3$.
