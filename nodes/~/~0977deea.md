## Example

Let $T := \mathsf{PA} + \neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, where $\neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ is $\exists y \, \mathrm{Prf}_{\mathsf{PA}}(y, \ulcorner 0 = S0 \urcorner)$.
We show that $T$ is [[consistent|VM8TQ70CVJ]] but not [[ω-consistent|~08020cc1]].

1. $\mathsf{PA}$ is consistent, because $\N$ is a model of it.
2. By the [[second incompleteness theorem|~c79a35b5]], $\mathsf{PA} \nvdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$.
3. If $T$ were inconsistent, then $\mathsf{PA} + \neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA}) \vdash 0 = S0$, and by the deduction theorem $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA}) \to 0 = S0$. With $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \neg (0 = S0)$ this gives $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$, against step 2. So $T$ is consistent.
4. Let $\varphi(y) := \mathrm{Prf}_{\mathsf{PA}}(y, \ulcorner 0 = S0 \urcorner)$. Then $T \vdash \exists y \, \varphi(y)$, since this is an axiom of $T$.
5. Let $n \in \N$. By step 1, $n$ does not code a proof of $0 = S0$ in $\mathsf{PA}$.
6. By representability in $\mathsf{Q}$, $\mathsf{Q} \vdash \neg \varphi(\underline{n})$, so $T \vdash \neg \varphi(\underline{n})$.
7. Steps 4 and 6 show that $\varphi$ witnesses $\omega$-inconsistency.

## Remarks

The theory is also not $1$-consistent, because $\varphi$ is a $\Sigma_1$ formula and $T$ proves the false $\Sigma_1$ sentence $\exists y \, \varphi(y)$.
Its models are all [[non-standard|1QWN5CV4CG]], and the witness of $\exists y \, \varphi(y)$ is a non-standard number.
The same argument works for every consistent computably enumerable $T_0 \supseteq \mathsf{PA}$ in place of $\mathsf{PA}$: $T_0 + \neg \mathrm{Con}(T_0)$ is consistent and $\omega$-inconsistent.
Also $T \vdash \neg \mathrm{Con}(T)$, since $\mathsf{PA}$ proves that every proof in $\mathsf{PA}$ is a proof in $T$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This theory takes the rules of arithmetic and adds the false claim "arithmetic proves $0 = 1$".
It has no contradiction that can be written down, because arithmetic cannot rule out this claim.
But it says "a proof of $0 = 1$ exists", while it can check that $0$, $1$, $2$ and every other number is not such a proof.
That is exactly $\omega$-inconsistency.

### L2 human

1. Add $\neg \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{PA})$ to $\mathsf{PA}$; the result is consistent by the second incompleteness theorem.
2. Take the formula "$y$ codes a proof of $0 = S0$".
3. The theory proves that such a $y$ exists, and refutes it for each numeral.

The typical use is to show that consistency does not imply $\omega$-consistency.
A trap: $T$ is consistent, so it has a model, and in that model the witness is not a numeral.
