## Statement

Let $c \in \mathbb{C}$, let $r > 0$, and let $f$ be [[holomorphic|~1a187203]] on the punctured disc $0 < |z - c| < r$.
Assume that $c$ is an [[essential singularity|~75d899a4]] of $f$.
Then for each $\delta$ with $0 < \delta < r$, the image $f(\{z : 0 < |z - c| < \delta\})$ is dense in $\mathbb{C}$.
Thus for each $w \in \mathbb{C}$ there is a sequence $z_n \to c$ with $z_n \neq c$ and $f(z_n) \to w$.

## Remarks

Proof idea: if the image of a punctured disc misses a disc $|w - w_0| < \varepsilon$, then $g(z) = 1/(f(z) - w_0)$ is bounded near $c$. By [[Riemann's theorem|~fb06e3b3]], $g$ is holomorphic at $c$, and then $c$ is removable or a pole of $f$.
[[Picard's great theorem|~fa54fd9e]] is a much stronger statement.
In the Russian literature, the theorem has the name of Sokhotski.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Near an essential singularity, the function comes as close as you like to each complex number.
For example, $e^{1/z}$ comes close to $5$, to $-i$ and to $0$ on each small disc around $0$.
