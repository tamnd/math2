## Statement

The number $\sqrt{\pi}$ is not a [[constructible number|~012d5a00]].
So the circle cannot be [[squared|~6381a53b]] with straightedge and compass.

## Remarks

The proof: every constructible number is [[algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$|~f4af4673]]. If $\sqrt{\pi}$ were constructible, then $\pi = (\sqrt{\pi})^2$ would be constructible and so algebraic.
This contradicts the [[transcendence of $\pi$|~6cd0b453]].

## History

The impossibility was proved in 1882, as a consequence of Lindemann's proof that $\pi$ is transcendental.
It had been known for decades that the transcendence of $\pi$ would imply the impossibility.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A square with the area of the unit circle has side $\sqrt{\pi}$.
Constructible numbers are algebraic, and $\pi$ is not, so this side cannot be constructed.
