## Statement

Let $L$ be a signature and let $T$ be a [[complete|655K8Y7VS8]] $L$-theory with infinite models.

1. For every $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]] $\mathcal{M}$ and every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, $\mathcal{M}$ has a $\kappa^+$-[[saturated|RMTZ5J64FX]] elementary extension of size at most $(\abs{M} + 2^{\abs{L}})^{\kappa}$.
2. If $\kappa$ is an uncountable cardinal with $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$ and $\abs{L} < \kappa$, then $T$ has a saturated model of size $\kappa$.
3. If $\lambda \ge \abs{L}$ is a [[regular cardinal|13ZV2F8JAR]] and $T$ is [[stable|T7JW5QY6KR]] in $\lambda$, then $T$ has a saturated model of size $\lambda$.
4. The theory of [[dense linear orders|2W4S2H4VYA]] without endpoints has a saturated model of size $\aleph_1$ if and only if the [[continuum hypothesis|0MN2MER9WN]] holds.
5. The theory of dense linear orders without endpoints has no saturated model of singular cardinality.

Here $\kappa^{<\kappa}$ is the cardinality of the set of all functions from a cardinal $\mu < \kappa$ to $\kappa$, for all such $\mu$ together.
The theory $T$ is *stable in $\lambda$* when $\abs{S_1(A)} \le \lambda$ for every set $A$ of size at most $\lambda$ in a model of $T$.

## Remarks

The proofs are in [[proof of the existence of saturated models|~05e77581]].

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The condition $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$ in part 2: by part 4, if the continuum hypothesis fails, then no dense order of size $\aleph_1$ is saturated, and part 5 shows that none exists in singular sizes.
- The bound $\abs{L} < \kappa$: if $L$ has $\kappa$ unary predicates $P_\alpha$ and $T$ says that they are independent, then $S_1(\emptyset)$ has $2^\kappa > \kappa$ points, and a model of size $\kappa$ cannot realize all of them.
- Uncountable $\kappa$: for the theory of countably many independent unary predicates, $S_1(\emptyset)$ has $2^{\aleph_0}$ points, so there is no countable saturated model, although $\aleph_0^{<\aleph_0} = \aleph_0$.
- Regularity in part 3: the proof builds a chain of length $\lambda$ and needs each small set to lie in one link of the chain.

### Consequences

- Under the generalized continuum hypothesis, $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$ for every regular uncountable $\kappa$, so every complete $T$ with infinite models has a saturated model in each regular $\kappa > \abs{L}$.
- If $\kappa$ is an [[inaccessible cardinal|NW4DREQ5DQ]] with $\abs{L} < \kappa$, then $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$, and $T$ has a saturated model of size $\kappa$; this is one way to build a [[monster model|~84f3dbc1]].
- An $\omega$-stable countable theory is stable in every infinite $\lambda$, so it has a saturated model in every infinite regular cardinal, with no assumption on cardinal arithmetic.

### Converse

The condition $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$ is not necessary for a given theory.
An algebraically closed field of transcendence degree $\kappa$ is saturated for every infinite $\kappa$, singular or not; see [[saturated algebraically closed fields|~b53b2a60]].

### Generalizations

Part 1 can also be proved with ultrapowers: a countably incomplete ultrapower in a countable language is $\aleph_1$-saturated; see [[saturation of ultraproducts|DYXQP8WS63]].
When no saturated model of size $\kappa$ exists, a [[special model|~82e5b5a7]] of size $\kappa$ can take its place for strong limit $\kappa$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every structure can be enlarged until it realizes all types over small sets, by adding elements in many rounds.
A model that is saturated in its own size is harder to get, because each new element creates new parameter sets.
The construction closes off only when the size of the model is large compared to the number of its small subsets.
This is a condition on cardinal arithmetic, such as the continuum hypothesis for size $\aleph_1$.

### L2 human

We check the hypotheses of part 2 for $\kappa = \aleph_1$ under the continuum hypothesis and a countable $L$.

1. The cardinal $\aleph_1$ is uncountable, and $\abs{L} \le \aleph_0 < \aleph_1$.
2. The sets of size less than $\aleph_1$ are the countable sets, so $\aleph_1^{<\aleph_1} = \aleph_1^{\aleph_0}$.
3. Since $2^{\aleph_0} \le \aleph_1^{\aleph_0} \le (2^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}$, this equals $2^{\aleph_0}$, which is $\aleph_1$ by the continuum hypothesis.

So every complete countable theory with infinite models has a saturated model of size $\aleph_1$ under the continuum hypothesis.
The trap is to apply part 2 with $\kappa = \aleph_0$: countable saturated models exist only for theories with countably many types.

### L3 human

The existence of saturated models is the place where model theory meets cardinal arithmetic.
Stable theories avoid this dependence, which is one reason why stability theory works in saturated models freely.
For unstable theories, special models and monster models take the place of saturated models.
The bound $\kappa^{<\kappa}$ reappears in the study of [[Keisler's order|TD5D2G5PYP]] and of [[the stability spectrum|5D9DC20519]].
