## Example

Consider a structure that gives each program an arbitrary relation, as in a multimodal Kripke model with one box for each program term.
Let $W = \{0, 1\}$, let $R_a = \emptyset$, let the relation for $a^*$ be $S = W \times W$, and let $V(p) = \{0\}$.
We check the condition of [[propositional dynamic logic|VJ78A0Q22R]] for the star, and two axioms.

1. The reflexive [[transitive closure|05J9NWX07X]] of $R_a = \emptyset$ is the identity $\{(0, 0), (1, 1)\}$.
2. $S = \{(0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1)\}$ contains $(0, 1)$, which is not in the identity. So $S$ is not the relation of $a^*$ in a model of $\mathsf{PDL}$.
3. $[a^*] p$ at $0$, read with $S$: the $S$-successors of $0$ are $0$ and $1$, and $1 \notin V(p)$. So $[a^*] p$ is false at $0$.
4. $[a] \psi$ holds at every world for every $\psi$, because $R_a$ is empty.
5. The unfolding axiom $[a^*] p \leftrightarrow p \wedge [a][a^*] p$ at $0$: the right side is "$p$ at $0$" and "$[a][a^*] p$ at $0$". Both are true, by $0 \in V(p)$ and step 4.
6. By steps 3 and 5, the left side is false and the right side is true at $0$, so the unfolding axiom fails.
7. The induction axiom $[a^*](p \to [a] p) \to (p \to [a^*] p)$ at $0$: by step 4, $p \to [a] p$ holds at every world, so the first antecedent holds at $0$.
8. $p$ holds at $0$, and $[a^*] p$ fails at $0$ by step 3. So the induction axiom fails at $0$.

## Remarks

The example shows that the star axioms carry the meaning of the star: they rule out the extra pair $(0, 1)$.
If we replace $S$ by the identity, both axioms hold at both worlds, since then $[a^*] p$ is equivalent to $p$.
A relation that is smaller than the closure also fails: if $S$ is empty, then $[a^*] p \to p$ fails at $1$, since $[a^*] p$ holds at $1$ while $p$ does not.
If the two axioms hold for every valuation on the frame, the unfolding axiom forces $S$ to contain the closure of $R_a$.
The induction axiom, with $p$ true exactly at the worlds reachable from $w$ by $R_{a}$-chains, forces every $S$-successor of $w$ to be such a world.
So the two axioms together force $S$ to be the reflexive transitive closure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The star of a program means "repeat it zero or more times".
Here the basic step $a$ can never be taken, so "repeat $a$" can only mean "do nothing".
The structure pretends that "repeat $a$" can move from world $0$ to world $1$.
The axioms of $\mathsf{PDL}$ notice this mistake, because they describe the star exactly.

### L2 human

We check the claim of the remarks for the empty relation $S = \emptyset$.

1. $[a^*] p$ at $1$, read with $S = \emptyset$: there are no successors, so it is true.
2. $p$ is false at $1$, since $V(p) = \{0\}$.
3. So $[a^*] p \to p$ fails at $1$.
4. This formula is valid in $\mathsf{PDL}$, by the unfolding axiom and propositional logic.

The typical use of such structures is to test which axioms pin down a program operation.
A trap: the axioms are about all formulas, so a structure can satisfy an instance for one $p$ and fail it for another.
