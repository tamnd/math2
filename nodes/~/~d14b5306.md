## Example

We prove two sentences in [[Peano arithmetic|~9b54df02]] by single instances of the [[induction schema|~59894b26]].
Neither sentence needs a lemma, and the first one is not provable in [[Robinson arithmetic|TT8MXRTVBM]].

Claim 1: $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \forall x\,(S x \ne x)$.

1. Use induction on $x$ for the open formula $\varphi(x) := S x \ne x$.
2. Base: $S 0 \ne 0$ is an instance of $\mathsf{Q}1$.
3. Step: assume $S x \ne x$, and suppose $S S x = S x$.
4. By $\mathsf{Q}2$, applied to $S x$ and $x$, we get $S x = x$, against the assumption. So $S S x \ne S x$.
5. The induction axiom for $\varphi$ gives $\forall x\,(S x \ne x)$.

Claim 2: the axiom $\mathsf{Q}3$, $\forall x\,(x \ne 0 \to \exists y\,(x = S y))$, follows from induction without using $\mathsf{Q}3$.

6. Use induction on $x$ for $\psi(x) := x = 0 \lor \exists y\,(x = S y)$.
7. Base: $0 = 0$, so $\psi(0)$ holds.
8. Step: $S x = S x$, so $\exists y\,(S x = S y)$ holds with $y = x$. So $\psi(S x)$ holds, even without the induction hypothesis.
9. The induction axiom gives $\forall x\, \psi(x)$, which is equivalent to $\mathsf{Q}3$.

The step in Claim 2 used no axiom at all, only logic, and the base used only $0 = 0$.

## Remarks

The example separates $\mathsf{PA}$ from $\mathsf{Q}$.
In [[this model of Q|~1e15aa46]], $S(a) = a$, so $\mathsf{Q} \nvdash \forall x\,(S x \ne x)$.
Claim 2 shows that $\mathsf{Q}3$ is redundant in $\mathsf{PA}$, but it is not redundant in $\mathsf{Q}$.
For a witness, take the polynomials in one variable $X$ with coefficients in $\N$, with $S(p) = p + 1$ and the usual sum and product.
This structure satisfies $\mathsf{Q}1$, $\mathsf{Q}2$ and $\mathsf{Q}4$ to $\mathsf{Q}7$, but $X \ne 0$ and $X$ is not of the form $p + 1$.
Claim 1 uses induction for an open formula, and Claim 2 for a $\Sigma_1$ formula, so both hold already in the fragment $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$ of $\mathsf{PA}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"No number is its own next number" sounds obvious, but the rules of Robinson arithmetic do not prove it.
With induction it is quick: $0$ is not its own next number, and if $x$ is not, then neither is $x + 1$, since equal successors have equal predecessors.
The second claim says that every number other than $0$ has a predecessor, and induction proves it in one line.

### L2 human

We check the step of Claim 1 at $x = \overline{2}$, in the standard model.

1. The hypothesis $S\,\overline{2} \ne \overline{2}$ says $3 \ne 2$.
2. Suppose $S S\,\overline{2} = S\,\overline{2}$, that is, $4 = 3$.
3. $\mathsf{Q}2$ would give $3 = 2$, which contradicts the hypothesis.
4. So $4 \ne 3$, which is $\varphi(\overline{3})$.

The typical use of Claim 2 is to shorten the axiom list of $\mathsf{PA}$.
A trap is to think that Claim 2 proves $\mathsf{Q}3$ in $\mathsf{Q}$: it uses an induction axiom, which $\mathsf{Q}$ lacks.
