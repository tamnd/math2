## Example

In the [[topological semantics|~088e3e8d]] on the [[real line|08J2M1JYAG]], $[\![\neg \varphi]\!] = \mathrm{Int}(\R \setminus [\![\varphi]\!])$ and $[\![\varphi \to \psi]\!] = \mathrm{Int}((\R \setminus [\![\varphi]\!]) \cup [\![\psi]\!])$.
Take $V(p) = (0, \infty)$ and $V(q) = (-\infty, 0)$.

1. $[\![p \wedge q]\!] = \emptyset$, so $[\![\neg (p \wedge q)]\!] = \mathrm{Int}(\R) = \R$.
2. $[\![\neg p]\!] = \mathrm{Int}((-\infty, 0]) = (-\infty, 0)$ and $[\![\neg q]\!] = \mathrm{Int}([0, \infty)) = (0, \infty)$.
3. So $[\![\neg p \vee \neg q]\!] = \R \setminus \set{0}$.
4. $[\![\neg (p \wedge q) \to \neg p \vee \neg q]\!] = \mathrm{Int}(\emptyset \cup (\R \setminus \set{0})) = \R \setminus \set{0}$.
5. So the De Morgan law fails at $0$, and the open sets of $\R$ do not form a model of $\mathsf{KC}$.
6. Here $V(q) = [\![\neg p]\!]$. So this is the instance $q := \neg p$ from step 8 of the proof of [[the theorem|CX032ACTMZ]], which turns the De Morgan law into weak excluded middle.
7. So the failure of weak excluded middle at $0$, computed in [[the real-line countermodels|~d2c827d5]], is the same failure.

## Remarks

The open sets of a topological space form a [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]], and this one has logic exactly $\mathsf{IPC}$, by [[Tarski's theorem|~088e3e8d]].
A space whose open algebra satisfies weak excluded middle is one in which the closures of disjoint open sets are disjoint; these are the extremally disconnected spaces.
The real line is connected, so it is far from this.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The positive and the negative numbers are two disjoint regions.
"Not both" holds everywhere, but at $0$ neither "not positive" nor "not negative" holds with a margin of error.
So the De Morgan law fails exactly at the boundary point.

### L2 human

1. Choose two disjoint open sets whose closures meet.
2. Their meet is empty, so the premise of the law is the whole space.
3. The conclusion misses the common boundary point.

The typical use is to show that a natural Heyting algebra does not validate $\mathsf{KC}$.
A trap: the De Morgan law with $\vee$ and $\wedge$ exchanged, $\neg (p \vee q) \to \neg p \wedge \neg q$, holds in every Heyting algebra.
