## Proof

1. By the [[existence of the reduced row echelon form|FCM29NQ6DN]], a sequence of row operations turns $A$ into a matrix $R$ in [[reduced row echelon form|0WF5AQYTW6]].
2. Each operation is a swap, a scaling by some $c \neq 0$, or an addition of a multiple of a row.
3. By the [[effect of row operations|09YB3WQG38]], each operation multiplies $\det$ by $-1$, by $c \neq 0$, or by $1$.
4. So $\det R = \kappa \det A$ with $\kappa \neq 0$. Hence $\det A \neq 0$ if and only if $\det R \neq 0$.
5. If $R = I_n$, then $\det R = 1 \neq 0$ by the [[determinant of the identity|0Z4BWARC38]].
6. If $R \neq I_n$, then $R$ has fewer than $n$ pivots, since $n$ pivots in a square reduced matrix give $I_n$. So the last row of $R$ is zero.
7. Then $\det R = 0$ by the rule for a [[zero row|0K4VQB7HWR]].
8. By steps 4 to 7, $\det A \neq 0$ if and only if $R = I_n$.
9. By the [[RREF criterion|1ZK55FS2BF]], $R = I_n$ if and only if $A$ is invertible. This proves that conditions 1 and 2 are equivalent.
10. By the same criterion, $A$ is invertible if and only if $Ax = 0$ has only the solution $x = 0$.
11. $Ax = \sum_j x_j c_j$ for the columns $c_j$ of $A$. So $Ax = 0$ has only $x = 0$ exactly when the columns are independent. So conditions 1 and 3 are equivalent.
12. The rows of $A$ are the columns of $A^{T}$, and $\det A^{T} = \det A$ by the [[transpose rule|0ZZQ2E1MTE]].
13. By steps 9 to 11 for $A^{T}$, the rows of $A$ are independent if and only if $\det A \neq 0$. So conditions 2 and 4 are equivalent.

## Idea

Row operations multiply the determinant by nonzero numbers, so they do not change whether it is zero.
At the end of row reduction we have $I_n$, with determinant $1$, or a matrix with a zero row, with determinant $0$.
The first case happens exactly for invertible matrices.

## Variants

A second proof uses the [[adjugate|~827aa2ab]] and works over every commutative ring.
The row reduction proof is also the practical test: it decides invertibility in about $n^3$ operations.
