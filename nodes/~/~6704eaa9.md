## Proof

**Axiomatic and Leibniz.**

1. By part 1 of the [[existence and uniqueness theorem|~1e90e396]], $L$ is multilinear and alternating in the rows, with $L(I_n) = 1$.
2. By the [[definition|0C3CFHDN6R]], $\det$ is the unique function with these properties. So $\det A = L(A)$. This is also the [[Leibniz formula|016PE4VZ8Q]].

**Axiomatic and Laplace recursion.** We prove $D_n(A) = \det A$ by induction on $n$.

3. For $n = 1$, the axioms force $\det(a) = a \det(1) = a$, by linearity in the single row. So $D_1 = \det$.
4. Let $n \ge 2$, and assume $D_{n-1}(B) = \det B$ for all $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ matrices $B$.
5. By the [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]] along row $1$, $\det A = \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{1+j} a_{1j} \det A_{1j}$.
6. By the induction hypothesis, $\det A_{1j} = D_{n-1}(A_{1j})$ for each $j$.
7. So $\det A = \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{1+j} a_{1j} D_{n-1}(A_{1j}) = D_n(A)$.

**Rows and columns.**

8. Let $\det'$ be multilinear and alternating in the columns, with $\det'(I_n) = 1$. Define $E(B) = \det'(B^{\mathsf{T}})$.
9. The rows of $B$ are the columns of the [[transpose|0W809K6AW0]] $B^{\mathsf{T}}$. So $E$ is multilinear and alternating in the rows of $B$, and $E(I_n) = \det'(I_n) = 1$.
10. By the definition of $\det$, $E = \det$. So $\det'(A) = E(A^{\mathsf{T}}) = \det A^{\mathsf{T}}$.
11. By the [[determinant of the transpose|0ZZQ2E1MTE]], $\det A^{\mathsf{T}} = \det A$. So $\det'(A) = \det A$.
12. Existence of $\det'$ also follows: the function $A \mapsto \det A^{\mathsf{T}}$ has the column properties by step 9 read backwards.

## Idea

The axioms single out one function, and the Leibniz sum has the axiomatic properties, so the two agree.
The Laplace expansion along the first row is a theorem about $\det$, and induction on $n$ turns it into the recursive definition.
Transposition exchanges rows and columns, so the column axioms describe $A \mapsto \det A^{\mathsf{T}}$, which is $\det A$.

## Variants

One can start from the recursion and prove by induction that $D_n$ is multilinear, alternating and normalized; then the uniqueness theorem gives $D_n = \det$.
This is longer, because the alternating property of $D_n$ for equal rows that include row $1$ needs a separate argument.
