## Example

Let $P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $Q = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ over $\R$.

1. The columns of $P$ are $e_1$ and $0$, so its [[column space|03AJKXH1AV]] is the span of $e_1$.
2. The columns of $Q$ are $0$ and $e_1$, so its column space is also the span of $e_1$.
3. $Px = 0$ means $x_1 = 0$, so the [[null space|0PHDYNR24D]] of $P$ is the span of $e_2$.
4. $Qx = 0$ means $x_2 = 0$, so the null space of $Q$ is the span of $e_1$.
5. The null spaces differ, so by [[the theorem on row equivalence|~e3949f1c]], $P$ and $Q$ are not [[row equivalent|095DDGZF16]].

## Remarks

Both matrices are already in reduced row echelon form, and they are different, which gives a second proof.
The row spaces also differ: they are the spans of $(1, 0)$ and of $(0, 1)$.
Column operations, not row operations, turn $P$ into $Q$: swap the two columns.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Row moves keep the equations' solutions.
The equation $x_1 = 0$ and the equation $x_2 = 0$ have different solutions.
So no row moves can turn one into the other, even though both tables use the same column vector.

### L2 human

1. To show that two matrices are not row equivalent, find one invariant that differs.
2. Good invariants are the null space, the row space, the rank and the RREF.
3. The column space is not an invariant of row operations, so it cannot prove row equivalence.

A trap: equal column spaces do not give row equivalence, and row equivalence does not give equal column spaces.
