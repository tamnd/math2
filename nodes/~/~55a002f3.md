## Summary

Bounded arithmetic is a family of first-order theories of the natural numbers whose induction is restricted to bounded formulas and whose provably total functions grow at most as fast as $2^{p(\log x)}$ for a polynomial $p$.
The main theories are $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$, $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \Omega_1$, and Buss's theories $S^i_2$ and $T^i_2$ for $i \ge 0$.
Buss's theories use the language $L_2 = \set{0, S, +, \cdot, \floor{x/2}, \abs{x}, \#, \le}$, where $\abs{x}$ is the length of $x$ in binary and $x \# y = 2^{\abs{x} \cdot \abs{y}}$.
$T^i_2$ has induction for $\Sigma^b_i$ formulas, and $S^i_2$ has the weaker polynomial induction for $\Sigma^b_i$ formulas.
The point of these theories is that their provably total functions, defined in the right way, are exactly the functions of a complexity class: for $S^1_2$ the class is the polynomial-time functions.
So bounded arithmetic is a formal model of feasible reasoning, in the way that [[Peano arithmetic|HACXBCM7SS]] is a formal model of ordinary number theory.

## Motivation

A proof in [[Peano arithmetic|HACXBCM7SS]] that a number exists can give a witness that is too large to write down.
Parikh asked which proofs give witnesses of feasible size, and showed that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ gives polynomial bounds, by [[Parikh's theorem|~5b376b4b]].
Polynomial bounds in the input $x$ are exponential in the length $\abs{x}$, so $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ still does not match polynomial time.
Buss chose a language and induction schemes so that a proof of $\forall x \, \exists y \, A(x, y)$ with $A$ in $\Sigma^b_1$ gives a polynomial-time algorithm that finds $y$.
The theories then turn questions of [[complexity theory|ESPYW94XGR]] into questions about provability, and back.
For example, if the hierarchy of theories $S^i_2$ collapses, then the [[polynomial hierarchy|TDQ7AXGM6P]] collapses.

## Notation

- $\abs{x} = \ceil{\log_2(x + 1)}$ is the binary length of $x$, so $\abs{0} = 0$ and $\abs{5} = 3$.
- $x \# y = 2^{\abs{x} \cdot \abs{y}}$ is the smash function. The terms of $L_2$ express $2^{q(\abs{x})}$ for every polynomial $q$ with coefficients in $\N$.
- A bounded quantifier is $\forall x \le t$ or $\exists x \le t$, with $x$ not in $t$. A sharply bounded quantifier is $\forall x \le \abs{t}$ or $\exists x \le \abs{t}$.
- $\Sigma^b_0 = \Pi^b_0$ is the class of formulas with only sharply bounded quantifiers. $\Sigma^b_i$ and $\Pi^b_i$ count the alternations of bounded quantifiers and ignore the sharply bounded ones.
- $\Sigma^b_i$-$\mathsf{IND}$ is the scheme $A(0) \land \forall x \, (A(x) \to A(x + 1)) \to \forall x \, A(x)$ for $A \in \Sigma^b_i$.
- $\Sigma^b_i$-$\mathsf{PIND}$ is the scheme $A(0) \land \forall x \, (A(\floor{x/2}) \to A(x)) \to \forall x \, A(x)$ for $A \in \Sigma^b_i$.
- $\Sigma^b_i$-$\mathsf{LIND}$ is the scheme $A(0) \land \forall x \, (A(x) \to A(x + 1)) \to \forall x \, A(\abs{x})$ for $A \in \Sigma^b_i$.
- $\mathsf{BASIC}$ is a fixed finite set of universal closures of open formulas. It fixes the meaning of the symbols of $L_2$, and it proves the axioms of [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]].
- $T^i_2 = \mathsf{BASIC} + \Sigma^b_i$-$\mathsf{IND}$ and $S^i_2 = \mathsf{BASIC} + \Sigma^b_i$-$\mathsf{PIND}$. $S_2$ is the union of the $S^i_2$, and $T_2$ is the union of the $T^i_2$.
- $\Omega_1$ is the axiom "$x^{\abs{x}}$ exists for every $x$", written with a $\Delta_0$ formula for the graph of $x \mapsto x^{\abs{x}}$.

The literature has a second, two-sorted tradition, due to Cook and Nguyen, with numbers and finite sets (binary strings) as the two sorts.
The table matches the main theories.

| One-sorted (Buss) | Two-sorted (Cook-Nguyen) | Relation | Definable functions |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ | $V^0$ | $V^0$ is conservative over $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ | $\mathrm{FAC}^0$ for $V^0$ |
| $S^i_2$, $i \ge 1$ | $V^i$ | RSUV isomorphism | $\mathrm{FP}^{\Sigma^p_{i-1}}$ |
| $T^i_2$, $i \ge 1$ | $TV^i$ | RSUV isomorphism | $\mathrm{FP}^{\Sigma^p_i}$ for $\Sigma^b_{i+1}$ definitions |
| $\mathsf{PV}$ | $\mathsf{VPV}$ | $\mathsf{VPV}$ is built on $\mathsf{PV}$ | $\mathrm{FP}$ |

The RSUV isomorphism reads a number of the one-sorted theory as a binary string of the two-sorted theory.
Under it, the binary length $\abs{x}$ becomes a number of the two-sorted theory.

## Remarks

### Examples

- $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$, the [[fragment of Peano arithmetic|T70PYM1F36]] with induction for $\Delta_0$ formulas. Its induction is on bounded formulas, and by Parikh's theorem it does not prove that $2^x$ is total.
- $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \Omega_1$. The axiom $\Omega_1$ gives the growth rate of the smash function and no more; [[this example|~e2b71b10]] computes the two functions for small numbers.
- $S^1_2$. Its $\Sigma^b_1$-definable functions are the polynomial-time functions, by [[Buss's witnessing theorem|~ad7bce8a]]. [[This example|~a28a9bef]] gives a proof in $S^1_2$ that uses polynomial induction.
- $T^1_2$. It proves $S^1_2$. By a theorem of Buss and Krajíček, its provable $\forall \exists \Sigma^b_1$ sentences are witnessed by a polynomial local search problem followed by a polynomial-time function.
- [[Cook's theory PV|~98af8889]], an equational theory with a function symbol for each polynomial-time algorithm. Its first-order version $\mathsf{PV}_1$ has the same $\forall \Sigma^b_1$ theorems as $S^1_2$.

### Non-examples

- [[Elementary function arithmetic|2JD80TE553]] proves that $2^x$ is total, so its provably total functions are not bounded by $2^{p(\abs{x})}$; [[this non-example|~cd1224e1]] checks it.
- $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$ and Peano arithmetic have induction for unbounded formulas. They prove $2^x$ total, by induction on $\exists y \, \mathrm{Exp}(x, y)$.
- [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]] has bounded growth, but it has no induction at all. It is a base theory, and it does not prove simple laws such as $x + y = y + x$.

### Basic properties

- For $i \ge 1$, $T^i_2 \vdash S^i_2$ and $S^{i+1}_2 \vdash T^i_2$, so the theories form one chain $S^1_2 \subseteq T^1_2 \subseteq S^2_2 \subseteq T^2_2 \subseteq \dots$. In particular $S_2 = T_2$.
- $T_2$ and $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \Omega_1$ prove the same theorems, modulo the translation between the two languages.
- For $k \ge 1$, a set of natural numbers is defined by a $\Sigma^b_k$ formula exactly when it is in the class $\Sigma^p_k$ of the polynomial hierarchy. This is due to Stockmeyer and Wrathall, and Kent and Hodgson proved the full version.
- [[Parikh's theorem|~5b376b4b]] holds for $S^i_2$ and $T^i_2$: a provable $\forall \exists$ sentence with bounded matrix has a bound given by a term of $L_2$.
- The $\Sigma^b_i$-definable functions of $S^i_2$ are the class $\mathrm{FP}^{\Sigma^p_{i-1}}$, by [[Buss's witnessing theorem|~ad7bce8a]].
- For $i \ge 1$, $S^{i+1}_2$ is conservative over $T^i_2$ for $\forall \exists \Sigma^b_{i+1}$ sentences, by a theorem of Buss.
- Whether the chain of the first property is strict is an [[open problem|~4ddef1ef]]. By the [[Krajíček-Pudlák-Takeuti theorem|~2d7d49f7]], a collapse of the chain implies a collapse of the polynomial hierarchy.

### History

Bennett showed in his 1962 thesis that the $\Delta_0$-definable relations are exactly Smullyan's rudimentary relations.
Parikh studied $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ in 1971 and proved his bounding theorem.
Cook defined the equational theory $\mathsf{PV}$ in 1975.
Buss introduced $S^i_2$ and $T^i_2$ in his 1985 Princeton thesis, published as the book *Bounded Arithmetic* in 1986.
The smash function comes from Nelson's *Predicative Arithmetic*, and Wilkie and Paris introduced $\Omega_1$ independently.

### Common errors

- To read $\abs{x}$ as an absolute value. In bounded arithmetic $\abs{x}$ is the binary length of $x$.
- To apply Buss's theorem to $\forall x \, \exists y \, A$ with $A$ in $\Pi^b_1$. The theorem needs $A \in \Sigma^b_1$, and a $\Delta^b_1$ formula counts only when $S^1_2$ proves it equivalent to a $\Sigma^b_1$ formula.
- To think that the theories prove only bounded sentences. They prove sentences such as $\forall x \, \exists y \, (y = x \cdot x)$; Parikh's theorem only says that the witnesses are bounded.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary proofs about numbers sometimes say "there is a number with this property" without any way to find it fast.
The basic theory of bounded arithmetic is a set of rules for proofs in which every promised number can be found by a fast computer program.
Fast means that the time grows like a polynomial in the number of digits of the input.
For example, a proof that every number has a square gives the program "multiply the number by itself".
For a number with $10$ binary digits this program takes about $100$ simple steps.
A proof that every number $x$ has the power $2^x$ is not allowed, since $2^x$ has $x + 1$ binary digits, far too many to write down.

### L2 human

We compute some values and classify some formulas.

1. $\abs{5} = 3$, since $5$ is $101$ in binary. $\abs{8} = 4$, since $8$ is $1000$.
2. $5 \# 8 = 2^{3 \cdot 4} = 4096$. In general $x \# y$ has $\abs{x} \cdot \abs{y} + 1$ binary digits, so it has polynomial length.
3. The formula $\exists y \le x \, (y \cdot y = x)$ says that $x$ is a perfect square. It has one bounded $\exists$ and no other quantifier, so it is $\Sigma^b_1$.
4. The formula $\exists i \le \abs{x} \, (i \cdot i = x)$ uses only a sharply bounded quantifier, so it is $\Sigma^b_0$.
5. The formula $\forall y \le x \, \exists z \le x \, (y + z = x)$ has a bounded $\forall$ in front of a bounded $\exists$, so it is $\Pi^b_2$.

The typical use is to show that an algorithm or a combinatorial principle has a feasible proof: one writes the proof in $S^1_2$, and Buss's theorem gives the algorithm.
A trap is to count the sharply bounded quantifiers: they do not raise the level, because a polynomial-time machine can try all $\abs{t} + 1$ values.

### L3 human

The theories $S^i_2$ and $T^i_2$ match the levels of the polynomial hierarchy in the way that $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n$ matches the levels of the [[arithmetical hierarchy|7CVJCQKY41]].
The analogue of the [[provably total functions|Z742YPHPNS]] of $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$, the primitive recursive functions, is the class of polynomial-time functions for $S^1_2$.

The two-sorted theories of Cook and Nguyen extend this picture below polynomial time: $V^0$ matches $\mathrm{AC}^0$, and other theories match logarithmic space and $\mathrm{NC}^1$.
Bounded arithmetic is also the uniform side of [[proof complexity|1Q046J0AW2]]: proofs in a bounded theory translate into short proofs in a propositional proof system.
The [[Paris-Wilkie translation|~c9c38d6d]] does this for bounded-depth Frege systems, and Cook's translation does it for extended Frege systems.
Open problems such as the separation of the hierarchy of theories are closely tied to $\mathrm{P} \ne \mathrm{NP}$ and to lower bounds for proof systems.
