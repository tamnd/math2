## Statement

For every real $x$,
$$\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1.$$

## Remarks

Let $f(x) = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x$. By the [[derivatives|~c1876d3c]] [[of sine and cosine|~c6b3fb1a]], $f'(x) = 2 \sin x \cos x - 2 \cos x \sin x = 0$. So $f$ is [[constant|~5b96a02f]], and $f(0) = 1$.
So $|\sin x| \le 1$ and $|\cos x| \le 1$.
Geometrically, the point $(\cos x, \sin x)$ lies on the unit circle. Mathlib states it as `Real.sin_sq_add_cos_sq`.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a right triangle with hypotenuse $1$, the legs are $\cos x$ and $\sin x$.
The Pythagorean theorem then says that their squares add up to $1$.
