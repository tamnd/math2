## Definition

Let $f \colon A \to B$ be a [[function|~d103e2ff]] and let $S$ be a [[subset|~a9b9ba7e]] of $A$.
The image of $S$ under $f$ is the set
$$f(S) = \{f(s) \mid s \in S\} = \{b \in B \mid \exists s \in S, \, f(s) = b\}.$$

## Remarks

Another notation is $f[S]$. It avoids a confusion with $f(a)$ for an element $a$.
The image $f(A)$ of the whole domain is the [[range|~54a6afbb]] of $f$.
Example: for $f(x) = x^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$, $f([-1, 2]) = [0, 4]$.
The image of a union is the union of the images, but the image of an intersection can be smaller. See [[image of an intersection|~c1dd5afd]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

To compute $f(S)$, apply $f$ to each element of $S$ and collect the results.
For $f(x) = x + 1$ and $S = \{1, 2, 3\}$, the image is $\{2, 3, 4\}$.
