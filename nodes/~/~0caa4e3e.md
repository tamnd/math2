## Proof

We prove (1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2), then (1) $\Rightarrow$ (3) $\Rightarrow$ (2), then (1) $\Rightarrow$ (4) $\Rightarrow$ (3).
Together these give the equivalence of all four conditions.

### Conditions 1 and 2

1. (1) $\Rightarrow$ (2). The formula $\exists y \, \varphi$ is a formula, so (1) gives a quantifier-free equivalent.
2. (2) $\Rightarrow$ (1). We show by [[induction on formulas|12MQ2NJ29A]] that every formula $\chi$ has a quantifier-free equivalent modulo $T$.
3. Atomic formulas are quantifier-free. If $\chi_1$ and $\chi_2$ have equivalents $\psi_1, \psi_2$, then $\neg \psi_1$ and $\psi_1 \wedge \psi_2$ are equivalents of $\neg \chi_1$ and $\chi_1 \wedge \chi_2$.
4. Let $\chi = \exists y \, \chi_1$, and let $\psi_1(\bar x, y)$ be a quantifier-free equivalent of $\chi_1$. Then $\chi$ is equivalent to $\exists y \, \psi_1$.
5. Bring $\psi_1$ to [[disjunctive normal form|0GVDFAY4KW]] $\alpha_1 \vee \dots \vee \alpha_k$, with each $\alpha_i$ a conjunction of literals.
6. The quantifier $\exists y$ distributes over $\vee$, so $\chi$ is equivalent to $\exists y \, \alpha_1 \vee \dots \vee \exists y \, \alpha_k$.
7. By (2), each $\exists y \, \alpha_i$ has a quantifier-free equivalent $\beta_i(\bar x)$. So $\beta_1 \vee \dots \vee \beta_k$ is an equivalent of $\chi$.
8. The connectives $\vee$, $\to$, $\forall$ are expressed by $\neg$, $\wedge$, $\exists$, so the induction is complete.

### Condition 3 from condition 1

1. Let $M, N$, $A$, $\bar a$ and $\varphi$ be as in (3), with $M \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$.
2. By (1), $\exists y \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$ is equivalent modulo $T$ to a quantifier-free $\psi(\bar x)$. So $M \models \psi(\bar a)$.
3. Quantifier-free formulas have the same truth value in a structure and in a substructure, by [[this result|C9494XYEMN]]. So $A \models \psi(\bar a)$, and then $N \models \psi(\bar a)$.
4. Since $N \models T$, we get $N \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$.

### Condition 2 from condition 3: the set of consequences

Assume (3), and fix $\theta(\bar x) = \exists y \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$ with $\varphi$ a conjunction of literals.

1. Add new constant symbols $\bar c = (c_1, \dots, c_n)$ to $L$. Let $\Gamma(\bar c)$ be the set of quantifier-free $\psi(\bar c)$ with $T \models \forall \bar x \, (\theta(\bar x) \to \psi(\bar x))$.
2. We claim that $T \cup \Gamma(\bar c) \models \theta(\bar c)$. Suppose not, and let $M \models T \cup \Gamma(\bar c) \cup \set{\neg \theta(\bar c)}$.
3. Let $A$ be the substructure of $M$ generated by the values of $\bar c$. Every element of $A$ is the value of a term $t(\bar c)$, by [[this description|XV8GX9C2AR]]; the constant symbol of $L$ makes $A$ nonempty when $n = 0$.
4. Let $\Delta$ be the set of literals without variables in $L(\bar c)$ that are true in $M$.
5. We show that $T \cup \Delta \cup \set{\theta(\bar c)}$ is consistent. Otherwise, by [[compactness|1NR3HAWG9D]], there are $\delta_1, \dots, \delta_k \in \Delta$ with $T \models \theta(\bar c) \to \neg(\delta_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \delta_k)$.
6. The constants $\bar c$ do not occur in $T$, so $T \models \forall \bar x \, (\theta(\bar x) \to \neg \delta(\bar x))$, where $\delta = \delta_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \delta_k$.
7. So $\neg \delta(\bar c) \in \Gamma$, and $M \models \neg \delta(\bar c)$. This contradicts $M \models \delta(\bar c)$, which holds because each $\delta_i$ is in $\Delta$.

### Condition 2 from condition 3: the conclusion

1. Let $N \models T \cup \Delta \cup \set{\theta(\bar c)}$. Define $h(t^M(\bar c)) = t^N(\bar c)$ for each term $t$.
2. The map $h$ is well defined and injective: $t_1(\bar c) = t_2(\bar c)$ or its negation is in $\Delta$. In the same way $h$ preserves the functions and relations, so $h \colon A \to N$ is an embedding.
3. We identify $A$ with its image. Then $A$ is a common substructure of $M$ and $N$, and $N \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar c, y)$.
4. By (3), with the roles of $M$ and $N$ exchanged, $M \models \theta(\bar c)$. This contradicts the choice of $M$, and the claim of the previous part holds.
5. By compactness, $T \models \psi_1(\bar c) \wedge \dots \wedge \psi_m(\bar c) \to \theta(\bar c)$ for some $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_m \in \Gamma$. Let $\psi = \psi_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \psi_m$, or $\psi = \top$ if $m = 0$.
6. The constants $\bar c$ are new, so $T \models \forall \bar x \, (\psi \to \theta)$. Each $\psi_i$ is in $\Gamma$, so $T \models \forall \bar x \, (\theta \to \psi)$.
7. So $\psi$ is a quantifier-free equivalent of $\theta$, which proves (2).

### Condition 4 from condition 1

1. Let $M, N, A, f, b$ be as in (4), and let $\bar a$ be a finite tuple that generates $A$.
2. Let $p(y)$ be the set of quantifier-free $\varphi(f(\bar a), y)$ with $M \models \varphi(\bar a, b)$.
3. For finitely many formulas of $p$, their conjunction $\varphi$ satisfies $M \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$. By (1) this is equivalent to a quantifier-free $\psi(\bar a)$, true in $M$.
4. Then $A \models \psi(\bar a)$ by [[this result|C9494XYEMN]], so $N \models \psi(f(\bar a))$, since $f$ is an embedding. So $N \models \exists y \, \varphi(f(\bar a), y)$.
5. So $p$ is finitely satisfiable in $N$ with finitely many parameters. Since $N$ is $\omega$-[[saturated|RMTZ5J64FX]], some $c \in N$ realizes $p$.
6. Define $g(t^M(\bar a, b)) = t^N(f(\bar a), c)$ for each term $t$. As in the previous part, $g$ is a well-defined embedding, because $(\bar a, b)$ and $(f(\bar a), c)$ satisfy the same literals.
7. For $t$ without $y$ we get $g(t(\bar a)) = f(t(\bar a))$, so $g$ extends $f$.

### Condition 3 from condition 4

1. Let $M, N, A, \bar a, \varphi$ be as in (3), and let $b \in M$ with $M \models \varphi(\bar a, b)$.
2. By [[the existence of saturated models|GSQ833HAZT]], $N$ has an $\aleph_1$-saturated [[elementary extension|8WZF9SBP39]] $N'$, and $N'$ is $\omega$-saturated.
3. Let $A_0 = \langle \bar a \rangle$, which is finitely generated, and let $f \colon A_0 \to N'$ be the inclusion. By (4) there is an embedding $g \colon \langle A_0, b \rangle \to N'$ that extends $f$.
4. The formula $\varphi$ is quantifier-free, so it holds of $(\bar a, b)$ in $\langle A_0, b \rangle$, and of $(\bar a, g(b))$ in $N'$.
5. So $N' \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$. Since $N \preceq N'$, also $N \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$.

## Idea

The equivalence of (1) and (2) is an induction on formulas, where only one existential quantifier at a time needs work.
For (3) $\Rightarrow$ (2), compactness collects the quantifier-free consequences of $\theta$; if they did not imply $\theta$, two models with a common substructure would disagree on $\theta$.
The extension test is the embedding test with the witness found by saturation instead of by a formula.

## Variants

When $T$ is complete, one can replace the models $M$, $N$ by one monster model, and (4) becomes: every partial isomorphism between finitely generated substructures extends by one element.
A second route to (3) $\Rightarrow$ (1) uses the [[diagram lemmas|EP5E317TNW]] with a constant for every element of $A$, instead of the terms in $\bar c$.
For a single formula, the same compactness argument shows that $\theta$ has a quantifier-free equivalent when its truth only depends on the substructure generated by its parameters.
