## Statement

Consider a planar autonomous system $x' = f(x,y)$, $y' = g(x,y)$ with $f$ and $g$ continuously differentiable.
Let $R$ be a closed and bounded subset of the plane.
Assume that the system has no [[equilibrium points|0G4X36AE0X]] in $R$.
Let $(x(t), y(t))$ be a solution that is defined for all $t \ge t_0$ and stays in $R$ for all these times.

Then one of two things happens.
Either the solution is a periodic function of $t$, so its trajectory is a [[periodic orbit|01DSNP0B4R]].
Or the solution spirals towards a periodic orbit that lies in $R$, in the way that a trajectory approaches a [[limit cycle|0EERDDPSVT]].

## Remarks

The theorem holds only in the plane.
It is used by finding a bounded region that trajectories enter and never leave, and that has no equilibrium point.
Such a region must contain a periodic orbit.
