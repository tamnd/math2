## Proof

We recover $\ip{Tu}{w}$ from values of the form $\ip{Tx}{x}$, as in the [[polarization identity|05FKHRT0B4]].
The [[inner product|0VGSYBWHV2]] is linear in the first slot and conjugate linear in the second slot.

1. Let $u, w \in V$. Expanding with linearity in both slots gives $\ip{T(u + w)}{u + w} = \ip{Tu}{u} + \ip{Tu}{w} + \ip{Tw}{u} + \ip{Tw}{w}$.
2. In the same way, $\ip{T(u - w)}{u - w} = \ip{Tu}{u} - \ip{Tu}{w} - \ip{Tw}{u} + \ip{Tw}{w}$.
3. Subtracting step 2 from step 1 gives $\ip{T(u + w)}{u + w} - \ip{T(u - w)}{u - w} = 2\ip{Tu}{w} + 2\ip{Tw}{u}$.
4. Part 1. Let $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{C}$. Replace $w$ by $iw$ in step 3. Since $\ip{Tu}{iw} = \overline{i}\, \ip{Tu}{w} = -i \ip{Tu}{w}$ and $\ip{T(iw)}{u} = i \ip{Tw}{u}$, we get:
$$\ip{T(u + iw)}{u + iw} - \ip{T(u - iw)}{u - iw} = -2i \ip{Tu}{w} + 2i \ip{Tw}{u}.$$
5. Multiply step 4 by $i$, and add the result to step 3. Since $i \cdot (-2i) = 2$ and $i \cdot 2i = -2$, the terms with $\ip{Tw}{u}$ cancel, and we get:
$$4 \ip{Tu}{w} = \ip{T(u + w)}{u + w} - \ip{T(u - w)}{u - w} + i \ip{T(u + iw)}{u + iw} - i \ip{T(u - iw)}{u - iw}.$$
6. Assume $\ip{Tx}{x} = 0$ for all $x \in V$. Then each of the four terms on the right of step 5 is $0$. So $\ip{Tu}{w} = 0$ for all $u, w \in V$.
7. Take $w = Tu$. Then $\ip{Tu}{Tu} = 0$, so $Tu = 0$ by positivity. As $u$ is arbitrary, $T = 0$. This proves part 1.
8. Part 2, first direction. Let $T$ be [[self-adjoint|0MB8HQW495]]. For $v \in V$, conjugate symmetry and $T = T^*$ give $\overline{\ip{Tv}{v}} = \ip{v}{Tv} = \ip{Tv}{v}$. So $\ip{Tv}{v}$ is real.
9. Part 2, second direction. Let $\ip{Tv}{v}$ be real for all $v$. Then $\ip{Tv}{v} = \overline{\ip{Tv}{v}} = \ip{v}{Tv}$.
10. By the [[definition of the adjoint|0ST8QF9T3T]] and conjugate symmetry, $\ip{v}{Tv} = \overline{\ip{Tv}{v}} = \overline{\ip{v}{T^* v}} = \ip{T^* v}{v}$.
11. Steps 9 and 10 give $\ip{(T - T^*) v}{v} = 0$ for all $v \in V$. By part 1, $T - T^* = 0$. So $T$ is self-adjoint.
12. Part 3. Let $T$ be self-adjoint over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, with $\ip{Tx}{x} = 0$ for all $x$. Then the left side of step 3 is $0$.
13. Self-adjointness and conjugate symmetry give $\ip{Tw}{u} = \ip{w}{Tu} = \overline{\ip{Tu}{w}}$. So step 3 becomes $0 = 2\ip{Tu}{w} + 2\overline{\ip{Tu}{w}} = 4 \operatorname{Re} \ip{Tu}{w}$.
14. Take $w = Tu$. Then $0 = 4 \operatorname{Re} \ip{Tu}{Tu} = 4 \norm{Tu}^2$, because $\ip{Tu}{Tu}$ is real. So $Tu = 0$ for every $u$, and $T = 0$.

## Idea

The values $\ip{Tx}{x}$ on the four vectors $u \pm w$ and $u \pm iw$ determine the number $\ip{Tu}{w}$ over $\mathbb{C}$.
Over $\mathbb{R}$ only $u \pm w$ are available, and they determine only $\ip{Tu}{w} + \ip{Tw}{u}$.
Self-adjointness makes this sum equal to $2 \operatorname{Re} \ip{Tu}{w}$, and the choice $w = Tu$ finishes the proof.

## Variants

- For part 1 there is a proof with a basis. Over $\mathbb{C}$, choose an orthonormal basis and write $a_{jk} = \ip{T e_k}{e_j}$. The values at $e_j$, at $e_j + e_k$ and at $e_j + i e_k$ force first $a_{jj} = 0$, then $a_{jk} + a_{kj} = 0$, and then $a_{jk} - a_{kj} = 0$.
- For part 3 there is a proof with the [[real spectral theorem|06J4PXRG22]]: in an orthonormal eigenbasis, $\ip{T e_j}{e_j} = \lambda_j = 0$ for every $j$. This proof uses much more.
