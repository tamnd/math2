## Summary

Convex and discrete geometry is the study of convex sets, polytopes and finite or discrete configurations of points and bodies in Euclidean space.
A set is convex if it contains the whole segment between any two of its points.
The subject has applications in optimization, in the geometry of numbers, in crystallography and in computational geometry.
Minkowski developed the theory of convex bodies around 1900, and Hales gave a computer-assisted proof of Kepler's conjecture on the densest packing of balls in 1998.
This topic collects the convex and discrete geometry pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Euclidean geometry, Diophantine approximation and geometry of numbers, algebraic combinatorics and designs, matroids and finite geometries have their own topics.
