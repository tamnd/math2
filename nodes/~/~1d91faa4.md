## Statement

For a real $n \times n$ matrix $M$ with $I + M$ [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]], let $C(M) = (I - M)(I + M)^{-1}$.

1. If $A$ is [[skew-symmetric|03RNVFDEAN]], then $I + A$ and $I - A$ are invertible.
2. If $A$ is skew-symmetric, then $Q = C(A)$ is an [[orthogonal matrix|0QRBVFJ50X]] with $\det Q = 1$, and $-1$ is not an [[eigenvalue|0ZND7NQ970]] of $Q$.
3. If $Q$ is orthogonal and $-1$ is not an eigenvalue of $Q$, then $A = C(Q)$ is skew-symmetric.
4. $C(C(M)) = M$ for each $M$ with $I + M$ invertible.
5. So $C$ is a bijection from the real skew-symmetric $n \times n$ matrices onto the set of $Q \in O(n)$ without the eigenvalue $-1$. This set lies in $SO(n)$, and $C$ is its own inverse.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Part 3 needs that $-1$ is not an eigenvalue. For $Q = -I_2$, the matrix $I + Q = 0$ is not invertible, and $C(Q)$ is not defined.
- Part 2 needs a skew-symmetric $A$. For $A = \operatorname{diag}(1, 0)$, $I + A$ is invertible, but $C(A) = \operatorname{diag}(0, 1)$ is not orthogonal.
- Part 1 needs real entries. Over $\C$, the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & i \\ -i & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ satisfies $A^T = -A$, and $I + A$ is singular, because $\det(I + A) = 1 + i^2 = 0$.

### Consequences

- Each $Q \in SO(n)$ without the eigenvalue $-1$ is given by $\frac{n(n - 1)}{2}$ free real numbers, the entries of $A$ above the diagonal.
- If $A$ has rational entries, then $C(A)$ is an orthogonal matrix with rational entries. For $n = 2$, the first columns are all rational points of the unit circle except $(-1, 0)$, and they give the Pythagorean triples.
- For $n = 2$ and $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -t \\ t & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $C(A) = R(-\theta)$ with $t = \tan\frac{\theta}{2}$. So the image is $SO(2)$ without the half turn $R(\pi)$.
- No element of $O(n)$ with determinant $-1$ is in the image, because part 2 gives $\det C(A) = 1$.

### Converse

The image of $C$ is not all of $SO(n)$ for $n \ge 2$.
The rotation $\operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, 1, \dots, 1)$ is in $SO(n)$, and it has the eigenvalue $-1$, see [[this example|~dd657815]].
Each element $Q$ of $SO(n)$ is a product of two elements of the image.
By the [[normal form|~ff85d703]], $Q$ has a square root $Q_0$ in $SO(n)$ with all angles halved, and $Q_0$ does not have the eigenvalue $-1$.

### Generalizations

- For a complex skew-Hermitian matrix $A$, with $A^* = -A$, the matrix $C(A)$ is unitary without the eigenvalue $-1$. The map is again a bijection onto these unitary matrices.
- For a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form with matrix $J$, the same formula sends the matrices with $A^TJ = -JA$ to the matrices with $Q^TJQ = J$, when $I + A$ is invertible.

## History

Cayley published the transform in 1846.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem says that the recipe $(I - A)(I + A)^{-1}$ turns each skew-symmetric table into a rotation.
The same recipe, used on the rotation, gives the table back.
Only the rotations that send some direction to its opposite are missed.

### L2 human

Worked example in both directions: $A = \frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, see [[this example|~14d870ff]].

1. $\det(I + A) = \frac{4}{3}$, and $(I + A)^{-1} = \frac{1}{6}\begin{pmatrix} 5 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & 5 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. $C(A) = \frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = Q$, the [[rotation by sixty degrees|~129123c7]] about the line through $(1, 1, 1)$.
3. Back: $I - Q = \frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $I + Q = \frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 5 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & 5 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. $(I - Q)(I + Q)^{-1} = A$, by part 4.

The typical use: produce rotations with rational entries, or describe the rotations near $I$ by free parameters.
A trap: the formula for $C(Q)$ fails for the half turns, which have the eigenvalue $-1$.

### L3 human

The map $C$ is a birational isomorphism between the vector space $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ and the group variety $SO(n)$.
Its derivative at $0$ is $-2$ times the identity, so it is a chart of $SO(n)$ near $I$ with the tangent space $\mathfrak{so}(n)$.
The algebra of parts 2 to 4 works over any field of characteristic not $2$, whenever $I + A$ is invertible.
