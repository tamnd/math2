## Example

Let $R_x(\theta)$, $R_y(\theta)$ and $R_z(\theta)$ be the basic [[rotation matrices|~8cba9603]] of $\R^3$:
$$R_x(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & c & -s \\ 0 & s & c \end{pmatrix}, \quad R_y(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} c & 0 & s \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -s & 0 & c \end{pmatrix}, \quad R_z(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} c & -s & 0 \\ s & c & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},$$
with $c = \cos\theta$ and $s = \sin\theta$.
We compare the two products of $R_z(\pi/2)$ and $R_x(\pi/2)$.

1. With $c = 0$ and $s = 1$: $R_z(\pi/2) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $R_x(\pi/2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. $A = R_z(\pi/2)R_x(\pi/2) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. $B = R_x(\pi/2)R_z(\pi/2) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. So $A \neq B$, and the basic rotations do not commute.
5. $A(1, 1, 1) = (1, 1, 1)$, so the axis of $A$ is the line through $(1, 1, 1)$.
6. $B(1, -1, 1) = (0 + 1 + 0, \ 0 + 0 - 1, \ 1 + 0 + 0) = (1, -1, 1)$, so the axis of $B$ is the line through $(1, -1, 1)$.
7. Both traces are $0 = 1 + 2\cos\theta$, so both are rotations by $\theta = \frac{2\pi}{3}$, by [[Euler's rotation theorem|2ZVG43GSKA]].

## Remarks

- Each basic rotation is orthogonal with determinant $1$: its columns are orthonormal, and the $2 \times 2$ block $R(\theta)$ has determinant $c^2 + s^2 = 1$.
- The two products have the same angle, but different axes. They are conjugate in $SO(3)$.
- The rotation $A$ is also studied in [[this example|~942de4eb]], with the axis and the angle computed from the matrix.
- Because of this, a list of rotations about axes, like the [[Euler angles|YRSV2AASVB]], must state the order of the factors.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a book and turn it a quarter turn about one axis, and then about another axis.
Then start again and do the two turns in the other order.
The book ends in different positions.
The matrices show the same thing: the two products are different.

### L2 human

The worked computation is in the Example.
The typical use: when you combine rotations of space, keep the order of the factors.
A trap: in $R_y(\theta)$, the entry $-\sin\theta$ is in the third row, not the first row.
