## Proof

We prove the formula for variables $x_1, \dots, x_n$ by induction on $n$, and then substitute values.

1. Let $P_n = \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} (x_j - x_i)$, and let $V_n$ be the Vandermonde matrix of $x_1, \dots, x_n$.
2. First we prove $\det V_n = P_n$ in the [[polynomial ring|0Q9EZS90GY]] $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, by induction on $n$.
3. For $n = 1$, $\det V_1 = \det(1) = 1 = P_1$.
4. Let $n \ge 2$, and let $L$ be the [[field of fractions|0VF11ZTTMD]] of $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}]$.
5. Replace $x_n$ by a new variable $t$, and let $g(t)$ be the determinant of the result, in $L[t]$.
6. By the [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]] along row $n$, $g(t) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} t^{j-1} C_{nj}$, where the cofactors $C_{nj}$ do not involve $t$.
7. So $g$ has degree at most $n - 1$, and its coefficient of $t^{n-1}$ is $C_{nn} = \det V_{n-1}$.
8. By the induction hypothesis, $C_{nn} = P_{n-1}$.
9. For $i < n$, $g(x_i)$ is the determinant of a matrix whose rows $i$ and $n$ are equal.
10. So $g(x_i) = 0$, by the [[rule for equal rows|0K4VQB7HWR]].
11. Let $h(t) = g(t) - P_{n-1} \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} (t - x_i)$.
12. By steps 7 and 8, the terms of degree $n - 1$ cancel, so $h$ has degree at most $n - 2$ or $h = 0$.
13. By step 10, $h(x_i) = 0$ for the $n - 1$ distinct elements $x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}$ of $L$.
14. A nonzero polynomial over a field has at most as many roots as its [[degree|0KJVQV3E0F]], so $h = 0$.
15. So $g(t) = P_{n-1} \prod_{i<n} (t - x_i)$, and $t = x_n$ gives $\det V_n = P_{n-1} \prod_{i<n} (x_n - x_i) = P_n$.
16. Now let $K$ be any commutative ring and $a_1, \dots, a_n \in K$.
17. The map $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n] \to K$ with $x_i \mapsto a_i$ is a ring homomorphism.
18. By [[naturality of the determinant|03Q7RE2HXR]], it maps $\det V_n$ to the Vandermonde determinant of $a_1, \dots, a_n$.
19. It maps $P_n$ to $\prod_{i<j} (a_j - a_i)$, so the formula holds in $K$.

## Idea

As a polynomial in the last variable, the determinant has degree $n - 1$ and vanishes at the other $n - 1$ variables.
So it is its leading coefficient times $\prod_{i<n} (t - x_i)$, and the leading coefficient is the smaller Vandermonde determinant.

## Variants

Another version compares degrees: $\det V$ is homogeneous of degree $n(n-1)/2$ and is divisible by each $x_j - x_i$.
So it is a constant times $P_n$, and the term $x_2 x_3^2 \cdots x_n^{n-1}$ of the diagonal shows that the constant is $1$.
That version needs unique factorization in $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$.
