## Statement

Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over $\R$, with [[transpose|0W809K6AW0]] $A^\top$.
Then
$$\rank(A^\top A) = \rank(A A^\top) = \rank A,$$
where $\rank$ is the [[rank of a matrix|0ZC9YTXZRY]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The entries must be real. Over $\C$, the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $1$, but $A^\top A = 1 + i^2 = 0$. See the [[counterexample|~3e351f1e]].
- Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $1$, but $A^\top A = 1 + 1 = 0$.
- The proof needs $\lVert y \rVert^2 = y^\top y = 0$ only for $y = 0$. This holds over $\R$ and over every ordered field.

### Consequences

- The $n \times n$ matrix $A^\top A$ is invertible if and only if the columns of $A$ are independent.
- So the normal equations $A^\top A x = A^\top b$ of least squares have a unique solution when $\rank A = n$.
- $\operatorname{Col}(A^\top A) = \operatorname{Col}(A^\top) = \operatorname{Row}(A)$, since the first lies in the second and the dimensions agree.

### Converse

The equality of ranks does not give $A^\top A = A A^\top$.
For $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $A^\top A$ is $2 \times 2$ and $A A^\top = (1)$ is $1 \times 1$.

### Generalizations

Over $\C$, $\rank(A^* A) = \rank A$, with the conjugate transpose $A^*$.
For a linear map $T$ between finite-dimensional inner product spaces, $\rank(T^* T) = \rank T$.

## History

The matrix $A^\top A$ is the Gram matrix of the columns of $A$, named after Gram.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The matrix $A^\top A$ has the same rank as $A$, for real matrices.
Its entries are the dot products of the columns of $A$.

### L2 human

The key fact is that $A^\top A x = 0$ forces $Ax = 0$.
Indeed $x^\top A^\top A x$ is the squared length of $Ax$, and a real vector of length $0$ is zero.
So $A$ and $A^\top A$ have the same null space, and the same number of columns.

### L3 human

The statement fails over fields where a nonzero vector can have $y^\top y = 0$.
Such isotropic vectors exist over $\C$ and over every finite field.
