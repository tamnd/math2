## Example

In the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] $S_3$, compose permutations from right to left.
The [[normal closure|~99dbea13]] of $\{(1\,2)\}$ in $S_3$ is $S_3$.

1. The [[conjugates|0DXD4QSZ8A]] of $(1\,2)$ have the form $g(1\,2)g^{-1} = (g(1)\,g(2))$.
2. For $g = (2\,3)$ we get $(1\,3)$. For $g = (1\,3)$ we get $(3\,2) = (2\,3)$.
3. So the conjugates of $(1\,2)$ are the three transpositions $(1\,2)$, $(1\,3)$ and $(2\,3)$.
4. The normal closure is the [[subgroup generated|0RP7VWQ0YN]] by these conjugates.
5. It contains $(1\,2)(1\,3) = (1\,3\,2)$. So it contains $e$, three transpositions and at least one $3$-cycle, which makes at least $5$ elements.
6. By [[Lagrange's theorem|3C53TD]], its order divides $6$. The only divisor of $6$ that is at least $5$ is $6$.
7. So $\langle\langle (1\,2) \rangle\rangle = S_3$.

## Remarks

The subgroup $\langle (1\,2) \rangle = \{e, (1\,2)\}$ has $2$ elements, but the normal closure has $6$ elements.
The gap shows that $\{e, (1\,2)\}$ is far from normal.
The normal subgroups of $S_3$ are $\{e\}$, $A_3$ and $S_3$.
Only $S_3$ contains $(1\,2)$, and this gives a second proof.
For each $n \ge 2$, the same argument with the theorem that [[transpositions generate $S_n$|0AQNX7G443]] shows that the normal closure of a transposition in $S_n$ is $S_n$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The swap of $1$ and $2$ looks like the swap of $1$ and $3$ after a renaming of the numbers.
A normal subgroup that contains one swap must contain all the renamed swaps.
Two different swaps, done one after the other, give a rotation of the three numbers.
With swaps and rotations, we get all six permutations.
So the only normal subgroup that contains a swap is the whole group.

### L2 human

The method is to list the conjugates, then to generate a subgroup from them.
Step 5 uses the product of two conjugates, which is not itself a conjugate of $(1\,2)$.
Step 6 avoids a full list of products, because Lagrange's theorem leaves only one possible order.
A trap is to stop at step 3.
The three transpositions do not form a subgroup, because the product of two of them is a $3$-cycle.
