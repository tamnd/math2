## Example

Let $A = (1, 2, 3)$ and $B = (4, 0, 5)$.
We find the [[line|0399QFDFS8]] through $A$ and $B$ in three forms, and test two points.

1. Direction vector: $d = B - A = (3, -2, 2)$.
2. Vector form: $r = (1, 2, 3) + t(3, -2, 2)$.
3. Parametric form: $x = 1 + 3t$, $y = 2 - 2t$, $z = 3 + 2t$.
4. Symmetric form: $\dfrac{x - 1}{3} = \dfrac{y - 2}{-2} = \dfrac{z - 3}{2}$, since all components of $d$ are nonzero.
5. Test $P = (7, -2, 7)$: the $x$ equation gives $t = 2$. Then $y = -2$ and $z = 7$, so $P$ is on the line.
6. Test $Q = (2, 1, 4)$: the $x$ equation gives $t = \tfrac{1}{3}$. Then $y = \tfrac{4}{3} \neq 1$, so $Q$ is not on the line.
7. The midpoint of $AB$ is at $t = \tfrac{1}{2}$: $(\tfrac{5}{2}, 1, 4)$.

## Remarks

The point $P$ is at $t = 2$ and $B$ is at $t = 1$, so $B$ is the midpoint of $A$ and $P$.
The direction $2d = (6, -4, 4)$ or $-d = (-3, 2, -2)$ gives the same line with another speed.
With the base point $B$ the vector form is $(4, 0, 5) + s(3, -2, 2)$, and the point at $s$ is the point at $t = s + 1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To go from $A$ to $B$ we move by the arrow $(3, -2, 2)$.
Every point of the line is $A$ plus some multiple of this arrow.
To test a point, find the multiple from one coordinate, and then check the other coordinates.

### L2 human

The test in steps 5 and 6 uses one coordinate to find $t$ and the others to check it.
The point is on the line only if one value of $t$ fits all three coordinates.
A trap: the same check with the symmetric form is three fractions that must be equal; for $Q$ they are $\tfrac{1}{3}$, $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}$.
