## Statement

Let $(B, I)$ be an [[interior algebra|7X1HGZ6FK0]].
Let $B^\circ = \set{x \in B : Ix = x}$ be the set of open elements.
For $x, y \in B^\circ$ put $x \Rightarrow y = I(\neg x \lor y)$.

**Theorem.** $B^\circ$ contains $0$ and $1$, it is closed under $\land$ and $\lor$ of $B$, and $\langle B^\circ, \land, \lor, \Rightarrow, 0, 1 \rangle$ is a [[Heyting algebra|BND6A96S3D]].
Its pseudocomplement is $x \Rightarrow 0 = I \neg x$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. Without $IIx = Ix$, the element $I(\neg x \lor y)$ need not be open.
   [[The reflexive non-transitive frame|~47b1a5e5]] gives open $x = \set{2}$, $y = \emptyset$ with $I(\neg x \lor y) = \set{0}$ and $I\set{0} = \emptyset$.
2. Without $I(x \land y) = Ix \land Iy$, open elements need not be closed under $\land$.
   On the subsets of $\set{1, 2, 3}$, let $Ix$ be the union of the sets among $\emptyset$, $\set{1, 2}$, $\set{2, 3}$, $\set{1, 2, 3}$ that lie inside $x$.
   Then $\set{1, 2}$ and $\set{2, 3}$ are open, but their meet $\set{2}$ has $I\set{2} = \emptyset$.
3. Without $I1 = 1$, the top of $B$ need not be open.
   For $Ix = x \land a$ with $a \ne 1$, the open elements are the $x \le a$, so $1 \notin B^\circ$.

### Consequences

1. The open sets of a topological space form a Heyting algebra, with $U \Rightarrow V$ the interior of $(X \setminus U) \cup V$.
2. Dually, the closed elements form a [[co-Heyting algebra|6G03V0BVZT]], with the difference $x - y = C(x \land \neg y)$, where $Cx = \neg I \neg x$.
3. A formula of intuitionistic logic valid in $B^\circ$ corresponds to an S4 formula valid in $B$, through the [[Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation|BXVNEN55MR]].

### Converse

Every Heyting algebra is isomorphic to $B^\circ$ for some interior algebra $B$.
One can take for $B$ the free Boolean extension of the Heyting algebra; we cite this fact from the source and do not prove it here.
For a finite Heyting algebra $H$, Birkhoff's representation gives a finite poset $W$ whose up-sets form a lattice isomorphic to $H$.
Then the power set of $W$, with $Ix$ the largest up-set inside $x$, is an interior algebra with $B^\circ \cong H$; see [[this example|~faaa65ad]].

### Generalizations

1. The same proof works when $B$ is a Heyting algebra: the fixed points of an interior operator $I$ on $B$ form a Heyting algebra with $x \Rightarrow y = I(x \to y)$.
2. The [[Blok-Esakia theorem|BB9TA5Q9PN]] turns this correspondence into a lattice isomorphism between superintuitionistic logics and normal extensions of Grzegorczyk's logic.

## History

McKinsey and Tarski studied the open and closed elements of closure algebras in their papers of the 1940s.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a space, call a set open if it equals its own inside.
Open sets can be combined by "and" (overlap) and "or" (union), and the result is again open.
"Not" is different: the complement of an open set is usually not open.
So one takes the inside of the complement instead, and gets a new kind of "not".
With this "not", the open sets obey the rules of intuitionistic logic.

### L2 human

1. Take $B$ the power set of $\set{a, b}$ with $I\set{b} = \emptyset$ and $I x = x$ otherwise.
2. The open elements are $\emptyset$, $\set{a}$, $\set{a, b}$.
3. $\set{a} \Rightarrow \emptyset = I(\set{b} \cup \emptyset) = \emptyset$.
4. $\emptyset \Rightarrow \set{a} = I(\set{a, b}) = \set{a, b}$.
5. The open elements form the three-element Heyting chain, in which $p \lor \neg p$ fails at $\set{a}$.

The typical use: to build a Heyting algebra with a given property, build a topology.
The trap: the Heyting implication of open elements is not the Boolean $\neg x \lor y$; that element may fail to be open.

### L3 human

The theorem is the algebraic form of the fact that S4 contains intuitionistic logic through the translation $p \mapsto \Box p$.
In categorical terms, the open elements are the fixed points of a comonad on $B$ that preserves finite meets, and such fixed points always inherit a Heyting implication from a Boolean algebra.
