## Example

A student must compute $(3 + 4) \cdot 2$ and writes
$$3 + 4 = 7 \cdot 2 = 14.$$
We read the chain sign by sign.

1. A chain $a = b = c$ claims $a = b$ and $b = c$, so by transitivity it also claims $a = c$.
2. The first sign claims $3 + 4 = 7 \cdot 2$, that is, $7 = 14$. This is false.
3. The second sign claims $7 \cdot 2 = 14$. This is true.
4. By transitivity the chain also claims $3 + 4 = 14$, which is false.
5. So the chain is not a correct use of the [[equality|E918704SKV]] sign, even though the final answer $14$ is right.
6. A correct form is $(3 + 4) \cdot 2 = 7 \cdot 2 = 14$, where each expression in the chain has the value $14$.

## Remarks

The student uses $=$ as "and then I get", in the way a calculator shows a running result.
In mathematics each $=$ makes a claim about the two expressions next to it, so every expression in a chain must have the same value.
The error does not change the answer here, but in a proof a false line can lead to a false conclusion.
The page [[equality, definition and equivalence signs|~e4be298b]] lists the correct uses of the sign.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The sign $=$ means "the left side and the right side are the same number".
In $3 + 4 = 7 \cdot 2$, the left side is $7$ and the right side is $14$.
These are not the same, so the line is wrong, even though $14$ is the right answer.
Write $(3 + 4) \cdot 2 = 7 \cdot 2 = 14$ instead, or use two lines: $3 + 4 = 7$ and $7 \cdot 2 = 14$.

### L2 human

We repair a longer chain in the same way.

1. The wrong chain is $5^2 = 25 - 9 = 16 = \sqrt{16} = 4$, for the task "compute $\sqrt{5^2 - 9}$".
2. Its first sign claims $25 = 16$, and its third sign claims $16 = 4$. Both are false.
3. The correct chain is $\sqrt{5^2 - 9} = \sqrt{25 - 9} = \sqrt{16} = 4$.
4. Each expression in the correct chain has the value $4$, so every sign is true.

The typical use of a chain of equalities is a computation in which each step rewrites the whole expression.
A trap: a chain that ends with the right answer can still contain false signs, and a reader checks each sign, not only the last value.
