## Statement

Let $a \le b$ and let $f \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be [[continuous|~9520bdea]].
Then there are $c, d \in [a, b]$ with $f(c) \le f(x) \le f(d)$ for all $x \in [a, b]$.

## Remarks

By the [[boundedness theorem|~8040bfba]], $M = \sup f([a, b])$ is finite. If $f(x) < M$ for all $x$, then $1/(M - f(x))$ is continuous and unbounded, which is a contradiction.
Each hypothesis is needed. The function $x$ on $[0, 1)$ has no maximum, and $x$ on $[0, \infty)$ is not bounded above.
Mathlib states it for compact sets, as `IsCompact.exists_isMinOn` and `IsCompact.exists_isMaxOn`.

## History

Bernard Bolzano proved the theorem in the 1830s. His proof stayed unpublished until 1930.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a closed bounded interval, a continuous function has a highest value and a lowest value.
Its graph has a top point and a bottom point.
