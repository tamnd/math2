## Statement

Let $(I, <)$ be a nonempty [[total order|0FRXBVVR03]], and let $(\mathcal{M}_i)_{i \in I}$ be $L$-[[structures|983GZBQYT4]] such that $\mathcal{M}_i$ is a [[substructure|676H7EGTKJ]] of $\mathcal{M}_j$ whenever $i < j$.
Let $M = \bigcup_{i \in I} M_i$, the [[union|0X1GSWNBN1]] of the domains.

0. There is exactly one $L$-structure $\mathcal{M}$ with domain $M$ such that each $\mathcal{M}_i$ is a substructure of $\mathcal{M}$. It is called the union of the chain.
1. Elementary chains: if $\mathcal{M}_i \preceq \mathcal{M}_j$ whenever $i < j$, then $\mathcal{M}_i \preceq \mathcal{M}$ for every $i \in I$.
2. Chains of models of $\forall\exists$ theories: let $T$ be a [[theory|92SWXST1ZM]] whose axioms have the form $\forall \bar x \, \exists \bar y \, \theta(\bar x, \bar y)$ with $\theta$ quantifier-free. If every $\mathcal{M}_i$ is a model of $T$, then $\mathcal{M}$ is a model of $T$.

Here $\mathcal{M}_i \preceq \mathcal{M}_j$ means that $\mathcal{M}_i$ is an [[elementary substructure|8WZF9SBP39]] of $\mathcal{M}_j$.
A proof is in [[the proof of the elementary chain theorem|~bedbc3ae]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

Elementarity of the chain is needed in part 1.
Let $\mathcal{M}_n = (\set{k \in \Z : k \ge -n}, <)$ for $n \in \N$.
Each is isomorphic to $(\N, <)$, and $\mathcal{M}_n \subseteq \mathcal{M}_{n+1}$.
The union is $(\Z, <)$, and $\mathcal{M}_0 \not\preceq \Z$, because $0$ is least in $\mathcal{M}_0$ and not in $\Z$.
The form $\forall\exists$ is needed in part 2.
The same chain consists of models of $T = \mathrm{Th}(\N, <)$, but the union does not satisfy the $\exists\forall$ sentence $\exists x \, \forall y \, \neg(y < x)$ of $T$.
The chain condition is needed for part 0: two substructures that are not comparable may give different values $f(\bar a)$ for a function symbol $f$.

### Consequences

- The union of a chain of fields is a field, and the union of a chain of [[algebraically closed fields|0PF6TM8TWT]] is algebraically closed, because both theories have $\forall\exists$ axioms.
- Many constructions of models are transfinite elementary chains whose union is checked by part 1, for example the construction of [[saturated models|RMTZ5J64FX]].
- If each $\mathcal{M}_i \preceq \mathcal{N}$ and the $\mathcal{M}_i$ form a chain, then $\bigcup_i \mathcal{M}_i \preceq \mathcal{N}$, by the [[Tarski-Vaught test|60SA99BER7]].

### Converse

The converse of part 1 holds: if every $\mathcal{M}_i \preceq \mathcal{M}$, then $\mathcal{M}_i \preceq \mathcal{M}_j$ for $i < j$, since both agree with $\mathcal{M}$ on formulas with parameters in $M_i$.
The converse of part 2 is the Chang-Łoś-Suszko theorem: a theory whose class of models is closed under unions of chains is axiomatized by $\forall\exists$ sentences.
This theorem is proved in the sub-area on [[preservation theorems|JYJHB4YBAH]].

### Generalizations

The theorem holds for a directed system of elementary embeddings: the direct limit exists, and each structure embeds elementarily into it.
Part 1 holds for every logic whose quantifiers bind finitely many variables, for example for $L_{\infty \omega}$, where conjunctions may be infinite.

## History

Alfred Tarski and Robert Vaught proved the elementary chain theorem in their 1957 paper "Arithmetical extensions of relational systems".

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a sequence of structures, each one sitting inside the next one, like a growing collection of numbers.
Put them all together into one big structure.
If each step of the sequence keeps every first-order statement about the old elements true, then the big union also keeps them true.
For example, the step "add more numbers between the old ones, keeping the order dense" never changes a statement about the old numbers, and neither does the whole union.

### L2 human

We use part 2 to show that the union of the chain $\Q \subseteq \Q(\sqrt{2}) \subseteq \Q(\sqrt[4]{2}) \subseteq \Q(\sqrt[8]{2}) \subseteq \cdots$ of subfields of $\R$ is a field.

1. Each field in the chain is a subfield of the next, so the chain is a chain of substructures in $L = \set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$.
2. The field axioms are universal, except "every nonzero element has an inverse", which is $\forall x \, \exists y \, (x = 0 \lor x \cdot y = 1)$.
3. So the theory of fields is $\forall\exists$, and part 2 applies.
4. Directly: if $x \ne 0$ lies in the union, then it lies in some $\Q(\sqrt[2^n]{2})$, its inverse lies there too, and $x \cdot x^{-1} = 1$ holds in the union.

The typical use is to build a model in $\omega$ or more steps and to know that the limit is still a model.
A trap is to apply the theorem to a chain of substructures that are each models of a complete theory: without elementarity of the chain, the union may fail the theory, as the chain of copies of $\N$ shows.

### L3 human

Part 1 says that the class of models of a theory, with elementary embeddings, is closed under directed colimits.
This is one of the axioms of an abstract elementary class, and the chain theorem is the base of the theory of such classes.
Part 2 belongs to the preservation theorems: unions of chains preserve exactly the $\forall\exists$ sentences, as substructures preserve the universal sentences in the [[Łoś-Tarski theorem|9RN0KYXNT1]].
Chains of length $\omega$ and of uncountable length build [[saturated models|RMTZ5J64FX]], and continuous chains of elementary substructures of size $\aleph_0$ are a standard tool in set theory, where a club of countable elementary submodels of $V_\theta$ appears in many proofs.
