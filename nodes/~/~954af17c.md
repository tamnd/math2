## Proof

Call a map $f$ from a subset of an $L^+$-structure $\mathcal{A}$ into an $L^+$-structure $\mathcal{B}$ *$L$-elementary* if it is a [[partial elementary map|XTNYY0WRQ5]] from $\mathcal{A} | L$ to $\mathcal{B} | L$.

**(a) implies (b), and (b) implies (c).**
If $\mathcal{A} \models T$, then $\varphi(\mathcal{A}) = \psi_i(\mathcal{A})$ for some $i$, and automorphisms of $\mathcal{A} | L$ preserve the $L$-definable set $\psi_i(\mathcal{A})$.
The second implication is a special case.

**(c) implies (a).**
We prove the contrapositive; assume (a) fails.

1. Add new constants $\bar c$, $\bar d$ and let $\Sigma = T \cup \set{\varphi(\bar c), \neg \varphi(\bar d)} \cup \set{\psi(\bar c) \leftrightarrow \psi(\bar d) : \psi \text{ an } L\text{-formula}}$.
2. Suppose $\Sigma$ is inconsistent. By [[compactness|1NR3HAWG9D]] there are $L$-formulas $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_m$ such that, in every model $\mathcal{A}$ of $T$, tuples that agree on all $\psi_j$ agree on $\varphi$.
3. Then $\varphi(\mathcal{A})$ is a union of cells $\chi_s = \bigwedge_{j} \psi_j^{s(j)}$, $s \in \set{0, 1}^m$, where $\psi^1 = \psi$ and $\psi^0 = \neg \psi$. So $T \models \bigvee_{S} \forall \bar x \, (\varphi \leftrightarrow \bigvee_{s \in S} \chi_s)$ over all $S \subseteq \set{0, 1}^m$, which is (a). Hence $\Sigma$ is consistent.
4. By the downward [[Löwenheim-Skolem theorem|484M0TZ1S7]], take $\mathcal{A}_0 \models T$ of size at most $\kappa = \lvert L^+ \rvert + \aleph_0$ with $\bar a, \bar b$ realizing $\Sigma$. Then $f_0 = \set{\bar a \mapsto \bar b}$ is $L$-elementary from $\mathcal{A}_0$ to $\mathcal{A}_0$.
5. Lemma: if $f$ is $L$-elementary from $\mathcal{A}$ to $\mathcal{A}$, there are an $L^+$-[[elementary extension|8WZF9SBP39]] $\mathcal{B}$ of $\mathcal{A}$ of size at most $\lvert A \rvert + \kappa$ and an $L$-elementary $g \supseteq f$ from all of $\mathcal{A}$ into $\mathcal{B}$.
6. Proof of the lemma: add constants $e_a$ for $a \in A$. Let $\Gamma$ be the $L^+$-elementary diagram of $\mathcal{A}$ together with $\psi(\bar e)$ for every $L$-formula $\psi$ and tuple $\bar a$ with $\mathcal{A} \models \psi(\bar a)$. For $x$ in the domain of $f$, replace $e_x$ by the constant that names $f(x)$.
7. A finite part mentions $\psi(f(\bar x), \bar e_{\bar y})$ with $\mathcal{A} \models \psi(\bar x, \bar y)$. Since $f$ is $L$-elementary and $\mathcal{A} \models \exists \bar y \, \psi(\bar x, \bar y)$, there is $\bar y'$ in $A$ with $\mathcal{A} \models \psi(f(\bar x), \bar y')$. So $\Gamma$ is finitely satisfiable in $\mathcal{A}$; compactness and the downward Löwenheim-Skolem theorem give $\mathcal{B}$, and $g(a) = e_a$.
8. Apply the lemma to $f^{-1}$ as well. Alternating, build an $L^+$-elementary chain $\mathcal{A}_0 \preceq \mathcal{A}_1 \preceq \cdots$ and $L$-elementary maps $f_0 \subseteq f_1 \subseteq \cdots$ such that $f_{2k+1}$ has domain $A_{2k}$ and $f_{2k+2}$ has range containing $A_{2k+1}$, each map going into the next structure.
9. Let $\mathcal{A}_\omega = \bigcup_k \mathcal{A}_k$, an $L^+$-elementary extension of each $\mathcal{A}_k$ by the [[elementary chain theorem|NHCRZ74JHH]], and $f = \bigcup_k f_k$. Then $f$ is defined on all of $A_\omega$, onto $A_\omega$, and $L$-elementary, so it is an [[automorphism|DSQ17TNW1C]] of $\mathcal{A}_\omega | L$.
10. $f(\bar a) = \bar b$, while $\mathcal{A}_\omega \models \varphi(\bar a) \wedge \neg \varphi(\bar b)$. So $f$ does not map $\varphi(\mathcal{A}_\omega)$ onto itself, and $\lvert A_\omega \rvert \le \kappa$, so (c) fails.

**Parts 2 and 3.**
If $T$ is complete, it proves one of the sentences $\forall \bar x \, (\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi_i)$, so $k = 1$.
For part 3, let $T = \mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{M})$ in $L^+$; if $\varphi(\mathcal{M})$ is not $L$-definable, (a) fails for $T$ by part 2.
In step 4, $\Sigma$ together with the elementary diagram of $\mathcal{M}$ is consistent: a finite part $\delta(\bar m)$ of the diagram may be replaced by the sentence $\exists \bar z \, \delta(\bar z)$, which lies in $T$.
So $\mathcal{A}_0$ can be chosen as an elementary extension of $\mathcal{M}$, and then $\mathcal{A}_\omega$ is an elementary extension of $\mathcal{M}$ with an automorphism of $\mathcal{A}_\omega | L$ that moves $\varphi(\mathcal{A}_\omega)$.

## Idea

If no finite set of $L$-formulas pins down $\varphi$, two tuples with the same $L$-type can disagree on $\varphi$.
Sending one tuple to the other is a partial $L$-elementary map, and a back-and-forth construction along an elementary chain extends it to a full automorphism of the $L$-part, which then moves $\varphi$.

## Variants

- If $T$ is complete and has a saturated model, the automorphism of step 9 exists in that model directly, by the homogeneity of saturated models.
- For countable $L^+$, the models in (c) are countable, so automorphisms of countable models already test definability.
