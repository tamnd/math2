## Summary

Gaussian elimination is an algorithm that changes a matrix into a [[row echelon form|~a56c300b]] by [[elementary row operations|~6bd67cbb]].
It works column by column, from left to right.
In each column, it chooses a nonzero entry as [[pivot|~a3760fc0]] and moves its row up.
Then it adds multiples of that row to the rows below, to make their entries in that column zero.
Applied to the [[augmented matrix|~77d11eff]] of a [[system of linear equations|~12095b88]], it gives an equivalent system that [[back substitution|~a2f0b72a]] solves.
For an $n \times n$ matrix, it uses on the order of $n^3$ arithmetic operations.

## Remarks

The algorithm also computes the [[rank|~802904f9]] of a matrix and, with a record of the row operations, its [[determinant|~362fc72a]].
It also tests if a system is [[consistent|~7f8fd4d0]]: the system is inconsistent exactly when a row $(0, \dots, 0 \mid c)$ with $c \ne 0$ appears.
The method was known in China in the book *The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art*, long before Gauss.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gaussian elimination is the method of solving equations by removing one unknown at a time.
We use the first equation to remove $x_1$ from all the other equations, then the second to remove $x_2$, and so on.
At the end, the last equation has the fewest unknowns, and we solve upwards.
