## Example

Let $M$ be a countable [[non-standard model|1QWN5CV4CG]] of $\mathsf{PA}$, and let $\mathcal{K}$ be the class of the cuts $I$ of $M$ with $I \models \mathsf{PA}$.
Let $Y(a, b) = \max\set{c : a \cdot 2^c \le b}$ be the [[doubling indicator|~7946003b]].
We check that $Y$ is not an [[indicator|~1d28aa25]] for $\mathcal{K}$.

1. Let $c \in M$ be non-standard, with $c \ge 2$. Put $a = c$ and $b = c^2$.
2. $Y(c, c^2)$ is the greatest $k$ with $2^k \le c$. It is non-standard, because $2^k \le c$ holds for every standard $k$.
3. Let $I$ be a cut with $c \in I$ and $I \models \mathsf{PA}$, where $I$ carries the operations of $M$.
4. Then $I$ is closed under multiplication, so $c^2 \in I$.
5. So no $I \in \mathcal{K}$ has $c \in I < c^2$, although $Y(c, c^2)$ is non-standard.
6. This breaks condition 3 of the definition. So $Y$ is not an indicator for $\mathcal{K}$.

## Remarks

The other direction of condition 3 holds: every cut that is a model of $\mathsf{PA}$ is closed under doubling.
So $Y$ errs only in one direction: it reports a gap where there is no model of $\mathsf{PA}$.
An indicator for $\mathcal{K}$ must grow much more slowly, like the function of the [[Paris-Harrington proof|~a32fde15]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The doubling count between $c$ and $c^2$ is infinite when $c$ is infinite.
But a model of arithmetic that contains $c$ must contain $c \cdot c$.
So this count sees a gap where no model of arithmetic fits, and it cannot detect such models.

### L2 human

The check uses only one pair, $c$ and $c^2$, and one closure property of models of $\mathsf{PA}$.
The typical use is to see why an indicator for models of $\mathsf{PA}$ needs a strong principle such as the Paris-Harrington principle.
The trap: the doubling indicator is correct for its own class of cuts, and it fails only for the smaller class of models of $\mathsf{PA}$.
