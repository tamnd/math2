## Statement

Let $\mathrm{TAUT}$ be the set of codes of propositional tautologies over a fixed finite alphabet.
A [[propositional proof system|~8ae21f34]] is a polynomial-time function onto $\mathrm{TAUT}$.
It is *polynomially bounded* if there is a polynomial $q$ such that every tautology $\tau$ has a proof of length at most $q(\abs{\tau})$.
Let [[NP|SBDQH0TJ5X]] and [[coNP|NBSD5FG7NY]] be the usual complexity classes.

Then the following two conditions are equivalent.

1. There is a polynomially bounded propositional proof system.
2. $\mathrm{NP} = \mathrm{coNP}$.

The measure is *size*: the number of symbols of a proof.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The theorem needs the condition that the proof system runs in polynomial time. Without it, the map with $f(\tau) = \tau$ on $\mathrm{TAUT}$ (and $f(x) = p \lor \neg p$ otherwise) would count as a polynomially bounded system, so condition 1 would hold outright. The equivalence would then assert $\mathrm{NP} = \mathrm{coNP}$, which is not known.
- The theorem needs the bound in terms of $\abs{\tau}$. Every tautology has some proof in every proof system, but the length of that proof is not bounded by a polynomial in general.

### Consequences

- *Cook's program.* To prove $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$, it is enough to show that no proof system is polynomially bounded. Researchers study ever stronger systems: [[resolution|~586b5042]], [[bounded-depth Frege|~2cc592a7]], [[cutting planes|~f9bde1dc]], [[Frege|~3f0577bc]] and [[extended Frege|~6925f591]].
- A superpolynomial lower bound for every proof system implies $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$, hence $\mathrm{P} \ne \mathrm{NP}$, a case of the [[P versus NP problem|MFN5GYZH5Z]].
- If $\mathrm{NP} = \mathrm{coNP}$, then the proof system built in the proof is polynomially bounded, and it is optimal by the [[basic properties|~8ae21f34]] of proof systems.

### Converse

The theorem is an equivalence, so its converse is the theorem itself.
A lower bound for a single system, such as [[Haken's theorem|~dd16430c]] for resolution, does not imply $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$.
It excludes only that system.

### Generalizations

The same proof works for every nonempty language $L$: a polynomially bounded proof system for $L$ exists if and only if $L \in \mathrm{NP}$.
For a coNP-complete language $L$, the condition $L \in \mathrm{NP}$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{NP} = \mathrm{coNP}$.

## History

Stephen Cook and Robert Reckhow published the theorem in 1979, in "The relative efficiency of propositional proof systems".
The same paper introduced the general definition of a propositional proof system and the notion of p-simulation.
Cook's 1975 work on the theory PV and the propositional translations of its theorems was a precursor; see [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A tautology is a formula that is true in every case.
The theorem says that short, quickly checkable evidence for every tautology exists exactly when a famous open problem of computer science has the answer "yes".
That problem asks whether every statement that can be refuted by a short counterexample can also be confirmed by a short certificate.
For instance, a formula with $50$ variables is refuted by one assignment of $50$ values, but no one knows a short certificate that it is a tautology.

### L2 human

We use the theorem in the direction "lower bounds for proofs separate classes".

1. Suppose that every proof system has tautologies whose shortest proofs are longer than every polynomial.
2. Then no proof system is polynomially bounded.
3. By the theorem, $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$.
4. Since $\mathrm{P}$ is closed under complement, $\mathrm{P} = \mathrm{NP}$ would give $\mathrm{NP} = \mathrm{coNP}$. So also $\mathrm{P} \ne \mathrm{NP}$.

The typical use is as a motivation: each lower bound for a concrete proof system is one step of Cook's program.
A trap is to read a lower bound for resolution as evidence of the same lower bound for Frege systems.
Frege systems prove the pigeonhole formulas in polynomial size, and resolution does not.

### L3 human

The theorem identifies polynomially bounded proof systems with NP certificates for a coNP-complete language.
It places proof complexity next to circuit complexity: lower bounds for circuits approach $\mathrm{P} \ne \mathrm{NP}$, and lower bounds for proofs approach $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$.
Through the correspondence with [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]], lower bounds for strong proof systems are linked with the independence of $\mathrm{NP} \ne \mathrm{coNP}$ and related statements from weak theories of arithmetic.
