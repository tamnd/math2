## Example

Let $C_2$ be the frame with a root $r$ below a top $t$.
Its up-sets $\emptyset \subset \set{t} \subset \set{r, t}$ form the three-element chain algebra $\mathbf{3} = \set{0, \tfrac12, 1}$, with coatom $\tfrac12$.
For a frame $G$, write $C_2 \leq G$ as in [[the Jankov-de Jongh theorem|~6e548f0e]].

1. Claim: $G \nvDash \neg \neg p \to p$ iff some world of $G$ has a proper successor.
2. Suppose $w \Vdash \neg \neg p$ and $w \nVdash p$. If $w \Vdash \neg p$, then $w \Vdash \bot$, which is impossible. So some $v \geq w$ has $v \Vdash p$, and $v \neq w$ because $w \nVdash p$.
3. Conversely, suppose $w < v$. Let $V(p) = \set{x : x > w}$, an up-set. Then $w \nVdash p$.
4. Every $y \geq w$ sees a world in $V(p)$: $w$ sees $v$, and $y > w$ lies in $V(p)$. So $w \Vdash \neg \neg p$, and $w \nVdash \neg \neg p \to p$.
5. Claim: some world has a proper successor iff $C_2 \leq G$.
6. If $w < v$, map $\mathord{\uparrow} w$ to $C_2$ by $w \mapsto r$ and $x \mapsto t$ for $x > w$. This map is monotone and onto. The back condition at $w$ is met by $v$, and elsewhere by the world itself. So it is a p-morphism.
7. If $f \colon H \to C_2$ is an onto p-morphism and $f(w) = r$, the back condition gives $w' \geq w$ with $f(w') = t$. So $w' \neq w$ is a proper successor.
8. By steps 1 and 5 and part 2 of the theorem, $\neg \neg p \to p$ and $\chi(C_2)$ are valid on exactly the same frames.
9. On algebras: if $B$ is a Heyting algebra and $x \vee \neg x \neq 1$, let $y = x \vee \neg x$. Then $\neg y = \neg x \wedge \neg \neg x = 0$, so $y \neq 0$.
10. Then $\set{0, y, 1}$ is closed under $\wedge$, $\vee$ and $\to$, since $y \to 0 = 0$ and $1 \to y = y$, and it is a copy of $\mathbf{3}$. So $\mathbf{3} \in \mathbf{SH}(B)$ and $B \nvDash \chi(\mathbf{3})$.
11. So an algebra validating $\chi(C_2)$ is Boolean and validates $\neg \neg p \to p$. Conversely $\chi(C_2) \in \mathsf{CPC}$, because $\mathbf{3}$ is not in $\mathbf{SH}(\mathbf{2})$.
12. By item 4 of [[Heyting algebra semantics|~32a20f35]], $\mathsf{IPC} + \chi(C_2) = \mathsf{IPC} + \neg \neg p \to p = \mathsf{CPC}$.

## Remarks

By part 3 of the theorem, every superintuitionistic logic either contains $\chi(C_2)$ or is contained in $\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{3})$.
With step 12 this gives: every intermediate logic other than $\mathsf{CPC}$ is contained in $\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{3})$, the logic of the frame $C_2$.
So $\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{3})$ is the largest intermediate logic other than $\mathsf{CPC}$; compare [[the greatest consistent logic|~4621c6fb]].
The short formula $\neg \neg p \to p$ does the job of $\chi(C_2)$, which has three variables.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Double negation elimination fails exactly when some state can still grow.
The smallest growing structure is two states, one before the other.
So "no growth" and "no copy of the two-state picture" are the same condition.
Any logic that keeps double negation elimination out has the two-state picture as a model.

### L2 human

1. On the three-element chain $a < b < c$, let $p$ be true at $c$ only.
2. Every world sees $c$, so $\neg \neg p$ holds everywhere.
3. $p$ fails at $a$, so $\neg \neg p \to p$ fails at $a$.
4. The p-morphism $a \mapsto r$, $b \mapsto t$, $c \mapsto t$ shows $C_2 \leq G$ directly.

The typical use is to replace a large Jankov formula by a short formula valid on the same frames.
A trap: equality on frames alone does not give equality of logics; step 12 uses algebras for that.
