## Summary

The average rate of change of a [[function|0XHDTE5YMZ]] $f$ on an interval tells how much the output changes per unit of input, taken over the whole interval.

Take an interval with endpoints $a$ and $b$, where $a \ne b$. The average rate of change of $f$ on it is the number
$$\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.$$

If the interval starts at $a$ and has length $h \ne 0$, then $b = a + h$, and the same number is
$$\frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}.$$

The two forms describe one quantity. It is the [[difference quotient|AZBDB5F4M4]] of $f$ between the two endpoints, and it is the slope of the secant line through the points of the graph at $a$ and at $b$.
