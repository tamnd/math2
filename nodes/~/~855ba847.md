## Example

The object is [[Gentzen's consistency proof|QPV0HNP22S]] for Peano arithmetic, in the form with a sequent calculus and ordinals below [[epsilon nought|KT50SMH3EC]].
We check the features of the [[epsilon substitution method|~de0d8cf6]] one at a time.

1. Language. The substitution method works with $\varepsilon$-terms and critical formulas. Gentzen's proof uses a sequent calculus with quantifiers and has no $\varepsilon$-terms.
2. The objects that change. The substitution method changes a finite assignment of numbers to terms. Gentzen's proof changes a derivation, by reduction steps such as cut reductions.
3. The goal of the process. The substitution method looks for values that make finitely many critical formulas true. Gentzen's proof reduces each derivation of the empty sequent to another derivation of the empty sequent.
4. The ordinals. Gentzen assigns an ordinal below $\varepsilon_0$ to each derivation, and each reduction step lowers it. There is no infinite decreasing sequence of ordinals, so no derivation of the empty sequent exists.
5. So the proof has none of the three features of the substitution method, and it is not an instance of it.

## Remarks

The two proofs have the same strength: both prove the consistency of Peano arithmetic, and both need transfinite induction up to $\varepsilon_0$.
They differ in what they transform: Gentzen's proof works on derivations, and the substitution method works on values of terms.
Gentzen's method became the basis of [[ordinal analysis|C5E5TG8PGP]] with sequent calculi, and the substitution method was later extended to stronger theories as well.

## Explanations

### L1 human

There are two famous ways to show that ordinary arithmetic never proves a false equation.
Gentzen's way takes a supposed proof of a contradiction and simplifies it again and again, which is impossible to do forever.
Hilbert's way guesses numbers for the unknown witnesses and repairs the guesses.
Both work, but they are different methods, so Gentzen's proof is not an example of Hilbert's method.

### L2 human

To decide whether a proof uses the substitution method, ask three questions.
Are there $\varepsilon$-terms and critical formulas, does a process change values of terms, and does it stop with all critical formulas true?
For Gentzen's proof the answer to all three is no.
A trap is to confuse the methods because both use the ordinal $\varepsilon_0$, which comes from the strength of Peano arithmetic, not from the method.
