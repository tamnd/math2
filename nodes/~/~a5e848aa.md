## Example

Let $(W, R)$ be a Kripke frame and let $A = \mathcal{P}(W)$.
We check that $(W, R, A)$ is a [[general frame|~84a9e3fa]], and that it validates the same formulas as $(W, R)$.

1. Finite unions: the union of two subsets of $W$ is a subset of $W$.
2. Complements: $W \setminus X$ is a subset of $W$.
3. The operator: $m_R(X) = \set{w : w R v \text{ for some } v \in X}$ is a subset of $W$.
4. Every valuation $V$ has $V(p) \subseteq W$, so every valuation is admissible.
5. So "true under all admissible valuations" is "true under all valuations", and the two notions of validity agree.
6. The full frame is differentiated, with $a = \set{u}$ for $u \neq v$, and tight, with $a = \set{v}$ when $u R v$ fails.

## Remarks

The example shows that general frames extend Kripke frames, so that results about general frames include results about Kripke frames.
The full frame is compact exactly when $W$ is finite.
If $W$ is infinite, the sets $W \setminus \set{w}$ have the finite intersection property and their intersection is empty.
So an infinite full frame is refined but not descriptive.
Its complex algebra is the full powerset algebra with $m_R$, a complete and atomic modal algebra.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Kripke frame lets a statement be true on any set of points.
Allowing every set as a region gives a general frame with nothing removed.
So the old notion of a frame is the special case of the new notion with all regions allowed.

### L2 human

1. Take $W = \N$ with $R = \set{(n, n+1)}$ and all subsets admissible.
2. The sets $\N \setminus \set{0, \ldots, n}$ are admissible, and any finitely many of them meet.
3. Their intersection over all $n$ is empty. So this full frame is not compact.

The typical use is to read every theorem about general frames as a theorem about Kripke frames, by taking all subsets as admissible.
A trap: full frames are descriptive only when they are finite, so results about descriptive frames do not apply to infinite Kripke frames directly.
