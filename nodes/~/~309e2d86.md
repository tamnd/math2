## Example

Let $T$ be the [[theory of dense linear orders without endpoints|2W4S2H4VYA]].
We show that every model of $T$ is [[atomic|~fbef995e]], and that $(\Q, <)$ is a [[prime model|S8W40PYNHX]] of $T$.

1. Let $M \models T$ and $\bar a = (a_1, \dots, a_n) \in M^n$. Let $\varphi_{\bar a}(\bar x)$ be the conjunction of the formulas $x_i < x_j$ and $x_i = x_j$ that hold of $\bar a$, over all pairs $i, j$.
2. Let $N \models T$ and let $\bar b \in N^n$ satisfy $\varphi_{\bar a}$. By the [[Löwenheim-Skolem theorem|484M0TZ1S7]], take countable [[elementary substructures|8WZF9SBP39]] $M_0 \preceq M$ containing $\bar a$ and $N_0 \preceq N$ containing $\bar b$.
3. $M_0$ and $N_0$ are countable models of $T$, and $\bar a \mapsto \bar b$ is an order isomorphism of finite sets. The [[back-and-forth method|6F2DB4HTJR]] extends it to an isomorphism $M_0 \cong N_0$.
4. So $\operatorname{tp}^M(\bar a) = \operatorname{tp}^{M_0}(\bar a) = \operatorname{tp}^{N_0}(\bar b) = \operatorname{tp}^N(\bar b)$. The formula $\varphi_{\bar a}$ isolates the type of $\bar a$.
5. So every model of $T$ is atomic. In particular $(\Q, <)$ is countable and atomic, hence prime by [[this theorem|~ddda0a28]].
6. $(\R, <)$ is atomic, by step 5, but not prime: it is uncountable, so it does not embed into $(\Q, <)$.

## Remarks

Step 4 shows that $T$ has only finitely many $n$-types for each $n$, one for each order pattern of $n$ points.
For the pattern of $n$ distinct points, there are $n!$ ways to order them, and patterns with equalities add more.
Every type is isolated, as the [[Ryll-Nardzewski theorem|1513J40TKA]] predicts for an $\aleph_0$-categorical theory.
Step 6 shows that the word countable cannot be dropped from the characterization of prime models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For dense orders without endpoints, a finite list of points is described completely by saying which point is left of which.
So every finite list is pinned down by one formula, and every model is atomic.
The fractions form the smallest model, and a copy of them sits inside every dense order without endpoints.

### L2 human

We find a copy of $(\Q, <)$ inside $(\R, <)$ by step 5.

1. The inclusion $\Q \subseteq \R$ preserves the order.
2. By step 4, the type of a tuple of rationals in $\Q$ and in $\R$ is fixed by its order pattern, which is the same in both.
3. So the inclusion is an elementary embedding.

The typical use is to see the difference between atomic and prime.
A trap is to call $(\R, <)$ prime because it is atomic.
