## Example

In $\mathbb{R}^2$, let $E = ((1, 0), (0, 1))$ be the standard basis and $B = ((2, 0), (0, 1))$.
Let $v_1 = (1, 2)$ and $v_2 = (3, 4)$.

1. In $E$ the coordinate matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, so $\det_E(v_1, v_2) = 4 - 6 = -2$.
2. The coordinates of $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ in $E$ give $\det_E(B) = 2 \cdot 1 - 0 \cdot 0 = 2$.
3. In $B$: $(1, 2) = \tfrac{1}{2} (2, 0) + 2 (0, 1)$ and $(3, 4) = \tfrac{3}{2} (2, 0) + 4 (0, 1)$.
4. So the [[definition|0V94H674MB]] gives $\det_B(v_1, v_2) = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & 3/2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = 2 - 3 = -1$.
5. Check by the [[change of basis rule|~89aed52d]]: $\det_E(v_1, v_2) = \det_E(B) \cdot \det_B(v_1, v_2) = 2 \cdot (-1) = -2$, which agrees with step 1.

## Remarks

The basis $B$ doubles the first unit vector, so the unit square of $B$ has area $2$.
Measured in this larger unit, the parallelogram of $v_1$ and $v_2$ has half the signed area.
Both values are negative, because $v_1, v_2$ has the orientation opposite to the one of $E$ and of $B$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The determinant of two vectors measures the signed area of their parallelogram.
The unit of area is the parallelogram of the basis.
A basis with a larger unit gives a smaller number.

### L2 human

Steps 1 to 4 compute the value in each basis directly, and step 5 links the two values.
A trap: $\det_B(v_1, v_2)$ is not $\det_E(v_1, v_2)$ times $\det_E(B)$; it is the quotient.
