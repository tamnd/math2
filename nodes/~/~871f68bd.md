## Statement

$$\cos \pi = -1, \qquad \sin \pi = 0.$$

## Remarks

By the [[definition of $\pi$|~9376aee3]], $\cos(\pi/2) = 0$. The [[Pythagorean identity|~3b6893cc]] gives $\sin(\pi/2) = 1$, since $\sin > 0$ on $(0, 2]$.
The [[double angle formulas|~b24c628a]] then give $\cos \pi = 2 \cos^2(\pi/2) - 1 = -1$ and $\sin \pi = 2 \sin(\pi/2) \cos(\pi/2) = 0$.
Together with $\sin \pi = 0$, it gives Euler's identity $e^{i \pi} = -1$ in complex form. Mathlib states the first claim as `Real.cos_pi`.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Half a turn around the unit circle takes the point $(1, 0)$ to the point $(-1, 0)$.
So $\cos \pi = -1$ and $\sin \pi = 0$.
