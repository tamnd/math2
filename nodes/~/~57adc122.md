## Statement

Let $F$ be a field and $A, B \in M_n(F)$.
The *characteristic matrix* of $A$ is $xI - A \in M_n(F[x])$.
Two matrices $M, N \in M_n(F[x])$ are [[equivalent|0CMHSGTWXB]] over $F[x]$ when $N = UMV$ with $U, V$ invertible in $M_n(F[x])$, that is, with $\det U$ and $\det V$ nonzero constants.

1. The following are equivalent:
   (a) $A$ and $B$ are [[similar|027KDW2K72]] over $F$;
   (b) $xI - A$ and $xI - B$ are equivalent over $F[x]$;
   (c) $xI - A$ and $xI - B$ have the same [[Smith normal form|MV28Z5N9XX]] over $F[x]$, with monic diagonal entries.
2. The Smith normal form of $xI - A$ is $\operatorname{diag}(1, \dots, 1, d_1, \dots, d_r)$, where $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$ are the [[invariant factors|~bcfda459]] of $A$ and there are $n - r$ entries equal to $1$.
3. Let $D_0 = 1$ and, for $1 \le k \le n$, let $D_k$ be the monic [[greatest common divisor|0C8WYW4KYQ]] of all $k \times k$ [[minors|0BT9BYWVRY]] of $xI - A$, the $k$-th *determinantal divisor*. Then the $k$-th diagonal entry of the Smith normal form is $D_k / D_{k-1}$, and $D_n = \det(xI - A)$ is the [[characteristic polynomial|0VSSPAFM2C]] of $A$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

Equivalence over $F$ instead of $F[x]$ is too weak: over $F$, two invertible matrices of the same size are always equivalent, and $xI - A$ is not a matrix over $F$.
In (b) the matrices $U, V$ must be invertible over $F[x]$, not only over $F(x)$: over the field $F(x)$, $xI - A$ and $xI - B$ are both invertible and hence always equivalent.
Unlike similarity, equivalence of $A$ and $B$ themselves over $F$ only compares ranks, so the [[equivalent matrices that are not similar|6CKAMR5WK9]] show that the variable $x$ is essential.

### Consequences

- Similarity is decidable by row and column operations over $F[x]$, with no root finding.
- $A$ is similar to its transpose, since $xI - A^{\mathsf T} = (xI - A)^{\mathsf T}$ has the same determinantal divisors as $xI - A$.
- The minimal polynomial is $D_n / D_{n-1}$.
- $A$ is cyclic if and only if $D_{n-1} = 1$.

### Converse

Each part is an equivalence, so the converse is included.
Not every matrix over $F[x]$ is equivalent to a characteristic matrix: the Smith normal form of $xI - A$ has nonzero entries with degrees summing to $n$.

### Generalizations

For a principal ideal domain $R$ and an $R$-module with presentation matrix $M$, the Smith normal form of $M$ gives the invariant factors of the module.
For $R = \mathbb{Z}$ this computes the structure of a finitely generated abelian group.
The pencil version, the Kronecker theory of $xB - A$, classifies pairs of matrices up to strict equivalence.

## History

The Smith normal form is named after Henry John Stephen Smith, who studied it for integer matrices.
The use of $xI - A$ for similarity is the classical theory of $\lambda$-matrices, in which the variable is written $\lambda$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To decide whether two matrices are the same map in different coordinates, we can work with matrices of polynomials.
We subtract each matrix from $x$ times the identity.
Then we simplify these polynomial matrices by row and column operations until they are diagonal, in a standard form.
The two matrices are similar exactly when the two standard forms are equal.

### L2 human

Compare $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = 2I_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

1. $xI - A = \begin{pmatrix} x - 2 & -1 \\ 0 & x - 2 \end{pmatrix}$. Its entries include $-1$, so $D_1 = 1$.
2. $D_2 = \det(xI - A) = (x - 2)^2$. So the Smith normal form is $\operatorname{diag}(1, (x - 2)^2)$.
3. $xI - B = \operatorname{diag}(x - 2, x - 2)$. Every entry is divisible by $x - 2$, and $x - 2$ is an entry, so $D_1 = x - 2$.
4. $D_2 = (x - 2)^2$, so the Smith normal form is $\operatorname{diag}(x - 2, x - 2)$.
5. The forms differ, so $A$ and $B$ are not similar, although $\chi_A = \chi_B$.
6. The invariant factors are $(x - 2)^2$ for $A$, and $x - 2, x - 2$ for $B$.

The typical use is to compute invariant factors by hand for small matrices, or by computer algebra for large ones.
The trap is to divide by a polynomial during the elimination: only the multiplication of a row by a nonzero constant is allowed, since other polynomials are not units in $F[x]$.

### L3 human

The matrix $xI - A$ is a presentation matrix of the module $F^n_A$: there is an exact sequence $F[x]^n \xrightarrow{xI - A} F[x]^n \to F^n_A \to 0$.
So the theorem is the statement that a module over a principal ideal domain is determined by the Smith normal form of any square presentation matrix.
The same idea computes the homology of chain complexes over $\mathbb{Z}$ and the structure of finitely presented abelian groups.
