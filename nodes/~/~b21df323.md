## Summary

The separation axioms are conditions on a topological space that say how well open sets can separate points and closed sets.
The main ones, from weak to strong, are [[T0|~5b4f8168]], [[T1|~4808a5a2]], [[Hausdorff|~a1fe0c25]], [[regular|~9f103e07]] and [[normal|~f4483346]].

## Remarks

Each of these axioms, together with T1, implies the axioms before it in this list.
Each metric space satisfies all of them, by [[metric spaces are Hausdorff|~ed9b4114]] and [[metrizable spaces are normal|~07c6c39a]].
Authors do not agree on all names. For example, some authors include T1 in the definition of a regular or normal space, and some do not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The separation axioms measure how many open sets a space has.
With more open sets, you can put distinct points, or points and closed sets, into disjoint open sets.
