## Example

Let $L = \set{E}$ with one binary relation symbol $E$, and let $T_{\mathrm{gr}}$ be the theory of simple [[graphs|00C0BR7D7H]]:
$$\gamma_1 = \forall x \, \neg E(x, x), \qquad \gamma_2 = \forall x \, \forall y \, (E(x, y) \to E(y, x)).$$
Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the path graph on three vertices: the domain is $\set{1, 2, 3}$ and $E^{\mathcal{P}} = \set{(1, 2), (2, 1), (2, 3), (3, 2)}$.
We check that $\mathcal{P}$ is a [[model|CWQV9PTCB3]] of $T_{\mathrm{gr}}$.

1. For $\gamma_1$, by the clause for $\forall$ of [[satisfaction|~cf18e0d7]], we check $\neg E(x, x)$ under $x \mapsto a$ for $a = 1, 2, 3$.
2. The pairs $(1, 1)$, $(2, 2)$ and $(3, 3)$ are not in $E^{\mathcal{P}}$. So $E(x, x)$ fails for each $a$, and $\neg E(x, x)$ holds.
3. So $\mathcal{P} \models \gamma_1$.
4. For $\gamma_2$, we check $E(x, y) \to E(y, x)$ under each of the nine pairs $x \mapsto a$, $y \mapsto b$.
5. For the five pairs $(a, b)$ not in $E^{\mathcal{P}}$, the antecedent fails, so the implication holds by the clause for $\to$.
6. For the four pairs in $E^{\mathcal{P}}$, the reversed pair is also in $E^{\mathcal{P}}$: $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 1)$ swap, and $(2, 3)$ and $(3, 2)$ swap. So the consequent holds.
7. So $\mathcal{P} \models \gamma_2$, and $\mathcal{P} \models T_{\mathrm{gr}}$.

## Remarks

The example shows the general method: check each axiom of the theory, quantifier by quantifier, on the given structure.
The models of $T_{\mathrm{gr}}$ are exactly the simple graphs, with $E$ read as adjacency.
The same theory has many other models, such as the cycle on four vertices and the empty graph on any nonempty set.
The structure on $\set{1, 2, 3}$ with $E = \set{(1, 2)}$ is not a model, because $\gamma_2$ fails under $x \mapsto 1$, $y \mapsto 2$.
The directed $3$-cycle, with $E = \set{(1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 1)}$, satisfies $\gamma_1$ but not $\gamma_2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Three dots in a row, with a line from the first to the middle and from the middle to the last, form a graph.
The rules of a graph say: no dot is joined to itself, and if one dot is joined to another, then the other is joined back.
Both rules hold in the picture, so the picture is a model of the rules.

### L2 human

We check $\gamma_2$ on the pair $x \mapsto 3$, $y \mapsto 2$, one of the four pairs where the antecedent holds.

1. $(3, 2) \in E^{\mathcal{P}}$, so $E(x, y)$ holds.
2. $(2, 3) \in E^{\mathcal{P}}$, so $E(y, x)$ holds.
3. So $E(x, y) \to E(y, x)$ holds under this assignment.

The typical use is to show that a structure belongs to a class of structures given by axioms.
A trap is to list each edge once, as $\set{(1, 2), (2, 3)}$; that structure is a directed graph, and it fails $\gamma_2$.
