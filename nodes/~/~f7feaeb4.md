## Summary

The theory of automorphic forms studies functions on symmetric spaces that are invariant under arithmetic groups, together with their L-functions and their Galois representations.
A modular form is a holomorphic function on the upper half-plane that transforms in a fixed way under a congruence subgroup of SL2(Z) and is holomorphic at the cusps.
The Fourier coefficients of modular forms carry arithmetic data, for example the number of representations of an integer by a quadratic form and the number of points of an elliptic curve modulo a prime.
The Langlands program, which Langlands formulated in the late 1960s, predicts a correspondence between automorphic representations and Galois representations, and class field theory is its abelian case.
This topic collects the automorphic forms and the Langlands program pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Arithmetic geometry, algebraic number theory, representation theory, and Lie theory and topological groups have their own topics.
