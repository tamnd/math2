## Proof

Let $T$ be complete with infinite models in a countable signature.
For tuples $\bar a$ in $M$ and $\bar b$ in $N$, the map $\bar a \mapsto \bar b$ is a [[partial elementary map|XTNYY0WRQ5]] exactly when $\operatorname{tp}^M(\bar a) = \operatorname{tp}^N(\bar b)$.

### Key step

Let $M$ be atomic and $N \models T$.
Let $\bar a \in M^k$ and $\bar b \in N^k$ have the same type, and let $c \in M$.
We find $d \in N$ with $\operatorname{tp}^M(\bar a, c) = \operatorname{tp}^N(\bar b, d)$.

1. $M$ is atomic, so some formula $\varphi(\bar x, y)$ isolates $\operatorname{tp}^M(\bar a, c)$.
2. $M \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$. This formula is in the type of $\bar a$, so $N \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar b, y)$. Choose $d$ with $N \models \varphi(\bar b, d)$.
3. $\operatorname{tp}^N(\bar b, d)$ contains $\varphi$, and $\varphi$ implies every formula of $\operatorname{tp}^M(\bar a, c)$ modulo $T$. Two complete types with this relation are equal.

### Atomic and countable implies prime

1. Let $M = \set{m_0, m_1, \dots}$ be countable and atomic, and $N \models T$.
2. The empty tuples have the same type in $M$ and $N$, because $T$ is complete.
3. The key step gives $d_0, d_1, \dots$ in $N$ with $\operatorname{tp}^M(m_0, \dots, m_k) = \operatorname{tp}^N(d_0, \dots, d_k)$ for all $k$.
4. The map $m_i \mapsto d_i$ preserves every formula, so it is an [[elementary embedding|CGH8C8EA9N]].

### Countable atomic models are isomorphic

Let $M$ and $N$ be countable and atomic.
Use the [[back-and-forth method|6F2DB4HTJR]], with the key step for $M$ in the forth steps and the key step with $M$ and $N$ swapped in the back steps.
The union of the maps is a bijection that preserves all formulas, so it is an isomorphism.

### Prime implies countable and atomic

1. By the [[Löwenheim-Skolem theorem|484M0TZ1S7]], $T$ has a countable model $N_0$. A prime $M$ embeds into $N_0$, so $M$ is countable.
2. Suppose that some $\bar a \in M^n$ has a type $p$ that is not isolated.
3. The [[omitting types theorem|7MFGBVDVG9]] gives a countable model $N$ of $T$ that omits $p$.
4. An elementary embedding $f : M \to N$ keeps types, so $f(\bar a)$ realizes $p$ in $N$. This contradicts step 3, so $M$ is atomic.

Clause 3 of the statement follows from clauses 1 and 2.

## Idea

A complete formula is a recipe for a tuple that works in every model.
Following the recipes one element at a time embeds an atomic model into any model, and doing so in both directions gives an isomorphism.
In the other direction, a non-isolated type can be omitted, so a model that embeds everywhere cannot realize it.

## Variants

The same key step, with types over a set $A$, proves the uniqueness of countable atomic models over $A$.
The proof of homogeneity starts the back-and-forth with two tuples of the same type in $M = N$ instead of the empty tuples.
