## Example

Let $A = I_2$ and $A' = \operatorname{diag}(1, 2)$ over $\Q$.
We show that they are not [[congruent|NZJDNC6EP6]] over $\Q$, but that they are congruent over $\R$.

1. Suppose that $A' = P^T A P$ with $P$ an invertible rational matrix.
2. By part 5 of the [[invariance statement|~12295b2d]], $\det A' = (\det P)^2 \det A$. So $2 = (\det P)^2$.
3. The number $\det P$ is rational, since $P$ has rational entries.
4. But $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, so no rational number has square $2$. This contradicts step 2.
5. So $A$ and $A'$ are not congruent over $\Q$.
6. Over $\R$, take $P = \operatorname{diag}(1, \sqrt{2})$. Then $P^T I_2 P = \operatorname{diag}(1, 2)$, so the two matrices are congruent over $\R$.

## Remarks

The example shows that congruence depends on the field.
Both matrices are symmetric, of rank $2$ and positive definite, so rank and signature do not separate them over $\Q$.
The determinant modulo squares, the discriminant, separates them.
If we replace $2$ by $4$, the matrices become congruent over $\Q$ with $P = \operatorname{diag}(1, 2)$.
If we replace $2$ by $5$, the same argument shows that they are not congruent over $\Q$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Over the real numbers we can stretch the second axis by $\sqrt{2}$, and the form $x^2 + y^2$ becomes $x^2 + 2y^2$.
Over the rational numbers we cannot use $\sqrt{2}$.
The determinant shows that no other rational change of coordinates works either.

### L2 human

The test with the determinant works as follows.

1. Compute $\det A = 1$ and $\det A' = 2$.
2. Compute the ratio $2 / 1 = 2$.
3. Ask whether the ratio is a square in the field. In $\Q$ it is not, in $\R$ it is.

The typical use is a fast proof that two forms are not equivalent over $\Q$ or over a finite field.
A trap: the test works only in one direction.
Equal square classes of determinants do not prove congruence.
