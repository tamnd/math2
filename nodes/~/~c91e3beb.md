## Statement

Let $V$ be a real inner product space of finite dimension $n \ge 1$, let $u \in V$ with $u \neq 0$, and let $s_u(x) = x - 2\frac{\ip{x}{u}}{\ip{u}{u}}u$ be the [[reflection|025158VEYZ]] in $u^\perp$.

1. $s_u$ is linear, $s_u(x) = x$ for each $x \in u^\perp$, and $s_u(u) = -u$.
2. $s_u \circ s_u = \id_V$, $s_u$ is self-adjoint, and $s_u$ is a [[linear isometry|0717XXPWM6]].
3. $\det s_u = -1$.
4. For a unit vector $u \in \R^n$, the matrix of $s_u$ is $H = I_n - 2uu^T$. It is symmetric and [[orthogonal|0QRBVFJ50X]], with $\det H = -1$.
5. If $x, y \in V$ with $\norm{x} = \norm{y}$ and $x \neq y$, then $s_{x - y}(x) = y$ and $s_{x - y}(y) = x$.
6. Each $Q \in O(n)$ is a product of reflections of $\R^n$, where $I_n$ is the empty product. So the reflections generate the [[orthogonal group|~dc22fe40]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Part 5 needs $\norm{x} = \norm{y}$. An isometry cannot send $x = (1, 0)$ to $y = (2, 0)$.
- Part 5 needs $x \neq y$, because $s_0$ is not defined. For $x = y$, the identity already sends $x$ to $y$.
- The formula needs a real inner product, or a Hermitian one. For a form with $\ip{u}{u} = 0$, the formula is not defined.

### Consequences

- The proof of part 6 gives at most $n$ reflections for each $Q \in O(n)$. This is the real case of the [[Cartan-Dieudonné theorem|0VMWGYY35Q]].
- An element of $SO(n)$ is a product of an even number of reflections, and an element with determinant $-1$ is a product of an odd number, by part 3.
- Part 5 is the basic step of the Householder QR decomposition: one reflection sends a column $x$ to $\norm{x}e_1$.

### Converse

An orthogonal map $T$ with $T^2 = \id$ and $\det T = -1$ is not always a reflection.
The map $-I_3$ satisfies all three conditions, but it fixes only $0$.
The correct converse: an orthogonal map that fixes a hyperplane pointwise, and is not the identity, is the reflection in this hyperplane.

### Generalizations

- For a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form $B$ over a field of characteristic not $2$, the map $x \mapsto x - 2\frac{B(x, u)}{B(u, u)}u$ with $B(u, u) \neq 0$ has the same properties. The general Cartan-Dieudonné theorem uses these reflections.
- In a complex inner product space, $s_u(x) = x - 2\frac{\ip{x}{u}}{\ip{u}{u}}u$ is unitary and self-adjoint, with $\det s_u = -1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A reflection keeps the mirror fixed and flips the direction across the mirror.
Two flips in the same mirror undo each other.
For two arrows of the same length, there is always a mirror that swaps them: the mirror halfway between them.
With enough mirrors, one after the other, we get every rotation and every mirror image of space.

### L2 human

Worked example: we find a reflection that sends $x = (3, 4)$ to $y = (5, 0)$.

1. $\norm{x} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5 = \norm{y}$, so part 5 applies.
2. $u = x - y = (-2, 4)$, and $\ip{u}{u} = 20$.
3. $s_u(x) = x - 2\frac{\ip{x}{u}}{20}u = (3, 4) - 2 \cdot \frac{-6 + 16}{20}(-2, 4) = (3, 4) - (-2, 4) = (5, 0)$.
4. The matrix is $I - \frac{2}{20}uu^T = \frac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 4 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$, with determinant $\frac{1}{25}(-9 - 16) = -1$.

The typical use: to bring a vector onto a coordinate axis with an orthogonal matrix, use one reflection.
A trap: $s_{x - y}$ swaps $x$ and $y$, but $s_{x + y}$ sends $x$ to $-y$.

### L3 human

The theorem shows that $O(n)$ is generated by a single conjugacy class, the reflections.
The determinant gives the parity of the number of reflections, which is the homomorphism $O(n) \to \set{\pm 1}$.
The finite subgroups of $O(n)$ generated by reflections are the finite reflection groups, which belong to the area of group theory.
