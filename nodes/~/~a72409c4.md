## Example

Part A: the bilattice $\mathrm{FOUR}_2$.
On $\mathbf{4} = \set{\mathbf{N}, \mathbf{F}, \mathbf{T}, \mathbf{B}}$ take two orders.

1. The information order: $x \le_i y$ iff $x \subseteq y$. So $\mathbf{N}$ is the bottom, $\mathbf{B}$ is the top, and $\mathbf{T}$, $\mathbf{F}$ are incomparable.
2. The truth order: $x \le_t y$ iff $T \in x$ implies $T \in y$ and $F \in y$ implies $F \in x$. So $\mathbf{F}$ is the bottom, $\mathbf{T}$ is the top, and $\mathbf{N}$, $\mathbf{B}$ are incomparable.
3. Both orders are [[lattices|WH4SFAACAS]], and $\land$, $\lor$ of [[FDE|~95890a5e]] are the meet and join for $\le_t$. For example $\mathbf{N} \land \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{F}$, the only common lower bound of $\mathbf{N}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ for $\le_t$.
4. Negation reverses $\le_t$ and keeps $\le_i$: it swaps $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{F}$ and fixes $\mathbf{N}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.

Part B: the Routley star.
A *star model* is a set $W$ of worlds, a map $* \colon W \to W$ with $a^{**} = a$, and a set $V(p) \subseteq W$ for each variable $p$.
Truth at a world $a$ is defined by: $p$ is true at $a$ iff $a \in V(p)$; $A \land B$ and $A \lor B$ by the classical clauses at $a$; $\neg A$ is true at $a$ iff $A$ is not true at $a^*$.

5. Fix a world $a$ and put $v(A) = \set{T : A \text{ true at } a} \cup \set{F : A \text{ not true at } a^*}$.
6. Negation: $T \in v(\neg A)$ iff $\neg A$ is true at $a$ iff $A$ is not true at $a^*$ iff $F \in v(A)$.
7. Also $F \in v(\neg A)$ iff $\neg A$ is not true at $a^*$ iff $A$ is true at $a^{**} = a$ iff $T \in v(A)$.
8. Conjunction: $T \in v(A \land B)$ iff both are true at $a$; $F \in v(A \land B)$ iff one of them is not true at $a^*$, iff $F \in v(A)$ or $F \in v(B)$.
9. Disjunction is dual to step 8.
10. So $v$ is a valuation in the Belnap-Dunn tables, and "$A$ true at $a$" means "$T \in v(A)$".
11. Concrete case: $W = \set{a, b}$, $a^* = b$, $b^* = a$, $V(p) = \set{a}$, $V(q) = \emptyset$. Then $p$ and $\neg p$ are true at $a$, and $q$ is not, so $v(p) = \mathbf{B}$ and explosion fails at $a$.

## Remarks

1. Conversely, every valuation $v$ in $\mathbf{4}$ arises from the two-world model with $a \in V(p)$ iff $T \in v(p)$ and $b \in V(p)$ iff $F \notin v(p)$.
2. So validity in all star models at all worlds is the same relation as $\models_{\mathsf{FDE}}$.
3. A world $a$ with $a^* = a$ is classical at $a$; worlds with $a^* \ne a$ produce gluts or gaps.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The four values can be ordered by how true they are, or by how much we were told.
In the first order, true is at the top and false at the bottom.
In the second, "both" is at the top and "nothing" at the bottom.
The star trick checks "not $A$" at a partner world, which can make $A$ and "not $A$" true together.

### L2 human

1. Build the truth order from the two rules "more $T$ is better" and "less $F$ is better".
2. Read $\land$ and $\lor$ as meet and join in this order.
3. For the star semantics, compute truth at $a$ and at $a^*$, and pair them as in step 5.

The typical use is to move between algebraic and world-based counter-models.
A trap: the star map must satisfy $a^{**} = a$; otherwise double negation fails, and the semantics is no longer the one of $\mathsf{FDE}$.
