## Example

We list standard first-order [[theories|92SWXST1ZM]], each with its [[signature|WDVVEYD6AT]] and axioms, and check for each that its models are the intended structures.

1. *Groups*, $\set{\cdot, {}^{-1}, e}$: associativity, $x \cdot e = x = e \cdot x$, $x \cdot x^{-1} = e = x^{-1} \cdot x$, all universally quantified. The models are the [[groups|G5S1M9]]; add $x \cdot y = y \cdot x$ for abelian groups.
2. *Rings*, $\set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$: abelian group axioms for $+$, associativity of $\cdot$, the unit $1$ and both distributive laws. The models are the [[rings|0B1261B5ED]] with unit.
3. *Fields*: the ring axioms, $x \cdot y = y \cdot x$, $0 \ne 1$ and $\forall x \, (x \ne 0 \to \exists y \, (x \cdot y = 1))$. Add $p \cdot 1 = 0$ for characteristic $p$, or $\neg(p \cdot 1 = 0)$ for every prime $p$ for characteristic $0$.
4. *Linear orders*, $\set{<}$: irreflexivity, transitivity and $x < y \lor x = y \lor y < x$. Add density, no least and no greatest element, and at least two elements for [[DLO|2W4S2H4VYA]].
5. *Graphs*, $\set{E}$: $\neg E(x, x)$ and $E(x, y) \to E(y, x)$. The models are the simple undirected [[graphs|00C0BR7D7H]].
6. *Algebraically closed fields*: the field axioms and, for each $d \ge 1$, "every monic polynomial of degree $d$ has a root".
7. *Real closed fields*, $\set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1, <}$: ordered field axioms, every positive element is a square, and every polynomial of odd degree has a root.
8. *Peano arithmetic*, $\set{0, S, +, \cdot, <}$: the defining equations of $S$, $+$ and $\cdot$, and the induction schema, one axiom $\varphi(0) \land \forall x \, (\varphi(x) \to \varphi(S x)) \to \forall x \, \varphi(x)$ for each formula $\varphi$.

## Remarks

### Vector spaces

For a fixed field $K$, a [[vector space|000AYPA9AJ]] over $K$ is a one-sorted structure in the signature $\set{+, -, 0} \cup \set{\lambda_k : k \in K}$, with one unary function $\lambda_k$ for scalar multiplication by $k$.
The axioms are those of abelian groups and, for all $k, l \in K$, the sentences $\lambda_k(x + y) = \lambda_k(x) + \lambda_k(y)$, $\lambda_{k + l}(x) = \lambda_k(x) + \lambda_l(x)$, $\lambda_{kl}(x) = \lambda_k(\lambda_l(x))$ and $\lambda_1(x) = x$.
For infinite $K$ this is an infinite signature.
To treat all fields at once, use a [[two-sorted signature|Z8Z0HGXK6D]], with one sort for scalars and one for vectors.

### Finite and infinite axiomatizations

Groups, rings, fields, linear orders, DLO and graphs are finitely axiomatizable.
Fields of characteristic $0$, algebraically closed fields, real closed fields, Peano arithmetic and infinite-dimensional vector spaces use infinite schemas.
For algebraically closed fields and Peano arithmetic, no finite axiomatization exists.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A first-order theory is a list of rules written in a fixed formal language.
The rules for groups say how multiplication behaves; the rules for orders say how "less than" behaves.
Some lists are short, like the group rules, and some need infinitely many rules, like "every polynomial has a root", which needs one rule for each degree.

### L2 human

We check that $(\Z, <)$ is a model of the theory of linear orders and not of DLO.

1. Irreflexivity: $n < n$ is false for every integer $n$.
2. Transitivity: $m < n$ and $n < k$ give $m < k$.
3. Totality: for integers $m, n$, one of $m < n$, $m = n$, $n < m$ holds.
4. Density fails at $0 < 1$, so $(\Z, <)$ is not a model of DLO.

The typical use of the list is as a stock of examples for every later notion.
A trap is to put "every element has finite order" or "the order is complete" into a first-order theory; these are not first-order.
