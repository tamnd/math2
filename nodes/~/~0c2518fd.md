## Example

We solve $2x + y = 5$ and $5x + 3y = 13$.

1. The system is $A(x, y)^{\mathsf{T}} = (5, 13)^{\mathsf{T}}$ with $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. The determinant is $2 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 5 = 1$, so the [[2 × 2 formula|8XWC7RKNN1]] gives $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. Check: $AA^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 - 5 & -2 + 2 \\ 15 - 15 & -5 + 6 \end{pmatrix} = I_2$.
4. $(x, y)^{\mathsf{T}} = A^{-1}(5, 13)^{\mathsf{T}} = (15 - 13, \; -25 + 26)^{\mathsf{T}} = (2, 1)^{\mathsf{T}}$.
5. Check in the equations: $2 \cdot 2 + 1 = 5$ and $5 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 1 = 13$.

## Remarks

By the [[solution theorem|3RQX3T70NX]], $(2, 1)$ is the only solution.
For a new right-hand side, say $(1, 0)$, the solution is column 1 of $A^{-1}$, namely $(3, -5)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We write the two equations as one matrix equation, find the inverse matrix, and multiply.
The answer is $x = 2$ and $y = 1$.

### L2 human

The method uses three steps: write the matrix, invert it, multiply.
A trap: the vector $(5, 13)$ must stand to the right of $A^{-1}$.
