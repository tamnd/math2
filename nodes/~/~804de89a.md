## Summary

A proof "without loss of generality" (wlog) proves a statement only in one special case $C$, and shows or states that every other case can be moved into $C$ by a symmetry that does not change the statement.
The symmetry is a change of the variables, such as exchanging $a$ and $b$, changing all signs, relabelling points, or translating and scaling a figure, under which the hypotheses and the conclusion stay the same.
If every case is the image of a case in $C$ under such a symmetry, a proof in $C$ proves the statement in all cases.
The method shortens a [[proof by cases|0WCP0PVET6]] in which the cases are copies of each other.

## Motivation

Many statements treat their variables alike.
The identity $\max(a, b) + \min(a, b) = a + b$ does not change when $a$ and $b$ are exchanged.
A proof by cases would treat $a \leq b$ and $b \leq a$, and the second case would repeat the first word for word with the letters exchanged.
"Without loss of generality, $a \leq b$" records that the second case is the first one after a renaming, and it saves the repetition.
In geometry, coordinates can often be chosen so that a point is at the origin or a circle has radius $1$, and this choice is also a wlog step.

## Notation

The phrase is written "without loss of generality", "wlog", "WLOG" or "w.l.o.g."; see [[mathematical abbreviations|4ZNYMYRRY3]] and [[a sentence with the abbreviations s.t., wlog and iff|K35RJ9YHC0]].
Other forms are "by symmetry, we may assume that ..." and "we may assume that ...".
A careful proof names the symmetry: "Since both sides are symmetric in $a$ and $b$, we may assume $a \leq b$."
In Lean, the Mathlib tactic `wlog h : a ≤ b` creates two goals: the reduction, which derives the case `¬ a ≤ b` from the case `a ≤ b`, and the main goal with the hypothesis `h : a ≤ b`.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[The sum of the maximum and the minimum of two numbers|~4b41ccb4]]: wlog $a \leq b$, since both sides are symmetric in $a$ and $b$.
2. [[A square root inequality by symmetry|~997a8101]]: $\left|\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y}\right| \leq \sqrt{|x - y|}$ for $x, y \geq 0$, wlog $x \geq y$.
3. For the triangle inequality $|x + y| \leq |x| + |y|$, wlog $x + y \geq 0$: replacing $x$ and $y$ by $-x$ and $-y$ changes neither side, and it turns a negative sum into a positive one.
4. For integers $a$ and $b$, $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, a) = \gcd(|a|, |b|)$. So in a proof about the [[greatest common divisor|02WF878BH1]], wlog $a \geq b \geq 0$.
5. In a statement about a circle that does not change under translation and scaling, wlog the circle is the unit circle with centre at the origin.

### Non-examples

1. [[A wlog step for a statement that is not symmetric|~efedf66c]]: "wlog $|a| \geq |b|$" for the false claim $a^2 - b^2 \geq 0$. Exchanging $a$ and $b$ changes the sign of $a^2 - b^2$.
2. "For every real $x$, $x^2 + x \geq 0$. Wlog $x \geq 0$, and then both terms are at least $0$." The statement is not invariant under $x \mapsto -x$, and it fails at $x = -\frac{1}{2}$, where $x^2 + x = -\frac{1}{4}$.
3. "Wlog $n$ is even" in a statement about all integers, when no symmetry of the statement turns an odd $n$ into an even one. The odd case then needs its own proof.

### Basic properties

1. A wlog step is valid when there is a transformation that keeps the statement true or false, and that maps every case into the chosen case.
2. A wlog step is a proof by cases in which every case except one is reduced to that one. The reduction is part of the proof, even when it is only one sentence.
3. The transformation must keep both the hypotheses and the conclusion. A symmetry of the conclusion alone is not enough.
4. Several wlog steps can be combined, but each later step must keep the earlier assumptions. After "wlog $a \leq b$", the step "wlog $a \geq 0$ by changing all signs" is wrong, because changing the signs reverses $a \leq b$.

### History

John Harrison studied the formal content of wlog arguments in his paper "Without loss of generality" at the conference TPHOLs in 2009, and implemented support for them in the HOL Light proof assistant.
Mathlib, the library of the Lean proof assistant, provides the tactic `wlog` for the same purpose.

### Common errors

1. Using wlog for a statement that is not symmetric, as in non-examples 1 and 2.
2. Not saying which symmetry justifies the step, so that a reader cannot check it.
3. Combining wlog assumptions that cannot hold together, as in basic property 4.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two friends compare their heights.
To say something about "the taller one and the shorter one", we can just call the taller one Anna.
If Ben is the taller one, we exchange the names, and nothing else changes.
So we lose nothing by assuming that Anna is the taller one.
That is what "without loss of generality" means: a choice that only fixes the names.

### L2 human

We prove: for all real $x$ and $y$, $x^2 + y^2 \geq 2|xy|$.

1. Replacing $x$ by $-x$ or $y$ by $-y$ changes neither $x^2 + y^2$ nor $|xy|$.
2. So wlog $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$. Then $|xy| = xy$.
3. Now $x^2 + y^2 - 2xy = (x - y)^2 \geq 0$.
4. So $x^2 + y^2 \geq 2xy = 2|xy|$, and by step 1 the inequality holds for all real $x$ and $y$.

The typical use: when absolute values or squares make the signs irrelevant, assume that all variables are at least $0$.

A trap: step 1 must check both sides.
For the claim $x^2 + y^2 \geq -3xy$, the step "wlog $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$" is wrong, because replacing $x$ by $-x$ changes $-3xy$.
That claim holds when $x, y \geq 0$, but it fails at $x = 1$, $y = -1$, where it says $2 \geq 3$.

### L3 human

In general, a group $G$ acts on the set of cases, the statement is invariant under the action, and a wlog step chooses a representative of each orbit.
A proof on a set that meets every orbit proves the statement everywhere.
Normal forms in algebra are wlog steps of this kind: a statement about matrices that is invariant under conjugation can be proved for matrices in a normal form, such as Jordan form over $\mathbb{C}$.
In formal proof, the reduction step that a book leaves to the reader must be proved, and this is the part that Harrison's work and the Lean `wlog` tactic make explicit.
Symmetry arguments are related to invariants and extremal choices, which are treated in the topic [[counting, invariant and extremal arguments|GB14W4SYPK]].
