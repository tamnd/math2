## Example

Consider the system over $\R$
$$x + y = 2, \qquad x - y = 0, \qquad x + 2y = 4.$$
It has $3$ equations and $2$ unknowns.
We show by [[Gaussian elimination|0JVJ2WCXZH]] that it is [[inconsistent|0X6V9B1V10]].

1. The augmented matrix is $\left[\begin{array}{rr|r} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 4 \end{array}\right]$.
2. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - R_1$ gives $(0, -2 \mid -2)$.
3. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_1$ gives $(0, 1 \mid 2)$.
4. $R_2 \leftarrow -\tfrac12 R_2$ gives $(0, 1 \mid 1)$.
5. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_2$ gives $(0, 0 \mid 1)$.
6. The last row says $0x + 0y = 1$, so the system has no solution.

## Remarks

Any two of the three lines do meet.
The first two meet at $(1, 1)$, which fails the third equation: $1 + 2 = 3 \ne 4$.
The first and third meet at $(0, 2)$, which fails the second equation: $0 - 2 \ne 0$.
The second and third meet at $(\tfrac43, \tfrac43)$, which fails the first equation: $\tfrac83 \ne 2$.
So the lines form a triangle, and no point lies on all three.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw the three lines: they make a small triangle.
Each corner lies on two lines but not on the third.
So no point is on all three lines, and the system has no solution.

### L2 human

The typical use of this pattern is to test whether measured data fit a model exactly.

1. Reduce the augmented matrix.
2. Look for a row $(0, \dots, 0 \mid c)$ with $c \ne 0$.
3. If you find one, the data do not fit exactly; [[least squares|0BYTQ4X1HC]] gives the best fit.

A trap: checking only two of the equations would wrongly suggest that $(1, 1)$ is a solution.
