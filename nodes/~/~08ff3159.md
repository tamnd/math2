## Example

Statement: for every [[integer|0C4JS9RF5M]] $n$, if $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd.
Definition used: an integer $x$ is odd when $x = 2m + 1$ for some integer $m$.
We give a [[direct proof|0NTF6DMVAP]].

1. Let $n$ be an integer, and assume that $n$ is odd. This is the hypothesis.
2. By the definition of odd, there is an integer $k$ with $n = 2k + 1$.
3. Square both sides: $n^2 = (2k + 1)^2 = 4k^2 + 4k + 1$.
4. Factor $2$ from the first two terms: $n^2 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1$.
5. Put $m = 2k^2 + 2k$. Then $m$ is an integer, because sums and products of integers are integers.
6. So $n^2 = 2m + 1$, and $n^2$ is odd by the definition of odd.

The proof starts from the hypothesis in step 1 and ends with the conclusion in step 6.
It never assumes anything about $n^2$.

## Remarks

The example shows the three parts of most direct proofs: unfold the definition in the hypothesis (step 2), compute (steps 3 and 4), and fold the result into the definition of the conclusion (steps 5 and 6).

A change of the exponent keeps the method.
For $n^3$ we get $(2k + 1)^3 = 2(4k^3 + 6k^2 + 3k) + 1$, so the cube of an odd integer is odd.
A change of the hypothesis changes the method.
The statement "if $n^2$ is odd, then $n$ is odd" is true, but a direct proof is awkward, because "$n^2 = 2m + 1$" does not give a formula for $n$.
Its [[proof by contrapositive|0BB5TPGQ1N]] is short: if $n$ is even, then $n = 2k$ and $n^2 = 2(2k^2)$ is even.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the odd number $5$.
It is $2 \cdot 2 + 1$, one more than an even number.
Its square is $25 = 24 + 1$, again one more than an even number.
The proof does this calculation for a general odd number $2k + 1$.
The square is $4k^2 + 4k + 1$, and the first two terms together are even.
So the square is one more than an even number, which means it is odd.

### L2 human

We check the proof on $n = -7$, to see that it also covers negative integers.

1. $n = -7$ is odd, because $-7 = 2 \cdot (-4) + 1$. So $k = -4$.
2. The formula gives $m = 2k^2 + 2k = 2 \cdot 16 - 8 = 24$.
3. Then $2m + 1 = 49$, and indeed $(-7)^2 = 49$.

The typical use of this fact is as a lemma: its contrapositive "if $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even" is a step in the proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

A trap: writing "$n = 2n + 1$" reuses the letter $n$ and gives the false equation $n = -1$.
The integer $k$ in step 2 must be a new letter.
