## Statement

Let $\Gamma$ be a set of propositional formulas and $\varphi$ a formula.
$\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{i}} \varphi$ means derivability in the Hilbert system of [[intuitionistic logic|52XCYMK0WW]] from $\Gamma$.
$\Gamma \Vdash \varphi$ means: in every [[intuitionistic Kripke model|~11b532e9]], every world that forces all members of $\Gamma$ forces $\varphi$.

**Theorem.**

1. Soundness: if $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{i}} \varphi$, then $\Gamma \Vdash \varphi$.
2. Strong completeness: if $\Gamma \Vdash \varphi$, then $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{i}} \varphi$.
3. Finite model property: if $\mathsf{IPC} \nvdash \varphi$, then $\varphi$ fails at the root of a finite rooted model with at most $2^n$ worlds, where $n$ is the number of subformulas of $\varphi$.

The proof is in [[the proof by prime theories|~1962f700]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. The valuations must be up-sets. Without this, the axiom $p \to (q \to p)$ fails; see [[a valuation that is not an up-set|~ee20c6f6]].
2. The order must be transitive. Take a reflexive relation with $a \leq b \leq c$ but not $a \leq c$. Let $p$ be true only at $c$, and $q$ at $b$ and $c$.
3. Then $a$ forces $\neg p$, but $b$ does not, so the axiom instance $\neg p \to (q \to \neg p)$ fails at $a$.
4. Antisymmetry is not needed: preorders give the same valid formulas, since a preorder and its quotient poset force the same formulas.

### Consequences

1. $p \vee \neg p$, $\neg\neg p \to p$ and Peirce's law are not theorems of $\mathsf{IPC}$; see [[the two-point countermodel|~bc4c4586]].
2. $\mathsf{IPC}$ is decidable: one searches in parallel for a derivation and for a finite countermodel of bounded size.
3. $\mathsf{IPC}$ has the [[disjunction property|~59f11dc8]], by gluing models.
4. $\mathsf{IPC}$ is complete for the class of finite trees, since every finite rooted frame is the image of a finite tree under a map that preserves forcing.

### Converse

Soundness and completeness are converses of each other, and both hold.
The finite model property has no converse to state: a formula refuted on a finite model is a non-theorem by soundness.

### Generalizations

1. Intuitionistic predicate logic is complete for Kripke models with growing domains; see [[intuitionistic predicate logic|~d18966da]].
2. Many [[intermediate logics|~57af80cc]] are complete for classes of frames, such as [[Gödel-Dummett logic|~2b41c6c2]] for chains.
3. Not every intermediate logic is complete for a class of Kripke frames.

## History

Kripke proved the theorem in 1965, in "Semantical analysis of intuitionistic logic I".
The proof with prime theories, as given here, is the standard proof of later textbooks.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem says that the maps of growing knowledge capture exactly the laws of intuitionistic logic.
Every derivable formula holds at every state of every map.
And every formula that can not be derived fails at some state of some map, even a finite map.
For example, "$p$ or not $p$" fails on a map with two states.

### L2 human

1. To show that $\mathsf{IPC} \nvdash \neg\neg p \to p$, take worlds $u \leq v$ with $p$ true at $v$.
2. Every world above $u$ sees $v$, so $u \Vdash \neg\neg p$.
3. $u \nVdash p$, so $u \nVdash \neg\neg p \to p$.
4. By soundness, $\neg\neg p \to p$ is not derivable.

The typical use of the theorem is in this direction: a small countermodel proves non-derivability.
A trap: completeness gives a countermodel, but the canonical one is infinite; finite countermodels need the finite model property.

### L3 human

The completeness proof is the intuitionistic form of the Henkin method: maximal consistent sets become prime theories, and they are ordered by inclusion.
The canonical model is the prime filter space of the Lindenbaum algebra, so the proof is the representation theorem for Heyting algebras in disguise.
The finite model property comes from filtration, as in modal logic; see [[filtration|6ZZSHAKG5F]].
