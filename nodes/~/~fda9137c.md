## Statement

Let $G$ be a [[solvable group|~d517865e]] and let $H$ be a [[subgroup|~b600248b]] of $G$.
Then $H$ is solvable.

## Remarks

A proof uses the [[derived series|~fc177e14]]: $H^{(i)} \subseteq G^{(i)}$ for each $i$, so $G^{(n)} = \{e\}$ implies $H^{(n)} = \{e\}$.
More generally, if there is an injective homomorphism from $H$ into a solvable group, then $H$ is solvable.
For example, each subgroup of $S_4$ is solvable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Commutators of elements of $H$ are also commutators in $G$.
So the derived series of $H$ stays inside the derived series of $G$.
If the larger series reaches the identity, the smaller one does too.
