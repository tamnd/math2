## Example

Let $S_3$ be the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] on $\{1, 2, 3\}$.
It has six elements: $e$, $(1\,2)$, $(1\,3)$, $(2\,3)$, $(1\,2\,3)$ and $(1\,3\,2)$.
We compose from right to left: in $\sigma\tau$, we apply $\tau$ first.
Let $a = (1\,2)$ and $b = (1\,2\,3)$.
We show that the [[subgroup generated|0RP7VWQ0YN]] by $a$ and $b$ is all of $S_3$.

1. $e$, $a$ and $b$ lie in $\langle a, b \rangle$.
2. $b^2 = (1\,2\,3)(1\,2\,3)$ sends $1 \mapsto 2 \mapsto 3$, $2 \mapsto 3 \mapsto 1$ and $3 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 2$. So $b^2 = (1\,3\,2) \in \langle a, b \rangle$.
3. $ba = (1\,2\,3)(1\,2)$. Apply $(1\,2)$ first: $1 \mapsto 2 \mapsto 3$, $2 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 2$ and $3 \mapsto 3 \mapsto 1$. So $ba = (1\,3) \in \langle a, b \rangle$.
4. $ab = (1\,2)(1\,2\,3)$. Apply $(1\,2\,3)$ first: $1 \mapsto 2 \mapsto 1$, $2 \mapsto 3 \mapsto 3$ and $3 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 2$. So $ab = (2\,3) \in \langle a, b \rangle$.
5. Steps 1 to 4 give all six elements of $S_3$. So $\langle a, b \rangle = S_3$.

## Remarks

The set $\{(1\,2), (1\,2\,3)\}$ is a [[generating set|0MBV2VHMXA]] of $S_3$.
No single element generates $S_3$, because $S_3$ is not abelian and [[cyclic groups are abelian|04RG02S6BA]].
For example, $ab = (2\,3)$ and $ba = (1\,3)$ are different.
So $2$ is the least size of a generating set of $S_3$.
For each $n \geq 2$, the transposition $(1\,2)$ and the $n$-cycle $(1\,2\,\cdots\,n)$ generate $S_n$.
For $n \geq 3$, the group $S_n$ is not abelian, so it also needs two generators.
Each subgroup of $S_3$ generated by one of $a$ or $b$ is proper: $\langle a \rangle = \{e, a\}$ and $\langle b \rangle = \{e, b, b^2\}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put three cards in a row.
One move swaps the first two cards.
Another move takes the card at the front to position $2$, the card at position $2$ to position $3$, and the card at position $3$ to the front.
By doing these two moves again and again, in a good order, we can put the cards in each of the six possible orders.
So the two moves generate all the shuffles of three cards.

### L2 human

We use the products of $a$ and $b$ to reach new elements.

1. The powers of $b$ give $e$, $b$ and $b^2$, the three rotations of a triangle with corners $1$, $2$ and $3$.
2. The products $a$, $ba$ and $ab$ give the three transpositions, the three reflections of the triangle.
3. Together these are the six elements of $S_3$.

The typical use is to give a large group by two small elements.
For example, two homomorphisms from $S_3$ to another group are equal if they agree on $a$ and on $b$.

A trap: $ab$ and $ba$ are different elements.
A computation with the wrong order of composition gives $ab = (1\,3)$, which is wrong with our convention.
