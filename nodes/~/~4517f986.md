## Proof

Write $\vdash$ for $\vdash_{\mathsf{IPC}}$ and $\mathrel{|\!\sim}$ for admissibility in $\mathsf{IPC}$.
Part A proves item 1 in full, and Part B proves item 2 from three lemmas, of which one is cited.

Part A: each $V_n$ is admissible.

1. Take an instance of $V_n$, with formulas $A_i$, $B_i$, $A_{n+1}$, $A_{n+2}$, $C$ in place of the variables. Let $E = \bigwedge_{i=1}^{n} (A_i \to B_i)$.
2. Assume $\vdash (E \to A_{n+1} \vee A_{n+2}) \vee C$. By the [[disjunction property|~59f11dc8]], $\vdash C$ or $\vdash E \to A_{n+1} \vee A_{n+2}$.
3. If $\vdash C$, then the conclusion is a theorem. So assume $\vdash E \to A_{n+1} \vee A_{n+2}$.
4. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\nvdash E \to A_j$ for every $j \leq n+2$.
5. By [[Kripke completeness|~ac029000]], for each $j$ some world of some model forces $E$ and not $A_j$. Let $K_j$ be the submodel generated by that world, with root $k_j$.
6. Forcing at a world depends only on the worlds above it. So $k_j \Vdash E$ and $k_j \nVdash A_j$ in $K_j$.
7. Form a new model $K$: the disjoint union of $K_1, \dots, K_{n+2}$, with a new root $\rho$ below all worlds, and no atom true at $\rho$.
8. The valuation of $K$ is persistent, so $K$ is a [[Kripke model|~11b532e9]]. Each world of $K_j$ forces in $K$ the same formulas as in $K_j$.
9. Claim: $\rho \nVdash A_j$ for each $j$. Otherwise, by persistence, $k_j \Vdash A_j$, which contradicts step 6.
10. Claim: $\rho \Vdash E$. Fix $i \leq n$ and a world $w \geq \rho$.
11. If $w = \rho$, then $w \nVdash A_i$ by step 9, so the condition for $A_i \to B_i$ holds at $w$.
12. If $w$ lies in some $K_j$, then $k_j \leq w$, and $w \Vdash A_i \to B_i$ by step 6 and persistence. So $\rho \Vdash A_i \to B_i$.
13. By soundness and step 3, $\rho \Vdash E \to A_{n+1} \vee A_{n+2}$. With step 10, $\rho \Vdash A_{n+1}$ or $\rho \Vdash A_{n+2}$.
14. This contradicts step 9. So $\vdash E \to A_j$ for some $j$, and the conclusion of the instance is a theorem.

Part B: the $V_n$ form a basis.

15. The relation $\mathrel{|\!\sim}$ is a structural consequence relation: it is closed under substitution, weakening and composition. It contains $\vdash$ and, by Part A, every $V_n$.
16. So $A \vdash_V B$ implies $A \mathrel{|\!\sim} B$, since $\vdash_V$ is the smallest such relation.
17. For the converse, call $P$ *projective* if it has a unifier $\sigma$ with $P \vdash q \leftrightarrow \sigma q$ for every variable $q$.
18. Lemma 1, cited (Ghilardi): every formula $A$ has a finite set $\Pi(A)$ of projective formulas. Each $P \in \Pi(A)$ satisfies $P \vdash A$, and each unifier of $A$ unifies some $P \in \Pi(A)$.
19. Lemma 2: if $P$ is projective and $P \mathrel{|\!\sim} B$, then $P \vdash B$. Proof: take $\sigma$ as in step 17. Since $\vdash \sigma P$, admissibility gives $\vdash \sigma B$.
20. By induction on $B$, $P \vdash B \leftrightarrow \sigma B$, since each connective preserves provable equivalence under the hypothesis $P$. So $P \vdash B$.
21. Lemma 3, cited (Iemhoff): $A \vdash_V \bigvee \Pi(A)$, where the empty disjunction is $\bot$. This is the core of Iemhoff's argument, and we do not prove it.
22. Now let $A \mathrel{|\!\sim} B$, and let $P \in \Pi(A)$. Every unifier of $P$ unifies $A$, since $P \vdash A$, and so unifies $B$. So $P \mathrel{|\!\sim} B$.
23. By Lemma 2, $P \vdash B$ for every $P \in \Pi(A)$. By disjunction elimination, $\bigvee \Pi(A) \vdash B$.
24. With Lemma 3 and composition, $A \vdash_V B$.
25. Rules with several premises reduce to this case, since $A_1, \dots, A_k \mathrel{|\!\sim} B$ iff $A_1 \wedge \dots \wedge A_k \mathrel{|\!\sim} B$, and similarly for $\vdash_V$.

## Idea

Admissibility of $V_n$ is a gluing argument, as in the semantic proof of the disjunction property.
A new root below countermodels to all disjuncts of the conclusion forces the conjunction $E$, because $E$ is a conjunction of implications whose antecedents fail at the root.
For the basis, Ghilardi's lemma shows that $\bigvee \Pi(A)$ is the strongest admissible consequence of $A$.
Projective formulas behave like classical ones: for them, admissible and derivable consequences coincide.
Iemhoff's lemma says that the Visser rules suffice to pass from $A$ to $\bigvee \Pi(A)$.

## Variants

1. By Lemma 1, $A \mathrel{|\!\sim} \bigvee \Pi(A)$. So Lemma 3 is equivalent to item 2, given Lemmas 1 and 2.
2. Lemmas 1 and 2 already give a decision procedure for admissibility, since $\Pi(A)$ is computable; see [[Rybakov's theorem|~63370b14]].
3. The same gluing argument shows that the Kreisel-Putnam rule is admissible, with $\neg A$ in place of $E$.
