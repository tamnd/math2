## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite connected simple [[graph|~3f82cef4]] with [[maximum degree|~41541976]] $\Delta$.
Suppose that $G$ is not a [[complete graph|~b29ee1b7]] and not a [[cycle graph|~19143ce5]] of odd length.
Then $\chi(G) \le \Delta$.

## Remarks

A [[greedy colouring|~b2a39f97]] gives $\chi(G) \le \Delta + 1$ for every graph. Brooks' theorem improves this bound by $1$ in all other cases.
The two exceptions need $\Delta + 1$ colours: $\chi(K_n) = n$ with $\Delta = n - 1$, and an odd cycle has $\chi = 3$ with $\Delta = 2$.

## History

R. Leonard Brooks published the theorem in 1941.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If each vertex has at most $\Delta$ neighbours, then $\Delta + 1$ colours are always enough.
Brooks' theorem says that $\Delta$ colours are enough, except for complete graphs and odd cycles.
