## Statement

Let $A$ be a real $n \times n$ matrix with real eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$, which need not be distinct.
Suppose $\vec{v}_1, \dots, \vec{v}_n$ are [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]] [[eigenvectors|0B5H4208Z9]] of $A$, where $\vec{v}_k$ belongs to $\lambda_k$.
Then every solution of the [[homogeneous linear system with constant coefficients|0VJF99CZ0Z]] $\vec{x}' = A\vec{x}$ has the form
$$\vec{x}(t) = c_1 e^{\lambda_1 t} \vec{v}_1 + \dots + c_n e^{\lambda_n t} \vec{v}_n$$
for some constants $c_1, \dots, c_n$.

## Remarks

Each term $e^{\lambda_k t}\vec{v}_k$ is itself a solution, so every such combination solves the system.
The $n$ terms are independent solutions, so the formula gives the whole solution space.
