## Statement

Let $p$ be a prime number and let $n \ge 1$.
For each divisor $m$ of $n$, the field $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ has exactly one [[subfield|~00a26465]] of order $p^m$, namely $\{ a : a^{p^m} = a \}$.
It has no other subfields.

## Remarks

If $K$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ of order $p^m$, then $n = m \cdot [\mathbb{F}_{p^n} : K]$ by the [[tower law|~1fa33f36]], so $m$ divides $n$.
For example, the subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{64} = \mathbb{F}_{2^6}$ are $\mathbb{F}_2$, $\mathbb{F}_4$, $\mathbb{F}_8$ and $\mathbb{F}_{64}$.
The subfield of order $p^m$ is the fixed field of $\varphi^m$, where $\varphi$ is the [[Frobenius automorphism|~88b222eb]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The subfields of a finite field match the divisors of the exponent.
The field with $2^6$ elements contains the fields with $2^1$, $2^2$ and $2^3$ elements, but not one with $2^4$ elements.
