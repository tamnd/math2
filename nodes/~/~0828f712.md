## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and let $W$ be a [[linear subspace|~92144797]] of $V$.
Then $W$ is finite-dimensional, and $\dim W \le \dim V$, where $\dim$ is the [[dimension|~3d1088d4]].

## Remarks

A basis of $W$ is a linearly independent family in $V$, so it has at most $\dim V$ vectors.
Equality holds only when $W = V$, see [[a subspace of full dimension is the whole space|~c799afe4]].
For infinite-dimensional $V$, the inequality $\dim W \le \dim V$ of cardinals still holds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A subspace cannot have more independent directions than the space around it.
A plane in ordinary space has dimension $2$, which is at most $3$.
The only subspace with the same dimension is the whole space.
