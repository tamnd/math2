## Statement

Let $R$ be a ring and let $A$ and $B$ be [[upper triangular|0MKABWDRNC]] $n \times n$ matrices over $R$.
Then $AB$ is upper triangular, and its diagonal entries are $(AB)_{ii} = a_{ii} b_{ii}$ for $1 \le i \le n$.
If $A$ and $B$ are strictly upper triangular, so is $AB$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Both factors must be upper triangular. With $U = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and the lower triangular $L = U^{\mathsf{T}}$, the [[product UL|~23722604]] is $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
- Commutativity of $R$ is not used. The diagonal entry is $a_{ii} b_{ii}$ in this order.

### Consequences

- The upper triangular matrices of order $n$ form a subring of $M_n(R)$.
- By induction, $(A_1 \cdots A_k)_{ii} = (A_1)_{ii} \cdots (A_k)_{ii}$, and $(A^k)_{ii} = a_{ii}^k$.
- For a polynomial $p$ over a commutative ring, $p(A)$ is upper triangular with diagonal $p(a_{11}), \dots, p(a_{nn})$.
- The lower triangular case follows by the [[transpose of a product|09GK5PS824]]: $(AB)^{\mathsf{T}} = B^{\mathsf{T}} A^{\mathsf{T}}$.
- The map $A \mapsto (a_{11}, \dots, a_{nn})$ is a ring homomorphism from upper triangular matrices to $R^n$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When we multiply two upper triangular matrices, the zeros below the diagonal stay zero.
Each diagonal entry of the product is the product of the two diagonal entries in the same place.

### L2 human

In the sum $(AB)_{ij} = \sum_k a_{ik} b_{kj}$, the factor $a_{ik}$ needs $k \ge i$ and $b_{kj}$ needs $k \le j$.
For $i > j$ no $k$ satisfies both, and for $i = j$ only $k = i$ does; the [[proof|~5bdf0fb0]] writes this out.

### L3 human

The upper triangular matrices are the matrices that preserve the standard flag $V_1 \subset \cdots \subset V_n$, with $V_j = \langle e_1, \dots, e_j \rangle$.
Maps that preserve a flag compose to maps that preserve it, and they act on each $V_j / V_{j-1}$ by the scalar $a_{jj}$.
