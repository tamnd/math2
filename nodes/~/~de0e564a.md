## Statement

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [[independent|~296c063d]] real random variables with $E[X^2] < \infty$ and $E[Y^2] < \infty$.
Then $\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = 0$, so $X$ and $Y$ are uncorrelated.

## Remarks

Proof: by the [[product rule for independent variables|~6cdbb2b0]], $E[XY] = E[X] \, E[Y]$. So $\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X] \, E[Y] = 0$.
The converse is false. The node [[uncorrelated random variables that are not independent|~7cc645fa]] gives a counterexample.
For a pair $(X, Y)$ with a joint multivariate normal law, uncorrelated does imply independent.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If two variables do not influence each other at all, they have no linear relation either.
But "no linear relation" is a much weaker property than independence.
