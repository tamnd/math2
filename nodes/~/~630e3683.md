## Statement

Let $T$ be a [[complete theory|655K8Y7VS8]] in a countable first-order signature.
Let $I(T, \aleph_0)$ be the number of [[countable|01TQB88H4C]] models of $T$ up to isomorphism.
Then $I(T, \aleph_0) \le \aleph_1$ or $I(T, \aleph_0) = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

An outline of the proof is [[here|~8b0c6152]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Countable signature. The proof codes countable models as points of a Polish space, which needs countably many symbols.
- Completeness is not needed. The proof works for any countable theory, and for any sentence of the [[infinitary logic|PQG7VAZ1R2]] $L_{\omega_1 \omega}$.

### Consequences

- Under the [[continuum hypothesis|0MN2MER9WN]], the number of countable models is at most $\aleph_0$ or equal to $2^{\aleph_0}$.
- With [[Vaught's never-two theorem|~ea319e25]], the possible numbers are $1, 3, 4, 5, \dots$, $\aleph_0$, $\aleph_1$ and $2^{\aleph_0}$, and only $\aleph_1$ is in doubt when $\aleph_1 < 2^{\aleph_0}$.

### Converse

The values $1, 3, 4, \dots$, $\aleph_0$ and $2^{\aleph_0}$ occur for first-order theories, and $\aleph_1 < 2^{\aleph_0}$ is open; see the [[Vaught conjecture|YVBHE2SEE6]].
For sentences of $L_{\omega_1 \omega}$, a preprint of October 2026 gives a sentence with exactly $\aleph_1$ countable models and no perfect set of non-isomorphic ones.

### Generalizations

The proof gives more: if $I(T, \aleph_0) > \aleph_1$, there is a perfect set of pairwise non-isomorphic countable models.
The same dichotomy holds for any analytic equivalence relation on a Polish space: it has at most $\aleph_1$ classes, or a perfect set of pairwise inequivalent points.

## History

Michael Morley proved the theorem in 1970, in a paper on the number of countable models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Count the countable worlds of a theory.
Morley's theorem says that the count is either at most the first uncountable number, $\aleph_1$, or as large as possible, the number of real numbers.
If there is no size between these two, the count is countable or as large as possible.

### L2 human

We use the theorem with the never-two theorem to list the possible counts.

1. A finite count is $1$ or at least $3$, by Vaught's theorem.
2. An infinite count is $\aleph_0$, $\aleph_1$ or $2^{\aleph_0}$, by Morley's theorem.
3. Under the continuum hypothesis $\aleph_1 = 2^{\aleph_0}$, so the list is $1, 3, 4, \dots, \aleph_0, 2^{\aleph_0}$.

The typical use is to reduce the Vaught conjecture to the single case of $\aleph_1$ models.
A trap is to think that the theorem uses the continuum hypothesis; it is proved in ZFC.

### L3 human

The proof uses [[Scott sentences|KAWWZ2DF0G]] and the perfect set property of [[analytic sets|3RWWZYPAFV]].
Models are compared in countable fragments of $L_{\omega_1 \omega}$, one for each countable ordinal.
If some fragment has uncountably many types realized in countable models, a [[perfect set|H47ZGRR13V]] of types gives $2^{\aleph_0}$ models.
Otherwise each fragment contributes countably many classes, and there are $\aleph_1$ fragments.
