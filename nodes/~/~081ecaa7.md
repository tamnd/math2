## Summary

Algebraic geometry is the study of the solution sets of systems of polynomial equations.
Its basic object is an algebraic variety, the set of common zeros of a family of polynomials, and in the modern theory a scheme, a space that is built locally from commutative rings.
The subject connects algebra with geometry, and it has applications in number theory, in mathematical physics, and in coding theory and cryptography.
The Italian school studied curves and surfaces at the end of the 19th century, and Grothendieck rebuilt the foundations of the subject on schemes around 1960.
This topic collects the algebraic geometry pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Commutative algebra, arithmetic geometry, complex geometry and several complex variables, and algebraic combinatorics have their own topics.
