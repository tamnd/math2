## Example

Let $B$ be a [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]].
Put $x ; y = x \wedge y$, $\breve{x} = x$ and $1' = 1$.
We check [[the definition|RDSY11X6N2]].

1. Clause 1 holds by assumption.
2. $(B, \wedge, 1)$ is a monoid, since meet is associative and $1$ is its unit.
3. Clause 3 is the distributive law $(x \vee y) \wedge z = (x \wedge z) \vee (y \wedge z)$.
4. Clause 4 is trivial for the identity map.
5. Clause 5 reads $x \wedge y = y \wedge x$.
6. Clause 6 reads $x \wedge -(x \wedge y) \le -y$, and the left side is $x \wedge (-x \vee -y) = x \wedge -y$.

So $B$ is a [[relation algebra|GQK4DSA11K]].

## Remarks

1. These are exactly the relation algebras with $1' = 1$: if $1' = 1$, then $x ; y = x \wedge y$ and $\breve{x} = x$ follow from the axioms.
2. They are representable. By Stone's theorem $B$ embeds in $\mathcal{P}(U)$ for a set $U$; send $X \subseteq U$ to the relation $\set{(u, u) : u \in X}$.
Diagonal relations compose by intersection, are their own converses, and the image of $U$ is the identity relation.
So $B$ embeds in the relations below the identity of $U$, which form the product of the [[full relation algebras|~1daf4bfe]] on the one-point sets $\set{u}$.
3. The two-element case is the full relation algebra on a one-point set.
4. In a general relation algebra, the elements below $1'$ form a Boolean algebra of this kind, with $;$ equal to meet.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Any Boolean algebra is a relation algebra if one chains two elements by taking their meet and reverses each element by leaving it alone.
It is the algebra of relations that only relate each point to itself.

### L2 human

1. Take $B = \mathcal{P}(\set{1, 2})$.
2. $\set{1} ; \set{1, 2} = \set{1}$ and $\set{1} ; \set{2} = \emptyset$.
3. Under the embedding, $\set{1}$ becomes the relation $\set{(1, 1)}$ on $\set{1, 2}$, and $\set{(1, 1)} ; \set{(2, 2)} = \emptyset$ too.

The typical use: Boolean algebras are the degenerate relation algebras, and they help to test whether a law is specific to relations.
The trap: here $1' = 1$, while in the full relation algebra on a set with two or more points the identity is strictly below the top.
