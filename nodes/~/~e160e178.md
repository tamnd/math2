## Example

In the system SKS of [[deep inference|~110b1691]], contraction is a rule only for atoms:
$$\mathsf{ac}{\downarrow}: \quad a \vee a \ \text{ to } \ a .$$
We derive contraction for the conjunction $a \wedge b$, that is, a derivation from $(a \wedge b) \vee (a \wedge b)$ to $a \wedge b$.
The medial rule is
$$\mathsf{m}: \quad (A \wedge B) \vee (C \wedge D) \ \text{ to } \ (A \vee C) \wedge (B \vee D) .$$

1. Start from $(a \wedge b) \vee (a \wedge b)$.
2. Apply $\mathsf{m}$ in the empty context, with $A = a$, $B = b$, $C = a$ and $D = b$. This gives $(a \vee a) \wedge (b \vee b)$.
3. Apply $\mathsf{ac}{\downarrow}$ in the context $\{\ \} \wedge (b \vee b)$. This gives $a \wedge (b \vee b)$.
4. Apply $\mathsf{ac}{\downarrow}$ in the context $a \wedge \{\ \}$. This gives $a \wedge b$.

We check that medial is sound.

5. Suppose that $(A \wedge B) \vee (C \wedge D)$ is true. Then $A$ and $B$ are true, or $C$ and $D$ are true.
6. In the first case $A \vee C$ and $B \vee D$ are true because $A$ and $B$ are. In the second case they are true because $C$ and $D$ are.
7. So $(A \vee C) \wedge (B \vee D)$ is true.

We check that the derivation does not copy or erase any formula larger than an atom.

8. Medial in step 2 rearranges the four atom occurrences $a, b, a, b$ into a new order. It neither copies nor deletes any of them.
9. Steps 3 and 4 each remove one occurrence of an atom.

## Remarks

The example is the case $A = a \wedge b$ of a general fact: contraction from $A \vee A$ to $A$ is derivable for every formula $A$.
The proof is by induction on $A$.

- If $A$ is an atom or a negated atom, use $\mathsf{ac}{\downarrow}$.
- If $A$ is a unit, use the equations $\mathsf{t} \vee \mathsf{t} = \mathsf{t}$ and $\mathsf{f} \vee \mathsf{f} = \mathsf{f}$.
- If $A = B \vee C$, the equations turn $(B \vee C) \vee (B \vee C)$ into $(B \vee B) \vee (C \vee C)$, and the induction hypothesis applies to each part.
- If $A = B \wedge C$, medial turns $(B \wedge C) \vee (B \wedge C)$ into $(B \vee B) \wedge (C \vee C)$, and the induction hypothesis applies to each part.

Medial is the rule that makes the last case work: it brings the two copies of $B$ together without copying anything.
In a sequent calculus a contraction acts on a whole formula of the sequent, since its rules cannot reach inside formulas.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Contraction says: "$X$ or $X$" can be replaced by "$X$".
For a long formula $X$, this step must compare two big copies, which is a lot of work.
Deep inference splits it into small steps.
For "($a$ and $b$) or ($a$ and $b$)", the medial rule first regroups it as "($a$ or $a$) and ($b$ or $b$)".
Then two steps on single letters turn "$a$ or $a$" into $a$ and "$b$ or $b$" into $b$.

### L2 human

The derivation has two phases.

1. Medial: pair the matching parts of the two copies, giving $(a \vee a) \wedge (b \vee b)$.
2. Atomic contraction: contract each pair of atoms, inside the conjunction.

The typical use of medial is to make contraction local, so that each contraction step acts on a single atom.
A trap is to read medial as distributivity: medial never copies a subformula, while distributivity copies one.
