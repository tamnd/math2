## Example

Let $S(\alpha) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos 2\alpha & \sin 2\alpha \\ \sin 2\alpha & -\cos 2\alpha \end{pmatrix}$, the [[reflection|025158VEYZ]] in the line through $0$ at the angle $\alpha$.
We take the mirror lines $y = x$, at the angle $\frac{\pi}{4}$, and $y = 0$, at the angle $0$.

1. $S(\pi/4) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $S(0) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. $S(\pi/4)S(0) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 0 & 0 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot (-1) \\ 1 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 0 & 1 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot (-1) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. This is $R(\pi/2)$, the rotation by $\frac{\pi}{2} = 2\left(\frac{\pi}{4} - 0\right)$, twice the angle between the lines.
4. In the other order, $S(0)S(\pi/4) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot 1 & 1 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 0 \\ 0 \cdot 0 + (-1) \cdot 1 & 0 \cdot 1 + (-1) \cdot 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
5. This is $R(-\pi/2)$, the rotation by $2\left(0 - \frac{\pi}{4}\right)$.
6. Check on a point: $S(0)$ sends $(1, 0)$ to $(1, 0)$, and then $S(\pi/4)$ sends it to $(0, 1)$. So the product turns $(1, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$, a quarter turn.

## Remarks

- The example illustrates the rule $S(\alpha)S(\beta) = R(2(\alpha - \beta))$ of [[the classification of O(2)|~233ca0a4]].
- Both reflections have determinant $-1$, and the product has determinant $(-1)(-1) = 1$, as a rotation must.
- The two orders give opposite rotations. So reflections do not commute, unless the lines are equal or perpendicular.
- For perpendicular lines, $\alpha - \beta = \frac{\pi}{2}$, and both orders give $R(\pi) = -I_2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put two mirrors through the origin, at an angle of $45$ degrees.
Reflect a picture in the first mirror and then in the second mirror.
The picture is not mirrored any more: it is turned by $90$ degrees, twice the angle between the mirrors.

### L2 human

The worked computation is in the Example.
The typical use: to build a rotation by $\theta$ from reflections, take two mirror lines at the angle $\frac{\theta}{2}$.
A trap: the angle of the rotation is twice the angle between the mirrors, not equal to it.
