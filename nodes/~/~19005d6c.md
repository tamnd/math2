## Description

The cardinality of the continuum, written $\mathfrak{c}$, is the [[cardinal number|~85baa4e0]] $2^{\aleph_0}$.
It is the cardinality of the [[power set|~1481eeb8]] of $\mathbb{N}$, and of the set of all sequences of $0$ and $1$.
It is also the cardinality of $\mathbb{R}$. See [[the reals have the cardinality of the continuum|~1aa1c136]].

## Remarks

By [[Cantor's theorem|~54780446]], $\mathfrak{c} > \aleph_0$.
Other sets of cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$ are each nonempty open interval of $\mathbb{R}$, the plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the set of irrational numbers.
The [[continuum hypothesis|~988ad063]] says that $\mathfrak{c} = \aleph_1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

$\mathfrak{c}$ is the size of the real line.
It is a larger infinity than $\aleph_0$.
Surprisingly, a short interval, a whole line and a whole plane all have this same size.
