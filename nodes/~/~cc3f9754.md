## Example

Let $V$ be the space of real polynomials of degree at most $2$, with the bases $B = (1, x, x^2)$ and $B' = (1, x - 1, (x - 1)^2)$.

1. Expand the new basis in the old one: $1 = 1$, $x - 1 = -1 + x$, and $(x - 1)^2 = 1 - 2x + x^2$.
2. By the [[definition|0KWXYZPJKN]], the change of basis matrix from $B$ to $B'$ is $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. Expand the old basis in the new one: $x = 1 + (x - 1)$ and $x^2 = 1 + 2(x - 1) + (x - 1)^2$. So $P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. Check $PP^{-1} = I_3$ with the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]]. Row 1 of $P$ times columns 2 and 3 of $P^{-1}$: $1 - 1 + 0 = 0$ and $1 - 2 + 1 = 0$. Row 2 times column 3: $2 - 2 = 0$. The diagonal entries are $1$.
5. Let $p = x^2$, with [[coordinates|0BMVBHTWV2]] $[p]_B = (0, 0, 1)$. Then $[p]_{B'} = P^{-1}(0, 0, 1) = (1, 2, 1)$.
6. Check: $1 + 2(x - 1) + (x - 1)^2 = 1 + 2x - 2 + x^2 - 2x + 1 = x^2$.

## Remarks

- The new coordinates of $p$ are $p(1)$, $p'(1)$ and $p''(1)/2$, the Taylor coefficients at $1$. For $p = x^2$ these are $1$, $2$ and $1$.
- Both matrices are upper triangular with ones on the diagonal, so $\det P = 1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Changing to the basis of powers of $x - 1$ rewrites a polynomial around the point $1$.
The change of basis matrix collects the expansions of $1$, $x - 1$ and $(x - 1)^2$.

### L2 human

The inverse matrix collects the expansions of $1$, $x$ and $x^2$ in powers of $x - 1$.
Multiplying by it gives the Taylor coefficients at $1$.
