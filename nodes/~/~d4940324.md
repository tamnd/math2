## Definition

An [[arithmetic function|~0abb0ec7]] $f$ is multiplicative if $f(1) = 1$ and $f(mn) = f(m) f(n)$ for all [[coprime|~8f403547]] positive integers $m$ and $n$.

## Remarks

A multiplicative function is determined by its values at prime powers: if $n = p_1^{e_1} \cdots p_k^{e_k}$, then $f(n) = f(p_1^{e_1}) \cdots f(p_k^{e_k})$.
Examples are [[Euler's totient function|~97075527]], the [[divisor function|~90e2e22f]] and the [[Möbius function|~23b71668]].
A function with $f(mn) = f(m) f(n)$ for all $m$ and $n$ is [[completely multiplicative|~0101a637]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a multiplicative function, you can compute $f(n)$ from the prime factorization of $n$.
For example, $\varphi(12) = \varphi(4) \varphi(3) = 2 \cdot 2 = 4$, because $4$ and $3$ are coprime.
