## Proof

Part A: truth sets are barred up-sets.
We show by induction on $\varphi$ two facts: persistence ($w \Vdash \varphi$ and $w \leq v$ give $v \Vdash \varphi$), and bar closure (if a bar for $w$ forces $\varphi$, then $w \Vdash \varphi$).

1. For atoms, both facts are the conditions on $V(p)$. For $\bot$, persistence holds, and bar closure holds since the empty set is not a bar.
2. For $\wedge$, both facts follow from the induction hypothesis, since a bar inside $[\![\varphi \wedge \psi]\!]$ lies inside $[\![\varphi]\!]$ and inside $[\![\psi]\!]$.
3. Persistence for $\varphi \vee \psi$: let $B$ be a bar for $w$ whose nodes force $\varphi$ or $\psi$, and let $v \geq w$.
4. If some $b \in B$ has $b \leq v$, then $v$ forces $\varphi$ or $\psi$ by the induction hypothesis. So $\set{v}$ is the bar we need.
5. Otherwise, let $B' = \set{b \in B : b \geq v}$. A path through $v$ passes through $w$, so it meets $B$ at some $b$.
6. In a tree, $b$ and $v$ lie on one path, so they are comparable. By the case assumption, $b \geq v$. So $B'$ is a bar for $v$.
7. Bar closure for $\varphi \vee \psi$: let $B$ be a bar for $w$ and let each $b \in B$ have a bar $B_b$ of nodes forcing $\varphi$ or $\psi$.
8. A path through $w$ meets $B$ at some $b$, so it is a path through $b$ and meets $B_b$. So $\bigcup_b B_b$ is a bar for $w$, and $w \Vdash \varphi \vee \psi$.
9. Persistence for $\varphi \to \psi$ follows from transitivity of $\leq$, as in Kripke semantics.
10. Bar closure for $\varphi \to \psi$: let $B$ be a bar for $w$ inside $[\![\varphi \to \psi]\!]$. Let $v \geq w$ force $\varphi$.
11. Put $C = \set{c : c \geq v \text{ and } c \geq b \text{ for some } b \in B}$. A path through $v$ meets $B$ at some $b$ comparable with $v$, and the larger of $b$, $v$ is in $C$. So $C$ is a bar for $v$.
12. Each $c \in C$ forces $\varphi$ and $\varphi \to \psi$, by persistence from $v$ and from $b$. So $c \Vdash \psi$.
13. By bar closure for $\psi$, $v \Vdash \psi$. Hence $w \Vdash \varphi \to \psi$.

Part B: soundness.

14. The axioms without $\vee$ and modus ponens are checked as for Kripke models, since the clauses for $\wedge$, $\to$ and $\bot$ are the same; see [[the Kripke soundness proof|~1962f700]].
15. Axiom $\varphi \to \varphi \vee \psi$: if $v \Vdash \varphi$, then the bar $\set{v}$ gives $v \Vdash \varphi \vee \psi$.
16. Axiom $(\varphi \to \chi) \to ((\psi \to \chi) \to (\varphi \vee \psi \to \chi))$: let $x$ force $\varphi \to \chi$, $\psi \to \chi$ and $\varphi \vee \psi$, the first two by persistence.
17. A bar $B$ for $x$ has nodes forcing $\varphi$ or $\psi$. By persistence, each $b \in B$ forces both implications, so $b \Vdash \chi$.
18. By bar closure, $x \Vdash \chi$. This proves item 1.

Part C: from Kripke models to Beth models.

19. Let $\mathfrak{K} = (K, \leq, V)$ be a Kripke model with root $w_0$. Let $T$ be the set of finite sequences $(w_0, w_1, \ldots, w_n)$ with $w_0 \leq w_1 \leq \cdots \leq w_n$ in $K$.
20. Order $T$ by "is an initial segment of". This is a tree with root $(w_0)$. Let $\ell(\sigma)$ be the last entry of $\sigma$; the map $\ell$ is onto $K$.
21. Put $\sigma \in V'(p)$ if and only if $\ell(\sigma) \in V(p)$. This is an up-set, since entries never go down.
22. Claim: $\sigma \Vdash_{\mathfrak{B}} \chi$ if and only if $\ell(\sigma) \Vdash_{\mathfrak{K}} \chi$. We use induction on $\chi$.
23. For each $\sigma$, the *stationary path* $\sigma, \sigma \ell(\sigma), \sigma \ell(\sigma) \ell(\sigma), \ldots$ repeats the last world. Every node on it has the label $\ell(\sigma)$.
24. Atoms: if a bar for $\sigma$ lies inside $V'(p)$, it meets the stationary path at a node with label $\ell(\sigma)$. So $\ell(\sigma) \in V(p)$ and $\sigma \in V'(p)$. Hence $V'(p)$ is barred, and the claim holds for $p$.
25. Disjunction: if $\ell(\sigma)$ forces $\chi_1$ or $\chi_2$, the bar $\set{\sigma}$ works. Conversely, a bar of nodes forcing $\chi_1$ or $\chi_2$ meets the stationary path, at a node with label $\ell(\sigma)$.
26. Implication: the nodes $\tau \geq \sigma$ have labels $\ell(\tau) \geq \ell(\sigma)$, and each $v \geq \ell(\sigma)$ is the label of the node $\sigma v$. So the Beth clause and the Kripke clause agree, by the induction hypothesis.
27. The cases $\bot$ and $\wedge$ are direct. This proves item 3.
28. Completeness: let $\mathsf{IPC} \nvdash \varphi$. By [[Kripke completeness|~ac029000]], some rooted Kripke model refutes $\varphi$ at its root $w_0$.
29. By step 22, the root $(w_0)$ of the Beth model does not force $\varphi$. So $\varphi$ is not Beth valid, which proves item 2.

## Idea

Part A shows that the Beth clauses keep truth sets inside the barred up-sets, and Part B checks the axioms.
For completeness, a Kripke model becomes a tree of histories that never go down.
The stationary path, which stays at one world for ever, forces every bar to contain a node with the original label.
So "eventually on every path" at a node reduces to "now" at its label.

## Variants

1. A topological proof uses the fact that barred up-sets are the open sets of the path space. Then [[Tarski's completeness theorem for the Cantor space|~088e3e8d]] gives completeness for Beth models on the full binary tree.
2. Completeness can also be proved directly, with a tree of finite sequences of prime theories in place of worlds.
