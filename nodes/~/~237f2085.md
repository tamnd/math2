## Example

The claim is: for every real number $x > 0$, we have $x + \frac{1}{x} \ge 2$.
We first find a proof in scratch work, which goes backward from the claim.
Then we write the final proof, which goes forward from a known fact.
This is the method of [[scratch work and the final proof|~d29be80b]].

Scratch work, which is not part of the proof:

1. We want $x + \frac1x \ge 2$. Since $x > 0$, multiplication by $x$ keeps the direction of an inequality, and so does division by $x$. So the claim is equivalent to $x^2 + 1 \ge 2x$.
2. Subtracting $2x$ from both sides gives the equivalent inequality $x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0$.
3. The left side is $(x - 1)^2$, because $(x - 1)^2 = x^2 - 2x + 1$.
4. A square of a real number is at least $0$. So the last inequality is true.
5. Each step was an equivalence, so we can run the chain in the other direction. That reversed chain is the proof.

The final proof:

> Let $x$ be a real number with $x > 0$.
> The square of a real number is non-negative, so $(x - 1)^2 \ge 0$.
> Expanding the square gives $x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0$.
> Adding $2x$ to both sides gives $x^2 + 1 \ge 2x$.
> Since $x > 0$, we may divide both sides by $x$ and keep the direction of the inequality.
> This gives $x + \frac1x \ge 2$.
> $\square$

We check the final proof.

1. It starts from a fact that holds without the claim, $(x - 1)^2 \ge 0$.
2. Each step is an implication from the line before it, with its reason: expansion, addition of $2x$, division by $x > 0$.
3. It ends with the claim, and it uses the hypothesis $x > 0$ exactly at the division.
4. A numerical check: for $x = 2$ we get $2 + \frac12 = 2.5 \ge 2$, and for $x = 1$ we get equality, $1 + 1 = 2$.

## Remarks

1. The final proof hides how it was found. A reader may ask why it starts with $(x - 1)^2$; the scratch work answers this, but the proof does not need the answer.
2. The proof also shows when equality holds. Equality $x + \frac1x = 2$ forces $(x - 1)^2 = 0$, so $x = 1$.
3. The hypothesis $x > 0$ is needed. For $x = -1$ we get $x + \frac1x = -2 < 2$. In the scratch work, multiplication by a negative $x$ would reverse the inequality.
4. The claim is the [[inequality of arithmetic and geometric means|0DRF0F3XC9]] for the two numbers $x$ and $\frac1x$, whose product is $1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To find a proof, we often start at the end and ask what would make the claim true.
Here the claim is that a positive number plus its reciprocal is at least $2$, for example $4 + \frac14 = 4.25$.
Working backward, we find that the claim comes down to "a square is never negative".
In the written proof we tell the story in the other order: we start from the square and arrive at the claim.
The backward search is like a draft, and the proof is the clean copy.

### L2 human

We apply the same method to the claim $a^2 + b^2 \ge 2ab$ for all real numbers $a$ and $b$.

1. Scratch: the claim is equivalent to $a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \ge 0$, by subtracting $2ab$.
2. Scratch: the left side is $(a - b)^2$, which is non-negative.
3. Proof: let $a$ and $b$ be real numbers. Then $(a - b)^2 \ge 0$, because a square is non-negative.
4. Proof: expanding gives $a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \ge 0$.
5. Proof: adding $2ab$ to both sides gives $a^2 + b^2 \ge 2ab$.

The typical use of this pattern is every inequality that reduces to a sum of squares.
A trap is a backward step that is not an equivalence, such as multiplying by a number of unknown sign.
Then the reversed chain fails at that step.
