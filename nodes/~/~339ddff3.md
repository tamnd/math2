## Statement

Let $U$ and $W$ be subspaces of $\R^n$ with $1 \le p = \dim U \le q = \dim W$, and let $\theta_1 \le \cdots \le \theta_p$ be their [[principal angles|~179768d8]].
Let $Q_1$ be an $n \times p$ matrix and $Q_2$ an $n \times q$ matrix whose columns are [[orthonormal bases|0S9GGTR0EX]] of $U$ and $W$.
Let $\sigma_1 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_p \ge 0$ be the [[singular values|09VMDSRXWJ]] of the $p \times q$ matrix $M = Q_1^{\mathsf{T}} Q_2$, that is, the square roots of the eigenvalues of $M M^{\mathsf{T}}$.
Let $P_U$ and $P_W$ be the [[orthogonal projections|0Q8HZTJC7G]] onto $U$ and $W$.
Then:

1. $\sigma_1 \le 1$, and $\cos \theta_k = \sigma_k$ for $k = 1, \dots, p$. In particular, the principal angles do not depend on the choice of the maximizing pairs or of $Q_1$ and $Q_2$.
2. If $M = Y \Sigma Z^{\mathsf{T}}$ is a [[singular value decomposition|000NPTMX6J]] with columns $y_k$ of $Y$ and $z_k$ of $Z$, then $u_k = Q_1 y_k$ and $w_k = Q_2 z_k$ form a choice of principal vectors.
3. The number of indices $k$ with $\theta_k = 0$ equals $\dim (U \cap W)$.
4. For $p = 1$ and $U = \R u$ with $\norm{u} = 1$: $\cos \theta_1 = \norm{P_W u}$.
5. In the [[Frobenius norm|JT8Q204E4R]], $\norm{P_U - P_W}_F^2 = p + q - 2 \sum_{k=1}^{p} \cos^2 \theta_k$. For $p = q$ this is $2 \sum_{k=1}^{p} \sin^2 \theta_k$.

The [[proof|~a7fbc171]] turns the maximization into one for the bilinear form $a^{\mathsf{T}} M c$ and splits off one singular value at a time.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Orthonormal columns: for $U = W = \R^1$ with $Q_1 = Q_2 = (2)$, the matrix $Q_1^{\mathsf{T}} Q_2 = (4)$ has singular value $4$, which is not a cosine. With a basis that is not orthonormal, the singular values measure lengths as well as angles.
- $p \le q$: the definition pairs each of the $p$ directions of $U$ with one of $W$. For $p > q$, exchange the roles of $U$ and $W$.

### Consequences

- $U = W$ if and only if $p = q$ and all principal angles are $0$, by part 3.
- $U \perp W$ if and only if $M = 0$, that is, all principal angles are $\pi/2$.
- Two planes in $\R^3$ that are not equal meet in a line, so by part 3 one principal angle is $0$; [[the two-plane example|~d2e159b0]] shows that the other is the dihedral angle.

### Generalizations

Over $\C$, the same statement holds with $Q_1^* Q_2$ and $\abs{\ip{u}{w}}$.
In computation, $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ come from a [[QR decomposition|0ERWG4NXQ9]] of matrices whose columns span $U$ and $W$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To find the angles between two subspaces, write orthonormal bases of both as the columns of two matrices.
Multiply the transpose of the first by the second.
The singular values of this small matrix are the cosines of the principal angles.

### L2 human

For the planes $x + y = 0$ and $y + z = 0$ in $\R^3$, $M$ is a $2 \times 2$ matrix with singular values $1$ and $1/2$, so the angles are $0$ and $\pi/3$.
The typical use is to compare two subspaces, for example the column spaces of two data matrices, by a few numbers.
The trap is to use bases that are not orthonormal.

### L3 human

Part 5 shows that the chordal distance between subspaces of the same dimension depends only on the principal angles.
Part 2 gives bases of $U$ and $W$ in which the matrix of the map $u \mapsto P_W u$ is diagonal with entries $\cos \theta_k$.
