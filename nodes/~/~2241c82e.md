## Statement

The [[units|~11c1c339]] of the [[Gaussian integers|~ed4f3a97]] $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ are $1$, $-1$, $i$ and $-i$.

## Remarks

The proof uses the [[norm|~53ae0d2c]]: if $u v = 1$, then $N(u) N(v) = 1$, so $N(u) = 1$. The only solutions of $a^2 + b^2 = 1$ in integers are $(\pm 1, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 1)$.
Conversely, an element of norm $1$ is a unit, because $(a + b i)(a - b i) = 1$.
The group of units is cyclic of order $4$, generated by $i$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A unit must have norm $1$, so it lies on the unit circle.
The only points with integer coordinates on the unit circle are $1$, $i$, $-1$ and $-i$.
