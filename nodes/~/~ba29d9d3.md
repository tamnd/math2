## Proof

A *chain basis* for $N$ is a basis of the form in part 1: the vectors $N^j u_i$ with $0 \le j < k_i$, where $N^{k_i} u_i = 0$.
We prove existence by induction on $n = \dim V$.

1. If $n = 0$, the empty family is a chain basis. Let $n \ge 1$ and assume existence for all nilpotent maps on spaces of smaller dimension.
2. Let $W = \operatorname{im} N$. Then $N(W) \subseteq W$, and $N|_W$ is nilpotent. Since $N$ is [[nilpotent|063D5Z2C70]] and $V \ne 0$, $N$ is not injective: if $N^m = 0$ with $m$ least, then $N^{m-1} v \ne 0$ for some $v$ and $N (N^{m-1} v) = 0$. By the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]], $\dim W < n$.
3. By the induction hypothesis, $W$ has a chain basis for $N|_W$: vectors $w_1, \dots, w_t$ and lengths $\ell_1, \dots, \ell_t \ge 1$ with $N^{\ell_i} w_i = 0$, such that the $N^j w_i$ with $0 \le j < \ell_i$ form a basis of $W$.
4. Each $w_i$ lies in $W = \operatorname{im} N$, so we can choose $u_i \in V$ with $N u_i = w_i$. Then $N^{\ell_i + 1} u_i = 0$.
5. The vectors $N^{\ell_i} u_i = N^{\ell_i - 1} w_i$, for $i = 1, \dots, t$, lie in $\ker N$, since $N^{\ell_i} w_i = 0$. They are independent, since they are part of the basis of $W$ in step 3.
6. Extend them to a basis of $\ker N$ by vectors $z_1, \dots, z_q$. So $\dim \ker N = t + q$.
7. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the family of the vectors $N^j u_i$ with $1 \le i \le t$ and $0 \le j \le \ell_i$, together with $z_1, \dots, z_q$. It has $\sum_i (\ell_i + 1) + q = \dim W + t + q = \dim \operatorname{im} N + \dim \ker N$ members, which is $n$ by the rank-nullity theorem.
8. $\mathcal{S}$ is independent. Let $\sum_{i, j} a_{ij} N^j u_i + \sum_m c_m z_m = 0$. Apply $N$. The $z_m$ and the vectors $N^{\ell_i} u_i$ go to $0$, and $N^{j+1} u_i = N^j w_i$. So $\sum_i \sum_{j < \ell_i} a_{ij} N^j w_i = 0$.
9. These $N^j w_i$ form the basis of $W$ in step 3, so $a_{ij} = 0$ for all $j < \ell_i$. What remains is $\sum_i a_{i \ell_i} N^{\ell_i} u_i + \sum_m c_m z_m = 0$. This is a relation among the basis of $\ker N$ from step 6, so all these coefficients are $0$ as well.
10. By steps 7 and 9, $\mathcal{S}$ is a basis of $V$. It is a chain basis: the chain of $u_i$ has length $\ell_i + 1$, and the chain of $z_m$ has length $1$, since $N z_m = 0$. This completes the induction. Sort the chains so that $k_1 \ge \cdots \ge k_s$.
11. Matrix. In the ordered chain $N^{k-1} u, \dots, N u, u$, call the vectors $f_1, \dots, f_k$. Then $N f_1 = N^k u = 0$ and $N f_p = f_{p-1}$ for $p \ge 2$. By [[part 1 of the lemma on Jordan blocks|~d1324a3e]], this is the action of $J_k(0)$ on the standard basis. Each chain spans an $N$-invariant subspace, so the matrix of $N$ is the [[block diagonal matrix|0M0SNKZ482]] $J_{k_1}(0) \oplus \cdots \oplus J_{k_s}(0)$. This proves part 1.
12. Part 2. The basis has $\sum_i k_i$ vectors and $\dim V = n$.
13. Part 3. By part 5 of the lemma on Jordan blocks, $\dim \ker J_k(0)^j = \min(j, k)$. Kernels of a block diagonal matrix are direct sums of the kernels of the blocks, so $\dim \ker N^j = \sum_i \min(j, k_i)$.
14. Hence $\dim \ker N^j - \dim \ker N^{j-1} = \sum_i (\min(j, k_i) - \min(j - 1, k_i))$. Each term is $1$ if $k_i \ge j$ and $0$ otherwise. So the difference is $b_j = \#\{i : k_i \ge j\}$. By rank-nullity it equals $r_{j-1} - r_j$.
15. The number of $i$ with $k_i = j$ is $b_j - b_{j+1} = r_{j-1} - 2 r_j + r_{j+1}$. This proves part 3.
16. Part 4. The ranks $r_j$ depend only on $N$, not on a basis. By step 15 they fix the number of blocks of each size, so they fix the partition. [[Similar|027KDW2K72]] nilpotent matrices have the same ranks of all powers, since $(P^{-1} N P)^j = P^{-1} N^j P$. Conversely, two nilpotent matrices with the same partition are both similar to the same block diagonal matrix by part 1, so they are similar.
17. Part 5. Column $j$ of the [[Young diagram|096AHMB3P4]] of $(k_1, \dots, k_s)$ has one box for each row of length at least $j$. By step 14 it has $b_j = r_{j-1} - r_j$ boxes.

## Idea

The image $\operatorname{im} N$ is smaller and has chains by induction.
Pull each chain back one step, so that it starts one level higher, and add new chains of length one to fill the kernel.
The ranks of the powers count how many chains reach each level, and this gives uniqueness.

## Variants

The module proof views $V$ as an $F[x]$-module killed by a power of $x$ and applies the structure theorem for modules over a principal ideal domain, which gives $V \cong \bigoplus_i F[x]/(x^{k_i})$.
Another elementary proof goes from the top down.
Choose a complement of $\ker N^{m-1}$ in $V$, push it down with $N$, and extend at each level $\ker N^j$ by a complement of $\ker N^{j-1} + N(\ker N^{j+1})$.
