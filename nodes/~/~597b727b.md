## Statement

Let $K$ be a [[field|~92ce2090]], let $f \in K[x]$ be nonzero, and let $L$ and $L'$ be [[splitting fields|~8d6926f1]] of $f$ over $K$.
Then there is a ring isomorphism $\sigma \colon L \to L'$ with $\sigma(c) = c$ for every $c \in K$.

## Remarks

The proof is by induction on $[L : K]$. It extends an isomorphism one root at a time, with the [[structure of simple extensions|~973f8b7f]].
The isomorphism $\sigma$ is usually not unique. The isomorphisms from $L$ to itself that fix $K$ form the [[Galois group|~e9ba4fad]] of $f$.
So one speaks of "the" splitting field of $f$ over $K$.
The choice of the irreducible factors in the construction does not change the result, up to isomorphism.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two splitting fields of the same polynomial are the same field with different names for the elements.
The proof extends a matching of the base field, one root at a time.
