## Example

Statement: for all integers $a$, $b$ and $c$, if $a \mid b$ and $b \mid c$, then $a \mid c$.
Definition used: $a \mid b$, read "$a$ [[divides|096PW7Q014]] $b$", means that $b = ak$ for some integer $k$.
We give a [[direct proof|0NTF6DMVAP]].

1. Let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be integers, and assume $a \mid b$ and $b \mid c$.
2. By the definition of $a \mid b$, there is an integer $k$ with $b = ak$.
3. By the definition of $b \mid c$, there is an integer $l$ with $c = bl$. We use a new letter $l$, because the two quotients can differ.
4. Substitute step 2 into step 3: $c = (ak)l$.
5. By the associative law of multiplication, $c = a(kl)$.
6. The number $kl$ is an integer, because a product of integers is an integer.
7. So $c = a \cdot (kl)$ with $kl$ an integer, and $a \mid c$ by the definition of divisibility.

## Remarks

The example shows a direct proof with two hypotheses: each hypothesis is unfolded with its own new letter, and the two equations are combined.

Numbers make the steps concrete.
With $a = 3$, $b = 12$ and $c = 60$, we have $k = 4$ and $l = 5$, and the proof gives $60 = 3 \cdot 20$ with $kl = 20$.

The same pattern proves other closure facts.
If $a \mid b$ and $a \mid c$, then $b = ak$ and $c = al$, so $b + c = a(k + l)$ and $a \mid b + c$.
Transitivity and reflexivity ($a = a \cdot 1$) together say that divisibility is a preorder on the integers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"$3$ divides $12$" means that $12$ is $3$ times a whole number, here $3 \cdot 4$.
"$12$ divides $60$" means that $60$ is $12$ times a whole number, here $12 \cdot 5$.
Put the two together: $60 = 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 5 = 3 \cdot 20$.
So $3$ divides $60$.
The proof does the same thing with letters, so it works for all numbers at once.

### L2 human

We use the result in a short chain.
Claim: if $6 \mid n$, then $3 \mid n$ and $2 \mid n$.

1. $3 \mid 6$, because $6 = 3 \cdot 2$.
2. From $3 \mid 6$ and $6 \mid n$, transitivity gives $3 \mid n$.
3. $2 \mid 6$, because $6 = 2 \cdot 3$, and transitivity gives $2 \mid n$.

The typical use of transitivity is to pass divisibility along a chain of numbers.

A trap: writing $b = ak$ and $c = bk$ with the same $k$.
This proves the result only when the two quotients are equal, for example for $3 \mid 12$ and $12 \mid 48$, and not for $3 \mid 12$ and $12 \mid 60$.
