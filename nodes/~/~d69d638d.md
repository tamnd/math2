## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, the matrix of the [[3 by 3 defective example|~5db5cf19]].

1. There, $p_A(t) = t^3 - 5t^2 + 8t - 4 = (t - 1)(t - 2)^2$, found in two ways. Here $\tr A = 5$, the sum of the principal $2 \times 2$ minors is $8$, and $\det A = 4$.
2. By the [[Cayley-Hamilton theorem|0F765KFC2J]], $A^3 - 5A^2 + 8A - 4I = 0$. So $A(A^2 - 5A + 8I) = 4I$.
3. $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 4 & 0 \\ -4 & 8 & 0 \\ -3 & 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. $A^2 - 5A + 8I = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$.
5. So $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{4}(A^2 - 5A + 8I) = \begin{pmatrix} 3/4 & -1/4 & 0 \\ 1/4 & 1/4 & 0 \\ 1/2 & -1/2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
6. Check the rows of $A A^{-1}$. Row 1: $(3/4 + 1/4,\ -1/4 + 1/4,\ 0) = (1, 0, 0)$.
7. Row 2: $(-3/4 + 3/4,\ 1/4 + 3/4,\ 0) = (0, 1, 0)$.
8. Row 3: $(-3/4 + 1/4 + 1/2,\ 1/4 + 1/4 - 1/2,\ 1) = (0, 0, 1)$. So $AA^{-1} = I$.

## Remarks

- The constant term of $p_A$ is $-4 = (-1)^3 \det A$. The sign comes from the odd size $n = 3$.
- The inverse is a polynomial of degree $2$ in $A$, because $m_A = p_A$ has degree $3$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The characteristic polynomial gives the equation $A^3 - 5A^2 + 8A = 4I$.
Factoring out $A$ shows that $A$ times $(A^2 - 5A + 8I)/4$ is the identity.
So the inverse needs only $A^2$, $A$ and $I$.

### L2 human

The typical use is to find $A^{-1}$ when $A^2$ is easy to compute.
A trap is to use the constant term $-4$ with the wrong sign: dividing by $-4$ gives $-A^{-1}$.
