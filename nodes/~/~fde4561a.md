## Statement

Let $S$ be a nonempty subset of the real numbers that is [[bounded below|~98a1bdde]].
Then $S$ has an [[infimum|~f17927f2]] in $\mathbb{R}$.

## Remarks

Proof: the set $-S = \{-s : s \in S\}$ is nonempty and bounded above. By the [[completeness axiom|~2aea0c9b]], it has a supremum $b$. Then $-b$ is the infimum of $S$.
Another proof: the set of lower bounds of $S$ is nonempty and bounded above by each element of $S$, and its supremum is $\inf S$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If a set of real numbers has a floor, then it has a highest floor.
This follows from the completeness axiom by a reflection of the number line through $0$, which turns floors into ceilings.
