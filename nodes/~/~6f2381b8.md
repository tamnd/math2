## Statement

A *superintuitionistic logic* is a set of intuitionistic formulas that contains [[intuitionistic propositional logic|Q11N8X99FK]] $\mathsf{IPC}$ and is closed under modus ponens and uniform substitution.
These logics, ordered by inclusion, form a lattice $\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})$.
$\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$ is the lattice of normal modal logics that contain [[Grzegorczyk's logic|2Q8E4GQF3B]] $\mathsf{Grz} = \mathsf{S4} \oplus \square(\square(p \to \square p) \to p) \to p$; see [[the lattice of normal modal logics|~d43c867f]].
Let $T$ be the [[Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation|~15f8f42e]], and define
$$\sigma L = \mathsf{Grz} \oplus \set{T(A) : A \in L}, \qquad \rho M = \set{A : M \vdash T(A)}.$$

**Theorem.** The map $\sigma$ is a lattice isomorphism from $\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})$ onto $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$, and its inverse is the restriction of $\rho$ to $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$.
In particular, $\rho \sigma L = L$ for every superintuitionistic logic $L$, and $\sigma \rho M = M$ for every $M \in \mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$.

The proof is in [[the proof of the Blok-Esakia theorem|~104f7a15]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. $\mathsf{Grz}$ cannot be replaced by $\mathsf{S4}$. The logics $\mathsf{S4}$ and $\mathsf{Grz}$ both satisfy $\rho M = \mathsf{IPC}$, so $\rho$ is not injective on $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{S4})$.
2. $\mathsf{Grz}$ cannot be replaced by a stronger logic such as $\mathsf{S4.2}$. Every extension of $\mathsf{S4.2}$ proves $T(\neg p \vee \neg \neg p)$, so $\rho$ would not reach $\mathsf{IPC}$.

### Consequences

1. Every superintuitionistic logic $L$ has $\sigma L$ as its greatest modal companion, and $\mathsf{Grz}$ is the greatest modal companion of $\mathsf{IPC}$.
2. $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$ has $2^{\aleph_0}$ elements, since $\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})$ does, by a theorem of Jankov.
3. The table of companions: $\mathsf{IPC}$ goes to $\mathsf{Grz}$, the logic $\mathsf{KC}$ of weak excluded middle to $\mathsf{Grz.2}$, the Gödel-Dummett logic $\mathsf{LC}$ to $\mathsf{Grz.3}$, and classical logic to $\mathsf{Triv} = \mathrm{Log}(\circ)$. The least companions are $\mathsf{S4}$, $\mathsf{S4.2}$, $\mathsf{S4.3}$ and $\mathsf{S5}$.
4. Lattice properties transfer: for example, $L$ is a coatom of $\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})$ exactly when $\sigma L$ is a coatom of $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$.

### Converse

The map $\rho$ is defined on all of $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{S4})$, and it is onto $\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})$ by the theorem.
The fibre $\rho^{-1}(L)$ is the set of modal companions of $L$, which is the interval $[\tau L, \sigma L]$ with $\tau L = \mathsf{S4} \oplus \set{T(A) : A \in L}$.
So the theorem selects the top element of each fibre, and these tops form exactly $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$.

### Generalizations

1. Algebraically, the theorem is a lattice isomorphism between the varieties of [[Heyting algebras|NG943XNQWH]] and the varieties of Grz-algebras, the [[interior algebras|BDEX7HET71]] that satisfy the Grz equation.
2. An analogous isomorphism holds between the extensions of the Kuznetsov-Muravitsky intuitionistic modal logic and $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{GL})$.
3. Versions exist for intuitionistic modal logics and their classical bimodal companions.

## History

Blok and Esakia proved the theorem independently in the 1970s.
Blok's proof is algebraic, through varieties of interior algebras, and Esakia's uses the duality between Heyting algebras and Esakia spaces.
The theory of modal companions, with the maps $\tau$ and $\sigma$, was developed at the same time by Esakia, Maksimova and Rybakov.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Intuitionistic logics are logics of proof, and modal logics above $\mathsf{Grz}$ are logics of a special "necessarily".
The theorem says that the two families match one to one: each intuitionistic logic has exactly one partner above $\mathsf{Grz}$.
The partners are ordered in the same way, so any fact about the order of one family is a fact about the other.

### L2 human

1. Take $L = \mathsf{IPC} + (p \vee \neg p)$, classical logic.
2. Its translation adds $T(p \vee \neg p) = \square p \vee \square \neg \square p$ to $\mathsf{Grz}$.
3. A finite partial order validates this formula exactly when no point sees a different point, that is, when the order is the identity.
4. So $\sigma L$ is the logic of reflexive points, $\mathsf{Triv}$, and $\rho\, \mathsf{Triv}$ is classical logic, since $T(A)$ is equivalent to $A$ in $\mathsf{Triv}$.

The typical use is to transfer results, such as counts of logics or lattice properties, between intuitionistic and modal logic.
A trap: $\sigma$ adds $\mathsf{Grz}$, not $\mathsf{S4}$; the map $\tau$ into $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{S4})$ is injective but not onto.

### L3 human

The isomorphism comes from the free Boolean extension: every Heyting algebra $H$ generates a Boolean algebra $B(H)$ with an interior operator whose open elements are $H$, and $B(H)$ is a Grz-algebra.
Blok's lemma says that every Grz-algebra satisfies the same equations as its subalgebra generated by open elements, so Grz-varieties are determined by their Heyting algebras of open elements.
On the dual side, Esakia's proof uses [[Esakia duality|CD9Y7YP4EJ]] between Heyting algebras and Esakia spaces.
