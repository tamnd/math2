## Example

Let $L = \set{<}$ with one binary relation symbol, and view $(\Z, <)$ and $(\Q, <)$ as $L$-structures with the usual [[strict orders|P33CMYHJW9]].
We check that two maps are [[embeddings|SCRB74SWQN]]: the inclusion $\iota \colon \Z \to \Q$, and the map $d \colon \Z \to \Z$ with $d(n) = 2n$.

1. $\iota$ is [[injective|09NWETVTNY]], since it is the identity on elements.
2. For $m, n \in \Z$, $m < n$ in $\Z$ if and only if $m < n$ in $\Q$, because the order of $\Q$ extends the order of $\Z$. So $\iota$ preserves and reflects $<$.
3. $L$ has no function or constant symbols, so $\iota$ is an embedding.
4. $d$ is injective, since $2m = 2n$ gives $m = n$.
5. For $m, n \in \Z$, $m < n$ if and only if $2m < 2n$, because multiplication by the positive number $2$ preserves the order and its inverse multiplication by $1/2$ does too. So $d$ is an embedding of $(\Z, <)$ into itself.

The map $d$ is not surjective, since $1$ is not in its image; so it is an embedding that is not an isomorphism, but it is an isomorphism of $(\Z, <)$ onto the substructure $(2\Z, <)$.

## Remarks

The two maps show the limits of the [[preservation theorem|~9a0a1ffd]].

- The universal sentences of linear orders, irreflexivity, transitivity and totality, hold in $\Q$, so they hold in $\Z$, which is a substructure.
- The density sentence $\forall x \, \forall y \, (x < y \to \exists z \, (x < z \land z < y))$ is true in $\Q$ and false in $\Z$: it is not universal, so it need not pass down.
- The formula $\exists z \, (x < z \land z < y)$ holds of $(0, 2)$ in $(\Z, <)$, with $z = 1$. Its image under $d$ is $(0, 4)$, and it holds there with $z = 2 = d(1)$; existential formulas go up.
- The formula $\neg\exists z \, (x < z \land z < y)$ holds of $(0, 1)$ in $\Z$, but it fails of $(d(0), d(1)) = (0, 2)$ in $\Z$, with $z = 1$; so an embedding of a structure into itself need not preserve formulas with quantifiers.

The last point shows that $d$ is not an [[elementary embedding|CGH8C8EA9N]] of $(\Z, <)$ into itself, although its domain and codomain are the same structure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put the whole numbers on the number line, among all the fractions.
The order stays the same: $2 < 5$ among whole numbers and among fractions.
Doubling every whole number also keeps the order: $2 < 5$ gives $4 < 10$.
But doubling changes the gaps: $0$ and $1$ are neighbors, while their doubles $0$ and $2$ have $1$ between them.

### L2 human

We check one quantifier-free formula along $d$ and one formula with a quantifier.

1. The formula $x < y \land y < z$ holds of $(1, 3, 4)$, and it holds of $(2, 6, 8)$; part 2 of the preservation theorem predicts this.
2. The formula "$y$ is the successor of $x$", that is $x < y \land \neg\exists z \, (x < z \land z < y)$, holds of $(3, 4)$.
3. It fails of $(6, 8)$, because $7$ lies between them.

The typical use of order embeddings is to compare orders by size and shape, as in the study of well-orders and ordinals.
A trap is to think that a self-embedding preserves the successor relation; it is defined with a quantifier, so it can fail.
