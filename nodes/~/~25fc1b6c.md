## Example

Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, with blocks $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $C = (1, 2)$ and $D = (3)$.

1. $\det A = 2 - 1 = 1$, and the [[$2 \times 2$ formula|8XWC7RKNN1]] gives $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. $A^{-1}B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$, the first column of $A^{-1}$.
3. $CA^{-1} = (1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot (-1),\ 1 \cdot (-1) + 2 \cdot 2) = (-1, 3)$.
4. The [[Schur complement|CA1J41NWGS]] is $S = D - CA^{-1}B = 3 - (-1 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 0) = 3 + 1 = 4$. It is invertible, so $M$ is invertible, by the [[block inverse formula|~801e2989]].
5. $\det M = \det A \cdot S = 1 \cdot 4 = 4$.
6. Upper left block: $A^{-1} + A^{-1}B\, S^{-1}\, CA^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} + \tfrac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ 1 & -3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3/4 & -1/4 \\ -3/4 & 5/4 \end{pmatrix}$.
7. Upper right block: $-A^{-1}B\, S^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} -1/4 \\ 1/4 \end{pmatrix}$.
8. Lower left block: $-S^{-1} CA^{-1} = (1/4, -3/4)$.
9. Lower right block: $S^{-1} = 1/4$.
10. So $M^{-1} = \tfrac{1}{4} \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 & -1 \\ -3 & 5 & 1 \\ 1 & -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
11. Check by multiplying back. Row 1 of $4M^{-1}$ times $M$ is $(6 - 1 - 1,\ 3 - 1 - 2,\ 3 - 0 - 3) = (4, 0, 0)$. Row 2 gives $(-6 + 5 + 1,\ -3 + 5 + 2,\ -3 + 0 + 3) = (0, 4, 0)$. Row 3 gives $(2 - 3 + 1,\ 1 - 3 + 2,\ 1 + 0 + 3) = (0, 0, 4)$. So $M^{-1} M = I_3$.

## Remarks

In step 6 the product $A^{-1}B \cdot CA^{-1}$ is a column times a row, so it is the rank one matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} (-1, 3) = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ 1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$.
The block formula used one $2 \times 2$ inverse and one division, while row reduction of $[M \mid I_3]$ needs nine row operations or more.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We split the matrix into a $2 \times 2$ block, a column, a row and a single number.
We invert the small block, compute one number $S$, and build the inverse from these pieces.
Multiplying back confirms the answer.

### L2 human

The Schur complement $S = 4$ is also the last pivot in elimination on $M$: $R_2 \to R_2 - \tfrac{1}{2} R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - \tfrac{1}{2} R_1$ give rows $(0, \tfrac12, -\tfrac12)$ and $(0, \tfrac32, \tfrac52)$.
Then $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_2$ gives $(0, 0, 4)$.
The pivots $2$, $\tfrac12$ and $4$ have product $4 = \det M$.
