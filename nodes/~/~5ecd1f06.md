## Proof

1. Assume that $T$ is cyclic with cyclic vector $v$, so $Z(v; T) = V$.
2. By part 6 of the [[lemma on cyclic subspaces|~9c6edc2a]], the restriction of $T$ to $Z(v; T) = V$, which is $T$ itself, has minimal and characteristic polynomial $m_v$.
3. So $m_T = m_v = \chi_T$.
4. Conversely, assume $m_T = \chi_T$. Then $\deg m_T = \deg \chi_T = n$.
5. By the [[lemma on a vector of maximal annihilator|~a478c4ba]], there is $v \in V$ with $m_v = m_T$.
6. By part 3 of the lemma on cyclic subspaces, $\dim Z(v; T) = \deg m_v = n = \dim V$.
7. A subspace of $V$ of [[dimension|03GSZ8RTZJ]] $\dim V$ is $V$ itself. So $Z(v; T) = V$, and $T$ is cyclic.
8. Finally, for a cyclic vector $v$, part 5 of the lemma on cyclic subspaces gives the matrix $C(m_v) = C(\chi_T)$ in the basis $v, Tv, \dots, T^{n-1} v$.

## Idea

A cyclic vector $v$ gives a basis in which $T$ is a companion matrix, and a companion matrix has equal minimal and characteristic polynomials.
Conversely, a vector with annihilator $m_T$ generates a cyclic subspace of dimension $\deg m_T$, which is $n$ when $m_T = \chi_T$.

## Variants

With the [[cyclic decomposition theorem|~168ae65b]]: $V = Z(v_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus Z(v_r)$ with $\chi_T = d_1 \cdots d_r$ and $m_T = d_r$.
Since each $d_i$ is nonconstant, $m_T = \chi_T$ holds if and only if $r = 1$, that is, $T$ is cyclic.
