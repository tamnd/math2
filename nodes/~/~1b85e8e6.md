## Proof

Write $\Omega(X)$ for the set of open sets of $X$.
For modal formulas, read $\square$ as $\mathrm{Int}$, with any subsets of $X$ as values of variables, as in the [[McKinsey-Tarski theorem|2VF0546VXK]].
Let $T$ be the [[Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation|BXVNEN55MR]].

Part A: the Heyting algebra of opens.

1. $\Omega(X)$ is a bounded distributive lattice under $\cap$ and $\cup$, with bounds $\emptyset$ and $X$.
2. Let $U, W, O$ be open. Then $O \cap U \subseteq W$ if and only if $O \subseteq (X \setminus U) \cup W$.
3. Since $O$ is open, $O \subseteq (X \setminus U) \cup W$ if and only if $O \subseteq \mathrm{Int}((X \setminus U) \cup W)$.
4. So $U \to W = \mathrm{Int}((X \setminus U) \cup W)$ is the relative pseudocomplement, and $\Omega(X)$ is a [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]]. This proves item 1.

Part B: the translation lemma.

5. Let $V$ be a topological valuation. View $V$ also as a modal valuation.
6. Claim: $[\![\varphi]\!] = [\![T(\varphi)]\!]$ for every intuitionistic formula $\varphi$, where the right side is the modal value. We use induction on $\varphi$.
7. Variables: $[\![T(p)]\!] = \mathrm{Int}\, V(p) = V(p)$, since $V(p)$ is open.
8. The cases $\bot$, $\wedge$ and $\vee$ follow from the induction hypothesis, since $T$ commutes with these connectives.
9. Implication: $[\![T(\varphi \to \psi)]\!] = \mathrm{Int}((X \setminus [\![T(\varphi)]\!]) \cup [\![T(\psi)]\!])$. By the induction hypothesis this is $[\![\varphi \to \psi]\!]$.

Part C: soundness.

10. Let $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash \varphi$. By the soundness half of the Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski theorem, $\mathsf{S4} \vdash T(\varphi)$.
11. By item 1 of the McKinsey-Tarski theorem, $T(\varphi)$ is valid in $X$ under every modal valuation.
12. In particular this holds for every topological valuation. By step 6, $[\![\varphi]\!] = X$. This proves item 2.

Part D: completeness.

13. Let $X$ be $\R$, and let $\varphi$ be valid in $X$. Let $V$ be any modal valuation on $X$.
14. Put $V'(p) = \mathrm{Int}\, V(p)$. This is a topological valuation.
15. In $T(\varphi)$, each variable $p$ occurs only inside the subformula $\square p$.
16. $[\![\square p]\!]_V = \mathrm{Int}\, V(p) = \mathrm{Int}\, \mathrm{Int}\, V(p) = [\![\square p]\!]_{V'}$. So $[\![T(\varphi)]\!]_V = [\![T(\varphi)]\!]_{V'}$, by induction on subformulas.
17. By step 6 and the validity of $\varphi$, $[\![T(\varphi)]\!]_{V'} = [\![\varphi]\!]_{V'} = X$.
18. So $T(\varphi)$ is valid in $\R$ under every modal valuation. By item 2 of the McKinsey-Tarski theorem, $\mathsf{S4} \vdash T(\varphi)$.
19. By faithfulness of the Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation, $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash \varphi$. This proves item 3.
20. For the Cantor space we cite, without proof, the generalization of McKinsey and Tarski: every dense-in-itself separable metric space has modal logic $\mathsf{S4}$. The Cantor space is such a space.
21. Steps 13 to 19, with this cited result in place of step 18, prove item 4.
22. Item 5 follows from items 2, 3 and 4.

## Idea

The open-set clauses are exactly what the Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation computes in the interior semantics of $\mathsf{S4}$.
So the intuitionistic value of a formula equals the modal value of its translation.
Soundness and completeness then come from the modal side: from $\mathsf{S4}$ soundness, from the McKinsey-Tarski theorem, and from faithfulness of the translation.
Replacing each value $V(p)$ by its interior does not change the translation, which settles arbitrary modal valuations.

## Variants

1. A direct proof avoids modal logic. It uses [[Kripke completeness|~ac029000]] with finite rooted frames, and builds a continuous open map from $\R$ onto each finite rooted frame with its up-set topology.
2. For the Cantor space, a direct proof goes through [[Beth semantics|~957fdb6a]] on the full binary tree, whose barred up-sets are the opens of the Cantor space.
