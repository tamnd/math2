## Definition

Let $ABC$ and $A'B'C'$ be [[triangles|~68dd3a3a]] in the Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$, and let $d$ be the [[Euclidean distance|~5eb2e3e8]].
The triangles are similar, written $ABC \sim A'B'C'$, if there is a real number $r > 0$ such that
$$d(A', B') = r \, d(A, B), \quad d(B', C') = r \, d(B, C) \quad \text{and} \quad d(C', A') = r \, d(C, A).$$
The number $r$ is the ratio of the similarity.

## Remarks

Similar triangles have equal corresponding angles. Conversely, the [[angle-angle criterion|~fcaf8969]] says that two pairs of equal angles are enough for similarity.
[[Congruent triangles|~cba4b231]] are the similar triangles with the ratio $r = 1$.
Two triangles are similar if and only if a [[homothety|~b96c7299]] followed by an isometry sends one onto the other, vertex by vertex.
Any two equilateral triangles are similar.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A photo of a triangle, enlarged or reduced, gives a similar triangle. All sides change by the same factor, and the angles stay the same. For example, the triangles with sides $3, 4, 5$ and $6, 8, 10$ are similar with the ratio $2$.
