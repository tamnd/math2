## Proof

Let $g = \dim E_\lambda$ and $m$ be the algebraic multiplicity of $\lambda$.

Lower bound.

1. By the definition of an [[eigenvalue|0ZND7NQ970]], there is $v \ne 0$ with $T(v) = \lambda v$.
2. Then $v \in E_\lambda$, by the definition of the [[eigenspace|0AYEPBQMAW]]. So $E_\lambda \ne 0$ and $g \ge 1$.

Upper bound.

3. Choose a basis $u_1, \dots, u_g$ of $E_\lambda$. It is independent in $V$, so it [[extends to a basis|0M4SXE5WW3]] $B = (u_1, \dots, u_g, w_1, \dots, w_{n-g})$ of $V$.
4. For $j \le g$, $T(u_j) = \lambda u_j$, because $u_j \in E_\lambda$.
5. By the definition of the [[matrix of a linear map|0YYMY95FWS]], column $j$ of $[T]_B$ is $\lambda e_j$ for $j \le g$.
6. So $[T]_B = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda I_g & C \\ 0 & D \end{pmatrix}$ for some $g \times (n - g)$ block $C$ and some $(n - g) \times (n - g)$ block $D$.
7. If $g = n$, then $[T]_B = \lambda I_n$, and $p_T = (t - \lambda)^n$. So $m = n = g$.
8. If $g < n$, then the theorem on the [[characteristic polynomial of an endomorphism|~756cfc1f]], part 2, gives $p_T = p_{\lambda I_g} \cdot p_D$.
9. $t I_g - \lambda I_g$ is diagonal with entries $t - \lambda$, so $p_{\lambda I_g} = (t - \lambda)^g$, by the [[determinant of a triangular matrix|0K1KKMFHYH]].
10. So $p_T = (t - \lambda)^g \, p_D$, and $(t - \lambda)^g$ divides $p_T$.
11. By the definition of the [[algebraic multiplicity|0S7A0EMPF4]], $m$ is the largest exponent $k$ with $(t - \lambda)^k \mid p_T$. So $g \le m$.

The matrix case follows by applying this to the map $x \mapsto Ax$ on $F^n$.

## Idea

Start a basis with a basis of the eigenspace.
In this basis the matrix has the block $\lambda I_g$ in the top left corner and zeros below it.
The characteristic polynomial then contains the factor $(t - \lambda)^g$, so the root $\lambda$ has multiplicity at least $g$.
The factor $p_D$ can contain more factors $t - \lambda$, which is why the inequality can be strict.

## Variants

- With the [[generalised eigenspace|0Z3GDY89T5]] $G_\lambda$: $E_\lambda \subseteq G_\lambda$ and $\dim G_\lambda = m$. This needs the generalised eigenspace decomposition, a later and longer result.
- With the [[Jordan normal form|085HQ5G5TE]] over an algebraically closed field: $g$ is the number of blocks for $\lambda$ and $m$ is their total size.
