## Proof

We expand each term by the vector triple product and add.

1. By the [[vector triple product|03FTB26QYD]], $a \times (b \times c) = (a \cdot c) \, b - (a \cdot b) \, c$.
2. By the same identity with $(b, c, a)$ in place of $(a, b, c)$, $b \times (c \times a) = (b \cdot a) \, c - (b \cdot c) \, a$.
3. By the same identity with $(c, a, b)$ in place of $(a, b, c)$, $c \times (a \times b) = (c \cdot b) \, a - (c \cdot a) \, b$.
4. The [[dot product|0S52TNNTH9]] is symmetric, so $b \cdot a = a \cdot b$, $c \cdot b = b \cdot c$ and $c \cdot a = a \cdot c$.
5. Add steps 1 to 3. By step 4, the terms with $b$ are $(a \cdot c) \, b - (a \cdot c) \, b = 0$.
6. By step 4, the terms with $c$ are $-(a \cdot b) \, c + (a \cdot b) \, c = 0$.
7. By step 4, the terms with $a$ are $-(b \cdot c) \, a + (b \cdot c) \, a = 0$.
8. So the sum of the three terms is $0$. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Each term of the Jacobi sum is a combination of $a$, $b$, $c$ with dot products as coefficients.
The cyclic shift makes each coefficient appear twice, once with each sign.

## Variants

- Direct coordinates: both sides are polynomials in the nine coordinates. The first coordinate of the sum has twelve terms that cancel in pairs.
- Lie algebra proof: the map $a \mapsto [a]_\times$ to $3 \times 3$ antisymmetric matrices turns $\times$ into the commutator. The [[Jacobi identity for commutators|ZKFN197MBR]] then gives the result.
