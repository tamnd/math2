## Example

Let $u = (1, 2, 3)$ and $v = (1, 1, 1)$.
We compute the [[vector projection|0JW13B48YP]] $p$ of $u$ along $v$ and the perpendicular part $q = u - p$.

1. The [[dot products|0S52TNNTH9]] are $u \cdot v = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6$ and $v \cdot v = 3$.
2. So $c = \tfrac{6}{3} = 2$ and $p = 2v = (2, 2, 2)$.
3. The perpendicular part is $q = u - p = (-1, 0, 1)$.
4. Check of perpendicularity: $q \cdot v = -1 + 0 + 1 = 0$.
5. Check of Pythagoras: $\|u\|^2 = 14$, $\|p\|^2 = 12$ and $\|q\|^2 = 2$, and $12 + 2 = 14$.
6. The scalar component is $\operatorname{comp}_v u = \tfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = 2\sqrt{3}$, which is $\|p\|$.

## Remarks

The vector $p = (2, 2, 2)$ has all components equal to $2$, the mean of $1$, $2$ and $3$.
This is general: the projection of any $u \in \mathbb{R}^n$ along $(1, \dots, 1)$ is the mean of the components times $(1, \dots, 1)$.
The perpendicular part $q$ is the list of deviations from the mean, and $\|q\|^2 = 2$ is the sum of the squared deviations.
If we replace $v$ by $(2, 2, 2)$, the projection does not change, since the factor $2$ cancels.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The direction $(1, 1, 1)$ means "the same amount in each component".
The part of $(1, 2, 3)$ in this direction is $(2, 2, 2)$, the average value in each place.
What is left, $(-1, 0, 1)$, says how far each component is from the average.

### L2 human

The distance from the point $(1, 2, 3)$ to the line through $0$ in the direction $(1, 1, 1)$ is $\|q\| = \sqrt{2}$.
Statistics uses the same split: the mean and the deviations are perpendicular parts of the data vector.
A trap: dividing by $\|v\| = \sqrt{3}$ instead of $v \cdot v = 3$ gives the wrong vector $(2\sqrt{3}, 2\sqrt{3}, 2\sqrt{3})$.
