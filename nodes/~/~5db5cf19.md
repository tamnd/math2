## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\R$.

1. Expand $\det(tI - A)$ along the third column, whose only nonzero entry is $t - 1$. This gives $p_A(t) = (t - 1)\big((t - 1)(t - 3) + 1\big) = (t - 1)(t - 2)^2$.
2. Second method, by the [[coefficients of the characteristic polynomial|0FRYPZ6D0X]]: $\tr A = 5$. The principal $2 \times 2$ minors are $4$, $1$ and $3$, with sum $8$. $\det A = 4$. So $p_A(t) = t^3 - 5t^2 + 8t - 4$, which equals $(t - 1)(t - 2)^2$.
3. Eigenvalue $1$: $A(0, 0, 1) = (0, 0, 1)$.
4. Eigenvalue $2$: $A(1, 1, 0) = (1 + 1,\ -1 + 3,\ -1 + 1) = (2, 2, 0) = 2(1, 1, 0)$.
5. $A - 2I = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $2$. By the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]], the [[geometric multiplicity|06YSJVR356]] of $2$ is $1$, but its [[algebraic multiplicity|0S7A0EMPF4]] is $2$.
6. $A - I = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $2$, so the geometric multiplicity of $1$ is $1$.
7. $(A - I)(A - 2I) = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \ne 0$.
8. $(A - 2I)^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \ne 0$.
9. Every proper monic divisor of $p_A$ divides $(t - 1)(t - 2)$ or $(t - 2)^2$. By steps 7 and 8, neither of these annihilates $A$, so no proper divisor does.
10. By the [[Cayley-Hamilton theorem|0F765KFC2J]], $p_A(A) = 0$. Since the [[minimal polynomial divides every annihilating polynomial|0VTAD25A3G]], $m_A$ is a monic divisor of $p_A$. By step 9, $m_A = p_A = (t - 1)(t - 2)^2$.

## Remarks

- The matrix is $A = PJP^{-1}$ with $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $J = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
- The eigenvectors span only a plane, so $A$ is not [[diagonalisable|07SQCNV233]].
- The squared factor $(t - 2)^2$ in $m_A$ shows the same failure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The eigenvalue $2$ is a double root of the characteristic polynomial.
But it has only one line of eigenvectors.
So the matrix cannot be made diagonal.

### L2 human

The typical use is to find $m_A$ by testing the divisors of $p_A$ that contain each linear factor.
Here the two candidates of degree $2$ both fail, so $m_A = p_A$.
A trap is to stop after finding that $(A - I)(A - 2I)$ has a zero row.
