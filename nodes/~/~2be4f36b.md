## Proof

1. Let $n = \dim V$. The $n + 1$ vectors $v, Tv, \dots, T^n v$ are linearly dependent, since $V$ has dimension $n$.
2. So there is a nonzero $g \in F[x]$ of degree at most $n$ with $g(T) v = 0$. Dividing by its leading coefficient, we can take $g$ monic.
3. So the set of monic $f$ with $f(T)v = 0$ is not empty. Let $m_v$ be one of least degree.
4. Let $f \in F[x]$ with $f(T)v = 0$. By [[division with remainder|0MES3KS0ZC]], $f = q m_v + r$ with $r = 0$ or $\deg r < \deg m_v$.
5. Then $r(T) v = f(T)v - q(T) m_v(T) v = 0$, since $(q m_v)(T) = q(T) m_v(T)$ for a [[polynomial of an endomorphism|0E3MV6E6MG]].
6. If $r \ne 0$, then $r$ divided by its leading coefficient is monic, kills $v$ and has smaller degree than $m_v$. This contradicts step 3. So $r = 0$ and $m_v \mid f$.
7. Conversely, if $m_v \mid f$, say $f = q m_v$, then $f(T) v = q(T) m_v(T) v = 0$. This proves part 2.
8. If $m'$ is another monic polynomial of least degree that kills $v$, then $m_v \mid m'$ by step 6, and both are monic of the same degree, so $m' = m_v$. This proves part 1.
9. Let $k = \deg m_v$ and write $m_v = x^k + c_{k-1} x^{k-1} + \cdots + c_0$. Suppose $\sum_{i<k} a_i T^i v = 0$ with some $a_i \ne 0$. Then $h = \sum_{i<k} a_i x^i$ is nonzero, kills $v$ and has degree less than $k$.
10. By step 6, $m_v \mid h$, which is impossible for a nonzero $h$ with $\deg h < \deg m_v$. So $v, Tv, \dots, T^{k-1} v$ are [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]].
11. Let $f \in F[x]$. By step 4, $f = q m_v + r$ with $\deg r < k$ or $r = 0$, and $f(T) v = r(T) v$ by step 5. So $f(T) v$ is a combination of $v, \dots, T^{k-1} v$.
12. Since $Z(v; T)$ is the set of all $f(T) v$, steps 10 and 11 show that $v, \dots, T^{k-1} v$ is a [[basis|0KCQ9Y3QKA]] of $Z(v; T)$. This proves part 3.
13. For $w = f(T) v$ in $Z(v; T)$, $Tw = (x f)(T) v \in Z(v; T)$. So $Z(v; T)$ is [[$T$-invariant|0ACX5QT8TE]].
14. If $U$ is $T$-invariant and $v \in U$, then $T^i v \in U$ for all $i$ by induction on $i$, so $Z(v; T) \subseteq U$. This proves part 4.
15. Let $v \ne 0$ and $b_i = T^{i-1} v$ for $1 \le i \le k$. Then $T b_i = b_{i+1}$ for $i < k$.
16. Also $T b_k = T^k v = -c_0 v - c_1 T v - \cdots - c_{k-1} T^{k-1} v$, since $m_v(T) v = 0$.
17. The columns of the [[matrix of $T|_{Z(v;T)}$|0YQSGNYE3K]] are the coordinates of $T b_1, \dots, T b_k$. By steps 15 and 16, this matrix is the [[companion matrix|0VH1TC1PWF]] $C(m_v)$. This proves part 5.
18. By the [[theorem on the polynomials of a companion matrix|~dd227218]], $C(m_v)$ has minimal and characteristic polynomial $m_v$. These are the polynomials of $T|_{Z(v;T)}$, since they do not depend on the basis. For $v = 0$ both equal $1$. This proves part 6.

## Idea

The vectors $v, Tv, T^2 v, \dots$ stay independent until the first one that depends on the earlier ones.
The dependency at that step is the annihilator $m_v$, and division with remainder reduces every $f(T) v$ to the earlier vectors.
In the resulting basis, $T$ shifts each vector to the next one, which is the shape of a companion matrix.

## Variants

In module language: the map $F[x] \to Z(v; T)$, $f \mapsto f(T)v$, is surjective with kernel $(m_v)$, so $Z(v; T) \cong F[x]/(m_v)$.
The classes of $1, x, \dots, x^{k-1}$ form a basis of $F[x]/(m_v)$ by division with remainder, and this gives part 3 again.
