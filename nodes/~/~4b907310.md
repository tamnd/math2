## Proof

We use the system $H$ and the notation of the [[statement|~331f6430]].
For a [[structure|983GZBQYT4]] $\mathcal{A}$ with universe $A$, an assignment $s$ and an element $a \in A$, write $s(x \mid a)$ for the assignment that agrees with $s$ except that it sends $x$ to $a$.
Write $\bar s(t)$ for the value of a [[term|6D3WA7N2W4]] $t$ in $\mathcal{A}$ under $s$.
We use two facts about [[satisfaction|2RBQPV02AS]], both proved by induction on formulas in the semantics of first-order logic (Enderton, Chapter 2).

- Coincidence lemma: if $s_1$ and $s_2$ agree on the free variables of $\alpha$, then $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s_1]$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s_2]$.
- Substitution lemma: if $t$ is substitutable for $x$ in $\alpha$, then $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha^x_t[s]$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s(x \mid \bar s(t))]$.

A formula is valid when it is true in every structure under every assignment.

Step A: every logical axiom is valid.

1. If $\lambda$ is valid, then $\forall x \, \lambda$ is valid. By the clause for $\forall$, $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \lambda[s]$ means $\mathcal{A} \models \lambda[s(x \mid a)]$ for every $a \in A$, and each of these holds because $\lambda$ is valid.
2. By step 1 and induction on $k$, it is enough to show that each formula $\lambda$ of the six groups is valid. Fix $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ for steps 3 to 13.
3. Group 1. Define a truth assignment $v$ on the prime formulas by $v(\beta) = \text{true}$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \beta[s]$.
4. Let $\bar v$ be the extension of $v$ to all formulas by the truth tables of $\neg$ and $\to$. The clauses of satisfaction for $\neg$ and $\to$ are the same truth tables. So $\bar v(\gamma) = \text{true}$ if and only if $\mathcal{A} \models \gamma[s]$, by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $\gamma$ over its prime parts.
5. A [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]] $\alpha$ has $\bar v(\alpha) = \text{true}$ for every $v$. By step 4, $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s]$.
6. Group 2. Assume $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \alpha[s]$. Then $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s(x \mid a)]$ for every $a$, in particular for $a = \bar s(t)$.
7. The term $t$ is substitutable for $x$ in $\alpha$ by the side condition of group 2. The substitution lemma turns step 6 into $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha^x_t[s]$. So the implication of group 2 is true at $s$.
8. Group 3. Assume $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, (\alpha \to \beta)[s]$ and $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \alpha[s]$. For each $a \in A$, both $\alpha \to \beta$ and $\alpha$ are true at $s(x \mid a)$, so $\beta$ is true at $s(x \mid a)$. Hence $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \beta[s]$.
9. Group 4. Assume $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s]$, where $x$ is not free in $\alpha$. For each $a$, the assignments $s$ and $s(x \mid a)$ agree on the free variables of $\alpha$.
10. The coincidence lemma gives $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s(x \mid a)]$ for every $a$, so $\mathcal{A} \models \forall x \, \alpha[s]$.
11. Group 5. The formula $x = x$ is true at $s$, because $s(x) = s(x)$.
12. Group 6. Assume $s(x) = s(y)$. Let $u'$ come from a term $u$ by replacing some occurrences of $x$ by $y$. Then $\bar s(u') = \bar s(u)$, by induction on $u$: the replaced variable has the same value, and a function symbol applied to arguments with equal values gives equal values.
13. An atomic $\alpha$ is $P u_1 \dots u_n$ or $u_1 = u_2$, and $\alpha'$ is $P u'_1 \dots u'_n$ or $u'_1 = u'_2$. By step 12 the arguments have the same values, so $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ have the same truth value at $s$. Hence $x = y \to (\alpha \to \alpha')$ is true at $s$.

Step B: derivations keep truth.

14. [[Modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] keeps truth: if $\mathcal{A} \models \alpha[s]$ and $\mathcal{A} \models (\alpha \to \beta)[s]$, then $\mathcal{A} \models \beta[s]$, by the clause for $\to$.
15. Let $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ with a derivation $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n = \varphi$. Let $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ satisfy every member of $\Gamma$.
16. We show $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi_i[s]$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$ by [[strong induction|0KMVFSHFGZ]] on $i$. Assume it for all $j < i$.
17. Case 1: $\varphi_i$ is a logical axiom. It is valid by Step A, so it is true at $s$.
18. Case 2: $\varphi_i \in \Gamma$. It is true at $s$ by the choice of $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ in step 15.
19. Case 3: $\varphi_i$ follows by MP from $\varphi_j$ and $\varphi_k = (\varphi_j \to \varphi_i)$ with $j, k < i$. Both are true at $s$ by the induction hypothesis, so $\varphi_i$ is true at $s$ by step 14.
20. The three cases cover every line of a derivation, so $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi[s]$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ were arbitrary, $\Gamma \models \varphi$. This is part 1.
21. Part 2 is part 1 with $\Gamma = \emptyset$.
22. Part 3: let $\mathcal{A}$ and $s$ satisfy $\Gamma$, and suppose $\Gamma \vdash \psi$ and $\Gamma \vdash \neg \psi$. Part 1 gives $\mathcal{A} \models \psi[s]$ and $\mathcal{A} \models \neg \psi[s]$. The clause for $\neg$ makes these incompatible, so $\Gamma$ is [[consistent|VM8TQ70CVJ]].

## Idea

The valid formulas include all axioms, and modus ponens takes true formulas to a true formula.
So an induction along a derivation shows that each line is true in every structure, under every assignment, that satisfies the hypotheses.
The only delicate cases are the quantifier axioms, where the side conditions on substitution and on free variables are used through the substitution and coincidence lemmas.

## Variants

- In a Hilbert system with the rule of generalization, the induction has a fourth case. It is sound when the generalized variable is not free in any member of $\Gamma$ used above it, by the coincidence lemma.
- For natural deduction and the sequent calculus, the induction runs over the derivation tree. Each rule is checked once, for example the right rule for $\forall$ with its eigenvariable condition.
- The [[soundness proof for propositional logic|WFBG5RAG36]] is the same induction without Steps 6 to 13.
