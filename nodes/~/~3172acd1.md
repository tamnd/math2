## Proof

We prove $1 \Rightarrow 2 \Rightarrow 3 \Rightarrow 4 \Rightarrow 1$, and then the claim about $[A \mid I_n]$.

**$1 \Rightarrow 2$.**

1. Let $Ax = 0$. By [[associativity|073E4XC016]], $x = I_n x = (A^{-1} A) x = A^{-1}(Ax) = A^{-1} 0 = 0$.

**$2 \Rightarrow 3$.**

2. By [[Gauss-Jordan elimination|098D6FQN3N]], a finite sequence of row operations turns $A$ into its [[reduced row echelon form|0WF5AQYTW6]] $R$.
3. By the [[row operation theorem|~11309c07]], $R = EA$, where $E$ is a product of elementary matrices, and each elementary matrix has an elementary inverse.
4. So $Ax = 0$ implies $Rx = E(Ax) = 0$; and $Rx = 0$ implies $Ax = E_1^{-1} \cdots E_k^{-1} R x = 0$, where $E = E_k \cdots E_1$. Thus $Ax = 0$ and $Rx = 0$ have the same solutions.
5. Suppose that some column $k$ of $R$ contains no [[pivot|0J0BBCMJ5W]]. Let the pivots of $R$ be in rows $1, \dots, r$ and columns $j_1 < \dots < j_r$.
6. Define $x \in F^n$ by $x_k = 1$, $x_{j_i} = -r_{ik}$ for $i = 1, \dots, r$, and $x_l = 0$ for the other indices $l$.
7. Row $i \le r$ of $Rx$ is $\sum_l r_{il} x_l$. The pivot column $j_i$ gives $1 \cdot (-r_{ik})$, column $k$ gives $r_{ik} \cdot 1$. The other pivot columns give $0$, since a pivot column of $R$ has zeros outside its pivot row. The other non-pivot columns give $0$, since $x_l = 0$ there. So row $i$ of $Rx$ is $0$.
8. Rows $i > r$ of $R$ are zero, so they give $0$ as well. So $Rx = 0$ with $x \ne 0$, since $x_k = 1$.
9. By step 4, $Ax = 0$ with $x \ne 0$. This contradicts condition 2, so each column of $R$ is a pivot column.
10. So $R$ has $n$ pivots in $n$ different rows, and the columns $j_1 < \dots < j_n$ are $1, \dots, n$; hence $j_i = i$.
11. Each pivot is $1$ and each pivot column has zeros elsewhere, so column $i$ of $R$ is $e_i$. Thus $R = I_n$.

**$3 \Rightarrow 4$.**

12. By steps 2 and 3, $I_n = E_k \cdots E_1 A$ with elementary matrices $E_s$.
13. Multiply on the left by $E_1^{-1} \cdots E_k^{-1}$. By associativity, $E_s^{-1} E_s = I_n$ cancels from the inside out, and we get $A = E_1^{-1} E_2^{-1} \cdots E_k^{-1}$.
14. Each $E_s^{-1}$ is elementary, by the row operation theorem. So $A$ is a product of elementary matrices.

**$4 \Rightarrow 1$.**

15. Let $A = F_1 F_2 \cdots F_k$ with elementary matrices $F_s$, and let $B = F_k^{-1} \cdots F_2^{-1} F_1^{-1}$, where each $F_s^{-1}$ exists by the row operation theorem.
16. We show $AB = I_n$ by [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $k$. For $k = 1$, $F_1 F_1^{-1} = I_n$. For $k \ge 2$, $AB = F_1 (F_2 \cdots F_k F_k^{-1} \cdots F_2^{-1}) F_1^{-1}$ by associativity, and the middle factor is $I_n$ by the induction hypothesis; so $AB = F_1 F_1^{-1} = I_n$.
17. In the same way, $BA = F_k^{-1} (F_{k-1}^{-1} \cdots F_1^{-1} F_1 \cdots F_{k-1}) F_k = F_k^{-1} F_k = I_n$.
18. By steps 16 and 17 and the [[definition|0TWTS88TWN]], $A$ is invertible with $A^{-1} = B$.

**The inverse by row reduction.**

19. Let the conditions hold, and let row operations with matrices $E_1, \dots, E_k$ reduce $A$ to $I_n$. Let $E = E_k \cdots E_1$, so $EA = I_n$.
20. By the uniqueness argument of [[the uniqueness of the inverse|~139b320d]], $E = E(AA^{-1}) = (EA)A^{-1} = A^{-1}$.
21. The same operations act on the block matrix $[A \mid I_n]$ by left multiplication with $E$, so they give $[EA \mid EI_n] = [I_n \mid A^{-1}]$.

## Idea

Elimination is left multiplication by invertible matrices, so it does not change the solutions of $Ax = 0$.
A missing pivot gives a free variable and so a nonzero solution.
With no missing pivot, the reduced form is $I_n$, and the record of the row operations is the inverse.

## Variants

The step $2 \Rightarrow 3$ can also be phrased with rank.
If $Ax = 0$ only for $x = 0$, then the columns are independent.
So the [[rank|0ZC9YTXZRY]] is $n$ and there are $n$ pivots.
