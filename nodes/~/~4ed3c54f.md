## Definition

Let $G$ be a [[graph|~3f82cef4]].
A perfect matching of $G$ is a [[matching|~4a67f6e1]] $M$ such that every vertex of $G$ is an end of an edge of $M$.

## Remarks

In a perfect matching, each vertex is an end of exactly one edge of $M$. Thus a finite graph with a perfect matching has an even number of vertices.
[[Hall's theorem for bipartite graphs|~b72f8931]] characterizes the bipartite graphs with a perfect matching. [[Tutte's theorem|~7eb98711]] does this for all finite graphs.
The [[complete graph|~b29ee1b7]] $K_4$ has $3$ perfect matchings.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A perfect matching pairs up all vertices of a graph along edges, with nobody left alone.
For this, the number of vertices must be even.
