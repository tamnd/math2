## Definition

A [[set|~bd92ceee]] $A$ is uncountable when it is not [[countable|~589c0629]], that is, when there is no [[injective function|~3e705960]] from $A$ to $\mathbb{N}$.

## Remarks

The real numbers and the [[power set|~1481eeb8]] of $\mathbb{N}$ are uncountable.
With the axiom of choice, a set is uncountable exactly when its cardinality is at least $\aleph_1$, the least cardinal larger than $\aleph_0$.
A superset of an uncountable set is uncountable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For an uncountable set, every attempt to list its elements misses some of them.
[[Cantor's diagonal argument|~f8f55f65]] shows how to find a missing element.
