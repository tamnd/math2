## Example

Solve $Ax = b$ and $Ax = c$ over $\R$ for $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$, $b = (5, 6)^T$ and $c = (1, 0)^T$.

1. Form the [[augmented matrix|0YCDZ7N8VD]] $\left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 5 & 1 \\ 3 & 4 & 6 & 0 \end{array}\right]$.
2. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1$ gives $(0, -2 \mid -9, -3)$.
3. $R_2 \leftarrow -\tfrac12 R_2$ gives $(0, 1 \mid \tfrac92, \tfrac32)$.
4. $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 2R_2$ gives $(1, 0 \mid -4, -2)$.
5. Read the columns: $Ax = b$ has $x = (-4, \tfrac92)$, and $Ax = c$ has $x = (-2, \tfrac32)$.
6. Check the first: $-4 + 9 = 5$ and $-12 + 18 = 6$.
7. Check the second: $-2 + 3 = 1$ and $-6 + 6 = 0$.

## Remarks

The [[elementary row operations|04XWH28452]] depend only on $A$, so one reduction serves both right sides.
With $c$ and $(0, 1)^T$ as the two right sides, the same work gives the inverse of $A$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Both systems have the same left sides, so the same steps solve both.
We write both right sides behind the bar and do the steps once.

### L2 human

The typical use is to solve $AX = B$ for a matrix $X$, column by column.

1. Put all columns of $B$ behind the bar.
2. Reduce until the left block is the identity.
3. The right block is then $X$.

A trap: a pivot that appears in a column right of the bar does not belong to $A$; it means that one of the systems is inconsistent.
