## Statement

Let $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ be the subsystem of [[second-order arithmetic|DC60FYCSPM]] with the basic axioms, the induction axiom for sets, and the [[comprehension scheme|5GS6DRJ5SY]] for $\Pi^1_1$ formulas with set parameters.
Let $(\mathrm{OT}, \prec)$ be [[Buchholz's ordinal notation system|E7WEFRMQTB]], a [[primitive recursive|5NVA2JRHPV]] set of terms with a primitive recursive linear order, as in the page on [[ordinal notation systems|~a8ed0faf]].
Let $\tau \in \mathrm{OT}$ be the term for $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$.
For $a \in \mathrm{OT}$, let $\mathrm{WO}(a)$ be the $\Pi^1_1$ sentence

$$\forall X \, \Big[ \forall x \, \big( \forall y \, (y \prec x \to y \in X) \to x \in X \big) \to \forall x \, (x \prec a \to x \in X) \Big],$$

which says that $\prec$ restricted to the terms below $a$ is well-founded.
Then the following hold.

- (a) For every $a \in \mathrm{OT}$ with $a \prec \tau$, the theory $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ proves $\mathrm{WO}(a)$.
- (b) The theory $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ does not prove $\mathrm{WO}(\tau)$.
- (c) Primitive recursive arithmetic with quantifier-free [[transfinite induction|0ZHQMQ2C1N]] along $\prec$ restricted to the terms below $\tau$ proves the [[consistency|VM8TQ70CVJ]] of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$.

In particular, the [[proof-theoretic ordinal|C72RBH0CDR]] of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ is $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$, in Buchholz's notation.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The restriction of induction to sets matters. The theory $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}$ with the induction scheme for all formulas has the larger ordinal $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega \cdot \varepsilon_0)$ (Wikipedia, "Ordinal analysis", table), so it proves $\mathrm{WO}(\tau)$ and (b) fails for it.
- Adding bar induction gives $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA} + \mathrm{BI}$, whose ordinal is the [[Takeuti-Feferman-Buchholz ordinal|~1df2a94e]] $\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega + 1})$, far above $\tau$.
- The notation system is part of the statement. Part (c) speaks about a particular primitive recursive ordering, and the ordinal $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$ is the order type of that ordering. The value of a proof-theoretic ordinal is only meaningful for a natural notation, in which the order and the terms come from the collapsing functions.
- The bound $\tau$ is sharp. Part (a) gives every term below $\tau$, and part (b) shows that $\tau$ itself is not reached.

### Consequences

- $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ and $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$, the union of the theories of finitely [[iterated inductive definitions|5VNT564Y2W]], have the same ordinal $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$.
- By (c) and [[Gödel's second incompleteness theorem|X9WYNYE29C]], $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ does not prove quantifier-free transfinite induction along $\prec$ below $\tau$. Since $\mathrm{WO}(\tau)$ implies this induction (each quantifier-free formula defines a set), this gives a second proof of (b).
- Combinatorial statements that imply $\mathrm{WO}(\tau)$ over a weak base theory are not provable in $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$. Friedman's extended Kruskal theorem and the graph minor theorem give such statements.

### Converse

The ordinal does not determine the theory.
Other theories have the same ordinal $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$, for example $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ and $\Delta^1_2\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ (Wikipedia, "Ordinal analysis", table).
The first theory is a first-order theory, and the second proves comprehension for $\Delta^1_2$ formulas.
So a theory that proves $\mathrm{WO}(a)$ for exactly the terms $a \prec \tau$ need not prove the same theorems as $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$.

### Generalizations

- Michael Rathjen extended the methods to stronger theories, for example to parameter-free $\Pi^1_2$-comprehension (Archive for Mathematical Logic, 2005) and to the set theory $\mathrm{KPM}$.
- The Wikipedia article on ordinal analysis lists $\Pi^1_2\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ and full second-order arithmetic among the theories whose ordinals have no explicit description yet. Their ordinal analysis is an open problem.
- The same methods, with collapsing functions based on large cardinals, apply to set theories with reflection principles, such as Kripke-Platek set theory with $\Pi_3$-reflection.

## History

Gaisi Takeuti proved the consistency of $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension in "Consistency proofs of subsystems of classical analysis", Annals of Mathematics 86 (1967).
His proof used reductions of proofs in a Gentzen-style calculus, with ordinal diagrams as the measure of complexity.
Buchholz and Pohlers analysed the theories $\mathrm{ID}_\nu$ with the $\Omega_{\mu+1}$-rule and with [[local predicativity|~af6dfa84]], and these results appear in Lecture Notes in Mathematics 897 (1981).
Buchholz's functions $\psi_\nu$ of 1986 gave the standard form of the ordinal, $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A theory is a list of axioms, and its proof-theoretic ordinal measures how much induction it can prove.
For ordinary arithmetic the measure is $\varepsilon_0$.
The theory $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ lets us form sets described with one "for all sets" quantifier, and this is much stronger.
Its measure is the ordinal $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$, named with Buchholz's collapsing functions and a ladder of infinities $\Omega_1, \Omega_2, \dots$.
The theorem says that the theory can prove well-ordering for every notation below this one, and not for this one.

### L2 human

We see how (a) follows from the analysis of the theories $\mathrm{ID}_n$.

1. The ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_n$ is $\psi_0(\Omega_{n+1})$, and $\mathrm{ID}_n$ proves that every term below $\psi_0(\Omega_{n+1})$ lies in the accessible part of $\prec$.
2. The ordinal of $\mathrm{ID}_{<\omega}$ is $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$, so every term $a \prec \tau$ lies below $\psi_0(\Omega_{n+1})$ for some $n$.
3. The theory $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ interprets $\mathrm{ID}_n$: each inductively defined set is the intersection of all sets closed under its operator.
4. Under this interpretation, "$a$ is in the accessible part" becomes $\mathrm{WO}(a)$.

The typical use of the theorem is to show that a mathematical statement is not provable in $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$, by deriving $\mathrm{WO}(\tau)$ from it.
The trap is to give $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ the ordinal of $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA} + \mathrm{BI}$: these theories differ in induction, and their ordinals differ.

### L3 human

The proofs of this theorem and of its relatives for $\mathrm{ID}_\nu$ created the modern tools of impredicative proof theory: collapsing functions, the $\Omega$-rule and local predicativity.
In reverse mathematics, $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$ is the strongest of the "big five" subsystems, and many theorems of analysis and combinatorics are equivalent to it over a weak base.
The ordinal $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$ is the reason that some finite combinatorial statements, such as forms of the graph minor theorem, are not provable in this system.
The next steps of the program are theories with $\Pi^1_2$-comprehension, where the large cardinals of the notation must be replaced by stronger reflecting cardinals, as in [[Rathjen's psi function|~0d062a69]].
