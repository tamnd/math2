## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. $A^{\mathsf{T}} = A$, so $A$ is [[symmetric|0N28QN4CYA]].
2. $A^* = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and its entry $(1, 2)$ is $-i \neq i$. So $A$ is not [[Hermitian|0EKZCX4K3P]].
3. The characteristic polynomial is $(t - 1)^2 - i^2 = (t - 1)^2 + 1$, so the eigenvalues are $1 + i$ and $1 - i$.
4. A Hermitian matrix has only real eigenvalues, so this confirms that $A$ is not Hermitian.

## Remarks

$A = I + iB$ with $B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and this is its [[Cartesian decomposition|~016d9f4f]] with $H = I$ and $K = B$.
The matrix $iI$ is skew-Hermitian, not Hermitian, because $(iI)^* = -iI$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For complex matrices, being equal to the transpose is not enough.
A Hermitian matrix must equal its transpose with all entries conjugated.

### L2 human

For real matrices, symmetric and Hermitian mean the same.
For complex matrices they differ, and only the Hermitian condition gives real eigenvalues.
