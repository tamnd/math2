## Definition

A *quantifier* on a [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]] $A$ is a map $\exists : A \to A$ with $\exists 0 = 0$, $p \le \exists p$ and $\exists(p \land \exists q) = \exists p \land \exists q$ for all $p, q$.

A *polyadic algebra* is a quadruple $(A, I, S, \exists)$, where $A$ is a Boolean algebra and $I$ is a set.
Here $S$ assigns a Boolean endomorphism $S(\tau)$ of $A$ to each map $\tau : I \to I$, and $\exists$ assigns a quantifier $\exists(J)$ on $A$ to each $J \subseteq I$, such that:

1. $\exists(\emptyset) = \mathrm{id}_A$;
2. $\exists(J \cup K) = \exists(J)\exists(K)$;
3. $S(\mathrm{id}_I) = \mathrm{id}_A$;
4. $S(\sigma \tau) = S(\sigma) S(\tau)$;
5. $S(\sigma)\exists(J) = S(\tau)\exists(J)$ if $\sigma(i) = \tau(i)$ for all $i \in I \setminus J$;
6. $\exists(J) S(\tau) = S(\tau) \exists(\tau^{-1} J)$ if $\tau$ is injective on $\tau^{-1} J$.

## Notation

The set $I$ is the set of variables, and $|I|$ is the degree.
$S(\tau)$ is "replace each $v_i$ by $v_{\tau(i)}$", and $\exists(J)$ is "there exist $v_j$, $j \in J$".

## Remarks

1. Example: [[the functional algebra on $\set{0, 1}^{\set{1, 2}}$|~a3e8d3e7]].
2. Example: [[the algebra of a first-order theory|~1212e7c3]].
3. Non-example: [[universal quantifiers|~f55e9f3e]] satisfy clauses 1 to 6 in the functional model, but they are not quantifiers.
4. Clause 5 says that after quantifying $J$, the values of $\sigma$ on $J$ do not matter.
5. Clause 6 says when a quantifier can be moved past a substitution: the substitution must not merge a quantified variable with another one.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A polyadic algebra has statements, operations that rename variables, and operations that say "there exist".
The six laws are the usual rules for renaming and for bound variables.

### L2 human

1. Let $\tau$ send every variable to $v_1$.
2. Then $\tau^{-1}\set{1} = I$, and $\tau$ is not injective on $I$ if $|I| \ge 2$.
3. So clause 6 does not apply to $\exists(\set{1}) S(\tau)$: in logic, $\exists v_1 \varphi(v_1, v_1)$ is not obtained from $\exists v_1 \exists v_2 \varphi(v_1, v_2)$ by substitution.

The typical use: the clauses let one compute with substitutions without any syntax.
The trap: clause 4 composes maps in the same order as the endomorphisms, which matches the action $x \mapsto x \circ \tau$ on assignments.
