## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring and let $n \ge 0$ be an integer.
A [[square matrix|~92565f6b]] $U = (u_{ij})$ of order $n$ over $R$ is *upper triangular* when $u_{ij} = 0$ for all $i > j$.
It is *strictly upper triangular* when $u_{ij} = 0$ for all $i \ge j$.

## Remarks

Sums and products of upper triangular matrices of the same order are upper triangular.
The [[determinant of a triangular matrix|~aa149925]] is the product of its diagonal entries.
The diagonal entries of an upper triangular matrix over a field are its [[eigenvalues|~13fe038c]].
A system $Ux = b$ with $U$ upper triangular and invertible is solved by [[back substitution|~a2f0b72a]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An upper triangular matrix has zeros everywhere below its diagonal.
Its nonzero entries fill a triangle at the top right.
For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ is upper triangular.
