## Statement

Let $\mathcal F_1$ and $\mathcal F_2$ be [[Frege systems|~3f0577bc]].
Then $\mathcal F_2$ [[p-simulates|~8ae21f34]] $\mathcal F_1$.
So any two Frege systems are p-equivalent.

The theorem holds when $\mathcal F_1$ and $\mathcal F_2$ use different finite functionally complete sets of connectives.
Then the tautologies of $\mathcal F_1$ are first translated into the connectives of $\mathcal F_2$ by a fixed polynomial-time translation.

For two systems over the same set of connectives, the bounds are explicit.
There is a constant $c$ such that an $\mathcal F_1$-proof with $m$ lines and size $S$ becomes an $\mathcal F_2$-proof of the same formula with at most $c m$ lines and size at most $c S^2$.
The measures are the number of lines and the size.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- $\mathcal F_2$ must be implicationally complete. A system that proves every tautology, but cannot derive a conclusion from hypotheses, gives no way to imitate a rule of $\mathcal F_1$ step by step.
- The rules of $\mathcal F_1$ must be finitely many. The constant $c$ is the maximum of the lengths of finitely many fixed derivations, one for each rule.
- The rules of $\mathcal F_1$ must be sound. An unsound rule cannot be derived in the sound system $\mathcal F_2$.

### Consequences

- The size $s_{\mathcal F}(\tau)$ is determined up to a polynomial by the formula $\tau$ alone. So statements such as "Frege systems prove the pigeonhole formulas in polynomial size" do not depend on the choice of the system.
- The open problem of [[lower bounds for Frege systems|~828540d8]] is a question about one class, not about one system.
- In the same way, all [[extended Frege systems|~6925f591]] are p-equivalent, by the same proof.

### Converse

The converse is not a question here: the theorem gives simulation in both directions.
A system that p-simulates every Frege system need not be a Frege system; [[extended Frege|~6925f591]] is an example.

### Generalizations

- The propositional sequent calculus with cut, and natural deduction, are p-equivalent to Frege systems.
- For [[bounded-depth Frege|~2cc592a7]], the proof of the same-connective case keeps the depth bounded, so the class of bounded-depth Frege systems over one basis also does not depend on the choice of rules.

## History

Robert Reckhow proved the theorem in his 1976 doctoral thesis at the University of Toronto, written under Stephen Cook.
The theorem appeared in the 1979 paper of Cook and Reckhow, which introduced the general notion of a propositional proof system.
The case of different connectives is the harder part, because translating a formula into a new basis can make it exponentially longer when done naively.

## Explanations

### L1 human

There are many textbook proof systems with different axioms.
The theorem says that the choice does not matter much.
A proof in one system can be rewritten in any other, and the length grows by at most a fixed polynomial.
For example, a proof of $100$ lines in one system becomes a proof of at most $100 c$ lines in another, for a constant $c$ that depends only on the two systems.

### L2 human

We apply the proof to one rule.

1. Let $\mathcal F_1$ have the rule "from $\varphi$ and $\psi$ infer $\varphi \land \psi$".
2. Let $\mathcal F_2$ be a system without this rule.
3. Since $p, q \models p \land q$, the system $\mathcal F_2$ has a fixed derivation $D$ of $p \land q$ from $p$ and $q$, with some number $c$ of lines.
4. In an $\mathcal F_1$-proof, replace each use of the rule, with $\varphi$ and $\psi$ for $p$ and $q$, by the derivation $D$ with the same substitution.
5. Each use costs at most $c$ lines, so the line count grows by the factor $c$ at most.

The typical use is to say "Frege" without naming a system.
A trap: with different connectives, the translation of formulas must keep their size polynomial; this needs a separate argument.

### L3 human

The theorem shows that the Frege class does not depend on the choice of rules, as the circuit class $\mathrm{NC}^1$ does not depend on the choice of gates.
Its proof is the propositional form of the fact that one Hilbert calculus derives the rules of another, with the added count of sizes.
Proof complexity therefore measures tautologies against classes of systems: Frege, [[extended Frege|~6925f591]], [[bounded-depth Frege|~2cc592a7]] of each depth.
