## Example

Let $V$ be the space of all functions $\R \to \R$, with pointwise operations.
Let $E$ be the set of [[even functions|0XE690QF65]], with $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x$, and let $O$ be the set of odd functions, with $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x$.
We check that $V = E \oplus O$.

1. $E$ and $O$ are [[subspaces|0EPTJA5QV4]]. The zero function is even and odd. If $f, g$ are even and $a \in \R$, then $(f + ag)(-x) = f(x) + a g(x)$. The same computation works for odd functions.
2. Intersection. If $f \in E \cap O$, then $f(x) = f(-x) = -f(x)$ for each $x$. So $2 f(x) = 0$ and $f(x) = 0$. Hence $E \cap O = \{0\}$.
3. Sum. For $f \in V$, let $f_e(x) = \frac{1}{2}(f(x) + f(-x))$ and $f_o(x) = \frac{1}{2}(f(x) - f(-x))$.
4. Then $f_e(-x) = \frac{1}{2}(f(-x) + f(x)) = f_e(x)$, so $f_e \in E$. Also $f_o(-x) = \frac{1}{2}(f(-x) - f(x)) = -f_o(x)$, so $f_o \in O$.
5. And $f_e + f_o = f$. So $V = E + O$.
6. By step 2 and the [[criteria for two subspaces|~26635bdc]], the sum is [[direct|041JTZCK9Q]], and $V = E \oplus O$.
7. For $f(x) = e^x$, the parts are $f_e = \cosh$ and $f_o = \sinh$.

## Remarks

Step 2 divides by $2$.
Over a field of characteristic $2$, the conditions $f(-x) = f(x)$ and $f(-x) = -f(x)$ are the same, so $E = O$ and the decomposition fails.
The same construction splits square matrices into symmetric and skew-symmetric parts, with the transpose in place of $x \mapsto -x$.
For polynomials, $f_e$ keeps the even powers and $f_o$ keeps the odd powers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each function is a symmetric part plus an antisymmetric part, and there is only one way to split it.
For example, $e^x$ splits into $\cosh x$ and $\sinh x$.

### L2 human

The formulas in step 3 come from solving $f = g + h$ and $f(-x) = g(x) - h(x)$ for an even $g$ and an odd $h$.
Adding and subtracting the two equations gives $g$ and $h$.
So the formulas are forced, which proves uniqueness a second time.
