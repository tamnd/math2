## Statement

Let $R$ be a [[unique factorisation domain|~4ed89225]].
Then the [[polynomial ring|~ac7cbaa8]] $R[x]$ is a unique factorisation domain.

## Remarks

The proof uses [[Gauss's lemma|~21cf423e]] and unique factorisation in $K[x]$, where $K$ is the field of fractions of $R$.
The irreducible elements of $R[x]$ are the irreducible elements of $R$ and the primitive polynomials of positive degree that are irreducible in $K[x]$.
By induction, $R[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ is a unique factorisation domain. In particular, $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ and $K[x, y]$ are.
These rings are not [[principal ideal domains|~91f9e139]]: $(2, x)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ and $(x, y)$ in $K[x, y]$ are not principal.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Integer polynomials factor uniquely into irreducible pieces, like integers do.
The pieces are prime numbers and irreducible primitive polynomials.
For example, $2x^2 - 2 = 2 (x - 1)(x + 1)$.
