## Statement

Let $K$ be a commutative ring, let $n$ be odd, and let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $K$.

1. If $A^T = -A$ and $2$ is not a zero divisor in $K$, then $\det A = 0$, where $\det$ is the [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]].
2. If $A$ is alternating, that is $A^T = -A$ and $a_{ii} = 0$ for all $i$, then $\det A = 0$ for every $K$.

In particular, a [[skew-symmetric matrix|03RNVFDEAN]] of odd size over a field of characteristic other than $2$ is singular.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Odd size: the $2 \times 2$ matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is skew-symmetric with determinant $1$.
- Characteristic: over $\mathbb{F}_2$, $-1 = 1$, so the $1 \times 1$ matrix $(1)$ satisfies $A^T = -A$. Its determinant is $1 \ne 0$.
- That matrix is not alternating, since its diagonal entry is $1$; so part 2 does not apply to it.

### Consequences

- A real skew-symmetric matrix of odd size has the eigenvalue $0$.
- The cross product matrix $v \mapsto a \times v$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ is skew-symmetric of size $3$, so it is singular; indeed it sends $a$ to $0$.
- Every principal submatrix of odd size of an alternating matrix is singular. This is one step in the proof that the rank of an alternating matrix over a field is even.

### Converse

A singular matrix of odd size need not be skew-symmetric: $\operatorname{diag}(1, 0, 0)$ is singular and not skew-symmetric.
For even size, a skew-symmetric matrix can be singular or not.

### Generalizations

The Pfaffian of an alternating matrix of odd size is set to $0$, and then $\operatorname{pf}(A)^2 = \det A$ holds in odd size too.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A skew-symmetric matrix changes sign when it is transposed.
Transposing does not change the determinant, and changing the sign of an odd-size matrix changes the sign of the determinant.
So the determinant equals its own negative, and it must be $0$.

### L2 human

Take $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 & 2 \\ 1 & -2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. Sarrus's rule gives $0 + 2 - 2 - 0 - 0 - 0 = 0$.
2. A vector in the kernel is $(2, 1, 1)$: the three rows give $1 - 1 = 0$, $-2 + 2 = 0$ and $2 - 2 = 0$.

The typical use is to see at once that such a matrix has a nonzero kernel.
A trap: in characteristic $2$, skew-symmetric means symmetric, and the statement needs the diagonal to be $0$.

### L3 human

An alternating matrix is the Gram matrix of an alternating bilinear form.
Over a field, such a form has a basis in which its matrix has blocks $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and zeros, so its rank is even.
In odd size the rank is less than the size, which is a second reason for $\det A = 0$.
