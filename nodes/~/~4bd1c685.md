## Example

The Harrop rule, also called the Kreisel-Putnam rule, is: from $\vdash \neg \varphi \to \psi \vee \chi$ infer $\vdash (\neg \varphi \to \psi) \vee (\neg \varphi \to \chi)$.
We prove that $\mathsf{IPC}$ is closed under it, with the [[slash|~0d19f928]].

1. Let $\Gamma = \set{\neg \varphi}$. The formula $\neg \varphi = \varphi \to \bot$ is Harrop, since its consequent $\bot$ is Harrop.
2. So $\Gamma$ is self-slashing, by basic property 4 of the [[slash|~0d19f928]].
3. Assume $\vdash \neg \varphi \to \psi \vee \chi$. Then $\Gamma \vdash \psi \vee \chi$ by modus ponens.
4. By Part B of [[the slash proof|~35cd3b76]], $\Gamma \mid \psi \vee \chi$.
5. So $\Gamma \mid \psi$ or $\Gamma \mid \chi$, by the clause for $\vee$.
6. By Part A, $\Gamma \vdash \psi$ or $\Gamma \vdash \chi$.
7. By the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]], $\vdash \neg \varphi \to \psi$ or $\vdash \neg \varphi \to \chi$.
8. In both cases, $\vdash (\neg \varphi \to \psi) \vee (\neg \varphi \to \chi)$.

## Remarks

The rule is admissible, but the implication from premise to conclusion is not a theorem of $\mathsf{IPC}$.
A Kripke model with a root below three maximal worlds refutes that implication; see [[admissible rules|~41ee4c18]].
Adding the implication as an axiom scheme gives the Kreisel-Putnam logic $\mathsf{KP}$.
The same argument works for every Harrop formula in place of $\neg \varphi$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose we can prove that "if $A$ is impossible, then $B$ or $C$".
The slash shows that the proof already decides which of $B$ and $C$ follows from "$A$ is impossible".
The reason is that a negative hypothesis contains no "or" that could hide a choice.

### L2 human

1. Take $\varphi = p$, $\psi = q$, $\chi = \neg q$, and suppose $\vdash \neg p \to q \vee \neg q$.
2. Then $\set{\neg p} \vdash q$ or $\set{\neg p} \vdash \neg q$.
3. Neither holds. The valuation with $p$ and $q$ false refutes $\neg p \to q$, and the valuation with $p$ false and $q$ true refutes $\neg p \to \neg q$.
4. So $\nvdash \neg p \to q \vee \neg q$, which we also get from the countermodel to excluded middle.

The typical use is to show that a rule is admissible without a semantic argument.
A trap: the proof needs a Harrop hypothesis, and it fails for $\set{p \vee q}$.
