## Summary

Electromagnetism is the study of electric charges, currents and the electric and magnetic fields that they make, and optics is the study of light.
The electric and magnetic fields are vector fields in space and time, and Maxwell's equations fix how charges and currents produce them and how they change.
The theory explains electric circuits, motors, radio, radar and optical instruments, and light itself is an electromagnetic wave.
Maxwell wrote his equations in the 1860s and predicted electromagnetic waves, and Hertz produced such waves in the laboratory in the late 1880s.
This topic collects the electromagnetism and optics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Potential theory, partial differential equations, relativity, quantum theory and thermodynamics have their own topics.
