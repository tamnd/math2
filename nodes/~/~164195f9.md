## Summary

A Venn diagram is a picture of the relations between a small number of sets.
Each set is a closed curve, often a circle, inside a rectangle for the [[universal set|~cc7398f6]].
For $n$ sets, the curves cut the plane into $2^n$ regions, one for each combination of "in" and "out" of the sets.

## Remarks

John Venn made these diagrams popular in the 1880s.
The diagrams make laws such as [[De Morgan's laws for sets|~ff3aaab8]] visible, but a picture is not a proof.
An Euler diagram is similar, but it shows only the regions that are not empty.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw two overlapping circles $A$ and $B$.
The overlap is $A \cap B$, both circles together are $A \cup B$, and the region outside both circles is the complement of $A \cup B$.
