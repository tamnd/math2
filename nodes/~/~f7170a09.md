## Statement

Let $G$ be a [[graph|0JD5C82QMB]] whose edges carry weights. The length of a [[path|0JDE56HWQ8]] is the sum of the weights of its edges. This is the setting of Dijkstra's algorithm.

Let $r$ and $x$ be vertices of $G$. Let $P=(u_0,u_1,\dots,u_t)$ be a shortest path from $r=u_0$ to $x=u_t$.

Then, for each integer $j$ with $0<j<t$, two things hold:

- the initial part $(u_0,\dots,u_j)$ is a shortest path from $r$ to $u_j$;
- the final part $(u_j,\dots,u_t)$ is a shortest path from $u_j$ to $x$.

## Remarks

Proof idea: if a shorter path existed between the ends of one part, replace that part of $P$ by it. This gives a shorter route from $r$ to $x$, which is a contradiction.
