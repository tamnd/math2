## Definition

Let $L$ be a signature and let $\varphi$ be an $L$-[[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]].
The formula $\varphi$ is valid, written $\models \varphi$, when $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s]$ for every $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]] $\mathcal{M}$ and every assignment $s \colon \mathrm{Var} \to M$, in the sense of [[satisfaction|~cf18e0d7]].
The formula $\varphi$ is invalid when some structure and some assignment do not satisfy it; such a pair is a countermodel to $\varphi$.

## Notation

$\models \varphi$ is the special case $\emptyset \models \varphi$ of [[logical consequence|KKNB9JY1YH]].
Structures have nonempty domains in this definition.

## Remarks

A sentence is valid exactly when it is true in every structure, because its satisfaction does not depend on the assignment.
To prove validity, fix an arbitrary structure and an arbitrary assignment and use the clauses of satisfaction.
To refute validity, give one countermodel.
The concept page [[validity|5SDS9T8E7J]] gives examples, non-examples and the link with provability.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A formula is valid when no choice of objects and no choice of meanings can make it false.

### L2 human

We check the definition on $\varphi = P(x) \to \exists y \, P(y)$.

1. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a structure and $s$ an assignment. If $s(x) \notin P^{\mathcal{M}}$, the antecedent fails, and $\varphi$ holds by the clause for $\to$.
2. If $s(x) \in P^{\mathcal{M}}$, the variant $s[y \mapsto s(x)]$ satisfies $P(y)$.
3. So $\mathcal{M} \models \exists y \, P(y)[s]$ by the clause for $\exists$, and $\varphi$ holds.
4. Both cases give $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi[s]$, so $\varphi$ is valid.

The typical use is to check a logical axiom of a proof system.
A trap is to treat the free variable $x$ as fixed; validity quantifies over all assignments, so $x$ ranges over all elements.
