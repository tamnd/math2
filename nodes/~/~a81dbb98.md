## Example

Let $A$ be a formula of [[linear logic|H408H4WHBA]].
The formula $!A \multimap A \otimes A$ is derivable in the one-sided calculus, with one use of the contraction rule $?C$.

1. By the definition of $\multimap$, and since $(!A)^{\perp} = ?A^{\perp}$, the formula is $?A^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} (A \otimes A)$.
2. $\vdash A^{\perp}, A$ is an axiom.
   We use two copies of it.
3. The rule $\otimes$ on the two axioms gives $\vdash A^{\perp}, A^{\perp}, A \otimes A$.
4. The rule $?D$ (dereliction) on each $A^{\perp}$ gives $\vdash ?A^{\perp}, ?A^{\perp}, A \otimes A$.
5. The rule $?C$ merges the two copies: $\vdash ?A^{\perp}, A \otimes A$.
6. The rule $\mathbin{\wp}$ gives $\vdash ?A^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} (A \otimes A)$.

## Remarks

Step 5 is an instance of [[contraction|~053ab224]], restricted to a formula with the exponential $?$.
Without the exponential, the sequent $\vdash A^{\perp}, A \otimes A$ is [[not derivable|~4201f767]].
The rule $\otimes$ splits the context, so each factor of $A \otimes A$ needs its own copy of $A^{\perp}$.
Contraction makes the two copies from one, and the exponential marks $A$ as a reusable resource.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In linear logic, a hypothesis is used exactly once, unless it carries the mark "!".
From one euro we cannot get two euros.
From an unlimited supply of euros we can take two.
The mark "!" says that the supply is unlimited.

### L2 human

We check the derivation in two-sided notation, where it reads $!A \vdash A \otimes A$.

1. $A \vdash A$ twice, and the right rule for $\otimes$ gives $A, A \vdash A \otimes A$.
2. Dereliction on the left changes each $A$ into $!A$: $!A, !A \vdash A \otimes A$.
3. Contraction on the left, allowed for $!$-formulas, gives $!A \vdash A \otimes A$.

The typical use of this pattern is to copy an argument, as a function does when it uses its input twice.
A trap: dereliction must come before contraction, since contraction applies only to two copies of $!A$.
