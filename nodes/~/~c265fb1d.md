## Definition

Let $T$ and $T^*$ be [[theories|92SWXST1ZM]] in the same signature $L$.

1. $T$ and $T^*$ are *mutually model consistent* if every [[model|MVCH6N9SNW]] of $T$ [[embeds|SCRB74SWQN]] into a model of $T^*$, and every model of $T^*$ embeds into a model of $T$.
2. $T^*$ is a *model companion* of $T$ if $T^*$ is [[model complete|ERVV6RWNNX]] and $T$ and $T^*$ are mutually model consistent.
3. $T^*$ is a *model completion* of $T$ if $T^*$ is a model companion of $T$ and, for every model $M$ of $T$, the theory $T^* \cup \mathrm{Diag}(M)$ is [[complete|655K8Y7VS8]] in the signature $L_M$.

Here $\mathrm{Diag}(M)$ is the atomic diagram of $M$, as in the [[diagram lemmas|EP5E317TNW]].

## Notation

The model companion, when it exists, is unique up to equivalence of theories, so one writes $T^*$ for it.
Two theories are mutually model consistent exactly when they have the same universal consequences, $T_\forall = T^*_\forall$.

## Remarks

Every model completion is a model companion, by definition.
The extra condition says that the theory of a model of $T^*$ over a submodel $M \models T$ is fixed by $M$ alone.
So $T^*$ "completes" each model of $T$ in a unique elementary way.

Example: the theory $\mathrm{ACF}$ of algebraically closed fields is the model completion of the theory of fields; see [[this example|~5fc11853]].

Example: the theory of real closed ordered fields is the model completion of the theory of ordered fields; see [[this example|~4e7545e0]].

Non-example: the theory of groups has no model companion; see [[this example|~826703ac]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A model companion of a theory is a second theory whose models are the "closed" versions of the models of the first.
Each model of the first theory sits inside a model of the companion, and conversely.
For example, every field sits inside an algebraically closed field, and every algebraically closed field is a field.
The algebraically closed fields are also model complete, so they form the model companion of the fields.

### L2 human

We check the three conditions for $T$ the theory of fields and $T^* = \mathrm{ACF}$.

1. Every field $K$ embeds into its algebraic closure, which is a model of $\mathrm{ACF}$.
2. Every model of $\mathrm{ACF}$ is a field, so it embeds into a model of $T$ by the identity.
3. $\mathrm{ACF}$ is model complete, since it has quantifier elimination.
4. For a field $K$, the theory $\mathrm{ACF} \cup \mathrm{Diag}(K)$ is complete, by quantifier elimination: every sentence with parameters from $K$ is equivalent to a quantifier-free one, decided by $\mathrm{Diag}(K)$.

So $\mathrm{ACF}$ is the model completion of the theory of fields.
The typical use of the definition is to identify a theory of "closed" structures.
A trap is to require $T^* \supseteq T$: a model companion need not contain $T$, but it does when $T$ is inductive.
