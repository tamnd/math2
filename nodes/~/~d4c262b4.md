## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

1. Down diagonals: $1 \cdot 5 \cdot 10 = 50$, $2 \cdot 6 \cdot 7 = 84$ and $3 \cdot 4 \cdot 8 = 96$. Their sum is $230$.
2. Up diagonals: $3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 = 105$, $1 \cdot 6 \cdot 8 = 48$ and $2 \cdot 4 \cdot 10 = 80$. Their sum is $233$.
3. The [[rule of Sarrus|~ca424b6f]] gives $\det A = 230 - 233 = -3$.
4. Check by row reduction. Subtract $4$ times row $1$ from row $2$, and $7$ times row $1$ from row $3$. The rows become $(1, 2, 3)$, $(0, -3, -6)$ and $(0, -6, -11)$.
5. Subtract $2$ times row $2$ from row $3$. Row $3$ becomes $(0, 0, 1)$.
6. These operations add multiples of rows, so they do not change the determinant, by the [[effect of row operations|09YB3WQG38]].
7. The result is upper triangular, so its determinant is $1 \cdot (-3) \cdot 1 = -3$, by the [[triangular rule|0K1KKMFHYH]]. The two values agree.

## Remarks

The two sums $230$ and $233$ are large and close, and the determinant is their small difference.
This happens often, so an error of one unit in one product changes the answer a lot.
With $a_{33} = 9$ in place of $10$, the rows are in arithmetic progression and the determinant is $-3 + (9 - 10)(-3) = 0$, because $\det A$ is linear in $a_{33}$ with coefficient $1 \cdot 5 - 2 \cdot 4 = -3$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The three down products add up to $230$, and the three up products add up to $233$.
The determinant is the difference, $-3$.
Row reduction gives the same number in another way.

### L2 human

Row reduction is the safer method for larger matrices, and it checks the rule here.
A trap: if a row swap is used during the reduction, the sign of the determinant changes, and the swap must be counted.
