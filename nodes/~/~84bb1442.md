## Example

Let $f_n(x) = x^n$ on $[0, 1]$.
The sequence converges [[pointwise|056FSKG32J]] to $f$, where $f(x) = 0$ for $0 \le x < 1$ and $f(1) = 1$.
Each $f_n$ is continuous, and $f$ is not [[continuous at|0EEE2CZB2A]] $1$, as the page [[x^n converges pointwise but not uniformly on [0, 1]|0YSRR1JTKZ]] proves.
We try the [[epsilon over three argument|~9f05a67c]] at the point $c = 1$ with $\varepsilon = \frac12$, and we find the step where it breaks.

1. The chain is $\lvert f(x) - f(1) \rvert \le \lvert f(x) - f_N(x) \rvert + \lvert f_N(x) - f_N(1) \rvert + \lvert f_N(1) - f(1) \rvert$.
2. The third term is $\lvert 1 - 1 \rvert = 0$ for every $N$.
3. For $0 \le x < 1$ the first term is $\lvert 0 - x^N \rvert = x^N$, and the middle term is $\lvert x^N - 1 \rvert = 1 - x^N$.
4. So the first two terms add up to exactly $1$ at each $x < 1$, for each $N$. They cannot both be less than $\frac16$.
5. The method asks for an $N$ with $x^N < \frac16$ at all $x$ near $1$. For each fixed $N$, the value $x^N$ tends to $1$ as $x \to 1$, so no such $N$ exists. This is the step that breaks.
6. At a single point an index exists, but it depends on the point. The smallest $N$ with $x^N < \frac16$ is $18$ for $x = 0.9$, $179$ for $x = 0.99$ and $1791$ for $x = 0.999$.
7. If $N$ is chosen after $x$, the function $f_N$ in the middle term is not fixed. Then the continuity of $f_N$ at $1$ gives a $\delta$ that depends on $N$, and so on $x$, which is not a valid choice.

So the argument is not an instance of the method, and its conclusion is false: $\lvert f(x) - f(1) \rvert = 1$ for every $x < 1$.

## Remarks

1. The failure is local. On $[0, b]$ with $b < 1$ we have $\sup_{0 \le x \le b} x^n = b^n \to 0$, so the convergence is uniform there.
2. So at each point $c < 1$ the method works on the neighbourhood $[0, b]$ with $c < b < 1$, and $f$ is continuous at $c$. The method needs uniform convergence only near the point.
3. The broken step is the choice of $N$, not the use of the triangle inequality. The inequality in step 1 is true, but its right side is never small.
4. The same check applies to any pointwise limit: compute $\sup_x \lvert f_N(x) - f(x) \rvert$ near the point. If it does not tend to $0$, the outer terms cannot be made small with one $N$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The functions $x^n$ are a bridge from $f(x)$ to $f(1)$ that does not hold.
For $x$ a little below $1$, the power $x^n$ is close to $0$ only when $n$ is very large, and how large depends on how close $x$ is to $1$.
For any fixed $n$, the points close enough to $1$ have $x^n$ close to $1$, far from the limit value $0$.
So one function of the sequence cannot be close to the limit at all the points that the argument needs.

### L2 human

We test the step "choose $N$" with the numbers of step 6.

1. For $x = 0.9$ we need $N \ge 18$, since $0.9^{17} \approx 0.167$ and $0.9^{18} \approx 0.150$.
2. Fix $N = 18$. At $x = 0.99$ the first term is $0.99^{18} \approx 0.835$, which is far above $\frac16$.
3. Each larger $N$ fails in the same way at a point closer to $1$. For $N = 1791$ the point $x = 0.9999$ gives $x^N \approx 0.836$.

The typical use of this test is to decide whether the epsilon over three argument applies to a pointwise limit.
We look for the supremum of $\lvert f_N - f \rvert$ near the point.
A trap is to check the outer terms only at the point $c$.
Here $f_N(1) = f(1)$ for every $N$, so the term at $c$ is always $0$, and the failure shows only at the nearby points $x$.
