## Example

We show $p, \neg p \nvDash_{D_2} q$ with the [[definition of discussive consequence|~fc40c0b9]].

1. Let $W = \set{w_1, w_2}$, with every world seeing every world, as in [[S5|VC3A2VWXND]].
2. Let $p$ be true at $w_1$ and false at $w_2$, and let $q$ be false at $w_1$ and at $w_2$.
3. $\Diamond p$ is true, because $p$ is true at $w_1$.
4. $\Diamond\neg p$ is true, because $\neg p$ is true at $w_2$.
5. $\Diamond q$ is false, because $q$ is true at no world.
6. So the S5 model makes $\Diamond p$ and $\Diamond\neg p$ true and $\Diamond q$ false. Hence $\Diamond p, \Diamond\neg p \nvDash_{\mathrm{S5}} \Diamond q$.

By the definition, $p, \neg p \nvDash_{D_2} q$.
So $D_2$ is not [[explosive|TTKZGJEEK7]].

## Remarks

The model needs two worlds.
With one world, $p$ and $\neg p$ cannot both hold, so $\Diamond p$ and $\Diamond\neg p$ cannot both be true.
In the reading of a discussion, two participants disagree about $p$, and neither asserts $q$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ann says "$p$" and Bob says "not $p$".
Nobody says "$q$".
So the discussion contains a contradiction but does not contain $q$.
This shows that a contradiction does not prove everything in discussive logic.

### L2 human

The counterexample has two worlds and one atom that changes between them.
The extra atom $q$ is false everywhere.
A trap is to make $q$ true at some world; then $\Diamond q$ is true, and the model does not refute the inference.
