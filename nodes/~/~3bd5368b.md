## Statement

Let $I$ be an interval and let $f \colon I \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous and [[strictly increasing|~3219e65d]].
Then $J = f(I)$ is an interval, and the inverse function $f^{-1} \colon J \to I$ is continuous and strictly increasing.

## Remarks

The set $J$ is an interval by the [[intermediate value theorem|~ba9d91ae]].
The inverse is strictly increasing and its image is the interval $I$. A monotone function whose image is an interval has no jumps, so $f^{-1}$ is continuous.
The same holds for strictly decreasing functions.
This gives the continuity of $n$-th roots, of the [[logarithm|~317c1d19]], and of the inverse trigonometric functions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The graph of $f^{-1}$ is the graph of $f$ reflected in the line $y = x$.
A rising unbroken graph stays rising and unbroken after the reflection.
