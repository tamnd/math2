## Proof

We prove part 1 of the statement.
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be an infinite $L$-structure, let $\kappa$ be infinite with $\abs{L} \le \kappa \le \abs{M}$, and let $A \subseteq M$ with $\abs{A} \le \kappa$.
We use the facts of [[cardinal arithmetic|SEMEEYYWT0]] that $\kappa \cdot \kappa = \kappa$ and that a set of size $\kappa$ has $\kappa$ finite tuples.

1. Witness functions. For each $L$-[[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] $\varphi(x_1, \dots, x_n, y)$, the [[axiom of choice|0HTGB13FE2]] gives a function $f_\varphi \colon M^n \to M$ such that $\mathcal{M} \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$ implies $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi(\bar a, f_\varphi(\bar a))$.
2. Count of formulas. Formulas are finite strings of symbols of $L$, variables and logical symbols, so there are at most $\max(\abs{L}, \aleph_0) \le \kappa$ of them.
3. Start. Since $\kappa \le \abs{M}$, choose $B \subseteq M$ with $\abs{B} = \kappa$, and put $A_0 = A \cup B$. Then $\abs{A_0} = \kappa$, because $\abs{A}, \abs{B} \le \kappa$ and $\abs{B} = \kappa$.
4. Closure step. Put $A_{k+1} = A_k \cup \set{f_\varphi(\bar a) : \varphi \text{ a formula}, \ \bar a \text{ a tuple in } A_k}$.
5. Size. By [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $k$, $\abs{A_k} = \kappa$: if $\abs{A_k} = \kappa$, then the new elements are indexed by at most $\kappa \cdot \kappa = \kappa$ pairs $(\varphi, \bar a)$, by step 2.
6. Union. Put $N = \bigcup_{k \in \N} A_k$, a [[union|0X1GSWNBN1]] of countably many sets of size $\kappa$. So $\kappa \le \abs{N} \le \aleph_0 \cdot \kappa = \kappa$.
7. Closure of $N$. Let $\varphi(\bar x, y)$ be a formula and $\bar a$ a tuple in $N$. The tuple is finite and the $A_k$ increase, so $\bar a$ lies in some $A_k$. Then $f_\varphi(\bar a) \in A_{k+1} \subseteq N$.
8. $N$ is a [[substructure|676H7EGTKJ]]. For a constant $c$, step 7 with $\varphi(y) = (y = c)$ gives $c^{\mathcal{M}} \in N$. For a function symbol $g$, step 7 with $\varphi(\bar x, y) = (y = g(\bar x))$ gives $g^{\mathcal{M}}(\bar a) \in N$. Also $N \supseteq B$ is nonempty.
9. Tarski-Vaught condition. If $\bar a$ lies in $N$ and $\mathcal{M} \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$, then $b = f_\varphi(\bar a)$ satisfies $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi(\bar a, b)$ by step 1, and $b \in N$ by step 7.
10. By the [[Tarski-Vaught test|60SA99BER7]], $\mathcal{N} \preceq \mathcal{M}$. Also $A \subseteq A_0 \subseteq N$ and $\abs{N} = \kappa$, as required.

## Idea

Choose one witness function for each formula, start from a set of the right size that contains $A$, and close it under all these functions in countably many rounds.
The closed set passes the Tarski-Vaught test by construction.
The number of functions is at most $\kappa$ because $\abs{L} \le \kappa$, so each round keeps the size at $\kappa$.

## Variants

The functions $f_\varphi$ are Skolem functions, and $N$ is the Skolem hull of $A_0$; see [[Skolem functions and Skolem hulls|~71af893e]].
With Skolem functions added to the signature, step 7 says that $N$ is a substructure of the Skolem expansion, and steps 4 to 6 are the usual bound on the size of a generated substructure.
For a countable theory, Skolem's 1922 proof avoids the axiom of choice and gives a countable model, but not a countable elementary substructure of a given model.
