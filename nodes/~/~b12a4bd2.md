## Example

The lower limit logic is CLuN: positive classical logic plus $A \lor \neg A$.
A CLuN model is a map $v$ from formulas to $\set{0, 1}$ that is classical for $\land$, $\lor$, $\to$, with $v(\neg A) = 1$ whenever $v(A) = 0$; when $v(A) = 1$, $v(\neg A)$ can be $0$ or $1$.
The abnormalities are the formulas $A \land \neg A$.
Take the premises $\Gamma = \set{p, \neg p \lor r, \neg r \lor s, \neg s, s \lor t}$.

A dynamic proof, with the condition of each line in the last column:

| line | formula | justification | condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $p$ | premise | $\emptyset$ |
| 2 | $\neg p \lor r$ | premise | $\emptyset$ |
| 3 | $\neg r \lor s$ | premise | $\emptyset$ |
| 4 | $\neg s$ | premise | $\emptyset$ |
| 5 | $s \lor t$ | premise | $\emptyset$ |
| 6 | $t$ | 4, 5, conditional DS | $\set{s \land \neg s}$ |
| 7 | $r$ | 1, 2, conditional DS | $\set{p \land \neg p}$ |
| 8 | $s$ | 3, 7, conditional DS | $\set{p \land \neg p, r \land \neg r}$ |
| 9 | $(p \land \neg p) \lor (r \land \neg r) \lor (s \land \neg s)$ | 1 to 5, CLuN | $\emptyset$ |

Each conditional step uses [[disjunctive syllogism|6EFJF9NAYX]]: from $\neg A \lor B$ and $A$, or from $A \lor B$ and $\neg A$, infer $B$ on the condition $\set{A \land \neg A}$.
This is sound for CLuN in the form: $\neg A \lor B, A \vDash B \lor (A \land \neg A)$.

1. Line 9 holds in every CLuN model of $\Gamma$, by [[cases|0WCP0PVET6]]. If $v(\neg p) = 1$, then $p \land \neg p$ is true. Otherwise $v(r) = 1$ by line 2. Then if $v(\neg r) = 1$, $r \land \neg r$ is true; otherwise $v(s) = 1$ by line 3, and with line 4, $s \land \neg s$ is true.
2. No disjunction of a proper subset of the three abnormalities follows. The model $v(p) = v(r) = v(s) = 1$, $v(\neg p) = v(\neg r) = 0$, $v(\neg s) = 1$, $v(t) = 0$ satisfies $\Gamma$, and its only true abnormality is $s \land \neg s$. Similar models make $p \land \neg p$ alone, or $r \land \neg r$ alone, the only abnormality: for example $v(p) = v(\neg p) = 1$, $v(r) = v(s) = 0$, $v(t) = 1$.
3. So line 9 is a minimal Dab-formula, and all three abnormalities are unreliable.
4. Under the reliability strategy, lines 6, 7 and 8 are marked, because each condition contains an unreliable formula. So $t$ is not derived.
5. Under minimal abnormality, $t$ is also not a consequence: the first model in step 2 is minimally abnormal and makes $t$ false.

At stage 8 of the proof, line 6 is unmarked, and $t$ looks derived.
At stage 9 it is marked.
This change, with fixed premises, is the internal dynamics of an [[adaptive logic|~132437fd]].

## Remarks

In step 2, the values of $\neg B$ for compound $B$ are set to $1 - v(B)$, so that no compound formula is abnormal.
The premise $s \lor t$ is the only source of $t$, and its use needs $s$ to behave consistently.
A classical reasoner derives everything from $\Gamma$, since $\Gamma$ is classically inconsistent.
The logic of paradox derives neither $t$ nor $r$, since it lacks disjunctive syllogism altogether.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We first use "$s$ or $t$, not $s$, so $t$", assuming $s$ is not contradictory.
Later, a chain of similar steps shows that $p$, $r$ or $s$ is contradictory, and we cannot tell which.
Since $s$ might be the culprit, the step to $t$ is cancelled.

### L2 human

Read the table line by line.
Lines 6 to 8 are classical steps that carry a condition.
Line 9 is an unconditional disjunction of abnormalities, and it contains each condition used.
A trap is to keep line 7 because $p \land \neg p$ is never derived on its own; the minimal disjunction is enough to mark it.
