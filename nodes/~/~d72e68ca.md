## Example

The Petersen graph is the [[graph|~3f82cef4]] whose vertices are the $2$-element subsets of $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$.
Two vertices are adjacent if and only if they are disjoint subsets.

It has $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ vertices and $15$ edges, and it is $3$-[[regular|~4e16b1c4]].

## Remarks

Its shortest cycle has length $5$. It is not [[Hamiltonian|~2d8c7d85]], and it is not [[planar|~276b1882]].
Its [[chromatic number|~5301b5d7]] is $3$, and its edges cannot be coloured with $3$ colours.
A common drawing shows it as a pentagon outside, a pentagram inside, and five spokes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Petersen graph is a small graph with $10$ vertices and $15$ edges.
It is a standard counterexample: many statements that seem true for all cubic graphs fail for it.
