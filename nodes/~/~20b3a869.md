## Summary

Statistical inference is the theory of how to learn about an unknown probability law from data, and of how to judge the procedures that do it.
A statistical model is a family of probability laws indexed by a parameter, and the data are taken as a sample from one unknown member of the family.
Inference gives the methods by which the sciences, medicine and industry draw conclusions from data with a measured uncertainty.
Fisher introduced maximum likelihood and sufficiency in the 1920s, and Neyman and Pearson gave their theory of optimal tests in 1933.
This topic collects the statistical inference pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Statistical models and methods, probability theory and information theory have their own topics.
