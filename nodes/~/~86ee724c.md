## Example

Take $\mathbb{Z}$ with its usual order, $x \land y = \min(x, y)$, $x \lor y = \max(x, y)$, $x \cdot y = x + y$ and unit $0$.
Put $x \backslash z = z - x$ and $z / y = z - y$.

1. Lattice. $\mathbb{Z}$ is a chain, so $\min$ and $\max$ are the meet and the join.
2. Monoid. $(\mathbb{Z}, +, 0)$ is a commutative [[monoid|0N6R902FG1]].
3. Residuation. $x + y \le z$ iff $y \le z - x$, by subtracting $x$ on both sides. Also $x + y \le z$ iff $x \le z - y$.

So $\mathbb{Z}$ is a [[residuated lattice|E337A6WVH3]].

## Remarks

1. The same works in every [[lattice-ordered group|G5AN43WE9D]] $G$: put $x \backslash z = x^{-1} z$ and $z / y = z y^{-1}$. Then $xy \le z$ iff $y \le x^{-1}z$ iff $x \le zy^{-1}$, since multiplication by a fixed element preserves the order.
2. The lattice has no top and no bottom, and the unit $0$ is not the top. So this residuated lattice is not integral and not bounded.
3. The negative cone $\set{x \in \mathbb{Z} : x \le 0}$ is an integral residuated lattice, with unit $0$ as the top and $x \backslash z = \min(0, z - x)$.
4. In an ℓ-group, $x \backslash e = x^{-1}$, so every element is invertible. In a [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]], only the top is invertible.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Adding a number is like multiplying in this structure.
The question "how large can $y$ be with $3 + y \le 10$?" has the best answer $7 = 10 - 3$.
Subtraction gives this best answer in every case.

### L2 human

1. Take $x = 3$ and $z = 10$. Then $x \backslash z = 10 - 3 = 7$.
2. Check: $3 + 7 = 10 \le 10$, and $3 + 8 = 11 > 10$.
3. In the negative cone, take $x = -2$ and $z = -1$. Then $z - x = 1 > 0$, so $x \backslash z = \min(0, 1) = 0$.
4. Check: $-2 + 0 = -2 \le -1$, and $0$ is the largest element of the cone.

The typical use: ℓ-groups are the residuated lattices in which multiplication has inverses, and they connect residuated lattices with ordered algebra and with [[MV-algebras|8T4M7RN9AC]].
The trap: the unit $e$ of an ℓ-group is in the middle of the order, not at the top.
