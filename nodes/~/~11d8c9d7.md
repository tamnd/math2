## Example

Global consequence is defined as follows.
We have $\Gamma \models_g \varphi$ when every [[Kripke model|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]] that makes all formulas of $\Gamma$ true at every world makes $\varphi$ true at every world.
We show that $p \models_g \square p$, but $\not\models p \to \square p$.

1. Let $\mathfrak{M}$ be a model with $p$ true at every world.
2. Let $w$ be a world of $\mathfrak{M}$. Every successor of $w$ has $p$, by step 1, so $\square p$ is true at $w$.
3. So $\square p$ is true at every world of $\mathfrak{M}$, and $p \models_g \square p$.
4. Now take $W = \set{1, 2}$, $R = \set{(1, 2)}$ and $V(p) = \set{1}$.
5. At $1$, $p$ is true, and the successor $2$ lacks $p$, so $\square p$ is false.
6. So $p \to \square p$ fails at $1$, and $\not\models p \to \square p$.
7. So the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] in the form "$\Gamma, \varphi \models_g \psi$ implies $\Gamma \models_g \varphi \to \psi$" fails, with $\Gamma = \varnothing$.
8. The same happens in derivations. If [[necessitation|G989VP2NGP]] may be applied to the hypothesis $p$, then $p \vdash \square p$ in one step. But $\mathsf{K} \nvdash p \to \square p$, by step 6 and [[soundness|~d189ee4b]].

## Remarks

The example shows that necessitation behaves like an inference from a hypothesis only for global consequence.
There are two correct repairs.
First, local consequence: $\Gamma \models_l \varphi$ when every world that makes $\Gamma$ true makes $\varphi$ true.
In derivations this means that necessitation is applied to theorems of [[normal modal logic K|~b1c67ad7]] only, and then the deduction theorem holds.
Second, a weaker form for global consequence: if $\mathsf{K} \vdash (\varphi \wedge \square\varphi \wedge \dots \wedge \square^n\varphi) \to \psi$, then $\varphi \models_g \psi$.
For the proof, let $\varphi$ be true at every world of a model.
Step 2 above, applied to $\varphi$ instead of $p$, shows that $\square\varphi$ is true at every world, and by induction each $\square^k\varphi$ is too.
So the antecedent is true at every world, the theorem is true at every world by soundness, and $\psi$ is true at every world.
The converse also holds, but its proof needs compactness and is not given here.
For $\varphi = p$ and $\psi = \square p$, the case $n = 1$ applies, since $p \wedge \square p \to \square p$ is a tautology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a rule holds in every room of a house.
Then in every room it is true that the rule holds in all rooms that one can enter next.
But in one room, a fact can be true while it is false in the next room.
So "if $p$ holds everywhere, then box $p$ holds everywhere" is right.
And "in each room, $p$ implies box $p$" is wrong.

### L2 human

1. Take $\varphi = \square p \to p$ and $\psi = \square\square p \to \square p$.
2. $\mathsf{K} \vdash \square\varphi \to \psi$: from $\square(\square p \to p)$, axiom $\mathrm{K}$ gives $\square\square p \to \square p$.
3. So $\varphi \models_g \psi$ by the weaker form with $n = 1$.
4. But $\not\models \varphi \to \psi$: on $W = \set{1, 2, 3}$, $R = \set{(1, 2), (2, 3)}$, $V(p) = \set{3}$, world $1$ makes $\varphi$ true and $\psi$ false.

The typical use of global consequence is to reason about a fixed class of models, for example with a set of global axioms.
A trap: a proof with necessitation on hypotheses gives a global consequence, so it cannot be turned into a theorem $\varphi \to \psi$.
