## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\R$.

1. $tI - A$ is upper triangular. By the [[determinant of a triangular matrix|0K1KKMFHYH]], $p_A(t) = (t - 1)(t - 2)$.
2. Second method: $\tr A = 3$ and $\det A = 2$, so $p_A(t) = t^2 - 3t + 2 = (t - 1)(t - 2)$.
3. Right [[eigenvectors|0B5H4208Z9]]: $A(1, 0)^{\mathsf T} = (1, 0)^{\mathsf T}$, and $A(1, 1)^{\mathsf T} = (2, 2)^{\mathsf T} = 2(1, 1)^{\mathsf T}$.
4. [[Left eigenvectors|~d0c39701]]: $(1, -1)A = (1, 1 - 2) = (1, -1)$, and $(0, 1)A = (0, 2) = 2(0, 1)$.
5. Biorthogonality: the left eigenvector $(1, -1)$ for $1$ and the right eigenvector $(1, 1)^{\mathsf T}$ for $2$ give $(1, -1)(1, 1)^{\mathsf T} = 0$.
6. The left eigenvector $(0, 1)$ for $2$ and the right eigenvector $(1, 0)^{\mathsf T}$ for $1$ give $(0, 1)(1, 0)^{\mathsf T} = 0$.
7. Let $S = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, with the right eigenvectors as columns. Then $S^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, whose rows are the left eigenvectors.
8. Check: $S^{-1} A S = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} S = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.

## Remarks

- The left and right eigenvectors for the same eigenvalue differ: $(1, -1)$ and $(1, 0)^{\mathsf T}$ for $1$.
- They coincide up to transpose only in special cases, for example for symmetric matrices.
- The [[right eigenvector that is not a left eigenvector|~1a0caa3d]] shows the difference for this matrix.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A left eigenvector is a row vector that is multiplied by $A$ from the left side.
For this matrix, the left and the right eigenvectors are different.
But each left eigenvector is perpendicular to the right eigenvectors of the other eigenvalue.

### L2 human

The typical use is to read the left eigenvectors from the rows of $S^{-1}$ when $S^{-1}AS$ is diagonal.
A trap is to transpose a right eigenvector and expect a left eigenvector.
