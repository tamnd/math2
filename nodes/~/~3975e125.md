## Statement

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [[topological spaces|~77bfcc55]], and let $K \subseteq X$ and $L \subseteq Y$ be [[compact|~56dabfb9]].
Then $K \times L$ is compact in $X \times Y$ with the [[product topology|~be34ec23]].

## Remarks

The proof uses the tube lemma. For each $x \in K$, finitely many basic open sets $U \times V$ cover $\{x\} \times L$, and their intersection over the first factor gives an open set about $x$.
By induction, a product of finitely many compact sets is compact. For example, $[0, 1]^n$ is compact.
For arbitrary products of compact spaces, the statement is [[Tychonoff's theorem|~c8a075cb]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rectangle made of two compact sides is compact.
For example, the square $[0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ is compact, because $[0, 1]$ is compact.
