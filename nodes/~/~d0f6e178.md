## Example

Let $A$ and $B$ be formulas.
The formula $A \otimes B \multimap B \otimes A$ is derivable in multiplicative [[linear logic|H408H4WHBA]] (MLL).

1. By definition, $A \otimes B \multimap B \otimes A = (A \otimes B)^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} (B \otimes A)$, and the De Morgan law gives $(A \otimes B)^{\perp} = A^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} B^{\perp}$.
2. $\vdash B^{\perp}, B$ and $\vdash A^{\perp}, A$ are axioms.
3. The rule $\otimes$, from $\vdash \Gamma, C$ and $\vdash \Delta, D$ infer $\vdash \Gamma, \Delta, C \otimes D$, with $\Gamma = B^{\perp}$, $C = B$, $\Delta = A^{\perp}$, $D = A$, gives $\vdash B^{\perp}, A^{\perp}, B \otimes A$.
4. Sequents are multisets, so this is the sequent $\vdash A^{\perp}, B^{\perp}, B \otimes A$.
   The rule $\mathbin{\wp}$ on $A^{\perp}$ and $B^{\perp}$ gives $\vdash A^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} B^{\perp}, B \otimes A$.
5. The rule $\mathbin{\wp}$ on the two formulas gives $\vdash (A^{\perp} \mathbin{\wp} B^{\perp}) \mathbin{\wp} (B \otimes A)$, which is the formula of step 1.

## Remarks

The derivation uses only the axiom, $\otimes$ and $\mathbin{\wp}$, each connective once, and no cut.
Step 4 uses [[exchange|YAFD18WYJ8]], which is built into linear logic by the use of multisets.
In two-sided notation the derivation reads: from $B \vdash B$ and $A \vdash A$ get $A, B \vdash B \otimes A$, then $A \otimes B \vdash B \otimes A$.
In a non-commutative logic such as the Lambek calculus, the same steps would give $p, q \Rightarrow q \cdot p$ only after an exchange, which is not available.
The [[proof net|~626a1be1]] of this derivation shows the crossing of the two axiom links.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Having a coin and a stamp is the same as having a stamp and a coin.
The derivation shows this with the rules of linear logic.
It takes the coin and the stamp from the hypothesis and puts them together in the other order.

### L2 human

We read the derivation in two-sided form and count the uses of each hypothesis.

1. $A \vdash A$ and $B \vdash B$ use each hypothesis once.
2. The right rule of $\otimes$ joins them into $A, B \vdash B \otimes A$; the contexts $A$ and $B$ are split between the premises.
3. The left rule of $\otimes$ joins $A, B$ on the left into $A \otimes B \vdash B \otimes A$.
4. Each of $A$ and $B$ is used exactly once, as linear logic requires.

The typical use of this example is as a first exercise in the rules for $\otimes$ and $\mathbin{\wp}$.
A trap: in step 3 of the example the premise with $B$ must be the left premise, since it gives the left factor of $B \otimes A$.
