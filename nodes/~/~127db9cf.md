## Example

Let $\mathcal{G}$ be the class of finite [[graphs|00C0BR7D7H]], without an order.
We show that $\mathcal{G}$ is not a [[Ramsey class|~8398d787]].

1. Let $A = B = K_2$, the edge on $\set{0, 1}$, and $k = 2$.
2. Let $C \in \mathcal{G}$ be arbitrary, and fix a linear order $\prec$ on the vertices of $C$.
3. Color an [[embedding|SCRB74SWQN]] $e \colon K_2 \to C$ red if $e(0) \prec e(1)$ and blue otherwise.
4. Let $g \colon B \to C$ be an embedding, and let $s$ be the swap of $0$ and $1$.
5. The embeddings $g$ and $g \circ s$ both lie in $\set{g \circ e : e \in \binom{B}{A}}$, and they get different colors.
6. So $C \not\to (B)^A_2$, for every $C$.

## Remarks

The failure comes from the automorphism of $K_2$, not from any lack of edges in $C$.
If one colors edges as sets, the classical Ramsey theorem for the [[complete graph|03WKB2H474]] gives monochromatic copies; the embedding version is stricter.
Adding a linear order to the signature kills the automorphism, and finite ordered graphs do form a Ramsey class.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An edge can be placed onto another edge in two ways, by flipping it.
A coloring can give the two placements different colors, using any fixed ordering of the vertices.
Then no edge is monochromatic, and the Ramsey property fails.

### L2 human

We take $C = K_3$ on $\set{p, q, r}$ with $p \prec q \prec r$.

1. The embedding $0 \mapsto p$, $1 \mapsto q$ is red.
2. The embedding $0 \mapsto q$, $1 \mapsto p$ is blue.
3. Both have image the edge $pq$, so $pq$ is not a monochromatic copy of $K_2$.

The typical use is to explain why structural Ramsey theory works with ordered structures.
A trap is to color edges as sets; that is a different and weaker statement than the Ramsey property for embeddings.
