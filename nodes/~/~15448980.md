## Proof

**Part 1.**

1. Form the $(n + k) \times (n + k)$ [[block matrix|01AXG9KZQF]] $N = \begin{pmatrix} A & U \\ V & -C^{-1} \end{pmatrix}$.
2. The upper left block $A$ is invertible. Its [[Schur complement|CA1J41NWGS]] is $N/A = -C^{-1} - VA^{-1}U = -K$.
3. The lower right block $-C^{-1}$ is invertible, with inverse $-C$. Its Schur complement is $N/(-C^{-1}) = A - U(-C)V = A + UCV$.
4. By part 2 of the [[block inverse formula|~801e2989]], $N$ is invertible if and only if $-K$ is invertible. This holds if and only if $K$ is invertible.
5. By part 4 of the same theorem, $N$ is invertible if and only if $A + UCV$ is invertible.
6. By steps 4 and 5, $A + UCV$ is invertible if and only if $K$ is invertible.
7. Now let both be invertible. By part 4 of the block inverse formula, the upper left block of $N^{-1}$ is $(A + UCV)^{-1}$.
8. By part 3 of the block inverse formula with $S = -K$, the upper left block of $N^{-1}$ is $A^{-1} + A^{-1}U(-K)^{-1}VA^{-1} = A^{-1} - A^{-1}UK^{-1}VA^{-1}$.
9. The inverse $N^{-1}$ is unique, by the [[uniqueness of the inverse|~139b320d]]. So the blocks in steps 7 and 8 are equal, which is the formula.

**Part 2.**

10. Take $k = 1$, $C = (1)$, $U = u$ and $V = v^{\mathsf{T}}$. Then $K = (1 + v^{\mathsf{T}}A^{-1}u)$ is invertible exactly when $1 + v^{\mathsf{T}}A^{-1}u \ne 0$.
11. Part 1 gives the formula, with $K^{-1}$ equal to division by the scalar $1 + v^{\mathsf{T}}A^{-1}u$.

## Idea

Put $A$, $U$, $V$ and $-C^{-1}$ into one block matrix.
Its two Schur complements are $-K$ and $A + UCV$.
The two block inverse formulas compute the same upper left block of the inverse, and comparing them gives the identity.

## Variants

The [[proof by multiplication|~44e07b6f]] is shorter, but it only checks a formula that one must already know.
