## Definition

The inverse trigonometric functions are the inverses of the restrictions of the trigonometric functions to the following intervals:
$\sin$ on $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$, $\cos$ on $[0, \pi]$, and $\tan$ on $(-\pi/2, \pi/2)$.
They are the [[arcsine|~fe8125e8]] $\arcsin \colon [-1, 1] \to [-\pi/2, \pi/2]$, the [[arccosine|~0541adf5]] $\arccos \colon [-1, 1] \to [0, \pi]$ and the [[arctangent|~a8014aaf]] $\arctan \colon \mathbb{R} \to (-\pi/2, \pi/2)$.

## Remarks

The trigonometric functions are not injective on $\mathbb{R}$. The restrictions to these intervals are strictly monotone and continuous, so they have continuous inverses.
The values are the principal values. Other notations are $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ and $\tan^{-1}$.
Similar definitions give $\operatorname{arccot}$, $\operatorname{arcsec}$ and $\operatorname{arccsc}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An inverse trigonometric function gives back an angle from a value of sine, cosine or tangent.
We restrict to one interval so that the answer is unique.
