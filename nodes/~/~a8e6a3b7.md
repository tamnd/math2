## Proof

Let $J$ be a two-sided ideal of $M_n(F)$ with $J \neq \{0\}$.
We show $J = M_n(F)$.

1. Choose $A \in J$ with $A \neq 0$, and choose indices $j, k$ with $a_{jk} \neq 0$.
2. For any $i$ and $l$, the [[product of matrix units|~eda9ba19]] gives $E_{ij} A E_{kl} = a_{jk} E_{il}$. Indeed $A = \sum_{p,q} a_{pq} E_{pq}$, and $E_{ij} E_{pq} E_{kl}$ is $E_{il}$ when $p = j$ and $q = k$, and $0$ otherwise.
3. $J$ is a two-sided ideal and $A \in J$, so $E_{ij} A E_{kl} \in J$. Thus $a_{jk} E_{il} \in J$.
4. The [[scalar matrix|0FRM62SG7D]] $a_{jk}^{-1} I$ is in $M_n(F)$, because $F$ is a field. So $(a_{jk}^{-1} I)(a_{jk} E_{il}) = E_{il} \in J$.
5. Step 4 holds for every pair $(i, l)$, so every [[matrix unit|~2c685e2c]] lies in $J$.
6. An ideal is closed under addition, so $I_n = E_{11} + E_{22} + \dots + E_{nn} \in J$.
7. For every $B \in M_n(F)$, $B = B I_n \in J$. So $J = M_n(F)$.

## Idea

The products $E_{ij} A E_{kl}$ cut out one entry of $A$ and move it to any position.
Over a field, a single nonzero entry can be scaled to $1$.
So one nonzero matrix generates every matrix unit, and then the identity.

## Variants

- Over a division ring $D$, the same proof works, with $a_{jk}^{-1}$ on the left in step 4.
- Over a general ring $R$, the same computation shows that the entries of the matrices of $J$ form a two-sided ideal $I$ of $R$, and that $J = M_n(I)$.
