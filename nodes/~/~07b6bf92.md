## Statement

Let $(X, d)$ be a [[metric space|~200ea42b]].
Then the set of all [[open balls|~43faf552]] $B(x, r)$, with $x \in X$ and real $r > 0$, is a [[basis|~b57c1608]] of the [[metric topology|~2852bf47]] of $X$.
Also, the balls $B(x, 1/n)$ with $x \in X$ and integer $n \ge 1$ form a basis.

## Remarks

Proof: each ball is open, by [[open balls are open|~054efe4e]]. If $U$ is open and $x \in U$, then some ball $B(x, r)$ lies in $U$, and $B(x, 1/n) \subseteq B(x, r)$ for $n > 1/r$.
For each point $x$, the balls $B(x, 1/n)$ form a countable neighbourhood basis at $x$. Thus each metric space is [[first-countable|~4a1e5226]].
In $\mathbb{R}$, the open balls are the bounded open intervals.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each open set of a metric space is a union of open balls.
So, to check a property for all open sets, it is often enough to check it for balls.
