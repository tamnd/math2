## Proof

*Item 1.*
Since $\mathsf{IPC} \subseteq \mathsf{KC}$, every negation-free theorem of $\mathsf{IPC}$ is a theorem of $\mathsf{KC}$.
For the other inclusion, let $\varphi$ be negation-free with $\mathsf{IPC} \nvdash \varphi$.
We show $\mathsf{KC} \nvdash \varphi$.

1. By the finite model property in [[Kripke completeness|~ac029000]], there is a finite rooted [[Kripke model|~11b532e9]] $M = (W, \leq, V)$ with root $r$ and $r \nVdash \varphi$.
2. Let $t$ be a new world. Let $W' = W \cup \set{t}$, with $w \leq' t$ for all $w \in W'$ and $\leq'$ equal to $\leq$ on $W$.
3. Let $V'(p) = V(p) \cup \set{t}$ for every variable $p$. Each $V'(p)$ is an up-set of $W'$, since $V(p)$ is an up-set of $W$ and $t$ is above everything. Call the new model $M'$.
4. Claim A: $t \Vdash' \psi$ for every negation-free $\psi$. We use induction on $\psi$.
    - Variables are true at $t$ by step 3.
    - For $\psi_1 \wedge \psi_2$ and $\psi_1 \vee \psi_2$, use the induction hypothesis at $t$.
    - For $\psi_1 \to \psi_2$, the only world above $t$ is $t$, and $t \Vdash' \psi_2$.
5. Claim B: for every negation-free $\psi$ and every $w \in W$, $w \Vdash' \psi$ iff $w \Vdash \psi$. We use induction on $\psi$.
    - For variables, $V'(p) \cap W = V(p)$.
    - For $\wedge$ and $\vee$, use the induction hypothesis at $w$.
    - For $\psi_1 \to \psi_2$, the worlds above $w$ in $M'$ are those above $w$ in $M$, together with $t$.
    - At $t$, the condition "if $\psi_1$ then $\psi_2$" holds by Claim A.
    - At the old worlds, the condition is unchanged by the induction hypothesis.
6. The induction has no case for $\bot$, because $\varphi$ is negation-free. This is where the hypothesis is used: $\bot$ is false at $t$, so Claim A fails for it.
7. By Claim B, $r \nVdash' \varphi$. So $\varphi$ fails on the frame $(W', \leq')$.
8. This frame is finite and rooted with root $r$, and $t$ is its greatest element.
9. By item 4 of [[the weak excluded middle theorem|CX032ACTMZ]], every theorem of $\mathsf{KC}$ is valid on such a frame. So $\mathsf{KC} \nvdash \varphi$.

*Item 2.*

10. Item 2 is Jankov's theorem, and we cite it without proof from the memoriam by Citkin and Vandoulakis.
11. Together, items 1 and 2 show that $\mathsf{KC}$ is the largest intermediate logic whose positive fragment is that of $\mathsf{IPC}$. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Negation-free formulas cannot see a world where everything is true.
So one may glue such a world on top of any countermodel, and the countermodel survives.
The new model has a greatest world, and these are exactly the models of $\mathsf{KC}$.

## Variants

The proof works for any model, not only finite ones, once the new world is placed above all old worlds.
In algebraic terms one adds a new bottom element to a finite Heyting algebra, and the result validates $\neg p \vee \neg \neg p$ because the old bottom becomes the unique atom.
