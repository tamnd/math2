## Example

Let $\mathrm{T}$ be the axiom $\square p \to p$.
By [[the correctness of the standard translation|~a9fa4297]], a [[frame|6BQ71KQ6QT]] $\mathfrak{F} = (W, R)$ validates $\mathrm{T}$ if and only if it satisfies the [[second-order|G6X7SZSNC5]] sentence
$$\forall P\, \forall x\, (\forall y\, (Rxy \to Py) \to Px).$$
We show that this sentence is equivalent to the first-order sentence $\forall x\, Rxx$.

1. First, assume $\forall x\, Rxx$, so $R$ is [[reflexive|0X8NMST0J2]].
2. Let $P \subseteq W$ and $x \in W$, and assume $\forall y\, (Rxy \to Py)$.
3. Take $y = x$. Then $Rxx$ holds, so $Px$ holds.
4. As $P$ and $x$ were arbitrary, the second-order sentence holds.
5. Conversely, assume the second-order sentence, and let $x_0 \in W$.
6. Choose $P = \set{y \in W : R x_0 y}$, the minimal valuation for $\square p$ at $x_0$.
7. Then $\forall y\, (R x_0 y \to Py)$ holds by the choice of $P$.
8. The sentence, with this $P$ and $x = x_0$, gives $P x_0$.
9. By the definition of $P$, this means $R x_0 x_0$.
10. As $x_0$ was arbitrary, $\forall x\, Rxx$ holds.

A concrete check: take $W = \set{a, b}$ and $R = \set{(a, b), (b, b)}$, which is not reflexive at $a$.

11. Step 6 with $x_0 = a$ gives $P = \set{b}$.
12. Then $\square p$ is true at $a$, since its only successor $b$ is in $P$, and $p$ is false at $a$.
13. So $\mathrm{T}$ fails at $a$ under $V(p) = \set{b}$, as step 9 predicts.

## Remarks

The quantifier $\forall P$ ranges over all subsets of $W$, so the frame condition is second-order at first sight.
The minimal valuation of step 6 removes it: one well-chosen $P$ decides the whole quantifier.
This is the method of [[common modal axiom schemata|FDFQFWPM4X]] in the language of the [[standard translation|~ff160d1c]].
Sahlqvist's theorem makes the method uniform for a large class of formulas.
Some formulas, such as the McKinsey formula, admit no such elimination over all frames.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"What is necessary is true" holds on a frame exactly when every world sees itself.
To test the law at a world, we make $p$ true exactly at the worlds it sees.
Then "necessarily $p$" holds there by design.
The law then forces $p$ at the world itself, so the world sees itself.

### L2 human

1. Write frame validity of $\mathrm{T}$ as $\forall P\, \forall x\, (\forall y\, (Rxy \to Py) \to Px)$.
2. Put $P := R[x]$.
3. Read off $Rxx$.

The typical use is to compute the first-order correspondent of an axiom by one substitution for $P$.
A trap: the minimal valuation depends on $x$, so the substitution is made after $x$ is fixed.

### L3 human

The equivalence is between frame validity and a first-order condition; model validity of $\mathrm{T}$ is a different and weaker notion.
In Sahlqvist's method, the minimal valuation is read off from the antecedent of the formula, and the same recipe works for every Sahlqvist formula.
