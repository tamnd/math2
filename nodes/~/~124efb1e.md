## Summary

Analytic number theory is the study of the integers, and above all of the primes, with the methods of real and complex analysis.
Its central object is the Riemann zeta function, which is the sum of the reciprocals of the s-th powers of the positive integers and which is equal to a product over the primes.
The zeros of the zeta function control the error in the count of primes, so many questions on primes become questions on the zeros of zeta and L-functions.
Dirichlet used L-functions in 1837 to prove that each arithmetic progression with coprime first term and difference contains infinitely many primes, and Hadamard and de la Vallée Poussin proved the prime number theorem in 1896.
This topic collects the analytic number theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Elementary number theory, additive number theory, Diophantine approximation, and automorphic forms and the Langlands program have their own topics.
