## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite [[bipartite graph|~729625d7]] with parts $X$ and $Y$.
For $W \subseteq X$, let $N(W)$ be the set of vertices of $Y$ that are adjacent to some vertex of $W$.
Then $G$ has a [[matching|~4a67f6e1]] that covers every vertex of $X$ if and only if $|N(W)| \ge |W|$ for every $W \subseteq X$.

## Remarks

If $|X| = |Y|$, such a matching is a [[perfect matching|~4ed3c54f]]. Thus the condition then characterizes the perfect matchings.
A consequence: a finite regular bipartite graph with at least one edge has a perfect matching.
The theorem is the graph form of [[Hall's marriage theorem|~c249f6ac]]: let $X$ index the sets and let $Y$ be their union.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of $X$ as workers and $Y$ as jobs, with an edge when a worker can do a job.
Every worker can get a different job exactly when this holds.
Each group of workers can do, together, at least as many jobs as there are workers in the group.
