## Example

Let $P$ be an atomic formula other than $\bot$, and let $A$ be the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]] $P \vee \neg P$, where $\neg P$ is $P \to \bot$.
We compute the Gödel-Gentzen [[negative translation|QEZR7PZ2DD]] $A^N$ and derive it in intuitionistic [[natural deduction|PAQ4ZX2TX1]] NJ.

1. By the clause for atomic formulas, $P^N = \neg \neg P$.
2. By the clauses for $\to$ and $\bot$, $(\neg P)^N = (P \to \bot)^N = \neg \neg P \to \bot = \neg \neg \neg P$.
3. By the clause for $\vee$, $A^N = \neg (\neg \neg \neg P \wedge \neg \neg \neg \neg P)$.
4. In NJ, assume $\neg \neg \neg P \wedge \neg \neg \neg \neg P$ with the label $u$.
5. The rule ${\wedge}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\neg \neg \neg P$, and again ${\wedge}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\neg \neg \neg \neg P$.
6. The formula $\neg \neg \neg \neg P$ is $\neg \neg \neg P \to \bot$, so ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ with step 5 gives $\bot$.
7. The rule ${\to}\mathrm{I}^u$ discharges $u$ and gives $\neg (\neg \neg \neg P \wedge \neg \neg \neg \neg P)$, with no open assumptions.

So $\vdash_i A^N$, although $A$ itself is not provable in [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]].

## Remarks

The example shows that the translation of a classical axiom can have a short intuitionistic proof.
The proof uses only the rules for $\wedge$ and $\to$, as the [[double-negation translation|HGJVM49PA4]] removes $\vee$.
With the Kolmogorov translation the result is $\neg \neg (\neg \neg P \vee \neg \neg \neg \neg \neg P)$, and its proof needs the rules for $\vee$ as well.
For a formula $B$ in place of $P$, the same steps 4 to 7 work with $\neg B^N$ in place of $\neg \neg \neg P$.
In arithmetic with $P$ an equation, the translation can keep $P$ without $\neg \neg$; then $A^N$ is $\neg (\neg P \wedge \neg \neg P)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The law of excluded middle says "a statement is true or it is false".
Intuitionistic logic does not accept this law in general.
It accepts a weaker claim: "it cannot be that the statement is both not true and not false".
The negative translation produces exactly this weaker claim.
Its proof takes four short steps: assume both, pick out the two parts, and see that they contradict each other.

### L2 human

We repeat the derivation for $B = \forall x\, Q(x)$ in place of $P$.

1. $B^N = \forall x\, \neg \neg Q(x)$, and $(B \vee \neg B)^N = \neg (\neg B^N \wedge \neg \neg B^N)$.
2. Assume $\neg B^N \wedge \neg \neg B^N$.
3. The rule ${\wedge}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\neg B^N$ and $\neg \neg B^N$.
4. The formula $\neg \neg B^N$ is $\neg B^N \to \bot$, so ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\bot$.
5. ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ gives $(B \vee \neg B)^N$.

The typical use is the case of excluded middle in the [[proof of the Gödel-Gentzen theorem|~7122a50c]].
A trap is to stop at $\neg \neg P \vee \neg \neg \neg P$, the result of translating only the atomic formulas; this formula is not intuitionistically provable.
