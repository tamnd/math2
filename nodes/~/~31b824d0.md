## Example

Let $G = (\mathbb{Z}, +)$ be the [[integers|0C4JS9RF5M]] with addition.
Let $H = 4\mathbb{Z}$ and $K = 6\mathbb{Z}$ be the [[subgroups|0X1JXT47GA]] of multiples of $4$ and of multiples of $6$.
In additive notation, the $(H, K)$ [[double coset|~d8f44326]] of $g$ is $H + g + K = \{h + g + k : h \in H, k \in K\}$.

1. Addition is commutative, so $H + g + K = g + (H + K)$.
2. The [[product|0PT52XQZD7]] $H + K$ is $2\mathbb{Z}$, by [[this example|~2a781943]].
3. Thus $H + g + K = g + 2\mathbb{Z}$, the coset of $2\mathbb{Z}$ that contains $g$.
4. For $g$ even this is $2\mathbb{Z}$, and for $g$ odd it is $1 + 2\mathbb{Z}$.

Thus $4\mathbb{Z} \backslash \mathbb{Z} / 6\mathbb{Z}$ has exactly two elements, the even and the odd integers.

## Remarks

The double coset $2\mathbb{Z}$ is a union of [[left cosets|03DEH43F4H]] of $K$ and a union of [[right cosets|0NE1DK7H4T]] of $H$.
In the group $\mathbb{Z}$ these are the same kind of set, because the group is abelian.
We have $2\mathbb{Z} = 6\mathbb{Z} \cup (2 + 6\mathbb{Z}) \cup (4 + 6\mathbb{Z})$, with $3 = [4\mathbb{Z} : 12\mathbb{Z}]$ cosets of $6\mathbb{Z}$.
We also have $2\mathbb{Z} = 4\mathbb{Z} \cup (2 + 4\mathbb{Z})$, with $2 = [6\mathbb{Z} : 12\mathbb{Z}]$ cosets of $4\mathbb{Z}$.
Here $12\mathbb{Z} = H \cap K$.

The same argument works in each [[abelian group|082GHNPJ53]] $G$.
There $H + g + K = g + (H + K)$, so the double cosets are the cosets of the subgroup $H + K$.
In particular, all double cosets have the same size in an abelian group.
Different sizes, as in $S_3$, need a group that is not abelian.

All sets here are infinite, so the size formula $|H| \, |K| / |H \cap gKg^{-1}|$ does not apply.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start with a number $g$, then add any multiple of $4$ and any multiple of $6$.
You can reach $g + 2$, because $2 = 6 - 4$.
You cannot change the parity of $g$, because $4$ and $6$ are even.
So from $g$ you reach exactly the numbers with the same parity as $g$.
There are two pieces: the even numbers and the odd numbers.

### L2 human

We do the same computation for $H = 10\mathbb{Z}$ and $K = 15\mathbb{Z}$.

1. The sum $H + K$ is $5\mathbb{Z}$, because $\gcd(10, 15) = 5$ and $5 = 15 - 10$.
2. Thus the double coset of $g$ is $g + 5\mathbb{Z}$.
3. There are $5$ double cosets, one for each remainder modulo $5$.

The typical use is a quick check: in an abelian group, double cosets give nothing new.
A trap is to take $H \cap K$ in place of $H + K$.
The double cosets are cosets of the larger subgroup $H + K$, not of $12\mathbb{Z}$.
