## Summary

Fluid mechanics is the study of liquids and gases at rest and in motion.
A fluid is a material that deforms without limit under a shear stress, however small, and its motion is described by velocity, pressure and density fields that obey balance laws of mass, momentum and energy.
The subject explains the flow of air over wings, of water in pipes, rivers and oceans, of the atmosphere and of the interiors of stars, and it is used in engineering, meteorology, geophysics and biology.
Euler wrote the equations of ideal fluid motion in the 1750s, and Navier and Stokes added the effects of viscosity in the first half of the nineteenth century.
This topic collects the fluid mechanics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
The analysis of the fluid equations as partial differential equations, computational fluid dynamics, continuum and solid mechanics, and thermodynamics have their own topics.
