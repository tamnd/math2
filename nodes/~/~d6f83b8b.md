## Statement

Let $L$ be a finite signature of unary and binary relation symbols.
The two-variable fragment $\mathrm{FO}^2$ is the set of first-order $L$-formulas, with equality, that use only the two variables $x$ and $y$; a variable may be quantified again inside its own scope.

1. Scott normal form. From each $\mathrm{FO}^2$ sentence $\varphi$ one computes in polynomial time a sentence $\psi = \forall x \, \forall y \, \chi \wedge \bigwedge_{i=1}^m \forall x \, \exists y \, \chi_i$, with $\chi$ and each $\chi_i$ quantifier-free, over $L$ plus new unary symbols. Every model of $\psi$ satisfies $\varphi$, and every model of $\varphi$ expands to a model of $\psi$.
2. Exponential model property. If an $\mathrm{FO}^2$ sentence $\varphi$ has a model, then it has a finite model with at most $2^{c \abs{\varphi}}$ elements, where $c$ is a constant and $\abs{\varphi}$ is the length of $\varphi$.
3. Consequently, [[satisfiability|QN7M1JCGT9]] of $\mathrm{FO}^2$ sentences is decidable, and it lies in the [[complexity class|ESPYW94XGR]] NEXPTIME of problems solved in nondeterministic exponential time.

The proof page proves parts 1 to 3 completely.
The matching lower bound, that satisfiability of $\mathrm{FO}^2$ is NEXPTIME-hard, is cited and not proved.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Two variables are needed. The prefix class $[\forall \exists \forall]$ is undecidable, and its sentences use three variables, so three-variable logic $\mathrm{FO}^3$ is undecidable; see [[decidable prefix classes|~48b4c091]].
- The finite model property fails with three variables: the sentence [[with only infinite models|~27146afc]] uses three variables for transitivity.
- Function symbols are excluded. With one unary function symbol and two variables, $\forall x \, \forall y \, (f(x) = f(y) \to x = y) \wedge \exists y \, \forall x \, (f(x) \neq y)$ has only infinite models.
- Counting quantifiers break the finite model property. In the extension $\mathrm{C}^2$ with counting quantifiers, where $\exists^{\le 1} y$ means "for at most one $y$", the sentence $\forall x \, \exists^{\le 1} y \, R(y, x) \wedge \forall x \, \exists y \, R(x, y) \wedge \exists x \, \forall y \, \neg R(y, x)$ says that $R$ contains an injective function that is not onto, which needs an infinite domain.

### Consequences

1. Many modal and description logics translate into $\mathrm{FO}^2$, so their satisfiability problems are decidable. For example, the standard translation of basic [[modal logic|C3RZY20ETK]] uses only two variables.
2. Every satisfiable $\mathrm{FO}^2$ sentence has a finite model, so for $\mathrm{FO}^2$ the satisfiable sentences and the finitely satisfiable sentences coincide.
3. Validity of $\mathrm{FO}^2$ sentences is decidable, because $\varphi$ is valid if and only if $\neg \varphi$, also in $\mathrm{FO}^2$, has no model.

### Converse

Part 3 follows from part 2, but decidability does not imply the finite model property in general.
The counting extension $\mathrm{C}^2$ is decidable, by the results cited below, but it lacks the finite model property, by the last item above.

### Generalizations

- The two-variable logic with counting quantifiers $\exists^{\ge n}$, written $\mathrm{C}^2$, is decidable; this was proved in 1997 by Grädel, Otto and Rosen, and independently by Pacholski, Szwast and Tendera.
- Pratt-Hartmann proved in 2005 that satisfiability of $\mathrm{C}^2$ is in NEXPTIME, also with numbers written in binary.
- The [[guarded fragment|~cca08292]] generalizes the modal side of $\mathrm{FO}^2$ in another direction, by restricting quantifiers instead of variables.

## History

Scott introduced the normal form in 1962 and reduced the two-variable fragment without equality to the Gödel class.
Mortimer proved in 1975 that two-variable logic with equality has the finite model property, so it is decidable.
Grädel, Kolaitis and Vardi proved in 1997 that every satisfiable sentence has a model of exponential size, and that satisfiability is NEXPTIME-complete.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A logical statement uses names such as $x$ and $y$ for the objects it talks about.
If a statement may use only two names, and may reuse them, it can still say a lot, for example "everyone has a friend who has a friend".
Such statements are always decidable by a computer.
The reason is that a statement with two names cannot force an infinite world: whenever it has a world, it has a finite one, and the computer can search for it.

### L2 human

We put the sentence $\varphi = \forall x \, \exists y \, (R(x, y) \wedge \exists x \, (R(y, x) \wedge P(x)))$ in Scott normal form.
It says that each element has an $R$-successor that has an $R$-successor in $P$, and it reuses $x$.

1. The innermost quantified subformula is $\exists x \, (R(y, x) \wedge P(x))$, with free variable $y$. We introduce a new unary symbol $A$ and replace this subformula by $A(y)$.
2. The definition $\forall y \, (A(y) \leftrightarrow \exists x \, (R(y, x) \wedge P(x)))$ splits into $\forall x \, \forall y \, (R(y, x) \wedge P(x) \to A(y))$ and $\forall y \, \exists x \, (A(y) \to R(y, x) \wedge P(x))$.
3. The formula becomes $\forall x \, \exists y \, (R(x, y) \wedge A(y))$, which already has the form $\forall x \, \exists y \, \chi_1$.
4. Renaming the variables of the second part of step 2 gives $\chi_2 = A(x) \to R(x, y) \wedge P(y)$ with prefix $\forall x \, \exists y$.
5. So $\psi = \forall x \, \forall y \, (R(y, x) \wedge P(x) \to A(y)) \wedge \forall x \, \exists y \, (R(x, y) \wedge A(y)) \wedge \forall x \, \exists y \, (A(x) \to R(x, y) \wedge P(y))$.

A model of $\varphi$ is the two-element structure with $R = \set{(a, b), (b, a)}$ and $P = \set{a, b}$, and $A = \set{a, b}$ expands it to a model of $\psi$.
The typical use of the normal form is to reduce questions about $\mathrm{FO}^2$ to the simple shape $\forall \forall \wedge \forall \exists$.
A trap is to count the variables wrongly: the sentence "$R$ is transitive" needs three variables, so it is not in $\mathrm{FO}^2$.

### L3 human

Two-variable logic is the first-order counterpart of modal logic: the standard translation of modal formulas uses two variables, and both logics have a small model property.
The proof of the exponential model property is a combinatorial surgery on models, with "kings", elements whose 1-type occurs once, and three copies of the other elements that serve as witnesses for each other in a cycle.
Extensions with counting, with two equivalence relations, or with a linear order have been studied in detail; some remain decidable and some become undecidable.
The [[guarded fragment|~cca08292]] and the [[decidable prefix classes|~48b4c091]] are the other main families of decidable fragments of the classical decision problem.
