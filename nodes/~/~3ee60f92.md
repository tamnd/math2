## Example

Let $H = \set{0, a, 1}$ with $0 < a < 1$.
In a chain, $x \to y = 1$ if $x \leq y$, and $x \to y = y$ otherwise.
We use the [[Heyting algebra semantics|~32a20f35]].

1. $H$ is a Heyting algebra: for $x \leq y$ every $z$ has $z \wedge x \leq y$; for $x > y$, $z \wedge x \leq y$ holds exactly when $z \leq y$.
2. The negations are $\neg 0 = 1$, $\neg a = 0$ and $\neg 1 = 0$.
3. With $v(p) = a$, $v(p \vee \neg p) = a \vee 0 = a \neq 1$. So the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]] fails.
4. With $v(p) = a$, $v(\neg \neg p \to p) = 1 \to a = a$. So double negation elimination fails.
5. With $v(p) = a$ and $v(q) = 0$, $v(p \to q) = 0$ and $v((p \to q) \to p) = 1$. So [[Peirce's law|GMSZ75D5WE]] gets $1 \to a = a$.
6. For every $x$, $\neg x \vee \neg \neg x$ is $1 \vee 0$ or $0 \vee 1$. So the [[law of weak excluded middle|CX032ACTMZ]] is valid in $H$.
7. For all $x, y$, one of $x \to y$ and $y \to x$ is $1$. So $(p \to q) \vee (q \to p)$ is valid in $H$.

## Remarks

The algebra $H$ is the algebra of up-sets of the two-point chain, a Kripke frame.
Its logic is the three-valued Gödel logic, the extension of [[Gödel-Dummett logic|~cfc28e48]] by the axiom of depth two.
By the soundness half of the theorem, steps 3 to 5 show that the three formulas are not theorems of $\mathsf{IPC}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The three values are "false", "unsettled" and "true".
A statement that is unsettled is not refuted, so its negation is false.
Then "$p$ or not $p$" is only unsettled, not true.
This one small table shows that excluded middle is not an intuitionistic law.

### L2 human

1. Compute $a \to 0$: the largest $z$ with $z \wedge a \leq 0$ is $0$.
2. Compute $0 \to a$: every $z$ has $z \wedge 0 \leq a$, so the value is $1$.
3. Compute $1 \to a$: the largest $z$ with $z \wedge 1 \leq a$ is $a$.

The typical use is to refute an intuitionistic formula by a finite table.
A trap: the chain validates weak excluded middle and prelinearity, so it can not refute them; a larger algebra is needed.
