## Summary

Coding theory is the study of codes that detect and correct errors in data that is sent over a noisy channel or kept in storage.
A block code of length n over a finite alphabet is a set of words of length n, and its minimum distance is the least number of positions in which two different codewords differ.
A code with minimum distance d corrects every pattern of fewer than d/2 errors, and a good code has both a large distance and a high rate.
The subject started from 1948 to 1950 with Shannon's channel coding theorem and with the codes of Hamming and Golay, and it grew to use algebra, combinatorics, algebraic geometry and probability.
This topic collects the coding theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Information theory, mathematical cryptography and the theory of computation have their own topics, and so do designs and finite geometries.
