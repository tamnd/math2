## Statement

Let $p$ and $q$ be [[propositions|~c068c095]].
The [[implication|~06eaf3d7]] $p \to q$ is [[logically equivalent|~01a642be]] to its contrapositive $\neg q \to \neg p$.
$$p \to q \equiv \neg q \to \neg p$$

## Remarks

The direction from $p \to q$ to $\neg q \to \neg p$ is valid in intuitionistic logic. The other direction needs classical logic.
This equivalence is the basis of [[proof by contrapositive|~717fff78]].
Do not confuse the contrapositive with the [[converse|~1b2070a2]] $q \to p$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"If it rains, the street is wet" says the same as "if the street is not wet, it does not rain".
Both sentences are false in exactly one case: rain and a dry street.
