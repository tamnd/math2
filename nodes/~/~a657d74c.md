## Proof

Let $L_1$ be the language of the non-logical symbols of $\varphi$, and $L_2$ that of $\psi$.
Put $L_0 = L_1 \cap L_2$.
Assume $\varphi \models \psi$.
We use part 1 of [[Robinson's joint consistency theorem|~6d43779a]], proved by a [[joint Henkin construction|~3d4d4be9]].

1. Suppose that no sentence $\theta$ of $L_0$ satisfies both $\varphi \models \theta$ and $\theta \models \psi$.
2. Let $\Gamma = \set{\varphi}$, a set of sentences of $L_1$, and $\Sigma = \set{\neg \psi}$, a set of sentences of $L_2$.
3. Suppose a sentence $\theta$ of $L_0$ separates $\Gamma$ from $\Sigma$: $\varphi \vdash \theta$ and $\neg \psi \vdash \neg \theta$.
4. By the [[soundness theorem|~331f6430]], $\varphi \models \theta$ and $\neg \psi \models \neg \theta$.
5. The second fact gives $\theta \models \psi$. Indeed, a model of $\theta$ that fails $\psi$ would satisfy $\neg \psi$, so it would satisfy $\neg \theta$.
6. So $\theta$ is an interpolant, which contradicts step 1. Hence no sentence of $L_0$ separates $\Gamma$ from $\Sigma$.
7. Robinson's theorem gives a model $\mathcal{A}$ of $\set{\varphi, \neg \psi}$.
8. Then $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi$ and $\mathcal{A} \not\models \psi$, which contradicts $\varphi \models \psi$.
9. So some sentence $\theta$ of $L_0$ has $\varphi \models \theta$ and $\theta \models \psi$. Its non-logical symbols lie in $L_0$, so they occur in both $\varphi$ and $\psi$.

## Idea

If no sentence of the common language lies between $\varphi$ and $\psi$, then $\varphi$ and $\neg \psi$ cannot be separated in the common language.
Robinson's theorem then joins a model of $\varphi$ and a model of $\neg \psi$ into one structure.
That structure contradicts $\varphi \models \psi$.

## Variants

- Classical form: let $\mathcal{M} \models \neg \psi$ satisfy every $L_0$-consequence of $\varphi$. Apply part 2 of Robinson's theorem with $T = \mathrm{Th}_{L_0}(\mathcal{M})$, using compactness to show $T \cup \set{\varphi}$ consistent. Then compactness gives a finite conjunction of $L_0$-consequences of $\varphi$ as $\theta$.
- The [[proof-theoretic proof|~ca2164eb]] by the cut-free sequent calculus constructs $\theta$ from a proof, which this proof does not.
