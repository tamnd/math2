## Example

Let $f \colon \R \to \R^3$ be $f(t) = (t, t, t)$ and $g \colon \R^3 \to \R^2$ be $g(x, y, z) = (x - y, y - z)$.
We check that $0 \to \R \xrightarrow{f} \R^3 \xrightarrow{g} \R^2 \to 0$ is a [[short exact sequence|8MCYV0P04T]], and we split it.

1. $f$ is injective: $f(t) = 0$ forces $t = 0$.
2. $g \circ f = 0$: $g(t, t, t) = (0, 0)$. So $\operatorname{im} f \subseteq \ker g$.
3. The [[kernel|02YFME3RGW]] of $g$: $g(x, y, z) = 0$ means $x = y$ and $y = z$, so $\ker g = \R(1, 1, 1)$, which is the [[image|0JB228DCWB]] of $f$.
4. $g$ is surjective: $g(a + b, b, 0) = (a, b)$ for all $(a, b)$.
5. So the sequence is exact at each place.
6. Splitting: $s(a, b) = (a + b, b, 0)$ is linear, and $g \circ s = \operatorname{id}_{\R^2}$ by step 4.
7. The map $\R \oplus \R^2 \to \R^3$, $(t, (a, b)) \mapsto f(t) + s(a, b) = (t + a + b, t + b, t)$, is an isomorphism. Its inverse is $(x, y, z) \mapsto (z, (x - y, y - z))$.
8. Dimensions: $1 - 3 + 2 = 0$, as the [[alternating sum formula|~78f0e572]] requires.

## Remarks

The image of $s$ is the $xy$-plane $\{z = 0\}$, which is a complement of $\ker g = \R(1, 1, 1)$.
Another complement gives another splitting.
For instance, $s'(a, b) = (a + b, b, 0) - \tfrac{a + 2b}{3}(1, 1, 1)$ has image the plane $x + y + z = 0$.
A left inverse of $f$ is $r(x, y, z) = z$, read off from step 7.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The line $\R(1, 1, 1)$ sits inside $\R^3$, and the map $g$ measures how far a vector is from that line.
The plane $z = 0$ is a second piece, and $\R^3$ is the line plus the plane.

### L2 human

To check exactness in the middle, both inclusions are needed.
Step 2 gives $\operatorname{im} f \subseteq \ker g$, and step 3 gives the reverse inclusion by solving $g = 0$.
