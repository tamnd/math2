## Summary

Fix a sentence $\mathsf{P}$ of [[second-order arithmetic|DC60FYCSPM]] and a sentence $\mathsf{Q}$; we say that $\mathsf{P}$ *implies* $\mathsf{Q}$ *over* $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ when $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathsf{P}$ proves $\mathsf{Q}$.
The *reverse mathematics zoo* is a family of principles $\mathsf{P}$, mostly from combinatorics and measure theory.
Each of them is provable in a standard system such as $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ or $\mathsf{ACA}_0^+$, but is not known to be equivalent over [[RCA0|~58b63877]] to any of the five main subsystems.
The zoo also records the known implications and non-implications between these principles over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
Typical members are $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$, $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$, $\mathsf{COH}$, $\mathsf{ADS}$, $\mathsf{CAC}$, $\mathsf{EM}$, $\mathsf{DNR}$, $\mathsf{WWKL}$ and $\mathsf{HT}$.
The name comes from the Reverse Mathematics Zoo website of Damir Dzhafarov, a database and a diagram of these principles.
The zoo matters because it shows that the five main subsystems do not describe the strength of all theorems of ordinary mathematics.

## Motivation

[[Reverse mathematics|7SQS69T17R]] finds that most theorems of ordinary mathematics are provable in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ or are equivalent over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ to one of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$.
[[Ramsey's theorem|00TQV3F5S4]] for pairs is the best-known theorem that breaks this pattern.
Ramsey's theorem for triples is equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, and Seetapun showed in 1995 that $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ does not imply $\mathsf{ACA}_0$.
Liu showed in 2012 that $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ does not imply $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.
Conversely, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ does not imply $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$.
So $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ is equivalent to none of the five systems.
To understand it, logicians split it into weaker principles and compared these with each other.
Each new principle met in this work was added to the list, and the list became the zoo.

## Notation

Each principle below is a sentence of second-order arithmetic, and "infinite" means "not bounded in $\N$".
For a set $X \subseteq \N$, write $[X]^2$ for the set of pairs $\set{x, y}$ with $x < y$ in $X$.

- $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$: for every colouring $c : [\N]^2 \to \set{0, 1}$ there is an infinite set $H$ such that $c$ is constant on $[H]^2$. Such an $H$ is *homogeneous* for $c$.
- $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$: the same statement for *stable* colourings, those $c$ such that for every $x$ the value $c(x, y)$ is the same for all large enough $y$.
- $\mathsf{COH}$: for every sequence $(R_i)_{i \in \N}$ of sets there is an infinite set $C$ such that, for each $i$, either $C \cap R_i$ is finite or $C \setminus R_i$ is finite. Such a $C$ is *cohesive* for the sequence.
- $\mathsf{ADS}$: every infinite [[linear order|0FRXBVVR03]] has an infinite ascending sequence or an infinite descending sequence.
- $\mathsf{CAC}$: every infinite [[partial order|0QV2JS9RJ3]] has an infinite chain or an infinite [[antichain|03HF498P1J]].
- $\mathsf{EM}$ (the Erdős-Moser principle): every infinite [[tournament|M2F9TN9MW5]] on a subset of $\N$ has an infinite transitive subtournament.
- $\mathsf{DNR}$: for every set $X$ there is a function $f : \N \to \N$ with $f(e) \ne \Phi^X_e(e)$ for every $e$ such that $\Phi^X_e(e)$ is defined. Here $\Phi^X_e$ is the $e$-th Turing functional with oracle $X$.
- $\mathsf{WWKL}$: [[weak weak König's lemma|~7de8ca26]], every subtree of $2^{<\N}$ of positive measure has an infinite path.
- $\mathsf{HT}$: [[Hindman's theorem|ZTWTZ8CSGV]], for every finite colouring of $\N$ there is an infinite set $S$ whose finite sums of distinct elements all have the same colour.
- $\mathsf{ACA}_0^+$: the system $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ together with the axiom that for every set $X$ the $\omega$-th [[Turing jump|60P2XWY5QX]] $X^{(\omega)}$ exists.

We write $\mathsf{P} \to \mathsf{Q}$ for "$\mathsf{P}$ implies $\mathsf{Q}$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$".
Papers in the area also use $\le_\omega$ (implication in all $\omega$-models), $\le_c$ (computable reduction) and $\le_W$ (Weihrauch reduction).

## Remarks

### Examples

- $\mathsf{RT}^2_2 \to \mathsf{CAC}$: colour a pair by whether its two points are comparable. The [[example of this implication|~17c1a4f3]] checks each step.
- $\mathsf{CAC} \to \mathsf{ADS}$: a linear order gives a partial order whose chains ascend and whose antichains descend. The [[example of this implication|~5213e658]] checks each step.
- $\mathsf{RT}^2_2 \to \mathsf{EM}$: colour $\set{x, y}$ with $x < y$ by $1$ when the tournament has the edge $x \to y$. On a homogeneous set of colour $1$ every edge goes from the smaller to the larger number, so the subtournament is transitive; colour $0$ is symmetric.
- $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ proves $\mathsf{WWKL}$: a tree of positive measure has a string of every length, so it is infinite, and [[weak König's lemma|~e7c6fa0e]] gives a path.
- $\mathsf{RT}^2_2 \to \mathsf{SRT}^2_2$, because a stable colouring is a colouring.

### Non-examples

- Ramsey's theorem for triples $\mathsf{RT}^3_2$ is not a member of the zoo. It is equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, as the [[non-example|~a23d1c2d]] shows.
- The [[Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem|0KDJD249DT]] for sequences in $[0, 1]$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$. It belongs to the Big Five pattern.
- The [[intermediate value theorem|00KAFBR32E]] is provable in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$. A principle that $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ already proves adds nothing, so it is not studied as a member of the zoo.

### Basic properties

The following relations hold over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
Each arrow that is not marked as strict may or may not reverse.

1. $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2 + \mathsf{COH}$. Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman stated this in 2001. The direction $\mathsf{RT}^2_2 \to \mathsf{COH}$ needed a correction of the induction used, given by Mileti and, independently, by Jockusch and Lempp.
2. $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$ does not imply $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$. Chong, Slaman and Yang proved this in 2014 with a model whose first-order part is not standard. Monin and Patey later gave an $\omega$-model.
3. $\mathsf{RT}^2_2 \to \mathsf{CAC} \to \mathsf{ADS} \to \mathsf{COH}$, and $\mathsf{CAC}$ and $\mathsf{ADS}$ are strictly weaker than $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ (Hirschfeldt and Shore, 2007).
4. $\mathsf{CAC}$ does not imply $\mathsf{DNR}$ (Hirschfeldt and Shore). Since $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2 \to \mathsf{DNR}$ (Hirschfeldt, Jockusch, Kjos-Hanssen, Lempp and Slaman), $\mathsf{CAC}$ does not imply $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$.
5. $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{EM} + \mathsf{ADS}$. This splitting goes back to Bovykin and Weiermann. Lerman, Solomon and Towsner showed that $\mathsf{EM}$ is strictly weaker than $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$.
6. $\mathsf{WKL}_0 \to \mathsf{WWKL} \to \mathsf{DNR}$, and both arrows are strict. Yu and Simpson proved the first strictness in 1990, and Ambos-Spies, Kjos-Hanssen, Lempp and Slaman the second in 2004.
7. $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ does not imply $\mathsf{WWKL}$ (Liu, 2015), and so it does not imply $\mathsf{WKL}_0$.
8. $\mathsf{ACA}_0^+$ proves $\mathsf{HT}$, and $\mathsf{HT}$ implies $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ (Blass, Hirst and Simpson, 1987). Whether $\mathsf{HT}$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, to $\mathsf{ACA}_0^+$, or to neither is an open problem.

A non-implication $\mathsf{P} \not\to \mathsf{Q}$ is usually proved by an $\omega$-model $(\N, \mathcal{S})$ of $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathsf{P}$ in which $\mathsf{Q}$ fails.
The $\omega$-models of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ are the Turing ideals, so this is a question of [[Turing degrees|P5N2ND3VCG]]: one builds solutions to instances of $\mathsf{P}$ that do not compute solutions to some instance of $\mathsf{Q}$.
The finer reductions, above all Weihrauch reducibility, are studied in [[Weihrauch reducibility and the effective content of theorems|4G2JQ13XZ0]].
The [[Π¹₁ conservation and first-order strength of RT²₂|~848bb923]] are treated on the page of Ramsey's theorem for pairs.

### History

Specker and Jockusch studied the computability of homogeneous sets in the early 1970s.
Hirst's thesis of 1987 studied Ramsey's theorem in subsystems of second-order arithmetic.
Seetapun's theorem of 1995 and the paper of Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman in 2001 started the systematic study of principles below $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$.
Hirschfeldt and Shore added $\mathsf{ADS}$ and $\mathsf{CAC}$ in 2007.
Dzhafarov's website, the Reverse Mathematics Zoo, collects the known results in one diagram.

### Common errors

- Reading "$\mathsf{P} \to \mathsf{Q}$ is not known" as "$\mathsf{P}$ does not imply $\mathsf{Q}$". Many arrows of the zoo are open, and the diagram marks them as open.
- Treating a computable reduction as a proof over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$. A proof over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ may use only $\Sigma^0_1$ induction, and the first proof of $\mathsf{RT}^2_2 \to \mathsf{COH}$ failed at exactly this point.
- Thinking that an implication in all $\omega$-models gives an implication over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$. Every $\omega$-model satisfies $\mathsf{B}\Sigma^0_2$, but $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ does not prove $\mathsf{B}\Sigma^0_2$. So a separation may need a model whose first-order part is not standard, as in the first proof of item 2.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Logicians measure how strong a theorem is by the axioms that it needs.
For most theorems the answer is one of five standard systems of axioms.
Some theorems from combinatorics do not fit any of the five.
Here is a statement of this kind: in any endless list of different fractions, one can pick an endless sub-list that goes always up or always down.
For the list $3, 1, 4, 5, 9, 2, 6, \dots$ a sub-list that goes up starts with $3, 4, 5, 9$.
The principle $\mathsf{ADS}$ says the same for every infinite ordering, and it is not equivalent to any of the five systems.
The zoo is the collection of all such in-between statements, with a map of which one implies which.

### L2 human

We prove $\mathsf{RT}^2_2 \to \mathsf{EM}$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.

1. Let $T$ be an infinite tournament on $\N$: for $x \ne y$ exactly one of $x \to y$ and $y \to x$ holds. By renumbering the vertices in increasing order, we may assume that the vertex set is $\N$.
2. Define $c(\set{x, y}) = 1$ if $x < y$ and $x \to y$, and $c(\set{x, y}) = 0$ if $x < y$ and $y \to x$. The set $c$ exists by $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension, because its definition has no unbounded quantifier.
3. $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ gives an infinite set $H$ on which $c$ is constant.
4. If the colour is $1$, then $x \to y$ for all $x < y$ in $H$. If $x < y < z$ are in $H$ with $x \to y$ and $y \to z$, then $x \to z$, because $c(\set{x, z}) = 1$.
5. So the subtournament on $H$ is transitive. If the colour is $0$, the same argument applies with all edges reversed.

The typical use of the zoo is this kind of short reduction, together with a long construction of an $\omega$-model for the converse.
The trap: the reduction must be carried out inside $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, so each set that it uses must exist by $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension.

### L3 human

The zoo is not a linear order: $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ and $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ are incomparable, by Jockusch and by Liu.
Modern work measures principles by several reductions at once: implication over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, implication in $\omega$-models, computable reduction, and the uniform Weihrauch reduction of computable analysis.
A second line asks for the first-order part of each principle, that is, the arithmetic sentences that it proves; for $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ this is the topic of the work of Chong, Slaman and Yang.
The measure-theoretic members, $\mathsf{WWKL}$ and $\mathsf{DNR}$, connect the zoo to [[algorithmic randomness|KW3V44GPPC]], and the subsystems themselves are surveyed in [[reverse mathematics and subsystems of second-order arithmetic|BY8AAFN7C5]].
