## Definition

A *Nelson algebra* is a structure $(A, \land, \lor, \to, \sim, 0, 1)$ such that, for all $x, y, z \in A$:

1. $(A, \land, \lor, \sim, 0, 1)$ is a [[De Morgan algebra|CDGBK86D23]]: a bounded distributive lattice with $\sim \sim x = x$ and $\sim(x \lor y) = \sim x \land \sim y$;
2. Kleene condition: $x \land \sim x \le y \lor \sim y$;
3. $x \land z \le \sim x \lor y$ iff $z \le x \to y$;
4. $(x \land y) \to z = x \to (y \to z)$.

## Notation

Clauses 1 and 2 define a Kleene algebra in the sense of many-valued logic.
The strong implication is $x \Rightarrow y := (x \to y) \land (\sim y \to \sim x)$.

## Remarks

1. Example: [[the three-element Nelson chain|~e8311f8f]].
2. Example: [[the five-element Nelson chain|~0e98da42]].
3. Non-example: [[the four-element De Morgan algebra with two fixed points|~43aa03ed]] fails clause 2.
4. Clause 3 says that $x \to y$ is the largest $z$ with $x \land z \le \sim x \lor y$, so $\to$ is determined by the lattice and $\sim$.
5. For every [[Heyting algebra|BND6A96S3D]] $H$, the pairs $(a, b)$ with $a \land b = 0$, with $\sim(a, b) = (b, a)$ and $(a, b) \to (c, d) = (a \to c, a \land d)$, satisfy clauses 1 to 4.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Nelson algebra is a lattice of truth values with a negation that turns the order upside down.
The implication $x \to y$ is the weakest $z$ that, together with $x$, leads to "not $x$, or $y$".
Clause 4 lets one move a conjunction in front of an implication.

### L2 human

1. Take the three-element chain $0 < u < 1$ with $\sim$ swapping $0$ and $1$.
2. For $x = u$, clause 3 asks for the largest $z$ with $u \land z \le u \lor y$.
3. Every $z$ qualifies, since $u \land z \le u$. So $u \to y = 1$ for every $y$.
4. For $x = 1$, the condition is $z \le y$, so $1 \to y = y$.

The typical use: clause 3 gives a direct way to compute $\to$ in a finite example.
The trap: clause 3 is not residuation for $\land$, because $\sim x \lor y$ appears instead of $y$.
