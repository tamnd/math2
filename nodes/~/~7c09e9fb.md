## Statement

Let $a < b$ and let $f \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be monotone.
Then $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a, b]$.

## Remarks

Assume $f$ increasing, and take the partition into $n$ equal pieces. Then $M_i = f(x_i)$ and $m_i = f(x_{i-1})$, so the sum telescopes:
$$U(f, P) - L(f, P) = \frac{b - a}{n} (f(b) - f(a)).$$
This tends to $0$, so [[Riemann's criterion|~68cbdcd9]] applies.
A monotone function can have infinitely many discontinuities, and it is still integrable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For an increasing function, the gaps between upper and lower rectangles stack up to one column of total height $f(b) - f(a)$.
Its width shrinks to $0$ as the partition gets finer.
