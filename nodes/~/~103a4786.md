## Example

Take the three lines $x + y \leq 1$, $y + z \leq 1$ and $x + z \leq 1$ in the variables $x, y, z$.
They are the lines of the clauses $\neg x \vee \neg y$, $\neg y \vee \neg z$ and $\neg x \vee \neg z$, which say that at most one of $x, y, z$ is true.
We derive $x + y + z \leq 1$ with one division of the [[cutting-planes system|~f9bde1dc]].

1. In the $\geq$ form the lines are $-x - y \geq -1$, $-y - z \geq -1$ and $-x - z \geq -1$.
2. Linear combination of the first two with $\alpha = \beta = 1$ gives $-x - 2y - z \geq -2$.
3. Linear combination of this with the third line gives $-2x - 2y - 2z \geq -3$.
4. Every coefficient is $-2$, so we can divide by $c = 2$. The result is $-x - y - z \geq \ceil{-3/2} = -1$.
5. Since $-3/2 = -1.5$, the least integer that is at least $-1.5$ is $-1$.
6. So the derived line is $x + y + z \leq 1$, which in the $\leq$ form reads $x + y + z \leq \floor{3/2} = 1$.

The derived line is a Chvátal-Gomory cut.

## Remarks

The cut removes the point $(1/2, 1/2, 1/2)$.

1. At this point, $x + y = y + z = x + z = 1$, so the point satisfies the three lines.
2. It satisfies the Boolean axioms, since each coordinate is in $[0, 1]$.
3. But $x + y + z = 3/2 > 1$, so it violates the derived line.

The cut keeps all $0$-$1$ solutions.
The $0$-$1$ points that satisfy the three lines have at most one coordinate equal to $1$, so they satisfy $x + y + z \leq 1$.
The line $x + y + z \leq 1$ is not a linear combination of the axioms, since every linear combination holds at $(1/2, 1/2, 1/2)$.

For $m$ variables with all pairwise lines $x_i + x_j \leq 1$, the sum of all these lines is $(m - 1) \sum x_i \leq \binom{m}{2}$, and one division by $m - 1$ gives only $\sum x_i \leq \floor{m/2}$.
To reach $\sum x_i \leq 1$, the induction of the [[pigeonhole refutation|~89a278ed]] uses $m - 1$ divisions.
In integer programming the same cut is the clique inequality of the triangle, and it is a cut of the same kind as the [[Gomory fractional cut|AZJMC8A93H]] of the simplex method.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Three lamps are given, and no two of them may be on at the same time.
Then at most one lamp is on.
If lamps could be half on, all three could be half on: each pair would give $1/2 + 1/2 = 1$, which is allowed.
Adding the three rules gives "two times the total is at most $3$", so the total is at most $1.5$.
Since the total is a whole number, it is at most $1$, and this removes the half-on point.

### L2 human

The recipe for a Chvátal-Gomory cut has three steps.

1. Choose non-negative weights for the lines, here $1, 1, 1$.
2. Add the weighted lines, here $2x + 2y + 2z \leq 3$.
3. Divide by a common factor of the coefficients and round the right side, here $3/2$ down to $1$.

The typical use is to remove a fractional vertex of the linear relaxation.
A trap is to forget the divisibility condition: the factor must divide every coefficient on the left side.
