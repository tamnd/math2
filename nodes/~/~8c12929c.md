## Statement

Let $X$ be a [[topological space|~77bfcc55]] and let $K \subseteq X$.
Then $K$ is [[compact|~56dabfb9]] if and only if it has the property that follows.
Let $(F_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of [[closed subsets|~dfeacff3]] of $X$ with $K \cap F_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap F_{i_n} \neq \emptyset$ for all finitely many indices $i_1, \ldots, i_n \in I$.
Then also $K \cap \bigcap_{i \in I} F_i \neq \emptyset$.

## Remarks

A family of sets has the finite intersection property when each finite subfamily has a nonempty intersection.
Proof: take complements. The sets $X \setminus F_i$ cover $K$ if and only if $K \cap \bigcap_i F_i = \emptyset$. Then use [[compactness via open covers|~6bb86ae8]].
For $K = X$: a space is compact if and only if each family of closed sets with the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection.
[[Cantor's intersection theorem|~619681b5]] is a special case.

## Explanations

### L1 human

This is the dual form of compactness, with closed sets instead of open sets.
Suppose that each finite subfamily of the closed sets has a common point in $K$. Then the whole family has a common point in $K$.
