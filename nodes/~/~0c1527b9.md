## Statement

Let $\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k (x - x_0)^k$ be a power series. Let $A$ be either of the two limits $\lim_{k \to \infty} |a_{k+1}/a_k|$ or $\lim_{k \to \infty} |a_k|^{1/k}$. Assume that this limit exists, with the value $\infty$ allowed.

If $A = 0$, then the [[radius of convergence|0660QX1VPR]] of the series is $\infty$.

If $0 < A < \infty$, then the radius of convergence is $1/A$.

If $A = \infty$, then the series diverges for every $x \ne x_0$. So the radius of convergence is $0$.

## Remarks

The proof applies the [[ratio test|0QNY6ZBXPN]] or the [[root test|05H4DDDYBZ]] to the terms $a_k (x - x_0)^k$. Their ratio, or their $k$-th root, tends to $A |x - x_0|$.

For example, $\sum x^k / k!$ has $A = 0$, and $\sum 2^k x^k$ has $A = 2$ and radius $1/2$.
