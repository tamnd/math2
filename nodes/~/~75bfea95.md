## Example

Let $\mathrm{Pr}$ be the set of [[prime numbers|01826KMG2H]].
Let $\pi(x)$ be the formula
$$S0 < x \land \forall u < x \, \forall v < x \, \neg (u \cdot v = x).$$
We check that $\pi$ is $\Delta_0$ and defines $\mathrm{Pr}$ in $\N$, so that $\mathrm{Pr}$ lies in the [[linear-time hierarchy|~38fa6f6e]].

1. $\Delta_0$. The quantifiers $\forall u < x$ and $\forall v < x$ are bounded by $x$, which contains neither $u$ nor $v$. The rest is a Boolean combination of atomic formulas.
2. If $p$ is prime, then $1 < p$. Suppose $u \cdot v = p$ with $u, v < p$. Since $p$ is prime, $u = 1$ or $v = 1$, so the other factor is $p$, against $u, v < p$. So $\N \models \pi(p)$.
3. If $n \le 1$, then $\N \models \neg \pi(n)$, since the first conjunct fails.
4. If $n > 1$ is not prime, then $n = a \cdot b$ with $1 < a < n$ and $1 < b < n$. The pair $u = a$, $v = b$ refutes the second conjunct, so $\N \models \neg \pi(n)$.
5. For $n = 15$: $u = 3$ and $v = 5$ are below $15$ and $3 \cdot 5 = 15$, so $\N \models \neg \pi(15)$.
6. For $n = 7$: no product $u \cdot v$ with $u, v < 7$ equals $7$, since $7$ has no divisor other than $1$ and $7$. So $\N \models \pi(7)$.
7. By steps 1 to 4 and the equality $\Delta_0 = \mathrm{LinH}$, the set $\mathrm{Pr}$ is in $\mathrm{LinH}$.

## Remarks

The formula has two universal bounded quantifiers, so it is a $\Pi$-type definition: the alternating machine guesses $u$ and $v$ in universal states.
The complement, the set of composite numbers and of $0$ and $1$, has the dual $\Sigma$-type definition.
That primality is in polynomial time is a much deeper result, the [[AKS primality test|XMHQCDWZW1]].
The $\Delta_0$ definition only gives membership in $\mathrm{LinH}$.
Paris, Wilkie and Woods showed that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \Omega_1$ proves that there are infinitely many primes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A prime number, like $7$, cannot be written as a product of two smaller numbers.
The number $15$ is not prime, since $15 = 3 \cdot 5$.
To test a number, it suffices to look at products of numbers smaller than it.
The search never goes past the number itself, so being prime is a bounded property.

### L2 human

We evaluate $\pi(9)$ step by step.

1. $1 < 9$, so the first conjunct holds.
2. The pairs $(u, v)$ with $u, v < 9$ include $(3, 3)$.
3. $3 \cdot 3 = 9$, so the second conjunct fails.
4. So $\pi(9)$ is false, and $9$ is not prime.

The typical use of such a formula is to state number-theoretic facts inside $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$, for example "there is a prime between $x$ and $2x$".
A trap is to write the bound as $u \le x$: then $u = 1$ and $v = x$ give $u \cdot v = x$, and no number satisfies the formula.
