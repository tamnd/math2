## Example

Let $U = \R(1, 0)$ and $W = \R(1, 2)$ in $\R^2$.
We check that $\R^2 = U \oplus W$.

1. $U$ and $W$ are [[subspaces|0EPTJA5QV4]], since each is the span of one vector.
2. Intersection. If $a(1, 0) = b(1, 2)$, then the second coordinates give $0 = 2b$, so $b = 0$, and then $a = 0$. So $U \cap W = \{0\}$.
3. By the [[criteria for two subspaces|~26635bdc]], the sum $U + W$ is [[direct|041JTZCK9Q]].
4. Sum. For $(x, y) \in \R^2$, we solve $(x, y) = a(1, 0) + b(1, 2)$. The second coordinate gives $b = y/2$, and the first gives $a = x - y/2$.
5. So each vector of $\R^2$ lies in $U + W$, and $\R^2 = U \oplus W$.
6. For $(3, 4)$: $b = 2$ and $a = 1$, so $(3, 4) = (1, 0) + (2, 4)$, with $(1, 0) \in U$ and $(2, 4) \in W$.

## Remarks

The two lines are not perpendicular, and no inner product is used.
Any two different lines through $0$ in $\R^2$ give a direct sum decomposition in the same way.
The map $(x, y) \mapsto (x - y/2, 0)$ that keeps the $U$ part is the [[projection|033DA1WE8Y]] onto $U$ along $W$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The horizontal line and the slanted line through $(1, 2)$ meet only at the origin.
Each point of the plane is reached in exactly one way by a step along the first line followed by a step along the second.

### L2 human

The computation in step 4 is a $2 \times 2$ linear system with the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
Its determinant is $2 \ne 0$, so the system has exactly one solution for each $(x, y)$.
This is existence and uniqueness of the decomposition at once.
