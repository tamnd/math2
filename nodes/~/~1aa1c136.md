## Statement

The set $\mathbb{R}$ of real numbers has [[cardinality|~ec7b0a04]] $2^{\aleph_0}$:
$$|\mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c} = |\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})|.$$

## Remarks

One proof uses the [[Schröder–Bernstein theorem|~5f17792e]]. A real number gives a set of rationals, the rationals less than it, so $|\mathbb{R}| \le |\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Q})| = 2^{\aleph_0}$. Binary sequences give an injection in the other direction, for example through decimal expansions with digits $0$ and $1$.
With [[Cantor's theorem|~54780446]], this gives another proof that $\mathbb{R}$ is [[uncountable|~5d49ab7f]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The real numbers and the subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ have the same size.
Roughly, a real number is an infinite sequence of digits, and an infinite sequence of $0$ and $1$ is a subset of $\mathbb{N}$.
