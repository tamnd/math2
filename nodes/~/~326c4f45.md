## Statement

Let $m$ and $n$ be distinct natural numbers.
Then the [[Fermat numbers|~04e5f905]] $F_m$ and $F_n$ are [[coprime|~8f403547]].

## Remarks

The proof uses $F_0 F_1 \cdots F_{n-1} = F_n - 2$. A common divisor of $F_m$ and $F_n$ with $m < n$ divides $2$. Fermat numbers are odd, so the divisor is $1$.
The result is often called Goldbach's theorem, after Christian Goldbach.
A consequence is a new proof that there are [[infinitely many primes|~9f34945a]]: each $F_n$ has a prime factor, and these primes are all distinct.

## Explanations

### L1 human

No two Fermat numbers share a prime factor.
For example, $F_2 = 17$ and $F_3 = 257$ are coprime, and $F_5 = 641 \cdot 6700417$ has no factor in common with $3$, $5$, $17$, $257$ or $65537$.
