## Example

Let $K$ be an [[algebraically closed field|0PF6TM8TWT]] whose [[transcendence degree|D7TR0YWR9H]] over its prime field is an infinite cardinal $\kappa$.
Then $\abs{K} = \kappa$, and $K$ is saturated.

1. Let $A \subseteq K$ with $\abs{A} < \kappa$, and let $k$ be the subfield generated by $A$. A transcendence basis of $k$ can be chosen inside $A$, so $\operatorname{trdeg} k \le \abs{A} < \kappa$.
2. By quantifier elimination for [[algebraically closed fields|MT8TDVPBXB]], the type of $b$ over $A$ is fixed by the polynomials over $k$ that vanish at $b$.
3. These polynomials form the zero ideal or the ideal of an irreducible $g \in k[x]$, as in [[the type space of algebraically closed fields|~4b7c25db]].
4. An algebraic type, with ideal generated by $g$, is realized by a root of $g$ in $K$, which exists because $K$ is algebraically closed.
5. The transcendental type over $A$ is realized by an element of $K$ transcendental over $k$, which exists because $\operatorname{trdeg} K = \kappa > \operatorname{trdeg} k$.
6. So every complete $1$-type over $A$ is realized, and $K$ is [[saturated|RMTZ5J64FX]].
7. Universality: a field $F \equiv K$ of size at most $\kappa$ has transcendence degree at most $\kappa$, so it embeds into $K$ by mapping a transcendence basis into one of $K$. Embeddings of algebraically closed fields are elementary, so $K$ is [[universal|~40536de0]].
8. Homogeneity: by [[uniqueness of saturated models|~a2cbf168]], part 1, with $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{N} = K$, the field $K$ is [[strongly homogeneous|~ee122c2b]].
9. Special: for uncountable $\kappa$, let $(b_\alpha)_{\alpha < \kappa}$ be a transcendence basis, and let $K_\mu$ be the algebraic closure in $K$ of the prime field with all $b_\alpha$, $\alpha < \mu^+$. For $\aleph_0 \le \mu < \kappa$, each $K_\mu$ is saturated of size $\mu^+$, and their union is $K$. So $K$ is [[special|~82e5b5a7]].

## Remarks

For example, $\C$ has transcendence degree $2^{\aleph_0}$ over $\Q$, so it is a saturated model of size $2^{\aleph_0}$.
For algebraically closed fields, saturation means that the transcendence degree equals the size.
The countable field $\overline{\Q}(t)^{\mathrm{alg}}$, of transcendence degree $1$, is not saturated: over $\set{t}$ it omits the type of an element transcendental over $\Q(t)$.
By [[Steinitz's theorem|CAEHMSX9RT]], algebraically closed fields of the same characteristic and transcendence degree are isomorphic, which gives the uniqueness of saturated models here without back-and-forth.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An algebraically closed field is saturated when it has as many independent transcendental numbers as it has elements.
Then for any smaller set of given numbers, the field still has a number that is transcendental over them, and a root of every polynomial built from them.
The complex numbers are such a field.

### L2 human

We check $\aleph_0$-saturation for the countable field $K$ of transcendence degree $\aleph_0$ over $\Q$.

1. A finite set $A$ generates a field $k$ of finite transcendence degree $d$.
2. A transcendence basis of $K$ is infinite, so some basis element is transcendental over $k$, and it realizes the transcendental type.
3. Roots of irreducible polynomials over $k$ exist in $K$ and realize the algebraic types.

The typical use is to have a concrete saturated model for computations with types.
The trap is to think that $\overline{\Q}$ is saturated because it is algebraically closed: it has transcendence degree $0$.
