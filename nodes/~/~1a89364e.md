## Statement

Let $(X, d)$ be a [[metric space|~200ea42b]] and let $K$ be a [[compact subset|~56dabfb9]] of $X$.
Then $K$ is [[bounded|~f6a5aaba]].

## Remarks

Proof: if $K$ is empty, it is bounded. Otherwise fix $x \in K$. The [[open balls|~43faf552]] $B(x, n)$ for $n \ge 1$ cover $K$, so finitely many of them do. The largest of these balls contains $K$.
A compact subset of a metric space is also [[closed|~dfeacff3]], because metric spaces are Hausdorff.
The converse fails in general: a closed bounded set need not be compact. With the metric $\min(|x - y|, 1)$, the real line is closed and bounded, but not compact.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A compact set can be covered by finitely many balls about one point, and then it fits in the largest of them.
