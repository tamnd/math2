## Statement

Let $a < b$ and let $u, v \colon [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be [[continuously differentiable|~29694c51]].
Then
$$\int_a^b u(x) v'(x) \, dx = u(b) v(b) - u(a) v(a) - \int_a^b u'(x) v(x) \, dx.$$

## Remarks

By the [[product rule|~65fb72b3]], $uv$ is an antiderivative of $u v' + u' v$. Apply the [[second fundamental theorem|~be640cd3]].
For indefinite integrals, the formula is $\int u \, dv = u v - \int v \, du$.
For example, $\int_0^1 x e^x \, dx = e - (e - 1) = 1$.
Mathlib states it as `intervalIntegral.integral_mul_deriv_eq_deriv_mul`.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Integration by parts is the product rule read backwards.
It moves the derivative from one factor to the other, at the cost of a boundary term.
