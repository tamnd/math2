## Proof

1. Assume that $A$ is [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]]. By the [[product rule|04CPJ6AF00]], $\det A \det(A^{-1}) = \det I_n$.
2. By the [[determinant of the identity|0Z4BWARC38]], $\det I_n = 1$. So $\det A \neq 0$.
3. Conversely, assume $\det A \neq 0$. By the [[adjugate identity|0528HKWZ1Z]], $A \operatorname{adj} A = \operatorname{adj} A \cdot A = (\det A) I_n$.
4. Put $B = (\det A)^{-1} \operatorname{adj} A$. Then step 3 gives $AB = BA = I_n$. So $A$ is invertible, with inverse $B$.
5. Steps 1 to 4 prove that conditions 1 and 2 are equivalent.
6. By the [[RREF criterion|1ZK55FS2BF]], $A$ is invertible if and only if $Ax = 0$ has only the solution $x = 0$.
7. Since $Ax = \sum_j x_j c_j$ for the columns $c_j$, this happens exactly when the columns are independent. So conditions 1 and 3 are equivalent.
8. Apply step 7 to $A^{T}$. With $\det A^{T} = \det A$ from the [[transpose rule|0ZZQ2E1MTE]], conditions 2 and 4 are equivalent.

## Idea

If $A$ has an inverse, the product rule turns $A A^{-1} = I_n$ into $\det A \det A^{-1} = 1$.
If $\det A$ is nonzero, the adjugate divided by $\det A$ is an explicit inverse.

## Variants

Steps 1 to 4 use no division except by $\det A$.
So they prove, over any commutative ring, that $A$ is invertible if and only if $\det A$ is a unit; this is the [[criterion over commutative rings|03Q7RE2HXR]].
The [[proof by row reduction|~7cf54c7d]] needs a field.
