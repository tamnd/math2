## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite [[group|G5S1M9]] of [[order|0B85S2V2DR]] $|G| \leq 5$.
Then $G$ is [[abelian|082GHNPJ53]].

## Remarks

The [[proof|~e3c89752]] treats each order from $1$ to $5$.
The orders $2$, $3$ and $5$ are prime, and the order $4$ needs a separate argument.

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The bound $5$ is the best possible.
The [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]] $S_3$ has order $6$, and [[it is not abelian|QSMVQ4EJ3D]].

### Consequences

The smallest order of a group that is not abelian is $6$.
So $S_3$ is a smallest group that is not abelian.
Up to [[isomorphism|08MG7H8JW5]], it is the only group of order $6$ that is not abelian, but this needs more work than the present statement.

### Converse

The converse is false.
For each $n \geq 1$, the group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is abelian and has order $n$, so there are abelian groups of each order.

### Generalizations

[[Each group of order $p^2$ is abelian|09JZ9P4899]], for each prime $p$.
Each [[group of prime order is cyclic|05MM4EK4SA]], and so abelian.
For $n \leq 20$, there is a group of order $n$ that is not abelian exactly when $n$ is $6$, $8$, $10$, $12$, $14$, $16$, $18$ or $20$.
For these even orders $n = 2m$ with $m \geq 3$, the [[dihedral group|0JZ27DMJ5D]] of order $2m$ is an example.
Each group of order $15$ is cyclic, and so abelian, although $15$ is neither a prime nor the square of a prime.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A group with at most five elements always has the property that the order of two elements in a product does not matter.
The first group where the order matters has six elements.
It is the group of the six ways to rearrange three objects.

### L2 human

We see how the proof treats a group $G$ of order $4$.

1. Each element $g$ has an order that divides $4$, so the order is $1$, $2$ or $4$.
2. If some $g$ has order $4$, the powers $e, g, g^2, g^3$ are four different elements, so they are all of $G$. Then $G$ is cyclic, and so abelian.
3. Otherwise each element has order $1$ or $2$, so $g^2 = e$ for each $g$, and $G$ is abelian by the result on exponent $2$.

Up to isomorphism, the two cases give the two groups of order $4$: the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ and the [[Klein four-group|DX6X1D21FE]].
A trap is to think that all small groups are cyclic: the Klein four-group is abelian but not cyclic.

### L3 human

The statement is the first step in the classification of groups of small order.
Up to isomorphism, the groups of order at most $7$ are the cyclic groups, the Klein four-group and $S_3$.
A positive integer $n$ such that each group of order $n$ is abelian is sometimes called an abelian number.
A known criterion says that $n = p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_k^{a_k}$ is such a number exactly when each $a_i \leq 2$ and no $p_i$ divides $p_j^{a_j} - 1$.
The statement checks the cases $n \leq 5$ by hand.
