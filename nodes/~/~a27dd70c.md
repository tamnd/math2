## Example

On $\Q^2$ let $B(x, y) = x^T A y$ with $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, so $B(x, y) = x_1 y_1 + 2x_1 y_2 + 3x_2 y_2$.
Take the new basis $b'_1 = (1, 1)$ and $b'_2 = (1, -1)$.
We find the matrix of $B$ in the new basis in two ways.

1. The [[change of basis matrix|0KWXYZPJKN]] has the columns $b'_1$ and $b'_2$, so $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$. Its determinant is $-2 \neq 0$.
2. First way, by the [[change of basis rule|0SPYAGE7T0]]: $AP = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ 3 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$, and $P^T (AP) = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -4 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. Second way, by the definition of the [[matrix of a form|0P24YW565F]]: $B(b'_1, b'_1) = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6$.
4. $B(b'_1, b'_2) = 1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot (-1) + 3 \cdot 1 \cdot (-1) = -4$.
5. $B(b'_2, b'_1) = 1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot (-1) \cdot 1 = 0$.
6. $B(b'_2, b'_2) = 1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot (-1) + 3 \cdot (-1) \cdot (-1) = 2$.
7. Steps 3 to 6 give the matrix $A' = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -4 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, the same as step 2.
8. So $A$ and $A'$ are [[congruent|WJWNYSQKK8]].
9. The ranks agree: both matrices have rank $2$.
10. The determinants are $\det A = 3$ and $\det A' = 12 = (-2)^2 \cdot 3 = (\det P)^2 \det A$.

## Remarks

The example shows that the rule $P^T A P$ gives the values of the form on the new basis.
The similarity rule gives $P^{-1} A P = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, which is a different matrix, and the wrong one for a form.
The determinant changed from $3$ to $12$, but the square class of $3$ in $\Q^\times / (\Q^\times)^2$ stayed the same.
If we scale the basis to $2b'_1, 2b'_2$, then $P$ doubles, and $A'$ is multiplied by $4$.
The form is not symmetric, and $A'$ is not symmetric either, because congruence keeps the asymmetry.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A form gives a number for each pair of vectors.
In a new basis we store the numbers for pairs of new basis vectors.
We can compute these four numbers one by one, or all at once with the product of three matrices.
Both ways give $6, -4, 0, 2$.

### L2 human

The general recipe is the following.

1. Put the new basis vectors, in old coordinates, as the columns of $P$.
2. Compute $P^T A P$.
3. Check one entry with the formula of the form, for example $B(b'_1, b'_1) = 6$.

The typical use is to choose $P$ so that the new matrix is simpler, for example diagonal or block diagonal.
A trap: the new matrix is not $P^{-1} A P$, and congruent matrices need not have the same eigenvalues.
Here $A$ has eigenvalues $1$ and $3$, and $A'$ has eigenvalues $6$ and $2$.
