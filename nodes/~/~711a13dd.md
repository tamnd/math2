## Definition

A [[topological space|~77bfcc55]] $X$ is connected when there are no [[open sets|~159c5927]] $U, V \subseteq X$ with $U \neq \varnothing$, $V \neq \varnothing$, $U \cap V = \varnothing$ and $U \cup V = X$.

## Remarks

Equivalently, the only subsets of $X$ that are both open and [[closed|~dfeacff3]] are $\varnothing$ and $X$.
Equivalently, each [[continuous map|~18b16c9f]] from $X$ to the discrete space $\{0, 1\}$ is constant.
The interval $[0, 1]$ is connected. The set $[0, 1] \cup [2, 3]$ is not connected.
Under this definition the empty space is connected. Some authors require a connected space to be nonempty.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A connected space cannot be split into two separate open parts.
Connectedness is the topological form of the idea that a space is in one piece.
