## Proof

Let $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$, and let $B$ be nondegenerate and symmetric on $V$, with $\dim V = n$.
For subspaces, $X \perp Y$ means $X \oplus Y$ with $B(X, Y) = 0$.

Part 1, by induction on $n$.

1. If $V$ is [[anisotropic|~0d389e21]], including the case $n = 0$, take $m = 0$ and $V_{\mathrm{an}} = V$.
2. Otherwise there is an isotropic vector $u$. By the [[hyperbolic plane statement|~c9505cce]], $u$ lies in a [[hyperbolic plane|~70f96526]] $H_1$, and $V = H_1 \oplus H_1^\perp$ with $B$ nondegenerate on $H_1^\perp$.
3. $B(H_1, H_1^\perp) = 0$ by the definition of $H_1^\perp$. So $V = H_1 \perp H_1^\perp$.
4. $\dim H_1^\perp = n - 2 < n$. By the induction hypothesis, $H_1^\perp = H_2 \perp \dots \perp H_m \perp V_{\mathrm{an}}$.
5. Steps 3 and 4 give $V = H_1 \perp H_2 \perp \dots \perp H_m \perp V_{\mathrm{an}}$.

Part 2.

6. Each hyperbolic plane has a basis with the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. So any two hyperbolic planes are isometric, and so are any two orthogonal sums of $k$ hyperbolic planes. Write $kH$ for such a sum.
7. Let $V \cong mH \perp A \cong m'H \perp A'$ with $A, A'$ anisotropic. By symmetry of the roles, we can assume $m \leq m'$.
8. Then $mH \perp A \cong mH \perp \bigl((m' - m)H \perp A'\bigr)$.
9. All these summands are nondegenerate. A hyperbolic plane has an invertible matrix, an anisotropic space has no nonzero radical vector, and orthogonal sums of nondegenerate spaces are nondegenerate.
10. By [[Witt cancellation|~caf8e8bf]], $A \cong (m' - m)H \perp A'$.
11. Suppose that $m' > m$. Then the right side contains an isotropic vector of a hyperbolic pair. Its image in $A$ under the isometry is isotropic. This contradicts that $A$ is anisotropic.
12. So $m = m'$, and step 10 gives $A \cong A'$.

Part 3.

13. Let $W$ be a totally isotropic subspace with basis $w_1, \dots, w_k$. Let $\varphi \colon V \to F^k$ be $\varphi(v) = (B(w_1, v), \dots, B(w_k, v))$.
14. $\ker \varphi = W^\perp$ has dimension $n - k$, by part 2 of the [[dimension statement for orthogonal complements|~fb9287d6]]. By the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]], $\varphi$ has rank $k$, so it is onto.
15. So there is $z_1$ with $B(w_1, z_1) = 1$ and $B(w_j, z_1) = 0$ for $j \geq 2$. Put $v_1 = z_1 - \frac{B(z_1, z_1)}{2} w_1$.
16. Then $B(w_1, v_1) = 1$, and $B(w_j, v_1) = 0$ for $j \geq 2$, since $B(w_j, w_1) = 0$. Also $B(v_1, v_1) = B(z_1, z_1) - B(z_1, z_1) B(w_1, z_1) = 0$.
17. So $P_1 = \operatorname{span}(w_1, v_1)$ is a hyperbolic plane, and $V = P_1 \perp V_1$ with $V_1 = P_1^\perp$ nondegenerate, by the hyperbolic plane statement.
18. By step 16, $w_2, \dots, w_k \in V_1$, and they span a totally isotropic subspace of $V_1$.
19. Induction on $k$ with steps 13 to 18 gives $V = P_1 \perp \dots \perp P_k \perp V'$, with $P_i$ hyperbolic, $w_i \in P_i$ and $V'$ nondegenerate.
20. Now let $W$ be maximal. Suppose that $V'$ has an isotropic vector $z$. Then $B(w_i, z) = 0$ for each $i$, since $z \in V'$, and $B(z, z) = 0$.
21. $z \notin W$, since the sum in step 19 is direct and $W \subseteq P_1 \perp \dots \perp P_k$. So $W + Fz$ is totally isotropic of dimension $k + 1$. This contradicts maximality.
22. So $V'$ is anisotropic, and step 19 is a decomposition as in part 1 with $k$ planes. By part 2, $k = m$.

Part 4.

23. $H_1 \perp \dots \perp H_m$ has dimension $2m$, and it lies in $V$. So $2m \leq n$.
24. If $m = 0$, then $V = V_{\mathrm{an}}$ is anisotropic. If $m \geq 1$, then $H_1$ contains an isotropic vector, so $V$ is not anisotropic. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Each isotropic vector has a partner, and the pair spans a hyperbolic plane that splits off.
Repeat until no isotropic vector is left.
Cancellation shows that the number of planes and the remaining anisotropic part do not depend on the choices.

## Variants

Part 3 also follows from [[Witt's theorem|CCB8VNQCV0]]: an isometry between two totally isotropic subspaces of the same dimension extends to $V$.
The proof here does not use Witt's theorem.
