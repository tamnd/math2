## Example

Consider the lower bound
$$x^2 + 1 \ge 0 \quad \text{for all real } x.$$
We show that the bound is true, but that the constant $0$ is not [[sharp|~07c20fd3]].

1. The bound holds. For each real $x$, $x^2 \ge 0$, so $x^2 + 1 \ge 1 > 0$.
2. The bound is not attained. Step 1 shows $x^2 + 1 \ge 1$, so no real $x$ gives $x^2 + 1 = 0$.
3. A larger lower bound holds. Step 1 also shows $x^2 + 1 \ge 1$ for every real $x$, and $1 > 0$.
4. The bound $1$ is sharp. At $x = 0$ we get $x^2 + 1 = 1$, so no constant $c > 1$ gives a true bound $x^2 + 1 \ge c$.

So $0$ is a true lower bound but not the best one, and the sharp lower bound is $1$, attained at $x = 0$.

## Remarks

For a lower bound, "sharp" means that no larger constant works.
The sharp lower bound is the infimum of $x^2 + 1$ over the real numbers, which is $1$.
Every constant $c \le 1$ gives a true bound $x^2 + 1 \ge c$, but only $c = 1$ is sharp.
A bound that is true but not sharp is still useful when the proof needs only a weak estimate, such as $x^2 + 1 \ne 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The number $x^2 + 1$ is never negative.
But it is also never smaller than $1$.
So "at least $0$" is true, and "at least $1$" is true and better.
The value $x = 0$ gives exactly $1$, so "at least $1$" is the best bound of this kind.

### L2 human

To test whether a lower bound $f(x) \ge c$ is sharp, we compute $\inf f$ and compare it with $c$.

1. Here $f(x) = x^2 + 1$, and $f(x) \ge 1$ with $f(0) = 1$, so $\inf f = 1$.
2. Since $c = 0 < 1 = \inf f$, the bound with $c = 0$ is not sharp.

A trap: a bound that is not attained can still be sharp, as $x/(1 + x) < 1$ for $x > 0$ shows.
Here the reason for "not sharp" is that a larger constant also works, not that the bound is not attained.
