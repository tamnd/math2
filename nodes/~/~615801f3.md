## Definition

Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be open, and let $\omega = \sum_I f_I \, dx_I$ be a [[differential k-form|~b3bc6449]] on $U$ whose coefficients $f_I$ are of class $C^1$.
The exterior derivative of $\omega$ is the $(k + 1)$-form
$$d\omega = \sum_I \sum_{j=1}^n \frac{\partial f_I}{\partial x_j} \, dx_j \wedge dx_I,$$
where $\wedge$ is the [[wedge product|~06990bf6]].

## Remarks

For a function $f$, $df = \sum_j \partial_j f \, dx_j$ is its differential, the [[one-form|~1da7fd80]] that corresponds to the [[gradient|~35460963]].
The operator $d$ is linear and satisfies $d(\alpha \wedge \beta) = d\alpha \wedge \beta + (-1)^k \alpha \wedge d\beta$ for a $k$-form $\alpha$. For $C^2$ forms, [[d d = 0|~16e93e5e]].
In the plane, $d(L \, dx + M \, dy) = (\partial_x M - \partial_y L) \, dx \wedge dy$.
In $\mathbb{R}^3$, $d(F_1 \, dx + F_2 \, dy + F_3 \, dz)$ has the components of the [[curl|~979a322d]] of $F$ as coefficients of $dy \wedge dz$, $dz \wedge dx$ and $dx \wedge dy$. Also $d(F_1 \, dy \wedge dz + F_2 \, dz \wedge dx + F_3 \, dx \wedge dy) = (\nabla \cdot F) \, dx \wedge dy \wedge dz$, which is the [[divergence|~7f42c53b]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The exterior derivative is one operation that does the work of gradient, curl and divergence together.
You differentiate each coefficient in each direction and attach the new direction with a wedge product.
