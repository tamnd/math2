## Example

We solve the system over $\R$
$$x_1 + 2x_2 + x_3 = 4, \qquad 2x_1 + 4x_2 + 3x_3 = 9, \qquad 3x_1 + 7x_2 + 4x_3 = 14$$
by [[Gaussian elimination|0JVJ2WCXZH]].

1. The augmented matrix is $\left[\begin{array}{rrr|r} 1 & 2 & 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 3 & 9 \\ 3 & 7 & 4 & 14 \end{array}\right]$, and the first [[pivot|0J0BBCMJ5W]] is the entry $1$ in row 1.
2. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$ gives $(2, 4, 3 \mid 9) - (2, 4, 2 \mid 8) = (0, 0, 1 \mid 1)$.
3. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 3R_1$ gives $(3, 7, 4 \mid 14) - (3, 6, 3 \mid 12) = (0, 1, 1 \mid 2)$.
4. The entry in row 2, column 2 is now $0$, so it cannot be a pivot.
5. Row 3 has the nonzero entry $1$ in column 2, so we swap $R_2$ and $R_3$.
6. The result is $\left[\begin{array}{rrr|r} 1 & 2 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \end{array}\right]$, which is in echelon form with pivots in columns 1, 2 and 3.
7. [[Back substitution|0ATFVE08MW]]: $x_3 = 1$, then $x_2 = 2 - 1 = 1$, then $x_1 = 4 - 2 - 1 = 1$.
8. Check: $1 + 2 + 1 = 4$, $2 + 4 + 3 = 9$ and $3 + 7 + 4 = 14$.

So the unique solution is $(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (1, 1, 1)$.

## Remarks

The swap is one of the three [[elementary row operations|04XWH28452]], so it does not change the solution set.
A zero in a pivot place does not mean that the system is singular; here the matrix has three pivots and is invertible.
The matrix has no LU decomposition without the swap, for the same reason as in [[the counterexample on LU without row exchanges|~809067cc]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Sometimes, after we remove $x_1$, the second equation has no $x_2$ left.
Then we cannot use it to remove $x_2$ from the others.
We change the order of the equations instead, and we continue.

### L2 human

1. Do the eliminations in column 1 as usual.
2. Look at the place where the next pivot should be.
3. If the entry there is $0$, look lower in the same column for a nonzero entry.
4. Swap the two rows and continue the elimination.
5. If every entry in that column, from the current row down, is $0$, move to the next column; that column is a free column.

The typical use is in hand computation and in computer programs, which must test each pivot before they divide by it.
A trap: swapping with a row above the current row breaks the echelon form that is already built.
