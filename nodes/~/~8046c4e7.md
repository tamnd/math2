## Proof

We find the eigenvectors one after another as maximizers of $q(v) = \ip{Tv}{v}$ on smaller and smaller unit spheres.
Steps 1 to 10 prove the direction "only if" of part 1, and part 3.
Steps 11 to 13 prove the rest.

1. Let $V$ be an inner product space of dimension $n \geq 1$ over $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. Let $T \colon V \to V$ be [[self-adjoint|0MB8HQW495]] for the [[inner product|0VGSYBWHV2]] $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$.
2. We construct vectors $u_1, \dots, u_n$ and real numbers $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$, one at a time. Let $1 \leq k \leq n$. Assume that $u_1, \dots, u_{k-1}$ are orthonormal and that $T u_j = \lambda_j u_j$ for $j < k$.
3. Let $W_k$ be the [[orthogonal complement|079D68S3AW]] of the span of $u_1, \dots, u_{k-1}$. For $k = 1$, the span is $\set{0}$ and $W_1 = V$.
4. Orthonormal vectors are [[linearly independent|0JWM6GJNQW]], so the span has dimension $k - 1$. By [[the dimension of the orthogonal complement|0E2NYPVSP9]], $\dim W_k = n - k + 1 \geq 1$.
5. Let $w \in W_k$ and $j < k$. Self-adjointness gives $\ip{Tw}{u_j} = \ip{w}{T u_j} = \overline{\lambda_j} \ip{w}{u_j} = 0$. So $T w \in W_k$, and $W_k$ is invariant under $T$.
6. Let $S_k \colon W_k \to W_k$ be the restriction of $T$. For $w, w' \in W_k$, $\ip{S_k w}{w'} = \ip{w}{S_k w'}$, because $T$ is self-adjoint. So $S_k$ is self-adjoint on $W_k$.
7. Apply part 2 of [[the theorem on real eigenvalues|0C4NT4GT3X]] to $S_k$. The function $q$ attains a maximum $\lambda_k$ on the unit sphere of $W_k$, at a vector $u_k$.
8. The same theorem gives $T u_k = S_k u_k = \lambda_k u_k$. Here $\lambda_k = q(u_k)$, and $\lambda_k$ is real by part 1 of that theorem.
9. The vector $u_k$ is a unit vector in $W_k$, so it is orthogonal to $u_1, \dots, u_{k-1}$. So $u_1, \dots, u_k$ are orthonormal, and the assumption of step 2 holds for $k + 1$.
10. After $n$ steps, $u_1, \dots, u_n$ are $n$ orthonormal [[eigenvectors|0B5H4208Z9]] of $T$. They are linearly independent, so they form [[a basis|096V7H13H9]] of $V$. This is an [[orthonormal basis|0FWJ0VW561]] of eigenvectors with real eigenvalues.
11. Order of the eigenvalues. $W_{k+1}$ is a subspace of $W_k$, so its unit sphere is a subset of the unit sphere of $W_k$. A maximum over a smaller set is not larger. So $\lambda_1 \geq \lambda_2 \geq \dots \geq \lambda_n$.
12. Direction "if" of part 1. Let $V$ be real with an orthonormal eigenbasis. The matrix $D$ of $T$ in this basis is real diagonal. By [[the matrix of the adjoint|0DBTE6HVBD]], $T^*$ has the matrix $D^T = D$, so $T^* = T$.
13. Part 2. For a real [[symmetric|0N28QN4CYA]] $A$, the map $x \mapsto Ax$ is self-adjoint on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\ip{x}{y} = y^T x$. The orthonormal eigenvectors of step 10 are the columns of an [[orthogonal matrix|0QRBVFJ50X]] $Q$, and $AQ = QD$ gives $A = Q D Q^T$. Conversely, $(Q D Q^T)^T = Q D Q^T$.

## Idea

The largest value of $\ip{Tv}{v}$ on the unit sphere is an eigenvalue, and a maximizer is an eigenvector.
Remove this direction: its orthogonal complement is invariant, so we maximize again on the smaller sphere.
Each step gives one more orthonormal eigenvector, and the maxima decrease.

## Variants

- The proof gives more than the theorem. $\lambda_1$ is the maximum of $q$ on the whole unit sphere, so it is the largest eigenvalue. This is [[the extreme value statement for the Rayleigh quotient|~5c6d5f8a]].
- Step 7 can use Lagrange multipliers. A maximizer of $\ip{Tv}{v}$ under $\ip{v}{v} = 1$ satisfies $Tv = \lambda v$ for a multiplier $\lambda$, as in the Wikipedia article on the Rayleigh quotient.
- [[The induction proof|A81KPS16D7]] uses one maximum only and then applies induction to the orthogonal complement.
