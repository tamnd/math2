## Definition

A [[metric space|~200ea42b]] $(X, d)$ is proper when each [[closed ball|~ec6373a9]] $\bar{B}(x, r)$, with $x \in X$ and $r \ge 0$, is [[compact|~56dabfb9]].

## Remarks

In a proper metric space, a subset is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded. Thus the [[Heine–Borel theorem|~207cb5fd]] says that $\mathbb{R}^n$ is proper.
Each proper metric space is complete and [[locally compact|~0a18c57e]].
An infinite set with the [[discrete metric|~2a335c39]] is not proper: the closed ball of radius $1$ is the whole set, which is not compact.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A proper metric space is a space where the Heine–Borel test for compactness works: closed and bounded is enough.
Euclidean spaces are proper, but many infinite-dimensional spaces are not.
