## Example

Let $P_k$ be the space of real polynomials of degree at most $k$.
Let $D \colon P_2 \to P_1$ be the derivative, $D(p) = p'$.
Take the bases $B = (1, x, x^2)$ of $P_2$ and $C = (1, x)$ of $P_1$.

1. $D(1) = 0 = 0 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot x$. So column 1 of the matrix is $(0, 0)$.
2. $D(x) = 1 = 1 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot x$. So column 2 is $(1, 0)$.
3. $D(x^2) = 2x = 0 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot x$. So column 3 is $(0, 2)$.
4. So the [[matrix of $D$|0YYMY95FWS]] is the $2 \times 3$ matrix
$$[D]_{C,B} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
5. Take $p = 3 + 5x - 4x^2$. Its [[coordinate vector|0BMVBHTWV2]] is $[p]_B = (3, 5, -4)$.
6. The product is $[D]_{C,B}(3, 5, -4) = (0 \cdot 3 + 1 \cdot 5 + 0 \cdot (-4),\ 0 \cdot 3 + 0 \cdot 5 + 2 \cdot (-4)) = (5, -8)$.
7. Directly, $p' = 5 - 8x$, and $[p']_C = (5, -8)$. This agrees with step 6.

## Remarks

- The matrix has 2 rows because $\dim P_1 = 2$, and 3 columns because $\dim P_2 = 3$.
- With the order $C = (x, 1)$, the two rows change places: the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
- The first column is zero because the constants are in the kernel of $D$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Differentiate each basis polynomial and write the coefficients of the answer as a column.
Then multiplying the matrix by the coefficients of any $p$ gives the coefficients of $p'$.

### L2 human

The example shows that the matrix of a map depends on the ordered bases.
Steps 5 to 7 check the formula $[D(p)]_C = [D]_{C,B}[p]_B$ on one polynomial.
Differentiation of polynomials becomes the multiplication of a small matrix by a vector of coefficients.
