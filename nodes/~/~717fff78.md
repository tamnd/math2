## Summary

A proof by contrapositive proves an [[implication|~06eaf3d7]] $p \to q$ by a [[direct proof|~b901f440]] of $\neg q \to \neg p$.
The method is correct because $p \to q$ is logically equivalent to its [[contrapositive|~3587811f]].

## Remarks

Example: for an integer $n$, if $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even. The contrapositive is "if $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd", which has a short direct proof.
A [[proof by contradiction|~05010c83]] is different: it assumes $p$ and $\neg q$ together, and it gets any contradiction.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Sometimes the negation of the conclusion gives you more to work with.
"If $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even" is hard to start.
"If $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd" is easy, and it says the same thing.
