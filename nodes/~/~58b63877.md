## Summary

$\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is the subsystem of [[second-order arithmetic|DC60FYCSPM]] with the basic axioms, induction for $\Sigma^0_1$ formulas, and comprehension for $\Delta^0_1$ formulas, as in its [[definition|~59f891e2]].
Its sets are, in the $\omega$-models, closed under [[Turing reducibility|PA4PYQ1PZ0]] and join, and its least $\omega$-model consists of the [[computable sets|PPGD5KW8JC]].
It is the base theory of [[reverse mathematics|7SQS69T17R]]: it formalizes computable mathematics, and the other systems are measured over it.

## Motivation

A base theory must be strong enough to code reals, functions and countable structures, and to prove their simple properties.
It must also be weak enough that the theorems under study are not already provable.
$\Delta^0_1$ comprehension gives exactly the sets that are computable from given sets.
$\Sigma^0_1$ induction proves that a function defined by primitive recursion from existing functions exists.

## Notation

The name abbreviates "recursive comprehension axiom", and the subscript $0$ marks the restriction of induction.
Some texts write $\mathsf{RCA}$ for the system with the full induction scheme.

## Remarks

### Examples

- The [[computable sets|~f2c0a555]] form the least $\omega$-model of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
- The [[sets computable from the halting set|~b9dd8ce0]] form an $\omega$-model of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
- $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves the [[infinite pigeonhole principle for two colors|~03683221]].
- $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves the [[intermediate value theorem|~cffd2663]].
- The real number $\sqrt{2}$ has a [[code that exists in RCA0|~aa7e8c21]].

### Non-examples

- The [[computable sets together with the halting set|~346d41cf]] do not form a model of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, since the complement of the halting set is missing.
- The [[computable sets|~a21f55cb]] do not form a model of full $\mathsf{Z}_2$: $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is a proper subsystem.

### Basic properties

- The $\omega$-models of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ are exactly the [[Turing ideals|~d06d0faa]].
- $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves that every function defined by primitive recursion from given functions exists.
- The first-order part of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$: the arithmetic sentences that $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves are exactly those that $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ proves.
- $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves the intermediate value theorem, the Baire category theorem for complete separable metric spaces, and the existence of the algebraic closure of a countable field.
- Weak König's lemma is not provable in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, since it fails in the $\omega$-model of the computable sets.

### Continuous functions

A continuous function $f \colon \R \to \R$ is coded by a set $\Phi$ of quintuples $(n, a, r, b, s)$ with $a, b$ rational and $r, s$ positive rational.
Write $(a, r) \Phi (b, s)$ for $\exists n\, (n, a, r, b, s) \in \Phi$; it says that $f$ maps the open interval of center $a$ and radius $r$ into the closed interval of center $b$ and radius $s$.
The code must satisfy these conditions:

1. if $(a, r) \Phi (b, s)$ and $(a, r) \Phi (b', s')$, then $\abs{b - b'} \le s + s'$;
2. if $(a, r) \Phi (b, s)$ and $\abs{a' - a} + r' < r$, then $(a', r') \Phi (b, s)$;
3. if $(a, r) \Phi (b, s)$ and $\abs{b - b'} + s < s'$, then $(a, r) \Phi (b', s')$.

A real $x$ is in the domain of $f$ when for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $(a, r) \Phi (b, s)$ with $\abs{x - a} < r$ and $s < \varepsilon$.
Then $f(x)$ is the unique real $y$ with $\abs{y - b} \le s$ for all $(a, r) \Phi (b, s)$ with $\abs{x - a} < r$.

### History

Harvey Friedman introduced the base theory in the 1970s.
The present form, with $\Sigma^0_1$ induction, became the standard base theory of reverse mathematics.

### Common errors

- $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ does not prove $\Sigma^0_1$ comprehension. The range of a function, $\set{n : \exists k\, f(k) = n}$, need not exist.
- The subscript $0$ does not mean "without induction": $\Sigma^0_1$ induction is an axiom.
- A proof in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ may use classical logic. The law of excluded middle is valid, so a proof can split into cases that a computer cannot decide.

## Explanations

### L1 human

$\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is the theory of computable mathematics.
It allows a set to exist when a computer, given the sets that exist already, can decide which numbers belong to it.
It allows induction for statements of the form "some number has a checkable property".

### L2 human

In $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ one can do the usual constructions of computable analysis and computable algebra.
For example, the bisection method finds a zero of a continuous function, and it works in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ after a case split.
A typical use is as the background theory: each reverse mathematics result is a theorem of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ of the form "$\tau \leftrightarrow$ axiom".
A trap is to build a set with an unbounded existential quantifier, such as the set of nodes of a tree with an extension of every length.

### L3 human

$\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is $\Pi^0_2$-conservative over primitive recursive arithmetic, so its provably total functions are the primitive recursive functions.
Its models with non-standard first-order part can fail $\Sigma^0_2$ induction, and much work studies which combinatorial principles imply induction or bounding schemes over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
The weaker system $\mathsf{RCA}^*_0$ replaces $\Sigma^0_1$ induction by $\Sigma^0_0$ induction and adds the totality of exponentiation.
