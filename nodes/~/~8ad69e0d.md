## Definition

Let $ABC$ be a triangle in the Euclidean plane $\mathbb{R}^2$, with [[incentre|~c3673c9d]] $I$ and inradius $r$. Let $d$ be the Euclidean distance.
The incircle of $ABC$ is the [[circle|~452d68a3]] with the centre $I$ and the radius $r$:
$$\{\, X \in \mathbb{R}^2 \;:\; d(X, I) = r \,\}.$$

## Remarks

The incircle touches each side of the triangle at exactly one point, the foot of the perpendicular from $I$ to that side.
Let $T$ be the area of $ABC$ and $s$ half of its perimeter. Then $T = r s$, so $r = T / s$.
An excircle is a circle outside the triangle that touches one side and the extensions of the other two sides. Each triangle has three excircles.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The incircle is the biggest circle that fits inside a triangle. It touches each side once. Cut the triangle into three pieces from $I$ to the corners: each piece has the height $r$, and this gives $T = r s$.
