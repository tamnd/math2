## Summary

Information theory is the mathematical theory of information in the sense of Shannon, with the measures of information and the limits on the compression and the transmission of data.
The entropy of a discrete random variable is the expected value of the negative logarithm of its probability mass function, and it measures the average uncertainty of the variable.
The coding theorems give the least rate at which a source can be compressed and the greatest rate at which data can pass reliably through a noisy channel, and these limits guide the design of modern communication systems.
Shannon founded the subject in 1948 with his paper A Mathematical Theory of Communication.
This topic collects the information theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Coding theory, mathematical cryptography and statistical inference have their own topics.
