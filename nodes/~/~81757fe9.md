## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a vector space over $F$ of finite dimension $n \ge 1$, and let $T \colon V \to V$ be linear.
Write its [[minimal polynomial|03NDVCSHSB]] as
$$m_T = p_1^{e_1} \cdots p_k^{e_k},$$
where $p_1, \dots, p_k$ are pairwise distinct monic [[irreducible polynomials|03FKX7Z5DC]] over $F$ and each $e_i \ge 1$.
Let $W_i = \ker p_i(T)^{e_i}$.

1. $V = W_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus W_k$, a [[direct sum|0XYYT5KNTX]].
2. Each $W_i$ is a nonzero $T$-[[invariant subspace|0ACX5QT8TE]].
3. The minimal polynomial of the [[restriction|0KN7EPH7Z0]] $T|_{W_i}$ is $p_i^{e_i}$.
4. The projection of $V$ onto $W_i$ along the other summands is a polynomial in $T$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The exponents must be the full exponents $e_i$. For the [[shear|~58e14c85]], $m = (t - 1)^2$, and $\ker(S - I)$ is only a line, while $\ker(S - I)^2 = F^2$.
- The factors must be grouped by distinct irreducible polynomials. If the same factor is split as $p \cdot p$, the two kernels $\ker p(T)$ coincide, and their sum is not direct.
- Finite dimension ensures that the minimal polynomial exists. The derivative on $\R[x]$ satisfies no nonzero polynomial equation, but the [[kernels lemma|~4b2aa8fd]] still applies to each polynomial that kills a given subspace.

### Consequences

- In a basis adapted to the decomposition, the matrix of $T$ is [[block diagonal|001T5FJDC1]], with one block for each $p_i$.
- If $m_T$ splits, with $p_i = t - \lambda_i$, the $W_i$ are the [[generalized eigenspaces|0AJ035ZTZE]], and this is the [[decomposition into generalized eigenspaces|0Z3GDY89T5]].
- If all $p_i$ are linear and all $e_i = 1$, the $W_i$ are eigenspaces, and $T$ is diagonalizable, as in the [[minimal polynomial criterion|0MPAJRB23T]].
- Every map that commutes with $T$ preserves each $W_i$, since the projections are polynomials in $T$.

### Converse

Part 1 has a partial converse.
If $V = U_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus U_k$ with invariant $U_i$ and the minimal polynomials of the $T|_{U_i}$ are pairwise coprime, then $m_T$ is their product, and the $U_i$ are the kernels of these factors evaluated at $T$.

### Generalizations

For a finitely generated torsion module over a principal ideal domain, the same argument splits the module into its primary parts, the first step of the [[structure theorem|TXMV0YDACG]].
Here $V$ is a torsion module over $F[t]$, with $t$ acting as $T$.
Each $W_i$ splits further into cyclic pieces, which gives the rational canonical form and, when $p_i$ is linear, the [[Jordan normal form|085HQ5G5TE]].

## History

The name "primary decomposition theorem" for this result is used by Hoffman and Kunze, Section 6.8.
It is the linear algebra case of the decomposition of a torsion module over a principal ideal domain into primary components.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The minimal polynomial of a matrix breaks into prime pieces, like a number breaks into prime factors.
Each prime piece cuts out its own part of the space.
The whole space is the sum of these parts, and the matrix acts on each part separately.

### L2 human

Let $B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\R$.

1. Expanding along the third row, $p_B = (t - 2)(t^2 + 1)$. The factors are distinct and irreducible over $\R$, so $m_B = p_B$.
2. $B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, so $B^2 + I = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$ and $W_1 = \ker(B^2 + I) = \operatorname{span}(e_1, e_2)$.
3. $W_2 = \ker(B - 2I)$ is spanned by $(1, 3, 5)$, since $B(1, 3, 5) = (2, 6, 10)$.
4. Bézout: $t^2 + 1 = (t - 2)(t + 2) + 5$, so $1 = \tfrac{1}{5}(t^2 + 1) - \tfrac{1}{5}(t + 2)(t - 2)$.
5. The projection onto $W_2$ is $\tfrac{1}{5}(B^2 + I)$, with image spanned by $(1, 3, 5)$. The projection onto $W_1$ is $I - \tfrac{1}{5}(B^2 + I) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -\frac{1}{5} \\ 0 & 1 & -\frac{3}{5} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
6. On $W_1$, $B$ acts as the rotation $e_1 \mapsto e_2$, $e_2 \mapsto -e_1$, with minimal polynomial $t^2 + 1$. On $W_2$, $B$ acts as $2$.

The typical use is to reduce a question about $T$ to the pieces $T|_{W_i}$, each with a single prime factor.
The trap is to look for eigenvectors in $W_1$: over $\R$, the piece for $t^2 + 1$ has none.

### L3 human

The decomposition is functorial: a map $S$ with $S T = T S$ preserves each $W_i$.
The idempotents giving the projections are the images in $F[t]/(m_T)$ of the Chinese remainder idempotents of $F[t]/(p_1^{e_1}) \times \cdots \times F[t]/(p_k^{e_k})$.
For a semisimple $T$, all $e_i = 1$, and each $W_i$ is a vector space over the field $F[t]/(p_i)$.
