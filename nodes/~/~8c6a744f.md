## Example

Let $\mathbb{Z}$ be the set of [[integers|0C4JS9RF5M]], with the usual multiplication.
We check the group axioms and find the one that fails.

1. Closure holds: the product of two integers is an integer.
2. Associativity holds: $(ab)c = a(bc)$ for all integers $a, b, c$.
3. An identity exists: $1 \cdot a = a = a \cdot 1$ for each integer $a$.
4. Inverses fail. An [[inverse|0ZNZH0EXFX]] of $2$ would be an integer $m$ with $2m = 1$. If $m = 0$, then $2m = 0$. If $m \neq 0$, then $|m| \geq 1$, so $|2m| \geq 2$. In both cases $2m \neq 1$.

Thus $2$ has no inverse, and $(\mathbb{Z}, \cdot)$ is not a [[group|G5S1M9]].
It is a commutative [[monoid|0N6R902FG1]].

## Remarks

The same argument shows that each integer $a$ with $|a| \geq 2$ has no inverse, and $0$ has no inverse, because $0 \cdot m = 0$.
The only integers with an inverse are $1$ and $-1$, and the set $\{1, -1\}$ is a group under multiplication.
These two numbers are the [[units|0XVEZDE9J8]] of the ring $\mathbb{Z}$.
When we change $\mathbb{Z}$ to $\mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0\}$, each element has an inverse, and we get [[a group|~0d64c0a7]].
The integers with addition are a group, the [[additive group of integers|0BSCJA5G53]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

With multiplication of whole numbers, multiplying by $1$ changes nothing.
To undo multiplication by $2$, we would need a whole number that gives $1$ when we multiply it by $2$.
There is no such whole number: $2$ times a whole number is $0$, or it is at least $2$ in size.
So multiplication by $2$ cannot be undone, and the whole numbers with multiplication are not a group.

### L2 human

We decide which integers have an inverse for multiplication.

1. Let $a$ be an integer with an inverse $m$, so $am = 1$.
2. Then $|a| \cdot |m| = 1$, and both $|a|$ and $|m|$ are natural numbers.
3. A product of two natural numbers is $1$ only when both are $1$. So $|a| = 1$.
4. Thus $a = 1$ or $a = -1$, and indeed $1 \cdot 1 = 1$ and $(-1)(-1) = 1$.

The typical use of this non-example is to show that the inverse axiom is a real condition: associativity and an identity are not enough.
A trap is to answer that the inverse of $2$ is $\tfrac{1}{2}$; that number is not an integer, and the inverse must lie in the set.
