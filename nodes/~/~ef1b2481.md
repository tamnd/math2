## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, with $\det A = -3$.

1. The cofactor matrix is $C = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & -3 \\ 4 & -11 & 6 \\ -3 & 6 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$; see the [[cofactor example|~9bb9fd5d]].
2. By the [[definition of the adjugate|0GMPYM0NSR]], $\operatorname{adj} A = C^{T} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & -3 \\ 2 & -11 & 6 \\ -3 & 6 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. Row $1$ of $A$ times the columns of $\operatorname{adj} A$: $2 + 4 - 9 = -3$, $4 - 22 + 18 = 0$, $-3 + 12 - 9 = 0$.
4. Row $2$: $8 + 10 - 18 = 0$, $16 - 55 + 36 = -3$, $-12 + 30 - 18 = 0$.
5. Row $3$: $14 + 16 - 30 = 0$, $28 - 88 + 60 = 0$, $-21 + 48 - 30 = -3$.
6. So $A \operatorname{adj} A = -3 I_3$, as the [[adjugate identity|0528HKWZ1Z]] predicts.
7. By the [[inverse via the adjugate|0ENVM493VK]], $A^{-1} = -\tfrac{1}{3} \operatorname{adj} A = \begin{pmatrix} -2/3 & -4/3 & 1 \\ -2/3 & 11/3 & -2 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

## Remarks

Steps 3 to 5 are the check by a second method: they compute the product entry by entry.
The inverse has denominators $3$ because $\det A = -3$; over $\mathbb{Z}$ the matrix $A$ is not invertible.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The adjugate is built from the nine cofactors, flipped across the diagonal.
Multiplying $A$ by it gives $-3$ times the identity, so dividing by $-3$ gives the inverse.

### L2 human

The product $A \operatorname{adj} A$ is the best check of an adjugate: every entry off the diagonal must be $0$.
A trap: with $C$ in place of $C^{T}$ the check fails; see the [[non-example|~53fcebc0]].
