## Example

Problem: decide which number is larger, $2^{100}$ or $10^{30}$, and estimate their ratio.
We use a [[heuristic|~fd7a5df5]] estimate first and then an exact argument.

1. Heuristic. $2^{10} = 1024$, which is close to $1000 = 10^3$.
2. Raise both sides to the power $10$: $2^{100} = (2^{10})^{10} \approx (10^3)^{10} = 10^{30}$.
3. The heuristic says that the two numbers are close, but it does not say which one is larger. The rounding in step 1 lost the information.
4. Exact argument. $2^{10} = 1024 > 1000 = 10^3$, and both sides are positive.
5. For positive numbers, $a > b$ implies $a^{10} > b^{10}$. So $2^{100} > 10^{30}$.
6. Estimate the ratio. $\frac{2^{100}}{10^{30}} = \left(\frac{1024}{1000}\right)^{10} = 1.024^{10}$.
7. Bound it. By the binomial theorem, $1.024^{10} \ge 1 + 10 \cdot 0.024 = 1.24$. A direct computation gives $1.024^{10} \approx 1.268$.

So $2^{100}$ is larger, by a factor of about $1.27$.

## Remarks

The heuristic of step 2 gave the right order of size, $10^{30}$, at once.
It could not decide the comparison, because the two numbers differ by less than the error of the rounding.
The exact argument of steps 4 and 5 uses the same idea, $2^{10}$ against $10^3$, but keeps the direction of the inequality.
This is the usual relation between a heuristic and a proof: the heuristic finds the idea, and the proof keeps track of what the heuristic ignored.

With $2^{1000}$ against $10^{300}$, the ratio is $1.024^{100}$, which is about $10.7$, so the rounding error grows with the exponent.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The number $2^{10}$ is $1024$, which is a little more than $1000$.
So ten factors of $2^{10}$ give a little more than ten factors of $1000$.
That means $2^{100}$ is a little more than a $1$ followed by $30$ zeros.
The rough idea "$1024$ is about $1000$" told us the size, and the exact fact "$1024$ is more than $1000$" told us which number is bigger.

### L2 human

We compare $3^{20}$ with $10^{9}$ in the same way.

1. Heuristic: $3^2 = 9 \approx 10$, so $3^{20} = 9^{10} \approx 10^{10}$.
2. This suggests that $3^{20}$ is larger than $10^9$ by a factor of about $10$.
3. Exact: $3^{20} = 3486784401$, which is about $3.49 \cdot 10^9$.
4. So $3^{20} > 10^9$, but the factor is about $3.5$, not $10$.

The typical use: a rounded estimate gives the order of size quickly.

A trap: rounding $9$ up to $10$ in step 1 overestimates by a factor $\left(\frac{10}{9}\right)^{10} \approx 2.87$, so a rough estimate can be wrong by a large factor when the rounding is repeated many times.
