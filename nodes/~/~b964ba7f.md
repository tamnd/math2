## Example

Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 & 1 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 & 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$
over $\R$, with columns $a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4$ and rows $\rho_1, \rho_2, \rho_3$.

1. Pivots. The [[reduced row echelon form|0NNR68BFRY]] has the rows $(1, 2, 0, 1)$, $(0, 0, 1, 2)$, $(0, 0, 0, 0)$, by the [[column space example|~e2144491]]. It has two [[pivots|0BCMGDHZGR]], so $\rank A = 2$.
2. Columns. $a_2 = (2, 4, 6) = 2a_1$. Also $a_1 + 2a_3 = (1, 2, 3) + (0, 2, 2) = (1, 4, 5) = a_4$.
3. The columns $a_1 = (1, 2, 3)$ and $a_3 = (0, 1, 1)$ are not multiples of each other. So the column space has the basis $a_1, a_3$, and [[its dimension|0EWQEYRVA8]] is $2$.
4. Rows. $\rho_1 + \rho_2 = (3, 6, 1, 5) = \rho_3$. The rows $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ are not proportional, since $\rho_1$ has entry $0$ in position 3 and $\rho_2$ has entry $1$. So the row space has dimension $2$.
5. Minors. The $2 \times 2$ [[minor|0SD528P0EK]] on rows 1, 2 and columns 1, 3 is $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 1 \ne 0$.
6. Each $3 \times 3$ minor uses three of the four columns. Any three columns are dependent, by step 2 and $a_4 = \tfrac{1}{2} a_2 + 2a_3$. So all four $3 \times 3$ minors are $0$.
7. So the largest nonzero minor has order $2$. All four methods give $\rank A = 2$.

## Remarks

- Steps 3 and 4 give the same number, as [[row rank equals column rank|0TZAG60ZYT]] says.
- The rank $2$ is less than $\min(3, 4) = 3$, so $A$ does not have full rank.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rank of this matrix is $2$.
Two columns are combinations of the other two, and the third row is the sum of the first two.

### L2 human

Each method finds the same number from a different point of view.
Pivots count independent columns after row reduction.
Minors count the size of the largest invertible square submatrix.
