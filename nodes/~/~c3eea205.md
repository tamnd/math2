## Proof

1. Let $c_1, \dots, c_r$ be a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $\operatorname{Col}(A)$, which has dimension $r$ by the [[definition of rank|0EWQEYRVA8]]. Let $C$ be the $m \times r$ matrix with these columns.
2. $\operatorname{Col}(C)$ is the span of the basis, so $\operatorname{Col}(C) = \operatorname{Col}(A)$ and $\rank C = r$.
3. Each column $a_k$ of $A$ lies in $\operatorname{Col}(A)$. So $a_k = \sum_{l=1}^r \rho_{lk} c_l$ for unique scalars $\rho_{lk}$. Let $R = (\rho_{lk})$, an $r \times n$ matrix.
4. By the definition of the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]], column $k$ of $CR$ is $\sum_l \rho_{lk} c_l = a_k$. So $A = CR$.
5. The map $y \mapsto Cy$ from $F^r$ to $F^m$ is injective, since $Cy = \sum_l y_l c_l = 0$ forces $y = 0$ by independence.
6. Column $k$ of $A$ is $C$ times column $k$ of $R$. So $y \mapsto Cy$ maps $\operatorname{Col}(R)$ onto $\operatorname{Col}(A)$.
7. By steps 5 and 6 and the [[rank-nullity theorem for linear maps|0W4FSW1TVB]] for this injective map on $\operatorname{Col}(R)$, $\dim \operatorname{Col}(R) = \dim \operatorname{Col}(A) = r$. So $\rank R = r$. This proves part 1.
8. For part 2, let $p_1 < \dots < p_r$ be the pivot columns, and let $R_0$ be the reduced row echelon form of $A$. By the [[theorem on the column space|11CSKXQNG3]], $a_{p_1}, \dots, a_{p_r}$ is a basis of $\operatorname{Col}(A)$.
9. In the [[reduced form|0NNR68BFRY]], the pivot column $p_i$ is the unit vector $e_i$, and the last $m - r$ rows are zero. So column $k$ of $R_0$ is $\sum_{i=1}^r (R_0)_{ik} e_i$, that is, $\sum_i (R_0)_{ik} \cdot (\text{column } p_i \text{ of } R_0)$.
10. By the [[theorem on row equivalence|E4GKK727ZM]], $A$ and $R_0$ have the same null space. So they satisfy the same linear relations among their columns.
11. By steps 9 and 10, $a_k = \sum_{i=1}^r (R_0)_{ik} a_{p_i}$. So in step 3, with $c_i = a_{p_i}$, the matrix $R$ is the first $r$ rows of $R_0$. This proves part 2.
12. For part 3, let $\rho_l$ be row $l$ of $R$. Entry $(i, k)$ of $CR$ is $\sum_l c_{il} \rho_{lk}$, so $A = \sum_{l=1}^r c_l \rho_l$, a sum of $r$ products of a column and a row.
13. Each $c_l \rho_l$ has columns $\rho_{lk} c_l$, all multiples of $c_l$. So its rank is at most $1$.
14. Each $\rho_l$ is nonzero. By step 7, $\operatorname{Col}(R) = F^r$, so it contains the unit vector $e_l$. If row $l$ of $R$ were zero, every vector of $\operatorname{Col}(R)$ would have entry $l$ equal to $0$. With $c_l \ne 0$, $c_l \rho_l$ has rank exactly $1$.
15. Let $A' = \sum_{l=1}^s u_l v_l^\top$. Column $k$ of $A'$ is $\sum_l (v_l)_k u_l$, so $\operatorname{Col}(A')$ lies in the span of $u_1, \dots, u_s$. By the [[Steinitz exchange lemma|08ZDRN6H41]], $\rank A' \le s$. This proves part 3.
16. For part 4, if $\rank A = 1$, then part 1 with $r = 1$ gives $A = CR$ with $C = u$ a nonzero column and $R = v^\top$ a nonzero row.
17. Conversely, let $A = uv^\top$ with $u, v \ne 0$. Column $k$ is $v_k u$. Some $v_k \ne 0$, so $\operatorname{Col}(A) = \operatorname{span}(u)$ and $\rank A = 1$. This proves part 4.

## Idea

Choose a basis of the column space as the columns of $C$, and record the coordinates of each column of $A$ in $R$.
The pivot columns of $A$ give one such basis, and the reduced form gives the coordinates.
Expanding the product $CR$ column by row writes $A$ as a sum of $r$ rank-one matrices.

## Variants

- The proof that [[row rank equals column rank by a factorization|S1C1ATEFW1]] uses the same factorization.
- Over $\R$, the singular value decomposition gives $A = \sum_{l=1}^r \sigma_l u_l v_l^\top$ with orthonormal $u_l$ and $v_l$.
