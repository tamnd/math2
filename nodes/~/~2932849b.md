## Example

Let $S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ be the set of nonzero residues modulo $6$, with multiplication modulo $6$.
The set $S$ has $5$ elements.
We show that $S$ is not a [[group|G5S1M9]], so it is not a group of [[order|0B85S2V2DR]] $5$.

1. A group operation on $S$ must take each pair of elements of $S$ to an element of $S$.
2. Take the pair $(2, 3)$. Then $2 \cdot 3 = 6$, and $6 \equiv 0 \pmod 6$ by [[congruence modulo $6$|0WAY5WX8NY]].
3. The residue $0$ is not in $S$. Thus multiplication modulo $6$ is not a [[binary operation|0M418FE7BB]] on $S$.
4. A second axiom also fails. The products $2 \cdot x$ for $x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ are $2, 4, 0, 2, 4$ modulo $6$, and none of them is $1$. So $2$ has no inverse.

Thus $S$ is not a group, and the count of $5$ elements is not the order of a group.

## Remarks

The [[group of units|0F8X8ZRQ53]] of [[$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$|03VFZ2WETT]] is the subset $\{1, 5\}$ of residues coprime to $6$, by [[this criterion|0GXNHEHQNA]].
It is a group of order $2$, because $1 \cdot 1 = 1$, $1 \cdot 5 = 5$ and $5 \cdot 5 = 25 \equiv 1 \pmod 6$.
For a prime $p$ the situation changes.
The nonzero residues modulo $7$ are all coprime to $7$, so they form a group of order $6$ under multiplication.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the numbers $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ and multiply them as on a clock with $6$ hours.
Then $2$ times $3$ gives $6$, and on this clock $6$ is the same as $0$.
But $0$ is not one of the five numbers.
So the five numbers do not form a group, and it makes no sense to speak of a group of $5$ elements here.

### L2 human

Before we count the elements of a set, we must check that the set with its operation is a group.

1. The first test is closure. One product outside the set is enough to stop: $2 \cdot 3 \equiv 0$, $3 \cdot 4 = 12 \equiv 0$ and $4 \cdot 3 \equiv 0 \pmod 6$.
2. When we remove the residues that share a factor with $6$, we keep $\{1, 5\}$, and this set passes all tests.

The typical use of this example is to show why the multiplicative groups modulo $n$ use only the residues coprime to $n$.
A trap is to think that the nonzero residues always form a group; this is true only when $n$ is prime.
