## Summary

Algebraic combinatorics is the study of combinatorial objects with the methods of algebra, and of algebraic structures through combinatorial models.
A typical object is a standard Young tableau, a filling of a diagram of boxes with the numbers 1 to n that increase along each row and down each column.
Tableaux count the dimensions of the irreducible representations of the symmetric groups, and symmetric functions record the characters of these groups and of the general linear groups.
Young introduced tableaux in 1900, and Schur used symmetric functions to study the representations of the general linear group in his thesis of 1901.
This topic collects the algebraic combinatorics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Enumerative combinatorics, representation theory, commutative algebra and convex and discrete geometry have their own topics.
