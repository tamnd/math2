## Proof

We prove parts 1 and 2; parts 3 and 4 are cited from Tarski and Givant.
For a formula $\psi(v_0, v_1)$ with free variables among $v_0, v_1$, put $\psi(v_0, v_2) := \exists v_1 (v_1 = v_2 \land \psi)$ and $\psi(v_2, v_1) := \exists v_0 (v_0 = v_2 \land \psi)$; both use only $v_0, v_1, v_2$.

**Part 1, by induction on $t$.**

1. Put $\varphi_{x_i} := R_i(v_0, v_1)$, $\varphi_0 := \lnot (v_0 = v_0)$, $\varphi_1 := v_0 = v_0$ and $\varphi_{1'} := v_0 = v_1$.
2. Put $\varphi_{s \vee t} := \varphi_s \lor \varphi_t$, $\varphi_{s \wedge t} := \varphi_s \land \varphi_t$ and $\varphi_{-s} := \lnot \varphi_s$.
3. Put $\varphi_{s ; t} := \exists v_2 (\varphi_s(v_0, v_2) \land \varphi_t(v_2, v_1))$.
4. Put $\varphi_{\breve{s}} := \exists v_2 (v_2 = v_0 \land \exists v_0 (v_0 = v_1 \land \exists v_1 (v_1 = v_2 \land \varphi_s)))$.
5. Each $\varphi_t$ uses only $v_0, v_1, v_2$ and has free variables among $v_0, v_1$, by induction.
6. Fix $U$ and relations $\rho_i$, and let $t^U$ be the value of $t$ in [[the full relation algebra|~1daf4bfe]] $\mathrm{Re}(U)$. The base cases of the claim $t^U = \set{(a, b) : U \models \varphi_t[a, b]}$ are the definitions of $\rho_i$, $\emptyset$, $U \times U$ and the identity.
7. The Boolean cases follow because $\lor$, $\land$ and $\lnot$ are interpreted by union, intersection and complement in $U \times U$.
8. For $s ; t$: $U \models \varphi_{s ; t}[a, b]$ iff there is $c$ with $U \models \varphi_s[a, c]$ and $U \models \varphi_t[c, b]$, iff there is $c$ with $(a, c) \in s^U$ and $(c, b) \in t^U$, iff $(a, b) \in s^U ; t^U$.
9. For $\breve{s}$: evaluate step 4 at $(a, b)$. The outer quantifier sets $v_2 = a$, the next sets $v_0 = b$, and the inner sets $v_1 = a$. So the formula holds iff $U \models \varphi_s[b, a]$, iff $(b, a) \in s^U$, iff $(a, b) \in \breve{(s^U)}$.

**Part 2.**

10. By the definition in [[this theorem|~122d50e8]], every representable algebra embeds in a product of algebras $\mathrm{Re}(U)$. Equations are preserved by subalgebras and products, so $s = t$ holds in $\mathsf{RRA}$ iff it holds in every $\mathrm{Re}(U)$.
11. It holds in $\mathrm{Re}(U)$ iff $s^U = t^U$ for every choice of the $\rho_i$, iff, by part 1, $U \models \forall v_0 \forall v_1 (\varphi_s \leftrightarrow \varphi_t)$ for every interpretation of the $R_i$ on $U$.
12. Ranging over all nonempty sets $U$ and all interpretations, this is logical validity. The empty set adds nothing, since $\mathrm{Re}(\emptyset)$ is trivial and satisfies every equation. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Composition is the only operation that needs a bound variable, and three variables suffice because the middle point can be reused.
Converse is the swap of $v_0$ and $v_1$, done through $v_2$ as a temporary store.
Once each term is a formula, equations between terms are biconditionals between formulas.

## Variants

1. The translation also goes from cylindric algebra terms of dimension $n$ to formulas in $n$ variables, with the cylindrification $c_i$ as $\exists v_i$.
2. Part 3 needs a more careful induction on formulas, since a subformula may have all three variables free; see Tarski and Givant.
3. For part 4, the idea is that $p$ and $q$ code ordered pairs, so that a tuple of any length can be coded by one element and formulas with many variables can be simulated with three.
