## Statement

Let T be [[Gödel's system T|~62fef358]] as a quantifier-free theory, and let $A \mapsto A^D = \exists x\, \forall y\, A_D(x, y)$ be the [[Dialectica interpretation|ZREF8MTYNW]].
Here $x$ and $y$ are tuples of variables of finite type, and $A_D(x, y)$ is a quantifier-free formula of T.
The symbols $+$ and $\cdot$ of [[Heyting arithmetic|RXHJPVMPJH]] are read as their defining terms in T.

**Theorem (Gödel).**

1. Let $A$ be a formula of HA whose free variables are among $a_1, \dots, a_k$. If $\mathsf{HA} \vdash A$, then there is a tuple $t$ of terms of T, with free variables among $a_1, \dots, a_k$, such that $\mathsf{T} \vdash A_D(t, y)$.
2. If [[Peano arithmetic|0QFT7Z76N5]] proves $\forall n\, \exists m\, \varphi(n, m)$ with $\varphi$ quantifier-free, then a closed term $t : 0 \to 0$ of T satisfies $\mathsf{T} \vdash \varphi(n, t\, n)$.

Part 1 also holds for the theory $\mathsf{WE}\text{-}\mathsf{HA}^\omega + \mathrm{AC} + \mathrm{MP} + \mathrm{IP}_\forall$ in place of HA.
This theory is weakly extensional Heyting arithmetic in all finite types, with the axiom of choice in all finite types, Markov's principle for quantifier-free formulas, and independence of premise for universal premises.
We prove parts 1 and 2; the extension to the larger theory is cited.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- HA cannot be replaced by PA in part 1. The [[halting non-example|~8d002a7b]] is a theorem of PA whose interpretation has no witness in T. Part 2 needs the negative translation first.
- In part 2 the matrix must be quantifier-free. For $\forall n\, \exists m\, \forall k\, \psi$ the interpretation gives only a functional of a counterexample function, not a function of $n$; the [[non-example for Pi-0-3 sentences|~ca230195]] shows that no computable $m$ exists in general.
- The extension needs weak extensionality. Howard showed that the axiom of full extensionality in all finite types has no witness in T.

### Consequences

- If T is consistent, then HA and PA are consistent: $\bot^D = \bot$, so a proof of $\bot$ gives $\mathsf{T} \vdash \bot$.
- Every provably recursive function of PA is definable by a closed term of type $0 \to 0$ in T, so it is $\alpha$-recursive for some $\alpha < \varepsilon_0$.
- PA and HA prove the same $\Pi^0_2$ sentences, as [[Friedman's theorem|~af8f99cd]] also shows.

### Converse

The converse of part 1 is false: T may verify $A_D(t, y)$ although HA does not prove $A$.
An example is Markov's principle $\neg \neg \exists y\, \varphi \to \exists y\, \varphi$, which the [[identity functional interprets|~f0f007c4]] but HA does not prove.
For the larger theory, the characterization theorem gives a converse: $\mathsf{WE}\text{-}\mathsf{HA}^\omega + \mathrm{AC} + \mathrm{MP} + \mathrm{IP}_\forall$ proves $A \leftrightarrow A^D$.

### Generalizations

- Spector's theorem: with [[bar recursion|~a7df5e71]] added to T, the interpretation extends to classical analysis with countable choice; see [[Spector's theorem|~fb8feea7]].
- The monotone variant gives majorants of the witnesses instead of the witnesses; it is the base of [[proof mining|~7cf2af55]].
- Restricting the recursors of T to types of low level gives interpretations of the fragments $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_n$ of PA; this gives one proof of [[Parsons's theorem|~066e4d82]].

## History

Gödel published the interpretation and its soundness in 1958, in a volume of the journal Dialectica.
Spector extended it to analysis in 1962.
Howard later showed that full extensionality is not interpreted.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem says that every proof in intuitionistic arithmetic contains a program.
The program wins a game: for each statement, it answers every challenge of an opponent.
For example, a proof that "for every $n$ there is a prime larger than $n$" contains a program that takes $n$ and outputs such a prime.
The programs are built only from counting loops of system T, so they always stop.

### L2 human

We follow part 2 for the theorem $\forall n\, \exists m\, (n < m)$ of PA, with $<$ as a primitive recursive relation.

1. The [[Gödel-Gentzen theorem|~c9a9f7c2]] gives $\mathsf{HA} \vdash \forall n\, \neg \neg \exists m\, (n < m)$.
2. The interpretation of $\neg \neg \exists m\, (n < m)$ is $\exists m\, \neg \neg (n < m)$, and the clause for $\forall$ gives $\exists f\, \forall n\, \neg \neg (n < f n)$.
3. Part 1 gives a term $t$ with $\mathsf{T} \vdash \neg \neg (n < t\, n)$, and so $\mathsf{T} \vdash n < t\, n$.
4. For the usual proof, the term is $t = \lambda n.\, S\, n$.

The typical use is the extraction of an algorithm from a non-constructive proof.
A trap is to expect a small term: the term reflects the proof, and for proofs with much induction it grows fast.

### L3 human

The theorem shows that the computational content of arithmetic is exactly primitive recursion in all finite types.
Its proof is a translation of derivations into terms, like the Curry-Howard correspondence, but it uses decidability of atomic formulas for contraction.
Through the negative translation it measures PA by T, and so by the ordinal $\varepsilon_0$, a result also given by [[ordinal analysis|XTTKZTYDSD]].
Spector's extension by bar recursion, and the monotone and bounded variants, carry the method to analysis and to [[proof mining|~7cf2af55]].
