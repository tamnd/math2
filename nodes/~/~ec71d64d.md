## Proof

Part 1 compares $\det A$ with $\det(-A)$.
Part 2 reduces to part 1 with the generic alternating matrix.

1. Let $A^T = -A$. By the [[transpose rule|0ZZQ2E1MTE]], $\det A = \det A^T = \det(-A)$.
2. By the [[scalar multiple rule|01EXSMK864]], $\det(-A) = (-1)^n \det A = -\det A$, since $n$ is odd.
3. So $\det A = -\det A$, that is $2 \det A = 0$.
4. $2$ is not a [[zero divisor|0K26YKNBSA]] in $K$, so $\det A = 0$. This proves part 1.
5. For part 2, let $R = \mathbb{Z}[x_{ij} : 1 \le i < j \le n]$, a [[polynomial ring|0Q9EZS90GY]].
6. Let $X$ have entries $x_{ij}$ for $i < j$, $-x_{ji}$ for $i > j$, and $0$ on the diagonal. Then $X^T = -X$.
7. If $2g = 0$ in $R$, then $2c = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ for each coefficient $c$ of $g$. So $c = 0$ and $g = 0$.
8. So $2$ is not a zero divisor in $R$, and part 1 gives $\det X = 0$.
9. Let $A$ be alternating over $K$. Let $\varphi \colon R \to K$ be the ring homomorphism with $\varphi(x_{ij}) = a_{ij}$ for $i < j$.
10. Then $\varphi(X) = A$, because $a_{ji} = -a_{ij}$ and $a_{ii} = 0$.
11. By [[naturality of the determinant|03Q7RE2HXR]] (part 2), $\det A = \varphi(\det X) = \varphi(0) = 0$.

## Idea

Transposing keeps the determinant, and negating an odd-size matrix negates it.
So the determinant is killed by $2$, which is enough when $2$ can be cancelled.
For alternating matrices, the generic case over $\mathbb{Z}[x_{ij}]$ has no problem with $2$, and every other case is a specialization.

## Variants

Part 2 also follows from the Pfaffian: the rank of an alternating matrix over a field is even, so in odd size it is less than $n$.
Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the matrix $(1)$ shows that part 1 fails without its hypothesis on $2$.
