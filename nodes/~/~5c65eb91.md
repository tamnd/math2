## Proof

1. By the [[existence of the reduced form|FCM29NQ6DN]], $A$ is row equivalent to a [[reduced row echelon form|0NNR68BFRY]] $R$.
2. Let $p$ be the number of [[pivot columns|0BCMGDHZGR]] of $R$, and let $f$ be the number of the other columns, the free columns.
3. Each of the $n$ columns of $R$ is either a pivot column or a free column, and not both. So $p + f = n$.
4. By the [[theorem on the column space|11CSKXQNG3]], part 3, $\dim \operatorname{Col}(A) = p$. By the [[definition of rank|0EWQEYRVA8]], $r = p$.
5. By the [[theorem on the free variables|5NKND0CPKP]], part 2, the $f$ vectors $s_1, \dots, s_f$ form a basis of $\operatorname{Null}(A)$. So $k = f$.
6. By steps 3 to 5, $r + k = p + f = n$.

## Idea

Row reduction sorts the columns into pivot columns and free columns.
The pivot columns give a basis of the column space, and the free columns give a basis of the null space.
So the rank and the nullity count the two kinds of columns.

## Variants

- The [[rank-nullity theorem for linear maps|0W4FSW1TVB]], applied to $x \mapsto Ax$, gives the same result without row reduction.
- Over $\R$, the [[four fundamental subspaces|~c3d8720b]] give $\R^n = \operatorname{Row}(A) \oplus \operatorname{Null}(A)$, and so $n = r + k$.
