## Example

Let $A = \mathbf{P}_3^2$, the pairs $(x, y)$ with $x, y \in \set{0, 1, 2}$ and the coordinatewise operations of [[the chain $\mathbf{P}_3$|~9ff77138]].

1. Membership. A variety is closed under direct products, so $A$ is a Post algebra of order $3$; see [[the definition|G24036DJ5K]].
2. Size. $A$ has $9$ elements.
3. Complemented elements. A pair is complemented iff each coordinate is, so $B(A) = \set{0, 2}^2$, a four-element [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]].
4. Constants. $e_1 = (1, 1)$ and $e_2 = (2, 2)$.
5. Representation. For $a = (x, y)$, put $b_i$ to have $2$ in each coordinate where $a$ is at least $i$, and $0$ elsewhere. Then $b_1 \ge b_2$ and $a = (b_1 \land e_1) \lor (b_2 \land e_2)$.
6. Example. For $a = (2, 1)$: $b_1 = (2, 2)$ and $b_2 = (2, 0)$. Then $(1, 1) \lor (2, 0) = (2, 1)$.

So $A$ is a [[Post algebra|RD2A5Z7E2B]] of order $3$ that is not a chain.

## Remarks

1. Count the decreasing pairs $b_1 \ge b_2$ in the four-element Boolean algebra: for $b_1$ of size $0, 1, 1, 2$ there are $1, 2, 2, 4$ choices of $b_2$. The total is $9$, the size of $A$.
2. The order on $A$ is coordinatewise, so $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$ are incomparable.
3. $A$ is not simple: the projection to the first coordinate is a homomorphism onto $\mathbf{P}_3$ with a nontrivial kernel.
4. In general, a Post algebra of order $n$ with Boolean algebra $B(A) = B$ is in bijection with the decreasing chains of length $n - 1$ in $B$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two independent three-valued questions give nine possible pairs of answers.
Each pair is coded by two yes or no questions per coordinate: "at least middle?" and "true?".
A "true" answer is always an "at least middle" answer, which gives the chain condition.

### L2 human

1. Take $a = (1, 0)$. Then $b_1 = (2, 0)$ and $b_2 = (0, 0)$.
2. Compute $(b_1 \land e_1) \lor (b_2 \land e_2) = (1, 0) \lor (0, 0) = (1, 0)$.
3. Compute $a' = (2, 1)$, and recode: $b_1 = (2, 2)$ and $b_2 = (2, 0)$.

The typical use: this is the smallest Post algebra of order $3$ that shows the Boolean structure behind the levels.
The trap: two complemented elements of $A$ give a chain only when the first is above the second.
