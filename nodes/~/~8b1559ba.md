## Proof

We show that no valuation makes both premises true; then the consequence holds vacuously.

1. Let $v$ be a classical [[valuation|H31CS3HK5P]]. By the [[principle of bivalence|5DYNDW9526]], $v(\varphi)$ is exactly one of $0$ and $1$.
2. By the truth table of [[negation|029JBBF693]], $v(\neg\varphi) = 1 - v(\varphi)$.
3. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $v(\varphi) = 1$ and $v(\neg\varphi) = 1$.
4. By step 2, $v(\neg\varphi) = 1$ gives $v(\varphi) = 0$. With step 3, $v(\varphi) = 1$ and $v(\varphi) = 0$, which contradicts step 1.
5. So no valuation makes both $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ true.
6. By the definition of [[logical consequence|1HK0VYWTV7]], $\set{\varphi, \neg\varphi} \vDash \psi$ means: every valuation that makes $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ true makes $\psi$ true.
7. By step 5 there is no such valuation, so the condition in step 6 holds [[vacuously|0V4QXPGJQK]]. Hence $\set{\varphi, \neg\varphi} \vDash \psi$. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

A contradiction has no model.
A statement of the form "every model of the premises satisfies $\psi$" is true when there are no models.
So the conclusion $\psi$ can be any formula.

## Variants

- The [[Lewis argument|~9dabb726]] gives a derivation instead of a semantic argument.
- The same proof works for every logic with a semantics in which no valuation designates both $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$. Examples are strong Kleene logic K3 and intuitionistic Kripke semantics.
- In the logic of paradox, step 5 fails: the value $\mathsf{B}$ makes $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ both designated.
