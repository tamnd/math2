## Example

Wikipedia defines the ordinal sum of t-norms $T_i$ on disjoint intervals $(a_i, b_i)$ as follows.
On $[a_i, b_i]^2$, $T(x, y) = a_i + (b_i - a_i) \, T_i\!\left(\frac{x - a_i}{b_i - a_i}, \frac{y - a_i}{b_i - a_i}\right)$; elsewhere $T(x, y) = \min(x, y)$.
Take the Łukasiewicz [[t-norm|~a4e21349]] on $[0, \tfrac12]$ and the product t-norm on $[\tfrac12, 1]$.

1. $T(0.2, 0.4)$: both are in $[0, \tfrac12]$, with rescaled values $0.4$ and $0.8$, so $T = \tfrac12 \max(0, 0.4 + 0.8 - 1) = 0.1$.
2. $T(0.6, 0.8)$: both are in $[\tfrac12, 1]$, with rescaled values $0.2$ and $0.6$, so $T = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12 (0.2 \cdot 0.6) = 0.56$.
3. $T(0.3, 0.7)$: the arguments are in different intervals, so $T = \min(0.3, 0.7) = 0.3$.
4. $T(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$: the first formula gives $\tfrac12 \max(0, 1 + 1 - 1) = \tfrac12$, and the second gives $\tfrac12 + \tfrac12 \cdot 0 = \tfrac12$, so the two definitions agree.
5. The idempotents $T(e, e) = e$ are $0$, $\tfrac12$ and $1$.
   For $0 < e < \tfrac12$, $T(e, e) = \tfrac12 \max(0, 4e - 1) < e$; for $\tfrac12 < e < 1$, $T(e, e) = \tfrac12 + 2(e - \tfrac12)^2 < e$.
6. Both summands are continuous, so $T$ is continuous, by Wikipedia.

## Remarks

1. By the Mostert-Shields theorem, as Wikipedia states it, every continuous t-norm has this form: an ordinal sum of copies of the Łukasiewicz and product t-norms, with $\min$ elsewhere.
   The theorem is cited, not proved.
2. $T$ has zero divisors, since the Łukasiewicz summand starts at $0$: $T(0.2, 0.2) = \tfrac12 \max(0, -0.2) = 0$.
3. The logic of all continuous t-norms is [[BL|~c93e8aa5]]; this $T$ is one of its standard models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An ordinal sum glues small copies of known t-norms along the diagonal of the square.
Inside each copy you use that t-norm, rescaled; outside you use the minimum.

### L2 human

1. Find the interval of each argument.
2. If both are in the same interval, rescale, apply the summand, and scale back.
3. Otherwise, take the minimum.

A trap: forgetting to scale back.
In step 2 the product of the rescaled values is $0.12$, but $T(0.6, 0.8) = 0.56$, not $0.12$.
