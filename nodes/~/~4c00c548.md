## Example

Let $K$ be an [[algebraically closed field|0PF6TM8TWT]], viewed as a structure in the signature of rings $\set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$.
Let $k$ be the prime field of $K$, and let $B$ be an infinite [[transcendence basis|E45PA267S4]] of $K$ over $k$.
List $B$ without repetitions as $(b_i)_{i \in I}$.
We show that $(b_i)_{i \in I}$ is an [[indiscernible set|~cc1977c7]] over $\emptyset$.

1. Let $\bar{\imath}$ and $\bar{\jmath}$ be $n$-tuples of pairwise distinct indices. As $B$ is infinite, the map $b_{i_m} \mapsto b_{j_m}$ extends to a permutation $\pi$ of $B$.
2. The set $B$ is algebraically independent over $k$, so $k[B]$ is a polynomial ring in the variables $B$. Renaming the variables by $\pi$ gives a ring automorphism of $k[B]$, and it extends to an automorphism $\tau$ of the fraction field $k(B)$.
3. By the definition of a transcendence basis, $K$ is algebraic over $k(B)$. As $K$ is algebraically closed, $K$ is an [[algebraic closure|0X6AB5P3G1]] of $k(B)$.
4. An isomorphism of fields extends to an embedding of an algebraic closure of the first field into any algebraically closed field that contains the second. So $\tau$ extends to a field embedding $\sigma \colon K \to K$.
5. The image $\sigma(K)$ is algebraically closed, it contains $k(B)$, and $K$ is algebraic over $k(B)$. So every element of $K$ is a root of a polynomial over $k(B)$ that splits in $\sigma(K)$, and $\sigma(K) = K$.
6. So $\sigma$ is an [[automorphism|DSQ17TNW1C]] of $K$ with $\sigma(b_{\bar{\imath}}) = b_{\bar{\jmath}}$. Automorphisms preserve all formulas, so $b_{\bar{\imath}}$ and $b_{\bar{\jmath}}$ satisfy the same formulas.

## Remarks

The field $\C$ has a transcendence basis over $\Q$ of size $2^{\aleph_0}$, so $\C$ contains an indiscernible set of that size.
By [[quantifier elimination for algebraically closed fields|2NBBJADY5N]], a stronger fact holds: any two $n$-tuples of elements that are algebraically independent over $k$ have the same type.
The example fails for algebraic elements.
In $\overline{\Q}$ there is no infinite injective indiscernible sequence over $\emptyset$: if $f$ is the minimal polynomial of $a_0$ over $\Q$, the formula $f(x) = 0$ must hold of every $a_i$, and $f$ has finitely many roots.
For a finite transcendence basis with $d$ elements, the same steps show indiscernibility for formulas in at most $d$ variables.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The numbers $\pi$ and $e$ are believed to be algebraically independent, but this is not known.
For numbers that are algebraically independent, no polynomial equation with rational coefficients links them.
In the complex numbers, such numbers can be exchanged by a symmetry of the whole field.
So, in the language of addition and multiplication, nothing tells them apart.

### L2 human

We check one instance in $\C$: let $t_1, t_2$ be two members of a transcendence basis $B$ of $\C$ over $\Q$.

1. The permutation that swaps $t_1$ and $t_2$ and fixes the rest of $B$ gives an automorphism $\tau$ of $\Q(B)$.
2. The field $\C$ is an algebraic closure of $\Q(B)$, so $\tau$ extends to an automorphism $\sigma$ of $\C$.
3. The formula $\exists y \, (y \cdot y = x_1 + x_2)$ holds at $(t_1, t_2)$ because $\C$ is algebraically closed, and also at $(t_2, t_1)$, as the example predicts.
4. The formula $x_1 \cdot x_1 = x_2$ fails at $(t_1, t_2)$, because $t_1$ and $t_2$ are algebraically independent; by the example it also fails at $(t_2, t_1)$.

The typical use is in the analysis of models of $\mathrm{ACF}_p$, where a model is fixed up to isomorphism by the size of a transcendence basis.
The trap is to forget the prime field: the definition needs a basis over $k$, and over a larger field of parameters the indiscernibility may fail.
