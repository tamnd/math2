## Summary

Mathematical modelling is the work of turning a question about the real world into a mathematical problem, solving that problem, and testing the answer against the world.
A mathematical model is a set of variables, equations or rules that represents the main parts of a real system and the relations between them.
Models let scientists and engineers predict the behaviour of a system, compare designs and estimate quantities that they cannot measure directly.
Dimensional analysis goes back to Fourier and Rayleigh, and the Buckingham pi theorem of 1914 gave it a general form.
This topic collects the mathematical modelling and applied methods pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Ordinary and partial differential equations, numerical analysis, statistical models, fluid mechanics and mathematical biology have their own topics.
