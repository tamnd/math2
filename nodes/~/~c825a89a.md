## Proof

We show both inequalities between the rank and the largest order of a nonzero minor.

1. Let $A$ be a nonzero $m \times n$ matrix over the field $F$, and let $\rho = \operatorname{rank} A$, the dimension of its [[column space|0F0RPPJ2TT]].
2. First, let $S$ be a $k \times k$ submatrix with $\det S \ne 0$, with rows $I$ and columns $J$.
3. By the [[invertibility criterion|FBSAPTP1PR]], $S$ is invertible, so $Sc = 0$ only for $c = 0$.
4. Let $\sum_{j \in J} c_j a_j = 0$ be a relation among the columns $a_j$ of $A$ with $j \in J$.
5. Keeping only the rows in $I$ gives $Sc = 0$, so $c = 0$.
6. So the $k$ columns of $A$ in $J$ are [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]], and $k \le \rho$.
7. Conversely, the columns of $A$ span the column space, so $\rho$ of them form a basis, by the [[basis criterion|06MRNP4BNT]].
8. Let $A'$ be the $m \times \rho$ matrix of these $\rho$ columns; its column space has dimension $\rho$.
9. By [[row rank equals column rank|0TZAG60ZYT]], $A'$ has $\rho$ linearly independent rows.
10. Let $S'$ be the $\rho \times \rho$ matrix of these rows of $A'$.
11. The rows of $S'$ are independent, so its row rank is $\rho$, and its column rank is $\rho$ too.
12. So the columns of $S'$ span $F^{\rho}$, and $S'$ is invertible.
13. By the invertibility criterion, $\det S' \ne 0$, and $\det S'$ is a [[minor|0SD528P0EK]] of $A$ of order $\rho$.
14. By steps 6 and 13, $\rho$ is the largest order of a nonzero minor of $A$.

## Idea

A nonzero minor makes its columns independent, so the rank is at least its order.
Conversely, $\rho$ independent columns contain $\rho$ independent rows, and they meet in an invertible square block.
