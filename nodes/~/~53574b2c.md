## Statement

For all [[propositions|~c068c095]] $p$ and $q$, these two [[logical equivalences|~01a642be]] hold.
$$\neg (p \lor q) \equiv \neg p \land \neg q$$
$$\neg (p \land q) \equiv \neg p \lor \neg q$$

## Remarks

The first law is also valid in intuitionistic logic. In the second law, only the direction from $\neg p \lor \neg q$ to $\neg (p \land q)$ is.
The laws for sets have the same form. See [[De Morgan's laws for sets|~ff3aaab8]].
The [[negation of a universal statement|~6e67a95d]] is the analogue for quantifiers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To negate "and", change it to "or" and negate each part.
"$n$ is not both even and prime" means "$n$ is not even, or $n$ is not prime".
A [[truth table|~77601110]] with four rows proves each law.
