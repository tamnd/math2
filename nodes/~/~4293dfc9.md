## Statement

Let $\theta$ be a real number. Then the [[complex exponential|054VXHMCA3]] at the purely imaginary number $i\theta$ is given by the [[complex cosine and sine|0A7YDKRNR6]] as
$$e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta.$$
At $-i\theta$ the same function gives
$$e^{-i\theta} = \cos\theta - i\sin\theta.$$

## Remarks

The second identity follows from the first, because $\cos$ is even and $\sin$ is odd.
Together, the two identities let one write $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$ as combinations of $e^{i\theta}$ and $e^{-i\theta}$.
This turns complex roots of a characteristic polynomial into real solutions of a linear ODE with constant coefficients.
