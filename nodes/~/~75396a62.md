## Example

We compute $U(1) = \set{U \in M_1(\C) : U^*U = I_1}$, the [[unitary group|~4244c18b]] of degree $1$.

1. A complex $1 \times 1$ matrix is $U = (z)$ with $z \in \C$. Its conjugate transpose is $U^* = (\overline{z})$, the [[complex conjugate|0BJV24C4SY]].
2. The condition $U^*U = I_1$ says $\overline{z}z = \abs{z}^2 = 1$.
3. So $U(1) = \set{(z) : \abs{z} = 1}$, which is the [[circle group|04EAFVFTF7]] $\set{z \in \C : \abs{z} = 1}$ with complex multiplication.
4. Each such $z$ is $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ for some $\theta \in \R$, and $e^{i\theta}e^{i\varphi} = e^{i(\theta + \varphi)}$.
5. The map $x \mapsto e^{i\theta}x$ turns the complex plane by the angle $\theta$.
6. $\det(z) = z$, so $SU(1) = \set{(1)}$.

## Remarks

- By [[the index-two theorem|~dd100441]], $U(1)$ is isomorphic to $SO(2)$ by $a + ic \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} a & -c \\ c & a \end{pmatrix}$.
- The real elements of $U(1)$ are $\pm 1$, which form $O(1)$, see [[this example|~b0d52397]].
- $U(1)$ is commutative and connected, while $O(1)$ is not connected.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A complex number of size $1$ is a point on the unit circle.
Multiplication by such a number turns the complex plane about $0$.
These turns are exactly the unitary $1 \times 1$ matrices.

### L2 human

The worked computation is in the Example.
The typical use: the determinant of each unitary matrix lies in $U(1)$.
A trap: for real numbers, the same condition gives only $\pm 1$.
