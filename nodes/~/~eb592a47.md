## Proof

1. Let $v = (v_i)_{i \in I}$ lie in the [[external direct sum|02WZB422CE]]. Only finitely many $v_i$ are nonzero, and $f_i(0) = 0$ since $f_i$ is [[linear|0RJF668P1T]]. So $\sum_{i \in I} f_i(v_i)$ has finitely many nonzero terms, and we define $f(v)$ as this sum.
2. $f$ is additive: $f(v + w) = \sum_i f_i(v_i + w_i) = \sum_i f_i(v_i) + \sum_i f_i(w_i) = f(v) + f(w)$, by the additivity of each $f_i$ and by rearranging a finite sum.
3. $f$ is homogeneous: $f(a v) = \sum_i f_i(a v_i) = a \sum_i f_i(v_i) = a f(v)$, by the homogeneity of each $f_i$.
4. For $j \in I$ and $x \in V_j$, the family $\iota_j(x)$ has $x$ in place $j$ and $0$ elsewhere. So $f(\iota_j(x)) = f_j(x) + \sum_{i \ne j} f_i(0) = f_j(x)$. This proves existence.
5. For uniqueness, let $g$ be linear with $g \circ \iota_i = f_i$ for all $i$.
6. Let $v = (v_i)_i$, and let $S$ be the finite set of $i$ with $v_i \ne 0$. Then $v = \sum_{i \in S} \iota_i(v_i)$, since both sides have the same entry in each place.
7. By linearity of $g$, $g(v) = \sum_{i \in S} g(\iota_i(v_i)) = \sum_{i \in S} f_i(v_i) = f(v)$.
8. So $g = f$.

## Idea

Every vector of the direct sum is a finite sum of vectors from the summands.
A linear map is then forced on the whole sum by its values on the summands, and the forced formula is linear.

## Variants

- The same proof works for modules over any ring.
- For a finite family, one can instead write $f$ as the block row $\begin{pmatrix} f_1 & \cdots & f_k \end{pmatrix}$ acting on column vectors $(v_1, \dots, v_k)$.
