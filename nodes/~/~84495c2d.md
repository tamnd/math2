## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite simple [[graph|~3f82cef4]] with $n$ vertices and no cycle of length $3$.
Then $G$ has at most $\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$ edges.

## Remarks

The bound is sharp: the [[complete bipartite graph|~13c6e22b]] $K_{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor, \lceil n/2 \rceil}$ has no triangle and has $\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$ edges.
The theorem is the case $r = 2$ of [[Turán's theorem|~7f258f92]].

## History

Willem Mantel stated the theorem in 1907.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A graph on $n$ vertices without a triangle can have at most about a quarter of $n^2$ edges.
Split the vertices into two equal halves and join each pair across: this graph reaches the bound.
