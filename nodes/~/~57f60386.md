## Statement

There is no right triangle with integer side lengths whose area is the square of an integer.
Equivalently, the equation $x^4 - y^4 = z^2$ has no solution in positive integers $x$, $y$ and $z$.

## Remarks

In terms of a [[Pythagorean triple|~8a5ab82a]] $(a, b, c)$, the theorem says that $ab/2$ is never a perfect square.
A consequence is [[Fermat's Last Theorem for n = 4|~79e37342]], since $a^4 + b^4 = c^4$ gives $c^4 - b^4 = (a^2)^2$.
The proof is by [[infinite descent|~f7936b61]].

## History

The proof was published in 1670 among the works of Pierre de Fermat, after his death.
It is the only complete proof by Fermat that survives.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The triangle with sides $3$, $4$, $5$ has area $6$, and the one with sides $5$, $12$, $13$ has area $30$.
Fermat proved that such an area can never be a perfect square.
