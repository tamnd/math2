## Proof

Let $T$ be satisfiable.
We prove $1 \Rightarrow 4 \Rightarrow 3 \Rightarrow 2 \Rightarrow 1$.

1. *$1 \Rightarrow 4$.* Let $\mathcal{M} \models T$. Every consequence of $T$ is true in $\mathcal{M}$, so $\operatorname{Cn}(T) \subseteq \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$.
2. Let $\sigma \in \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$. If $T \not\models \sigma$, then $T \models \neg\sigma$ by condition 1, so $\mathcal{M} \models \neg\sigma$, a contradiction. So $\sigma \in \operatorname{Cn}(T)$, and $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M}) \subseteq \operatorname{Cn}(T)$.
3. *$4 \Rightarrow 3$.* $T$ is satisfiable, so it has a model $\mathcal{M}$, and condition 4 gives $\operatorname{Cn}(T) = \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$.
4. *$3 \Rightarrow 2$.* Let $\operatorname{Cn}(T) = \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$, and let $\mathcal{N} \models T$. Then $\mathcal{N}$ satisfies every sentence in $\operatorname{Cn}(T)$, so $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M}) \subseteq \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{N})$.
5. If $\sigma \in \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{N}) \setminus \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$, then $\neg\sigma \in \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M}) \subseteq \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{N})$, so $\mathcal{N}$ satisfies $\sigma$ and $\neg\sigma$, which is impossible. So $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{N}) = \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$.
6. So every model of $T$ has the theory $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$, and any two models of $T$ are elementarily equivalent.
7. *$2 \Rightarrow 1$.* Let $\sigma$ be a sentence with $T \not\models \sigma$. Then some $\mathcal{N} \models T$ has $\mathcal{N} \models \neg\sigma$.
8. Every model $\mathcal{N}'$ of $T$ satisfies $\mathcal{N}' \equiv \mathcal{N}$ by condition 2, so $\mathcal{N}' \models \neg\sigma$. Hence $T \models \neg\sigma$, and $T$ is complete.

## Idea

A model $\mathcal{M}$ of $T$ decides every sentence, so $T$ decides every sentence exactly when it agrees with $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$.
That happens exactly when no two models of $T$ disagree.

## Variants

With the completeness theorem, $T \models \sigma$ can be replaced by $T \vdash \sigma$ throughout, and condition 1 becomes the syntactic statement that $T$ proves $\sigma$ or $\neg\sigma$.
Then step 7 uses the completeness theorem to get the model $\mathcal{N}$ from $T \nvdash \sigma$.
