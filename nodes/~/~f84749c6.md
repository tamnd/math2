## Statement

The [[complex numbers|~bc6635c6]] $\mathbb{C}$, with their addition and multiplication, form a field.
That is, $\mathbb{C}$ is a commutative ring with $0 \neq 1$, and each complex number $z \neq 0$ has a multiplicative inverse $z^{-1}$ with $z z^{-1} = 1$.

## Remarks

The [[inverse of a complex number|~1f49e9c8]] $a + b i \neq 0$ is $\dfrac{a - b i}{a^2 + b^2}$.
The field $\mathbb{C}$ contains $\mathbb{R}$ as a subfield. It has dimension $2$ as a vector space over $\mathbb{R}$, with basis $1, i$.
The field $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, but it is not an ordered field.

## Explanations

### L1 human

You can add, subtract, multiply and divide complex numbers, as with real numbers.
The only exception is division by $0$.
For example, $\dfrac{1}{i} = -i$, because $i \cdot (-i) = -i^2 = 1$.
