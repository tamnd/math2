## Proof

1. Let $a_j$, $b_j$ be column $j$ of $A$ and of $B$. Column $j$ of $A + B$ is $a_j + b_j$.
2. By the [[definition of the column space|0F0RPPJ2TT]], $a_j \in \operatorname{Col}(A)$ and $b_j \in \operatorname{Col}(B)$. So $a_j + b_j \in \operatorname{Col}(A) + \operatorname{Col}(B)$.
3. The sum $\operatorname{Col}(A) + \operatorname{Col}(B)$ is a subspace that contains every column of $A + B$. So it contains $\operatorname{Col}(A + B)$.
4. By the [[dimension of a subspace|0QW084460E]], $\dim \operatorname{Col}(A + B) \le \dim(\operatorname{Col}(A) + \operatorname{Col}(B))$.
5. By the [[dimension formula for a sum|06HTJNCMPA]], $\dim(\operatorname{Col}(A) + \operatorname{Col}(B)) = \dim \operatorname{Col}(A) + \dim \operatorname{Col}(B) - \dim(\operatorname{Col}(A) \cap \operatorname{Col}(B))$.
6. The last term is at least $0$. So steps 4 and 5 give $\rank(A + B) \le \rank A + \rank B$, by the [[definition of rank|0EWQEYRVA8]].

## Idea

The column space of a sum lies in the sum of the column spaces.
The dimension of a sum of two subspaces is at most the sum of the dimensions.

## Variants

A second proof writes $A$ and $B$ as sums of $\rank A$ and $\rank B$ rank-one matrices, by the [[rank factorization theorem|~5d7700d1]].
Then $A + B$ is a sum of $\rank A + \rank B$ rank-one matrices, and part 3 of that theorem bounds its rank.
