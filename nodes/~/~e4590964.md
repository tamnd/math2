## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite [[group|G5S1M9]], let $H$ be a [[normal subgroup|~ebf813cb]] of $G$, and let $P$ be a [[Sylow p-subgroup|~e7f618d0]] of $H$.
Then $G = N_G(P) H$, where $N_G(P)$ is the [[normaliser|~cd2fd4dc]] of $P$ in $G$.

## Remarks

For $g \in G$, the subgroup $gPg^{-1}$ lies in $H$, because $H$ is normal, and it is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.
By the [[second Sylow theorem|~30ed79e7]] in $H$, there is $h \in H$ with $gPg^{-1} = hPh^{-1}$, so $h^{-1}g \in N_G(P)$.
The argument is used in the proof that a finite [[nilpotent group|~744d1682]] is the [[direct product of its Sylow subgroups|~24f1b8b2]].

## History

Giovanni Frattini used the argument in a paper from 1885.
He took it, as he said himself, from a paper of Alfredo Capelli from 1884.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Conjugation by an element of $G$ moves $P$ to another Sylow subgroup of $H$.
An element of $H$ can move it back.
So each element of $G$ is an element of $H$ times an element that normalises $P$.
