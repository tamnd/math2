## Statement

Let $C$ be a [[circle|~452d68a3]] in the Euclidean plane $\mathbb{R}^2$, and let $d$ be the [[Euclidean distance|~5eb2e3e8]]. Let $P$ be a point outside $C$.
Let a [[tangent line|~fe67ebc1]] through $P$ touch $C$ at $T$. Let a second line through $P$ meet $C$ at the two points $G_1$ and $G_2$.
Then
$$d(P, T)^2 = d(P, G_1)\, d(P, G_2).$$

## Remarks

Both sides are equal to the [[power|~d6f2de4f]] of $P$ with respect to $C$.
It is the limit case of the [[intersecting secants theorem|~f0322180]] where one secant becomes a tangent.
The theorem is Proposition 36 of Book III of Euclid's Elements.

## Explanations

### L1 human

From a point outside a circle, the square of the tangent length equals the product of the two distances along any secant. For the unit circle and $P = (3, 0)$, the secant along the $x$-axis meets the circle at distances $2$ and $4$. So the tangent length is $\sqrt{8}$.
