## Definition

Let $A$ be a set.
An equivalence relation on $A$ is a [[binary relation|~41fc00d0]] $\sim$ on $A$ that is [[reflexive|~cda728b2]], [[symmetric|~33169516]] and [[transitive|~675931ef]]. For all $a, b, c \in A$:

1. $a \sim a$;
2. $a \sim b$ implies $b \sim a$;
3. $a \sim b$ and $b \sim c$ imply $a \sim c$.

## Remarks

For a fixed integer $n \ge 1$, congruence modulo $n$ on $\mathbb{Z}$ is an equivalence relation: $a \sim b$ when $n$ divides $a - b$.
An equivalence relation cuts the set into [[equivalence classes|~6bf9e94d]], and these form a [[partition|~3d920058]].
Each [[function|~d103e2ff]] gives an equivalence relation. See [[equivalence relation induced by a function|~18f69fe4]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An equivalence relation says when two objects are "the same for our purpose".
Two integers are congruent modulo $2$ when they have the same parity.
Then all even numbers count as one kind, and all odd numbers as another.
