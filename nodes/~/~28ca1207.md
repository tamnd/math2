## Example

Let $x_0 > 0$ and consider the [[initial value problem|~40f94cf8]] $x'(t) = x(t)^2$, $x(0) = x_0$.
[[Separation of variables|~3b1dee4f]] gives the solution
$$x(t) = \frac{x_0}{1 - x_0 t}, \qquad t \in \left(-\infty, \frac{1}{x_0}\right).$$
This is the [[maximal solution|~3f3a6157]]: $x(t) \to \infty$ as $t \to 1/x_0$ from below.
So the solution blows up at the finite time $1/x_0$, although the field $f(x) = x^2$ is smooth on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

## Remarks

The blow-up time $1/x_0$ depends on the initial value. Larger initial values blow up sooner.
For $x_0 < 0$, the maximal interval is $(1/x_0, \infty)$, and for $x_0 = 0$ the solution is $0$ on $\mathbb{R}$.
The field $x^2$ is not globally Lipschitz. A [[globally Lipschitz field|~4ebe50fe]] has no blow-up.
Near the blow-up time, $x(t)$ behaves like $(1/x_0 - t)^{-1}$. This is a finite-time singularity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rate of growth is the square of the size, so the growth feeds itself very fast.
The solution reaches infinity in finite time, unlike exponential growth.
