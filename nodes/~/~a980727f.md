## Statement

Let $n$ be a positive integer.
Then there are infinitely many [[prime numbers|~f3c51c16]] $p$ such that $p \equiv 1 \pmod{n}$.

## Remarks

This is the case $a = 1$ of [[Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions|~2bb76ecf]].
The case $a = 1$ has an elementary proof with cyclotomic polynomials. A prime factor $p$ of $\Phi_n(m)$ that does not divide $n$ satisfies $p \equiv 1 \pmod n$.
For example, the primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod{10}$ start with $11, 31, 41, 61, 71$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Fix a number $n$, for example $n = 6$. Then infinitely many primes leave the remainder $1$ when you divide them by $6$.
The first ones are $7, 13, 19, 31, 37$.
