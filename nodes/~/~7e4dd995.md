## Example

Let $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, and let $f_n \colon S \to \mathbb{R}$ be [[uniformly continuous|0M4JJT780X]] for each $n$.
Suppose that $f_n \to f$ [[uniformly on $S$|08HJRTG5N7]].
We prove by an [[epsilon over three argument|~9f05a67c]] that $f$ is uniformly continuous on $S$.

1. Let $\varepsilon > 0$. We must find $\delta > 0$ such that $\lvert f(x) - f(y) \rvert < \varepsilon$ for all $x, y \in S$ with $\lvert x - y \rvert < \delta$.
2. By uniform convergence, there is an index $N$ with $\lvert f_N(z) - f(z) \rvert < \varepsilon/3$ for all $z \in S$. We fix this $N$. It does not depend on any point.
3. The function $f_N$ is uniformly continuous. So there is $\delta > 0$ with $\lvert f_N(x) - f_N(y) \rvert < \varepsilon/3$ for all $x, y \in S$ with $\lvert x - y \rvert < \delta$.
4. Let $x, y \in S$ with $\lvert x - y \rvert < \delta$. By the [[triangle inequality|03HF5PMY5T]],
$$\lvert f(x) - f(y) \rvert \le \lvert f(x) - f_N(x) \rvert + \lvert f_N(x) - f_N(y) \rvert + \lvert f_N(y) - f(y) \rvert.$$
5. The first term is less than $\varepsilon/3$ by step 2 with $z = x$, and the third term is less than $\varepsilon/3$ by step 2 with $z = y$.
6. The middle term is less than $\varepsilon/3$ by step 3.
7. So $\lvert f(x) - f(y) \rvert < \varepsilon$. The number $\delta$ depends only on $\varepsilon$, through $N$, so $f$ is uniformly continuous.

## Remarks

1. The proof is the proof of the [[uniform limit theorem|0X9XT05JVC]] with one change: in step 3 the $\delta$ does not depend on a point. The epsilon over three argument passes this uniformity from $f_N$ to $f$.
2. A concrete case: $f_n(x) = \sqrt{x + 1/n}$ on $[0, \infty)$ converges uniformly to $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$. Indeed, $\sqrt{x + 1/n} - \sqrt{x} = \frac{1/n}{\sqrt{x + 1/n} + \sqrt{x}} \le \frac{1/n}{\sqrt{1/n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt n}$ for every $x \ge 0$.
3. In this case each $f_n$ is Lipschitz, with constant $\frac{\sqrt n}{2}$, because $f_n'(x) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x + 1/n}} \le \frac{\sqrt n}{2}$. The limit $\sqrt{x}$ is uniformly continuous, as the example proves, but it is not Lipschitz near $0$.
4. So the epsilon over three argument passes uniform continuity to the limit, but not a Lipschitz bound. The Lipschitz constants grow with $n$, and a property of the form "for each $\varepsilon$ there is $\delta$" survives, while a fixed bound need not.
5. With pointwise convergence the result fails. The functions $x^n$ on $[0, 1]$ are uniformly continuous, and their pointwise limit is not even continuous, as the [[non-example of the method|~84bb1442]] shows.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A function is uniformly continuous when one closeness rule for the inputs works everywhere at once.
Suppose each function of a sequence has such a rule, and the functions get close to a limit function everywhere at the same speed.
Then we pick one function of the sequence that is within a third of the tolerance of the limit at every point.
Its closeness rule, with the two small errors at the ends, gives a rule for the limit.

### L2 human

We check the steps of the proof for the concrete case $f_n(x) = \sqrt{x + 1/n}$ on $[0, \infty)$ and $\varepsilon = 0.3$.

1. Step 2 asks for $N$ with $\frac{1}{\sqrt N} < 0.1$. Since $\frac{1}{\sqrt{101}} < 0.1$, we can take $N = 101$.
2. Step 3 asks for $\delta$ for $f_{101}$ and $0.1$. Since $f_{101}$ is Lipschitz with constant $\frac{\sqrt{101}}{2} < 5.03$, the choice $\delta = 0.1 / 5.03$ works, which is about $0.0199$.
3. For $\lvert x - y \rvert < \delta$, the three terms are each less than $0.1$, so $\lvert \sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y} \rvert < 0.3$.

The typical use of the result is to show that a function defined as a uniform limit, for example a power series on a compact interval, is uniformly continuous.
A trap is to use, in step 3, a $\delta$ that is good only near one point.
Then the conclusion is only continuity, not uniform continuity.
