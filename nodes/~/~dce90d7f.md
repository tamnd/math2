## Summary

The philosophy of mathematics studies the nature of mathematical objects, mathematical knowledge and mathematical truth.
Its central questions ask whether mathematical objects exist, how we can know facts about them, and why mathematics applies to the physical world.
The answers to these questions bear on the foundations of mathematics and on the philosophy of science.
Plato and Aristotle already discussed the status of mathematical objects, and the debate between logicism, formalism and intuitionism shaped the field in the early 20th century.
This topic collects the philosophy of mathematics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Reasoning and proof, mathematical logic, set theory, and type theory and constructive mathematics have their own topics.
