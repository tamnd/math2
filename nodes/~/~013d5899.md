## Proof

Let $U = \mathrm{Th}_\forall(\mathcal{K})$, the set of universal sentences true in every member of $\mathcal{K}$.

### Part 2

1. Let $A$ embed in an ultraproduct $M$ of members of $\mathcal{K}$. By [[Łoś's theorem|AGYBSTD7BG]], $M \models U$. By [[this statement|C9494XYEMN]], universal sentences pass to substructures, so $A \models U$.
2. Conversely, let $A \models U$. Let $I$ be the set of finite subsets of $\mathrm{Diag}(A)$, the atomic diagram in the [[diagram lemmas|EP5E317TNW]].
3. For $i \in I$, write the conjunction of $i$ together with $c_a = c_a$ for one fixed $a \in A$ as $\delta_i(\bar c)$, with $\delta_i(\bar x)$ quantifier-free.
4. Some $K_i \in \mathcal{K}$ has elements $\bar k$ with $K_i \models \delta_i(\bar k)$. Otherwise $\forall \bar x \, \neg\delta_i(\bar x)$ lies in $U$, while $A \models \delta_i(\bar a)$, a contradiction.
5. Expand $K_i$ to $K_i^*$ by interpreting $\bar c$ as $\bar k$ and every other constant $c_b$ as some fixed element.
6. For $\delta \in \mathrm{Diag}(A)$, let $X_\delta = \set{i \in I : \delta \in i}$. The set $X_{\delta_1} \cap \dots \cap X_{\delta_m}$ contains $\set{\delta_1, \dots, \delta_m}$, so it is nonempty.
7. By the [[ultrafilter lemma|3MKZST79MK]], there is an [[ultrafilter|J64Y3JC0RE]] $D$ on $I$ that contains every $X_\delta$.
8. For $\delta \in \mathrm{Diag}(A)$, every $i \in X_\delta$ has $K_i^* \models \delta$. Since $X_\delta \in D$, Łoś's theorem gives $\prod_i K_i^* / D \models \delta$.
9. So $\prod_i K_i^* / D \models \mathrm{Diag}(A)$, and $A$ embeds in its $L$-reduct by the diagram lemma. That reduct is the [[ultraproduct|AX9Q86GB8M]] $\prod_i K_i / D$.

### Part 1

10. Let $\mathcal{K}$ be universal, with axioms $\Sigma$. Isomorphisms preserve all sentences, substructures preserve universal sentences, and ultraproducts preserve all sentences by Łoś's theorem. So $\mathcal{K}$ has the three closure properties.
11. Conversely, let $\mathcal{K}$ have the three closure properties. Each member of $\mathcal{K}$ satisfies $U$.
12. If $A \models U$, part 2 gives an embedding of $A$ into an ultraproduct $M$ of members of $\mathcal{K}$. Then $M \in \mathcal{K}$, the image of $A$ is a substructure of $M$, and $A$ is isomorphic to it, so $A \in \mathcal{K}$.
13. So $\mathcal{K}$ is the class of models of $U$, and it is universal.

### Part 3

14. Substructures of members of $\mathcal{K}$ are in $\mathcal{K}$ by part 1, which gives one direction.
15. Suppose $A \notin \mathcal{K}$. Some axiom $\forall \bar x \, \theta$ of $\mathcal{K}$ fails in $A$, so $A \models \neg\theta(\bar a)$ for a tuple $\bar a$.
16. Let $B$ be the substructure generated by $\bar a$, or by one element if $\bar a$ is empty. Quantifier-free formulas keep their truth value in substructures, so $B \models \neg\theta(\bar a)$ and $B \notin \mathcal{K}$.

### Part 4

17. Let $\mathcal{K}$ be universal, and let $\mathcal{F}$ be the class of finite $L$-structures not in $\mathcal{K}$. In a relational signature every nonempty subset is a substructure, and finite sets generate finite substructures.
18. By part 3, $A \in \mathcal{K}$ if and only if no finite substructure of $A$ lies outside $\mathcal{K}$, that is, if and only if no member of $\mathcal{F}$ embeds in $A$.
19. Conversely, let $\mathcal{F}$ be given. For $F \in \mathcal{F}$ with elements $f_1, \dots, f_n$, let $\delta_F(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ be the conjunction of the atomic and negated atomic formulas true of $f_1, \dots, f_n$ in $F$. It is finite because $L$ is finite and relational.
20. A structure $A$ satisfies $\forall \bar x \, \neg\delta_F(\bar x)$ exactly when $F$ does not embed in $A$, by the diagram lemma. These universal sentences axiomatize $\mathcal{K}$.

## Idea

A model $A$ of the universal theory of $\mathcal{K}$ has each finite piece of its diagram realized in some member of $\mathcal{K}$.
An ultrafilter that concentrates on larger and larger pieces glues these members into one ultraproduct that contains all of $A$.
Closure under ultraproducts and substructures then puts $A$ into $\mathcal{K}$.

## Variants

- When $\mathcal{K}$ is already elementary, part 1 follows from the [[Łoś-Tarski theorem|9RN0KYXNT1]], whose proof embeds a model of $U$ in a model of the theory of $\mathcal{K}$ by compactness. The ultraproduct proof does not need $\mathcal{K}$ to be elementary.
- Part 2 is an instance of the general form $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathrm{Th}_\forall(\mathcal{K})) = \mathbf{I}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{P}_U(\mathcal{K})$; the analogue for universal Horn sentences replaces $\mathbf{P}_U$ by reduced products.
