## Example

Let $(E, F)$ be any fixed point of the strong Kleene jump $\mathcal{J}$ of [[Kripke's theorem|~1dd87a57]], with extension $E$ and anti-extension $F$.
We write $F$ and not $A$ for the anti-extension, because $A$ is a sentence variable of $\mathsf{KF}$.
Write $\|\varphi\|$ for $\|\varphi\|_{(E, F)}$.
Let $\mathfrak{M} := (\mathcal{N}, E)$ be the classical $\mathcal{L}_T$-structure that expands the [[standard model|WP27CABPA4]] and interprets $T$ by $E$.
We check that $\mathfrak{M}$ satisfies every axiom of [[the Kripke-Feferman theory|~a625d303]] and also $\mathsf{Cons}$.
In $\mathfrak{M}$, the variables $A$, $B$ range over codes of actual sentences, since every element of $\mathcal{N}$ is standard.

1. *Fixed point property.* Since $\mathcal{J}(E, F) = (E, F)$, a sentence $\varphi$ has $\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner \in E$ if and only if $\|\varphi\| = 1$.
2. In the same way, $\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner \in F$ if and only if $\|\varphi\| = 0$. So $\ulcorner \neg \varphi \urcorner \in E$ if and only if $\|\varphi\| = 0$.
3. *Axiom 1.* $\mathfrak{M}$ expands $\mathcal{N}$, so it satisfies the axioms of $\mathsf{PA}$ without induction.
4. Let $\psi(x)$ be any $\mathcal{L}_T$ formula with parameters, and let $X := \{n : \mathfrak{M} \models \psi(\underline{n})\}$.
5. If $0 \in X$ and $X$ is closed under $n \mapsto n + 1$, then $X = \mathbb{N}$ by induction in $\mathbb{N}$. So every induction axiom holds.
6. *Axiom 2.* For an atomic arithmetical $A$, step 1 gives $A \in E$ iff $\|A\| = 1$ iff $A$ is true in $\mathcal{N}$ iff $\mathrm{Tr}_0(A)$.
7. *Axiom 3.* By step 2, $[\neg A] \in E$ iff $\|A\| = 0$ iff $A$ is false in $\mathcal{N}$.
8. *Axiom 4.* By step 1, $[T\dot A] \in E$ iff $\|T(\dot A)\| = 1$ iff $A \in E$, by the clause for $T$.
9. *Axiom 5.* By step 2, $[\neg T\dot A] \in E$ iff $\|T(\dot A)\| = 0$ iff $A \in F$.
10. Since $A$ is a sentence, step 2 gives $A \in F$ iff $\|A\| = 0$ iff $[\neg A] \in E$.
11. *Axiom 6.* $[\neg \neg A] \in E$ iff $\|\neg \neg A\| = 1$ iff $\|A\| = 1$ iff $A \in E$.
12. *Axioms 7 and 8.* $\|A \wedge B\| = 1$ iff both are $1$. $\|A \wedge B\| = 0$ iff one is $0$. Steps 1 and 2 translate this.
13. *Axioms 9 and 10.* The clauses for $\vee$ are dual, and the same translation applies.
14. *Axioms 11 and 12.* $\|\forall v\, A(v)\| = 1$ iff every $\|A(\underline{n})\| = 1$, and it is $0$ iff some $\|A(\underline{n})\| = 0$.
15. *Axioms 13 and 14.* The clauses for $\exists$ are dual, and the same translation applies.
16. *$\mathsf{Cons}$.* If $A \in E$ and $[\neg A] \in E$, then $\|A\| = 1$ and $\|A\| = 0$ by steps 1 and 2. This is impossible.
17. So $\mathfrak{M} \models \mathsf{KF} + \mathsf{Cons}$.

## Remarks

The example shows that $\mathsf{KF} + \mathsf{Cons}$ is consistent.
It is also $\omega$-consistent, because $\mathfrak{M}$ has the standard natural numbers.
This is the contrast with the Friedman-Sheard theory, which [[McGee's theorem|~e7fe60d3]] shows to be $\omega$-inconsistent.
The SEP entry on the liar calls $\mathfrak{M}$ the closed-off model: the sentences with value $\mathrm{u}$ are put into the false part.
Take the least fixed point and the [[liar|~005ac188]] $\lambda$.
By [[this example|~c48c7e65]], $\|\lambda\| = \mathrm{u}$, so neither $\lambda$ nor $\neg \lambda$ is in $E$.
So $\mathfrak{M} \models \neg T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$, and hence $\mathfrak{M} \models \lambda$, by the diagonal equivalence.
So $\lambda$ is true in $\mathfrak{M}$ but not in the extension of $T$, and $\mathfrak{M}$ refutes $\mathsf{Compl}$.
Since $F \cap E = \emptyset$, the pair $(E, \mathbb{N} \setminus E)$ is above $(E, F)$, and its strong Kleene values are the classical values in $\mathfrak{M}$.
So, by monotonicity in Kripke's theorem, a sentence with Kripke value $1$ or $0$ has the same value in $\mathfrak{M}$.
Steps 1 to 16 use only the fixed point property.
So the same check works for every fixed point, for example a maximal one.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Kripke's construction sorts sentences into "true", "false" and "not yet decided".
We now make a classical picture: everything that is not in the "true" box counts as not true.
In this picture, every axiom of the Kripke-Feferman theory holds.
For example, "it is true that '$1 + 1 = 2$' is true" holds, because the inner claim is in the true box.
The liar is not in the true box, so in this picture the liar is not true, and so what it says holds.

### L2 human

We check axiom 5 for the truth-teller $\tau$ in the least fixed point.

1. In the least fixed point, $\|\tau\| = \mathrm{u}$, by [[the truth-teller page|~66f66b7a]].
2. So $\|T\ulcorner \tau \urcorner\| = \mathrm{u}$, and $\ulcorner \neg T\ulcorner \tau \urcorner \urcorner \notin E$.
3. Also $\|\neg \tau\| = \mathrm{u}$, so $\ulcorner \neg \tau \urcorner \notin E$.
4. Both sides of axiom 5 for $A := \ulcorner \tau \urcorner$ are false in $\mathfrak{M}$, so the biconditional holds.

The typical use of the closed-off model is to prove that an extension of $\mathsf{KF}$ is consistent.
The trap is to think that $\mathfrak{M}$ satisfies the T-sentence for $\lambda$: it satisfies $\lambda$ and $\neg T\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner$.
