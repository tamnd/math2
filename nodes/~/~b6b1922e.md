## Summary

Extremal and probabilistic combinatorics is the study of how large or small a finite structure can be under a given restriction.
A typical result is Turán's theorem, which gives the largest number of edges in a graph on n vertices with no complete subgraph on r + 1 vertices.
The probabilistic method proves that a structure exists by showing that a random choice has the required property with positive probability, and it is one of the main tools of the area.
Ramsey proved his theorem in 1930, Turán proved his theorem in 1941, and Erdős used random colourings in 1947 to give a lower bound for Ramsey numbers.
This topic collects the extremal and probabilistic combinatorics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Graph theory, additive and combinatorial number theory, probability theory and algebraic topology have their own topics.
