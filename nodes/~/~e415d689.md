## Statement

Let $L$ be a signature with at least one constant symbol, and let $T$ be an $L$-[[theory|92SWXST1ZM]].
A *literal* is an atomic formula or the negation of an atomic formula.
A structure $A$ is a *common substructure* of $M$ and $N$ when $A$ is a [[substructure|676H7EGTKJ]] of both.
The following are equivalent.

1. $T$ has [[quantifier elimination|RE5TFTDP4D]].
2. (One-quantifier test.) For every conjunction of literals $\varphi(\bar x, y)$, the [[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] $\exists y \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$ is equivalent modulo $T$ to a quantifier-free formula $\psi(\bar x)$.
3. (Embedding test.) For all [[models|MVCH6N9SNW]] $M, N$ of $T$, every common substructure $A$ of $M$ and $N$, every tuple $\bar a$ in $A$ and every conjunction of literals $\varphi(\bar x, y)$: if $M \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$, then $N \models \exists y \, \varphi(\bar a, y)$.
4. (Extension test.) For all models $M, N$ of $T$ with $N$ $\omega$-[[saturated|RMTZ5J64FX]], every finitely generated substructure $A \subseteq M$, every [[embedding|SCRB74SWQN]] $f \colon A \to N$ and every $b \in M$, there is an embedding $g \colon \langle A, b \rangle \to N$ that extends $f$.

Here $\langle A, b \rangle$ is the substructure of $M$ generated by $A \cup \set{b}$.
The equivalence of (1) and (2) holds without the constant symbol.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The constant symbol. Let $L = \set{P}$ with $P$ unary, and let $T$ say that there is exactly one element. Condition (3) holds, since two models with a common substructure are equal to it. But $\exists x \, P(x)$ is equivalent to neither $\top$ nor $\bot$ modulo $T$.
- Saturation of $N$ in (4). For algebraically closed fields of characteristic $0$, let $M$ have a transcendental element $b$ and let $N$ be the algebraic numbers. The inclusion of $\Z$ into $N$ has no extension to $\Z[b]$, although the theory has quantifier elimination.
- Finite generation of $A$ in (4). Let $N$ be the algebraic closure of $\Q(s_1, s_2, \dots)$, which is $\omega$-saturated, and let $M$ be the algebraic closure of $N(t)$. The identity of $A = N$ has no extension to $\langle N, t \rangle$, since $N$ has no element transcendental over itself.

### Consequences

- A theory with quantifier elimination is [[model complete|YR6HCMABTE]], since embeddings preserve quantifier-free formulas.
- If $T$ has quantifier elimination and some structure embeds into every model of $T$, then $T$ is complete. The ring $\Z$ embeds into every field of characteristic $0$, so quantifier elimination for algebraically closed fields gives completeness of each $\mathrm{ACF}_0$.
- Condition (4) says that the isomorphisms between finitely generated substructures of two $\omega$-saturated models form a [[back-and-forth system|6F2DB4HTJR]].

### Converse

All four conditions are equivalent, so each implication has a true converse.
The proof of (3) $\Rightarrow$ (2) also works for one formula: if the truth of $\theta(\bar a)$ depends only on the substructure that contains $\bar a$, then $\theta$ has a quantifier-free equivalent.

### Generalizations

- In (4) one can take $N$ $\kappa$-saturated and $A$ of size less than $\kappa$, for any infinite cardinal $\kappa$.
- The proof of (3) $\Rightarrow$ (2) works for any set $\Delta$ of formulas closed under $\wedge$ and $\vee$. A formula $\theta$ is equivalent modulo $T$ to a formula in $\Delta$ when the following holds: if $\bar b$ in $N$ satisfies every formula of $\Delta$ that $\bar a$ satisfies in $M$, and $M \models \theta(\bar a)$, then $N \models \theta(\bar b)$.
- With $\Delta$ the existential formulas, the same kind of argument leads to the [[Łoś-Tarski theorem|9RN0KYXNT1]] and to [[Robinson's test|~3847820a]] for model completeness.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To show that every question with "there is" can be answered without searching, it is enough to look at one unknown at a time.
The question is then a system of simple conditions on one unknown $y$, with known numbers in it.
The embedding test says: if one model of the theory has a solution, every other model that contains the same known numbers also has one.
For example, the equation $y^2 = 2$ has a solution in the complex numbers.
So it has a solution in every algebraically closed field that contains the integers, for example in the field of algebraic numbers.

### L2 human

We use the embedding test to prove quantifier elimination for the [[theory of infinite sets|~7a954c5b]].

1. Let $M, N$ be infinite sets with a common subset $A$, and let $\bar a$ be in $A$.
2. Let $\varphi(\bar a, y)$ be a conjunction of literals, and let $b \in M$ satisfy it.
3. If $\varphi$ contains $y = a_i$, then $b = a_i$, and $c = a_i$ is a solution in $N$.
4. Otherwise $\varphi$ says that $y \ne a_j$ for some $j$, together with conditions on $\bar a$ only. The conditions on $\bar a$ have the same truth value in $M$ and $N$.
5. Since $N$ is infinite, some $c \in N$ differs from all $a_j$, and $c$ is a solution in $N$.

The typical use of the test is to prove quantifier elimination without writing the quantifier-free formula.
A trap is to forget the constant symbol: the empty signature has none, so sentences need a separate check.
For infinite sets the check holds, because the theory is complete.

### L3 human

The tests turn a syntactic property into a property of embeddings.
Condition (4) is a one-step extension property, as in the definition of [[ultrahomogeneity|H8VM37SSAX]], but in an $\omega$-saturated model instead of in one countable structure.
This explains why the proofs for algebraically closed fields, real closed fields and Presburger arithmetic all follow one pattern.
In each case one adds one element $b$ to a finitely generated substructure, describes the substructure $\langle A, b \rangle$ by algebra, and finds a copy of $b$ in the saturated model.
The same pattern, with the existential formulas in place of the quantifier-free formulas, gives model completeness and the theory of [[model companions|~7269c0e3]].
