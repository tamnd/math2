## Example

Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a [[vector space|000AYPA9AJ]] over $F$ with an infinite [[basis|0KCQ9Y3QKA]] $B$.
View $V$ as a [[structure|983GZBQYT4]] in the signature $L_F = \set{+, -, 0} \cup \set{f_\lambda : \lambda \in F}$, where $f_\lambda(v) = \lambda v$.
List $B$ without repetitions as $(b_i)_{i \in I}$.
We show that $(b_i)_{i \in I}$ is an [[indiscernible set|~cc1977c7]] over $\emptyset$.

1. Let $\bar{\imath} = (i_1, \dots, i_n)$ and $\bar{\jmath} = (j_1, \dots, j_n)$ be tuples of pairwise distinct indices. The map $b_{i_k} \mapsto b_{j_k}$ is a bijection between two subsets of $B$ with $n$ elements.
2. Both complements in $B$ are infinite and have the same cardinality as $B$, so the bijection extends to a permutation $\pi$ of $B$.
3. A linear map is fixed by its values on a basis, so $\pi$ extends to a unique linear map $\sigma \colon V \to V$. The inverse permutation extends to the inverse map, so $\sigma$ is a linear bijection.
4. A linear bijection preserves $+$, $-$, $0$ and every $f_\lambda$, so $\sigma$ is an [[automorphism|DSQ17TNW1C]] of the $L_F$-structure $V$.
5. Automorphisms preserve the truth of every formula. So $V \models \varphi(b_{\bar{\imath}})$ exactly when $V \models \varphi(\sigma(b_{\bar{\imath}}))$, that is, when $V \models \varphi(b_{\bar{\jmath}})$.

So every two tuples of distinct basis vectors of the same length have the same type, and $B$ is an indiscernible set.

## Remarks

The example uses only that every permutation of $B$ is induced by an automorphism.
The same argument works for a finite basis with at least $n$ elements, for formulas in $n$ variables, since a bijection between subsets of a finite set of the same size also extends to a permutation.
Let $B_0 \subseteq B$ be finite and let $A$ be a subset of the span of $B_0$.
Then $B \setminus B_0$ is an indiscernible set over $A$, since permutations of $B$ that fix $B_0$ give automorphisms that fix the span of $B_0$ pointwise.
The family is no longer indiscernible over $A = \set{b_{i_0}}$ if it contains $b_{i_0}$, since the formula $x = b_{i_0}$ separates $b_{i_0}$ from the other members.
A [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]] set that is not a basis is also indiscernible, since it extends to a basis.
In the language of index structures, the basis is a family indexed by a set with no structure, so it is an example of [[generalized indiscernibles|~9c5e0903]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the plane, the vectors $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ are a basis.
Swapping the two coordinates is a symmetry of the plane that swaps the two vectors.
So every property of $(1, 0)$ that uses only addition and scalar multiplication is also a property of $(0, 1)$.
In a space with infinitely many basis vectors, the same is true for any finite group of them.

### L2 human

We compare $(b_1, b_2)$ and $(b_2, b_1)$ in the rational vector space $\Q^{(\N)}$ with basis $b_0, b_1, b_2, \dots$.

1. The permutation $\pi$ swaps $b_1$ and $b_2$ and fixes the other basis vectors.
2. Its linear extension $\sigma$ maps $(q_0, q_1, q_2, \dots)$ to $(q_0, q_2, q_1, \dots)$.
3. The formula $\varphi(x_1, x_2) := \exists y \, (y + y = x_1 + x_2)$ holds at $(b_1, b_2)$, with $y = \frac{1}{2} b_1 + \frac{1}{2} b_2$.
4. Then $\sigma(y) = y$ witnesses $\varphi(b_2, b_1)$, as step 5 of the example predicts.

The typical use is to see that, in the theory of infinite vector spaces, the basis gives the models with many automorphisms.
The trap is to add a parameter from the span of the basis: then the vectors that occur in its expression are no longer interchangeable with the others.
