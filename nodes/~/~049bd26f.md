## Proof

We follow the method of [[Herbrand analysis|~22b3510a]], and we cite one result without proof.

*Setting.*
Formulate $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ as a [[sequent calculus|W72CA3R9MP]] in the language of PRA.
Its initial sequents are $A \Rightarrow A$ for atomic $A$ and quantifier-free sequents for equality and for the defining equations of PRA.
Its rules are the logical and structural rules of classical sequent calculus, the cut rule, and the $\Sigma_1$ induction rule: from $\Gamma, A(a) \Rightarrow A(a + 1), \Delta$ infer $\Gamma, A(0) \Rightarrow A(t), \Delta$, for $\Sigma_1$ formulas $A$ and $a$ not free in the conclusion.
In this proof a *$\Sigma_1$ formula* is a formula $\exists z\, \chi$ with $\chi$ quantifier-free, or a quantifier-free formula.

*Cited result (C).* Free-cut elimination: a derivation of a sequent of $\Sigma_1$ formulas can be transformed into a derivation of the same sequent in which every cut formula is $\Sigma_1$.
Full [[cut elimination|AYRZ98Y0NW]] is not available, because cuts on induction formulas cannot be removed.

*Structure.*

1. Assume $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1 \vdash \forall x\, \exists y\, \varphi(x, y)$. Then $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1 \vdash \exists y\, \varphi(a, y)$ for a variable tuple $a$.
2. The sequent calculus is complete for $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$, since the induction rule derives the induction axioms. So it derives $\Rightarrow \exists y\, \varphi(a, y)$.
3. By (C), there is such a derivation in which every cut formula is $\Sigma_1$.
4. Every formula in this derivation is a subformula instance of the end sequent, of an initial sequent, of a cut formula or of an induction formula. All of these are $\Sigma_1$, so every formula in the derivation is $\Sigma_1$.

*Witnessing lemma.* For each sequent $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$ of the derivation, write $\Gamma$ as $\exists u_j\, \gamma_j$ and $\Delta$ as $\exists v_i\, \theta_i$, with dummy quantifiers for quantifier-free formulas.
Then there are primitive recursive $f_i$ such that PRA proves $\bigwedge_j \gamma_j(w_j) \to \bigvee_i \theta_i(f_i(w))$, with all free variables of the sequent as extra arguments.
We prove it by induction on the derivation.

5. Initial sequents: all their formulas are quantifier-free, so the dummy witnesses work, and PRA proves the sequent.
6. A propositional rule has a quantifier-free principal formula, since a $\Sigma_1$ formula with a quantifier is not a conjunction, disjunction, implication or negation. The side formulas keep their witnesses.
7. Two-premise rules give witnesses $f_i$ and $f'_i$ for $\Delta$, and a [[case distinction|0WCP0PVET6]] chooses one. For $\wedge$ on the right, from $\Gamma \Rightarrow A, \Delta$ and $\Gamma \Rightarrow B, \Delta$, take $f_i$ if $A$ fails and $f'_i$ otherwise.
8. If $A \wedge B$ fails, then $A$ fails and the first premise gives $\bigvee_i \theta_i(f_i)$, or $B$ fails and the second gives $\bigvee_i \theta_i(f'_i)$. The left rules for $\vee$ and $\to$ are similar.
9. Weakening on the right adds a formula with witness $0$. Contraction on the left uses one input witness twice.
10. Contraction on the right of $\exists v\, \theta$ with witnesses $f$ and $f'$ uses $g = f$ if $\theta(f)$ holds, and $g = f'$ otherwise.
11. Right $\exists$ rule, from $\theta(t)$ to $\exists v\, \theta(v)$: the witness is the term $t$.
12. Left $\exists$ rule, from $\gamma(b)$ to $\exists u\, \gamma(u)$ with eigenvariable $b$: substitute the input witness $w$ for $b$ in the functions of the premise.
13. Cut on $C = \exists z\, \chi$: the left premise gives $c$ with $\chi(c)$ or $\bigvee \theta_i(f_i)$, and the right premise gives $f'_i(w)$ from a witness $w$ of $C$. Take $g_i = f'_i(c)$ if $\chi(c)$ holds, and $g_i = f_i$ otherwise.
14. Induction rule with $A(a) = \exists z\, \chi(z, a)$: the premise gives $g(a, w)$ with $\chi(w, a) \to \chi(g(a, w), a + 1) \vee \bigvee_i \theta_i(f_i(a, w))$.
15. Define $h(0) = w_0$, the input witness for $A(0)$, and $h(b + 1) = g(b, h(b))$, by [[primitive recursion|5NVA2JRHPV]].
16. Let $c^*$ be the least $c < t$ with $\neg \chi(h(c + 1), c + 1)$, found by bounded search. If there is no such $c$, the witness for $A(t)$ is $h(t)$.
17. Otherwise $\chi(h(c^*), c^*)$ holds, by quantifier-free induction in PRA, and $\chi(g(c^*, h(c^*)), c^* + 1)$ fails. So the premise at $a = c^*$ gives $\bigvee_i \theta_i(f_i(c^*, h(c^*)))$, and these are the witnesses for $\Delta$.
18. Bounded search, definition by cases and primitive recursion preserve primitive recursiveness, so all $f_i$ are primitive recursive.

*Conclusion.*

19. Apply the lemma to the end sequent $\Rightarrow \exists y\, \varphi(a, y)$, with empty $\Gamma$.
20. It gives a primitive recursive $f$ with $\mathsf{PRA} \vdash \varphi(a, f(a))$, as claimed.

## Idea

After free-cut elimination, a proof of a $\Sigma_1$ formula contains only $\Sigma_1$ formulas.
Such a proof is read as a program: each $\exists$ carries a witness, a cut composes programs, and an induction becomes a primitive recursion.
The only result used without proof is free-cut elimination.

## Variants

- Parsons, Mints and Takeuti proved the theorem independently, before this proof by Herbrand analysis; this outline does not follow their proofs.
- The [[Dialectica interpretation|HZ17ES9PDY]] gives another route: it interprets $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ in the fragment of [[system T|~62fef358]] with the recursor $R_0$ only, which defines exactly the primitive recursive functions.
- There are also model-theoretic proofs. They start from a model of PRA with an element $a$ such that $\varphi(a, t(a))$ fails for every primitive recursive term $t$, and build a model of $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ in which $\exists y\, \varphi(a, y)$ fails.
