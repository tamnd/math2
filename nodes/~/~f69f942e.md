## Proof

We check the axioms of an [[associative algebra|1TZSV19D85]] one at a time, then compare with matrices.

1. By the [[vector space theorem|~dbc9db14]] with $W = V$, $\End(V)$ is a vector space over $F$.
2. By the [[theorem on composites|~ed2e194c]], $S \circ T$ is linear for $S, T \in \End(V)$. So composition is an operation on $\End(V)$.
3. Associativity. For $v \in V$, $((RS)T)(v) = R(S(T(v))) = (R(ST))(v)$.
4. Unit. $\id_V$ is linear, and $\id_V(T(v)) = T(v) = T(\id_V(v))$ for each $v$.
5. Right distributivity. $((R + S)T)(v) = R(T(v)) + S(T(v)) = (RT + ST)(v)$, by the definition of the sum.
6. Left distributivity. $R$ is additive, so $(R(S + T))(v) = R(S(v) + T(v)) = R(S(v)) + R(T(v))$.
7. The right side of step 6 is $(RS + RT)(v)$. So $R(S + T) = RS + RT$.
8. Scalars. $(a(ST))(v) = a\,S(T(v))$, and $((aS)T)(v) = a\,S(T(v))$ by the definition of $aS$.
9. $S$ is homogeneous, so $(S(aT))(v) = S(a\,T(v)) = a\,S(T(v))$. So $a(ST) = (aS)T = S(aT)$.
10. Steps 1 to 9 prove part 1.
11. For part 2, let $B = (b_1, \dots, b_n)$ and $\mu(T) = [T]_B$. Column $j$ of $[T]_B$ holds the coordinates of $T(b_j)$.
12. $\mu$ is linear: the coordinates of $(S + aT)(b_j)$ are those of $S(b_j)$ plus $a$ times those of $T(b_j)$.
13. $\mu$ is bijective. For each matrix $A$, the [[theorem on bases|0R8X9XA83A]] gives exactly one $T$ with $T(b_j) = \sum_i a_{ij} b_i$.
14. $\mu(ST) = \mu(S)\,\mu(T)$, since the [[matrix of a composite is the product|05Q1B7MH0M]] of the matrices.
15. $\mu(\id_V) = I_n$, since $\id_V(b_j) = b_j$. So $\mu$ is an algebra isomorphism, which proves part 2.

## Idea

Each algebra axiom is an identity between maps.
Evaluate both sides at a vector $v$, and use the definitions of sum, scalar multiple and composite.
Linearity of the left factor is used exactly twice: for left distributivity and for $S(aT) = a(ST)$.

## Variants

- For finite-dimensional $V$, one can prove part 2 first and transfer the axioms from $M_n(F)$. This needs the algebra axioms for matrices, proved directly from the entries.
- Associativity of matrix multiplication can be proved the other way round, from step 3 and part 2. This is the [[proof by composition|KC9SCPKS2G]].
