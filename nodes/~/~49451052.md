## Proof

We write $x \le y$ for $x < y \lor x = y$.
We use one standard fact without proof.

**Fact A.** $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ proves that $<$ is a discrete linear order with least element $0$, and that $x < Sx$.
It proves that $+$ and $\cdot$ are commutative, associative and monotone: $x \le y \to x + z \le y + z$ and $x \le y \to x \cdot z \le y \cdot z$.
Each law follows by open induction, as in [[this example|GJ9KW45F3C]].

Let $T$ and $\varphi(\bar x, y)$ be as in the statement, with $\bar x = (x_1, \dots, x_m)$.
If $T$ is inconsistent, it proves every sentence, and the term $t = 0$ works.
So let $T$ be consistent.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that $T \nvdash \forall \bar x \, \exists y < t(\bar x) \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$ for every term $t(\bar x)$ of $L_A$.

**A model with no small witness.**

1. Add new constants $\bar c = (c_1, \dots, c_m)$ to $L_A$. Let $\Gamma$ be $T$ together with the sentences $\neg \exists y < t(\bar c) \, \varphi(\bar c, y)$, one for each term $t(\bar x)$ of $L_A$.
2. Let $\Gamma_0 \subseteq \Gamma$ be finite. It contains the new sentences for finitely many terms $t_1, \dots, t_k$. Let $s = t_1 + \dots + t_k$, and let $s = 0$ when $k = 0$.
3. By the assumption, $T \nvdash \forall \bar x \, \exists y < s(\bar x) \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$. By the [[completeness theorem|WQ1ZE255SF]] there is a model $M' \models T$ with elements $\bar a'$ such that $M' \models \neg \exists y < s(\bar a') \, \varphi(\bar a', y)$.
4. By Fact A, $M' \models t_i(\bar a') \le s(\bar a')$ for each $i$. So every $y < t_i(\bar a')$ satisfies $y < s(\bar a')$, by transitivity, and fails $\varphi(\bar a', y)$.
5. So $M'$, with $c_j$ read as $a'_j$, is a model of $\Gamma_0$.
6. By steps 2 to 5, every finite subset of $\Gamma$ has a model. The [[compactness theorem|1NR3HAWG9D]] gives a model $M$ of $\Gamma$. Let $\bar a$ be the values of $\bar c$ in $M$.

**The cut.**

7. Let $I = \set{b \in M : M \models b < t(\bar a) \text{ for some term } t(\bar x) \text{ of } L_A}$.
8. $I$ is closed downward: if $d < b$ and $b < t(\bar a)$, then $d < t(\bar a)$ by transitivity (Fact A).
9. $0 \in I$, since $0 < S0$. Each $a_j \in I$, since $a_j < S a_j$ and $S x_j$ is a term.
10. $I$ is closed under $S$, $+$ and $\cdot$. Let $b < t(\bar a)$ and $b' < t'(\bar a)$. By Fact A, $Sb \le t(\bar a) < S t(\bar a)$, $b + b' < t(\bar a) + t'(\bar a)$ and $b \cdot b' \le t(\bar a) \cdot t'(\bar a) < S(t(\bar a) \cdot t'(\bar a))$.
11. By steps 9 and 10, $I$ is the domain of a [[substructure|676H7EGTKJ]] of $M$ for $L_A$, with $<$ restricted to $I$. We also call this substructure $I$. For every term $r$ and $\bar b \in I$, the value of $r(\bar b)$ is the same in $I$ and in $M$.

**Absoluteness of $\Delta_0$ formulas.**

12. Claim: for every $\Delta_0$ formula $\theta(\bar z)$ and every $\bar b \in I$, $I \models \theta(\bar b)$ if and only if $M \models \theta(\bar b)$. We prove the claim by [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]] on $\theta$.
13. Atomic $\theta$: the values of terms agree by step 11, and $<$ on $I$ is the restriction of $<$ on $M$.
14. $\theta = \neg \theta_1$ or $\theta = \theta_1 \land \theta_2$: the claim for $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ gives the claim for $\theta$.
15. $\theta = \exists u < r(\bar z) \, \theta_1(u, \bar z)$, with $u$ not in $r$: the value $e = r(\bar b)$ lies in $I$ by step 11. Every $u \in M$ with $u < e$ lies in $I$ by step 8.
16. So the elements below $e$ are the same in $I$ and in $M$. The claim for $\theta_1$, applied to each of them, gives the claim for $\theta$.
17. $\theta = \forall u < r(\bar z) \, \theta_1$: this is equivalent to $\neg \exists u < r(\bar z) \, \neg \theta_1$, so steps 14 to 16 apply.

**The cut is a model of $T$.**

18. Each axiom of $\mathsf{Q}$ follows logically from a $\Pi_1$ sentence that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ proves. The axioms of [[$\mathsf{Q}$|3K1AKGXF8A]] for $S$, $+$ and $\cdot$, except $\mathsf{Q}3$, are already $\Pi_1$. The axiom $\mathsf{Q}3$, $x \ne 0 \to \exists y \, (x = Sy)$, follows from $\forall x \, (x \ne 0 \to \exists y < x \, (x = Sy))$. The model $M$ satisfies this sentence by $\mathsf{Q}3$, since $y < Sy$ by Fact A.
19. The axiom $\mathsf{Q}8$, $x < y \leftrightarrow \exists v \, (v + Sx = y)$, follows from two $\Pi_1$ sentences: $\forall x \, \forall y \, \forall v \, (v + Sx = y \to x < y)$ and $\forall x \, \forall y \, (x < y \to \exists v < Sy \, (v + Sx = y))$. The model $M$ satisfies both, since $v \le v + Sx < S(v + Sx)$ by Fact A.
20. Let $\forall \bar w \, \chi(\bar w)$ be a $\Pi_1$ sentence true in $M$, with $\chi$ in $\Delta_0$. For $\bar b \in I$, $M \models \chi(\bar b)$, so $I \models \chi(\bar b)$ by step 12. So $I \models \forall \bar w \, \chi(\bar w)$.
21. Steps 18 to 20 show that $I$ satisfies the axioms of $\mathsf{Q}$ and the extra $\Pi_1$ axioms of $T$.
22. Induction. Let $\theta(x, \bar z)$ be $\Delta_0$, and let $\bar b \in I$. Assume $I \models \theta(0, \bar b)$ and $I \models \forall x \, (\theta(x, \bar b) \to \theta(Sx, \bar b))$. Suppose $I \models \neg \theta(a, \bar b)$ for some $a \in I$.
23. Let $\psi(x)$ be $x \le a \to \theta(x, \bar b)$, a $\Delta_0$ formula with parameters $a, \bar b$. Then $M \models \psi(0)$, since $I \models \theta(0, \bar b)$ and step 12.
24. Let $x \in M$ with $M \models \psi(x)$. If $Sx \le a$, then $x < Sx \le a$, so $x \in I$ by step 8. So $M \models \theta(x, \bar b)$.
25. By step 12, $I \models \theta(x, \bar b)$. The assumption of step 22 gives $I \models \theta(Sx, \bar b)$, and step 12 gives $M \models \theta(Sx, \bar b)$. If $Sx \not\le a$, then $\psi(Sx)$ holds as an implication with a false premise.
26. So $M \models \forall x \, (\psi(x) \to \psi(Sx))$. Since $M \models \mathsf{I}\Delta_0$, $M \models \psi(a)$, so $M \models \theta(a, \bar b)$.
27. By step 12, $I \models \theta(a, \bar b)$. This contradicts step 22, so $I \models \forall x \, \theta(x, \bar b)$.
28. By steps 21 to 27, $I \models T$.

**The contradiction.**

29. $T \vdash \forall \bar x \, \exists y \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$ and $I \models T$, with $\bar a \in I$ by step 9. So there is $b \in I$ with $I \models \varphi(\bar a, b)$.
30. By step 12, $M \models \varphi(\bar a, b)$. By step 7, $b < t(\bar a)$ for some term $t$.
31. So $M \models \exists y < t(\bar c) \, \varphi(\bar c, y)$. This contradicts the sentence for $t$ in $\Gamma$, since $M \models \Gamma$.

So the assumption is false, and there is a term $t(\bar x)$ with $T \vdash \forall \bar x \, \exists y < t(\bar x) \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$.
This proves [[Parikh's theorem|~5b376b4b]].

## Idea

Suppose that no term bounds the witness, and use compactness to get a model $M$ with parameters $\bar a$ that have no witness below any term in $\bar a$.
The elements below the terms in $\bar a$ form a cut $I$ of $M$, closed under the operations.
Bounded formulas mean the same in $I$ and in $M$, so $I$ is again a model of $T$, and $I$ has a witness.
That witness lies below a term, which contradicts the choice of $M$.
The model $M$ is a [[nonstandard model of arithmetic|1QWN5CV4CG]] whenever some $a_j$ is nonstandard.

## Variants

- **Proof-theoretic proof.** Write $T$ in a sequent calculus with an induction rule for $\Delta_0$ formulas. Free-cut elimination, a form of the [[cut-elimination theorem|AYRZ98Y0NW]], gives a proof in which every formula is bounded. An induction on this proof then gives the bound term. The same proof works for $S^i_2$ and $T^i_2$.
- **Parikh's original proof.** Parikh gave a proof-theoretic proof based on Herbrand's theorem. He worked in a universal conservative extension of $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ with Skolem functions for $\Delta_0$ formulas.
- **Herbrand-style bound.** For a theory with universal axioms and an open formula $\varphi$, [[Herbrand's theorem|VZ21F10WRS]] gives terms $t_1, \dots, t_k$ with $T \vdash \varphi(\bar x, t_1) \lor \dots \lor \varphi(\bar x, t_k)$. Then $S(t_1 + \dots + t_k)$ is a bound.
