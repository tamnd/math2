## Definition

Let $S$ and $T$ be [[first-order theories|92SWXST1ZM]] in countable languages $L(S)$ and $L(T)$.
We assume that $L(T)$ has relation symbols and function symbols only, with constant symbols counted as $0$-ary function symbols.

A *relative interpretation* $I$ of $T$ in $S$ consists of the following [[formulas|HYJ10AF6RT]] of $L(S)$.

1. A *domain formula* $\delta(x)$ with one free variable.
2. For each $n$-ary relation symbol $R$ of $L(T)$, a formula $\varphi_R(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$.
3. For each $n$-ary function symbol $f$ of $L(T)$, a formula $\varphi_f(x_1, \ldots, x_n, y)$ for the graph of $f$.

The equality symbol of $L(T)$ is translated as the equality symbol of $L(S)$.

The *translation* $A \mapsto A^I$ sends formulas of $L(T)$ to formulas of $L(S)$.
First we unnest each atomic formula, so that it has the form $x = y$, $R(\bar x)$ or $f(\bar x) = y$ with variables only.
Then we define $A^I$ by induction on the formula.

1. $(x = y)^I$ is $x = y$, $R(\bar x)^I$ is $\varphi_R(\bar x)$, and $(f(\bar x) = y)^I$ is $\varphi_f(\bar x, y)$.
2. $(\lnot A)^I = \lnot A^I$ and $(A \land B)^I = A^I \land B^I$, and the same for $\lor$ and $\to$.
3. The quantifiers are relativized to $\delta$:
$$(\forall x \, A)^I = \forall x \, (\delta(x) \to A^I), \qquad (\exists x \, A)^I = \exists x \, (\delta(x) \land A^I).$$

The relative interpretation $I$ is an *interpretation of $T$ in $S$* when $S$ proves the following.

1. $\exists x \, \delta(x)$: the domain is not empty.
2. For each $n$-ary function symbol $f$: $\forall \bar x \, \big(\delta(x_1) \land \cdots \land \delta(x_n) \to \exists! y \, (\delta(y) \land \varphi_f(\bar x, y))\big)$, so $\varphi_f$ defines a total function on the domain.
3. For each axiom $\sigma$ of $T$: the translation $\sigma^I$.

The theory $T$ is *interpretable in* $S$ when an interpretation of $T$ in $S$ exists.
We also say that $S$ *interprets* $T$.

## Notation

We write $S \rhd T$ or $T \lhd S$ for "$S$ interprets $T$".
Some authors write $T \le S$ or $T \trianglelefteq S$ for the same relation.
We write $A^I$ or $A^\tau$ for the translation of $A$, where $\tau$ is the translation map.
In a [[structure|983GZBQYT4]] $\mathcal M$ of $L(S)$, the formulas of $I$ define a set and relations and functions on it; we write $\mathcal M^I$ for this structure of $L(T)$.

## Remarks

For a constant symbol $c$, the formula $\varphi_c(y)$ has one free variable, and condition 2 says that it defines exactly one element of the domain.
Unnesting replaces nested terms by new variables: for example, $f(g(x)) = y$ becomes $\exists u \, (g(x) = u \land f(u) = y)$.

The main property of an interpretation is that it carries theorems to theorems.

**Lemma.** If $I$ interprets $T$ in $S$ and $\sigma$ is a sentence of $L(T)$ with $T \vdash \sigma$, then $S \vdash \sigma^I$.

There are two proofs: item 1 is syntactic, and items 2 to 4 are semantic.

1. Syntactic proof: by induction on a derivation of $\sigma$ in $T$, each formula $A$ of the derivation with free variables $\bar x$ gives a derivation in $S$ of $\delta(\bar x) \to A^I$. The quantifier rules work because the domain is not empty and because each $\varphi_f$ defines a total function.
2. Semantic proof: let $\mathcal M$ be a [[model|MVCH6N9SNW]] of $S$. The set defined by $\delta$ in $\mathcal M$ is not empty, and the formulas of $I$ define on it a structure $\mathcal M^I$ of $L(T)$.
3. By induction on a formula $A$ of $L(T)$: for each tuple $\bar a$ of the domain, $\mathcal M^I \models A[\bar a]$ if and only if $\mathcal M \models A^I[\bar a]$.
4. So $\mathcal M^I$ is a model of $T$ and hence of $\sigma$, and $\mathcal M \models \sigma^I$. Since $\mathcal M$ is arbitrary, the [[completeness theorem|WQ1ZE255SF]] gives $S \vdash \sigma^I$.

A variant of the definition allows equality of $L(T)$ to be translated by a formula $\varepsilon(x, y)$ of $L(S)$.
Then $S$ must prove that $\varepsilon$ is an equivalence relation on the domain and a congruence for each $\varphi_R$ and each $\varphi_f$.
The structure $\mathcal M^I$ is then the quotient of the domain by $\varepsilon$.
Another variant allows the domain to consist of $k$-tuples, with a formula $\delta(x_1, \ldots, x_k)$.
These variants give more interpretations, but the basic facts below hold for them in the same form.

The concept node [[interpretability between theories|~95e26594]] gives the local, global and weak forms of the relation, the link to consistency and the examples.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To interpret one theory in another is to show how to speak the language of the first theory with the words of the second one.
Each word of the first theory gets a definition in the second theory.
The objects of the first theory are some of the objects of the second one, picked out by a fixed property.
The definitions must turn every axiom of the first theory into a theorem of the second one.
For example, the whole numbers can be defined inside set theory: $0$ is the empty set, $1$ is the set $\{0\}$, $2$ is the set $\{0, 1\}$, and so on.

### L2 human

We interpret the theory $T$ of a strict linear order without a greatest element in the theory $S$ of [[Peano arithmetic|0QFT7Z76N5]].

1. The language of $T$ has one binary relation symbol $<$. The axioms say that $<$ is irreflexive, transitive and total, and that for each $x$ there is $y$ with $x < y$.
2. We take the domain formula $\delta(x) := (x = x)$, so the domain is all of the numbers.
3. We translate $x < y$ as the formula $\varphi_<(x, y) := \exists z \, (x + S(z) = y)$ of PA.
4. The translation of "for each $x$ there is $y$ with $x < y$" is $\forall x \, (x = x \to \exists y \, (y = y \land \exists z \, (x + S(z) = y)))$. PA proves it with $y := S(x)$ and $z := 0$.
5. The other three axioms translate to properties of $<$ on the natural numbers that PA proves by induction.

The typical use of an interpretation is a relative consistency proof: a contradiction in $T$ would translate to a contradiction in $S$.
A trap is to forget the relativization of the quantifiers to $\delta$; without it, the translation of an axiom speaks about all objects of $S$, not about the domain.
