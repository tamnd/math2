## Statement

Let $R$ be a [[commutative ring|~79bc52c4]], let $f \in R[x]$ and let $c \in R$.
Then $x - c$ divides $f$ in $R[x]$ if and only if $c$ is a [[root|~4d191b0b]] of $f$.

## Remarks

The proof uses the [[remainder theorem|~f1ef1bde]]: $f = (x - c) q + f(c)$. So $x - c$ divides $f$ exactly when $f(c) = 0$.
For example, $1$ is a root of $x^3 - 1$, and $x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$.
The theorem gives the bound on the [[number of roots|~3cfee977]] of a polynomial over a field.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each root gives a linear factor.
If $f(c) = 0$, then you can write $f$ as $(x - c)$ times another polynomial.
Conversely, a factor $x - c$ makes $f(c) = 0$.
