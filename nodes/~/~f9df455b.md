## Proof

We check the three conditions in the [[definition of a subspace|0EPTJA5QV4]].

1. By the [[lemma on zero|~c10ad39e]], $T(0) = 0$. So $0 \in \ker T$.
2. Let $u, v \in \ker T$. Then $T(u) = 0$ and $T(v) = 0$.
3. Additivity gives $T(u + v) = T(u) + T(v) = 0 + 0 = 0$. So $u + v \in \ker T$.
4. Let $a \in F$ and $v \in \ker T$. Homogeneity gives $T(av) = a\,T(v) = a \cdot 0 = 0$. So $av \in \ker T$.
5. Steps 1, 3 and 4 show that $\ker T$ is a subspace of $V$.
6. For the second claim, let $u, v \in V$. Linearity gives $T(u - v) = T(u) - T(v)$.
7. So $T(u) = T(v)$ holds if and only if $T(u - v) = 0$, which means $u - v \in \ker T$.

## Idea

The kernel is defined by the equation $T(v) = 0$.
The right side is $0$, and $0$ is kept by sums and scalar multiples, so the set of solutions is kept as well.

## Variants

- The kernel is the preimage $T^{-1}(\{0\})$ of the subspace $\{0\}$ of $W$. The preimage of any subspace of $W$ under a linear map is a subspace of $V$, with the same proof.
- In one step: if $u, v \in \ker T$ and $a \in F$, then $T(au + v) = aT(u) + T(v) = 0$.
