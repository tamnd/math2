## Statement

Let $A$ be a square [[transition matrix|0F462493EA]] of a [[Markov chain|0G48PYM96Q]] whose entries are all strictly positive. Each column of $A$ is a probability vector, so its entries sum to $1$.

Order the [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]] of $A$ as $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots$ so that $\lambda_1$ is the largest in absolute value. Then:

1. $\lambda_1 = 1$, and $\abs{\lambda_j} < 1$ for every $j > 1$.
2. There is exactly one steady-state vector $q$ of $A$, that is, one probability vector with $Aq = q$. All entries of $q$ are positive.
3. For every Markov chain $x_{k+1} = A x_k$ defined by $A$, the vectors $x_k$ converge to $q$ as $k \to \infty$.

## Remarks

Part 1 says that $1$ is a simple eigenvalue and the only eigenvalue on the unit circle. Parts 2 and 3 follow from it, because $A^k x_0$ is dominated by the component along the eigenvector of $1$. The limit is the [[stationary distribution|0KRH5157WP]] of the chain.

This is the positive case of the Perron-Frobenius theorem, written for stochastic matrices.
