## Proof

We prove part 4 and one direction of part 3 for the formula algebra; parts 1 and 2, and part 3 in full, are cited from Maksimova, Blok and Hoogland, and Blok and Pigozzi.

**Part 4.** Let $A$ be a [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]], and write $c \leftrightarrow d = (c \to d) \wedge (d \to c)$.

1. For a filter $F$ of $A$, let $\theta_F = \set{(c, d) : c \leftrightarrow d \in F}$. The inequalities $(c \leftrightarrow d) \wedge (d \leftrightarrow e) \le c \leftrightarrow e$ and $(c \leftrightarrow d) \wedge (c' \leftrightarrow d') \le (c \star c') \leftrightarrow (d \star d')$ for $\star \in \set{\wedge, \vee, \to}$ hold in Heyting algebras, so $\theta_F$ is a [[congruence|DCSXXT4SER]].
2. Let $\theta$ be any congruence and $F = \set{c : c \mathrel{\theta} 1}$, a filter. If $c \mathrel{\theta} d$, then $c \leftrightarrow d \mathrel{\theta} d \leftrightarrow d = 1$.
3. Conversely, if $c \leftrightarrow d \mathrel{\theta} 1$, then $c = c \wedge 1 \mathrel{\theta} c \wedge (c \leftrightarrow d) = c \wedge d$, and in the same way $d \mathrel{\theta} c \wedge d$. So $\theta = \theta_F$.
4. By steps 2 and 3, a congruence contains $(a, b)$ iff its filter contains $a \leftrightarrow b$. So the congruence generated by $(a, b)$ is $\theta_F$ for the least such filter, $F = \set{e : e \ge a \leftrightarrow b}$.
5. Hence $(c, d)$ lies in it iff $c \leftrightarrow d \ge a \leftrightarrow b$, which is the equation $(a \leftrightarrow b) \wedge (c \leftrightarrow d) \approx a \leftrightarrow b$.

**Part 3, from a deduction-detachment theorem, on $\mathrm{Fm}$.**

6. Let $\Delta(x, y) = \set{\delta_1, \ldots, \delta_n}$. Iterating the deduction theorem gives a finite set $I^*(\vec{u}, v)$ with $\delta_1, \ldots, \delta_n \vdash \chi$ iff $\vdash I^*(\vec{\delta}, \chi)$.
7. By [[the isomorphism theorem|~59c5f9d4]], part 1, the least $K$-congruence of $\mathrm{Fm}$ containing $(\varphi, \psi)$ corresponds to the theory of $\Delta(\varphi, \psi)$. So $(\gamma, \delta)$ lies in it iff $\Delta(\varphi, \psi) \vdash \Delta(\gamma, \delta)$.
8. By step 6 and (A1), this holds iff $K$ satisfies the equations $E[I^*(\Delta(\varphi, \psi), \chi)]$ for all $\chi \in \Delta(\gamma, \delta)$, which is a finite set of equations in $\varphi, \psi, \gamma, \delta$.
9. The same argument with the filters of $\vdash$ on an arbitrary $A \in K$ in place of theories gives the equational definition on $A$; this extension of [[the isomorphism theorem|~59c5f9d4]] to all algebras is due to Blok and Pigozzi. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

The logical side and the algebraic side are linked by the isomorphism between theories and relative congruences.
A deduction theorem describes the theory generated by one more formula, and this becomes a description of the congruence generated by one more pair.
For interpolation, an interpolant of $\varphi \to \psi$ corresponds to a common subalgebra of two free algebras, and amalgamation over that subalgebra produces it.
For the Beth property, an implicitly defined variable is a new element that every pair of homomorphisms must treat alike, which is an epimorphism; explicit definability makes the epimorphism surjective.

## Variants

1. For Heyting algebras, part 4 can also be read off from the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] of intuitionistic logic and part 1 of the isomorphism theorem.
2. The converse of part 3 builds $I(x, y)$ from the equations $\tau$ with the help of the translations $E$ and $\Delta$; see Blok and Pigozzi.
