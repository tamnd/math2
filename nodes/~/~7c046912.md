## Proof

Let $G = (V, E)$, $N$, $d$ and $\chi$ be as in the statement.
For a set $U \subseteq V$, write $\mathrm{PAR}_U$ for the conjunction of the parity constraints of the vertices in $U$.

*Part 1: width.*

1. *Every proper subset is satisfiable.* Let $U \ne V$, and fix $v_0 \in V \setminus U$. Start with any assignment. For each vertex $u \in U$ whose constraint fails, flip the edges of a path from $u$ to $v_0$. Flipping a path changes the parity at its two ends only. After these flips, every constraint in $U$ holds. So $\mathrm{PAR}_U$ is satisfiable.
2. *A measure.* For a clause $C$, let $\mu(C)$ be the least $\abs{U}$ such that $\mathrm{PAR}_U$ implies $C$.
3. A clause of the constraint of $v$ is implied by $\mathrm{PAR}_{\set{v}}$, so $\mu(C) \le 1$ for every axiom $C$.
4. $\square$ is implied by $\mathrm{PAR}_V$, since $T(G, \chi)$ is unsatisfiable. It is not implied by $\mathrm{PAR}_U$ for $U \ne V$, by step 1. So $\mu(\square) = N$.
5. If $C$ is the resolvent of $A$ and $B$, and $\mathrm{PAR}_{U_A} \models A$ and $\mathrm{PAR}_{U_B} \models B$, then $\mathrm{PAR}_{U_A \cup U_B} \models C$. So $\mu(C) \le \mu(A) + \mu(B)$.
6. Let $\pi$ be a refutation, and let $C$ be the first clause of $\pi$ with $\mu(C) \ge N/3$. It is not an axiom, since $N/3 > 1$. Its premises have $\mu < N/3$, so $\mu(C) < 2N/3$ by step 5.
7. Choose $U$ with $\abs{U} = \mu(C)$ and $\mathrm{PAR}_U \models C$. Then $N/3 \le \abs{U} < 2N/3$.
8. *Boundary edges occur in $C$.* Let $e = \set{u, w}$ be an edge with $u \in U$ and $w \notin U$. By the minimality of $U$, $\mathrm{PAR}_{U \setminus \set{u}}$ does not imply $C$. So some assignment $\alpha$ satisfies every constraint of $U \setminus \set{u}$ and falsifies $C$.
9. $\alpha$ falsifies the constraint of $u$, since otherwise it satisfies $\mathrm{PAR}_U$ and hence $C$.
10. Let $\alpha'$ be $\alpha$ with $x_e$ flipped. The parity changes at $u$ and $w$ only. So $\alpha'$ satisfies the constraint of $u$, and the other constraints of $U$ still hold, since $w \notin U$. So $\alpha'$ satisfies $\mathrm{PAR}_U$ and hence $C$.
11. $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ differ only in $x_e$, and they give $C$ different values. So $x_e$ occurs in $C$.
12. By steps 8 to 11, $C$ contains a literal for each edge of $E(U, V \setminus U)$. So $w(C) \ge e(G)$, and $w(\pi) \ge e(G)$. This proves part 1.

*Part 2: size.*

13. The formula $T(G, \chi)$ has $\abs{E} = dN/2$ variables, and its clauses have width $d$.
14. By the [[size-width relation|~d2afe9c2]] for general resolution, with $w(F) = d$ and $w(F \vdash \square) \ge e(G) \ge d + 1$, every refutation has at least $\exp\bigl((e(G) - d - 1)^2/(8 \cdot dN/2)\bigr)$ clauses.
15. Since $8 \cdot dN/2 = 4dN$, this is the bound of part 2.

*Corollary.*

16. If $e(G_N) \ge \varepsilon N$ and $N \ge 2(d + 1)/\varepsilon$, then $e(G_N) - d - 1 \ge \varepsilon N/2$.
17. So the bound is at least $\exp\bigl(\varepsilon^2 N^2/(16 d N)\bigr) = \exp\bigl(\varepsilon^2 N/(16 d)\bigr) = 2^{\Omega(N)}$, since $d$ and $\varepsilon$ are constants. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

A clause in the middle of a refutation summarizes the constraints of a medium-sized set $U$ of vertices.
It must mention every edge that leaves $U$, because flipping such an edge moves a parity violation out of $U$.
On an expander, every medium-sized set has many leaving edges, so some clause is wide, and wide refutations are long.

## Variants

- Urquhart's original proof of 1987 used a direct counting argument on the refutation, without width.
- The same measure $\mu$, with the pigeons in place of the vertices, gives the clause with a wide monotone transform in the [[proof of Haken's theorem|~8dac2cf0]].
