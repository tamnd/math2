## Example

The signature is $L = \set{+, -, 0}$ with $+$ binary, $-$ unary and $0$ a constant.
Let $M = (\Z, +, -, 0)$.
We show that the [[age|~41dc9926]] of $M$ consists of the trivial group and the groups isomorphic to $\Z$.

1. A [[substructure|676H7EGTKJ]] of $M$ contains $0$ and is closed under $+$ and $-$, so it is a subgroup of $\Z$.
2. Every subgroup of $\Z$ has the form $n\Z$ for some $n \ge 0$, by division with remainder: $n$ is the least positive element, or $n = 0$.
3. The subgroup $n\Z$ is generated by the finite set $\set{n}$, so every substructure of $M$ is [[finitely generated|082DWWXMRJ]].
4. For $n = 0$ we get the trivial group. For $n \ge 1$ the map $k \mapsto nk$ is an isomorphism from $\Z$ onto $n\Z$.
5. So every member of $\operatorname{Age}(M)$ is trivial or isomorphic to $\Z$, and both types occur.

## Remarks

The example shows that an age can contain infinite structures when the signature has function symbols.
The age has HP and JEP, as every age does: each member embeds into $\Z$, which is itself in the age.
For $M = (\Q, +, -, 0)$, the age becomes the class of finitely generated subgroups of $\Q$, which are again trivial or infinite cyclic.
So $\Z$ and $\Q$ have the same age as groups, although they are not isomorphic.
For $M = (\Z^2, +, -, 0)$ the age also contains $\Z^2$, a group of rank $2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Inside the integers, start with one number, say $6$, and keep adding and subtracting.
One gets all multiples of $6$, which look exactly like the integers, only spread out.
Every piece that a finite set generates looks like this, or is just $\set{0}$.
So the age of the integers has only two kinds of members, and one of them is infinite.

### L2 human

We find the substructure generated by $\set{4, 6}$.

1. It contains $4 - 6 = -2$, hence $2 = -(-2)$.
2. It contains all multiples of $2$, by adding $2$ or $-2$ repeatedly.
3. Every element built from $4$ and $6$ is even, so the substructure is $2\Z$.
4. The map $k \mapsto 2k$ is an isomorphism from $\Z$ onto $2\Z$.

The typical use is to see the difference between "finitely generated" and "finite" in an age.
A trap is to drop $-$ from the signature: then $(\N, +, 0)$ is a substructure of $(\Z, +, 0)$, and the age contains monoids that are not groups.
