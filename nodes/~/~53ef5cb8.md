## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} i & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. By the [[definition|0EX045E2BP]], $A^* = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -i & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B^* = \begin{pmatrix} -i & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \cdot i + i \cdot 1 & 1 \cdot 1 + i \cdot 0 \\ 0 \cdot i + 2 \cdot 1 & 0 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2i & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. So $(AB)^* = \begin{pmatrix} -2i & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. $B^* A^* = \begin{pmatrix} -i \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot (-i) & -i \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 2 \\ 1 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot (-i) & 1 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2i & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, the same matrix.
5. $(iA)^* = \begin{pmatrix} i & -1 \\ 0 & 2i \end{pmatrix}^* = \begin{pmatrix} -i & 0 \\ -1 & -2i \end{pmatrix} = -i A^*$, not $i A^*$.

## Remarks

Step 4 checks $(AB)^* = B^* A^*$ in one case.
Step 5 shows that scalars come out conjugated.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each entry moves to its mirror position and changes the sign of its imaginary part.
The rule for products reverses the order, as for the transpose.

### L2 human

The product rule needs $\overline{zw} = \overline{z}\,\overline{w}$, which holds because $\mathbb{C}$ is commutative.
Conjugation is a ring automorphism of $\mathbb{C}$, so it passes through all sums and products of entries.
