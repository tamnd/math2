## Proof

1. By the [[definition of a Jordan block|0PBFS92AJP]], column $i$ of $J$ has $\lambda$ in row $i$ and, for $i \ge 2$, $1$ in row $i - 1$. So $J e_i = \lambda e_i + e_{i-1}$ for $i \ge 2$ and $J e_1 = \lambda e_1$. Subtracting $\lambda e_i$ gives $N e_1 = 0$ and $N e_i = e_{i-1}$.
2. By induction on $j$, $N^j e_i = e_{i-j}$ if $i > j$ and $N^j e_i = 0$ if $i \le j$. The step is $N^{j+1} e_i = N e_{i-j} = e_{i-j-1}$ when $i - j \ge 2$, and $0$ otherwise.
3. So column $i + j$ of $N^j$ is $e_i$ for $1 \le i \le k - j$, and the first $j$ columns are $0$. This is the pattern in part 1. The nonzero columns $e_1, \dots, e_{k-j}$ are independent, so $\operatorname{rank} N^j = k - j$, and $N^k = 0$.
4. The matrices $\lambda I$ and $N$ commute. By the [[binomial theorem for commuting matrices|DS9TS6AFMW]], $J^m = (\lambda I + N)^m = \sum_{j=0}^{m} \binom{m}{j} \lambda^{m-j} N^j$. The terms with $j \ge k$ vanish by step 3. Reading off entry $(i, i + j)$ with step 3 proves part 2.
5. Part 3. Substituting $J$ for $x$ is a ring homomorphism $F[x] \to M_k(F)$, by the [[evaluation homomorphism|0FCCDXH97W]]. It sends $x - \lambda$ to $N$. So $f(J) = \sum_j b_j N^j$, and the terms with $j \ge k$ vanish by step 3.
6. The matrices $I, N, \dots, N^{k-1}$ are linearly independent, because by step 3 their nonzero entries lie on different diagonals.
7. Part 4, minimal polynomial. By step 5, $(x - \lambda)^k$ kills $J$. If $f(J) = 0$, then by steps 5 and 6, $b_0 = \cdots = b_{k-1} = 0$, so $(x - \lambda)^k$ divides $f$. So $(x - \lambda)^k$ is the monic polynomial of least degree that kills $J$, and it is the minimal polynomial by the [[divisibility property|0VTAD25A3G]].
8. Part 4, characteristic polynomial. $xI - J$ is upper triangular with all diagonal entries $x - \lambda$. The [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]] of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, so $\det(xI - J) = (x - \lambda)^k$.
9. Part 5. The eigenvalues are the roots of the characteristic polynomial, so $\lambda$ is the only one. By the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]] and step 3, $\dim \ker N^j = k - (k - j) = j$ for $j \le k$, and $\dim \ker N^j = k$ for $j \ge k$ since $N^j = 0$. For $j = 1$, $\ker N = F e_1$ by step 1.
10. Part 6. By step 2, $N^j e_k = e_{k-j}$ for $0 \le j \le k - 1$. These vectors are $e_k, \dots, e_1$, so they span $F^k$. Since $J = \lambda I + N$ and $N = J - \lambda I$, each $J^j e_k$ is a combination of the $N^i e_k$ with $i \le j$, and conversely. So $e_k, J e_k, \dots, J^{k-1} e_k$ also span $F^k$.

## Idea

Split $J$ into the scalar part $\lambda I$ and the shift $N$.
They commute, and $N$ dies after $k$ steps, so every power and every polynomial of $J$ is a short sum in the powers of $N$.

## Variants

In characteristic $0$, part 3 is the Taylor formula $f(J) = \sum_{j < k} \frac{f^{(j)}(\lambda)}{j!} N^j$.
Part 4 also follows from part 6: a cyclic matrix has equal minimal and characteristic polynomials.
