## Statement

Let $\mathsf{ML}$ be [[Medvedev's logic|~6aab2c7f]], the set of formulas valid on every Medvedev frame $M_n$.
For all propositional formulas $\varphi$ and $\psi$, if $\varphi \vee \psi \in \mathsf{ML}$, then $\varphi \in \mathsf{ML}$ or $\psi \in \mathsf{ML}$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The statement has no hypothesis on $\varphi$ and $\psi$, but it depends on the class of frames.
For the class of all finite rooted frames with a greatest world, the logic is $\mathsf{KC}$, by [[the theorem on weak excluded middle|CX032ACTMZ]], and $\neg p \vee \neg \neg p \in \mathsf{KC}$ while neither disjunct is.
The proof needs that any two frames of the class sit as disjoint generated subframes of a rooted frame of the class.

### Consequences

1. [[Weak excluded middle is not in ML|~09d77956]], since $\neg p \notin \mathsf{ML}$ and $\neg \neg p \notin \mathsf{ML}$.
2. $\mathsf{ML}$ is a proper intermediate logic with the [[disjunction property|~59f11dc8]], strictly above [[Kreisel-Putnam logic|~496da4a3]].

### Converse

The converse implication holds in every logic: if $\varphi \in \mathsf{ML}$, then $\varphi \vee \psi \in \mathsf{ML}$, since $\varphi \to \varphi \vee \psi$ is in $\mathsf{IPC}$.

### Generalizations

The same gluing argument works for every class of finite rooted frames in which two frames are always generated subframes of a third, disjoint and rooted.
By a theorem of Maksimova, cited without proof, $\mathsf{ML}$ is the largest extension of the weak Kreisel-Putnam logic with the disjunction property.

## History

The disjunction property is part of the basic theory of Medvedev's logic.
The proof given here follows Přenosil's 2024 note on the theorems of Levin and Prucnal.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose the logic proves "$A$ or $B$".
If neither $A$ nor $B$ were provable, there would be a puzzle where $A$ can fail and another puzzle where $B$ can fail.
Put the two puzzles side by side as one puzzle with all the candidates of both.
At the start, both $A$ and $B$ can still fail, so "$A$ or $B$" is not settled, which is impossible.

### L2 human

1. $\neg p$ fails on $M_1$ when $p$ is true, so $\neg p \notin \mathsf{ML}$.
2. $\neg \neg p$ fails on $M_1$ when $p$ is false, so $\neg \neg p \notin \mathsf{ML}$.
3. Glue two copies of $M_1$: the candidates are $1$ and $2$, giving $M_2$, the fork.
4. Let $p$ be true at $\set{1}$ only. At the root, $\neg p$ fails because of $\set{1}$, and $\neg \neg p$ fails because of $\set{2}$.
5. So $\neg p \vee \neg \neg p \notin \mathsf{ML}$.

The typical use is to show that a disjunction is not a theorem by refuting each disjunct separately.
A trap: the root of the glued frame is a new world, and its valuation must be the one forced by persistence; here no atom is true at the root.

### L3 human

The property is the frame-theoretic counterpart of the gluing argument for $\mathsf{IPC}$ in [[the disjunction property|~59f11dc8]].
For $\mathsf{IPC}$ one adds a new root below two arbitrary models; here the new root comes for free, as the set of all candidates of both frames.
The disjunction property also holds for $\mathsf{KP}$ and Scott's logic, but there the proofs are harder.
For $\mathsf{KP}$, gluing a root below two of its frames may give a frame that refutes the Kreisel-Putnam axiom.
