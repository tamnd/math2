## Statement

Let $T$ be a [[theory|92SWXST1ZM]] in a signature $L$.
A [[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] is *existential* if it has the form $\exists \bar y \, \chi$ and *universal* if it has the form $\forall \bar y \, \chi$, with $\chi$ quantifier-free.
For [[models|MVCH6N9SNW]] $M \subseteq N$, write $M \preceq_1 N$ if every existential formula with parameters in $M$ that is true in $N$ is true in $M$.
The following are equivalent.

1. $T$ is [[model complete|ERVV6RWNNX]].
2. For all models $M, N$ of $T$ with $M$ a [[substructure|676H7EGTKJ]] of $N$, we have $M \preceq_1 N$.
3. Every formula $\varphi(\bar x)$ is equivalent modulo $T$ to a universal formula $\psi(\bar x)$.
4. Every formula $\varphi(\bar x)$ is equivalent modulo $T$ to an existential formula $\psi(\bar x)$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- Condition 2 asks for $M \preceq_1 N$ for all inclusions of models. One inclusion is not enough: for the theory of the group $\Z$, the inclusion of $\Z$ into itself is elementary, but [[the inclusion of the even integers|~4e820942]] is not.
- Condition 2 concerns only inclusions between models of $T$. For the inclusion of fields $\Q \subseteq \C$, the condition $\Q \preceq_1 \C$ fails, since $y^2 = 2$ has a solution only in $\C$. This does not contradict the model completeness of algebraically closed fields, since $\Q$ is not algebraically closed.
- In conditions 3 and 4, the equivalent formula must have the same free variables. Otherwise one could take $\psi = \varphi$ with extra variables, and the conditions would say nothing.

### Consequences

- A theory with [[quantifier elimination|7FDDXV3JK7]] is model complete, since quantifier-free formulas are both existential and universal.
- [[Real closed fields in the ring signature|~a3bccf2c]] are model complete, because the order and its negation are existentially definable.
- A model complete theory is axiomatized by its $\forall\exists$ consequences, so it is [[inductive|~719ccde0]].
- Condition 2 says that each model of $T$ is an [[existentially closed model|~524465b6]] of $T$. So $T$ is model complete exactly when all its models are existentially closed.

### Converse

The four conditions are equivalent, so every converse holds.
The weaker condition "every existential formula is equivalent to a universal formula" also implies condition 2.
An existential formula true in $N$ is then equivalent to a universal formula, and universal formulas with parameters in $M$ go down from $N$ to $M$.

### Generalizations

Lindström's test gives model completeness from categoricity.
Let $T$ be a theory in a countable signature with only infinite models, axiomatized by $\forall\exists$ sentences, and $\kappa$-categorical for some infinite cardinal $\kappa$.
Then $T$ is model complete.

1. Since $T$ is $\forall\exists$, unions of chains of models are models. A chain construction gives an existentially closed model of size $\kappa$.
2. By $\kappa$-categoricity, every model of size $\kappa$ is existentially closed.
3. Suppose that $M \subseteq N$ are models, and an existential formula with parameters $\bar a$ in $M$ is true in $N$ and false in $M$.
4. Apply compactness and the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem to $N$ with a new predicate for $M$ and constants for $\bar a$. This gives models $M' \subseteq N'$ of size $\kappa$ with the same property.
5. Step 4 contradicts step 2, so condition 2 holds.

For example, the theory of algebraically closed fields of a fixed characteristic and the theory of infinite vector spaces over a fixed countable field satisfy Lindström's test.

## History

Abraham Robinson introduced model completeness and the test that bears his name.
The criterion by categoricity is due to Per Lindström.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To show that a small model agrees with a large one on every question, it is enough to check the questions of the form "is there a solution".
If such a question has a solution in the large model, it must already have one in the small model.
For example, if a system of polynomial equations with real algebraic coefficients has a real solution, it has a real algebraic solution.

### L2 human

We use Robinson's test to show that the theory of [[infinite sets|~7a954c5b]] is model complete, without the use of quantifier elimination.

1. Let $M \subseteq N$ be infinite sets, and let $\exists \bar y \, \chi(\bar a, \bar y)$ be true in $N$, with $\chi$ quantifier-free and $\bar a$ in $M$.
2. Let $\bar b$ in $N$ be a witness. The formula $\chi$ only says which entries of $(\bar a, \bar b)$ are equal.
3. Replace each $b_j$ that is not equal to some $a_i$ by a new element of $M$. This is possible since $M$ is infinite. Equal entries get the same new element, and distinct entries get distinct new elements.
4. The new tuple satisfies the same equations, so it is a witness in $M$. By the test, the theory is model complete.

The typical use of the test is to prove model completeness by an algebraic argument about solutions of systems.
A trap is to check only existential sentences without parameters: the parameters $\bar a$ from $M$ are part of the condition.

### L3 human

Robinson's test is the existential version of the [[embedding test for quantifier elimination|~e415d689]].
The quantifier-free formulas there are replaced here by existential formulas, and common substructures by inclusions of models.
The test explains why model completeness is the right notion for the theory of existentially closed structures.
A theory $T_0$ has a model companion when the class of its existentially closed models is elementary, and then the test shows that the theory of this class is model complete.
Lindström's test shows that categoricity, a property of the models, often gives model completeness, a property of the embeddings.
