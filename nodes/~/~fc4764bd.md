## Summary

$\mathsf{GLP}$ is a propositional modal logic with one box $[n]$ for each natural number $n$, with $\langle n \rangle \varphi := \neg [n] \neg \varphi$.
Its axioms are those of $\mathsf{GL}$ for each $[n]$, the schema $[m] \varphi \to [n] \varphi$ for $m \leq n$, and the schema $\langle m \rangle \varphi \to [n] \langle m \rangle \varphi$ for $m < n$; its rules are modus ponens and necessitation for $[0]$.
The box $[0]$ is read as provability in $\mathsf{PA}$, and $[n]$ as a stronger provability notion, such as provability with $n$ nested uses of the $\omega$-rule.
Japaridze proved that $\mathsf{GLP}$ is arithmetically complete for this reading, as $\mathsf{GL}$ is for $\mathsf{PA}$ by [[Solovay's theorem|~5cc205a1]].
It matters because it is not complete for any class of Kripke frames, and because Beklemishev used it for a modal [[ordinal analysis|C5E5TG8PGP]] of $\mathsf{PA}$.

## Motivation

[[Provability logic|WJK0YA4GP2]] with one box describes one theory.
Proof theory compares theories of different strength, such as $\mathsf{PA}$ and $\mathsf{PA}$ with true $\Pi_1$ sentences or with [[reflection principles|NB485KFXKV]].
$\mathsf{GLP}$ describes a whole hierarchy of such provability notions at once.
Its consistency formulas $\langle n \rangle \top$ express $n$-consistency, a strengthening of consistency related to [[ω-consistency|4S29X1GTV4]] and to the [[arithmetical hierarchy|7CVJCQKY41]].

## Notation

1. $[n] \varphi$ is read "$\varphi$ is $n$-provable", and $\langle n \rangle \varphi$ is read "$\varphi$ is $n$-consistent".
2. A worm is a formula $\langle n_1 \rangle \langle n_2 \rangle \cdots \langle n_k \rangle \top$; the empty worm is $\top$.
3. $\langle n \rangle^k \varphi$ is $\langle n \rangle$ applied $k$ times to $\varphi$.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[The mixing lemma of GLP|~2dcd798f]]: for $m < n$, $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash \langle n \rangle A \wedge \langle m \rangle B \to \langle n \rangle (A \wedge \langle m \rangle B)$.
2. [[1-consistency implies iterated consistency in GLP|~9f426720]]: $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash \langle 1 \rangle \top \to \langle 0 \rangle^k \top$ for every $k$.
3. Each $[n]$ satisfies the theorems of $\mathsf{GL}$, for example $[n] p \to [n][n] p$, by part 1 of [[Löb's axiom in provability logic|QQ6PZFGGB3]].

### Non-examples

1. [[A bimodal frame that is not a GLP frame|~e78738fe]]: two GL frames $R_0 \supseteq R_1$ on the same worlds do not give a frame of $\mathsf{GLP}$ when $R_1$ is not empty.
2. The schema $[1] \varphi \to [0] \varphi$ is not a theorem. Its instance with $\varphi = \langle 0 \rangle \top$, the axiom $\langle 0 \rangle \top \to [1] \langle 0 \rangle \top$ and the $\mathsf{GL}$ theorem $[0] \langle 0 \rangle \top \to [0] \bot$ would give $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash \langle 0 \rangle \top \to [0] \bot$, hence $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash [0] \bot$, which fails by arithmetical soundness.
3. $\mathsf{GLP} \nvdash [1] \bot$, although $[1] \bot$ is valid on every Kripke frame of $\mathsf{GLP}$; see Basic properties.

### Basic properties

1. Kripke incompleteness. Every frame $(W, R_0, R_1, \dots)$ that validates $\mathsf{GLP}$ has $R_1 = \emptyset$, so it validates $[1] \bot$. The proof is in [[the non-example of a GLP frame|~e78738fe]].
2. Arithmetical soundness gives $\mathsf{GLP} \nvdash [1] \bot$: the theory $\mathsf{PA}$ with one use of the $\omega$-rule is consistent, since all its theorems are true. With item 1, $\mathsf{GLP}$ is not the logic of any class of Kripke frames.
3. Arithmetical completeness (Japaridze): $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash \varphi$ if and only if $\mathsf{PA}$ proves every realization of $\varphi$, where $[n]$ is realized by the $n$-provability predicate. We cite it.
4. $\mathsf{GLP}$ is decidable, and the problem of being a theorem is [[PSPACE|T9NTRY93ZT]]-complete.
5. Worms and ordinals (Beklemishev): order worms by $A <_0 B$ if $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash B \to \langle 0 \rangle A$. Up to provable equivalence this is a well-order of type $\varepsilon_0$, the ordinal of [[Gentzen's consistency proof|QPV0HNP22S]].

### History

Giorgi Japaridze introduced $\mathsf{GLP}$ in his 1986 thesis and proved its arithmetical completeness, published in 1988.
He also showed that $\mathsf{GLP}$ has no Kripke frames in the sense of Basic property 1.
Lev Beklemishev used $\mathsf{GLP}$ from 2004 on for ordinal notations up to $\varepsilon_0$ and an ordinal analysis of $\mathsf{PA}$, and later gave a simpler proof of the completeness theorem.
Decidability in polynomial space is due to Shapirovsky (2008).

### Common errors

1. Expecting a Kripke completeness proof as for $\mathsf{GL}$. The frame conditions of the axioms force $R_1 = \emptyset$; completeness needs more general semantics, such as topological or special Kripke models.
2. Reading $[m] \varphi \to [n] \varphi$ with $m > n$. Larger indices are stronger provability notions, so $[0] \varphi \to [1] \varphi$ holds, not the converse.
3. Forgetting that necessitation is given only for $[0]$. Necessitation for $[n]$ follows from it by the schema $[0] \varphi \to [n] \varphi$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

$\mathsf{GLP}$ is a logic with many kinds of "provable": $[0]$ is ordinary provability, $[1]$ allows stronger arguments, $[2]$ stronger still, and so on.
Each kind satisfies the laws of ordinary provability logic.
The axioms say that a stronger kind proves everything a weaker one proves, and that a stronger kind can see the consistency facts of a weaker one.
This logic is used to measure the strength of theories of arithmetic.

### L2 human

We derive necessitation for $[1]$ from the rules of $\mathsf{GLP}$.

1. Assume $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash \varphi$.
2. Necessitation for $[0]$ gives $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash [0] \varphi$.
3. The axiom $[0] \varphi \to [1] \varphi$ and modus ponens give $\mathsf{GLP} \vdash [1] \varphi$.

The typical use of $\mathsf{GLP}$ is to compare consistency statements, as in the example $\langle 1 \rangle \top \to \langle 0 \rangle^k \top$.
A trap: a finite Kripke countermodel does not show that a formula is not in $\mathsf{GLP}$, since $\mathsf{GLP}$ has no complete class of frames.

### L3 human

$\mathsf{GLP}$ is the polymodal analogue of $\mathsf{GL}$, and its Lindenbaum algebra encodes the proof-theoretic ordinal of $\mathsf{PA}$.
Beklemishev's worms give a purely modal ordinal notation system for $\varepsilon_0$, and the [[transfinite induction|0ZHQMQ2C1N]] up to $\varepsilon_0$ used by Gentzen becomes induction along the order of worms.
The Kripke incompleteness shows that the semantics of [[Kripke completeness of GL|~d95bfe82]] does not extend; Beklemishev and others use special models and topological semantics instead.
