## Summary

Symplectic, contact and Poisson geometry is the study of manifolds with a symplectic form, a contact structure or a Poisson bracket.
A symplectic form on a manifold is a closed 2-form that is nondegenerate at every point, and a manifold with such a form always has even dimension.
Symplectic geometry is the geometry of the phase spaces of classical mechanics, where the symplectic form turns each energy function into a Hamiltonian flow.
The subject grew from the work of Lagrange and Hamilton on mechanics, and the pseudoholomorphic curves of Gromov in 1985 and the homology theory of Floer in the late 1980s started modern symplectic topology.
This topic collects the symplectic, contact and Poisson geometry pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Differential geometry, dynamical systems, classical mechanics and low-dimensional topology have their own topics.
