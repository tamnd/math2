## Definition

A [[quasistatic process|PVDWD7HGFG]] of a fluid is *isobaric* if the pressure $p$ of the fluid is the same at all times of the process.

## Remarks

If the volume changes from $V_1$ to $V_2$, the [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] done on the fluid is $W = -p (V_2 - V_1)$.
The [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] absorbed is then $Q = \Delta U + p \Delta V = \Delta H$, where $U$ is the [[internal energy|F6JVQ12X43]] and $H = U + pV$ is the enthalpy.
For an ideal gas, an isobaric process satisfies $V / T$ constant, where $T$ is the thermodynamic temperature.
Heating a gas in a rigid closed container is not isobaric: its volume is fixed, so its pressure rises.
