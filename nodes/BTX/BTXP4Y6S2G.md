## Summary

The chain-ladder method is the standard way to estimate claims reserves in non-life insurance.
It arranges past cumulative claims in a run-off triangle by accident year and development year.
It estimates average growth factors from one development year to the next.
It then uses them to project each accident year to its final amount.
The reserve is the projected final amount minus what has already been paid or reported.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Claims from accidents in a given year keep coming in for several years afterwards.
For recent years the insurer has seen only the beginning of this flow.
The chain-ladder method looks at older years to see how much the totals typically grew from year one to year two, from year two to year three, and so on.
It then applies these growth factors to the recent years to guess their final totals.
For example, suppose older years grew on average by a factor $1.5$ after their first year and then stopped.
A recent year with $200$ after one year is then projected to end at $300$, so $100$ must be reserved.
