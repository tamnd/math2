## Statement

The infinite sets of weak König's lemma add no new theorems of the form "for every $n$ there is $m$", so every such theorem has a finitistic proof.
Let $\theta(n, m)$ be a $\Delta^0_0$ formula of first-order arithmetic, one whose quantifiers are all bounded, as in the [[arithmetical hierarchy of formulas|GE43CSZTS9]].
Suppose that [[WKL0|K5YPW1NTHZ]] proves the $\Pi^0_2$ sentence $\forall n\, \exists m\, \theta(n, m)$.
Then there is a primitive recursive function $f$ such that [[primitive recursive arithmetic|330YAP9Y4Q]] proves $\theta(n, f(n))$, with $n$ a free variable.

In particular, every $\Pi^0_1$ sentence provable in $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ is provable in $\mathsf{PRA}$.
So $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ is a [[conservative extension|N238J43XNE]] of $\mathsf{PRA}$ for $\Pi^0_2$ sentences.

## Remarks

### Why the system matters

The system cannot be replaced by $\mathsf{ACA}_0$.
By the [[Bolzano-Weierstrass example|JEN1781V0E]], $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ proves the consistency of $\mathsf{PRA}$, a $\Pi^0_1$ sentence that $\mathsf{PRA}$ does not prove.
The class of sentences matters too: $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ proves weak König's lemma, which is not even a sentence of first-order arithmetic.

### Consequences

If $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ proves $\forall n\, \exists m\, \theta(n, m)$, then a primitive recursive function computes a witness $m$ from $n$, as the [[example of a witness function|3F0Y21T6DK]] shows.
$\mathsf{WKL}_0$ is consistent if $\mathsf{PRA}$ is consistent.
A proof of $0 = 1$ in $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ would make $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ prove the false $\Pi^0_1$ sentence $\forall n\, (0 = 1)$, which $\mathsf{PRA}$ would then prove.
The provably total functions of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$, in the sense of $\Pi^0_2$ sentences, are exactly the primitive recursive functions.

### Watch out

The theorem concerns $\Pi^0_2$ sentences with a bounded matrix $\theta$, and it gives no information about sentences with more alternations of quantifiers.
For the use in Hilbert's program, the proof of the theorem must also be carried out in $\mathsf{PRA}$, and the [[proof|DEHQRGVK0K]] says which proofs do this.

## History

The theorem is due to Harvey Friedman, who founded reverse mathematics in a talk at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Vancouver in 1974.
Wilfried Sieg later gave a proof by cut elimination that can be formalized in $\mathsf{PRA}$ itself.
This finitistic proof makes $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ a [[partial realization of Hilbert's program|Z26S516BKH]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem says that the infinite objects of $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ never prove a new statement of the form "for every $n$ there is $m$ with a computable property".
Such a statement proved in $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ already has a finitistic proof.
The witness $m$ is computed from $n$ by a primitive recursive function, a program built from simple loops.

### L2 human

The typical use is the extraction of a bound from a proof that uses compactness.
For example, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ proves that every continuous function on $[0, 1]$ is uniformly continuous, and a $\Pi^0_2$ statement proved with that lemma still has a primitive recursive witness.
The trap is the form of the sentence: the matrix $\theta$ must have only bounded quantifiers.
A sentence such as "for every $n$ there is $m$ such that for all $k$, ..." is $\Pi^0_3$, and the theorem says nothing about it.

### L3 human

The theorem is the composition of three conservation results.
$\mathsf{WKL}_0$ is $\Pi^1_1$-conservative over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, the first-order part of $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ is $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$, and $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ is $\Pi^0_2$-conservative over $\mathsf{PRA}$ by Parsons's theorem.
In ordinal terms, $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ and $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ all have proof-theoretic ordinal $\omega^\omega$.
Stronger systems, such as $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ with ordinal $\varepsilon_0$, prove that more functions are total.
