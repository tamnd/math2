## Summary

Gain scheduling is a method of [[nonlinear control|RZ94FKGZ5Z]] that controls a nonlinear system with a family of linear controllers, one for each operating point.
Each controller is designed for the [[linearisation|09ZWYRQ41J]] of the system at its operating point.
A measured scheduling variable selects or interpolates the controller in use.
It is widely used in practice, for example in flight control, where the dynamics change with speed and altitude.
Its guarantees are local, and fast changes of the scheduling variable can destroy stability even when every frozen linear design is stable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An aircraft behaves differently at low speed and at high speed.
Instead of one controller for all conditions, engineers design a simple linear controller for each of many speeds.
In flight, the current speed is measured, and the controller for that speed, or a blend of the two nearest ones, is used.
The controller gains are thus scheduled by the speed, which gives the method its name.
