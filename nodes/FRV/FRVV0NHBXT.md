## Summary

A shock wave in a gas is a very thin layer across which pressure, density, temperature and velocity jump.
In inviscid gas dynamics it is modelled as a surface of discontinuity of a weak solution of the compressible [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]].
Gas crosses the shock, and conservation of mass, momentum and energy gives the [[Rankine–Hugoniot conditions|S4XMYJ3V42]], while the entropy of the gas must increase.
It is the gas-dynamic case of the general notion of a [[shock wave|JSDMGGTG7G]] for conservation laws.
See the [[definition|TV2BAWJET9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A supersonic aircraft cannot warn the air ahead of it, because the warnings travel at the speed of sound.
So the air is hit suddenly, and its pressure and density jump in a layer only a few molecular free paths thick.
This layer is the shock wave, heard on the ground as a sonic boom.
In a shock tube, gas at high pressure pushes into gas at low pressure, and a shock runs ahead into the low-pressure gas.
Air crossing a shock at twice the speed of sound has its pressure multiplied by $4.5$.
