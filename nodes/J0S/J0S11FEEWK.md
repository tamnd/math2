## Summary

A stationary state is a [[quantum state|4NQN47A92F]] that does not change under time evolution, apart from an overall phase of its state vector.
The stationary states are the eigenvectors of the [[Hamiltonian|VXDEQB46GV]], and the eigenvalue is the energy of the state.
Finding them means solving the time-independent [[Schrödinger equation|EPZR6H2WJN]] $H\psi = E\psi$.
All measurement probabilities in a stationary state are constant in time.
The energies of the stationary states of an atom are its energy levels, and the differences between them give its spectral lines.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a stationary state the wave function only turns its phase as time goes by, like the hand of a clock, at a rate set by the energy.
Since a phase changes no probability, nothing measurable changes.
The energy levels of an atom are the energies of its stationary states.
For the harmonic oscillator with $\hbar = m = \omega = 1$, the Gaussian $\pi^{-1/4} e^{-x^2/2}$ is the stationary state of lowest energy, $\frac12$.
