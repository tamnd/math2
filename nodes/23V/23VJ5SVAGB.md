## Summary

Evolutionary graph theory studies evolutionary dynamics in populations whose spatial or social structure is a graph.
Individuals sit at the vertices, and an offspring can only replace a neighbour of its parent.
The main question is how the graph changes the [[fixation probability|5APPKBE8Q4]] of a new mutant compared with the well-mixed [[Moran process|A9M7VKRRV9]].
Some graphs, such as the star, amplify selection, while others suppress it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a well-mixed population anyone can replace anyone.
Real populations live on islands, in networks of contacts, or in tissues of cells, where only neighbours compete.
Evolutionary graph theory puts each individual on a node of a network and lets offspring move only along links.
On a star, with one hub linked to many leaves, a beneficial mutant takes over more easily than in a well-mixed population of the same size.
