## Proof

Here $^*$ is the [[conjugate transpose|0EX045E2BP]], $(XY)^* = Y^* X^*$ by the [[transpose of a product|09GK5PS824]], and $\ip{x}{y} = y^* x$ on $\C^n$.

1. By [[the Gram matrix theorem|0AM050KCAW]], $A^* A$ is positive semidefinite. Let $P = (A^* A)^{1/2}$ be its [[positive semidefinite square root|~21ece919]]. Then $P^* = P$ and $P^2 = A^* A$.
2. For $x, y \in \C^n$, we get $\ip{Px}{Py} = y^* P^* P x = y^* P^2 x = y^* A^* A x = \ip{Ax}{Ay}$. In particular $\norm{Px} = \norm{Ax}$.
3. By step 2, $Px = 0$ if and only if $Ax = 0$. So $\ker P = \ker A$.
4. Define $U_0 \colon \im P \to \im A$ by $U_0(Px) = Ax$. It is well defined: if $Px = Px'$, then $P(x - x') = 0$, so $A(x - x') = 0$ by step 3. It is linear, and it maps onto $\im A$.
5. By step 2, $\ip{U_0 Px}{U_0 Py} = \ip{Ax}{Ay} = \ip{Px}{Py}$. So $U_0$ preserves inner products.
6. Let $r = \dim \im P$. By step 3 and the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]], $\dim \im A = n - \dim \ker A = n - \dim \ker P = r$.
7. By the [[existence of an orthonormal basis|0Y85GYEGSF]], let $e_1, \dots, e_r$ be an orthonormal basis of $\im P$. By step 5, $U_0 e_1, \dots, U_0 e_r$ are orthonormal, and they lie in $\im A$.
8. By the [[dimension of the orthogonal complement|0E2NYPVSP9]], $(\im P)^\perp$ and $(\im A)^\perp$ both have dimension $n - r$. Let $f_1, \dots, f_{n-r}$ and $g_1, \dots, g_{n-r}$ be orthonormal bases of them.
9. The lists $E = (e_1, \dots, e_r, f_1, \dots, f_{n-r})$ and $G = (U_0 e_1, \dots, U_0 e_r, g_1, \dots, g_{n-r})$ are orthonormal, since the vectors of each part are orthogonal to the vectors of the other part. They have $n$ vectors, so they are orthonormal bases of $\C^n$.
10. Let $E$ and $G$ also denote the $n \times n$ matrices with these columns. By the [[orthonormal columns criterion|83QE4F4E4V]], $E^* E = G^* G = I$. By the [[one-sided inverse theorem|0753Y2X5K6]], $E E^* = I$ as well.
11. Let $U = G E^*$. Then $U^* U = E G^* G E^* = E E^* = I$, so $U$ is [[unitary|0D6RMJ10M9]]. Also $U e_i = G E^* e_i = G \epsilon_i = U_0 e_i$, where $\epsilon_i$ is the $i$-th standard basis vector.
12. By step 11 and linearity, $U = U_0$ on $\im P$. So $U P x = U_0 P x = A x$ for every $x$, and $A = UP$. This proves part 1.
13. Uniqueness of $P$: let $A = U' P'$ with $U'$ unitary and $P'$ positive semidefinite. Then $A^* A = P'^* U'^* U' P' = P'^2$. By the uniqueness in [[the square root theorem|~21ece919]], $P' = (A^* A)^{1/2} = P$. This proves part 2.
14. Let $A$ be invertible, and let $A = U' P$ as in step 13. By the [[multiplicativity of the determinant|04CPJ6AF00]], $\det U' \det P = \det A \ne 0$. So $\det P \ne 0$, and $P$ is [[invertible|FBSAPTP1PR]].
15. Then $U' = A P^{-1}$ is determined by $A$. Since $P$ is invertible, $0$ is not an eigenvalue of $P$, so $P$ is positive definite by the [[eigenvalue test|0HYC2RHD3M]]. This proves part 3.
16. Let $A$ be real. Then $A^* A = A^T A$ is real, and $P$ is real by part 5 of the square root theorem. Steps 4 to 12 work in $\R^n$ with real orthonormal bases, so $U$ is real with $U^T U = I$. This proves part 4.
17. Let $P' = U P U^*$. It is [[Hermitian|0EKZCX4K3P]], and $x^* P' x = (U^* x)^* P (U^* x) \ge 0$, so it is [[positive semidefinite|0880F9EQCP]]. Also $P' U = U P U^* U = UP = A$.
18. $P'^2 = U P^2 U^* = U P P^* U^* = (UP)(UP)^* = A A^*$. By the uniqueness of square roots, $P' = (A A^*)^{1/2}$. This proves part 5.

## Idea

The matrix $P = (A^* A)^{1/2}$ satisfies $\norm{Px} = \norm{Ax}$ for every $x$.
So the map $Px \mapsto Ax$ is a well defined isometry from the range of $P$ onto the range of $A$.
The two orthogonal complements have the same dimension, so the isometry extends to a unitary $U$ with $UP = A$.

## Variants

- For invertible $A$ the extension in steps 7 to 12 is not needed. Let $U = A P^{-1}$. Then $U^* U = P^{-1} A^* A P^{-1} = P^{-1} P^2 P^{-1} = I$.
- The [[singular value decomposition|000NPTMX6J]] gives another proof. If $A = W \Sigma V^*$ with $W$, $V$ unitary and $\Sigma$ square, then $A = (W V^*)(V \Sigma V^*)$, and $V \Sigma V^*$ is positive semidefinite. The SVD is proved later, by the spectral theorem for $A^* A$.
