## Statement

No process can reduce the [[thermodynamic temperature|AJ8M9MGZGF]] of a system to absolute zero in a finite number of steps.

## Remarks

The usual cooling method alternates two kinds of steps.
An [[isothermal step|C9MM7GFRWN]] changes an external parameter $X$, such as a magnetic field, from $X_1$ to $X_2$ at fixed temperature.
An [[adiabatic step|W1RS8DA2H6]] changes it back at fixed [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]], which lowers the temperature.
Draw the entropy curves $T \mapsto S(T, X_1)$ and $T \mapsto S(T, X_2)$.
Suppose the two curves meet at $T = 0$, as the [[Nernst heat theorem|9MVG566SX3]] says.
Then each cycle lowers the temperature by a smaller amount, and $T = 0$ would need infinitely many steps.
If they did not meet at $T = 0$, a single adiabatic step could reach absolute zero.
Adiabatic demagnetisation of a paramagnetic salt is the standard example of this cooling process.

## Explanations

### L1 human

One can cool a system closer and closer to absolute zero, but never all the way.
Each cooling step gains less than the one before.

### L2 human

The unattainability principle is one of the usual forms of the third law of thermodynamics.
Under suitable assumptions on the system it is equivalent to the Nernst heat theorem.
