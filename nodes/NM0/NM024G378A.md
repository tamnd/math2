## Summary

A plane wave is a field in space whose value at each moment is constant on every plane perpendicular to a fixed direction.
The most important case is the sinusoidal plane wave $A \cos(k \cdot x - \omega t + \phi)$, with wave vector $k$ and angular frequency $\omega$.
Plane waves are the simplest solutions of the [[wave equation|5EHD2XBQDG]] and of [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]], and by Fourier analysis every solution is a superposition of them.
They model the light of a distant source, whose wavefronts are nearly flat.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a wave whose crests are flat sheets, all parallel, moving together in one direction.
Along each sheet the wave has the same value; it changes only as one moves across the sheets.
Waves on the sea far from any coast look like this, and so does sunlight on Earth, since the Sun is so far away.
In one formula: $\cos(x - ct)$ in three-dimensional space is a plane wave whose crests are planes $x = \text{const}$, moving in the $x$ direction at speed $c$.
