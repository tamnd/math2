## Example

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite [[graph|00C0BR7D7H]], let $J > 0$ be a coupling constant and let $\beta \ge 0$ be the inverse temperature.
A configuration assigns to each vertex $x$ an angle $\theta_x \in [0, 2\pi)$, that is, a unit vector $s_x = (\cos \theta_x, \sin \theta_x)$ in $\R^2$.
Its energy is
$$H(\theta) = -J \sum_{\set{x, y} \in E} \cos(\theta_x - \theta_y) = -J \sum_{\set{x, y} \in E} s_x \cdot s_y.$$
The *XY model* on $G$ is the probability measure on $[0, 2\pi)^V$ with density $e^{-\beta H(\theta)} / Z$ with respect to the product of the uniform measures.
Here $Z$ is the normalizing constant.
This is a [[Boltzmann distribution|GYNMZ9201M]] on a continuous spin space, so the XY model is a [[lattice model|PAEME5EQRM]] when $G$ is a box of $\Z^d$.

## Remarks

The energy is unchanged when every angle is shifted by the same $\alpha$, so the model has a continuous $O(2)$ symmetry.
It is the case $n = 2$ of the [[n-vector model|N0VMY0EF9G]].
On $\Z^2$ the [[Mermin–Wagner theorem|5C0QQFXSD7]] rules out spontaneous magnetization at every positive temperature.
Yet the model still has a change of behaviour of its correlations, the [[Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition|MN952HPZ50]].
On $\Z^d$ with $d \ge 3$ it has long-range order at low temperature.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put a small compass needle at each site of a grid, free to turn in the plane.
Neighbouring needles like to point the same way, and heat makes them wobble.
Because the needles can turn by any angle, slow twists cost very little energy, and in two dimensions these twists destroy order at long range.
