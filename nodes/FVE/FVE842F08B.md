## Definition

Let $\varepsilon_0, \mu_0 > 0$, let $\omega > 0$ be an angular frequency, and let $K \subset \R^3$ be a bounded set.
Let $E, H \colon \R^3 \setminus K \to \C^3$ be the complex amplitudes of time-harmonic fields $\mathrm{Re}(E(x) e^{-i\omega t})$ and $\mathrm{Re}(H(x) e^{-i\omega t})$ that solve the time-harmonic [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]]
$$\nabla \times E = i \omega \mu_0 H, \qquad \nabla \times H = -i \omega \varepsilon_0 E.$$
For $x \neq 0$ write $r = \abs{x}$ and $\hat x = x / r$.
The pair $(E, H)$ satisfies the *Silver–Müller radiation condition* if
$$\lim_{r \to \infty} r \left( \sqrt{\mu_0} \, H \times \hat x - \sqrt{\varepsilon_0} \, E \right) = 0$$
uniformly in the direction $\hat x$.

## Remarks

Here $H = B / \mu_0$ is the magnetic field strength.
Solutions that satisfy the condition are called radiating.
With the time factor $e^{i\omega t}$ instead of $e^{-i\omega t}$, the signs in the time-harmonic equations change, and so does the form of the condition in some texts.
Each Cartesian component of a radiating solution satisfies the [[Helmholtz equation|MNXKTXJ35G]] with $k = \omega \sqrt{\varepsilon_0 \mu_0}$ and the Sommerfeld radiation condition $\lim_{r \to \infty} r \left( \partial_r u - i k u \right) = 0$.

Example: let $a \in \C^3$ be a nonzero constant, $\Phi(x) = e^{ik\abs{x}} / (4 \pi \abs{x})$ with $k = \omega \sqrt{\varepsilon_0 \mu_0}$, $E = \nabla \times (a \Phi)$ and $H = \nabla \times E / (i \omega \mu_0)$.
Far away $E = i k \Phi \, \hat x \times a + O(r^{-2})$, so $\hat x \cdot E = O(r^{-2})$ and $H = \frac{k}{\omega \mu_0} \hat x \times E + O(r^{-2})$.
Then $\sqrt{\mu_0} H \times \hat x = \sqrt{\varepsilon_0} \big(E - (\hat x \cdot E) \hat x\big) + O(r^{-2}) = \sqrt{\varepsilon_0} E + O(r^{-2})$, and the condition holds.

Non-example: the same construction with $e^{-ik\abs{x}}$ in place of $e^{ik\abs{x}}$ gives an incoming wave.
Then $\sqrt{\mu_0} H \times \hat x - \sqrt{\varepsilon_0} E = -2 \sqrt{\varepsilon_0} E + O(r^{-2})$, which is of exact order $1/r$, so the limit is not $0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Far from the sources, an outgoing light wave has its magnetic field equal to the electric field turned about the outward direction, with a fixed ratio.
The condition asks that the fields obey this rule better and better as one goes out, faster than the fields themselves decay.
Incoming waves have the opposite orientation and fail the test.
