## Summary

An invariant bilinear form on a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] $\mathfrak g$ is a [[bilinear form|0SQZMXB6GP]] $B$ with $B([x, y], z) = B(x, [y, z])$ for all $x, y, z$.
Equivalently, every operator $\operatorname{ad} x$ is skew-adjoint for $B$.
The trace form $\tr(xy)$ on matrix Lie algebras and the Killing form are the basic examples.
Nondegenerate invariant symmetric forms let one identify $\mathfrak g$ with its dual, and they are used to build Casimir elements.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bilinear form pairs two elements of the Lie algebra and returns a number.
It is invariant when moving a bracket from one side of the pairing to the other does not change the result.
This is the infinitesimal version of a form that the symmetries of a group preserve.
For matrices, the pairing $\tr(xy)$ has this property, because the trace of a product does not change under cyclic shifts.
