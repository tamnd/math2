## Summary

Climate sensitivity measures how much the global mean surface temperature changes in response to a given [[radiative forcing|B8HRNPHNZF]].
Its most common form, the equilibrium climate sensitivity, is the warming that eventually follows a doubling of the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.
It combines the direct effect of the forcing with feedbacks, such as those of water vapour, clouds and ice.
It is one of the key numbers of climate science, because it sets the scale of future warming for a given path of emissions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If the amount of carbon dioxide in the air doubled and then stayed fixed, how much warmer would the Earth finally become?
The answer is the equilibrium climate sensitivity.
Without any feedbacks the answer would be about $1$ degree Celsius.
Feedbacks amplify it: a warmer atmosphere holds more water vapour, which is itself a greenhouse gas, and melting ice exposes darker ground.
The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change gives a best estimate of about $3$ degrees.
