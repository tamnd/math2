## Summary

An evanescent field is an oscillating field that does not propagate away from its source or from a surface but decays exponentially with distance.
It arises when a wave is forced to oscillate along a surface faster than a free wave of the same frequency can.
Evanescent fields appear beyond a totally reflecting surface, in waveguides below cutoff and near small sources.
A single evanescent wave carries no time-averaged power in its direction of decay.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When light is totally reflected inside glass, a little of the field still pokes out into the air.
This field wiggles along the surface but dies off quickly away from it, within about one wavelength.
If you bring a second piece of glass very close, the field can jump the gap, an optical cousin of quantum tunnelling.
Near-field microscopes use this short-range field to see details smaller than a wavelength.
