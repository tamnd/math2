## Summary

The Boussinesq approximation is a simplification of the equations of a fluid whose density varies a little, for example because of temperature.
It neglects the density variations everywhere except in the gravity term, where they create buoyancy.
The flow is then treated as [[incompressible|2JZ0C15A9C]].
It is the standard model of natural convection, of the ocean and of much of atmospheric dynamics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Warm water is only slightly lighter than cold water, by a fraction of a percent.
That small difference hardly changes how much force it takes to accelerate the water.
But gravity acts on every parcel all the time, so the small difference makes warm water rise and cold water sink.
The Boussinesq approximation keeps the density difference only where it causes this rising and sinking, and ignores it everywhere else.
