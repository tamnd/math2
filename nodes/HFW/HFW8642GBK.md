## Summary

An irreducible module over a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] $\mathfrak{g}$ is a nonzero [[representation|TZAZ163QGK]] with no submodules other than $0$ and itself.
Irreducible modules are the building blocks of representation theory of Lie algebras, in the same way as [[irreducible representations|FX3NEFYFFN]] of groups.
They are the [[simple modules|G00C22AEDH]] over the universal enveloping algebra.
For a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]], the finite-dimensional irreducible modules are classified by their highest weights.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A module is irreducible when it cannot be split into a smaller piece that the Lie algebra preserves.
Starting from any nonzero vector, applying elements of the Lie algebra again and again and taking linear combinations reaches every vector of the module.
A one-dimensional module is always irreducible.
The plane $\C^2$ with the action of $2 \times 2$ trace-zero matrices is irreducible, since no line is sent into itself by all of them.
