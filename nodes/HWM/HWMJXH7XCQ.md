## Summary

In number theory, the **Calkin–Wilf tree** is a [[tree|062D9KXJB1]] in which the vertices correspond [[one-to-one|0GG9TVW90M]] to the positive [[rational numbers|0NCJ5WXN6Z]]. The tree is rooted at the number 1, and any rational number $q$ expressed in simplest terms as the fraction $\tfrac{a}{b}$ has as its two children the numbers $\tfrac{1}{1+1/q} = \tfrac{a}{a + b}$ and $q + 1 = \tfrac{a + b}{b}$. Every positive rational number appears exactly once in the tree. It is named after Neil Calkin and Herbert Wilf, but appears in other works including Kepler's *Harmonices Mundi*.
