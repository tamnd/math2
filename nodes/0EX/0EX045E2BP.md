## Definition

Let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with complex entries.
The *conjugate transpose* of $A$ is the $n \times m$ matrix $A^*$ with entries
$$(A^*)_{ij} = \overline{a_{ji}}$$
for $1 \le i \le n$ and $1 \le j \le m$, where $\overline{z}$ is the complex conjugate of $z$.

## Notation

- Other notations are $A^{\mathsf{H}}$, $A^H$ and $A^\dagger$.
- In Mathlib, `Matrix.conjTranspose A` is written `Aᴴ`, for matrices over any ring with a `star` operation.

## Remarks

- For a real matrix, the conjugate transpose is the [[transpose|0W809K6AW0]].
- The rules $(A^*)^* = A$, $(cA)^* = \overline{c} A^*$ and $(AB)^* = B^* A^*$ hold.
- For the standard inner product on $\mathbb{C}^n$, $\langle Ax, y \rangle = \langle x, A^* y \rangle$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The conjugate transpose flips a complex matrix over its diagonal and conjugates each entry.
For example, the conjugate transpose of $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -i & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.

### L2 human

The definition is the [[transpose|0W809K6AW0]] of the entrywise conjugate $\overline{A}$.
The two operations commute, so $A^* = \overline{A^{\mathsf{T}}}$ as well.
