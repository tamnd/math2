## Summary

Low-dimensional topology and knot theory is the study of manifolds of dimension two, three and four, and of knots and links in them.
A knot is an embedding of a circle in three-dimensional space, and two knots are equivalent when an isotopy of space moves one onto the other.
These dimensions show phenomena that do not occur in higher dimensions, and the subject uses methods from hyperbolic geometry, gauge theory and quantum algebra.
Tait made the first tables of knots in the 1870s and 1880s, and Perelman proved Thurston's geometrization conjecture, and with it the Poincaré conjecture, in 2002 and 2003.
This topic collects the low-dimensional topology and knot theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Manifolds and differential topology, geometric group theory, algebraic topology and symplectic, contact and Poisson geometry have their own topics.
