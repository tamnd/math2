## Summary

In mathematics, zero is an even number. In other words, its parity—the quality of an [[integer|0C4JS9RF5M]] being even or odd—is even. This can be easily verified based on the definition of "even": zero is an integer multiple of 2, specifically 0 × 2. As a result, zero shares all the properties that characterize even numbers: for example, 0 is neighbored on both sides by odd numbers, any decimal integer has the same parity as its last digit—so, since 10 is even, 0 will be even, and if $y$ is even then $y + x$ has the same parity as $x$—indeed, $0 + x$ and $x$ always have the same parity.
