## Summary

A non-Newtonian fluid is a fluid whose stress is not a fixed linear function of the rate of deformation.
Its apparent [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] may change with the shear rate, it may need a minimum stress before it flows, or it may remember its past deformation.
Paints, blood, polymer solutions, ketchup and molten plastics are all non-Newtonian.
Their study, part of [[rheology|EF27XBPW3N]], explains effects that a [[Newtonian fluid|T4ZV12BVHN]] can never show, such as rod climbing and die swell.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Water flows the same way whether you stir it slowly or fast: its thickness, the viscosity, is one fixed number.
Many everyday fluids do not behave like this.
Ketchup is thick in the bottle but runs once you shake it, so it gets thinner when it is sheared faster.
A mix of corn starch and water does the opposite: it feels almost solid when you punch it but flows when you push slowly.
Toothpaste stays on the brush like a solid until you squeeze hard enough.
All these fluids are called non-Newtonian.
