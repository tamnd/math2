## Definition

Let $F$ be an [[algebraic number field|MWBDFS2KJ5]] and let $\mathcal{O}_F$ be its ring of integers, the integral closure of $\Z$ in $F$.
For $n \ge 0$, the *$n$-th K-group of $\mathcal{O}_F$* is Quillen's [[algebraic K-group|5GVFZWMWC8]] $K_n(\mathcal{O}_F)$.
So $K_0(\mathcal{O}_F)$ is the [[Grothendieck group of finitely generated projective modules|Q14JS2MA1C]].
For $n \ge 1$,
$$K_n(\mathcal{O}_F) = \pi_n\big(B\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}_F)^+\big),$$
the homotopy group of the [[plus construction|XFGZFM8PC6]] of the classifying space of the infinite general linear group $\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}_F) = \bigcup_N \mathrm{GL}_N(\mathcal{O}_F)$.

## Remarks

Since $\mathcal{O}_F$ is a Dedekind domain, $K_0(\mathcal{O}_F) \cong \Z \oplus \operatorname{Cl}(F)$, where $\Z$ records the rank and $\operatorname{Cl}(F)$ is the ideal class group.
By the theorem of Bass, Milnor and Serre, the determinant gives $K_1(\mathcal{O}_F) \cong \mathcal{O}_F^\times$.

Example: for $F = \Q$, $K_0(\Z) = \Z$, $K_1(\Z) = \{\pm 1\}$, $K_2(\Z) = \Z/2$ and $K_3(\Z) = \Z/48$.

Non-example: the K-groups of $F$ itself differ from those of $\mathcal{O}_F$.
For instance $K_1(\Q) = \Q^\times$ is not finitely generated, while every $K_n(\Z)$ is finitely generated by Quillen's theorem.
