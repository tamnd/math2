## Summary

Cheap talk is communication that costs nothing and does not bind anyone.
A cheap talk game is a [[signaling game|DNVNS5HNYR]] in which the message does not enter the payoffs directly.
Messages can still carry information in equilibrium, but only as far as the interests of sender and receiver are aligned.
The model of Crawford and Sobel (1982) shows that the closer the interests, the more information can be passed on.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An expert gives advice to a decision maker, and talking is free.
If the expert and the decision maker want exactly the same thing, the expert simply tells the truth.
If the expert is biased, for example always wants a slightly higher decision, the decision maker discounts the advice.
Then only rough information gets through: the expert can say "low", "medium" or "high", but finer statements are not believed.
There is always a "babbling" equilibrium in which messages are ignored and therefore meaningless.
