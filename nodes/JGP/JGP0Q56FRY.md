## Summary

A preference relation records how a decision maker ranks a set of alternatives, with ties allowed.
It is a [[binary relation|05AVARPXA2]] that is complete and transitive, so it is a total [[preorder|0VABYWJWH9]].
It is the basic object of decision theory and consumer theory, and utility functions are numerical summaries of it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a person comparing meals on a menu.
For any two meals, the person can say which one is at least as good as the other, and sometimes both, when they like them equally.
The answers fit together.
If soup is at least as good as salad and salad is at least as good as bread, then soup is at least as good as bread.
For example, take three fruits and the rule "an apple is as good as a pear, and both are better than a plum".
This rule gives a preference relation on the set of the three fruits.
