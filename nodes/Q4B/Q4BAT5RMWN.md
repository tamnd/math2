## Summary

The Maxwell equal-area construction corrects the isotherms of an equation of state such as the [[van der Waals equation|X576BQTPQ5]] below the critical temperature.
It replaces the non-monotone loop of an isotherm by a horizontal segment.
The segment lies at the pressure where the two areas cut off by it are equal.
The segment describes the coexistence of liquid and vapour, and its pressure is the vapour pressure.
The equal-area rule expresses the equality of the [[chemical potentials|WVH4PVW64P]] of the two coexisting phases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Below its critical temperature, the van der Waals equation draws a pressure-volume curve with an up-and-down wiggle.
Real fluids do not follow the wiggle: as you compress the gas, at some pressure it starts to condense, and the pressure stays fixed until all of it is liquid.
Maxwell's rule tells where to draw this flat line: at the height where the bump above the line and the dip below it have the same area.
The flat line joins the volume of the gas to the volume of the liquid at that temperature.
