## Statement

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an [[abelian category|Y9CZ3MTNF1]] whose isomorphism classes of objects form a set.
Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a nonempty [[full subcategory|V3BFGKBJF7]] of $\mathcal{A}$ that is closed under subobjects, quotient objects and finite direct sums in $\mathcal{A}$.
Then $\mathcal{B}$ is abelian and the inclusion is exact.
Assume that every object $A$ of $\mathcal{A}$ has a finite filtration $A = A_0 \supseteq A_1 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq A_r = 0$ with all quotients $A_{i-1}/A_i$ in $\mathcal{B}$.
Then the inclusion induces an isomorphism of [[Grothendieck groups|2H7Y1XC93S]] $K_0(\mathcal{B}) \to K_0(\mathcal{A})$, with inverse $[A] \mapsto \sum_{i=1}^{r} [A_{i-1}/A_i]$.

## Remarks

The point is that $\sum_i [A_{i-1}/A_i] \in K_0(\mathcal{B})$ does not depend on the filtration.
Any two such filtrations have refinements with isomorphic successive quotients, up to order, by the Schreier refinement theorem.
Refining a filtration does not change the sum, because the quotients of a refinement are subquotients of quotients in $\mathcal{B}$.
Additivity on short exact sequences comes from splicing a filtration of a subobject with a filtration of the quotient.
Quillen extended the theorem to all higher K-groups.

Example: let $R$ be a commutative Noetherian ring, $\mathcal{A}$ the category of $R$-modules of finite [[length|CZSCNZH8VY]], and $\mathcal{B}$ the semisimple ones.
A composition series is a filtration with simple quotients $R/\mathfrak{m}$, $\mathfrak{m}$ maximal, and $K_0(\mathcal{B})$ is free abelian on these simple modules.
So $K_0(\mathcal{A}) \cong \bigoplus_{\mathfrak{m}} \Z$, and the class of a module records how often each $R/\mathfrak{m}$ occurs in a composition series.
For a local Artinian ring $R$ with residue field $k$, this gives $G_0(R) \cong \Z$, generated by $[k]$, and $[M]$ is the length of $M$.

The filtration hypothesis is necessary.
For $\mathcal{A}$ the finitely generated abelian groups and $\mathcal{B}$ the finite ones, $\Z$ has no such filtration.
The map $K_0(\mathcal{B}) \to K_0(\mathcal{A})$ is zero, but $K_0(\mathcal{B}) \cong \bigoplus_p \Z$ over the primes $p$ is not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"Dévissage" is French for unscrewing: one takes an object apart into simpler pieces.
If every object can be built in finitely many steps from objects of a simpler kind, then for the purposes of K-theory the simple kind is enough.
For example, a module of finite length is built from simple modules, and its class is just the count of these simple pieces.

### L2 human

Dévissage is a basic tool for computing $G$-theory.
For a Noetherian ring $R$ and an ideal $I$, every finitely generated module killed by a power of $I$ has a filtration with quotients killed by $I$, which are $R/I$-modules.
So the $K_0$ of finitely generated modules supported on $V(I)$ is $G_0(R/I)$.
Combined with the localization sequence $G_0(R/I) \to G_0(R) \to G_0(R[s^{-1}]) \to 0$ for $I = (s)$, it lets one compute $G$-groups by cutting a ring into simpler pieces.
