## Summary

The magnetic vector potential is a vector field $A$ whose [[curl|0NF6GQNXMY]] is the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]], $B = \nabla \times A$.
It exists because $B$ is divergence-free, and it is unique only up to adding a gradient.
It plays for magnetism the role that the [[electric potential|SAEDXNM3Y1]] plays for electrostatics.
It is central in the Lagrangian and quantum descriptions of charged particles.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The magnetic field swirls around currents and has no sources.
Such a field can always be written as the swirl, or curl, of another field; that other field is the vector potential.
Near a straight wire, the vector potential points along the wire, like the current itself.
Many different vector potentials give the same magnetic field, and one picks the most convenient one.
