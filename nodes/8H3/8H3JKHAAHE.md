## Summary

A division algebra is a nonzero [[algebra over a field|1SENAHZW5A]] in which one can divide by every nonzero element on both sides.
The algebra need not be associative; an associative division algebra is a [[division ring|0EA5BTA0CX]] that contains the field in its center.
By the [[Frobenius theorem|QDJ7PQE9SW]], the finite-dimensional associative ones over $\R$ are $\R$, $\C$ and the [[quaternions|0GE4DVQKR8]].
The [[octonions|1HRMT3DSX2]] are a nonassociative one.
By the [[Bott–Milnor–Kervaire theorem|N1KGP57Y9D]], every finite-dimensional real division algebra has dimension $1$, $2$, $4$ or $8$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a division algebra the equations $ax = b$ and $ya = b$ have exactly one solution whenever $a \neq 0$.
So division works as in ordinary numbers, even if the order of factors or the brackets matter.
The quaternions are a division algebra in which $ij \neq ji$.
The $2 \times 2$ matrices are not one, because some nonzero matrices have no inverse.
