## Definition

Let two solids touch along an interface, and let heat cross the interface in [[steady state|RFWGC57K4H]] with normal [[heat flux|1Y36434YTE]] $q$.
Let $\Delta T \ne 0$ be the jump of [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]] across the interface, obtained by extrapolating the temperature profile of each solid to the interface.
The *thermal contact conductance* is
$$h_c = \frac{q}{\Delta T}.$$
Its unit is $\mathrm{W \, m^{-2} \, K^{-1}}$.

## Remarks

The reciprocal $1 / h_c$ is the contact [[thermal resistance|1CB45W4GA6]] of a unit area, and it adds in series to the resistances of the two solids.

Example: a flux of $10^4$ watts per square metre crosses a joint, and the profiles extrapolate to $50.0$ and $49.5$ degrees Celsius at the interface.
Then $\Delta T = 0.5$ kelvin and $h_c = 10^4 / 0.5 = 2 \cdot 10^4$ watts per square metre per kelvin.

Non-example: two pieces of one solid joined by perfect welding show no temperature jump.
Then $\Delta T = 0$, and no contact conductance is defined; the interface adds no resistance.
