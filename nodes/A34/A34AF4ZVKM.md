## Summary

Classical electromagnetism is the theory of electric and magnetic fields and their interaction with charged matter, without quantum effects.
Its laws are [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]], which say how charges and currents produce the fields.
They are completed by the [[Lorentz force|S3RZRCH0T5]] law, which says how the fields act on charges.
It explains electrostatics, magnetism, circuits, optics and radio waves in one framework.
It is a relativistic field theory, and its study led to special relativity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Classical electromagnetism is the physics of everything electric and magnetic at everyday scales.
Charges create an [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]], moving charges also create a [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]], and the two fields push on charges.
A changing magnetic field creates an electric field and the reverse, so the fields can travel on their own as waves.
Light, radio and X-rays are such waves.
The theory fails only at very small scales, where quantum effects such as photons become important.
