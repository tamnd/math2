## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a closed [[thermodynamic system|GYNNJBMTCP]] with [[internal energy|F6JVQ12X43]] $U$.
Let a process take $\Sigma$ from the equilibrium state $A$ to the equilibrium state $B$.
Let $W$ be the [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] done on $\Sigma$ in this process.
The *heat* absorbed by $\Sigma$ in the process is
$$Q = U(B) - U(A) - W.$$

## Remarks

Heat a gas in a rigid closed container, so that no work is done and $W = 0$.
Then the heat absorbed is $Q = U(B) - U(A)$, the rise of internal energy.
Stirring a liquid in an insulated vessel raises its internal energy, but the heat absorbed is $0$.
All of the energy then enters as work, so $W = U(B) - U(A)$.
With this definition, $\Delta U = Q + W$ is the first law of thermodynamics.
Many engineering texts use the work $W' = -W$ done by the system and write $\Delta U = Q - W'$.
