## Summary

The polarization density of a material is the [[electric dipole moment|BANHMXD2TD]] per unit volume.
It is a [[vector field|0299VCW1TH]] on a scale large compared with atoms.
It describes how an electric field slightly displaces the charges in a dielectric.
Its divergence gives the bound charge density of the material.
It is the bridge between microscopic dipoles and the macroscopic equations of electrostatics in matter.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an insulator such as glass, electrons cannot travel, but they can shift a little relative to their atoms.
An electric field makes each atom a tiny dipole.
Adding up all these tiny dipoles in a small volume and dividing by the volume gives the polarization density.
Where the polarization is uneven, a net charge appears, called bound charge.
On the surface of a uniformly polarized slab, a thin layer of bound charge appears on each face.
