## Summary

Born rigidity is the relativistic notion of a rigid motion of an extended body.
A motion is Born rigid if each small piece of the body keeps its shape and size as measured in its own instantaneous rest frame.
Signals cannot travel faster than light, so a body cannot be set into motion rigidly in the Newtonian sense.
Born rigidity is the best substitute, and it allows far fewer motions than in Newtonian mechanics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In Newtonian physics a rigid rod moves as a whole: push one end and the other moves at once.
In relativity this is impossible, because the push cannot travel faster than light.
Born rigidity asks less: each bit of the body should see its neighbours at constant distance, measured by its own rulers.
A rod accelerated so that its back end accelerates harder than its front end can satisfy this, but a rod whose two ends accelerate equally cannot.
