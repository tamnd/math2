## Summary

The scale height of an atmosphere is the height over which its pressure falls by a factor of $e \approx 2.718$, when the temperature is constant.
It equals $RT/(Mg)$, where $T$ is the temperature, $M$ the molar mass of the gas, $g$ the gravitational acceleration and $R$ the gas constant.
It sets the natural vertical length scale of a planetary atmosphere.
For the Earth's lower atmosphere it is about 8 km.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air gets thinner as you go up a mountain, and it does so at a steady percentage rate.
The scale height is the climb after which the pressure has dropped to about 37 percent of its starting value.
For the Earth it is about 8 km, a bit less than the height of Mount Everest.
A hotter or lighter gas, or weaker gravity, gives a larger scale height, so the atmosphere is more spread out.
