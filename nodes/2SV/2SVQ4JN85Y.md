## Summary

Baroclinic instability is the instability of a rotating, stably stratified flow whose speed changes with height, in [[thermal wind|73GW90ST91]] balance with a horizontal temperature gradient.
Small disturbances grow by releasing the available potential energy stored in the sloping density surfaces.
It is the mechanism that creates the cyclones and anticyclones of mid-latitude weather, and the mesoscale eddies of the ocean.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the mid-latitudes, warm air lies to the south and cold air to the north, and the boundary between them slopes.
This arrangement stores energy, like a tilted pile of sand.
A small wave on the boundary can let warm air slide north and up while cold air slides south and down.
This lowers the centre of mass and feeds the wave, which grows into a storm system about a thousand kilometres across within a few days.
The [[Eady model|KBCEHH0H7N]] is the simplest exact example.
