## Definition

Let $I$ be a countable index set and let $A = (a_{ij})_{i, j \in I}$ be a real [[symmetric matrix|0VR671PHE5]] such that:

1. $a_{ij} \le 0$ for all $i \neq j$;
2. if $a_{ii} > 0$, then $2 a_{ij} / a_{ii} \in \Z$ for all $j \in I$.

The *generalized Kac–Moody algebra* of $A$ is the real [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] generated by elements $e_i, f_i, h_i$ for $i \in I$, subject to the relations:

1. $[h_i, h_j] = 0$ for all $i, j$;
2. $[e_i, f_j] = \delta_{ij}\, h_i$ for all $i, j$, where $\delta_{ij}$ is the Kronecker delta;
3. $[h_i, e_j] = a_{ij}\, e_j$ and $[h_i, f_j] = -a_{ij}\, f_j$ for all $i, j$;
4. $(\operatorname{ad} e_i)^{1 - 2a_{ij}/a_{ii}}(e_j) = 0$ and $(\operatorname{ad} f_i)^{1 - 2a_{ij}/a_{ii}}(f_j) = 0$ whenever $a_{ii} > 0$ and $i \neq j$;
5. $[e_i, e_j] = 0$ and $[f_i, f_j] = 0$ whenever $a_{ij} = 0$.

Here $\operatorname{ad} x$ is the map $y \mapsto [x, y]$.

## Remarks

Example: let $A$ be a symmetric [[generalized Cartan matrix|38Z0HSXTCA]], so $a_{ii} = 2$ and $a_{ij} \in \Z_{\le 0}$.
Both conditions on $A$ hold, the exponent in relation 4 is $1 - a_{ij}$, and relation 5 follows from relation 4 because $a_{ij} = 0$ gives $(\operatorname{ad} e_i)(e_j) = 0$.
So the relations are the relations of the [[Kac–Moody algebra|HD13X0CNBD]] among $e_i$, $f_i$ and $h_i = \alpha_i^\vee$.
When $A$ is singular, the Kac–Moody algebra also contains extra elements of its larger Cartan subalgebra, which are not among the generators here.

Non-example: the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ is symmetric, but its off-diagonal entry $1$ is positive, so it fails condition 1 on $A$.

The index $i$ is called real if $a_{ii} > 0$ and imaginary if $a_{ii} \le 0$.
Many authors, following Borcherds, use the name more widely.
They also allow Lie algebras obtained from this one by adding commuting outer derivations to the Cartan part or by dividing by a central subalgebra.
