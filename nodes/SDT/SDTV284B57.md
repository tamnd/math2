## Proof

We write $\vdash$ for $\mathsf{IPC} \vdash$ and use the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] freely.
$\mathsf{CPC}$ is the [[Hilbert system|52XCYMK0WW]] of $\mathsf{IPC}$ with the extra axiom scheme $\psi \vee \neg \psi$.

Part A: three lemmas.

1. Lemma 1: $\vdash \psi \to \neg \neg \psi$. From $\psi$ and $\neg \psi$, modus ponens gives $\bot$.
2. Lemma 2: $\vdash \neg \neg (\psi \vee \neg \psi)$. Assume $\neg (\psi \vee \neg \psi)$ and $\psi$.
3. Then $\psi \vee \neg \psi$ by axiom 5, and $\bot$. Discharging $\psi$ gives $\neg \psi$.
4. Then $\psi \vee \neg \psi$ again by axiom 5, and $\bot$. Discharging the first assumption gives $\neg \neg (\psi \vee \neg \psi)$.
5. Lemma 3: $\vdash \neg \neg (\psi \to \chi) \to (\neg \neg \psi \to \neg \neg \chi)$. Assume $\neg \neg (\psi \to \chi)$, $\neg \neg \psi$ and $\neg \chi$.
6. Assume also $\psi \to \chi$. From $\psi$ we get $\chi$ and then $\bot$, so $\neg \psi$. With $\neg \neg \psi$ this gives $\bot$.
7. Discharging $\psi \to \chi$ gives $\neg (\psi \to \chi)$. With $\neg \neg (\psi \to \chi)$ this gives $\bot$.
8. Discharging $\neg \chi$ gives $\neg \neg \chi$, and discharging the other assumptions gives Lemma 3.

Part B: item 1.

9. Let $\mathsf{CPC} \vdash \varphi$. We show $\vdash \neg \neg \theta$ for every formula $\theta$ in a $\mathsf{CPC}$ derivation of $\varphi$, by induction along the derivation.
10. If $\theta$ is an axiom of $\mathsf{IPC}$, then $\vdash \theta$, and $\vdash \neg \neg \theta$ by Lemma 1.
11. If $\theta$ is $\psi \vee \neg \psi$, then $\vdash \neg \neg \theta$ by Lemma 2.
12. If $\theta$ comes from $\psi$ and $\psi \to \theta$ by [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]], the induction hypothesis gives $\vdash \neg \neg \psi$ and $\vdash \neg \neg (\psi \to \theta)$. Lemma 3 gives $\vdash \neg \neg \theta$.
13. So $\vdash \neg \neg \varphi$.
14. Conversely, let $\vdash \neg \neg \varphi$. Then $\mathsf{CPC} \vdash \neg \neg \varphi$, since $\mathsf{IPC} \subseteq \mathsf{CPC}$.
15. $\mathsf{CPC} \vdash \neg \neg \varphi \to \varphi$, by excluded middle for $\varphi$ and axiom 6 with axiom 7. So $\mathsf{CPC} \vdash \varphi$.

Part C: item 2.

16. Lemma 4: $\vdash \neg \neg \neg \psi \to \neg \psi$. Assume $\neg \neg \neg \psi$ and $\psi$. Lemma 1 gives $\neg \neg \psi$, and then $\bot$.
17. Let $\mathsf{CPC} \vdash \neg \varphi$. By item 1, $\vdash \neg \neg \neg \varphi$, and by Lemma 4, $\vdash \neg \varphi$.
18. The converse holds since $\mathsf{IPC} \subseteq \mathsf{CPC}$.

## Idea

The set of formulas whose double negation is intuitionistically provable contains every axiom of classical logic.
For excluded middle, this is the intuitionistic law $\neg \neg (\psi \vee \neg \psi)$.
The set is also closed under modus ponens, because double negation distributes over implication in one direction.
So it contains every classical theorem.

## Variants

1. A semantic proof uses finite Kripke models: at a maximal world every formula takes its classical value, and $\neg \neg \varphi$ holds at $w$ if $\varphi$ holds at every maximal world above $w$.
2. An algebraic proof uses that $x \mapsto \neg \neg x$ is a homomorphism from a Heyting algebra onto the Boolean algebra of its regular elements.
3. In predicate logic, the induction breaks at the rule of generalization, since $\forall x \neg \neg \psi \to \neg \neg \forall x\, \psi$ is not provable in $\mathsf{IQC}$.
