## Statement

For $n \geq 1$, let $V_n$ be the Visser rule of [[admissible rules|C5K35H0R4Y]]:
$$\frac{\bigl(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n} (p_i \to q_i) \to p_{n+1} \vee p_{n+2}\bigr) \vee r}{\bigvee_{j=1}^{n+2} \bigl(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n} (p_i \to q_i) \to p_j\bigr) \vee r}.$$
Let $\vdash_V$ be the smallest structural consequence relation that contains $\vdash_{\mathsf{IPC}}$ and all rules $V_n$.

**Theorem (Iemhoff).**

1. Each rule $V_n$ is admissible in $\mathsf{IPC}$.
2. The rules $V_n$ form a basis of the admissible rules of $\mathsf{IPC}$: for all formulas $A$ and $B$, $A \mathrel{|\!\sim}_{\mathsf{IPC}} B$ iff $A \vdash_V B$.

The proof is in [[the proof by gluing and projective approximation|A649SYY37P]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. The relation $\vdash_V$ is closed under substitution. Each $V_n$ has finitely many variables, and only its substitution instances cover all formulas.
2. All $n$ are needed. Rybakov's theorem says that no finite set of admissible rules is a basis, so no finite subfamily of the $V_n$ suffices.
3. The theorem is about $\mathsf{IPC}$. In classical logic, the empty set is a basis, since classical logic is structurally complete.

### Consequences

1. The admissible rules of $\mathsf{IPC}$ form a recursively enumerable set, since the $V_n$ are a recursive family. Together with co-enumerability this gives [[Rybakov's decidability theorem|GJN7A5DJHK]].
2. Mints's rule is the instance of $V_1$ with $p_1 = p$, $q_1 = q$, $p_2 = p$, $p_3 = r$ and $r = \bot$. So $V_1$ is not derivable.
3. Iemhoff proved that $\mathsf{IPC}$ is the only intermediate logic with the disjunction property that admits all Visser rules.

### Converse

The rules $V_n$ are admissible but not derivable in $\mathsf{IPC}$.
A Kripke model refutes the implication from premise to conclusion of $V_n$.
Its root $\rho$ forces no atom, and it lies below leaves $l_1, \dots, l_{n+2}$ and $u$.
At each $l_j$, all $q_i$ hold, $p_j$ fails, all other $p_k$ hold, and $r$ fails.
At $u$, $p_1$ holds, and $q_1$ and $r$ fail.
Then the root forces the premise, since $u$ refutes the conjunction and each $l_j$ forces $p_{n+1} \vee p_{n+2}$.
Each $l_j$ refutes the $j$-th disjunct of the conclusion, so the root does not force the conclusion.
The implication from premise to conclusion is classically valid, so adding it as an axiom gives a proper intermediate logic.

### Generalizations

1. Visser proved that the $V_n$ are also the admissible propositional rules of Heyting arithmetic.
2. Jeřábek found an independent basis for the admissible rules of $\mathsf{IPC}$.
3. The admissible rules of $\mathsf{IPC}$ are exactly the rules valid on all intuitionistic frames with reflexive tight predecessors.

## History

Visser and de Jongh introduced the rules $V_n$ and conjectured that they form a basis.
Iemhoff proved the conjecture in 2001, building on Ghilardi's 1999 work on unification in intuitionistic logic.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Intuitionistic logic has inferences that are safe for theorems but are not provable implications.
There are infinitely many such inferences, and one may ask whether a simple list generates them all.
Iemhoff's theorem says yes: one family of rules, found by Visser, generates every safe inference.

### L2 human

1. To use the theorem, show that a rule is derivable from $\mathsf{IPC}$ and the $V_n$. Then it is admissible.
2. Conversely, an admissible rule always has such a derivation.
3. For example, the [[Kreisel-Putnam rule|ZBTCC4C61S]] is admissible, so it is derivable from the $V_n$.

The typical use is to reduce questions about all admissible rules to questions about one explicit family.
A trap: the $V_n$ are applied to substitution instances of their premises, never to hypotheses that are not theorems.

### L3 human

In algebraic terms, the theorem axiomatizes the quasivariety generated by the free Heyting algebra on countably many generators.
The axioms are the quasi-identities that correspond to the $V_n$.
The proof uses Ghilardi's projective approximations, which make unification in Heyting algebras finitary.
