## Statement

Let $(M, g)$ be a strongly asymptotically predictable spacetime with a black hole, and let $H$ be its future [[event horizon|D8NZ32Z3SQ]].
Assume the null [[energy condition|ENWD1YR20P]]: $\operatorname{Ric}(k, k) \ge 0$ for every null vector $k$, where $\operatorname{Ric}$ is the Ricci curvature of $g$.
Let $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ be [[Cauchy surfaces|R8KDE9QYNP]] of the globally hyperbolic region, with $\Sigma_2$ to the future of $\Sigma_1$.
Then
$$\operatorname{Area}(H \cap \Sigma_2) \ge \operatorname{Area}(H \cap \Sigma_1).$$

## Remarks

The horizon is generated by null geodesics with no future end points.
If their expansion $\theta$ were negative somewhere, the [[Raychaudhuri equation|23Y7ZAFN7B]] with the null energy condition would force neighbouring generators to focus in finite affine parameter.
A small outward deformation of the horizon cross-section would then be a trapped surface visible from infinity, which strong asymptotic predictability rules out.
So $\theta \ge 0$ everywhere on $H$, and the area grows.
Hawking proved the theorem in 1971 assuming enough smoothness of the horizon; later work removed that assumption.

Example: when two Schwarzschild black holes of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ merge to one of mass $m$, the theorem gives $m^2 \ge m_1^2 + m_2^2$, since the horizon area is $16\pi m^2$ in units $G = c = 1$.
So at most a fraction $1 - \sqrt{m_1^2 + m_2^2}/(m_1 + m_2)$ of the total mass can be radiated, about $29\%$ for equal masses.

The null energy condition is necessary.
Quantum fields can violate it, and an evaporating black hole emitting [[Hawking radiation|Z1Y6E4YCTZ]] loses area.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theorem says that the surface area of a black hole can never decrease.
Black holes can grow by swallowing matter or by merging, but in classical physics they can never shrink or split.
This one-way behaviour resembles the growth of entropy in thermodynamics.

### L2 human

The area theorem is the second of the [[laws of black hole mechanics|46QNBD585F]].
Its analogy with the second law of thermodynamics led Bekenstein to propose that a black hole has an entropy proportional to its area, made precise as the [[Bekenstein–Hawking entropy|ANNJ7JR36T]].
Its proof is a typical use of the focusing of null geodesics, the same tool as in the [[Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems|WP4YAHJ6V4]].
