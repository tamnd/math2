## Summary

Depth-first search (DFS) explores a [[graph|00C0BR7D7H]] by going as deep as possible along each path before backtracking.
It runs in time $O(\abs{V} + \abs{E})$ and visits every vertex reachable from the start.
The order in which it enters and leaves vertices carries structural information.
This underlies algorithms for [[topological sorting|HMK2MT9FYZ]], cycle detection, strongly connected components and biconnected components.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To explore a maze, walk forward as long as you can.
When you hit a dead end, go back to the last junction with an unexplored corridor.
DFS does exactly this in a graph.
From a vertex, it moves to an unvisited neighbour, and only when all neighbours are visited does it step back.
Every corridor is walked at most twice, once forward and once back.
