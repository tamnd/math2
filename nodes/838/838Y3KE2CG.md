## Proof

We prove the two implications separately.

1. If $(a_n)$ converges, then it is a [[Cauchy sequence|0QZMTN5AXX]], because [[convergent sequences are Cauchy|0QY87R9CY3]].
2. Conversely, assume that $(a_n)$ is a Cauchy sequence. Then $(a_n)$ is bounded, because [[Cauchy sequences are bounded|0G6D8E7CTA]].
3. By the [[Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem|0KDJD249DT]], there is a [[subsequence|0R8J3ZQMJS]] $(a_{n_k})$ that converges to a real number $L$.
4. The indices satisfy $n_0 < n_1 < \cdots$. By [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $k$, $n_k \ge k$ for all $k$: $n_0 \ge 0$, and $n_k \ge k$ gives $n_{k+1} > k$, so $n_{k+1} \ge k + 1$.
5. Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $(a_n)$ is Cauchy, there is $N_1$ with $|a_m - a_n| < \varepsilon/2$ for all $m, n \ge N_1$.
6. By the [[epsilon-N definition|09G6KHS6GY]], there is $K$ with $|a_{n_k} - L| < \varepsilon/2$ for all $k \ge K$.
7. Let $n \ge N_1$. Choose $k = \max(K, N_1)$. By step 4, $n_k \ge k \ge N_1$.
8. By the [[triangle inequality|03HF5PMY5T]] and steps 5, 6 and 7,
$$|a_n - L| \le |a_n - a_{n_k}| + |a_{n_k} - L| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.$$
9. So $a_n \to L$, and $(a_n)$ is a [[convergent sequence|0AZ3R52593]].
10. Steps 1 and 9 prove that $(a_n)$ converges if and only if it is a Cauchy sequence.

## Idea

A Cauchy sequence is bounded, so the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem gives a subsequence with a limit $L$. The terms of the subsequence get close to $L$. All late terms of the sequence are close to the late terms of the subsequence, so they are close to $L$ too.
