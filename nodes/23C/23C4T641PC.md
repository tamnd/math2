## Example

The [[triangle inequality|03HF5PMY5T]] for real numbers states
$$|a + b| \le |a| + |b| \quad \text{for all real } a, b.$$
We show that the constant $1$ in front of $|a| + |b|$ is [[sharp|KWQV0G0VJ3]], and we find all the equality cases.

1. The bound holds.
   For each real $t$ the [[absolute value|0FFXKTKKQC]] gives $-|t| \le t \le |t|$.
   Add the cases $t = a$ and $t = b$:
   $$-(|a| + |b|) \le a + b \le |a| + |b|,$$
   and this says $|a + b| \le |a| + |b|$.
2. The bound is attained.
   At $a = 2$ and $b = 3$ the left side is $|5| = 5$ and the right side is $2 + 3 = 5$.
3. No smaller constant works.
   Let $c < 1$.
   At $a = 1$ and $b = 0$ the bound $|a + b| \le c(|a| + |b|)$ would say $1 \le c$, which is false.
4. We find the equality cases.
   Both sides are nonnegative, so we may compare their squares:
   $$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2, \qquad (|a| + |b|)^2 = a^2 + 2|ab| + b^2.$$
   They are equal exactly when $ab = |ab|$, that is, when $ab \ge 0$.

So the constant $1$ is best possible, and the extremal cases are the pairs $(a, b)$ in which $a$ and $b$ do not have opposite signs.

## Remarks

Sharpness is a statement about the whole class of pairs, not about each pair.
At $a = 1$ and $b = -1$ the left side is $0$ and the right side is $2$, so for this pair the bound is far from the truth.

The same proof works for vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the Euclidean length.
There the equality cases are the pairs in which one vector is a nonnegative multiple of the other, the vector form of "same sign".
The reverse triangle inequality $\big||a| - |b|\big| \le |a - b|$ is also sharp: $a = 2$ and $b = 1$ give $1 = 1$.

If you shrink the class to pairs with $ab < 0$, the equality cases disappear.
The constant $1$ is still sharp on that class, since $a = 1$ and $b = -\varepsilon$ give the ratio $\frac{1 - \varepsilon}{1 + \varepsilon}$, which tends to $1$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$, but it is no longer attained.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The distance from $0$ to $a + b$ is at most the distance $|a|$ plus the distance $|b|$.
For $2$ and $3$ both sides equal $5$.
For $2$ and $-3$ the left side is $1$ and the right side is $5$.
Because the two sides are equal for $2$ and $3$, no smaller bound of this kind can be true.

### L2 human

In the plane the triangle inequality says that one side of a triangle is at most the sum of the other two sides.
Put $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (1, 0)$ and $C = (2, 0)$ on one line, and measure:
$$AC = 2, \qquad AB + BC = 1 + 1 = 2.$$
So the bound is attained by a degenerate triangle, with $B$ on the segment $AC$.
For a triangle whose corners are not on one line the inequality is strict.
This means that a sharpness proof needs the degenerate triangles inside the class, and a theorem stated only for true triangles has a sharp but unattained constant.
