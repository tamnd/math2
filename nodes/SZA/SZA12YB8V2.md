## Summary

Nonlinear optics studies light in media whose [[polarization density|ASQAH710WN]] depends nonlinearly on the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] of the light.
The effects appear at high intensities, in practice with lasers.
They include the generation of new frequencies, such as [[second-harmonic generation|4C67FMA3M7]], and an intensity-dependent refractive index, the optical [[Kerr effect|Y7WQY4568B]].
Its mathematical models are nonlinear wave equations, such as the nonlinear Schrödinger equation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In everyday optics, light passes through glass without changing colour, and two beams cross without affecting each other.
This is because the response of the material is proportional to the field.
With a strong laser the response is no longer proportional, and new things happen.
Red light can come out blue, a beam can focus itself, and one beam can steer another.
The first such experiment, in 1961, turned red laser light into ultraviolet light at twice the frequency.
