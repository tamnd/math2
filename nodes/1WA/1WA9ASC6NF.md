## Summary

The Stokes parameters are four real numbers $S_0, S_1, S_2, S_3$ that describe the intensity and the [[polarization|PJ8ZSJ0QDW]] of a beam of light.
They are quadratic in the field and can be measured with a detector, a polarizer and a wave plate.
Unlike a [[Jones vector|D3XTQ5Z2QA]], they also describe partially polarized and unpolarized light.
They are the input of [[Mueller calculus|X17R7F1QH5]] and the coordinates of the [[Poincaré sphere|J1T8NZCVTB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A light detector only sees intensity, not the field itself.
The Stokes parameters are four intensity measurements that together tell the state of polarization.
$S_0$ is the total intensity, $S_1$ compares horizontal and vertical, $S_2$ compares the two diagonals, and $S_3$ compares the two circular states.
For example, light polarized horizontally has $(S_0, S_1, S_2, S_3) = (1, 1, 0, 0)$ in units of its intensity.
Sunlight is nearly unpolarized and has $(1, 0, 0, 0)$.
