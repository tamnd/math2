## Summary

A context-sensitive language is a [[formal language|5MFZ4VNY6D]] generated by a context-sensitive grammar.
The rules of such a grammar may rewrite a nonterminal depending on its neighbours, and they never shorten the string.
This class, of type 1, contains all [[context-free languages|Q56EWEQYE2]] and also languages such as $\set{a^n b^n c^n : n \ge 1}$.
Every context-sensitive language is decidable, and the class is closed under union, intersection and complement.
The context-sensitive languages are exactly the languages decided by nondeterministic Turing machines using linear space.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a context-sensitive grammar a placeholder may be replaced only in a given surrounding, for example $A$ may become $x$ only when a $b$ stands to its left.
Rules never make the string shorter, except for one rule that produces the empty word.
Since strings only grow, one can check membership of a word by trying all strings up to its length.
The language of words $a^n b^n c^n$, with three equal blocks, is context-sensitive but not context-free.
