## Summary

Brewster's angle is the angle of incidence at which light polarized in the plane of incidence is not reflected at all.
The reflection takes place at a flat interface between two transparent media.
For light going from index $n_1$ to index $n_2$ it equals $\arctan(n_2/n_1)$.
Unpolarized light reflected at this angle is therefore fully polarized perpendicular to the plane of incidence.
It is used in polarizers, laser windows and glare-reducing sunglasses.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light reflected from water or glass is partly polarized, and at one special angle it is completely polarized.
At that angle the reflected and refracted rays are at right angles to each other.
The tiny dipoles in the glass then oscillate along the direction where the reflected ray would go, and a dipole sends no light along its own axis.
For air to glass with index $1.5$ the angle is about $56^\circ$ from the normal.
