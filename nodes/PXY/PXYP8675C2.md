## Statement

Let $F$ be a [[number field|MWBDFS2KJ5]], and let $\mathcal{O}_F$ be its ring of integers, the [[integral closure|G0V86SJBQW]] of $\Z$ in $F$.
Then for every $n \ge 0$ the [[algebraic K-group|5GVFZWMWC8]] $K_n(\mathcal{O}_F)$ is a finitely generated abelian group.

## Remarks

In low degrees the statement is classical.
$K_0(\mathcal{O}_F) \cong \Z \oplus \operatorname{Cl}(F)$, and the ideal class group $\operatorname{Cl}(F)$ is finite.
$K_1(\mathcal{O}_F) \cong \mathcal{O}_F^\times$ by the [[Bass–Milnor–Serre theorem|PYPSVTYTZ8]], and this group is finitely generated by [[Dirichlet's unit theorem|1D99CSKX6N]].

Quillen's proof works with the [[Q-construction|J9XP7CS943]] of the finitely generated projective $\mathcal{O}_F$-modules, filtered by rank.
He identifies the successive layers with the homology of the groups $GL_n(\mathcal{O}_F)$ with coefficients in the Steinberg module, which comes from the [[Tits building|6BQJ50NEJ0]] of $GL_n(F)$.
The Borel–Serre compactification shows that these homology groups are finitely generated, so $BQ$ has finitely generated homology.
For this kind of space, finitely generated homology implies finitely generated homotopy groups.

The ranks of the groups $K_n(\mathcal{O}_F)$ were then computed by Borel.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The K-groups of a ring of integers could a priori be huge.
Quillen showed that each of them is built from finitely many generators, as the unit group is by Dirichlet's theorem.
This makes them good arithmetic invariants, comparable to class groups and unit groups.

### L2 human

The theorem generalizes the finiteness of the class number and Dirichlet's unit theorem to all K-groups.
It is the starting point for Borel's computation of ranks and for the conjectures relating K-groups to special values of zeta functions.
The Bass conjecture asks for the same finiteness for all regular rings of finite type over $\Z$, and Quillen's theorem is its case of rings of integers.
