## Summary

The thermal resistance of an object is the temperature difference across it divided by the heat rate through it, in steady state.
It plays the role of electrical resistance, with temperature difference for voltage and heat rate for current.
Resistances in series add, and conductances in parallel add.
So layered walls and insulated pipes can be treated like electric circuits.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A thick wool coat has a high thermal resistance: a large temperature difference drives only a little heat through it.
A thin metal sheet has a low resistance.
For a flat wall, the resistance grows with the thickness and falls with the area and the conductivity of the material.
Two walls one behind the other have a total resistance equal to the sum of the two, just like two resistors in a row.
