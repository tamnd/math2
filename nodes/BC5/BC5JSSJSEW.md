## Summary

Length of day variations are the changes in the time the Earth takes to complete one rotation, compared with the nominal $86400$ SI seconds.
They are a few milliseconds in size.
They come from exchanges of angular momentum with the atmosphere, oceans and core, from tides, and from changes in the moment of inertia.
Tidal friction makes the day longer on average over centuries, while seasonal winds change it by about a millisecond within a year.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A day is not always the same length.
In winter in the Northern Hemisphere strong winds blow eastward, and they carry angular momentum.
The solid Earth then turns slightly slower to keep the total the same, like a skater who passes some of her spin to a partner.
The tides of the Moon act like a very gentle brake, so over millions of years the day has grown longer.
Atomic clocks show these changes, and this is why leap seconds were introduced.
