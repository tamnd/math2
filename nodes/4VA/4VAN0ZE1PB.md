## Summary

Let a fluid flow along a wall whose temperature differs from that of the oncoming fluid.
The fluid temperature then changes from the wall value to the free-stream value within a thin layer next to the wall.
This layer is the thermal boundary layer, a [[boundary layer|YCFPD0GE5G]] for the temperature field.
Almost all of the temperature gradient sits in this layer, so its thickness controls the heat flux at the wall and the [[heat transfer coefficient|J821AD6YTE]].
Its thickness compared with the velocity boundary layer is set by the [[Prandtl number|C4KSBMR413]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a hot plate in a stream of cold air.
Far from the plate the air keeps its cold temperature, and right at the plate it is as hot as the plate.
The change happens in a thin sheet of air hugging the surface, and this sheet thickens as the air moves along the plate.
For air at a few metres per second over a flat plate, the sheet is a few millimetres thick some tens of centimetres from the front edge.
A thinner sheet means a steeper temperature drop, and so a faster flow of heat out of the plate.
