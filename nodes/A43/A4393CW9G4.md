## Summary

The Grothendieck group of vector bundles on a compact Hausdorff space $X$ is an abelian group $K(X)$.
It is built from isomorphism classes of [[vector bundles|P4DZM975BV]] on $X$, with direct sum as addition and formal differences allowed.
It is the group $K^0(X)$ of [[topological K-theory|6XCFW7YGX8]].
Vector bundles only form a monoid; this construction turns them into an abelian group and even a ring.
So the methods of algebra become available for bundles.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Vector bundles can be added by the direct sum, but they cannot be subtracted.
The construction adds formal differences $[E] - [F]$, just as the integers are built from the natural numbers.
Two bundles give the same class when they become isomorphic after adding the same trivial bundle to both.
For a point, a vector bundle is just a vector space, classified by its dimension, so the group is $\Z$.
On the circle, the Möbius line bundle and the trivial line bundle are not isomorphic, yet the sum of two Möbius bundles is trivial, which the group records.
