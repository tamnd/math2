## Summary

The circulation of a flow around a closed curve is the [[line integral|0H5MR9B6GT]] of the velocity along the curve.
By [[Stokes' theorem|0AKRKVHW94]] it equals the flux of [[vorticity|B3SXT9TWFA]] through any surface bounded by the curve.
Circulation around a wing determines its lift.
[[Kelvin's circulation theorem|BWMB87Q6DM]] says that in an ideal fluid it stays constant around a closed curve that moves with the fluid.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Walk once around a closed loop inside a moving fluid and add up how much the fluid pushes you along your path at each step.
The total is the circulation.
Suppose the fluid turns as a whole with angular speed $1$ and the loop is the unit circle.
Then the fluid pushes you along at speed $1$ all the way, and the circulation is the length $2\pi$.
If the fluid moves uniformly in one direction, the pushes on the two halves of the loop cancel, and the circulation is $0$.
