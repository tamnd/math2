## Summary

The Laplace tidal equations are the linear [[shallow water equations|NT73Y95RY5]] on a rotating sphere, forced by the [[tide-generating potential|2E2KCFNMHE]].
They were introduced by Pierre-Simon Laplace and they are the basis of the dynamic theory of the tides.
They include the [[Coriolis force|6HZRX3WSQD]] and the varying depth of the ocean.
They explain features that the [[equilibrium theory|BTZB3SYKJJ]] cannot, such as [[amphidromic points|DYR62DYV08]] and resonances.
Numerical solutions of these equations, with friction added, are the basis of modern global tide models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Moon's pull tries to push water around the globe, but the water has mass and needs time to move.
As it moves, the Earth's rotation deflects it sideways, and the coasts and the varying depth steer it.
Laplace wrote down the equations for this motion in a thin layer of water on a spinning ball.
The answer is a set of waves that sweep around ocean basins, rather than two bulges fixed under the Moon.
