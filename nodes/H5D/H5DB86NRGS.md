## Summary

The Bauschinger effect is the drop of the yield stress in the reverse direction after a material has been plastically deformed in one direction.
Stretch a metal beyond yield and then compress it.
It yields again in compression at a stress of smaller size than the tensile stress it reached.
It shows that [[work hardening|XD47SRCJJF]] is not just a growth of the elastic range but also a shift of it.
In [[plasticity|K8BBMTN0KV]] theory it is modelled by kinematic hardening, a translation of the [[yield surface|CWWCEXZ8BH]] described by a back stress.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pull a metal bar until it stretches permanently, so that it now needs more force to keep stretching.
Then push on it instead.
You might expect it to resist pushing just as strongly, but it gives way in compression sooner.
The material remembers the direction in which it was last deformed, much like a wire that is easier to bend back than to bend further.
