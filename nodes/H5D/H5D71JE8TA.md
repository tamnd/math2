## Summary

A Lie algebra homomorphism is a [[linear map|0NRYHJPQG2]] between two [[Lie algebras|5JJYBJ6A8K]] that preserves the bracket.
Homomorphisms are the maps that let one compare Lie algebras, and bijective ones are isomorphisms.
Their kernels are ideals and their images are subalgebras.
A representation of a Lie algebra is a homomorphism into the Lie algebra of endomorphisms of a vector space.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A homomorphism carries one Lie algebra into another without breaking its rules.
It respects sums and scalar multiples, and it sends the bracket of two elements to the bracket of their images.
For example, the trace sends an $n \times n$ matrix to a number.
Brackets of matrices have trace zero, and brackets of numbers are zero, so the trace is a homomorphism to the one-dimensional Lie algebra.
