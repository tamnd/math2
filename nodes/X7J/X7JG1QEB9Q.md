## Summary

A Waldhausen category is a [[category|A2DE66937D]] with a zero object and two chosen classes of morphisms that satisfy a few axioms.
These classes are the cofibrations and the weak equivalences.
It is the input of Waldhausen's S-construction, which defines algebraic K-theory in this generality.
Exact categories, categories of chain complexes with quasi-isomorphisms, and categories of spaces are all Waldhausen categories.
This allows K-theory of rings, of schemes through perfect complexes, and of spaces to be treated in one framework.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Quillen's K-theory needs short exact sequences, as for modules.
Waldhausen noticed that one only needs a way to form quotients $B/A$ along special maps $A \rightarrowtail B$, and a notion of when two objects should count as the same.
The special maps are the cofibrations, and the second notion is given by the weak equivalences.
For chain complexes, the cofibrations are the degreewise split injections and the weak equivalences are the quasi-isomorphisms.
Then $K_0$ is generated by objects, with $[B] = [A] + [B/A]$ and with weakly equivalent objects identified.
