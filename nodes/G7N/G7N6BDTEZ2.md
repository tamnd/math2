## Summary

Rings and modules is the study of rings that need not be commutative, of the modules over them, and of algebras in which the product need not be associative.
A ring is a set with an addition that makes it an abelian group and an associative multiplication that distributes over the addition.
A module over a ring is an abelian group on which the ring acts by multiplication with scalars.
Modules give one setting for vector spaces, abelian groups, ideals and the representations of groups, and noncommutative rings occur as matrix rings, group rings and rings of differential operators.
Wedderburn classified the semisimple finite-dimensional algebras in 1908, and Artin extended the result to rings with the descending chain condition in 1927.
This topic collects the rings and modules pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Commutative algebra, representation theory, Lie theory and homological algebra have their own topics.
