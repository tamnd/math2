## Statement

The theorem turns the infinite check "every length is kept" into a finite check on one orthonormal basis, or into one matrix equation.
Let $V$ and $W$ be finite-dimensional [[inner product spaces|01EK923SKP]] over $\mathbb{K} = \R$ or $\C$, with the [[induced norms|0KG4D8AP6G]].
Let $T \colon V \to W$ be a linear map, and let $T^* \colon W \to V$ be its [[adjoint|0ST8QF9T3T]].
Then the following five conditions are equivalent.

1. $T$ is a [[linear isometry|0717XXPWM6]]: $\norm{Tv} = \norm{v}$ for all $v \in V$.
2. $T$ keeps inner products: $\ip{Tu}{Tv} = \ip{u}{v}$ for all $u, v \in V$.
3. $T$ sends every [[orthonormal family|09VYHEF2VT]] of $V$ to an orthonormal family of $W$.
4. There is an [[orthonormal basis|0FWJ0VW561]] $e_1, \dots, e_n$ of $V$ such that $Te_1, \dots, Te_n$ is an orthonormal family of $W$.
5. $T^*T = I_V$, where $I_V$ is the identity map of $V$.

If $\dim V = \dim W$, then the five conditions are also equivalent to each of the following two conditions.

6. $T$ is invertible and $T^{-1} = T^*$.
7. $TT^* = I_W$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The equal dimensions matter for conditions 6 and 7.
The [[inclusion $\iota \colon \R^2 \to \R^3$|X132TDT8X2]], $(x, y) \mapsto (x, y, 0)$, satisfies conditions 1 to 5, but $\iota\iota^*$ is the projection onto the plane $z = 0$, so condition 7 fails.
In the other direction, the map $\iota^* \colon \R^3 \to \R^2$, $(x, y, z) \mapsto (x, y)$, satisfies $\iota^*(\iota^*)^* = \iota^*\iota = I$, so it has the form of condition 7, yet it sends $(0, 0, 1)$ to $0$ and is no isometry.

In condition 4 the starting basis must be orthonormal.
The linear map of $\R^2$ with $T(1, 0) = (1, 0)$ and $T(1, 1) = (0, 1)$ sends a basis to an orthonormal basis, but $\norm{T(1, 1)} = 1$ while $\norm{(1, 1)} = \sqrt{2}$.
The family must also be a whole basis.
The projection $(x, y) \mapsto (x, 0)$ sends the orthonormal family consisting of $(1, 0)$ alone to an orthonormal family, but it sends $(0, 1)$ to $0$.

Finite dimension is used to get an orthonormal basis for condition 4 and to use rank-nullity for conditions 6 and 7.

### What it gives you

Let $A$ be the matrix of $T$ in orthonormal bases of $V$ and $W$.
Then $A^*$ is the matrix of $T^*$, so $T$ is a linear isometry exactly when $A^*A = I$, that is, when the columns of $A$ are orthonormal.
For $V = W = \R^n$ this gives the [[conditions for an orthogonal matrix|F7M8ZXB4P7]], and for $V = W = \C^n$ the [[conditions for a unitary matrix|GZ7NG7J3J8]].
It also follows that the inverse of a linear isometry of a finite-dimensional inner product space onto itself is again a linear isometry, since $T^{-1} = T^*$ and $(T^*)^* T^* = T T^* = I$.

### Generalizations

Conditions 1, 2 and 3 are equivalent for all inner product spaces, with the same proof.
For a bounded operator $T$ between Hilbert spaces, condition 1 is equivalent to $T^*T = I$.
In infinite dimensions $T^*T = I$ does not give $TT^* = I$, even when $V = W$.
The unilateral shift on $\ell^2$ is the standard example, see [[unitary operators|DJHVKR63H5]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A linear map keeps all lengths exactly when it keeps one set of perpendicular unit arrows perpendicular and of length one.
So you only need to look at a few arrows.
For example, if a map of the plane sends $(1, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$ and $(0, 1)$ to $(-1, 0)$, then the two images are perpendicular arrows of length $1$.
The map is the quarter turn, and it keeps every length.

### L2 human

Here is a typical use: test $T(x, y) = \frac{1}{13}(5x - 12y, 12x + 5y)$ on $\R^2$ with condition 4.
Take the standard basis $e_1 = (1, 0)$ and $e_2 = (0, 1)$, which is orthonormal.
Compute the images and their inner products:
$$\norm{Te_1}^2 = \frac{25 + 144}{169} = 1, \qquad \norm{Te_2}^2 = \frac{144 + 25}{169} = 1, \qquad \ip{Te_1}{Te_2} = \frac{-60 + 60}{169} = 0.$$
So $Te_1 = \frac{1}{13}(5, 12)$ and $Te_2 = \frac{1}{13}(-12, 5)$ are orthonormal, and $T$ is a linear isometry by the theorem.
Check with one vector: $T(13, 0) = (5, 12)$, and both vectors have length $13$.
Condition 4 took three short computations, while condition 1 is a claim about every vector.
Watch out: condition 4 needs an orthonormal basis at the start, not only orthonormal images.

### L3 human

Condition 5 says that $T$ has a left inverse and that this left inverse is the adjoint.
The $m \times n$ matrices $A$ with $A^*A = I_n$ are the matrices with orthonormal columns, and they form the Stiefel manifold of orthonormal $n$-frames in $\mathbb{K}^m$.
For $m = n$ these are the [[orthogonal group|WWK2E8BRGF]] and the [[unitary group|VW5H9XFNJB]].
The gap between conditions 5 and 7 is the gap between an isometry and a unitary operator, and in operator theory it is described by the Wold decomposition of an isometry into a unitary part and shifts.
