## Summary

Quantum decoherence is the loss of interference between the parts of a superposition when a system interacts with its environment.
In the reduced [[density matrix|V1JTB52E8C]] of the system, the off-diagonal entries in a preferred basis shrink towards zero, while the diagonal entries stay.
It explains why an [[open quantum system|6D9YCNZ7X9]] in contact with many degrees of freedom shows classical statistics in that basis.
It is a main obstacle to building quantum computers, and it is central in discussions of the measurement problem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A qubit in a superposition of $0$ and $1$ can show interference, a typically quantum effect.
If the qubit touches its surroundings, the surroundings keep a small record of whether it is $0$ or $1$.
Once that record exists, the two parts of the superposition can no longer interfere, and the qubit behaves like a coin that is $0$ or $1$ with some probabilities.
For example, if the environment ends in two orthogonal states for $0$ and $1$, the qubit alone is described by a diagonal density matrix.
Nothing has collapsed: the joint state of qubit and environment is still a pure superposition.
