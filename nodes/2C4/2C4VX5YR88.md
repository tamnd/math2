## Definition

Let $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ be the [[Minkowski metric|G49G4R1K04]] on $\R^4$ with coordinates $x^0 = t, x^1, x^2, x^3$, in units $c = 1$.
In [[linearized gravity|AH62KSWA6Y]] the metric is $g = \eta + h$ with a symmetric tensor field $h_{\mu\nu}$, and only terms linear in $h$ are kept.
A *gravitational wave* in vacuum is a symmetric tensor field $h_{\mu\nu}$ such that:

1. $h$ satisfies the linearised vacuum Einstein equations, that is, the linearisation at $\eta$ of the Ricci tensor vanishes at $h$;
2. $h$ is not pure gauge, that is, $h_{\mu\nu}$ is not of the form $\partial_\mu \xi_\nu + \partial_\nu \xi_\mu$ for a vector field $\xi$.

## Remarks

Every such $h$ can be changed by a pure gauge term to a field in transverse-traceless (TT) gauge: $h_{0\mu} = 0$, $\partial^j h_{jk} = 0$ and $h_{jj} = 0$ (sum over $j$).
In TT gauge condition 1 becomes the [[wave equation|5EHD2XBQDG]] $\square h_{jk} = 0$ with $\square = -\partial_t^2 + \Delta$.
So the waves travel at the speed of light and have two independent polarisations, called plus and cross.

Example: let $A \ne 0$ and $\omega > 0$, and put $h_{11} = -h_{22} = A \cos(\omega(t - x^3))$, all other components $0$.
Each component is a function of $t - x^3$, so $\square h_{jk} = 0$.
The trace $h_{11} + h_{22}$ is $0$, and $h$ has no $t$ or $x^3$ components and does not depend on $x^1, x^2$, so $\partial^j h_{jk} = 0$.
This is a plus-polarised plane wave moving in the $x^3$ direction; it is not pure gauge, because its linearised Riemann tensor, with components such as $R_{1010} = -\frac{1}{2}\partial_t^2 h_{11}$, is not zero.

Non-example: $h_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \xi_\nu + \partial_\nu \xi_\mu$ satisfies clause 1 for every $\xi$, since it only describes a change of coordinates, but it fails clause 2.
Its linearised curvature vanishes, and it carries no energy.
