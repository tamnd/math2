## Proof

We prove the formula when $x_1, \dots, x_n$ are variables, by induction on $n$, and then we substitute values from any commutative ring.

1. Let $P_n = \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} (x_j - x_i)$, and let $V_n$ be the Vandermonde matrix of $x_1, \dots, x_n$.
   We first prove $\det V_n = P_n$ in the [[polynomial ring|0Q9EZS90GY]] $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, by induction on $n$.
2. For $n = 1$, $\det V_1 = \det(1) = 1$, and $P_1 = 1$ is an empty product.
3. Let $n \ge 2$, and assume $\det V_{n-1} = P_{n-1}$.
   Let $L$ be the [[field of fractions|0VF11ZTTMD]] of $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}]$.
4. Replace $x_n$ by a new variable $t$ in $V_n$, and let $g(t) \in L[t]$ be the determinant of the result.
5. By the [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]] along row $n$, whose entries are $1, t, \dots, t^{n-1}$,
$$g(t) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} t^{j-1} C_{nj},$$
   where the cofactors $C_{nj}$ come from the first $n - 1$ rows and do not involve $t$.
6. So $g$ has degree at most $n - 1$.
   Its coefficient of $t^{n-1}$ is $C_{nn} = (-1)^{2n} \det V_{n-1} = P_{n-1}$, by the induction hypothesis.
7. For $i < n$, the value $g(x_i)$ is the determinant of a matrix whose rows $i$ and $n$ are both $(1, x_i, \dots, x_i^{n-1})$.
   So $g(x_i) = 0$, by the [[rule for equal rows|0K4VQB7HWR]].
8. Let
$$h(t) = g(t) - P_{n-1} \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} (t - x_i).$$
   The product has degree $n - 1$ with leading coefficient $1$, so by step 6 the terms of degree $n - 1$ cancel.
   Hence $h = 0$ or $h$ has degree at most $n - 2$.
9. By step 7, $h(x_i) = 0 - 0 = 0$ for the $n - 1$ distinct elements $x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}$ of the field $L$.
10. A nonzero polynomial over a field has at most as many roots as its [[degree|0KJVQV3E0F]].
   Since $h$ has $n - 1$ roots and degree at most $n - 2$ if it is nonzero, we get $h = 0$.
11. So $g(t) = P_{n-1} \prod_{i<n} (t - x_i)$, and setting $t = x_n$ gives
$$\det V_n = P_{n-1} \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} (x_n - x_i) = P_n.$$
   This completes the induction.
12. Now let $K$ be any commutative ring and $a_1, \dots, a_n \in K$.
   The map $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n] \to K$ with $x_i \mapsto a_i$ is a ring homomorphism.
13. By the [[naturality of the determinant|03Q7RE2HXR]], this homomorphism sends $\det V_n$ to the Vandermonde determinant of $a_1, \dots, a_n$.
   It sends $P_n$ to $\prod_{i<j} (a_j - a_i)$, so the formula holds in $K$.

## Idea

View the determinant as a polynomial in the last point $t$.
It has degree $n - 1$, because $t$ appears only in the last row, with powers up to $t^{n-1}$.
It vanishes when $t$ equals any earlier point, because then two rows agree.
A polynomial of degree $n - 1$ with these $n - 1$ roots is its leading coefficient times $\prod_{i<n} (t - x_i)$.
The leading coefficient is the cofactor of the corner entry, which is the smaller Vandermonde determinant, and induction finishes the proof.

For $n = 3$ with points $1, 2, t$:
$$\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 1 & t & t^2 \end{pmatrix} = t^2 - 3t + 2 = (t - 1)(t - 2),$$
with leading coefficient $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = 1$.

## Variants

Another version compares degrees instead of counting roots.
Each $x_j - x_i$ divides $\det V$, because $\det V$ vanishes when $x_j = x_i$, and these factors are distinct primes of $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$.
The determinant is homogeneous of degree $0 + 1 + \dots + (n - 1) = n(n-1)/2$, which is also the number of factors.
So $\det V$ is a constant times $P_n$.
The monomial $x_2 x_3^2 \cdots x_n^{n-1}$ comes only from the diagonal in $\det V$, and only from choosing $x_j$ in every factor of $P_n$, with coefficient $1$ both times, so the constant is $1$.
That version needs unique factorization in $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$.
