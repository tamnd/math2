## Summary

A participating medium is a material that absorbs, emits and scatters [[thermal radiation|S6XR2K4N8G]] along the path of a ray.
Examples are a combustion gas, a cloud of soot and a semitransparent solid.
Radiative heat transfer in such a medium is governed by the [[radiative transfer equation|EX9YYRMAJA]] for the spectral intensity at each point and in each direction.
The divergence of the resulting radiative heat flux enters the energy equation of the medium.
It matters in furnaces, flames, glass manufacture, the atmosphere and stars.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Between two hot walls in a vacuum, radiation travels in straight lines without loss.
In the flame of a furnace, the hot gas and soot also absorb some of the radiation, add their own glow, and scatter it in new directions.
So the gas itself takes part in the exchange of heat by radiation, which is where the name comes from.
Water vapour and carbon dioxide in the air do the same, which is the basis of the greenhouse effect.
