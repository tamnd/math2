## Proof

We prove parts 1 and 4 and reduce part 3 to Monk's construction; parts 2 and 5 and the construction itself are cited.
Write $\mathrm{Re}(U)$ for the [[full relation algebra|BQ9EEFPS10]] on $U$, so $A \in \mathsf{RRA}$ iff $A$ embeds in a product $\prod_{i \in I} \mathrm{Re}(U_i)$.

**Part 1.**

1. Let $A \in \mathsf{RRA}$ with an embedding $e : A \to \prod_{i \in I} \mathrm{Re}(U_i)$, and let $B$ be a subalgebra of $A$. The restriction of $e$ to $B$ is an embedding, so $B \in \mathsf{RRA}$.
2. Let $A_j \in \mathsf{RRA}$ for $j \in J$, with embeddings $e_j : A_j \to \prod_{i \in I_j} \mathrm{Re}(U_{j,i})$.
3. The map $(a_j)_j \mapsto (e_j(a_j))_j$ embeds $\prod_j A_j$ in $\prod_{j} \prod_{i \in I_j} \mathrm{Re}(U_{j,i})$, since operations in products are computed coordinatewise.
4. The target is a product of full relation algebras indexed by the pairs $(j, i)$, so $\prod_j A_j \in \mathsf{RRA}$.

**Part 2.** Tarski proved that a homomorphic image of an algebra in $\mathsf{RRA}$ is again in $\mathsf{RRA}$.
With part 1 and Birkhoff's theorem, $\mathsf{RRA}$ is a variety.

**Part 3.**

5. Monk constructed relation algebras $A_k$, $k \in \omega$, none of them representable, such that a nonprincipal [[ultraproduct|AX9Q86GB8M]] $A^* = \prod_k A_k / D$ is representable.
6. Suppose a finite set $\Sigma$ of first-order sentences axiomatizes $\mathsf{RRA}$ within the class of all algebras of the signature, and let $\sigma$ be their conjunction.
7. Each $A_k$ is not representable, so $A_k \models \neg \sigma$.
8. By [[Łoś's theorem|AGYBSTD7BG]], $A^* \models \neg \sigma$, so $A^*$ is not representable. This contradicts step 5.

**Part 4.**

9. The class $\mathsf{RA}$ is axiomatized by the finitely many equations of [[the definition|RDSY11X6N2]].
10. If $\mathsf{RRA} = \mathsf{RA}$, these equations would axiomatize $\mathsf{RRA}$, against part 3.

**Part 5.** Hirsch and Hodkinson reduce a known undecidable problem to representability of finite relation algebras, as shown in their book.
$\blacksquare$

## Idea

Representability is preserved by subalgebras and products by definition, and Tarski's theorem adds homomorphic images.
Monk's argument is compactness in algebraic form: if finitely many sentences defined $\mathsf{RRA}$, both $\mathsf{RRA}$ and its complement would be closed under ultraproducts.
The algebras $A_k$ fail to be representable only for a reason that needs about $k$ points to detect, and in the limit the obstruction disappears.

## Variants

1. Lyndon's earlier route to part 4 is a direct construction of a finite relation algebra and a proof that it has no representation.
2. Lyndon gave an infinite list of first-order conditions, the Lyndon conditions, such that a finite relation algebra is representable iff it satisfies all of them.
3. The same pattern of proof gives the corresponding results for [[cylindric algebras of finite dimension|WZ1BZYVD5G]].
