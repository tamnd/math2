## Statement

For $n \ge 1$, let $F_n$ be the set of the $2^{2^n}$ [[Boolean functions|0X05GS525E]] $f \colon \set{0,1}^n \to \set{0,1}$.
Let $\mathrm{size}(f)$ be the [[circuit complexity|4PGX7PHJCK]] of $f$ over the basis of fan-in-two AND and OR gates and NOT gates.
Then the fraction of the functions $f \in F_n$ with
$$\mathrm{size}(f) \le \frac{2^n}{10 n}$$
tends to $0$ as $n \to \infty$.

## Remarks

The proof counts circuits.
A circuit with $t \le s$ gates is described by giving, for each gate, its type among three and its at most two inputs.
These inputs are chosen among the $n + t$ inputs and gates, and the last gate is the output.
So there are at most $(s + 1)\big(3 (n + s)^2\big)^{s}$ circuits with at most $s$ gates.
For $s = 2^n / (10 n)$ and large $n$, one has $n + s \le 2^n$, so the logarithm in base $2$ of this number is at most $s (2n + 2) + \log_2(s + 1) \le 2^n / 4$.
So at most $2^{2^n / 4}$ functions have circuits of size $s$, out of $2^{2^n}$ functions.

The bound is sharp up to a constant: Lupanov showed that every function in $F_n$ has circuit size $O(2^n / n)$.
The argument proves that hard functions exist but does not name one.

## Explanations

### L1 human

There are far more Boolean functions of $n$ bits than there are small circuits.
So almost every function needs a circuit with about $2^n / n$ gates, which is exponential in $n$.
The surprise is that, although almost every function is hard, no one can prove that any specific function in NP is hard.

### L2 human

Claude Shannon gave the counting argument in 1949, in his work on switching circuits.
It is a model for many existence proofs in complexity theory.
It also shows that the class [[P/poly|P0A5DVRYDW]] does not contain all languages.
The gap between this lower bound for random functions and the linear lower bounds known for explicit functions is the central difficulty of [[circuit complexity|4PGX7PHJCK]].
