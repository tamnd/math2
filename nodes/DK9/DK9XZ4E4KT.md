## Statement

Let $d \ge 2$ and let $\mathcal{G}$ be a finite subset of the [[special unitary group|CZCWFPNS4N]] $SU(d)$ with these two properties.

1. $\mathcal{G}$ is closed under inverses.
2. The subgroup generated by $\mathcal{G}$ is [[dense|0BSJSX7ME2]] in $SU(d)$.

Then there are constants $C > 0$ and $c > 0$, depending only on $\mathcal{G}$ and $d$, with the following property.
For every $U \in SU(d)$ and every $\varepsilon \in (0, 1/2)$, there are $g_1, \dots, g_\ell \in \mathcal{G}$ with $\ell \le C \log^c(1/\varepsilon)$ and
$$\norm{U - g_1 g_2 \cdots g_\ell} \le \varepsilon,$$
where $\norm{\cdot}$ is the [[operator norm|06EKW1AKXF]].

## Remarks

Proof idea: density and compactness give a finite word length $\ell_0$ whose words form an $\varepsilon_0$-net of $SU(d)$.
To improve an approximation $V$ of $U$, write $U V^{-1}$, which is close to the identity, as a group commutator $A B A^{-1} B^{-1}$ of elements close to the identity.
Approximate $A$ and $B$ recursively; the error of the commutator is much smaller than the errors of $A$ and $B$.
Each level multiplies the length by $5$ and turns an error $\varepsilon$ into about $\varepsilon^{3/2}$, which gives $c = \log 5 / \log(3/2) \approx 3.97$.
The proof is constructive and gives an algorithm that finds the sequence in time polylogarithmic in $1/\varepsilon$.

Example: in quantum computing, $\set{H, T}$ with the Hadamard gate $H$ and $T = \operatorname{diag}(1, e^{i\pi/4})$ generates a dense subgroup of $SU(2)$ modulo phases.
So every one-qubit gate can be approximated to accuracy $\varepsilon$ by $O(\log^c(1/\varepsilon))$ gates $H$, $T$ and $T^{-1}$.

Density alone gives some approximating word; the content of the theorem is the polylogarithmic bound on its length.
The standard proof uses the inverses in hypothesis 1 to form commutators.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A quantum computer can only build a few gates exactly.
If these gates generate all one-qubit operations approximately, one might fear that a very long sequence is needed for high accuracy.
The theorem says that the length grows only like a power of $\log(1/\varepsilon)$.
So any circuit can be compiled into a fixed gate set with modest overhead.

### L2 human

The theorem is a statement about compact Lie groups, used as a compilation tool.
A circuit of $m$ arbitrary gates, compiled to total accuracy $\varepsilon$, needs $O(m \log^c(m/\varepsilon))$ gates from a universal set.
This makes the class [[BQP|VP21SFE7ZA]] independent of the choice of a universal gate set.
Counting arguments show that length at least of order $\log(1/\varepsilon)$ is needed, so the exponent $c$ cannot go below $1$.
