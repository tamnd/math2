## Summary

Celestial mechanics is the part of mechanics that studies the motion of planets, moons, stars and spacecraft under gravity.
Its basic model is [[Newton's law of universal gravitation|29WRN5J730]] applied to point masses, which leads to the [[n-body problem|62SNWC5E45]].
The [[Kepler problem|TYXD1T6FRX]] is solved exactly, and most other questions are attacked by perturbation theory, qualitative dynamics and numerical integration.
The field gave rise to much of the theory of dynamical systems, including the work of Poincaré on chaos.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Celestial mechanics answers questions such as where a planet will be next year or how to send a probe to Mars.
Its starting point is that every body pulls every other body with a force that weakens with the square of the distance.
For one planet around the Sun the answer is a fixed ellipse, as Kepler found.
With more bodies the small pulls between planets slowly change the ellipses, and computing these changes is the main work of the field.
