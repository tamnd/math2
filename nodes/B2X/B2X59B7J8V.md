## Statement

Let $G$ be a group and let $R$ be a ring.
Let $E_{\mathcal{VC}}G$ be a classifying space for the family of virtually cyclic subgroups of $G$.
That is, $E_{\mathcal{VC}}G$ is a $G$-CW complex whose fixed point set $(E_{\mathcal{VC}}G)^H$ is contractible when $H \le G$ is virtually cyclic and empty otherwise.
Write $H^G_n(-; \mathbf{K}_R)$ for the $G$-equivariant homology theory of Davis and Lück with coefficients in [[algebraic K-theory|2C1M0NWPB0]] of $R$.

1. (K-theory) The [[assembly map|2KT6XR8MCZ]] $H^G_n(E_{\mathcal{VC}}G; \mathbf{K}_R) \to H^G_n(\mathrm{pt}; \mathbf{K}_R) = K_n(R[G])$ is an isomorphism for every $n \in \Z$.
2. (L-theory) If $R$ is a [[ring with involution|7W52X4N3NJ]], the assembly map $H^G_n(E_{\mathcal{VC}}G; \mathbf{L}^{\langle -\infty \rangle}_R) \to L^{\langle -\infty \rangle}_n(R[G])$ in [[L-theory|P5CRN7BZV0]] is an isomorphism for every $n \in \Z$.

## Remarks

Farrell and Jones stated the conjecture in their 1993 paper on isomorphism conjectures in algebraic K-theory.
It is open in general.
It is known for large classes of groups, including hyperbolic groups and CAT(0) groups, by work of Bartels, Lück, Reich and Wegner.
For torsion-free $G$ and regular $R$, the K-theory part reduces to the classical assembly map $H_n(BG; K(R)) \to K_n(R[G])$ being an isomorphism.
For torsion-free $G$ it implies that the Whitehead group $Wh(G)$, the reduced group $\widetilde{K}_0(\Z[G])$ and the negative K-groups of $\Z[G]$ vanish.
The L-theory part implies the Novikov conjecture for $G$.
The two parts together imply the Borel conjecture for closed aspherical manifolds of dimension at least $5$ with fundamental group $G$.
The K-theory part implies the Kaplansky idempotent conjecture for torsion-free $G$ and fields of characteristic zero.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The K-theory of a group ring is a basic invariant in the topology of manifolds, but it is very hard to compute.
The conjecture says that it can be assembled from two kinds of simpler data.
These are the K-theory of the coefficient ring and the K-theory of group rings of the virtually cyclic subgroups of $G$, glued together by equivariant homology.
If it holds for a group, many questions about manifolds with that fundamental group get definite answers.
