## Summary

The BBGKY hierarchy is a system of equations for the reduced distribution functions of a system of $N$ interacting classical particles.
The equation for the distribution of $s$ particles involves the distribution of $s + 1$ particles, so the system forms a chain of $N$ coupled equations.
It is exactly equivalent to the Liouville equation for the full $N$-particle density.
Closing the chain by an approximation leads to the kinetic equations, such as the [[Boltzmann equation|MH6CSWD7F0]] and the [[Vlasov equation|SJH7M2VCTE]].
The name comes from Bogoliubov, Born, Green, Kirkwood and Yvon.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Following all the molecules of a gas is hopeless, so one asks simpler questions: where is a typical single molecule, and with what speed?
The answer for one molecule depends on how it meets a second molecule, so one needs the joint law of two molecules.
That in turn depends on a third, and so on: this is the hierarchy.
In practice one cuts the chain, for example by assuming that two molecules about to collide are independent.
That cut leads from the hierarchy to the Boltzmann equation.
