## Summary

K-theory studies a space, a ring or an operator algebra through the abelian groups built from its vector bundles, projective modules or projections.
The first group $K_0$ is the [[Grothendieck group|5FDMY7PDYY]] of the objects under direct sum.
There are three main branches: [[algebraic K-theory|5GVFZWMWC8]] of rings, [[topological K-theory|6XCFW7YGX8]] of spaces and [[operator K-theory|DQZ8S67ZE6]] of C*-algebras.
The theory links algebra, topology, number theory and index theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Many mathematical objects come with pieces that can be added by a direct sum but not subtracted.
K-theory makes these pieces into an abelian group by allowing formal differences.
The group remembers how many different kinds of pieces there are, up to adding trivial ones.
For a field, the pieces are finite-dimensional vector spaces, and the group is $\Z$, given by the dimension.
For a sphere, the pieces are vector bundles, and the group detects twisting that the dimension cannot see.
