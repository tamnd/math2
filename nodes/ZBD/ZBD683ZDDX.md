## Summary

Relativistic mechanics is the mechanics of particles and bodies that is compatible with [[special relativity|8CJ62QP9SN]].
It replaces the momentum $m\vec v$ of Newtonian mechanics by the [[four-momentum|67N6B8MJ3X]] $m u$, where $u$ is the [[four-velocity|Y59V040THG]].
It replaces Newton's second law by the equation $dp/d\tau = F$ along the world line.
Its laws keep the same form in all inertial frames.
They reduce to [[classical mechanics|68W1NWECX8]] when all speeds are small compared with the speed of light $c$.
It is needed for fast particles, as in particle accelerators and cosmic rays.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Newton's laws work very well for cars and planets, but they fail for particles that move at a good fraction of the speed of light.
Relativistic mechanics fixes this: no particle with mass can be pushed up to the speed of light, however long a force acts on it.
Its momentum $\gamma m \vec v$ grows without bound as the speed approaches $c$, where $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$.
For example, an electron at $99\%$ of the speed of light has $\gamma \approx 7.1$, so it carries about seven times its Newtonian momentum.
