## Summary

Modern portfolio theory is the theory of choosing a portfolio of risky assets by trading off expected return against risk.
Risk is measured by the [[variance|0477R9PYAE]] of the return.
It was introduced by Harry Markowitz in 1952.
Its central object is the mean–variance problem: minimise the variance of a portfolio for a given expected return.
Its main lesson is diversification: combining assets that are not perfectly correlated lowers risk without lowering expected return.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Do not put all your eggs in one basket.
Two investments that do not move together partly cancel each other's ups and downs.
So a mix can be less risky than either investment on its own.
For example, two independent assets with the same expected return and the same variance $\sigma^2$, held half and half, give the same expected return with variance $\sigma^2/2$.
Modern portfolio theory turns this idea into an optimisation problem.
