## Summary

The kinetic theory of gases describes a gas as a large number of molecules in motion, through the statistical distribution of their positions and velocities.
Pressure, temperature and flow velocity become averages over this distribution.
Its central equation is the [[Boltzmann equation|MH6CSWD7F0]], and its equilibrium is the [[Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution|3QSVWQJX11]].
It explains the ideal gas law, viscosity and heat conduction, and it connects molecular mechanics with the equations of fluid dynamics.
See the [[definition|7199ZMVZ8X]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air in a room is made of countless molecules flying in all directions and bouncing off each other and the walls.
The push of these molecules on a wall is what we feel as pressure.
Their average kinetic energy is what a thermometer measures as temperature.
Kinetic theory does not follow single molecules: it keeps track of how many molecules are at each place with each velocity.
From this count one recovers, for example, the law $p = n k T$ linking pressure, number density and temperature.
