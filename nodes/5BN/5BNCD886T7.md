## Summary

Maxwell's equations in curved spacetime describe the electromagnetic field in the presence of gravity, where spacetime is a [[Lorentzian manifold|BY4QYYS788]].
They are obtained from the [[covariant formulation|H65N7N36K2]] by replacing partial derivatives with the [[covariant derivatives|5FZDQQHQKM]] of the Levi-Civita connection of the metric.
In the language of [[differential forms|0E3G0GRPZ6]] they need no metric for one half and only the [[Hodge star|1Y4WX4XQWJ]] for the other.
They describe the bending of light by gravity and, coupled to the Einstein equations, charged black holes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gravity, by general relativity, bends spacetime itself.
Light and other electromagnetic waves travel through this bent spacetime, so their equations must take the bending into account.
The curved-space Maxwell equations are the usual ones rewritten so that they make sense on a curved background.
They explain, for example, why starlight passing near the Sun is deflected.
