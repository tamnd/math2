## Summary

The nullity of a linear map counts the independent directions that the map sends to zero.
For a [[linear map|0NRYHJPQG2]] $T \colon V \to W$ it is the [[dimension|03GSZ8RTZJ]] of the [[kernel|0S23E6YE83]],
$$\operatorname{nullity} T = \dim \ker T,$$
and the [[formal definition|00K4FK4NBH]] states it.
For a matrix $A$, the nullity of $x \mapsto Ax$ is the number of free variables of $Ax = 0$.

## Motivation

The general solution of a homogeneous system $Ax = 0$ has some number of free parameters, and this number is the nullity of $A$.
It also tells how large the solution set of $Ax = b$ is, when that set is not empty, since every solution is one solution plus a kernel vector.

The nullity is the partner of the [[rank|0ZZXSBTR4J]].
The rank counts what the map keeps, the nullity counts what it loses, and the [[rank-nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]] says that together they give the dimension of the source.

## Notation

The nullity is written $\operatorname{nullity} T$, $\operatorname{null} T$ or $n(T)$.
For a matrix, $\operatorname{nullity} A = \dim \operatorname{Nul} A$, the dimension of the [[null space|0PHDYNR24D]].

## Remarks

### Examples

For [[a 3 × 3 matrix map|RZNXVEHTCE]] the kernel is a line, so the nullity is $1$.
For [[differentiation on cubic polynomials|SMEX3CNE69]] the kernel is the set of constants, so the nullity is $1$.
The projection $(x, y, z) \mapsto (x, y)$ has the $z$-axis as kernel, so its nullity is $1$.
The zero map $V \to W$ has nullity $\dim V$, and an injective map has nullity $0$.

### What it is not

The nullity is not [[the number of zero columns of a matrix|R7N2K2EVJP]]: the matrix with rows $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 4)$ has no zero column but nullity $1$.
The number of zero rows of $A$ is not the nullity either, since zero rows only reduce the number of equations.
For the $2 \times 3$ matrix with rows $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 0, 0)$ there is one zero row, but the nullity is $2$.

### Computing it

For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ in reduced row echelon form, the nullity is $n$ minus the number of pivots, which is the number of [[free variables|0J0BBCMJ5W]].
The vectors in the [[parametric form|KQT68YPHD1]] of the solution of $Ax = 0$ form a basis of the null space.
So $\operatorname{nullity} A = n - \rank A$, by the [[rank-nullity theorem for matrices|TR48YQGCCA]].

### Properties

The nullity is $0$ exactly when $T$ is injective, by the [[kernel test|0BJBYCGMWE]].
For finite-dimensional $V$, the formula $\operatorname{nullity} T + \rank T = \dim V$ holds.
So if $\dim V > \dim W$, then $\rank T \le \dim W$ forces $\operatorname{nullity} T \ge \dim V - \dim W > 0$, and $T$ is not injective.

### History

In 1884 Sylvester proved his law of nullity for matrices.
For square matrices it says that the nullity of a product is at most the sum of the nullities of the factors.

### Watch out

In $\operatorname{nullity} A = n - \rank A$, the number $n$ is the number of columns, not the number $m$ of rows.
Nullity $0$ means that the map is injective, not that it is surjective.
For the inclusion $\R \to \R^2$, $x \mapsto (x, 0)$, the nullity is $0$, but the map is not onto.

### Try it

First, find the nullity of $T(x, y, z) = (x - y, \; y - z)$ from $\R^3$ to $\R^2$.
The kernel is given by $x = y = z$, which is the line spanned by $(1, 1, 1)$, so the nullity is $1$.

Second, find the nullity of the matrix with rows $(1, 2, 3)$ and $(2, 4, 6)$.
The second row is twice the first, so the rank is $1$ and the nullity is $3 - 1 = 2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The nullity counts the dimensions that the map crushes to zero.
The projection of space onto a plane crushes one direction, so its nullity is $1$.
Rank counts what survives, and nullity counts what is lost.

### L2 human

To find the nullity of $A$, row reduce $A$ and count the columns without a pivot.
Each such column gives one free variable, and each free variable gives one basis vector of the null space.
For $A$ with the single row $(1, 1, 1)$, the free variables are $x_2$ and $x_3$, and the null space has basis $(-1, 1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0, 1)$.
Watch out: counting before row reduction misses dependent columns.

### L3 human

The nullity is the dimension of the kernel, and the corank is the dimension of the cokernel $W / \im T$.
For a map between finite-dimensional spaces, the rank-nullity theorem applied to $V$ and to $W$ gives nullity minus corank equal to $\dim V - \dim W$, the index of $T$.
The index keeps its meaning in infinite dimension for Fredholm operators, where both numbers are finite.
