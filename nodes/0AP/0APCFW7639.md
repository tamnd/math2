## Summary

The nullity of a [[linear map|0NRYHJPQG2]] $T \colon V \to W$ is the [[dimension|03GSZ8RTZJ]] of its [[kernel|0S23E6YE83]].
It counts the independent directions that the map sends to zero.
For a matrix $A$, the nullity of $x \mapsto Ax$ is the number of free variables of $Ax = 0$.

## Motivation

The general solution of a homogeneous system $Ax = 0$ has some number of free parameters.
This number is the nullity of $A$.
It also tells how large the set of solutions of $Ax = b$ is, when this set is not empty.

## Notation

The nullity is written $\operatorname{nullity} T$, $\operatorname{null} T$ or $n(T)$.
For a matrix, $\operatorname{nullity} A = \dim \operatorname{Nul} A$, the dimension of the [[null space|0PHDYNR24D]].

## Remarks

### Examples

- [[A $3 \times 3$ matrix map|RZNXVEHTCE]] has kernel a line, so its nullity is $1$.
- [[Differentiation on cubic polynomials|SMEX3CNE69]] has kernel the constants, so its nullity is $1$.
- The projection $(x, y, z) \mapsto (x, y)$ has kernel the $z$-axis, so its nullity is $1$.
- The zero map $V \to W$ has nullity $\dim V$, and an injective map has nullity $0$.

### Non-examples

- [[The number of zero columns of a matrix|R7N2K2EVJP]] is not its nullity.
- The number of zero rows of $A$ before row reduction is not the nullity either. Zero rows only reduce the number of equations.

### Basic properties

- $\operatorname{nullity} T = 0$ if and only if $T$ is injective, by the [[kernel test|0BJBYCGMWE]].
- $\operatorname{nullity} T + \rank T = \dim V$, by the [[rank–nullity theorem|0W4FSW1TVB]].
- For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ in reduced row echelon form, the nullity is $n$ minus the number of pivots, which is the number of [[free variables|0J0BBCMJ5W]].
- The vectors in the [[parametric form|KQT68YPHD1]] of the solution of $Ax = 0$ are a basis of the null space.
- For matrices, $\operatorname{nullity} A = n - \rank A$, by the [[rank–nullity theorem for matrices|TR48YQGCCA]].

### History

In 1884 Sylvester proved his law of nullity for matrices over a field.
For square matrices it says that the nullity of a product is at most the sum of the nullities of the factors.

### Common errors

- Counting zero columns of $A$ instead of free variables after row reduction.
- Using $m$, the number of rows, in place of $n$, the number of columns, in $\operatorname{nullity} A = n - \rank A$.
- Thinking that nullity $0$ means the map is surjective. It means injective.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The nullity counts the dimensions that the map crushes to zero.
The projection of space onto a plane crushes one direction, so its nullity is $1$.
Rank counts what survives, and nullity counts what is lost.

### L2 human

To find the nullity of $A$, row reduce $A$ and count the columns without a pivot.
Each such column gives one free variable, and each free variable gives one basis vector of the null space.
The trap is to count before row reduction, when dependent columns are not yet visible.

### L3 human

The nullity is the dimension of the kernel, and the corank is the dimension of the cokernel $W / \im T$.
For a map between finite-dimensional spaces, nullity minus corank is $\dim V - \dim W$, the index of $T$.
This index survives in infinite dimension for Fredholm operators, where both numbers are finite.
