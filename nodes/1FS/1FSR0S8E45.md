## Summary

The Lemke–Howson algorithm finds a Nash equilibrium of a finite two-player game.
It was published by Carlton Lemke and Joseph Howson in 1964.
It follows a path of pivoting steps on two polytopes, much like the simplex method of linear programming.
It gives a constructive proof that two-player games have equilibria, and in nondegenerate games the number of equilibria is odd.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each pure strategy of each player is a label.
An equilibrium is a pair of mixed strategies where every label is "covered": the strategy is either unused, or it is a best reply for the player who owns it.
The algorithm starts from an artificial point where all labels are covered, gives up one label, and walks along edges of a polytope until that label is covered again.
The walk cannot cycle back, so it must end at an equilibrium.
