## Definition

Let $f \colon \R^n \to \R$ be a [[convex function|07GR3TNW4H]].
Let $\partial f(x)$ be its set of [[subgradients|9C5WXYYQEK]] at $x$: the vectors $g$ with $f(y) \ge f(x) + g^T (y - x)$ for all $y \in \R^n$.
Let $x_0 \in \R^n$ and let $(\alpha_k)_{k \ge 0}$ be positive step sizes.
The *subgradient method* produces the sequence $(x_k)$ defined by
$$x_{k+1} = x_k - \alpha_k g_k, \qquad \text{where } g_k \in \partial f(x_k) \ (k \ge 0),$$
together with the best values $f^{\mathrm{best}}_k = \min\set{f(x_0), \dots, f(x_k)}$.

## Remarks

If $f$ has a minimiser $x^*$, all subgradients satisfy $\norm{g} \le G$, and $\sum_k \alpha_k = \infty$, $\sum_k \alpha_k^2 < \infty$, then $f^{\mathrm{best}}_k \to f(x^*)$.
The basic estimate is
$$f^{\mathrm{best}}_k - f(x^*) \le \frac{\norm{x_0 - x^*}^2 + G^2 \sum_{i=0}^k \alpha_i^2}{2 \sum_{i=0}^k \alpha_i}.$$

Example: $f(x) = \abs{x}$ on $\R$, $x_0 = 1$ and $\alpha_k = 1/(k+1)$.
Then $g_0 = 1$ and $x_1 = 0$, a minimiser; at $x = 0$ any $g \in [-1, 1]$ is a subgradient, and the choice $g_1 = 0$ keeps $x_k = 0$.

Non-example: for $f(x) = \abs{x}$, the step $x_{k+1} = x_k + \alpha_k \operatorname{sign}(x_k)$ is not a subgradient step, since it moves along $+g_k$ instead of $-g_k$.

Unlike gradient descent, the value $f(x_{k+1})$ can exceed $f(x_k)$: for $f(x) = \abs{x}$, $x_0 = 0.1$ and $\alpha_0 = 1$ one gets $x_1 = -0.9$.
