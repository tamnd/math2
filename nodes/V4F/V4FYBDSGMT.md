## Summary

The rotation curve of a disc galaxy gives the speed of stars and gas on circular orbits as a function of radius.
It measures how much mass lies inside each radius.
Observed rotation curves stay roughly flat far beyond the visible disc, which is a main piece of evidence for dark matter.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a disc galaxy, stars and gas go round the centre, much like planets go round the Sun.
Plot the orbital speed against the distance from the centre: that graph is the rotation curve.
For planets around the Sun the speed falls with distance like $1/\sqrt{r}$, because almost all the mass sits in the Sun.
In galaxies the curve rises near the centre and then stays almost level, even where few stars are left.
So there must be extra unseen mass far out, which pulls the outer stars along.
