## Summary

The nilradical of a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] is its largest [[nilpotent|MEJ9PGMJ9H]] ideal.
It is contained in the [[radical|33MD21KMNP]], the largest solvable ideal.
In characteristic $0$, the bracket of the whole algebra with the radical lies in the nilradical.
The nilradical appears in the conjugacy theorem for Levi subalgebras and in the refined form of Ado's theorem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Inside a Lie algebra, look for ideals in which repeated brackets always die out.
There is a biggest such ideal, and it is called the nilradical.
For the two-dimensional algebra with $[x, y] = y$, the nilradical is the line spanned by $y$.
The whole algebra is not nilpotent there, because bracketing $y$ with $x$ again and again keeps giving $y$.
