## Definition

Let a body with reference configuration $B \subseteq \R^3$ undergo a motion $\chi$, with [[deformation gradient|HTYRR2WDQ6]] $F(X, t)$ and [[Cauchy stress tensor|KG2XWEMJ30]] $\sigma(x, t)$.
A *constitutive equation* for the material of the body is a relation, characteristic of the material, that determines $\sigma$ at each material point.
It gives $\sigma$ in terms of the motion of the body and possibly of other fields, such as the temperature.
The simplest type is that of an *elastic material*: there is a function $\hat\sigma$ on the $3 \times 3$ matrices of positive determinant such that
$$\sigma(\chi(X, t), t) = \hat\sigma(F(X, t)) \quad \text{for all } X \in B \text{ and all times } t.$$

## Remarks

For an isotropic linearly elastic solid with Lamé constants $\lambda$ and $\mu$, the constitutive equation $\sigma = \lambda (\operatorname{tr} \varepsilon) I + 2 \mu \varepsilon$ links stress to the infinitesimal strain $\varepsilon$.
For a Newtonian fluid, $\sigma = -p I + \lambda (\operatorname{div} v) I + 2 \mu D$, where $D$ is the symmetric part of the velocity gradient in the [[Eulerian description|43949CJRF6]] and $p$ the pressure.
The balance of linear momentum $\rho\, Dv/Dt = \operatorname{div} \sigma + \rho b$ is not a constitutive equation: it holds for every material.
Admissible constitutive equations are restricted by general principles, chiefly material frame-indifference and the second law of thermodynamics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A constitutive equation is the law of a particular material.
It tells how that material's stress depends on how it is deformed or how fast it is flowing.
