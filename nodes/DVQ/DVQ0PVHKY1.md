## Summary

In statistical mechanics, an exactly solvable model is a model whose free energy in the [[thermodynamic limit|FPYQWE8X12]] can be computed in closed form.
Often some correlation functions can be computed as well.
Examples are the one-dimensional [[Ising model|R0KGM32QN6]], the two-dimensional Ising model without field, the [[six-vertex model|45HEVXM7RD]], the [[eight-vertex model|7642CPVVSF]] and the [[hard hexagon model|7MJQ2ZBQ66]].
Such models are rare, and their solutions give exact critical exponents that test approximate methods.
Many of them are tied to the [[Yang–Baxter equation|663BP8ZHS5]] and are close relatives of quantum [[integrable systems|N0N7CMRCMH]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Most models of many interacting particles can only be studied by approximations or computer simulation.
A few special ones can be solved with pen and paper: one can write down an exact formula for their free energy.
For example, the Ising chain without field has free energy per site $-\beta^{-1} \log(2 \cosh \beta J)$.
These rare cases act as benchmarks for everything else.
