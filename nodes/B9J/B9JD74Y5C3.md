## Statement

Let a cell membrane separate an inside and an outside solution at absolute temperature $T$.
Let one ion species $X$ of valence $z \ne 0$ be able to cross the membrane, with concentrations $[X]_{\mathrm{in}} > 0$ inside and $[X]_{\mathrm{out}} > 0$ outside.
Let $R$ be the gas constant and $F$ the Faraday constant, and treat the solutions as ideal.
Let $V = V_{\mathrm{in}} - V_{\mathrm{out}}$ be the membrane potential.
Then the net flux of $X$ across the membrane is zero exactly when $V$ equals the *reversal potential*
$$E_X = \frac{RT}{zF} \ln \frac{[X]_{\mathrm{out}}}{[X]_{\mathrm{in}}},$$
where $\ln$ is the [[natural logarithm|04DXAFN2ES]].

## Remarks

The idea of the proof is to balance two forces on the ion.
The electrochemical potential of one mole of $X$ is $\mu_0 + RT \ln [X] + zFV$ on each side.
Equilibrium means that it has the same value on both sides, and solving for $V_{\mathrm{in}} - V_{\mathrm{out}}$ gives $E_X$.
For potassium in a typical neuron, $[\mathrm{K}^+]_{\mathrm{out}} = 5$ mM and $[\mathrm{K}^+]_{\mathrm{in}} = 140$ mM at $37^\circ$C give $E_{\mathrm{K}} \approx 26.7 \ln(5/140)$ mV $\approx -89$ mV.
The equation concerns a single permeant species; with several permeant species the resting potential is given instead by the Goldman equation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ions drift down their concentration gradient, but an electric voltage can push them back.
The Nernst equation gives the voltage at which the two pushes cancel for one kind of ion.
The voltage grows with the logarithm of the concentration ratio, so a tenfold ratio gives about $60$ mV for a singly charged ion at body temperature.

### L2 human

In neuron models such as the Hodgkin–Huxley model, each ionic current has the form $g_X (V - E_X)$, with $E_X$ given by the Nernst equation.
So the Nernst potential is the voltage at which the current of that ion changes sign.
The potassium and sodium reversal potentials bound the range of the membrane potential during an action potential.
