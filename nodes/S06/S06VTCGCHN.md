## Definition

Let $G$ be a connected real or complex [[Lie group|7JBRGCVJRB]].
Its [[lower central series|0K98R7A4VC]] is $C^1 G = G$ and $C^{k+1} G = [G, C^k G]$ for $k \ge 1$, where $[G, H]$ is the subgroup generated by the commutators $g h g^{-1} h^{-1}$ with $g \in G$ and $h \in H$.
The group $G$ is a *nilpotent Lie group* if $C^{k} G = \set{e}$ for some $k \ge 1$, that is, if $G$ is a [[nilpotent group|0Q7MMQHC1P]].

## Remarks

A connected Lie group is nilpotent if and only if its [[Lie algebra|WKVBS76432]] $\mathfrak{g}$ is a [[nilpotent Lie algebra|MEJ9PGMJ9H]], that is, the series $\mathfrak{g} \supseteq [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \supseteq [\mathfrak{g}, [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]] \supseteq \cdots$ reaches $0$.

Example: the group $N$ of real upper triangular $3 \times 3$ matrices with $1$ on the diagonal.
Writing $[a, b, c]$ for the matrix with entries $a$, $b$ above the diagonal and $c$ in the corner, a direct computation shows that the commutator of $[a, b, c]$ and $[a', b', c']$ is $[0, 0, ab' - a'b]$.
So $C^2 N = \set{[0, 0, c]}$, which is the centre of $N$, and $C^3 N = \set{e}$; $N$ is nilpotent.

Non-example: the group $G$ of affine maps $x \mapsto a x + b$ of $\R$ with $a > 0$, written $(a, b)$.
The commutator of $(a, 0)$ and $(1, t)$ is $(1, (a - 1)t)$, so $C^2 G$ is the group of all translations $(1, b)$, and then $C^3 G = [G, C^2 G]$ again contains every translation.
So $C^k G$ is the translation group for all $k \ge 2$ and never trivial: $G$ is solvable but not nilpotent.
