## Definition

Let $(\R^4, \eta)$ be [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]] with inertial coordinates $x = (ct, \vec x)$ and $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$, where $c$ is the speed of light.
Let a particle of mass $m > 0$ move on a timelike world line, and let $u = dx/d\tau$ be its [[four-velocity|Y59V040THG]], where $\tau$ is [[proper time|YG74WHC3S9]].
The *four-momentum* of the particle is the [[four-vector|S8RKKKMSTG]]
$$p = m u.$$
For a massless particle, the four-momentum is a future-directed null vector $p$, that is, $\eta(p, p) = 0$ and $p^0 > 0$.

## Remarks

In an inertial frame where the particle has velocity $\vec v$, $u = \gamma(c, \vec v)$ with $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - \abs{\vec v}^2/c^2}$.
So $p = (E/c, \vec p)$ with energy $E = \gamma m c^2$ and momentum $\vec p = \gamma m \vec v$.
Since $\eta(u, u) = -c^2$, one gets $\eta(p, p) = -m^2 c^2$.
Example: a particle with $\vec v = (3c/5, 0, 0)$ has $\gamma = 5/4$, so $p = (5mc/4, 3mc/4, 0, 0)$, and indeed $-(5mc/4)^2 + (3mc/4)^2 = -m^2c^2$.
Non-example: the vector $(mc, m\vec v)$ with the Newtonian momentum is not a four-vector, because its components do not transform by Lorentz transformations, and its square $-m^2c^2 + m^2\abs{\vec v}^2$ depends on the frame.
For a photon of energy $E$ moving in the direction of the unit vector $\vec n$, $p = (E/c)(1, \vec n)$.
