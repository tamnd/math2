## Summary

The Stefan number is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] that compares sensible heat with [[latent heat|FN6Z9QKAH5]] in melting and freezing.
It is $\mathrm{Ste} = c \, \Delta T / L$, with specific heat $c$, temperature difference $\Delta T$ and latent heat $L$.
It is the main parameter of the [[Stefan problem|3B0HB3DHW1]].
When it is small, the phase boundary moves slowly compared with the spread of heat.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To melt ice, one must first bring it to its melting point and then supply a large amount of extra heat to change it into water.
The Stefan number compares the heat that changes temperature with the heat that changes phase.
For water warmed $10$ kelvin above its melting point, the number is about $4.2 \cdot 10 / 334 \approx 0.13$.
So most of the heat goes into the melting itself, and the ice front moves slowly.
