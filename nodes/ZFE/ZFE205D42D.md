## Definition

Consider a model neuron with input $x \in \R^n$, weights $w \in \R^n$ and output $y = w^T x$, where the input $x$ is drawn at random from a fixed distribution.
Let $\phi \colon \R \times [0, \infty) \to \R$ be a function such that, for each $\theta > 0$, $\phi(y, \theta) < 0$ for $0 < y < \theta$ and $\phi(y, \theta) > 0$ for $y > \theta$.
The *BCM learning rule* is the system
$$\tau_w \frac{dw}{dt} = \phi(y, \theta)\, x, \qquad \theta = \mathbb{E}[y^2],$$
where $\tau_w > 0$ is a time constant and $\theta$ is the *modification threshold*.
A standard choice is $\phi(y, \theta) = y\, (y - \theta)$.

## Remarks

In simulations the expectation is often replaced by a running average, $\tau_\theta\, d\theta/dt = y^2 - \theta$ with $\tau_\theta$ smaller than $\tau_w$.
Example: $\phi(y, \theta) = y (y - \theta)$ satisfies the sign condition, since for $\theta > 0$ the product is negative on $(0, \theta)$ and positive on $(\theta, \infty)$.
Non-example: $\phi(y, \theta) = y$ fails the condition, since it is positive on $(0, \theta)$; with it the rule is plain [[Hebbian learning|VQZVSFF179]] and the weights can grow without bound.
The threshold grows like the square of the output, faster than the output itself, and this is what stabilises the weights.
