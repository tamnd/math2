## Summary

Consider a linear program in equational form $A x = b$, $x \ge 0$.
A basic feasible solution is a feasible point whose nonzero coordinates belong to linearly independent columns of $A$.
Basic feasible solutions are exactly the vertices, that is, the [[extreme points|3MSGFJM2NX]], of the [[feasible region|1TTKMRW8PD]], and there are finitely many of them.
By the [[fundamental theorem of linear programming|QF0E0X7AQG]], an optimum, when one exists, is attained at a basic feasible solution.
The [[simplex algorithm|MH272QHWPM]] moves from one basic feasible solution to another.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The feasible region of a linear program is a many-sided shape, and its corners are special.
A basic feasible solution is the algebraic description of a corner: choose some columns, set all other variables to zero, and solve the remaining square system.
If the solution has no negative entries, it is a corner of the feasible region.
For example, for $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 1$ with $x \ge 0$ the basic feasible solutions are $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$ and $(0,0,1)$, the three corners of a triangle.
