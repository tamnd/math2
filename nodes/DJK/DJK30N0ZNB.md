## Summary

Algebraic topology is the study of topological spaces through algebraic invariants, such as groups, rings and modules, that do not change under homeomorphism or homotopy.
Its central example is homology, which assigns to each space a sequence of abelian groups that measure the holes of the space in each dimension.
These invariants show that spaces are different, give the degree and fixed-point theorems, and have applications in geometry, combinatorics and data analysis.
Poincaré started the subject in his memoir Analysis situs of 1895, and Eilenberg and Steenrod gave axioms for homology theories in the 1940s.
This topic collects the algebraic topology pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Homotopy theory, general topology, manifolds and differential topology and homological algebra and K-theory have their own topics.
