## Proof

We prove 1 implies 2, 2 implies 1, 1 implies 3, and 3 implies 1.
We use the [[first consequences of the axioms|5E4VBC0A4H]]: $0v = 0$ and $(-1)v = -v$.

1. (1 implies 2) Let $W$ be a [[subspace|0EPTJA5QV4]]. Then $0 \in W$, so $W$ is nonempty.
2. (1 implies 2) Let $u, v \in W$ and $a \in F$. By condition 3 of the definition, $av \in W$; by condition 2, $u + av \in W$.
3. (2 implies 1) Assume condition 2. Choose $w \in W$, which exists because $W$ is nonempty.
4. Take $u = v = w$ and $a = -1$. Then $w + (-1)w \in W$. Since $(-1)w = -w$, this vector is $w + (-w) = 0$. So $0 \in W$.
5. Let $u, v \in W$. Take $a = 1$: then $u + 1v = u + v \in W$, by axiom 6.
6. Let $v \in W$ and $a \in F$. Take $u = 0$, which is in $W$ by step 4: then $0 + av = av \in W$, by axiom 3.
7. By steps 4 to 6, $W$ satisfies the three conditions of the definition, so $W$ is a subspace.
8. (1 implies 3) Let $W$ be a subspace. By conditions 2 and 3 of the definition, addition and scalar multiplication restrict to maps $W \times W \to W$ and $F \times W \to W$.
9. Axioms 1, 2 and 5 to 8 of the [[vector space definition|05EGHEQZAW]] are identities for all vectors of $V$. So they hold in particular for vectors of $W$.
10. Axiom 3: $0 \in W$ by condition 1, and $w + 0 = w$ holds in $V$.
11. Axiom 4: for $w \in W$, the vector $-w = (-1)w$ lies in $W$ by condition 3, and $w + (-w) = 0$ holds in $V$.
12. So $W$ with the restricted operations is a vector space over $F$.
13. (3 implies 1) Assume condition 3. Closure under addition and scalar multiplication gives conditions 2 and 3 of the definition.
14. $W$ is a vector space, so it has a zero vector $z$ with $z + z = z$. In $V$, cancellation of $z$ gives $z = 0$. So $0 \in W$, which is condition 1.

## Idea

The axioms that are identities pass from $V$ to every subset that is closed under the operations.
Only the existence of $0$ and of negatives needs work, and the identities $0 = w + (-1)w$ and $-w = (-1)w$ give both.
