## Proof

We prove that condition 1 implies 2 and 3, and that each of 2 and 3 implies 1.
We use two [[first consequences of the axioms|5E4VBC0A4H]]: $0v = 0$ and $(-1)v = -v$ for every vector $v$.

1. (1 implies 2) Let $W$ be a [[subspace|0EPTJA5QV4]]. Then $0 \in W$ by condition 1 of the definition, so $W$ is nonempty.
2. (1 implies 2) Let $u, v \in W$ and $a \in F$. Condition 3 of the definition gives $av \in W$, and then condition 2 gives $u + av \in W$.
3. (2 implies 1) Assume condition 2. Since $W$ is nonempty, we can choose a vector $w \in W$.
4. Take $u = v = w$ and $a = -1$ in condition 2. Then $w + (-1)w \in W$, and this vector is $0$ because $(-1)w = -w$, so $0 \in W$:
$$w + (-1)w = w + (-w) = 0.$$
5. Let $u, v \in W$. Take $a = 1$ in condition 2: since $1v = v$ by axiom 6, we get $u + v \in W$.
6. Let $v \in W$ and $a \in F$. Take $u = 0$, which lies in $W$ by step 4: since $0 + av = av$ by axioms 2 and 3, we get $av \in W$.
7. Steps 4, 5 and 6 are the three conditions of the definition, so $W$ is a subspace.
8. (1 implies 3) Let $W$ be a subspace. Conditions 2 and 3 of the definition say that addition and scalar multiplication restrict to maps $W \times W \to W$ and $F \times W \to W$.
9. Axioms 1, 2 and 5 to 8 of the [[vector space definition|05EGHEQZAW]] are equations that hold for all vectors of $V$ and all scalars. So they hold in particular for the vectors of $W$.
10. Axiom 3 holds in $W$: the vector $0$ lies in $W$ by condition 1, and $w + 0 = w$ holds in $V$.
11. Axiom 4 holds in $W$: for $w \in W$, the vector $-w = (-1)w$ lies in $W$ by condition 3, and $w + (-w) = 0$ holds in $V$.
12. So $W$ with the restricted operations is a vector space over $F$.
13. (3 implies 1) Assume condition 3. The closure of $W$ under the two operations gives conditions 2 and 3 of the definition.
14. Since $W$ is a vector space, it has a zero vector $z \in W$, with $z + z = z$. Adding $-z$ in $V$ to both sides gives $z = 0$. So $0 \in W$, which is condition 1.
15. So the three conditions of the statement are equivalent.

## Idea

The axioms that are equations, such as $u + v = v + u$, pass from $V$ to every subset, because they hold for all vectors of $V$.
Only the axioms that claim existence need work: a zero vector and a negative for each vector.
Both come from scaling, since $0 = w + (-1)w$ and $-w = (-1)w$, so closure under the operations is what makes them stay inside $W$.
The one-line condition $u + av \in W$ contains addition (take $a = 1$) and scaling (take $u = 0$) at once.

## Variants

Some books state the test with two closure conditions in place of one: $W$ is nonempty, closed under addition and closed under scalar multiplication.
That form needs a shorter argument: closure under scaling gives $0 = 0w \in W$ for any $w \in W$, and then the three conditions of the definition hold.
Steps 8 to 12 then show, in the same way, that $W$ is a vector space.
