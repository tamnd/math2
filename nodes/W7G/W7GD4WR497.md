## Summary

The celestial sphere is an imaginary [[sphere|P9CM4GQ2T0]] centred at the observer on which every celestial object is drawn at the point in its direction.
It forgets distances and keeps only directions.
All astronomical coordinate systems are coordinate systems on this sphere.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The stars are at very different distances, but to the eye they all seem to lie on one big dome.
The celestial sphere makes this picture exact: a direction in space is a point on a sphere around you.
Two stars that look close in the sky are close on the sphere, even if one is much farther away than the other.
For example, the point straight above your head is the zenith, and the point the Earth's axis points to in the north is the north celestial pole.
