## Definition

Let $N$ be a [[Poisson process|0MTNR28K60]] on $[0, \infty)$ with intensity $\lambda > 0$.
Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be [[independent and identically distributed|0AJN7WHBJZ]] positive random variables with finite mean $\mu$, independent of $N$.
Let $u \ge 0$ and $c > 0$.
The *Cramér–Lundberg model* is the surplus process
$$U_t = u + c t - \sum_{i=1}^{N_t} X_i, \qquad t \ge 0,$$
in the sense of [[ruin theory|YSXZZ1HQR5]].
Here $u$ is the initial capital, $c$ the premium rate and $X_i$ the size of the $i$-th claim.

## Remarks

The model usually assumes the net profit condition $c > \lambda \mu$, often written $c = (1 + \theta) \lambda \mu$ with safety loading $\theta > 0$.
If $c \le \lambda \mu$, then $\psi(u) = 1$ for every $u \ge 0$: ruin is certain.
Example: if the $X_i$ have the [[exponential distribution|0E99F51GVB]] with mean $\mu$ and $\theta > 0$, then $\psi(u) = \frac{1}{1 + \theta} e^{-\theta u / ((1 + \theta) \mu)}$.
Non-example: if claims arrive at fixed times $1, 2, 3, \dots$ instead of at Poisson times, the process is not a Cramér–Lundberg model.
With general renewal arrival times one gets the Sparre Andersen model.
