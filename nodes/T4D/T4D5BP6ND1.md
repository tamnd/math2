## Summary

Classical geometries are the geometries other than plain Euclidean geometry that are defined by axioms or by a group of transformations, such as affine, projective, hyperbolic, spherical and inversive geometry.
In the view of Klein, a geometry is a space with a group that acts on it, and the theorems of the geometry are the properties that the group preserves.
Projective geometry, for example, is the geometry of the projective plane under the group of projective transformations, and its basic invariant is the cross-ratio of four points on a line.
The discovery of hyperbolic geometry by Lobachevsky and Bolyai around 1830 showed that the parallel postulate does not follow from the other axioms of Euclid, and Klein stated his Erlangen program in 1872.
This topic collects the classical geometries pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Euclidean geometry, designs, matroids and finite geometries, differential geometry and geometric group theory have their own topics.
