## Example

Let $k$ be a field and $n \ge 2$.
The *special linear Lie algebra* is
$$\mathfrak{sl}_n(k) = \set{X \in \mathfrak{gl}_n(k) : \tr X = 0},$$
the set of $n \times n$ matrices of [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]] zero, with the bracket $[X, Y] = XY - YX$ of the [[general linear Lie algebra|G8FEGYDSNE]].

Check that it is a Lie algebra: it is a linear subspace, and $\tr[X, Y] = \tr(XY) - \tr(YX) = 0$ for all $X, Y$.
So $\mathfrak{sl}_n(k)$ is closed under brackets, and it is even an [[ideal|JWMTXQ8A3A]] of $\mathfrak{gl}_n(k)$.
Its dimension is $n^2 - 1$.

For $n = 2$ the standard basis is
$$e = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad h = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad f = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
with $[h, e] = 2e$, $[h, f] = -2f$ and $[e, f] = h$.

## Remarks

For $k = \R$ or $\C$, $\mathfrak{sl}_n(k)$ is the Lie algebra of the [[special linear group|0V9PMN0RBH]] $SL_n(k)$, because $\det(e^{tX}) = e^{t \tr X}$.
Over a field of characteristic $0$, $\mathfrak{sl}_n(k)$ is simple for $n \ge 2$.
Over $\C$, $\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\C)$ is the simple Lie algebra of type $A_{n-1}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Inside all square matrices, keep those whose diagonal entries add up to zero.
Brackets of matrices always have this property, so these matrices form a Lie algebra.
They are the infinitesimal versions of the matrices of determinant one, the volume-preserving linear maps.
The smallest case, $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, with its three basis elements $e, h, f$, is the building block of the whole theory of semisimple Lie algebras.
