## Example

Consider the [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]] with the axiom schemes K, $A \to (B \to A)$, and S, $(A \to (B \to C)) \to ((A \to B) \to (A \to C))$, and the rule [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]].
Here is a derivation of $p \to p$.

1. $p \to ((p \to p) \to p)$, by K with $A = p$ and $B = p \to p$.
2. $(p \to ((p \to p) \to p)) \to ((p \to (p \to p)) \to (p \to p))$, by S with $A = p$, $B = p \to p$ and $C = p$.
3. $(p \to (p \to p)) \to (p \to p)$, by modus ponens from lines 1 and 2.
4. $p \to (p \to p)$, by K with $A = p$ and $B = p$.
5. $p \to p$, by modus ponens from lines 4 and 3.

We show that this derivation, and every other derivation of $p \to p$ in this system, fails the subformula property.

6. The subformulas of $p \to p$ are $p \to p$ and $p$.
7. Line 1 contains $(p \to p) \to p$, which is not in the list of step 6.
8. In any derivation, the last line $p \to p$ is not an instance of K, because K would need $p = B \to A$, and $p$ is a variable.
9. It is not an instance of S, because the consequent of S is an implication $(A \to B) \to (A \to C)$, and $p$ is not.
10. So the last line comes by modus ponens from some $A$ and $A \to (p \to p)$. The formula $A \to (p \to p)$ is not a subformula of $p \to p$.

## Remarks

The example shows that modus ponens breaks the subformula property: its premise $A$ can be any formula.
In the sequent calculus, modus ponens corresponds to a cut on $A$, and cut elimination removes it.
A cut-free LK derivation of $\Rightarrow p \to p$ is one step: ${\to}$R from the axiom $p \Rightarrow p$.
The same failure happens for every theorem of a Hilbert system whose last step is modus ponens.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Hilbert system proves things with a few fixed patterns and one rule: from $A$ and "$A$ implies $B$", get $B$.
To prove "$p$ implies $p$" this way, we must pass through longer statements, such as "$p$ implies that ($p$ implies $p$) implies $p$".
These statements are not pieces of the goal.

### L2 human

The check has two parts.
First list the subformulas of the goal: here only $p \to p$ and $p$.
Then find one line of the derivation outside this list: line 1 is such a line.
A trap is to think that a cleverer derivation avoids this: steps 8 to 10 show that every derivation of $p \to p$ fails.
