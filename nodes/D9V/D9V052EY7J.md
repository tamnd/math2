## Example

Take the [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]] with the axiom schemes K and S and the rule [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]].
$$\mathrm{K}: \ A \to (B \to A) \qquad \mathrm{S}: \ (A \to (B \to C)) \to ((A \to B) \to (A \to C))$$
Here is the standard derivation of $p \to p$.

1. $p \to ((p \to p) \to p)$, by K with $A = p$ and $B = p \to p$.
2. $(p \to ((p \to p) \to p)) \to ((p \to (p \to p)) \to (p \to p))$, by S with $A = p$, $B = p \to p$ and $C = p$.
3. $(p \to (p \to p)) \to (p \to p)$, by modus ponens from lines 1 and 2.
4. $p \to (p \to p)$, by K with $A = p$ and $B = p$.
5. $p \to p$, by modus ponens from lines 4 and 3.

We show that this derivation, and in fact every derivation of $p \to p$ in the system, fails the subformula property.

6. The subformulas of $p \to p$ are $p \to p$ and $p$.
7. Line 1 contains $(p \to p) \to p$, which is not in the list of step 6, so this derivation fails.
8. Now take any derivation, and look at its last line $p \to p$.
   It is not an instance of K, because K would need the consequent $p$ to have the form $B \to A$.
9. It is not an instance of S, because the consequent of S is an implication, and the consequent $p$ is a variable.
10. So the last line comes by modus ponens from some $A$ and $A \to (p \to p)$.
    The formula $A \to (p \to p)$ is neither $p$ nor $p \to p$, so it is not a subformula of $p \to p$.

## Remarks

The example shows that modus ponens breaks the [[subformula property|Z2NXKDK06G]]: its minor premise $A$ can be any formula, and the major premise $A \to B$ is never a part of $B$.
In the sequent calculus modus ponens becomes a cut on $A$, and cut elimination removes it.
A cut-free LK derivation of $\Rightarrow p \to p$ is a single step, ${\to}$R from the axiom $p \Rightarrow p$.

The argument of steps 8 to 10 does not depend on $p \to p$.
Every theorem of this Hilbert system that is not itself an axiom has the same problem, because its last step is modus ponens.
This is why Hilbert systems are good for metatheory, with few rules to check, and poor for proof search.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Hilbert system proves things with a few fixed patterns and one rule: from $A$ and "$A$ implies $B$", get $B$.
To prove "$p$ implies $p$" this way, we must pass through longer statements, such as "$p$ implies that ($p$ implies $p$) implies $p$".
These statements are not pieces of the goal.
The sequent calculus proves the same thing in one step, using only $p$.

### L2 human

The check has two parts.
First list the subformulas of the goal: here only $p \to p$ and $p$.
Then find one line of the derivation outside this list, and line 1 is such a line.
Watch out: a cleverer derivation does not escape, because steps 8 to 10 show that the last step of every derivation uses the formula $A \to (p \to p)$.
