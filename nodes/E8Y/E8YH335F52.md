## Definition

Let $\pi$ be a group and let $w \colon \pi \to \set{1, -1}$ be a group homomorphism.
Give the [[group ring|4NMTSX0XDS]] $\Z[\pi]$ the involution $\sum_g n_g g \mapsto \sum_g n_g w(g) g^{-1}$.
The *surgery obstruction groups* of $(\pi, w)$ are the [[L-groups|P5CRN7BZV0]] $L_n(\Z[\pi], w)$ of this ring with involution, for $n \in \Z$.
In the classification of manifolds up to diffeomorphism or homeomorphism one uses the simple version $L^s_n(\Z[\pi], w)$, built from based modules.

## Remarks

Wall's theorem: let $X$ be a finite Poincaré complex of dimension $n \ge 5$ with [[fundamental group|S1XK3HXHFX]] $\pi$ and orientation character $w$.
A degree one normal map $f \colon M \to X$ from a closed manifold has an obstruction $\sigma(f) \in L^s_n(\Z[\pi], w)$.
It vanishes if and only if $f$ is normally bordant to a [[simple homotopy equivalence|AR8SP73SXX]].
Example: for $\pi$ trivial, $L_n(\Z) \cong \Z, 0, \Z/2, 0$ for $n \equiv 0, 1, 2, 3 \pmod 4$.
For $n = 4k$ the obstruction is $(\operatorname{sign} M - \operatorname{sign} X)/8$, and for $n = 4k + 2$ it is an Arf invariant.
The groups depend on $\pi$: by Shaneson's splitting, $L^s_n(\Z[\pi \times \Z]) \cong L^s_n(\Z[\pi]) \oplus L^h_{n-1}(\Z[\pi])$ for trivial $w$, so $L_1(\Z[\Z]) \cong \Z$ while $L_1(\Z) = 0$.
They are the algebraic terms of the [[surgery exact sequence|Q7C480HQ9J]].
