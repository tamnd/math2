## Summary

Noncommutative harmonic analysis is [[harmonic analysis on groups|5E6KB3V8R0]] for locally compact groups that need not be abelian.
The characters of the abelian theory are replaced by the irreducible [[unitary representations|74BPYJNZTM]] of the group.
Its central questions are how the [[left regular representation|PR8C073VWY]] on $L^2(G)$ splits into irreducible pieces and how a function is recovered from its [[Fourier transform|N8E0GVVWG1]].
It covers the [[Peter–Weyl theorem|6VC9QE39XH]] for compact groups and the Plancherel theory of Lie groups such as $SL_2(\R)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For an abelian group, every function is a superposition of waves, and each wave is a single complex number at each point.
For a group whose elements do not commute, such as the rotations of space, single numbers are not enough.
The basic waves become matrices that multiply in the same way as the group elements, that is, representations.
For functions on the sphere, on which the rotation group acts, these pieces are the spaces of spherical harmonics of each degree.
Noncommutative harmonic analysis studies how functions on such groups split into these matrix-valued waves.
