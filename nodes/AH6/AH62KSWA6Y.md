## Summary

Linearized gravity is an approximation of general relativity for metrics close to the flat [[Minkowski metric|G49G4R1K04]].
The metric is written as the flat one plus a small perturbation, and only terms linear in the perturbation are kept.
The [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] then become linear wave equations, much like Maxwell's equations.
It is the basis for the theory of weak gravitational waves, for the Newtonian limit, and for gravitoelectromagnetism.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Far from black holes and neutron stars, spacetime is almost flat.
One can then write the geometry as flat space plus a tiny ripple, and ignore products of ripples with ripples.
What is left is a set of simple equations: ripples are created by matter and travel at the speed of light, like waves on a pond.
For example, the gravitational waves detected on Earth stretch distances by less than one part in $10^{20}$, so the linear approximation is extremely accurate there.
