## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
We find invertible $P$ and $Q$ with $PAQ = N_2 = (I_2 \ 0)$, as in the [[rank normal form theorem|T2H2QV0M06]].

Row operations:

1. $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$: row 2 becomes $(0, 0, 1)$. This is left multiplication by $T_{21}(-2)$.
2. $R_1 \to R_1 - 3R_2$: row 1 becomes $(1, 2, 0)$. This is left multiplication by $T_{12}(-3)$.
3. So $PA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, the reduced row echelon form, with $P = T_{12}(-3)\, T_{21}(-2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. Check: $PA$ has row 1 equal to $7(1, 2, 3) - 3(2, 4, 7) = (1, 2, 0)$ and row 2 equal to $-2(1, 2, 3) + (2, 4, 7) = (0, 0, 1)$.
5. There are two nonzero rows, so $\operatorname{rank} A = 2$.

Column operations:

6. The pivot columns are 1 and 3. Exchange columns 2 and 3, which is right multiplication by $P_{23}$: $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
7. $C_3 \to C_3 - 2C_1$, which is right multiplication by $T_{13}(-2)$: $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = N_2$.
8. So $Q = P_{23}\, T_{13}(-2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
9. Check: $(PA)Q$ has column 1 equal to column 1 of $PA$, which is $(1, 0)^{\mathsf{T}}$. Column 2 is column 3 of $PA$, which is $(0, 1)^{\mathsf{T}}$. Column 3 is $-2$ times column 1 plus column 2 of $PA$, which is $(-2 + 2, 0)^{\mathsf{T}} = (0, 0)^{\mathsf{T}}$. So $PAQ = N_2$.
10. Check the inverses: $P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$, since $\begin{pmatrix} 7 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 7 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 - 6 & 21 - 21 \\ -2 + 2 & -6 + 7 \end{pmatrix} = I_2$. Also $\det Q = -1$, so $Q$ is invertible.

## Remarks

So $A = P^{-1} N_2 Q^{-1}$.
Other choices work too: for example, the exchange in step 6 can be replaced by column replacements.
Only the block $N_2$ is determined by $A$, because the rank is.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We first clean up the rows, and then we clean up the columns.
What is left is an identity block of size $2$ and a column of zeros.
The size of the identity block is the rank of $A$.

### L2 human

Row operations alone stop at the reduced row echelon form $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
The entry $2$ in a non-pivot column cannot be removed by row operations, because they keep the relation "column 2 is twice column 1".
Column operations can remove it, and this is why equivalence is coarser than row equivalence.
