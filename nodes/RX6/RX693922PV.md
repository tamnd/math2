## Example

Row operations clean up the rows of a $2 \times 3$ matrix, column operations then clean up the columns, and what is left is the identity block whose size is the rank.
Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$$
over $\mathbb{Q}$.
We find invertible $P$ and $Q$ with $PAQ = N_2 = (I_2 \ 0)$, as in the [[rank normal form theorem|T2H2QV0M06]].

First the rows.

1. $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ turns row 2 into $(0, 0, 1)$. This is left multiplication by $T_{21}(-2)$.
2. $R_1 \to R_1 - 3R_2$ turns row 1 into $(1, 2, 0)$. This is left multiplication by $T_{12}(-3)$.

So $PA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, the reduced row echelon form, with
$$P = T_{12}(-3)\, T_{21}(-2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Check: row 1 of $PA$ is $7(1, 2, 3) - 3(2, 4, 7) = (1, 2, 0)$, and row 2 is $-2(1, 2, 3) + (2, 4, 7) = (0, 0, 1)$.
There are two nonzero rows, so $\operatorname{rank} A = 2$.

Then the columns.

3. The pivot columns are 1 and 3. Exchanging columns 2 and 3 is right multiplication by $P_{23}$, and gives $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. $C_3 \to C_3 - 2C_1$ is right multiplication by $T_{13}(-2)$, and gives $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = N_2$.

So
$$Q = P_{23}\, T_{13}(-2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Check: column 1 of $(PA)Q$ is column 1 of $PA$, which is $(1, 0)^{\mathsf{T}}$; column 2 is column 3 of $PA$, which is $(0, 1)^{\mathsf{T}}$; and column 3 is $-2$ times column 1 plus column 2 of $PA$, which is $(-2 + 2, 0)^{\mathsf{T}} = (0, 0)^{\mathsf{T}}$.
So $PAQ = N_2$.
Both factors are invertible: $\det P = 7 - 6 = 1$ with $P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$, and $\det Q = -1$.

## Remarks

### What is determined and what is not

The result can be read as $A = P^{-1} N_2 Q^{-1}$.
Only the block $N_2$ is determined by $A$, because the rank is; the matrices $P$ and $Q$ depend on the choices made.

### Watch out

Row operations alone stop at the reduced row echelon form $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
The entry $2$ in a non-pivot column cannot be removed by row operations, because they keep the relation "column 2 is twice column 1".
Column operations can remove it, and this is why equivalence is coarser than row equivalence.

### Try it

Reach $N_2$ from $PA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ using only column replacements, with no exchange.
Answer: $C_2 \to C_2 - 2C_1$ gives $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, then $C_2 \to C_2 + C_3$ gives $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and $C_3 \to C_3 - C_2$ gives $N_2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We first clean up the rows, and then we clean up the columns.
What is left is an identity block of size $2$ and a column of zeros.
The size of the identity block is the rank of $A$.

### L2 human

Record each operation as an elementary matrix, on the left for rows and on the right for columns.
Multiply the row matrices with the latest one on the left, and the column matrices with the latest one on the right.
