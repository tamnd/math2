## Example

Let $F$ be a field.
Every $A \in M_2(F)$ is [[similar|027KDW2K72]] to exactly one matrix in this list: a scalar matrix $\lambda I_2$ with $\lambda \in F$, or a [[companion matrix|0VH1TC1PWF]] $C(x^2 + bx + c) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -c \\ 1 & -b \end{pmatrix}$ with $b, c \in F$.

1. Scalar matrices. If $A = \lambda I_2$, then $P^{-1} A P = \lambda I_2$ for every invertible $P$. So the class of $\lambda I_2$ has one member.
2. If $A$ is not scalar, some vector $v$ is not an eigenvector. Indeed, if $e_1$, $e_2$ and $e_1 + e_2$ are eigenvectors with eigenvalues $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$, then $\alpha e_1 + \beta e_2 = \gamma e_1 + \gamma e_2$, so $\alpha = \beta$ and $A = \alpha I_2$.
3. So $v$ and $Av$ are linearly independent, and they form a basis of $F^2$.
4. Let $\chi_A = x^2 + bx + c$, with $b = -\operatorname{tr} A$ and $c = \det A$. By the [[Cayley-Hamilton theorem|0FCCDXH97W]], $A^2 v = -c\, v - b\, Av$.
5. In the basis $v, Av$, the matrix of $A$ has columns $(0, 1)$ and $(-c, -b)$. By the [[change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]], $A$ is similar to $C(\chi_A)$.
6. Uniqueness. Similar matrices have the same characteristic polynomial, and a scalar matrix is similar only to itself. So two nonscalar matrices are similar if and only if they have the same trace and the same determinant.
7. A worked case over $\mathbb{Q}$. Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, with trace $5$ and determinant $-2$. Take $v = e_1$, so $Av = (1, 3)$ and $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
8. Check. $AP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 7 \\ 3 & 15 \end{pmatrix}$ and $P \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 7 \\ 3 & 15 \end{pmatrix}$. So $A = P\, C(x^2 - 5x - 2)\, P^{-1}$.

## Remarks

In the language of the [[rational canonical form|042JWAR3Q0]], a scalar matrix $\lambda I_2$ has invariant factors $x - \lambda, x - \lambda$, and a nonscalar matrix has the single invariant factor $\chi_A$.
Over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ the list has $q$ scalar matrices and $q^2$ companion matrices, so $M_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$ has $q^2 + q$ similarity classes.
The invertible ones are the $q - 1$ scalar matrices with $\lambda \ne 0$ and the $q(q - 1)$ companion matrices with $c \ne 0$, in total $q^2 - 1$.
In characteristic $3$, step 7 fails for this $A$: then $Ae_1 = (1, 0)$, so $e_1$ is an eigenvector and another $v$ is needed.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A $2 \times 2$ matrix is either a multiple of the identity or not.
If it is not, its trace and determinant decide its class completely.

### L2 human

The proof picks a vector $v$ that $A$ moves off its own line.
Then $v$ and $Av$ are a basis, and $A$ sends $v$ to $Av$ and $Av$ to $A^2 v$, which Cayley-Hamilton writes in terms of $v$ and $Av$.
That is the companion matrix.
