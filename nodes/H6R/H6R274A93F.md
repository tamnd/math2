## Example

Let $F$ be any field.
Every $A \in M_2(F)$ is [[similar|027KDW2K72]] to exactly one matrix in this list: a scalar matrix $\lambda I_2$ with $\lambda \in F$, or a [[companion matrix|0VH1TC1PWF]]
$$C(x^2 + bx + c) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -c \\ 1 & -b \end{pmatrix}, \qquad b, c \in F.$$
We prove this in steps and then work one case.

1. If $A = \lambda I_2$, then $P^{-1} A P = \lambda P^{-1} P = \lambda I_2$ for every invertible $P$. So the class of a scalar matrix has one member.
2. If $A$ is not scalar, some vector is not an eigenvector. Indeed, suppose $e_1$, $e_2$ and $e_1 + e_2$ are eigenvectors with eigenvalues $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Then $\alpha e_1 + \beta e_2 = A(e_1 + e_2) = \gamma e_1 + \gamma e_2$, so $\alpha = \beta = \gamma$ and $A = \alpha I_2$.
3. Take $v$ that is not an eigenvector. Then $Av$ is not a multiple of $v$, so $v$ and $Av$ are linearly independent and form a basis of $F^2$.
4. Let $\chi_A = x^2 + bx + c$, where $b = -\operatorname{tr} A$ and $c = \det A$. By the [[Cayley-Hamilton theorem|0F765KFC2J]], $A^2 v = -c\, v - b\, Av$.
5. So $A$ sends the basis vector $v$ to $Av$, with coordinates $(0, 1)$, and sends $Av$ to $A^2 v$, with coordinates $(-c, -b)$. By the [[change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]], $A$ is similar to $C(\chi_A)$.
6. Similar matrices have the same characteristic polynomial, and $C(x^2 + bx + c)$ has characteristic polynomial $x^2 + bx + c$. So distinct companion matrices are not similar, and no companion matrix is similar to a scalar matrix by step 1.
7. Hence two nonscalar $2 \times 2$ matrices are similar exactly when they have the same trace and the same determinant.

Now a worked case over $\mathbb{Q}$.
Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, with trace $5$ and determinant $-2$, so $\chi_A = x^2 - 5x - 2$ and $C(\chi_A) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$.
Take $v = e_1$, so $Av = (1, 3)$, and put these two vectors as the columns of $P$:
$$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad AP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 7 \\ 3 & 15 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad P\, C(\chi_A) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 7 \\ 3 & 15 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Check: $AP = P\,C(\chi_A)$ and $\det P = 3 \ne 0$, so $A = P\, C(\chi_A)\, P^{-1}$.

## Remarks

In the language of the [[rational canonical form|042JWAR3Q0]], a scalar matrix $\lambda I_2$ has the invariant factors $x - \lambda, x - \lambda$, and a nonscalar matrix has the single invariant factor $\chi_A$.
So the list above is exactly the list of rational canonical forms of size $2$, and it works over every field, whether or not $\chi_A$ has roots.

The count over a finite field follows at once.
Over $\mathbb{F}_q$ the list has $q$ scalar matrices and $q^2$ companion matrices, one for each pair $(b, c)$, so $M_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$ has $q^2 + q$ similarity classes.
The invertible ones are the $q - 1$ scalar matrices with $\lambda \ne 0$ and the $q(q - 1)$ companion matrices with $c \ne 0$, in total $q^2 - 1$, as in the [[class count for GL_n|4FV9TDGWWH]].

The choice of $v$ depends on the field.
In characteristic $3$ the same matrix has $Ae_1 = (1, 3) = (1, 0)$, so $e_1$ is an eigenvector there and another $v$, such as $e_2$, is needed.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A $2 \times 2$ matrix is either a multiple of the identity or not.
A multiple of the identity looks the same in every basis.
Any other $2 \times 2$ matrix is decided, up to change of basis, by two numbers: its trace and its determinant.
For example, $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$ both have trace $5$ and determinant $-2$, so they describe the same map in two bases.

### L2 human

The proof picks a vector $v$ that $A$ moves off its own line.
Then $v$ and $Av$ are a basis, and in that basis $A$ sends the first vector to the second.
It sends the second vector $Av$ to $A^2 v$, and the Cayley-Hamilton theorem writes $A^2 v$ in terms of $v$ and $Av$ with the coefficients of $\chi_A$.
Those two images are the columns of the companion matrix.
Watch out: trace and determinant are not enough when one matrix is scalar, since $I_2$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ have the same trace and determinant but are not similar.
