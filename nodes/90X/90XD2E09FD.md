## Summary

Covariant Hamiltonian field theory is a Hamiltonian formulation of [[classical field theory|V6J6HAMXRM]] that treats all spacetime coordinates alike.
Each field component gets one conjugate momentum for every spacetime direction, called polymomenta, instead of one momentum conjugate to the time derivative.
The field equations become first-order covariant Hamilton equations on a finite-dimensional phase space, the Legendre bundle over spacetime.
It extends [[Hamiltonian mechanics|MPM0RHWCN6]] to fields without choosing a time coordinate, and it is related to multisymplectic and polysymplectic geometry.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In mechanics, the Hamiltonian picture pairs each position with a momentum and describes motion by first-order equations in time.
The usual Hamiltonian picture of fields singles out time and treats the field at a fixed time as an infinite-dimensional position.
The covariant picture keeps space and time on an equal footing: a field gets one momentum for each direction of spacetime.
For a field $\varphi$ on a plane with coordinates $x^0, x^1$, there are two momenta $p^0$ and $p^1$, and the wave equation splits into the first-order equations $\partial_0 \varphi = p^0$, $\partial_1 \varphi = -p^1$ and $\partial_0 p^0 + \partial_1 p^1 = 0$.
