## Statement

Let $n \ge 3$ and let $M = \R^n$ with the Minkowski form $\eta(x, y) = -x^0 y^0 + x^1 y^1 + \cdots + x^{n-1} y^{n-1}$.
For $x, y \in M$ write $x \ll y$ if $y - x$ is timelike and future-directed, that is, $\eta(y - x, y - x) < 0$ and $y^0 > x^0$; this is the chronological order of [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]].
Let $f \colon M \to M$ be a bijection such that, for all $x, y \in M$,
$$x \ll y \iff f(x) \ll f(y).$$
Then there are a real number $a > 0$, a [[Lorentz transformation|WEVGN4C7PQ]] $\Lambda$ that preserves the direction of time, and a vector $b \in M$ such that
$$f(x) = a \Lambda x + b \quad \text{for all } x \in M.$$

## Remarks

No continuity or linearity of $f$ is assumed: the causal order alone forces $f$ to be affine.
Proof idea: the order determines the [[light cones|JZ9ADDZX8D]], since $y$ lies on the future light cone of $x$ exactly when $y \ne x$, $x \not\ll y$, and $x \ll z$ for every $z \gg y$.
So $f$ maps light cones to light cones and null lines to null lines.
One then shows that $f$ maps lines to lines, so it is affine.
Its linear part preserves the light cone, so it is a positive multiple of a Lorentz transformation.
The hypothesis $n \ge 3$ is necessary.
For $n = 2$, use the null coordinates $u = x^0 - x^1$ and $v = x^0 + x^1$.
Then $x \ll y$ iff $u(x) < u(y)$ and $v(x) < v(y)$.
So every map $(u, v) \mapsto (\phi(u), \psi(v))$, with $\phi, \psi$ increasing bijections of $\R$, preserves the order, and most such maps are not affine.
The map $x \mapsto a x$ is a [[homothety|0ZG1PNEP35]]; it preserves the order but not the metric, so the order alone cannot fix the scale.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose all you know about events is which ones can causally influence which.
This theorem says that this information already fixes the geometry of spacetime, up to a change of units, a change of inertial frame and a shift of the origin.
So causality alone implies the Lorentz transformations.

### L2 human

The result says that the automorphism group of Minkowski space as a [[partially ordered set|0QV2JS9RJ3]] is generated by the orthochronous Poincaré group and the dilations $x \mapsto a x$ with $a > 0$.
It is a rigidity theorem, like the fundamental theorem of affine geometry.
That theorem says that real bijections sending lines to lines are affine in dimension at least $2$.
It motivates approaches to spacetime, such as causal set theory, in which the causal order is the basic structure.
