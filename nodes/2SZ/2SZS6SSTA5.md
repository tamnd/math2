## Proof

An [[interior algebra|BDEX7HET71]] is a [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]] $B$ with an operator $\square$ such that $\square 1 = 1$, $\square(a \wedge b) = \square a \wedge \square b$, $\square a \leq a$ and $\square \square a = \square a$.
Its open elements $O(B) = \set{a : \square a = a}$ form a [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]] with the meet and join of $B$ and with $a \Rightarrow b = \square(\neg a \vee b)$.
A *Grz-algebra* is an interior algebra that validates the axiom of [[Grzegorczyk's logic|2Q8E4GQF3B]].
A formula is valid in an algebra if its value is $1$ under every valuation.
We write $T$ for the [[Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski translation|BXVNEN55MR]].
Three results are cited without proof.

- (F1) Algebraic completeness. A superintuitionistic logic is the set of formulas valid in all Heyting algebras that validate it. A normal extension of $\mathsf{S4}$ is the set of formulas valid in all interior algebras that validate it. Both follow from the [[Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra|QC9M3Q8B8Q]] construction. Conversely, the formulas valid in a class of Heyting algebras, or of interior algebras, form a logic of the same type.
- (F2) Free Boolean extension. For every Heyting algebra $H$ there is a Grz-algebra $B(H)$ with $O(B(H)) = H$ as Heyting algebras.
- (F3) Blok's lemma. Let $B$ be a Grz-algebra, and let $B_0$ be the Boolean subalgebra generated by $O(B)$. Then every formula valid in $B_0$ is valid in $B$.

Part A: the translation in an algebra.

1. Claim: let $B$ be an interior algebra and $v$ a valuation in $B$. Let $v^\circ(p) = \square v(p)$, a valuation in $O(B)$. Then $v(T(A)) = v^\circ(A)$ for every intuitionistic $A$.
2. Proof by induction on $A$. For $p$, $v(T(p)) = \square v(p) = v^\circ(p)$. For $\bot$, both sides are $0$.
3. For $\wedge$ and $\vee$, the operations of $O(B)$ are those of $B$, and the induction hypothesis applies.
4. For $A \to C$, the induction hypothesis gives open values $x = v(T(A))$ and $y = v(T(C))$. Then $v(T(A \to C)) = \square(\neg x \vee y) = x \Rightarrow y$.
5. Every valuation $u$ in $O(B)$ has the form $v^\circ$, with $v = u$. So by step 1, $T(A)$ is valid in $B$ if and only if $A$ is valid in $O(B)$.
6. Let $M \in \mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{S4})$. By (F1) and step 5, $M \vdash T(A)$ iff $A$ is valid in $O(B)$ for every interior algebra $B$ that validates $M$.
7. So $\rho M$ is the set of formulas valid in a class of Heyting algebras. By (F1), $\rho M$ is a superintuitionistic logic.

Part B: $\rho \sigma L = L$ for every superintuitionistic logic $L$.

8. If $A \in L$, then $T(A) \in \sigma L$ by definition. So $L \subseteq \rho \sigma L$.
9. Conversely, let $A \notin L$. By (F1), there is a Heyting algebra $H$ that validates $L$ and a valuation $u$ in $H$ with $u(A) \neq 1$.
10. Let $B = B(H)$ from (F2). By step 5, $B$ validates $T(A')$ for every $A' \in L$, since $O(B) = H$ validates $L$.
11. $B$ is a Grz-algebra. By (F1), the formulas valid in $B$ form a normal extension of $\mathsf{Grz}$ that contains every $T(A')$. So $B$ validates $\sigma L$.
12. View $u$ as a valuation in $B$. Its values are open, so $u^\circ = u$, and step 1 gives $u(T(A)) = u(A) \neq 1$.
13. So $T(A)$ is not valid in $B$, and $\sigma L \nvdash T(A)$. Hence $A \notin \rho \sigma L$.

Part C: $\sigma \rho M = M$ for every $M \in \mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$.

14. $M$ contains $\mathsf{Grz}$, and $M \vdash T(A)$ for every $A \in \rho M$ by definition. $M$ is a normal logic, so $\sigma \rho M \subseteq M$.
15. Conversely, let $\varphi \notin M$. By (F1), there is an interior algebra $B$ that validates $M$ and refutes $\varphi$. $B$ is a Grz-algebra.
16. Let $B_0$ be as in (F3). Every element of $B_0$ is a finite meet of elements $\neg a \vee b$ with $a, b \in O(B)$.
17. Since $\square(\neg a \vee b) = a \Rightarrow b$ is open and $\square$ preserves finite meets, $B_0$ is closed under $\square$. So $B_0$ is an interior subalgebra of $B$, with $O(B_0) = O(B)$.
18. By (F3), $B_0$ refutes $\varphi$, since $B$ refutes it.
19. $B_0$ validates $\mathsf{Grz}$, since equations valid in $B$ hold in its subalgebras.
20. Let $A \in \rho M$. Then $B$ validates $T(A)$, so $O(B)$ validates $A$ by step 5. As $O(B_0) = O(B)$, step 5 shows that $B_0$ validates $T(A)$.
21. By steps 19 and 20 and (F1), $B_0$ validates $\sigma \rho M$. With step 18, $\varphi \notin \sigma \rho M$. So $M \subseteq \sigma \rho M$.

Part D: the isomorphism.

22. $\sigma L$ contains $\mathsf{Grz}$, so $\sigma$ maps $\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})$ into $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$. By step 7, $\rho$ maps $\mathrm{NExt}(\mathsf{Grz})$ into $\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})$.
23. Both maps preserve inclusion: $L \subseteq L'$ gives $\sigma L \subseteq \sigma L'$, and $M \subseteq M'$ gives $\rho M \subseteq \rho M'$.
24. By Parts B and C, the two maps are mutually inverse. Mutually inverse maps that preserve order are order isomorphisms.
25. An order isomorphism between lattices preserves meets and joins, so $\sigma$ is a lattice isomorphism with inverse $\rho$.

## Idea

The translation computes, inside an interior algebra, the intuitionistic value in its Heyting algebra of open elements.
So intuitionistic logics correspond to classes of Heyting algebras, and modal logics to classes of interior algebras.
Each Heyting algebra $H$ is the algebra of open elements of a Grz-algebra $B(H)$, which gives $\rho \sigma L = L$.
Blok's lemma says that a Grz-algebra is determined, up to its equations, by its open elements, which gives $\sigma \rho M = M$.
For $\mathsf{S4}$ the last step fails, because an interior algebra can have equations that its open elements do not control.

## Variants

Esakia's proof is dual: Heyting algebras correspond to Esakia spaces, and Grz-algebras correspond to modal spaces of a special form, by [[Esakia duality|CD9Y7YP4EJ]].
On the dual side, Blok's lemma becomes the claim that a Grz-space and its skeleton, the quotient by clusters, validate the same formulas.
A third route uses the theory of canonical formulas of Zakharyaschev, which axiomatize every logic in both lattices and are matched by the translation.
