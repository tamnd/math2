## Summary

The distance modulus of an object is the difference $m - M$ between its [[apparent magnitude|R8BRMEQ4DN]] and its [[absolute magnitude|D7EAZ5F0YT]].
It is a logarithmic measure of distance: $m - M = 5\log_{10}(d / 10\ \mathrm{pc})$ when there is no absorption.
Astronomers often quote distances to galaxies and clusters directly as distance moduli.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If you know how bright a star really is and how bright it looks, you can tell how far away it is.
The distance modulus packs this into one number.
Each step of $5$ in the distance modulus means ten times farther.
A distance modulus of $0$ means $10$ parsecs, and a distance modulus of $5$ means $100$ parsecs.
