## Summary

The Allee effect is a positive relation between the size of a population and the per capita growth rate at low population sizes.
It happens when individuals profit from others, for example to find mates or to defend themselves.
In its strong form there is a threshold below which the population declines to extinction.
This matters for conservation and for the control of invasive species.
It is named after the ecologist Warder Clyde Allee.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the simplest models, a small population grows fastest per individual, because each one has plenty of food.
For some species the opposite is true: a few scattered animals cannot find mates, so they do not reproduce well.
Then a very small population may shrink even though the habitat is good.
For example, in the model $N' = N(N - 10)(100 - N)$ a population of $5$ dies out, while a population of $20$ grows to $100$.
The value $10$ is the threshold that the population must exceed to survive.
