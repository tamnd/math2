## Summary

The J-integral is a contour integral around the tip of a crack that measures the energy flowing into the crack tip.
For a [[hyperelastic material|ZHK2EVRD90]], its value does not depend on the contour.
So it can be computed far from the tip, where the fields are easy to obtain.
For a linear elastic body it equals the [[energy release rate|D2D6BRJA5X]].
It is used as a fracture parameter in elastic–plastic [[fracture mechanics|SNRXGRZWR5]], where the stress intensity factor no longer applies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw a loop around the tip of a crack, from one face of the crack to the other.
Along the loop, add up the stored elastic energy and the work done by the forces acting through the loop.
The result is the same for every loop, small or large, because no energy is lost in between.
So the J-integral tells how much energy flows into the tip, using measurements taken far away from the tip.
