## Summary

In formal language theory, an alphabet is a finite nonempty [[set|0BSJ7RQ9BW]] whose elements are called symbols or letters.
[[Strings|1P6EP01HZF]] are finite sequences of symbols from an alphabet, and [[formal languages|5MFZ4VNY6D]] are sets of strings.
The usual alphabets are $\set{0, 1}$ for binary data and $\set{a, b}$ for examples.
The symbols have no meaning of their own; only their identity matters.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An alphabet is just the list of symbols one is allowed to write.
For a computer it is often the two bits $0$ and $1$.
For DNA it is the four letters A, C, G and T.
Words in the alphabet are then strings of these symbols, such as $0110$ or GATTACA.
