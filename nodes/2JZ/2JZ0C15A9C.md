## Summary

An incompressible flow is a flow in which the density of each fluid parcel stays constant as it moves.
By the [[continuity equation|FD4NR553YR]], this is the same as the condition that the velocity field has zero [[divergence|0MWV6ZTK6S]].
It is an excellent model for liquids and for gases moving much slower than the speed of sound.
It is the setting of the incompressible [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an incompressible flow, the fluid can change shape but not volume.
Any small blob of fluid may be stretched into a long thin thread, but its volume stays the same.
So as much fluid enters any small box as leaves it.
Water in a garden hose is an example: when you squeeze the end and the opening gets smaller, the water must come out faster.
