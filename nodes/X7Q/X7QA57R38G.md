## Definition

Let a planet of radius $a$ rotate with angular speed $\Omega$, and fix a reference latitude $\varphi_0$.
Use local Cartesian coordinates $x$ (east) and $y$ (north, the distance from latitude $\varphi_0$).
The *beta plane* at $\varphi_0$ is the model in which the equations of motion are written in these flat coordinates.
In it the [[Coriolis|6HZRX3WSQD]] parameter is
$$f = f_0 + \beta y, \qquad f_0 = 2 \Omega \sin \varphi_0, \qquad \beta = \frac{2 \Omega \cos \varphi_0}{a}.$$

## Remarks

The formula is the first-order Taylor expansion of $f = 2 \Omega \sin \varphi$ in $y = a (\varphi - \varphi_0)$, so it is accurate when $\abs{\beta y} \ll \abs{f_0}$.
The case $\beta = 0$ is the [[f-plane|287Z5PMWY5]].
At $\varphi_0 = 0$ one gets the *equatorial beta plane* $f = \beta y$ with $\beta = 2 \Omega / a$.

Example: for the Earth, $\Omega = 7.292 \times 10^{-5}$ s$^{-1}$ and $a = 6.371 \times 10^6$ m.
At $\varphi_0 = 45^\circ$ this gives $f_0 \approx 1.03 \times 10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$ and $\beta \approx 1.62 \times 10^{-11}$ m$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$.

Non-example: keeping $f = 2 \Omega \sin(\varphi_0 + y / a)$ exactly in flat coordinates is not a beta plane, since $f$ is not linear in $y$.
Such models lack the simple constant-coefficient wave solutions of the beta plane.

The beta-plane equations use flat geometry and drop all other curvature terms.
This is consistent only at leading order in $L / a$, where $L$ is the scale of the motion.
