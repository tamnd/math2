## Example

Take the frame and valuation
$$W = \set{a, b}, \quad R = \set{(a, b), (b, b)}, \quad V(p) = \set{b}.$$
We show that $Op \to p$ fails at $a$, so the formula is not a theorem of [[standard deontic logic|7B74J6J5JV]].

1. $R$ is [[serial|NRNKV2YYWJ]]: $a$ sees $b$, and $b$ sees $b$.
2. So the frame validates $\mathsf{SDL}$, by [[soundness of normal modal logics|E87G6ZTMTN]] and the correspondence of $\mathrm{D}$ with seriality in [[common modal axiom schemata|FDFQFWPM4X]].
3. The only ideal world of $a$ is $b$, and $p$ is true at $b$. So $a \models Op$.
4. $p$ is false at $a$, since $a \notin V(p)$.
5. So $Op \to p$ is false at $a$.
6. If $\mathsf{SDL}$ proved $Op \to p$, then by step 2 the formula would hold at $a$. Step 5 shows that it does not, so $\mathsf{SDL} \nvdash Op \to p$.

## Remarks

The world $a$ is not ideal for itself, since $(a, a) \notin R$.
This is the situation of a violated norm: $p$ ought to be the case at $a$, but it is not.
If we add $(a, a)$ to $R$, the frame becomes reflexive, $Op$ becomes false at $a$, and the countermodel is lost.
On reflexive frames $Op \to p$ is valid, and the logic becomes $\mathsf{KT}$, which is the logic of a necessity that is always met.
The weaker formula $O(Op \to p)$ holds on this frame: the only ideal world $b$ sees itself, so $Op \to p$ is true at $b$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rule says that the rent must be paid.
In the ideal situation the rent is paid.
In the real situation it is not paid.
So "the rent must be paid" is true and "the rent is paid" is false.
This is why "must" does not imply "is" in deontic logic.

### L2 human

1. Read worlds as situations and $R[w]$ as the situations where all rules valid at $w$ are followed.
2. $Op$ at $a$ asks only about $b$.
3. The truth of $p$ at $a$ is not tested by $Op$, because $a$ is not in $R[a]$.

The typical use of this countermodel is to separate $\mathsf{KD}$ from $\mathsf{KT}$ with two worlds.
A trap: seriality is not reflexivity.
Every reflexive frame is serial, but the converse fails here.
