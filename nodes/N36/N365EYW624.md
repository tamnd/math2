## Example

Take the following frame and valuation.
$$W = \{a, b\}, \quad R = \{(a, b), (b, b)\}, \quad V(p) = \{b\}$$
We show that $Op \to p$ fails at $a$, so the formula is not a theorem of [[standard deontic logic|7B74J6J5JV]].

1. The relation $R$ is [[serial|NRNKV2YYWJ]], since $a$ sees $b$ and $b$ sees $b$.
2. So the frame validates $\mathsf{SDL}$, by [[soundness of normal modal logics|E87G6ZTMTN]] and the match of $\mathrm{D}$ with seriality in [[common modal axiom schemata|FDFQFWPM4X]].
3. The only ideal world of $a$ is $b$, and $p$ is true at $b$, so $a \models Op$.
4. The atom $p$ is false at $a$, since $a \notin V(p)$.
5. So the implication $Op \to p$ is false at $a$.
6. If $\mathsf{SDL}$ proved $Op \to p$, then by step 2 the formula would hold at $a$.
   Step 5 shows that it does not, so $\mathsf{SDL} \nvdash Op \to p$.

## Remarks

The world $a$ is not ideal for itself, since $(a, a) \notin R$.
This is the situation of a violated norm: $p$ ought to be the case at $a$, but it is not.
If we add $(a, a)$ to $R$, the frame becomes reflexive, $Op$ becomes false at $a$, and the countermodel is lost.
On reflexive frames $Op \to p$ is valid, and the logic becomes $\mathsf{KT}$, a logic of a necessity that is always met.

The weaker formula $O(Op \to p)$ does hold on this frame.
The only ideal world of $a$ is $b$, and $b$ sees only itself, so $Op \to p$ is true at $b$.
So "it ought to be that obligations are met" can hold where "obligations are met" fails.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rule says that the rent must be paid.
In the ideal situation the rent is paid.
In the real situation it is not paid.
So "the rent must be paid" is true and "the rent is paid" is false.
This is why "must" does not imply "is" in deontic logic.

### L2 human

Read the worlds as situations and $R[w]$ as the situations where all the rules in force at $w$ are followed.
The formula $Op$ at $a$ asks only about $b$.
The truth of $p$ at $a$ is never tested by $Op$, because $a$ is not in $R[a]$.
This is the typical use of the countermodel: it separates $\mathsf{KD}$ from $\mathsf{KT}$ with two worlds.
Watch out that seriality is not reflexivity.
Every reflexive frame is serial, but this frame is serial and not reflexive.
