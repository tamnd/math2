## Summary

The group velocity of a wave is the velocity at which the envelope of a wave packet moves.
The envelope is the overall shape of a group of waves.
It is the gradient of the angular frequency with respect to the wave vector in the [[dispersion relation|R5P8M6SBG7]].
It differs from the [[phase velocity|FMDA4DKT0M]] in a dispersive medium, and in many situations it is the speed at which energy and information travel.
The method of stationary phase explains it: a packet is concentrated where the phases of its plane-wave components are stationary.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Throw a stone into a pond and watch the ring of ripples move out.
The individual crests run through the ring: they appear at its back, move forward and vanish at its front.
The ring itself, the group, moves at a different speed than the crests, and this speed is the group velocity.
For deep water waves the group moves at half the speed of the crests.
