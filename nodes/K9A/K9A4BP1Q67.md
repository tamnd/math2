## Definition

Let $(M, g)$ be a 4-dimensional [[Lorentzian manifold|D1SYF8F8RQ]], and let $(x^0, \dots, x^3)$ be local coordinates on an open set $U$.
Write $g = \det(g_{\mu\nu}) < 0$, and let
$$H^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta} = (-g) \bigl( g^{\mu\nu} g^{\alpha\beta} - g^{\alpha\nu} g^{\mu\beta} \bigr).$$
Take units with $G = 1$.
The *Landau–Lifshitz pseudotensor* in these coordinates is the array $t^{\mu\nu}_{LL}$ on $U$ defined by
$$16\pi (-g) \, t^{\mu\nu}_{LL} = \partial_\alpha \partial_\beta H^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta} - 2 (-g) \, G^{\mu\nu},$$
where $G^{\mu\nu}$ is the [[Einstein tensor|P13KTBC7MH]] of $g$.

## Remarks

The second derivatives of $g$ cancel on the right, so $t^{\mu\nu}_{LL}$ is a quadratic expression in the first derivatives of $g_{\mu\nu}$, with coefficients built from $g$.
It is symmetric in $\mu, \nu$, because $H^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta} = H^{\nu\beta\mu\alpha}$.
If the [[Einstein equations|7C2WP33E10]] $G^{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T^{\mu\nu}$ hold, then $\partial_\alpha \partial_\beta H^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta} = 16\pi (-g)(T^{\mu\nu} + t^{\mu\nu}_{LL})$.
Since $H^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}$ is antisymmetric in $\nu, \beta$, this gives $\partial_\nu \bigl( (-g)(T^{\mu\nu} + t^{\mu\nu}_{LL}) \bigr) = 0$.
Example: for Minkowski space in inertial coordinates, $H^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}$ is constant and $G^{\mu\nu} = 0$, so $t^{\mu\nu}_{LL} = 0$.
Non-example of tensorial behaviour: in spherical coordinates on Minkowski space, $H^{trtr} = -r^4 \sin^2\theta$, $H^{t\theta t\theta} = -r^2 \sin^2\theta$ and $H^{t\varphi t\varphi} = -r^2$.
So $16\pi (-g) \, t^{tt}_{LL} = -12 r^2 \sin^2\theta - 2 r^2 \cos 2\theta \ne 0$, although the metric is flat.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Landau–Lifshitz pseudotensor is a coordinate-dependent energy density of the gravitational field, chosen so that matter and gravity together satisfy an ordinary conservation law.
