## Summary

Fatigue is the weakening of a material under repeated loading.
It leads to failure at stresses well below those that break the material under a single load.
Small cracks start at points of stress concentration and grow a little with each cycle until the part breaks.
The fatigue life of a material is described by its S–N curve, the number of cycles to failure as a function of the stress amplitude.
Fatigue causes a large share of the failures of machines, vehicles and aircraft, so its prediction is a central task of engineering design.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Bend a paper clip once and it stays whole; bend it back and forth twenty times and it snaps.
No single bend was strong enough to break it, but each bend left a little damage behind.
Fatigue is this slow build-up of damage under repeated loads.
In metal parts it shows up as a crack that grows by a tiny step with each load cycle, often leaving visible rings on the broken surface.
