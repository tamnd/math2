## Statement

Let $k$ be a field of [[characteristic|1ZB13N78XW]] $p > 0$, and let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over $k$.
Then $\mathfrak g$ has a faithful finite-dimensional [[representation|TZAZ163QGK]].
Equivalently, $\mathfrak g$ is isomorphic to a Lie subalgebra of the [[general linear Lie algebra|G8FEGYDSNE]] $\mathfrak{gl}_n(k)$ for some $n$.

## Remarks

Idea of the proof: in characteristic $p$, the identity $(\operatorname{ad} x)^{p} = \operatorname{ad}(x^{p})$ holds in the universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak g)$.
For each $x$ in a basis of $\mathfrak g$, the endomorphism $\operatorname{ad} x$ of $\mathfrak g$ is killed by a nonzero polynomial whose exponents are all powers of $p$.
Evaluating this polynomial at $x$ gives a central element of $U(\mathfrak g)$.
Dividing $U(\mathfrak g)$ by the ideal generated by suitable such central elements gives a finite-dimensional algebra on which $\mathfrak g$ acts faithfully by left multiplication.

Together with Ado's theorem in characteristic $0$, this shows that every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over any field has a faithful finite-dimensional representation.
The [[adjoint representation|54XSPNAM1J]] alone does not suffice when the center is nonzero, since its kernel is the center.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of positive characteristic can be written as an algebra of matrices with the commutator bracket.
This is the positive characteristic companion of Ado's theorem.

### L2 human

In positive characteristic the proof is easier than in characteristic $0$.
The reason is that the universal enveloping algebra has a large center, generated by $p$-th power type elements, and it is a finite module over that center.
So it has many finite-dimensional quotients, and one of them carries a faithful action of $\mathfrak g$.
This is part of the theory of restricted Lie algebras and $p$-polynomials.
