## Summary

The equation of state of seawater is the function that gives the density of sea water from its salinity, temperature and pressure.
Density differences drive the deep circulation of the ocean, and they set its stratification, so this function enters every ocean model.
It is nonlinear, and the nonlinearity has physical effects such as cabbeling, the densification of a mixture of two waters of equal density.
The current international standard is TEOS-10, which derives density and the other thermodynamic properties from a single Gibbs function.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Sea water gets heavier when it is colder, when it is saltier, and when it is squeezed by the water above it.
The equation of state is the exact rule that turns these three numbers into a density.
At the surface, typical sea water has a density of about $1025 \ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, a little more than fresh water.
Small differences matter: water that is denser than its neighbours sinks, and this sinking drives slow currents through the whole deep ocean.
