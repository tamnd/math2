## Summary

A sequence of random variables can converge to a limit in several different senses.
The main modes are [[almost sure convergence|04P7D63W8P]], [[convergence in probability|0EFDDXTVD6]], [[convergence in mean|0TTA57SEJG]] and [[convergence in distribution|0JDAQHWA9N]].
Almost sure convergence and convergence in mean each imply convergence in probability, and convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution. The converse implications are false in general.

## Remarks

Convergence in distribution concerns only the laws of the random variables. So the random variables can be on different probability spaces.
The law of large numbers is a statement about convergence in probability or almost surely. The central limit theorem is a statement about convergence in distribution.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For numbers, there is one meaning of "$x_n \to x$". For random variables, there are several, because a random variable has many values at once.
Some modes ask that the values themselves come close, and the weakest mode asks only that the probabilities come close.
