## Summary

Self-focusing is the narrowing of an intense light beam in a medium whose refractive index increases with intensity.
This is the case of the optical [[Kerr effect|Y7WQY4568B]] with $n_2 > 0$.
The beam is brightest at its centre, so the medium acts like a converging lens.
Below a critical power, diffraction wins and the beam spreads; above it, the self-focusing wins and the beam collapses until other effects stop it.
In the simplest model it is described by the two-dimensional focusing [[nonlinear Schrödinger equation|GEJHRXC176]], whose solutions can blow up in finite distance.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A lens is thicker at the middle, so light through the middle is slowed more and the beam bends inward.
In some materials, bright light slows itself down.
A laser beam is brighter in the middle, so the material becomes a lens made by the beam itself.
If the beam is powerful enough, this lens beats the natural spreading of light and the beam squeezes itself to a tiny spot, which can damage the material.
