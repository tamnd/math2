## Summary

Biot consolidation theory is the three-dimensional theory of the slow settlement of a saturated porous solid under load, as the pore fluid drains away.
It is the quasi-static form of linear [[poroelasticity|987R9XR8KH]].
The displacement solves equations of elastic equilibrium with a pore pressure term, and the pore pressure solves a diffusion equation coupled to the volume change.
Maurice Biot published it in 1941 as a three-dimensional extension of Terzaghi's one-dimensional theory of consolidation.
It is the basis of settlement predictions for foundations, embankments and reservoirs.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a heavy building is placed on clay, the water in the clay first takes much of the load.
As water seeps out, the load moves onto the clay grains and the ground slowly sinks.
Biot's theory computes this sinking in time, in three dimensions, by tracking both the deformation of the soil frame and the pressure of the water.
Halving the distance the water has to travel makes the settling about four times faster, a sign that the process is a diffusion.
