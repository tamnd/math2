## Summary

The Gibbs free energy of a system is its enthalpy minus the product of its temperature and entropy.
It is the [[thermodynamic potential|MKZW03Q590]] whose natural variables are temperature and pressure.
At fixed temperature and pressure it is minimal in equilibrium.
So a process at fixed temperature and pressure can run by itself only if it lowers the Gibbs free energy.
This makes it the central quantity of chemical thermodynamics and of phase equilibria.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Most chemistry happens in open vessels at room temperature and air pressure.
Under these conditions a reaction goes forward by itself exactly when it lowers the Gibbs free energy.
The Gibbs free energy balances two tendencies: to lower the enthalpy, and to raise the entropy.
For example, ice melts above $0$ degrees Celsius at normal pressure because there liquid water has the lower Gibbs free energy.
