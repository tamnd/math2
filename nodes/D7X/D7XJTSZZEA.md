## Problem

Consider a [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker|ECJMS4VDTF]] model with spatial curvature $k$, Hubble parameter $H$ and [[scale factor|J70F32P86W]] $a$.
Its first [[Friedmann equation|PPHFS2P4G4]] can be written as
$$\Omega(t) - 1 = \frac{k}{a(t)^2 H(t)^2},$$
where $\Omega$ is the total density divided by the critical density $3H^2/(8\pi G)$.
In the radiation era $\abs{\Omega - 1}$ grows like $t$, and in the matter era like $t^{2/3}$.
Observations show that $\Omega$ is close to $1$ today.
So in the early universe $\Omega$ must have been equal to $1$ with extreme precision.
At the time of nucleosynthesis, a deviation of more than a tiny fraction would have led to a very different universe today.
The problem is to explain this fine tuning of the initial conditions of the [[Big Bang|202254GTP0]] model.

## Remarks

The growth rates follow from $aH = \dot a$: for $a \propto t^{1/2}$ one has $\dot a \propto t^{-1/2}$, so $1/\dot a^2 \propto t$; for $a \propto t^{2/3}$ one has $1/\dot a^2 \propto t^{2/3}$.
More generally $\abs{\Omega - 1}$ grows whenever $\dot a$ decreases, that is, whenever the expansion decelerates.
Cosmic inflation reverses this: while $\ddot a > 0$, $\dot a$ grows and $\Omega$ is driven toward $1$, which is the standard proposed solution.
Some authors question whether the fine tuning is a real problem, for example on probabilistic grounds, so its status is partly a matter of interpretation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The geometry of space today is almost exactly flat.
In a universe that only decelerates, any small curvature grows quickly with time, so flatness is an unstable state.
For space to be this flat now, it must have started incredibly close to perfectly flat, like a pencil balanced on its tip for billions of years.
Explaining why is the flatness problem.
