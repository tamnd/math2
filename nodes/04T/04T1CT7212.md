## Statement

Let $\varphi$ and $\psi$ be formulas of propositional logic.
The contrapositive of the [[implication|0ZNX6DQ3EJ]] $\varphi \to \psi$ is the implication $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$, where $\neg$ is [[negation|0XR7A7RSXB]].
The law of contraposition says that an implication and its contrapositive are [[logically equivalent|06GYGS41JK]]:
$$\varphi \to \psi \;\equiv\; \neg \psi \to \neg \varphi.$$
Equivalently, the biconditional $(\varphi \to \psi) \leftrightarrow (\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi)$ is a [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]].
The law consists of two directions.

1. Direction (a): $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$ is a [[logical consequence|1HK0VYWTV7]] of $\varphi \to \psi$.
2. Direction (b): $\varphi \to \psi$ is a logical consequence of $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$.

Both directions hold in classical logic.
In [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]], direction (a) holds and direction (b) does not hold in general.

In Mathlib, the classical equivalence is `not_imp_not`, stated as `¬a → ¬b ↔ b → a`.
Direction (a) alone is `mt`, of type `(a → b) → ¬b → ¬a`, and its proof uses no classical axiom.
Here `¬a` is `Not a`, which Lean defines as `a → False`.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The statement puts no condition on $\varphi$ and $\psi$, but the form of the contrapositive and the logic in use both matter.

1. Both parts must be negated. Without the negations we get the [[converse|09A3N7ZN5G]] $\psi \to \varphi$, which differs from $\varphi \to \psi$ in each valuation where exactly one of $\varphi$, $\psi$ is true.
2. Both parts must change places. Without the swap we get the [[inverse|VB7B8766D2]] $\neg \varphi \to \neg \psi$, which differs from $\varphi \to \psi$ in the same valuations.
3. Direction (b) needs classical logic, through the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]] or [[double negation elimination|0NFAGWH84K]]. In intuitionistic logic take $\varphi = \neg\neg r$ and $\psi = r$ for a propositional variable $r$. Then $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$ is $\neg r \to \neg\neg\neg r$, which holds intuitionistically. But $\varphi \to \psi$ is $\neg\neg r \to r$, which does not.

### Consequences

1. [[Proof by contrapositive|0BB5TPGQ1N]]: to prove $\varphi \to \psi$, it is enough to prove $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$. The proof method has its own page; this page is about the formula. The method uses direction (b), so it is a classical method.
2. [[Modus tollens|32AQPJQQAM]]: from $\varphi \to \psi$ and $\neg \psi$ we infer $\neg \varphi$. It is direction (a) followed by [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]].
3. The [[converse and the inverse|09A3N7ZN5G]] of an implication are logically equivalent, because the inverse $\neg \varphi \to \neg \psi$ is the contrapositive of the converse $\psi \to \varphi$.
4. For predicates, $\forall x\, (P(x) \to Q(x))$ is equivalent to $\forall x\, (\neg Q(x) \to \neg P(x))$, by the law applied to each $x$.
5. For sets, $A \subseteq B$ holds exactly when $B^c \subseteq A^c$, where ${}^c$ is the [[complement|0RKF1ZT5JJ]] in a fixed universe. This is consequence 4 with $P(x)$ "$x \in A$" and $Q(x)$ "$x \in B$".
6. A side hypothesis can stay in place: $(\varphi \land \chi) \to \psi$ is equivalent to $(\neg \psi \land \chi) \to \neg \varphi$. Both formulas are false exactly when $\varphi$, $\chi$ are true and $\psi$ is false.

### Converse

The law is a biconditional, so the question of its converse concerns each direction.
The converse of direction (a) is direction (b).
It is true in classical logic, by the [[truth table proof|SWH5EJP8PS]].
It is false in intuitionistic logic, by the example in item 3 above.
The [[proof by rules of inference|QB99VRZQ4Y]] shows the one step that uses excluded middle.

### Generalizations

1. In a [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]], $a \le b$ holds exactly when $b' \le a'$, where $'$ is the complement. Complementation reverses the order, and the law of contraposition is the case of the algebra of formulas.
2. In a [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]], $a \le b$ implies $\neg b \le \neg a$, but the converse can fail. This is the algebraic form of the intuitionistic status of the two directions.
3. Contraposition with several hypotheses: $(\varphi_1 \land \varphi_2) \to \psi$ is equivalent to $\neg \psi \to (\neg \varphi_1 \lor \neg \varphi_2)$, by contraposition and [[De Morgan's laws|09PKJKA6J0]].

## History

Modus tollens, the rule of inference behind direction (a), is in the list of indemonstrable arguments of the Stoic logician Chrysippus.
The truth-table proof uses the method of truth tables, which Wittgenstein and Post used in 1921.
Brouwer rejected the law of excluded middle, and in the intuitionistic logic that grew from his work only direction (a) remains valid.

## Explanations

### L1 human

"If it rains, then the street is wet" says the same as "if the street is not wet, then it does not rain".
Both sentences are broken by the same single situation: rain on a dry street.
In every other situation both sentences are kept.
So a statement and its contrapositive are always true together or false together.

### L2 human

We use the law to show that for every integer $n$, if $n^2$ is odd, then $n$ is odd.

1. Let $\varphi$ be "$n^2$ is odd" and $\psi$ be "$n$ is odd".
2. The contrapositive $\neg \psi \to \neg \varphi$ reads "if $n$ is even, then $n^2$ is even".
3. Suppose $n$ is even, so $n = 2k$ for an integer $k$. Then $n^2 = 4k^2 = 2(2k^2)$, which is even.
4. So the contrapositive is true for every $n$, and the law of contraposition gives the original implication.

The typical use is the one in this example: the contrapositive has a hypothesis that is easier to use, here "$n = 2k$".
A trap is to write the inverse "if $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even" and to call it the contrapositive.
That statement happens to be true, but it is equivalent to the converse "if $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd", not to the claim.

### L3 human

In a Boolean algebra negation is an order-reversing involution, and contraposition is the statement that it reverses the order in both directions.
In a Heyting algebra, $\neg a = a \to 0$ is only order reversing, and $\neg\neg a \ge a$ can be strict, so only direction (a) survives.
This is why Mathlib has the constructive `mt` and the classical `not_imp_not` as two separate results.

Contraposition also appears as duality in other places.
In a [[Galois connection|Q5JHEPRYQ6]] between two orders, an antitone pair of maps reverses order in the same way.
For a linear map $T$ between finite-dimensional spaces, $T$ is injective exactly when its [[transpose|09DXWYRD43]] is surjective.
In the classical [[sequent calculus|W72CA3R9MP]], negation moves a formula from one side of a sequent to the other.
This symmetry between the two sides is the structural reason for classical contraposition.
