## Summary

The causal structure of a [[Lorentzian manifold|BY4QYYS788]] describes which events can influence which other events.
It starts from the classification of tangent vectors into timelike, null and spacelike.
It then consists of the relations of being reachable by a future-directed timelike curve, or by a future-directed causal curve.
It depends only on the [[light cones|JZ9ADDZX8D]], so it is unchanged when the metric is multiplied by a positive function.
It is the language of global questions in general relativity: horizons, singularities, global hyperbolicity and time travel.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In relativity nothing travels faster than light, so an event can only affect events inside its future light cone.
The causal structure collects, for every event, the set of events it can affect and the set of events that can affect it.
In flat spacetime, the events that a flash of light at the origin can reach are those with $t \ge \abs{x}$.
In a curved spacetime the light cones tilt from place to place, and the causal structure can become much more interesting.
Near a black hole, for instance, the cones tilt so far that nothing inside can reach the outside.
