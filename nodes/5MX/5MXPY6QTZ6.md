## Summary

The Emden–Chandrasekhar equation is the [[ordinary differential equation|0NT64JV96A]] that describes a self-gravitating ball of gas at one fixed temperature, an isothermal sphere.
It is the limit of the [[Lane–Emden equation|ANRZZMH2Q0]] when the polytropic index tends to infinity.
Its solutions have infinite radius and infinite mass, so a real isothermal cloud must be bounded by outside pressure.
It is the basis of models of star clusters and of cold molecular clouds, and it leads to the [[Bonnor–Ebert mass|MEFJ35FV3F]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a cloud of gas that has the same temperature everywhere and is held together by its own gravity.
Its pressure is then proportional to its density, and the balance of pressure and gravity gives the Emden–Chandrasekhar equation.
The density is highest at the centre and falls off with distance, but never reaches zero.
Far from the centre it falls like the inverse square of the distance, so the total mass grows without bound.
