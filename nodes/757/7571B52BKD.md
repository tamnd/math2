## Summary

The alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) solves convex problems whose objective is a sum $f(x) + g(z)$ of two terms linked by a linear constraint.
It is a variant of the [[augmented Lagrangian method|AX0NJ6NK81]] that minimizes over $x$ and over $z$ one after the other instead of jointly.
Each subproblem then involves only one of the two functions, and it is often solvable in closed form.
ADMM is widely used in statistics, signal processing and distributed optimization.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some problems are hard as a whole but easy in two halves.
ADMM gives each half its own copy of the variables and adds the rule that the copies must agree.
It then improves one copy, then the other, and adjusts a price that punishes disagreement.
For example, a least squares fit with an absolute-value penalty splits into a smooth quadratic step and a simple "shrink toward zero" step.
