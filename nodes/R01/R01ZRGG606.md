## Example

"If A gives B and B gives C, then A gives C" seems too basic to prove, but a system with one rule has to build it from axioms.
Consider a [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]] for implication with modus ponens as its only rule and the two axiom schemas
$$\text{(A1)}\quad \varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi), \qquad \text{(A2)}\quad (\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)) \to ((\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi)).$$
We derive $p \to r$ from the hypotheses $p \to q$ and $q \to r$, so [[hypothetical syllogism|7MT8CPCEM4]] is a derived rule of the system.

1. $q \to r$, a hypothesis.
2. $(q \to r) \to (p \to (q \to r))$, by (A1) with $\varphi = q \to r$ and $\psi = p$.
3. $p \to (q \to r)$, by [[modus ponens|0Y0MQYST0M]] on lines 1 and 2.
4. $(p \to (q \to r)) \to ((p \to q) \to (p \to r))$, by (A2) with $\varphi = p$, $\psi = q$ and $\chi = r$.
5. $(p \to q) \to (p \to r)$, by modus ponens on lines 3 and 4.
6. $p \to q$, a hypothesis.
7. $p \to r$, by modus ponens on lines 6 and 5.

## Remarks

### What the example shows

The derivation uses each axiom schema once and modus ponens three times.
Since $p$, $q$ and $r$ can be replaced by any formulas, the same seven lines derive $\varphi \to \chi$ from $\varphi \to \psi$ and $\psi \to \chi$.
After this lemma, later derivations can use the rule as if it were primitive.
By the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] applied twice, $(q \to r) \to ((p \to q) \to (p \to r))$ is a theorem of the system.
Both axiom schemas are valid intuitionistically, so the derived rule holds in intuitionistic logic too.

### Check the strategy

Line 2 uses (A1) to put the hypothesis $q \to r$ under the extra condition $p$.
Line 4 uses (A2) to move the condition $p$ through the arrow, from "under $p$, $q$ gives $r$" to "if $p$ gives $q$, then $p$ gives $r$".
Lines 5 and 7 detach the conclusion by modus ponens.

### Watch out

In modus ponens the order of the premises matters.
Line 7 uses the antecedent $p \to q$ from line 6 and the implication from line 5, not the other way round.

### Try it

Which instance of (A1) gives line 2?
Take $\varphi = q \to r$ and $\psi = p$, so (A1) reads $(q \to r) \to (p \to (q \to r))$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Hilbert system has very few rules: some fixed formulas called axioms, and one rule that removes an "if".
Even the step "if A gives B and B gives C, then A gives C" has to be built from these.
The seven lines above build it once, so that it never needs to be built again.

### L2 human

Read the derivation as a strategy: first attach the extra condition $p$, then push it through the arrow, then detach.
(A1) attaches the condition, (A2) moves it, and modus ponens detaches.
Later derivations cite the lemma instead of repeating the seven lines.
