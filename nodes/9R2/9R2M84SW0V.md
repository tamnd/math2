## Summary

The actuarial present value of a payment stream is its expected discounted value, when both the timing and the occurrence of the payments are random.
It combines interest, through a discount factor, with mortality or other risks, through probabilities.
Premiums and reserves of life insurance and annuities are computed by setting actuarial present values equal.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Money paid later is worth less today, because today's money can earn interest.
An insurer also does not know whether a payment will be made at all, since it may depend on someone dying or surviving.
The actuarial present value first discounts each possible payment to today and then averages over all the ways things can turn out.
For example, a promise to pay $1000$ in one year if a person aged $60$ is then alive, with survival probability $0.99$ and interest rate $5\%$, is worth $1000 \cdot 0.99 / 1.05 \approx 942.86$ today.
