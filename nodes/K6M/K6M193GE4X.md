## Example

In *matching pennies* two players each show $H$ or $T$.
If the coins match, player 1 wins $1$ from player 2; otherwise player 2 wins $1$ from player 1.
The payoff matrix of player 1 is
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},$$
and player 2 gets $-A$, so this is a [[zero-sum game|8PC5CFGMAD]].

There is no pure [[Nash equilibrium|745WYP9YJ0]]: in each of the four cells the losing player gains $2$ by switching.
Let player 2 play $H$ with probability $q$.
Player 1 gets $2q - 1$ from $H$ and $1 - 2q$ from $T$, so she is indifferent only at $q = 1/2$.
By symmetry, the unique Nash equilibrium is that both players play each side with probability $1/2$, a pair of [[mixed strategies|P01KKHB155]].
The value of the game is $0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

One player wants the coins to match and the other wants them to differ.
Any predictable choice can be exploited, so the only stable plan is to flip the coin fairly.
This is the simplest game where randomising is needed.
