## Proof

We show that the standard basis is a basis with $n$ vectors.

1. For $i = 1, \dots, n$, let $e_i \in F^n$ have entry $1$ in position $i$ and $0$ elsewhere. These are the vectors of the [[standard basis|0V7ZD6P7RK]].
2. For $i \ne j$, the vectors $e_i$ and $e_j$ differ in position $i$. So $e_1, \dots, e_n$ are $n$ distinct vectors.
3. Let $a_1, \dots, a_n \in F$. By the operations of the [[coordinate space|0XM8BZD76G]], $a_1 e_1 + \cdots + a_n e_n = (a_1, \dots, a_n)$.
4. Each $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n) \in F^n$ equals $x_1 e_1 + \cdots + x_n e_n$, by step 3. So $e_1, \dots, e_n$ [[span|0BJKVF90Z6]] $F^n$.
5. If $a_1 e_1 + \cdots + a_n e_n = 0$, then $(a_1, \dots, a_n) = (0, \dots, 0)$ by step 3. So $a_1 = \cdots = a_n = 0$, and $e_1, \dots, e_n$ are [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]].
6. By steps 4 and 5, $e_1, \dots, e_n$ form a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $F^n$. For $n = 0$ this is the empty basis of $F^0 = \set{0}$.
7. By step 4, $F^n$ has a finite spanning set, so it is [[finite-dimensional|018W3V3TCT]].
8. By the [[dimension theorem|0WK0Y8RH1M]], all bases of $F^n$ have the same number of vectors. By the definition of [[dimension|05G2G0P224]] and steps 2 and 6, $\dim F^n = n$.

## Idea

Writing a vector of $F^n$ as a combination of $e_1, \dots, e_n$ just lists its entries. So the standard basis spans $F^n$, and a combination is zero only when all its coefficients are zero. A basis with $n$ vectors gives dimension $n$.
