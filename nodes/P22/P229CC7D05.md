## Definition

Consider a family of flows governed by the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] that depends on a control parameter, usually the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]] $\mathrm{Re}$.
A flow is *laminar* when its velocity field is smooth and regular, steady or periodic, with fluid layers moving in an orderly way.
*Laminar–turbulent transition* is the change of the flow from a laminar state to a [[turbulent|VY307F8Z0S]] state as $\mathrm{Re}$ increases.
In a spatially developing flow, such as a boundary layer, the change can also occur along the direction of the flow.

## Remarks

The definition is descriptive, since there is no single accepted mathematical definition of turbulence.
Two main scenarios are distinguished.

In supercritical, or natural, transition, the laminar flow loses linear [[stability|H028A17Z55]] at a critical Reynolds number.
Small disturbances such as [[Tollmien–Schlichting waves|TQE8EN865Z]] then grow, secondary instabilities follow, and the flow breaks down into turbulence.
In subcritical, or bypass, transition, turbulence appears at Reynolds numbers where the laminar flow is linearly stable, triggered by disturbances of finite size.

Example: in Reynolds' pipe experiments, transition occurred near $\mathrm{Re} \approx 2000$ based on mean speed and diameter.
Yet Hagen–Poiseuille flow is linearly stable at all Reynolds numbers for which it has been computed, so this is subcritical transition.
Non-example: the onset of steady Taylor vortices between rotating cylinders is a laminar-to-laminar bifurcation, not a transition to turbulence, since the new flow is still steady and regular.
