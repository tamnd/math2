## Summary

Quantum entanglement is the property of a state of a composite quantum system that cannot be described by assigning a state to each part separately.
Formally, a state on a [[tensor product of Hilbert spaces|EY0X2NKA3S]] is entangled when it is not a [[separable state|7F386TSFRW]].
Entangled states produce correlations that no local hidden-variable model reproduces, as [[Bell's theorem|7X9M1G56VP]] shows.
Entanglement is the main resource of quantum information, used in teleportation, dense coding and quantum key distribution.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two coins can be correlated: a friend puts one coin heads up and one tails up in two boxes, and opening one box tells you about the other.
Entangled particles are correlated in a stronger way that no such hidden preparation explains.
For two qubits in the state $(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$, each qubit alone looks completely random.
Yet measuring both in the same basis always gives equal results, and this holds in every basis of a certain family.
The whole pair has a definite state, while neither part has one of its own.
