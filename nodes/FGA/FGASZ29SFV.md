## Summary

An anomaly is a symmetry of a classical field theory that is lost in the quantum theory.
The classically conserved [[Noether|XHPDM5T2JG]] current acquires a nonzero divergence, a local expression in the fields, that no choice of regularization and local counterterms can remove.
Anomalies of global symmetries have physical effects, such as the decay of the neutral pion into two photons.
Anomalies of gauge symmetries would make a theory inconsistent, so their cancellation constrains the particle content, for example in the Standard Model.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In classical physics, a symmetry gives a conserved quantity.
When the theory is quantized, one must tame infinite sums, and some symmetries cannot survive this taming.
The conserved quantity then changes in a way that can be computed exactly.
The best-known case is the [[chiral anomaly|JT8G0FWR01]]: the number of left-handed minus right-handed massless fermions is not conserved in the presence of electric and magnetic fields that are parallel.
