## Proof

1. Let $G$ be a [[cyclic group|0KVGH0J5B3]], and let $g$ be a generator. So $G = \langle g \rangle$, the [[cyclic subgroup generated by $g$|08ABT85339]].
2. Let $x, y \in G$. Since each element of $\langle g \rangle$ is a power of $g$, there are integers $m$ and $n$ with $x = g^m$ and $y = g^n$.
3. By the [[laws of exponents|00DRK90Z6K]], $xy = g^m g^n = g^{m+n}$.
4. By the same law, $yx = g^n g^m = g^{n+m}$.
5. Addition of integers is commutative, so $m + n = n + m$, and thus $g^{m+n} = g^{n+m}$.
6. By steps 3 to 5, $xy = yx$. Since $x$ and $y$ were arbitrary, $G$ is [[abelian|082GHNPJ53]]. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Each element is a power of one generator, and the product of two powers of the same element is the power with the sum of the exponents.
So the commutativity of $G$ comes from the commutativity of addition in $\mathbb{Z}$.

## Variants

The map $\psi \colon \mathbb{Z} \to G$, $\psi(k) = g^k$, is a surjective [[group homomorphism|0JYTG5K9Y4]].
For $x = \psi(m)$ and $y = \psi(n)$, we get $xy = \psi(m + n) = \psi(n + m) = yx$.
So the proof is an instance of the fact that a homomorphic image of an abelian group is abelian.
