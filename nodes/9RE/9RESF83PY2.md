## Statement

The [[polar motion|QTRK8GS2Y1]] of the Earth contains a free oscillation, the Chandler wobble, with a period of about $433$ days.
In it the rotation pole moves around its mean position, counterclockwise as seen from above the North Pole.
This period is longer than the Euler period of about $305$ days.
The Euler period is the period of the [[Euler free nutation|9HF39441Y0]] of a rigid Earth with the same moments of inertia.

## Remarks

For a rigid axisymmetric body, Euler's equations give a free wobble with period $A/(C-A)$ rotation periods, where $A < C$ are the principal moments of inertia.
For the Earth this is about $305$ days.
The Earth is not rigid: the elastic yielding of the mantle, the oceans and the fluid core reduce the effective restoring effect of the equatorial bulge, which lengthens the period.
The wobble would decay by friction, so it must be excited continually; fluctuations of pressure in the atmosphere and the oceans are thought to be the main source.
It was discovered by the astronomer Seth Carlo Chandler in 1891 from variations of the latitudes of observatories.
Its amplitude varies over decades, and on the Earth's surface it is a motion of a few metres.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If you spin a slightly flattened ball whose spin axis is a little off its axis of symmetry, the spin axis wobbles around the symmetry axis.
The Earth does the same, and its spin axis traces a loop of a few metres around the pole about every fourteen months.
A rigid Earth would wobble every ten months, and the difference shows that the Earth gives a little, like a body that is partly elastic and partly fluid.

### L2 human

The Chandler period is a measurement of the Earth's response to slow changes of its centrifugal potential.
It depends on the [[Love numbers|C8HCN8NEQP]], in particular on $k_2$, and on the ocean and core, so it is a test of models of the Earth's interior.
The other large part of polar motion is an annual oscillation forced by seasonal shifts of air and water.
The sum of the two shows a beat of about six years.
