## Summary

Let a group act on a set.
A paradoxical decomposition of the set splits it into finitely many pieces.
Group elements then move these pieces to form two copies of the set.
A set with such a decomposition is called paradoxical.
The free group on two generators is paradoxical under its own left translations.
The unit ball of $\R^3$ is paradoxical under rigid motions, which is the [[Banach–Tarski paradox|FH250K068Z]].
[[Tarski's theorem|BTRKZSM2J4]] shows that paradoxical decompositions are exactly the obstruction to invariant finitely additive measures.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine cutting a set into a few pieces and then moving the pieces around by symmetries of the set.
In everyday life the total size stays the same, so one cannot make two copies from one.
For some infinite sets and groups this is possible, and the cutting is then called a paradoxical decomposition.
Take the free group on $a$ and $b$.
The words starting with $a$, together with $a$ times the words starting with $a^{-1}$, already make up the whole group.
The same holds with $b$ in place of $a$.
So four disjoint pieces of the group are enough to rebuild the group twice.
