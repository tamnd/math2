## Summary

Ecological stability is the ability of an ecological system to stay near a state, or to return to it, after a disturbance.
In models it is usually made precise as the [[asymptotic stability|0NB689782W]] of an [[equilibrium point|0N8NGKDZDZ]] of a [[population model|1YEMFQ1H32]] of the community.
Related notions are resilience, the speed of return after a small disturbance, and resistance, the size of the change caused by a disturbance.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a ball at the bottom of a bowl: push it and it rolls back.
A stable community behaves like this: if a drought kills some of the plants, the numbers of the species return to their old values.
An unstable community is like a ball on a hilltop: a small push sends it away, and some species may disappear.
How fast the ball rolls back is the resilience of the community.
