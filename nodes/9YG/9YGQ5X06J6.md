## Definition

A *context-sensitive grammar* is a [[formal grammar|DP1HXZGPCM]] $G = (N, \Sigma, P, S)$ in which every rule has one of the following forms:

1. $\alpha A \beta \to \alpha \gamma \beta$, where $A \in N$, $\alpha, \beta \in (N \cup \Sigma)^*$ and $\gamma \in (N \cup \Sigma)^*$ is nonempty;
2. $S \to \varepsilon$, allowed only if $S$ does not occur on the right side of any rule.

A [[formal language|5MFZ4VNY6D]] $L$ is *context-sensitive* if $L = L(G)$ for some context-sensitive grammar $G$.

## Remarks

Equivalently, $L$ is context-sensitive if it is generated by a *noncontracting* grammar, one whose rules $\alpha \to \beta$ satisfy $\abs{\alpha} \le \abs{\beta}$, again with the exception $S \to \varepsilon$.
Example: $\set{a^n b^n c^n : n \ge 1}$ is context-sensitive; the rules $S \to aSBC$, $S \to aBC$, $CB \to BC$, $aB \to ab$, $bB \to bb$, $bC \to bc$, $cC \to cc$ form a noncontracting grammar for it.
Non-example: a rule $AB \to A$ is not allowed, since it shortens the string and is not of the form of clause 2.
Every [[context-free language|Q56EWEQYE2]] is context-sensitive, and every context-sensitive language is decidable.
