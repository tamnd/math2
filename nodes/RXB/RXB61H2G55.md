## Definition

Let UT1 be the time scale defined by the [[rotation of the Earth|EF1Q2JF8QH]], and let TAI be atomic time measured in SI seconds.
The *length of day* $\mathrm{LOD}(t)$ near time $t$ is the interval of atomic time, in SI seconds, during which UT1 advances by $86400$ seconds.
The *excess length of day* is
$$\Delta\mathrm{LOD}(t) = \mathrm{LOD}(t) - 86400\ \mathrm{s},$$
and *length of day variations* are the changes of $\Delta\mathrm{LOD}$ with time.

## Remarks

To first order, $\Delta\mathrm{LOD}/86400\ \mathrm{s} = -\Delta\omega/\Omega$, where $\Omega$ is the nominal rate of rotation and $\Delta\omega = \omega - \Omega$ the deviation of the [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]] about the polar axis.
Example: $\Delta\mathrm{LOD} = 1$ ms corresponds to $\Delta\omega/\Omega \approx -1.16 \cdot 10^{-8}$; if it lasts for a year, UT1 falls behind atomic time by about $0.37$ s.
Non-example: the solar day of $86400$ s is longer than the sidereal day of about $86164$ s, but this difference is not a length of day variation.
It comes from the orbital motion of the Earth, not from a change of its rotation.
