## Summary

A climate model is a mathematical model of the climate system.
It covers the atmosphere, the ocean, the land surface and the ice, and the exchanges of energy, water and momentum among them.
Its equations are conservation laws of mass, momentum and energy, with the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] on the rotating Earth and radiative transfer at their core.
Climate models form a hierarchy, from conceptual energy balance models with one variable to general circulation models solved numerically on grids with millions of cells.
They are the main tool for understanding past climates and for projecting the response of the climate to changes in greenhouse gases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A weather forecast predicts the state of the atmosphere a few days ahead; a climate model predicts its statistics over decades: averages, seasons, extremes.
The simplest climate model treats the whole Earth as one ball that absorbs sunlight and radiates heat to space, and asks at which temperature the two balance.
The most complex ones divide the atmosphere and ocean into small boxes and compute winds, currents, clouds and ice in each box, step by step.
For example, the simple ball model gives an average temperature of about $255$ kelvin.
The gap to the observed $288$ kelvin is due to the greenhouse effect, which more detailed models include.
