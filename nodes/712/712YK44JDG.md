## Definition

A process of a thermodynamic system is *irreversible* if it is not [[reversible|F3DHZ45H6A]].
That is, no process restores both the system and its surroundings to their initial states.

## Remarks

The free expansion of an ideal gas into a vacuum is irreversible.
The gas can be compressed back, but that requires work, and heat must then be given to the surroundings, which keep a change.
The slow, frictionless isothermal expansion of a gas against a balancing piston is not irreversible.
By the second law, a process of an isolated system is irreversible exactly when its [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]] increases strictly.
