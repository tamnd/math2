## Summary

Density wave theory explains the spiral arms of disc galaxies as waves of higher density that rotate through the disc with a fixed pattern speed.
Stars and gas pass through the arms, like cars through a traffic jam, instead of staying in them.
The theory was proposed by C. C. Lin and F. H. Shu in 1964, and it solves the winding problem of material arms.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If spiral arms were made of the same stars forever, the faster inner parts of the disc would wind them up tightly within a few rotations.
Real galaxies do not show such tightly wound arms.
Density wave theory says an arm is a place where stars crowd together for a while, like a traffic jam on a road.
Each star enters the jam, slows down, and later leaves it, while the jam itself keeps its shape and moves at its own speed.
Gas squeezed in the arms forms new stars, which is why the arms shine with young blue stars.
