## Summary

The diffraction limit is the finest detail that an optical system can resolve when its only flaw is diffraction at its finite aperture.
A system that reaches it is called diffraction-limited: its lenses are good enough that aberrations no longer matter.
For a microscope the classical Abbe limit is the period $\lambda / (2\,\mathrm{NA})$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength and $\mathrm{NA}$ is the numerical aperture.
The [[Rayleigh criterion|K9GR534B1N]] gives a similar bound for two point sources.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light is a wave, and a wave squeezed through an opening spreads out.
So even a perfect lens turns a point into a small blurred spot, whose size is about the wavelength divided by the angular size of the lens.
Details smaller than this spot cannot be seen, however carefully the lens is polished.
This is why electron microscopes, with much shorter wavelengths, see finer details than light microscopes.
Large telescope mirrors are built partly to make this spot smaller.
