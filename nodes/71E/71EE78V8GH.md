## Statement

Use the notation of [[twistor theory|YVRVYE0ZWS]]: $Z = (\omega, \pi) \in \C^4$, the form $\Sigma(Z) = \omega^* \pi + \pi^* \omega$, and the line $L_x \subseteq \mathbb{CP}^3$ of a point $x$.
Let $\mathbb{PT}^+ \subseteq \mathbb{CP}^3$ be the open set where $\Sigma > 0$.
Let $\mathbb{CM}^+$ be the open set of points $x$ of complexified Minkowski space with $L_x \subseteq \mathbb{PT}^+$, the forward tube.
For an integer $k$, let $\mathcal{O}(k)$ be the sheaf of holomorphic sections of the $k$-th power of the dual of the [[tautological line bundle|BDB06N91FN]].
Let $h \ge 0$ be such that $2h$ is an integer.
Then there is a natural isomorphism, the *Penrose transform*, with domain the [[sheaf cohomology|C7E5EMV89Z]] group $H^1(\mathbb{PT}^+, \mathcal{O}(-2h-2))$.
Its image is the space of holomorphic massless fields of helicity $h$ on $\mathbb{CM}^+$.
For $h = 0$ these fields are the holomorphic solutions $\phi$ of the [[wave equation|5EHD2XBQDG]] $\square \phi = 0$.
For $h > 0$ they are the totally symmetric spinor fields $\phi_{A'_1 \dots A'_{2h}}$ with $\nabla^{A A'_1} \phi_{A'_1 \dots A'_{2h}} = 0$.
Here spinor indices are two-component indices, and $\nabla_{AA'}$ is the derivative with respect to the entry $X^{AA'}$ of the matrix $X$ of $x$.

## Remarks

A class in $H^1$ is represented by a function $f(\omega, \pi)$, holomorphic on the intersection of two open sets and homogeneous of degree $-2h-2$.
The field is the contour integral over a loop in the line $L_x$:
$$\phi_{A'_1 \dots A'_{2h}}(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint \pi_{A'_1} \cdots \pi_{A'_{2h}} \, f(iX\pi, \pi) \, \pi_{B'} \, d\pi^{B'}.$$
The integrand is homogeneous of degree $0$ in $\pi$, so the integral is well defined on the projective line.
Differentiating under the integral sign produces factors $\pi_{A'}$, and contracting two of them gives $\pi^{A'}\pi_{A'} = 0$; this is why the field equations hold.
The hard part of the proof is that the map is bijective, which uses the vanishing of suitable cohomology on the fibres of the correspondence.
Example: for $h = 1$ the fields are the solutions of the source-free Maxwell equations of one duality type, self-dual or anti-self-dual according to the convention.
The theorem holds for more general pairs of open sets, with conditions on the topology of the fibres; the forward tube is the classical case.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Penrose transform turns a holomorphic object on twistor space into a solution of a wave-type equation on spacetime, and every solution arises in this way.
The equations of massless fields, such as light, thus disappear: they are built into the geometry of twistor space.

### L2 human

The transform is the main example of the dictionary of twistor theory, in which differential equations on spacetime become cohomology on twistor space.
The degree $-2h-2$ of homogeneity encodes the helicity, and fields of the other sign of helicity correspond to the cohomology of sheaves $\mathcal{O}(k)$ with other values of $k$.
Related transforms describe self-dual Yang–Mills fields through holomorphic vector bundles on twistor space, by the Ward correspondence.
