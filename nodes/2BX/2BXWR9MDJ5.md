## Summary

The unit hydrograph of a drainage basin is the curve of direct runoff at the basin outlet caused by one unit of excess rainfall.
This rainfall falls uniformly over the basin during a fixed duration.
It treats the basin as a linear and time-invariant system.
So the runoff from any storm is a [[convolution|2Z29JN3VES]] of the rainfall with the unit hydrograph.
It is a basic tool of engineering [[hydrology|2XGF4FECN5]] for predicting floods from rainfall records.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a river basin as a machine that turns rain into river flow at its outlet.
If one centimetre of extra rain falls in one hour, the flow at the outlet rises, peaks and then falls back; that curve is the unit hydrograph.
The method assumes that twice the rain gives twice the flow, and that a later storm gives the same curve shifted in time.
For example, if two centimetres fall in the first hour and one in the second, the flow is twice the unit curve plus the unit curve delayed by one hour.
