## Summary

The Malthusian growth model is the [[population model|1YEMFQ1H32]] in which the growth rate of a population is proportional to its size.
Its solutions are exponential functions, so the population grows or decays geometrically.
It is named after Thomas Malthus, and it is the starting point of most models of population growth.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If every individual has, on average, the same number of offspring per unit of time, the number of births is proportional to the population.
So a population twice as large grows twice as fast.
This gives [[exponential growth|00SVP6W3CB]]: the population doubles in equal time steps.
For example, a bacterial culture that doubles every hour grows from $1000$ cells to about $10^6$ cells in $10$ hours, since $2^{10} = 1024$.
No real population can grow like this forever, so the model only fits short times or small populations.
