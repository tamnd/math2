## Summary

The rapidity of a velocity $v$ along a line is $\varphi = \operatorname{artanh}(v / c)$.
It is the hyperbolic angle of a [[Lorentz transformation|WEVGN4C7PQ]] boost.
In terms of rapidity, a boost looks like a rotation with [[hyperbolic functions|01PPDSDP2N]] in place of trigonometric ones.
Rapidities of boosts in the same direction simply add, while velocities do not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Velocities in relativity do not add: two boosts of $0.6c$ give $0.88c$, not $1.2c$.
Rapidity is a re-scaling of speed that makes the addition simple again.
At low speed it is almost $v / c$, but it grows without bound as $v$ approaches $c$.
For $v = 0.6c$ the rapidity is $\ln 2$; two such boosts give rapidity $2 \ln 2 = \ln 4$, and $\tanh(\ln 4) = 15/17 \approx 0.88$.
