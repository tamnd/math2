## Definition

Let a homogeneous isotropic linear elastic plate occupy $\set{x \in \R^3 : \abs{x_3} < h}$, with [[P-wave|EEPJJHC5JZ]] speed $c_P$ and [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]] speed $c_S$, and with both faces $x_3 = \pm h$ free of traction.
A *Lamb wave* is a nonzero [[elastodynamic|T1PYEBKBAJ]] solution without body force of the form
$$u(x, t) = \operatorname{Re} \left[ \left( U_1(x_3), \, 0, \, U_3(x_3) \right) e^{i (k x_1 - \omega t)} \right],$$
with wave number $k$ and angular frequency $\omega$.
Write $p^2 = \omega^2 / c_P^2 - k^2$ and $q^2 = \omega^2 / c_S^2 - k^2$.
Such solutions exist exactly when $(k, \omega)$ satisfies one of the Rayleigh–Lamb equations:

1. symmetric modes, with $U_1$ even and $U_3$ odd in $x_3$:
$$\frac{\tan(q h)}{\tan(p h)} = -\frac{4 k^2 p q}{(q^2 - k^2)^2};$$
2. antisymmetric modes, with $U_1$ odd and $U_3$ even in $x_3$:
$$\frac{\tan(q h)}{\tan(p h)} = -\frac{(q^2 - k^2)^2}{4 k^2 p q}.$$

## Remarks

Each equation has infinitely many branches $\omega(k)$, labelled $S_0, S_1, \dots$ and $A_0, A_1, \dots$.
Example: the lowest symmetric mode $S_0$ at long wavelength, $k h \to 0$, travels at the plate speed $\sqrt{E / (\rho (1 - \nu^2))}$, where $E$ is Young's modulus, $\nu$ Poisson's ratio and $\rho$ the density.
At short wavelength, $k h \to \infty$, the speeds of both $S_0$ and $A_0$ tend to the [[Rayleigh wave|X32RRM7ATM]] speed, because each face then behaves like the surface of a half-space.

Non-example: a horizontally polarized shear wave in the plate, with displacement along $x_2$, is a guided wave too.
Its displacement has no component in the $x_1 x_3$ plane, so it is not of the form above; it is called an SH plate mode.
