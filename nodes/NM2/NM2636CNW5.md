## Proof

We factor $A$ out of $A + u v^{\mathsf{T}}$ and then use the Weinstein-Aronszajn identity to turn the remaining $n \times n$ determinant into a $1 \times 1$ determinant.

1. Let $A$ be an invertible $n \times n$ matrix over the commutative ring $K$, and let $u, v \in K^n$ be column vectors.
   Put $w = A^{-1} u$, a column vector in $K^n$.
2. By [[distributivity|0RXD6EZYBK]] and [[associativity|073E4XC016]] of the matrix product,
$$A (I_n + w v^{\mathsf{T}}) = A + (A A^{-1}) u v^{\mathsf{T}}.$$
3. By the definition of the [[inverse|0TWTS88TWN]], $A A^{-1} = I_n$, the [[identity matrix|06DW9BAWB1]].
   So $A (I_n + w v^{\mathsf{T}}) = A + u v^{\mathsf{T}}$.
4. By [[multiplicativity of the determinant|04CPJ6AF00]], step 3 gives
$$\det(A + u v^{\mathsf{T}}) = \det A \cdot \det(I_n + w v^{\mathsf{T}}).$$
5. The column $w$ is an $n \times 1$ matrix and the row $v^{\mathsf{T}}$ is a $1 \times n$ matrix.
   The [[Weinstein-Aronszajn identity|0G24VQ1088]] for the sizes $n$ and $1$, applied to the column $w$ and the row $v^{\mathsf{T}}$, gives $\det(I_n + w v^{\mathsf{T}}) = \det(I_1 + v^{\mathsf{T}} w)$.
6. The matrix $I_1 + v^{\mathsf{T}} w$ is the $1 \times 1$ matrix with the single entry $1 + v^{\mathsf{T}} A^{-1} u$.
7. The [[Leibniz formula|016PE4VZ8Q]] for $n = 1$ has one term, so the determinant of a $1 \times 1$ matrix is its entry.
   Hence $\det(I_1 + v^{\mathsf{T}} w) = 1 + v^{\mathsf{T}} A^{-1} u$.
8. Steps 4, 5 and 7 together give
$$\det(A + u v^{\mathsf{T}}) = (1 + v^{\mathsf{T}} A^{-1} u) \det A.$$

## Idea

Write $A + u v^{\mathsf{T}} = A (I + A^{-1} u v^{\mathsf{T}})$, so that the determinant splits as $\det A$ times the determinant of the identity plus a column times a row.
The Weinstein-Aronszajn identity lets us swap the column and the row.
After the swap, the product is a single number, and the determinant of the identity plus that number is the number plus $1$.

## Variants

The same steps with $n \times m$ matrices $U$ and $V$ in place of $u$ and $v$ prove the general form $\det(A + U V^{\mathsf{T}}) = \det(I_m + V^{\mathsf{T}} A^{-1} U) \det A$.
The only change is in step 5, where the identity now produces an $m \times m$ determinant.

A second proof avoids the identity and uses one block matrix.
The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} A & -u \\ v^{\mathsf{T}} & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has two [[Schur complements|0KA7A4ENVJ]].
The complement of the corner $A$ is $1 + v^{\mathsf{T}} A^{-1} u$, and the complement of the corner $1$ is $A + u v^{\mathsf{T}}$.
Equating the two values of the block determinant gives $\det A \cdot (1 + v^{\mathsf{T}} A^{-1} u) = 1 \cdot \det(A + u v^{\mathsf{T}})$.
