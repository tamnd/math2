## Summary

The Parker instability is an instability of a stratified gas layer that is supported against gravity partly by a horizontal [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]].
If the field lines are bent into waves, the gas slides down along them into the valleys.
The crests become lighter, and magnetic buoyancy lifts them further.
It is a form of magnetic buoyancy instability in [[magnetohydrodynamics|T8JWCBK9M6]].
It is thought to gather interstellar gas of the galactic disk into clouds and to help magnetic flux rise through the Sun.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A magnetic field in a gas acts like a set of stretched elastic strings that also add pressure.
In a galaxy, gravity pulls the gas toward the midplane, while gas pressure and magnetic pressure hold it up.
If a field line bulges upward a little, the gas on it slides downhill toward the low points.
The emptied high part is now lighter than its surroundings and floats up even more, so the bulge grows.
Gas piles up in the valleys, which can seed dense clouds.
