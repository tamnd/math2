## Summary

Boyer–Lindquist coordinates are the standard coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$ for the Kerr and Kerr–Newman metrics.
They generalize Schwarzschild coordinates to rotating black holes.
In them the metric has only one off-diagonal term, $dt\, d\phi$, and its coefficients depend only on $r$ and $\theta$.
They are singular on the horizons, as Schwarzschild coordinates are.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To describe a rotating black hole one needs a time, a radius and two angles, chosen to fit the rotation.
Boyer–Lindquist coordinates do this: surfaces of constant $r$ are slightly flattened spheres, like the shape of a spinning planet.
When the black hole has no mass, these surfaces are confocal ellipsoids in flat space.
The only mixing between time and space is through the rotation angle, which reflects the swirling of spacetime around the axis.
They make the symmetries of the [[Kerr metric|X3FZC85THY]] visible, but they break down at the horizon.
