## Summary

A magnetic scalar potential is a scalar function whose negative gradient gives the magnetic field in a region without electric currents.
It plays the same role for magnetostatics in current-free regions as the [[electric potential|SAEDXNM3Y1]] plays in electrostatics.
It turns the field equations into a single scalar equation, often the Laplace equation, which is easier to solve than a vector equation.
It exists only where no current flows, so that the field has zero [[curl|0NF6GQNXMY]], and globally only on suitable regions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Where no current flows, the magnetic field has no swirl, so it behaves like a slope that points downhill.
The scalar potential is the height of that landscape, and the field points from high to low.
Between the poles of a permanent magnet one can use it to find the field by solving one equation instead of three.
Around a wire that carries a current, the field circles the wire, and going once around the wire the potential does not come back to its starting value.
So the potential there is many-valued, and one must cut the space to use it.
