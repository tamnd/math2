## Summary

Seismic surface waves, the [[Rayleigh waves|X32RRM7ATM]] and [[Love waves|A2E51V42RH]], travel at speeds that depend on their frequency.
Long waves reach deeper into the Earth, where the rock is usually faster, so they travel faster than short waves.
The [[phase velocity|FMDA4DKT0M]] and [[group velocity|C05DPKD4S0]] as functions of period are dispersion curves.
They can be inverted for the speed of shear waves at each depth.
Dispersion is why the surface waves of a distant earthquake arrive as a long wave train instead of a single pulse.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A surface wave shakes the ground down to a depth of about one wavelength.
A wave with a long period has a long wavelength and so feels deep, fast rock; a short-period wave feels only the slow rock near the surface.
So the long waves of an earthquake arrive first and the short ones follow, spreading the signal out.
Measuring how speed changes with period tells how rock speed changes with depth, a bit like an X-ray scan done in layers.
