## Summary

A structurable algebra is a unital [[nonassociative|YP5TQ6WFVN]] algebra with an involution whose triple product satisfies an identity of Jordan type.
The class contains the unital [[Jordan algebras|6C2MCVB8C7]], with the identity as involution, and the associative algebras with involution.
Structurable algebras were introduced by Bruce Allison, who used them to construct Lie algebras with a grading in degrees $-2$ to $2$.
This extends the [[Tits–Kantor–Koecher construction|FZM1A8HAHD]], which uses degrees $-1$ to $1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Jordan algebras give Lie algebras through the Tits–Kantor–Koecher construction, but they miss some Lie algebras.
Allowing an involution, a "conjugation" on the algebra, gives a wider class of algebras.
The octonions with their conjugation are a typical example that is not a Jordan algebra.
The defining rule says that certain operators built from the triple product behave like a Lie algebra of derivations.
