## Definition

Let a homogeneous isotropic body have [[thermal conductivity|XBQBT9E7JT]] $k > 0$, density $\rho > 0$ and specific heat $c > 0$.
Let $\tau > 0$ be a constant, the relaxation time.
The *Cattaneo–Vernotte equation* is the law for the [[heat flux|1Y36434YTE]] $\mathbf{q}$
$$\tau \, \partial_t \mathbf{q} + \mathbf{q} = -k \, \nabla T.$$
Together with the energy balance $\rho \, c \, \partial_t T + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{q} = 0$, it gives, for twice differentiable fields,
$$\tau \, \partial_t^2 T + \partial_t T = \alpha \, \Delta T, \qquad \alpha = \frac{k}{\rho \, c}.$$

## Remarks

Here $\alpha$ is the [[thermal diffusivity|CS4ZZX092Z]].
To derive the second equation, differentiate the energy balance in $t$ and use $\tau \, \partial_t \mathbf{q} = -k \nabla T - \mathbf{q}$.
The temperature equation is [[hyperbolic|92C3S03AQZ]], and its fronts move with speed $\sqrt{\alpha / \tau}$.
As $\tau \to 0$ one recovers [[Fourier's law|9YJZ1PP2Y8]] and the heat equation.

Example: in one dimension, try $T = e^{\lambda t} \cos(\kappa x)$ with $\kappa > 0$.
It is a solution exactly when $\tau \lambda^2 + \lambda + \alpha \kappa^2 = 0$.
If $4 \tau \alpha \kappa^2 > 1$, the roots $\lambda$ are complex, so the profile oscillates in time while it decays.

Non-example: the Fourier law $\mathbf{q} = -k \nabla T$ is the case $\tau = 0$, which is excluded.
For it, the same trial function gives only $\lambda = -\alpha \kappa^2$, a pure decay without oscillation.
