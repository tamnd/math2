## Summary

Doodson numbers are a compact code for the [[tidal harmonic constituents|Y07010W5HJ]].
A constituent's frequency is an integer combination of the rates of six astronomical angles.
The Doodson number lists the six integers, shifted to avoid negative digits.
They were introduced by Arthur Doodson in his expansion of the tide-generating potential, and they remain a standard way to label tidal frequencies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every tidal rhythm is built from six basic astronomical clocks.
They are the Earth's turning relative to the Moon, the motion of the Moon, the motion of the Sun, and three slow motions of the orbits.
To describe a rhythm one only needs to say how many times each clock is counted, a list of six whole numbers.
Doodson wrote these numbers as a six-digit code, adding $5$ to all but the first so that no digit is negative.
For example, the main lunar tide is $255.555$.
