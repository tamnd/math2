## Definition

Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a vector space over $F$ with a [[basis|0KCQ9Y3QKA]] $(e_1, \dots, e_n)$.
Let $B$ be a [[bilinear form|0SQZMXB6GP]] on $V$.
The *matrix* of $B$ in this basis is the $n \times n$ matrix $A$ with entries
$$A_{ij} = B(e_i, e_j) \qquad (1 \le i, j \le n).$$

## Notation

We write $[B]_{\mathcal{E}}$ for the matrix of $B$ in the basis $\mathcal{E} = (e_1, \dots, e_n)$, or $A$ when the basis is fixed.
The row index belongs to the first slot and the column index to the second slot.

## Remarks

If $x$ and $y$ are the coordinate vectors of $u$ and $v$, then $B(u, v) = x^T A y$.
The map $B \mapsto A$ is a linear bijection from the bilinear forms on $V$ to the $n \times n$ matrices over $F$, by the [[correspondence theorem|ZVMJAF7D53]].
For the dot product on $\R^n$ and the standard basis, $A$ is the identity matrix.
For an inner product, $A$ is the [[Gram matrix|00SPJSDT3Z]] of the basis.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bilinear form is fixed by its values on pairs of basis vectors.
We store these $n^2$ values in a matrix.
Then the form becomes a product of a row vector, the matrix and a column vector.

### L2 human

For $B(x, y) = x_1 y_2 - 3x_2 y_2$ on $F^2$, the values are $B(e_1, e_1) = 0$, $B(e_1, e_2) = 1$, $B(e_2, e_1) = 0$ and $B(e_2, e_2) = -3$.
So the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$.
A trap: the coefficient of $x_i y_j$ goes to row $i$ and column $j$.
