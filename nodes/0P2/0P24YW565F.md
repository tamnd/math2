## Definition

The matrix of a bilinear form records the values of the form on all ordered pairs of basis vectors.

Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a vector space over $F$ with a finite [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] $\mathcal{E} = (e_1, \dots, e_n)$.
Let $B$ be a [[bilinear form|0P799D6AMN]] on $V$.
The *matrix* of $B$ in the basis $\mathcal{E}$ is the $n \times n$ matrix $A$ over $F$ with entries
$$A_{ij} = B(e_i, e_j) \qquad (1 \le i, j \le n).$$

## Notation

We write $[B]_{\mathcal{E}}$ for the matrix of $B$ in the basis $\mathcal{E}$, or $A$ when the basis is fixed.
The row index belongs to the first slot and the column index to the second slot.

## Remarks

The basis must be finite, so that the table has finitely many rows and columns, and it must be ordered, because a new order of the same vectors permutes the rows and the columns of $A$.
The order of the slots matters too: the entry in row $i$ and column $j$ is $B(e_i, e_j)$, not $B(e_j, e_i)$.

If $x$ and $y$ are the coordinate vectors of $u$ and $v$, then $B(u, v) = x^{\mathsf{T}} A y$.
The map $B \mapsto A$ is a linear bijection from the bilinear forms on $V$ onto the $n \times n$ matrices over $F$, by the [[correspondence theorem|ZVMJAF7D53]].
For example, the form $B(x, y) = x_1 y_2 - 3 x_2 y_2$ on $F^2$ has the values $B(e_1, e_1) = 0$, $B(e_1, e_2) = 1$, $B(e_2, e_1) = 0$ and $B(e_2, e_2) = -3$, so its matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$.

The definition needs a bilinear map.
A map such as $x_1 y_1 + x_2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ has values on basis pairs, but those values do not determine the map, so no matrix represents it.
For an inner product, $A$ is the [[Gram matrix|00SPJSDT3Z]] of the basis.
The concept page [[matrix of a bilinear form|0QK6K857HS]] gives more examples and the properties that the matrix detects.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bilinear form is fixed by its values on pairs of basis vectors.
We store these $n^2$ values in a square table.
Then the form becomes a row vector times the table times a column vector.
For the dot product on the plane the table is the identity, with $1$ on the diagonal and $0$ elsewhere.

### L2 human

To find the matrix, evaluate the form on each ordered pair of basis vectors and write $B(e_i, e_j)$ in row $i$ and column $j$.
For a form given as a polynomial, this is the coefficient of $x_i y_j$.
For $B(x, y) = 5 x_2 y_1 + x_2 y_2$ on $\mathbb{Q}^2$, the coefficient of $x_2 y_1$ goes to row $2$ and column $1$, so the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 5 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
Check: $x^{\mathsf{T}} A y$ with $x = e_2$ and $y = e_1$ is the entry in row $2$ and column $1$, which is $5 = B(e_2, e_1)$.
Watch out: putting that coefficient in row $1$ and column $2$ gives the transpose, which is the matrix of a different form.
