## Definition

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]], and let $\hbar > 0$.
Let $t \mapsto V(t)$, $t \in \R$, be a strongly continuous family of bounded [[self-adjoint operators|0NEGN5CRSC]] on $\mathcal{H}$.
It is the perturbation written in the [[interaction picture|3R1P5CQ1Y7]].
For $t \ge t_0$ the *Dyson series* is
$$U(t, t_0) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( -\frac{i}{\hbar} \right)^n \int_{t_0}^{t} dt_1 \int_{t_0}^{t_1} dt_2 \cdots \int_{t_0}^{t_{n-1}} dt_n \; V(t_1) V(t_2) \cdots V(t_n).$$
The term $n = 0$ is the identity $I$, and the integrals are taken in the strong sense.

## Remarks

If $\norm{V(s)} \le K$ for $s \in [t_0, t]$, the $n$-th term has norm at most $\frac{1}{n!}\left( \frac{K (t - t_0)}{\hbar} \right)^n$, so the series converges in operator norm.
Its sum is the [[unitary|MM0EJ2GDE4]] solution of $i\hbar \, \partial_t U(t, t_0) = V(t) U(t, t_0)$ with $U(t_0, t_0) = I$.
Writing the $n$-fold integral over the cube $[t_0, t]^n$ with the factors ordered by decreasing time gives the notation $U(t, t_0) = \mathcal{T} \exp\left( -\frac{i}{\hbar} \int_{t_0}^{t} V(s)\, ds \right)$, the time-ordered exponential.

Example: if $V(t) = V$ does not depend on $t$, the $n$-fold integral equals $\frac{(t - t_0)^n}{n!} V^n$, and the series is $e^{-iV(t - t_0)/\hbar}$.

Non-example of the hypothesis: let $V(t)$ be unbounded, as for $V = x^4$ on $L^2(\R)$.
Then the terms need not be defined on a common domain, and the series need not converge.
It is then only a formal expansion.
