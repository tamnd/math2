## Definition

Let a body of matter have absolute [[temperature|AJ8M9MGZGF]] $T > 0$.
*Thermal radiation* is the [[electromagnetic radiation|AC6R5A3M1C]] that the body emits as a result of the thermal motion of its charged particles.
It is produced at the expense of the [[internal energy|F6JVQ12X43]] of the body.
Its spectrum and power depend on the temperature and on the material and surface of the body.

## Remarks

Example: the Sun, whose surface is at about $5800$ K, emits thermal radiation with most of its power in the visible and near infrared.
A body at room temperature emits thermal radiation mainly at wavelengths around $10$ micrometres.

Non-example: the radio waves sent by an antenna.
They are produced by an electric current driven by a transmitter, not by thermal motion, so they are not thermal radiation, even though they are electromagnetic.
The light of a laser is likewise not thermal.

In heat transfer, the range of wavelengths that matters is roughly from $0.1$ to $100$ micrometres.
