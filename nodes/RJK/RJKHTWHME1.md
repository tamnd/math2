## Statement

Let $A$ be a symmetrizable [[generalized Cartan matrix|38Z0HSXTCA]] and let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}(A)$ be its [[Kac–Moody algebra|HD13X0CNBD]].
Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be its Cartan subalgebra, with simple roots $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ and simple coroots $\alpha_1^\vee, \ldots, \alpha_n^\vee \in \mathfrak{h}$.
Let $\Delta_+$ be the set of positive roots, and for $\alpha \in \Delta_+$ let $\operatorname{mult} \alpha$ be the dimension of the root space $\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$.
Let $W$ be the [[Weyl group|YT1ZJ2S4EE]], generated by the reflections $s_i(\lambda) = \lambda - \lambda(\alpha_i^\vee)\, \alpha_i$.
For $w \in W$ let $\varepsilon(w) = (-1)^{\ell(w)}$, where $\ell(w)$ is the length of $w$ in these generators.
Fix $\rho \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ with $\rho(\alpha_i^\vee) = 1$ for all $i$.
Let $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ be dominant integral, that is, $\lambda(\alpha_i^\vee) \in \Z_{\ge 0}$ for all $i$.
Let $L(\lambda)$ be the irreducible [[highest weight|C5W8BE1DRY]] module of $\mathfrak{g}$ with highest weight $\lambda$.
Write $\operatorname{ch} L(\lambda) = \sum_{\mu \in \mathfrak{h}^*} \dim L(\lambda)_\mu\, e^\mu$ for its formal character.
Then
$$\operatorname{ch} L(\lambda) = \frac{\sum_{w \in W} \varepsilon(w)\, e^{w(\lambda + \rho) - \rho}}{\prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+} \left(1 - e^{-\alpha}\right)^{\operatorname{mult} \alpha}}.$$

## Remarks

The proof follows the algebraic proof of the finite-dimensional [[Weyl character formula|G2SA2YNA33]].
The product $\operatorname{ch} L(\lambda) \cdot \prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+} (1 - e^{-\alpha})^{\operatorname{mult} \alpha}$ is anti-invariant under $W$, after the shift by $\rho$.
A generalized Casimir operator, built from an invariant bilinear form that exists because $A$ is symmetrizable, shows that only the weights $w(\lambda + \rho) - \rho$ can occur in it.
Anti-invariance then fixes the coefficient of each such weight as $\varepsilon(w)$.

For $\lambda = 0$ the module $L(0)$ is trivial, and the formula becomes the denominator identity
$$\prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+} \left(1 - e^{-\alpha}\right)^{\operatorname{mult} \alpha} = \sum_{w \in W} \varepsilon(w)\, e^{w\rho - \rho}.$$
For [[affine Lie algebras|9A6RGRV056]] these denominator identities are the Macdonald identities, and for the affine algebra of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ the identity is equivalent to the [[Jacobi triple product|JFP2DGMNXT]].

The hypothesis that $\lambda$ is dominant integral is necessary.
For example, let $\mathfrak{g}$ be finite-dimensional and $\lambda = -\rho$, which is not dominant integral.
Then the numerator is $\sum_{w \in W} \varepsilon(w)\, e^{-\rho} = 0$, because $W$ has as many elements of sign $1$ as of sign $-1$, while $L(-\rho) \neq 0$ has a nonzero character.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The character of a representation lists how many independent vectors it has of each weight.
For finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebras, Weyl's formula gives this list as a quotient of two alternating sums.
Kac extended the formula to Kac–Moody algebras, which are usually infinite-dimensional.
The new feature is that the denominator becomes an infinite product, with each positive root counted with its multiplicity.

### L2 human

When $\mathfrak{g}$ is finite-dimensional, all multiplicities are $1$, the Weyl group is finite, and the formula is Weyl's character formula.
For affine Lie algebras, specializing the formal exponentials to powers of a variable $q$ turns both sides into $q$-series, and the formula produces identities between infinite products and theta-type sums.
This is the source of the link between affine Lie algebras and modular forms.
The theorem was later proved for all generalized Cartan matrices, without the symmetrizability hypothesis, by Kumar and by Mathieu.
