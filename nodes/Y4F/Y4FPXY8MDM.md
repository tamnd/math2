## Summary

The Aharonov–Bohm effect is a quantum effect of electromagnetic potentials on charged particles.
It occurs in regions where the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] is zero.
The particle picks up a phase equal to its charge times the flux enclosed by its path, divided by $\hbar$.
This phase shifts an interference pattern.
It shows that in quantum mechanics the [[magnetic vector potential|8PEMR2J7EF]] carries physical information that the field alone does not.
In geometric terms the phase is the [[holonomy|WHDZ3MK56B]] of a flat connection on a region that is not simply connected.
It was predicted by Aharonov and Bohm in 1959, and earlier by Ehrenberg and Siday in 1949.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Send electrons around both sides of a long thin coil that carries a current.
Outside the coil there is no magnetic field at all, so classically the electrons feel nothing.
Yet the interference pattern on a screen behind the coil moves when the current in the coil changes.
The electrons respond to the magnetic flux that their two paths enclose, even though they never touch the field.
