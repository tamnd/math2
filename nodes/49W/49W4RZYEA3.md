## Summary

The Gamow factor is the probability that two positively charged nuclei tunnel through their mutual electric repulsion.
Only then do they come close enough to fuse.
It is approximately $\exp(-\sqrt{E_G / E})$, where $E$ is the energy of the collision.
The Gamow energy $E_G$ depends on the charges and the masses of the nuclei.
It makes nuclear fusion possible in stars at temperatures far below those that classical physics would require.
Combined with the [[Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution|3QSVWQJX11]] of energies, it shows that most reactions happen in a narrow window of energies, the Gamow peak.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two protons repel each other, and in the core of the Sun they almost never have enough energy to touch in the classical picture.
Quantum mechanics lets them sometimes pass through the barrier anyway.
The Gamow factor is the chance of this, and it grows very fast with the collision energy.
Fast particles are rare, but slow ones almost never tunnel, so fusion happens mainly at a medium energy where both effects balance.
