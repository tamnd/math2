## Summary

Weyl–Lewis–Papapetrou coordinates are coordinates adapted to a stationary and axisymmetric spacetime.
In them the metric depends on only two coordinates $\rho$ and $z$ and is described by three functions.
For vacuum, the Einstein equations reduce to equations on flat three-dimensional space, which is the starting point of the [[Ernst equation|G0YMK0R648]].
The [[Kerr metric|X3FZC85THY]] and all static axisymmetric vacuum solutions can be written in this form.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rotating star or black hole that does not change in time has two symmetries: time translation and rotation about its axis.
These coordinates use the two symmetries as two of the four coordinates, so only the distance $\rho$ from the axis and the height $z$ remain.
All the information about the spacetime is then stored in a few functions of $\rho$ and $z$.
For vacuum, the coordinate $\rho$ comes for free from the geometry: it is fixed by how large the circles around the axis are, combined with the time direction.
This turns Einstein's equations into problems that look like classical potential theory in flat space.
