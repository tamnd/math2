## Summary

Aerodynamics is the part of [[fluid dynamics|F2JRWJNYN4]] that studies the flow of air, and of gases in general, around moving bodies.
Its central questions are the forces that the flow exerts on a body, mainly lift and drag.
It asks how they depend on the shape and speed of the body.
It ranges from nearly incompressible flow at low speed to [[compressible flow|FJC2GMVARF]] with shock waves at high speed.
Its models, such as potential flow with circulation and boundary layer theory, are classical sources of problems in analysis and numerical mathematics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Aerodynamics explains why an aircraft can fly, why a car uses fuel to push air aside and why a spinning ball curves.
Air is light, but at high speed it pushes hard on anything in its way.
A wing is shaped and tilted so that it pushes the passing air downwards, and the air pushes the wing up in return.
For example, the lift on a wing grows with the square of the speed.
So a plane that flies twice as fast gets about four times the lift at the same angle.
