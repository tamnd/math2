## Definition

A *Milnor square* is a commutative square of [[rings|0B1261B5ED]] with identity and [[ring homomorphisms|0VTH8KPCG2]]
$$\begin{array}{ccc} R & \xrightarrow{\ f_1\ } & R_1 \\ {\scriptstyle f_2}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle g_1} \\ R_2 & \xrightarrow{\ g_2\ } & R' \end{array}$$
such that:

1. $R$ is the [[pullback|Q05K04QJ71]] $R_1 \times_{R'} R_2 = \set{(a, b) \in R_1 \times R_2 : g_1(a) = g_2(b)}$, with $f_1$ and $f_2$ the two projections;
2. at least one of $g_1$ and $g_2$ is surjective.

## Remarks

Example: let $R \subseteq S$ be rings and let $I$ be an ideal of $S$ contained in $R$, hence also an ideal of $R$.
Then the square with $R \to S$, $R \to R/I$, $S \to S/I$ and $R/I \to S/I$ is a Milnor square: $S \to S/I$ is surjective, and $(s, r + I)$ with $s + I = r + I$ forces $s \in R$, so $R = S \times_{S/I} R/I$.
Example: for a field $k$, the ring $k[x, y]/(xy)$ is the pullback of $k[x] \to k \leftarrow k[y]$, both maps sending the variable to $0$.
Non-example: the square with $R = R_1 = R_2 = \Z$ and $R' = \Z/2\Z$, all maps natural, is commutative with surjective $g_i$, but it fails clause 1, since the pullback $\set{(a, b) \in \Z^2 : a \equiv b \pmod 2}$ is not $\Z$.
