## Example

Draw a $2 \times 2$ table and mark the two cells on the diagonal.
Every column contains a mark, but no row is marked in every column.
In the product of two $\mathsf{S5}$ frames, this table refutes $\square_2 \lozenge_1 p \to \lozenge_1 \square_2 p$, and the failure is the failure of $\forall y \, \exists x$ to imply $\exists x \, \forall y$.

Let $U_1, U_2$ be nonempty sets, and give each the universal relation, so each is a frame of [[S5|VC3A2VWXND]].
In the [[product frame|JA2EBWVW8A]] $U_1 \times U_2$, $(u, v) R_h (u', v')$ holds exactly when $v = v'$, and $(u, v) R_v (u', v')$ exactly when $u = u'$.
A valuation gives each variable $p$ a binary relation $P \subseteq U_1 \times U_2$.

1. $(u, v) \models \lozenge_1 \varphi$ holds exactly when $(u', v) \models \varphi$ for some $u' \in U_1$.
   So $\lozenge_1$ acts as $\exists x$, and $\square_1$ as $\forall x$.
2. In the same way, $\lozenge_2$ acts as $\exists y$, and $\square_2$ as $\forall y$.
3. So $\lozenge_1 \square_2 p$ reads $\exists x \, \forall y \, P(x, y)$, and $\square_2 \lozenge_1 p$ reads $\forall y \, \exists x \, P(x, y)$.
4. The valid formula $\lozenge_1 \square_2 p \to \square_2 \lozenge_1 p$ is the first-order law $\exists x \, \forall y \, P \to \forall y \, \exists x \, P$.
5. Now let $U_1 = U_2 = \{0, 1\}$, and let $P = \{(0, 0), (1, 1)\}$ be the diagonal.
6. For every $y$, $P(y, y)$ holds, so $\square_2 \lozenge_1 p$ is true at every point.
7. No $x$ has both $P(x, 0)$ and $P(x, 1)$, so $\lozenge_1 \square_2 p$ is false at every point.
8. So the converse $\square_2 \lozenge_1 p \to \lozenge_1 \square_2 p$ fails at every point.

## Remarks

The example shows the main idea of $\mathsf{S5} \times \mathsf{S5}$: it is a modal notation for a fragment of first-order logic with two variables $x$ and $y$.
The axiom $\square_1 \square_2 p \leftrightarrow \square_2 \square_1 p$ is the law $\forall x \, \forall y \leftrightarrow \forall y \, \forall x$.
The algebras of $\mathsf{S5} \times \mathsf{S5}$ are two-dimensional diagonal-free [[cylindric algebras|1MDT9BF81H]], with $\lozenge_1$ and $\lozenge_2$ as the cylindrifications.
The logic $\mathsf{S5} \times \mathsf{S5}$ is decidable, while $\mathsf{S5}^3$ is undecidable by the theorem of Hirsch, Hodkinson and Kurucz.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Fill a table with marks.
"Some row is marked in every column" is stronger than "every column has a mark in some row".
In the product of two $\mathsf{S5}$ frames, the two boxes say "in every row" and "in every column".
The modal formulas express exactly this difference.

### L2 human

Make the table larger: $U_1 = U_2 = \{0, 1, 2\}$, and let $P$ be the graph of the map $y \mapsto y + 1 \bmod 3$, read as $P(x, y)$ when $x = y + 1 \bmod 3$.
Every column $y$ has exactly one mark, in row $y + 1 \bmod 3$, so $\square_2 \lozenge_1 p$ is true everywhere.
Each row is marked in exactly one column, so $\lozenge_1 \square_2 p$ is false everywhere.
Any permutation table behaves the same way, as soon as the table has at least two rows.
The typical use is to read $\mathsf{S5}$ products as quantifier logics and to transfer results from first-order logic.
Watch out: the variables of the modal language stand for binary relations with a fixed order of arguments, so this is only a fragment of two-variable logic.
