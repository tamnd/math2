## Summary

A quasinormal mode is a damped oscillation of an open system, such as a perturbed [[black hole|YHJ0PDNY8T]], that loses energy by radiating it away.
It has a complex frequency: the real part gives the oscillation and the imaginary part the decay rate.
After two black holes merge, the remnant rings down in a sum of quasinormal modes, which carry [[gravitational waves|SJ0A4TZTFR]] to distant detectors.
For a Kerr black hole the frequencies depend only on the mass and the spin.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Strike a bell and it rings at certain pitches while the sound slowly dies away.
A black hole that has been disturbed does the same, but it rings in gravitational waves.
Because energy leaks out, to infinity and into the horizon, every tone fades quickly.
The pitch and the fading time of each tone are fixed by the mass and spin of the black hole, so the ringing is like a fingerprint.
