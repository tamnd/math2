## Summary

K-homology is the homology theory dual to topological K-theory.
In its analytic form it assigns to a separable C*-algebra $A$ the groups $K^0(A)$ and $K^1(A)$ of classes of [[Fredholm modules|Y30V3RBNGV]] over $A$.
For a compact metric space $X$, it gives the groups $K_i(X) = K^i(C(X))$.
Elliptic operators on a manifold give K-homology classes, and pairing them with K-theory classes gives Fredholm indices.
It is the special case $KK(A, \C)$ of [[KK-theory|1W9Q7W378J]], and the left-hand side of the Baum–Connes conjecture is an equivariant K-homology group.

## Explanations

### L1 human

K-theory of a space is built from vector bundles, and K-homology is its dual, built from abstract elliptic operators.
An elliptic operator on a closed manifold, such as the Dirac operator, has an index that is stable under deformation.
Twisting the operator by a vector bundle gives an index for each bundle, and this defines a pairing between K-homology and K-theory.
For the circle, the Hardy space projection gives a Fredholm module whose pairing with the unitary $z$ is the index of the Toeplitz operator with symbol $z$.
