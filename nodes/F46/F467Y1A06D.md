## Proof

We prove the two inclusions, using that the entries of $A^{\mathsf{T}} y$ are the dot products of $y$ with the columns of $A$.

1. Let $a_1, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb{R}^m$ be the columns of $A$; by the [[definition of the column space|0F0RPPJ2TT]], $\operatorname{Col}(A)$ is their span.
2. By the fact that [[transpose times a vector gives dot products with the columns|GNPP8QK9EM]], entry $j$ of $A^{\mathsf{T}} y$ is the [[dot product|05HQYM0R4G]] $a_j \cdot y$.
3. Let $y \in \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$; since each $a_j$ lies in $\operatorname{Col}(A)$, the [[definition of the orthogonal complement|079D68S3AW]] gives $a_j \cdot y = 0$ for each $j$.
4. By step 2, every entry of $A^{\mathsf{T}} y$ is $0$, so $y$ lies in the [[null space|0PQ2GGY1QV]] of $A^{\mathsf{T}}$, and $\operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp \subseteq \operatorname{Null}(A^{\mathsf{T}})$.
5. Conversely, let $A^{\mathsf{T}} y = 0$; by step 2, $a_j \cdot y = 0$ for each $j$.
6. Each $b \in \operatorname{Col}(A)$ has the form $b = c_1 a_1 + \dots + c_n a_n$ by step 1, and the dot product is linear in its first argument, so $b \cdot y = \sum_j c_j (a_j \cdot y) = 0$.
7. So $y \in \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$, and $\operatorname{Null}(A^{\mathsf{T}}) \subseteq \operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp$.
8. Steps 4 and 7 give $\operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp = \operatorname{Null}(A^{\mathsf{T}})$.

## Idea

The entries of $A^{\mathsf{T}} y$ are the dot products of $y$ with the columns of $A$.
So $A^{\mathsf{T}} y = 0$ says that $y$ is orthogonal to each column.
A vector orthogonal to each column is orthogonal to every linear combination of the columns, which is every vector of the column space.

## Variants

The same argument for $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ in place of $A$ gives $\operatorname{Row}(A)^\perp = \operatorname{Null}(A)$, which is steps 5 to 8 of the [[proof of the theorem on the four fundamental subspaces|A3KBTY9JW7]].
Over $\mathbb{C}$, the same proof with the Hermitian product and the conjugate transpose gives $\operatorname{Col}(A)^\perp = \operatorname{Null}(A^*)$.
The proof never uses positivity of the dot product, so it shows the equality of sets over every field, with the standard pairing $y^{\mathsf{T}} b$ in place of the dot product.
