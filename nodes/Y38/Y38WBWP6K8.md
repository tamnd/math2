## Summary

Intermittency in turbulence is the fact that the small scales of a turbulent flow are not statistically self-similar.
Strong velocity gradients and intense vortices fill only a small and irregular part of the flow.
They become rarer but more extreme at smaller scales.
It shows up as a growth of the flatness of velocity increments at small separations.
It also shows up as structure function exponents that depend nonlinearly on the order.
It is the main known departure from the [[Kolmogorov 1941 theory|A3HKV3BHDR]] and a central open problem in the theory of [[turbulence|VY307F8Z0S]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Record the velocity of the wind at one point and look at the changes from one instant to the next.
Most of the time the changes are small, but now and then they are huge, much more often than a bell curve would allow.
Look at shorter and shorter time lags, and these rare violent events become even more dominant.
So the small eddies are not spread evenly in space: they form a patchy set of thin, strong structures.
The word "intermittency" is also used for a different effect in [[dynamical systems|W2FNJ4KBYT]], where regular phases alternate with chaotic bursts.
