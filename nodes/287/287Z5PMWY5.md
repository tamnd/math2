## Summary

The f-plane is the simplest model of a rotating planet: a flat plane in which the Coriolis parameter $f$ is a constant.
It approximates a small patch of the planet near a fixed latitude.
It captures rotation effects such as [[geostrophic balance|CZ1S1P465Y]], inertial oscillations and Kelvin waves, but not the variation of $f$ with latitude.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Earth's rotation deflects moving air and water, and the strength of the deflection depends on latitude.
For a region a few hundred kilometres across, the latitude barely changes, so the deflection is nearly the same everywhere.
The f-plane treats it as exactly the same and treats the ground as flat.
For example, a lake or a small sea at latitude $45^\circ$ can be modelled with $f \approx 10^{-4}$ per second.
Because nothing changes with latitude, the f-plane has no Rossby waves.
