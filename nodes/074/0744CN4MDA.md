## Definition

A symmetric bilinear form is a bilinear form whose value does not change when the two arguments swap.
Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a vector space over $F$.
A [[bilinear form|0P799D6AMN]] $B$ on $V$ is *symmetric* when
$$B(u, v) = B(v, u) \qquad \text{for all } u, v \in V.$$

## Notation

A pair $(V, B)$ of a space and a symmetric form is called a symmetric bilinear space, and some books call it a quadratic space or an orthogonal space.

## Remarks

The condition is asked for every pair of vectors, and that is what makes orthogonality a relation that reads the same in both directions.
When $V$ has a basis $(e_1, \dots, e_n)$, it is enough to check the pairs of basis vectors, because both sides of the condition are bilinear in $(u, v)$.
So $B$ is symmetric exactly when its matrix $A$, with $A_{ij} = B(e_i, e_j)$, is a [[symmetric matrix|0VR671PHE5]].

For example, $B(x, y) = x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1$ on $F^2$ has $B(e_1, e_2) = 1 = B(e_2, e_1)$, so it is symmetric.
The form $B(x, y) = x_1 y_2$ fails the condition, since $B(e_1, e_2) = 1$ and $B(e_2, e_1) = 0$.
A single pair with equal values proves nothing: this second form gives $B(e_2, e_2) = 0$ in both orders, but it is still not symmetric.

The definition asks for no positivity, so a symmetric form can take the value $0$ or a negative value on $B(v, v)$.
When $1 + 1 \ne 0$ in $F$, symmetric forms correspond one to one with [[quadratic forms|0RBQS1JJCR]].
The concept page [[symmetric bilinear form|0AXZNE5XNH]] gives examples, the diagonal form by congruence, and the failure in characteristic $2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A symmetric bilinear form gives the same number for the pair $(u, v)$ and for the pair $(v, u)$.
The dot product of the plane is one: $(1, 2) \cdot (3, 4) = 3 + 8 = 11$ in both orders.

### L2 human

To test the definition, write the matrix and compare it with its transpose.
For $B(x, y) = 4x_1 y_1 - x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1$ on $\mathbb{Q}^2$, the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 4 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, which equals its transpose, so $B$ is symmetric.
Check: $B(e_1, e_2) = -1$ and $B(e_2, e_1) = -1$.
Watch out: symmetry on one pair of vectors does not give symmetry; you need all pairs of basis vectors.
