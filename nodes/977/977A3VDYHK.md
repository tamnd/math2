## Summary

In mathematics, **Bring's curve** (also called **Bring's surface** and, by analogy with the Klein quartic, **the Bring sextic**) is a highly-symmetric Riemann surface. It can be defined as an algebraic curve using five [[complex numbers|09FTAET5Z4]] as projective coordinates, as the space curve in the four-dimensional projective space $\mathbb{P}_4(\mathbb{C})$ cut out by the homogeneous equations

$$v+w+x+y+z=v^2+w^2+x^2+y^2+z^2=v^3+w^3+x^3+y^3+z^3=0.$$

It was named by (Klein 2003, p.157) after Erland Samuel Bring who studied a similar construction in 1786 in a Promotionschrift submitted to the University of Lund. Note that the roots *x*<sub>i</sub> of the Bring quintic $x^5+ax+b = 0$ satisfies Bring's curve since $\sum_{i=1}^5 x_i^k = 0$ for $k = 1,2,3.$
