## Summary

The Gibbons–Hawking–York boundary term is an integral over the boundary of a spacetime region that is added to the [[Einstein–Hilbert action|JPE3A1SN7V]].
Its integrand is the trace of the extrinsic curvature of the boundary, that is, its [[mean curvature|PA78NFEPCF]].
The scalar curvature contains second derivatives of the metric.
So without the term, the Einstein–Hilbert action has no well-posed variational principle when the metric is fixed on the boundary.
The term matters in the Hamiltonian formulation of gravity and in Euclidean quantum gravity, where it contributes to black hole entropy.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The action of general relativity involves the curvature of spacetime, and curvature involves second derivatives of the metric.
When one varies such an action over a region with a boundary, leftover terms with normal derivatives of the metric appear on the boundary.
Fixing only the metric on the boundary does not kill them.
The boundary term is chosen exactly to cancel these leftovers.
It measures how much the boundary bends inside the spacetime, much like the mean curvature of a surface in space.
For a sphere of radius $r$ in flat three-dimensional space, this bending is $2/r$.
