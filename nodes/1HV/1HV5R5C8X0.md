## Definition

Let $r$ be a constant risk-free rate and let $T > 0$.
In the *Merton model* the value $V_t$ of the assets of a firm is a [[geometric Brownian motion|D48DR46B97]] with volatility $\sigma > 0$ and $V_0 > 0$.
The firm has issued one zero-coupon bond with face value $D > 0$ and maturity $T$, and no other debt.
The firm defaults if $V_T < D$.
At time $T$ the equity holders receive $E_T = \max(V_T - D, 0)$ and the debt holders receive $\min(V_T, D)$.

## Remarks

The equity is a European call on $V$ with strike $D$, so in the [[Black–Scholes model|FFT8TF6QP2]] $E_0 = V_0 N(d_1) - D e^{-rT} N(d_2)$.
Here $N$ is the standard [[normal distribution|05AQE0QR0D]] function, $d_1 = (\ln(V_0/D) + (r + \sigma^2/2) T)/(\sigma \sqrt{T})$ and $d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{T}$.
The risk-neutral default probability is $N(-d_2)$, and the debt is worth $V_0 - E_0$.
Example: if $V_0 = 150$, $D = 100$, $r = 0$, $\sigma = 0.2$, $T = 1$, then $d_2 = \ln 1.5 / 0.2 - 0.1 \approx 1.93$, so the default probability is about $0.027$.
Non-example: a firm whose asset value falls below $D$ before $T$ and then recovers does not default in this model.
Default is checked only at $T$; first-passage models such as that of Black and Cox change this.
