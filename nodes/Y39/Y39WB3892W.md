## Statement

Let $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ be [[discrete random variables|0X2J0VY6DN]] with finitely many values on a common probability space.
Then
$$2 I(C; D) \le I(A; B) + I(A; C, D) + 3 I(C; D \mid A) + I(C; D \mid B),$$
where $I$ denotes the [[mutual information|A83CYGDXFG]] and the [[conditional mutual information|PW6PXZ2WV4]], and $(C, D)$ is treated as one random variable.
This [[information inequality|836E9NPN42]] is not of Shannon type: it is not a non-negative linear combination of basic Shannon inequalities.

## Remarks

The inequality was proved by Zhang and Yeung in 1998, as the first known non-Shannon-type information inequality.
The proof introduces auxiliary random variables that copy part of the given family, coupled with the rest through a conditional law.
It then applies Shannon inequalities to the enlarged family.
That it is not of Shannon type is shown by a vector in the cone cut out by the Shannon inequalities on four variables that violates it.

Example: if $A$ and $B$ are constant, then $I(A; B) = I(A; C, D) = 0$ and $I(C; D \mid A) = I(C; D \mid B) = I(C; D)$.
The right side becomes $4 I(C; D)$, and the inequality reduces to $2 I(C; D) \le 4 I(C; D)$, which is true.

Consequence: for four random variables, the closure of the set of entropy vectors is strictly smaller than the cone given by the Shannon inequalities.
Later work found many more non-Shannon inequalities on four variables.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For some time it was an open question whether every true rule about entropies follows from the basic fact that shared information is never negative.
The Zhang–Yeung inequality is a rule about four random variables that is true but does not follow from these basic facts.
It showed that the structure of entropy is richer than expected.

### L2 human

Information inequalities describe which entropy vectors can occur, and they bound the rates of network codes and of secret sharing schemes.
The Zhang–Yeung inequality gives bounds in these problems that Shannon inequalities alone can not give.
Entropy vectors are related to the sizes of subgroups of finite groups, so the inequality also has a group-theoretic form.
