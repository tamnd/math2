## Definition

Let $R$ be a [[ring|0B1261B5ED]], for example a field, and let $m, n \ge 0$ be integers.
An $m \times n$ *matrix* over $R$ is a family $A = (a_{ij})$ of elements $a_{ij} \in R$, indexed by the pairs $(i, j)$ with $1 \le i \le m$ and $1 \le j \le n$.
Equivalently, $A$ is a function $\{1, \dots, m\} \times \{1, \dots, n\} \to R$, and $a_{ij}$ is its value at $(i, j)$.
The element $a_{ij}$ is the *entry* of $A$ in row $i$ and column $j$.
Row $i$ of $A$ is the list $(a_{i1}, \dots, a_{in})$, and column $j$ is the list $(a_{1j}, \dots, a_{mj})$.
The pair $m \times n$ is the *size* of $A$.
We write $A$ as a rectangular array with $m$ rows and $n$ columns:
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & \cdots & a_{1n} \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{m1} & \cdots & a_{mn} \end{pmatrix}.$$
The set of all $m \times n$ matrices over $R$ is written $M_{m \times n}(R)$ or $R^{m \times n}$.
Two matrices $A$ and $B$ are *equal* when they have the same size and $a_{ij} = b_{ij}$ for all $i$ and $j$.

## Notation

- A $1 \times n$ matrix is a *row vector*, and an $m \times 1$ matrix is a *column vector*.
- When $m = n$, the matrix is [[square|0JXZKX526R]], and $M_n(R)$ means $M_{n \times n}(R)$.
- The notation $A = (a_{ij})_{1 \le i \le m,\, 1 \le j \le n}$ shows the ranges of the indices.

## Remarks

- The Mathlib declaration `Matrix m n α` is the type `m → n → α` for arbitrary types `m` and `n`; we record the match as a variant.
- Mathlib needs `Fintype` instances on the index types for products, traces and determinants.
- Mathlib often indexes by `Fin m = {0, …, m - 1}`, so its first entry is `A 0 0`, not $a_{11}$.
- The case $m = 0$ or $n = 0$ is allowed: there is exactly one $0 \times n$ matrix, the empty family.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A matrix is a table of numbers with a fixed number of rows and columns.
The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$ has 2 rows and 3 columns, so it is a $2 \times 3$ matrix.
We name an entry by its row first and then its column, so $a_{21} = 4$.

### L2 human

The definition as a function on pairs of indices makes equality precise.
Two matrices with the same entries in a different shape are different, such as a $1 \times 3$ row and a $3 \times 1$ column.
