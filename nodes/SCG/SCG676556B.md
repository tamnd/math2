## Definition

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a [[probability space|0NFPJVJJHX]] with a [[filtration|HNK1Y62G7V]] $(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t = 0, \dots, T}$.
A market has a riskless asset used as numéraire and $d$ risky assets.
Their prices in units of the numéraire form an adapted process $S_t = (S_t^1, \dots, S_t^d)$ of [[random vectors|0PNRKMRC55]].
A *trading strategy* is a process $\xi_t \in \R^d$, $t = 1, \dots, T$, with $\xi_t$ measurable with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$.
Its gain is
$$G_T = \sum_{t=1}^T \xi_t \cdot (S_t - S_{t-1}).$$
A self-financing strategy with initial value $V_0$ has final value $V_T = V_0 + G_T$, the riskless asset absorbing the rest.
An *arbitrage* is a self-financing strategy such that:

1. $V_0 \le 0$;
2. $V_T \ge 0$ almost surely;
3. $P(V_T > 0) > 0$.

The market is *free of arbitrage* if no arbitrage exists.

## Remarks

Equivalently, an arbitrage exists exactly when some trading strategy has $G_T \ge 0$ almost surely and $P(G_T > 0) > 0$.

Example: one period, two equally likely states, interest rate $0$, and a stock with $S_0 = 100$ and $S_1 \in \set{120, 110}$.
Borrow $100$ and buy one share: $V_0 = 0$, and $V_1 = 20$ or $10$.
All three clauses hold, so this is an arbitrage.

Non-example: if instead $S_1 \in \set{120, 90}$, holding $\xi$ shares gives $V_1 - V_0 = 20\xi$ or $-10\xi$.
For $\xi \ne 0$ one of these is negative, so clause 2 fails for every strategy with $V_0 = 0$, and the market is free of arbitrage.
