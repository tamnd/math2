## Definition

Let $v_c(R)$ be the circular speed of a disc galaxy at radius $R$, as in the [[rotation curve|V4FYBDSGMT]].
Let $\Omega(R) = v_c(R)/R$ be the [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]], and let $R_0$ be the radius of the observer.
The *Oort constants* are
$$A = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{v_c}{R} - \frac{d v_c}{d R} \right)\Big|_{R_0} = -\frac{1}{2} R_0 \, \Omega'(R_0),$$
$$B = -\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{v_c}{R} + \frac{d v_c}{d R} \right)\Big|_{R_0}.$$

## Remarks

They satisfy $A - B = \Omega(R_0)$ and $A + B = -v_c'(R_0)$.
The [[epicyclic frequency|GK34V4WF2V]] at $R_0$ is $\kappa^2 = -4 B (A - B)$.

Take a star on a circular orbit in the plane, at distance $d$ and [[galactic longitude|AQ3RM9F7QH]] $l$.
To first order in $d / R_0$, its velocity relative to the local circular motion has radial part $v_r = A \, d \sin 2l$ and transverse part $v_t = d \, (A \cos 2l + B)$.
This is how $A$ and $B$ are measured.
Measured values in the solar neighbourhood are roughly $A \approx 15$ and $B \approx -12$ km/s/kpc.

Example: for a flat rotation curve $v_c' = 0$, so $A = v_c / (2 R_0) = -B$.
Non-example: a rigid rotation $v_c = \Omega R$ has $v_c' = v_c / R$, so $A = 0$ and $B = -\Omega$; there is no shear, and the radial velocities of nearby stars show no $\sin 2l$ pattern.
