## Summary

Rigid body dynamics studies how [[rigid bodies|EZ9P9ERKNE]] move under forces and [[torques|9636MFFSGC]].
Its equations are the Newton–Euler equations.
The [[centre of mass|0HNK6KT05K]] moves like a particle carrying the total mass under the total force.
The [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] about the centre of mass changes at the rate of the total torque.
The angular momentum is the inertia tensor applied to the [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]], which makes the rotational part nonlinear and rich.
It is the basis of the theory of tops and gyroscopes, of vehicle and spacecraft dynamics, of robotics and of multibody simulation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A thrown hammer spins in a complicated way, yet its centre of mass follows a simple parabola, as if all its mass were concentrated there.
Rigid body dynamics explains both parts: the flight of the centre and the spinning about it.
The spinning depends on how the mass is spread out, which is why a long rod is easy to spin about its length and hard to spin end over end.
