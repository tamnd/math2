## Summary

Negative refraction is refraction in which the refracted ray lies on the same side of the normal as the incident ray.
Its basic case is the interface with a medium of negative [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]], where both the [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]] and the [[permeability|5MJQ5J5H8Z]] are negative.
In such a medium the phase moves against the flow of energy.
Veselago studied such media theoretically in 1967, and they were realised with [[metamaterials|CQ9A45JF68]] around 2000.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When light enters water from air, it bends toward the normal but stays on the far side of it.
In a medium with negative index, the light bends past the normal and comes out on the same side it came from.
A straw in such a liquid would seem to bend backwards.
A flat slab of such a material can bring light from a point back to a point, acting as a lens with no curved surface.
