## Summary

The electromagnetic wave equation is the vector [[wave equation|5EHD2XBQDG]] satisfied by the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] and the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] in a region without charges and currents.
It follows from [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] and shows that electromagnetic disturbances travel at the speed $c = 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_0 \mu_0}$ in vacuum.
Maxwell's identification of this speed with the measured speed of light showed that light is an electromagnetic wave.
In a linear homogeneous medium the same equation holds with the slower speed $1/\sqrt{\varepsilon \mu}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In empty space, a changing electric field makes a magnetic field and a changing magnetic field makes an electric field.
Combining the two rules, each field on its own obeys the same wave equation as a vibrating string, but in three dimensions and with vectors.
The speed in this equation comes out of two constants measured with charges and magnets, and it equals the speed of light.
Radio waves, visible light and X-rays are all solutions of this one equation.
