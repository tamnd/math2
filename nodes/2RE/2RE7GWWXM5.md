## Summary

This topic is a part of [[model theory|F3AXVABC35]].
It covers elementary embeddings and substructures, the tests and constructions that give them, and the theorems that give models of every infinite size.
The main tools are the Tarski-Vaught test, the method of diagrams, Skolem functions and elementary chains.
They lead to the Löwenheim-Skolem theorems, to elementary amalgamation, and to the partial elementary maps that later sub-areas extend one element at a time.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Elementary embedding|CGH8C8EA9N]]: maps that preserve the truth of every formula.
1. [[Elementary substructure|8WZF9SBP39]]: substructures that agree with the whole structure on formulas with parameters.
1. [[Tarski-Vaught test|60SA99BER7]]: the witness criterion for elementary substructures, and its use with automorphisms.
1. [[Diagram lemmas|~4a49d7ae]]: models of the atomic and elementary diagrams are extensions and elementary extensions.
1. [[Löwenheim-Skolem theorem|484M0TZ1S7]]: elementary substructures and extensions of every infinite cardinality at least $\abs{L}$.
1. [[Skolem functions and Skolem hulls|~71af893e]]: witness functions, Skolem theories, and hulls as small elementary substructures.
1. [[Elementary chain theorem|~26b49e10]]: unions of elementary chains, and unions of chains of models of $\forall\exists$ theories.
1. [[Elementary amalgamation theorem|~c9e11fa5]]: two elementary extensions of one structure fit into a common one.
1. [[Partial elementary map|~7e54c18a]]: maps between subsets that preserve all formulas, the steps of back-and-forth constructions.
1. [[Elementary equivalence versus isomorphism|~6b659f6b]]: three pairs of structures that satisfy the same sentences and are not isomorphic.

### Prerequisites

- [[Basic notions of model theory|AY09KYSW81]]: substructures, embeddings, elementary equivalence, complete theories and definable sets.
- [[Structures, satisfaction and truth|NJDME6F845]]: signatures, structures, formulas and the satisfaction relation.
- [[Completeness, compactness and interpolation|FBWW4J1XWG]]: the compactness theorem, used in rows 4, 5, 8 and 10.
