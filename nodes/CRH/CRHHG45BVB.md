## Summary

Eleven-dimensional supergravity is the [[supergravity|VWBEWVT29C]] theory in eleven spacetime dimensions, constructed by Cremmer, Julia and Scherk in 1978.
Eleven is the largest dimension in which a supersymmetric theory with fields of spin at most $2$ exists.
In this dimension the theory is unique.
Its fields are the metric, one gravitino and a three-form gauge field.
It is the low-energy limit of [[M-theory|FEC8C1595Y]], and its reductions on compact seven-manifolds give four-dimensional supergravities.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Supergravity theories come in many versions in lower dimensions, but in eleven dimensions there is just one.
It contains gravity, a fermionic partner of the graviton, and one more field, a higher-dimensional cousin of the electromagnetic potential.
That field couples naturally to membranes, two-dimensional objects moving in spacetime.
If one rolls up seven of the eleven dimensions into a small shape, one gets a four-dimensional theory with gravity and many other fields.
Its bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom balance exactly: $128$ of each.
