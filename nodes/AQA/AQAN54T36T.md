## Proof

1. Let $G$ be a [[cyclic group|0B85Q41RP6]] of finite order $n$ with generator $g$. So $G = \langle g \rangle$, the [[cyclic subgroup|0YT586TKXF]] generated by $g$.
2. If $g$ had infinite order, $\langle g \rangle$ would be infinite, by [[the order of a cyclic subgroup|031M8HX7DA]]. So $g$ has finite order, and that result gives $\operatorname{ord}(g) = \abs{\langle g \rangle} = n$.
3. Let $x \in G$. Since $\langle x \rangle \subseteq G$ is finite, the same result shows that $x$ has finite order. It also gives $\abs{\langle x \rangle} = \operatorname{ord}(x)$.
4. Existence. Let $d$ be a positive [[divisor|096PW7Q014]] of $n$ and put $c = n/d$, a positive integer.
5. Since $c$ divides $n$, $c$ is a common divisor of $n$ and $c$. Every divisor of $c$ is at most $c$. So $\gcd(n, c) = c$, by the definition of the [[greatest common divisor|02WF878BH1]].
6. By [[the order of a power|0VVPQNVM8S]], $\operatorname{ord}(g^c) = n/\gcd(n, c) = n/c = d$. By step 3, $\langle g^c \rangle$ is a [[subgroup|0N0PXCTYA7]] of order $d$.
7. Uniqueness. Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ of order $d$. Since [[subgroups of cyclic groups are cyclic|0KB1ND7XDS]], $H = \langle x \rangle$ for some $x \in G$.
8. Write $x = g^k$ with $k \in \Z$. By steps 2 and 3 and the order of a power, $d = \abs{H} = n/\gcd(n, k)$. So $\gcd(n, k) = n/d = c$.
9. So $c$ divides $k$; write $k = cu$ with $u \in \Z$. Each element of $H$ is $(g^k)^j$ for some $j \in \Z$.
10. By the [[laws of exponents|00DRK90Z6K]], $(g^k)^j = g^{cuj} = (g^c)^{uj} \in \langle g^c \rangle$. So $H \subseteq \langle g^c \rangle$.
11. Both $H$ and $\langle g^c \rangle$ have exactly $d$ elements. A subset of a finite set with as many elements is the whole set. So $H = \langle g^{n/d} \rangle$.
12. Every subgroup has this form. Let $H$ be any subgroup of $G$. As in steps 7 and 8, $H = \langle g^k \rangle$ for some $k \in \Z$, and $\abs{H} = n/\gcd(n, k)$.
13. Put $d = \abs{H}$, a positive integer. Since $\gcd(n, k)$ divides $n$, we have $n = d \cdot \gcd(n, k)$, so $d$ is a positive divisor of $n$.
14. By steps 7 to 11, $H$ is the unique subgroup of order $d$, namely $H = \langle g^{n/d} \rangle$.

## Idea

A subgroup of a cyclic group is generated by one power $g^k$, and its size is $n/\gcd(n, k)$, a divisor of $n$. The subgroup only depends on $\gcd(n, k)$: the generator can be replaced by $g^{\gcd(n,k)}$. So for each divisor $d$ there is exactly one subgroup of size $d$.
