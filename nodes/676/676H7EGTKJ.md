## Summary

Let $L$ be a [[signature|WDVVEYD6AT]] and let $\mathcal{M}$ be an $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]] with domain $M$.
An $L$-structure $\mathcal{N}$ with domain $N$ is a *substructure* of $\mathcal{M}$, written $\mathcal{N} \subseteq \mathcal{M}$, when $N \subseteq M$, each constant $c^{\mathcal{N}}$ equals $c^{\mathcal{M}}$, each function $f^{\mathcal{N}}$ is the restriction of $f^{\mathcal{M}}$ to $N$, and each relation $R^{\mathcal{N}}$ is $R^{\mathcal{M}}$ restricted to $N$.
So a nonempty [[subset|0BJR9QR08E]] $N \subseteq M$ carries a substructure exactly when it contains every constant and is closed under every function of $\mathcal{M}$, and then the substructure is unique.
Substructures give one definition for subgroups, subrings, subspaces and induced subgraphs, and they are the first tool to compare structures.

## Motivation

Algebra has many notions of "a part that is again an object of the same kind": subgroups, subrings, subfields, linear subspaces.
Each notion has its own definition, but the pattern is the same: a subset that is closed under the operations and contains the special elements.
In a structure, the operations are the function symbols and the special elements are the constants.
So one definition covers all cases, once the signature is fixed.
The definition also shows which properties pass from a structure to its parts: equations and other quantifier-free statements do, and statements with "there exists" in general do not.

## Notation

We write $\mathcal{N} \subseteq \mathcal{M}$, and we say that $\mathcal{M}$ is an *extension* of $\mathcal{N}$.
For $A \subseteq M$, the substructure *generated* by $A$ is the smallest substructure that contains $A$, written $\langle A \rangle$ or $\langle A \rangle_{\mathcal{M}}$.
Its domain is the set of values $t^{\mathcal{M}}(a_1, \dots, a_n)$ of all [[terms|6D3WA7N2W4]] $t$ at elements $a_i \in A$.
In Mathlib, `FirstOrder.Language.Substructure` is a subset closed under all functions; for a signature without constants it can be empty, while many textbooks require a nonempty domain.

## Remarks

### Examples

- In the signature $\set{\cdot, {}^{-1}, e}$ of groups, the substructures of a group are exactly its [[subgroups|0X1JXT47GA]]; see [[this example|M9G03PFN8E]].
- In the signature $\set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$ of rings, the substructures of a ring are its [[subrings|0GNCRPYXYV]].
- In the signature $\set{+, -, 0}$, the set $2\Z$ is the domain of a substructure of $(\Z, +, -, 0)$, because it contains $0$ and is closed under $+$ and $-$.
- A graph is a structure with one binary relation $E$, and its substructures are its [[induced subgraphs|0EREPBVG2G]].
- In $(\Z, +, -, 0)$ the substructure generated by $\set{4, 6}$ is $2\Z$; see [[the substructure generated by a set|XV8GX9C2AR]].

### Non-examples

- The set $\N$ is not the domain of a substructure of $(\Z, +, -, 0)$, because it is not closed under $-$; see [[this non-example|SYEAW0R7A4]].
- A subgraph that drops an edge between two of its vertices is not a substructure, because the relation must be the restriction of $E$.
- The set $\Z$ is not the domain of a substructure of the field $(\Q, +, -, \cdot, {}^{-1}, 0, 1)$ with an inverse symbol, because $2^{-1} \notin \Z$.

### Basic properties

- The intersection of a family of substructures is closed under the functions and contains the constants, so for nonempty $A$ the substructure $\langle A \rangle$ exists, and $A \mapsto \langle A \rangle$ is a [[closure operator|17FVAN6HXX]].
- The inclusion map of a substructure is an [[embedding|SCRB74SWQN]], and every embedding is an isomorphism onto a substructure.
- If $\mathcal{N} \subseteq \mathcal{M}$, then a quantifier-free formula holds of a tuple from $N$ in $\mathcal{N}$ exactly when it holds in $\mathcal{M}$; see [[embeddings preserve quantifier-free formulas|C9494XYEMN]].
- So universal sentences pass down to substructures and existential sentences pass up to extensions.
- If $L$ is countable, then $|\langle A \rangle| \le \max(|A|, \aleph_0)$, because there are only countably many terms.

### History

Substructures appeared first in algebra, under the names of subgroups, subrings and subfields.
Garrett Birkhoff studied subalgebras of algebras of any signature in 1935, in the paper that proved his theorem on varieties.
Model theory added relation symbols, so that a substructure must also carry the restricted relations.

### Common errors

- To forget that the substructures depend on the signature. The set $\N$ is a substructure of $(\Z, +, 0)$ but not of $(\Z, +, -, 0)$.
- To think that a substructure of a model of $T$ is a model of $T$. This holds for universal axioms only: $(\N, +, 0) \subseteq (\Z, +, 0)$, but $\N$ is not a group.
- To confuse a substructure with an [[elementary substructure|8WZF9SBP39]], which must satisfy the same formulas with parameters as the larger structure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a structure and keep only some of its elements.
If the operations, used on kept elements, always give kept elements, and the special elements are kept, the kept part is a structure of the same kind.
For example, the even numbers $\dots, -4, -2, 0, 2, 4, \dots$ with addition and subtraction form a part of the integers.
The sum of two even numbers is even, the difference of two even numbers is even, and $0$ is even.
The odd numbers do not form such a part, because $1 + 1 = 2$ is not odd.

### L2 human

We find the substructure of $(\Z, +, -, 0)$ generated by $A = \set{6, 10}$.

1. The terms built from $6$, $10$ and $0$ with $+$ and $-$ give all numbers $6a + 10b$ with $a, b \in \Z$.
2. Each such number is even, because $6$ and $10$ are even.
3. The number $2 = 2 \cdot 6 - 10$ is one of them, so every multiple $2k$ is one of them.
4. So $\langle A \rangle = 2\Z$, the even integers.

The typical use is to show that a property expressed by equations passes from a structure to a substructure.
A trap is to change the signature without noticing: in $(\Z, +, 0)$ the same set $A$ generates only the numbers $6a + 10b$ with $a, b \ge 0$.

### L3 human

The substructures of $\mathcal{M}$ form a complete lattice under inclusion, and the generated substructure is the closure operator of this lattice; this is the subalgebra lattice of [[universal algebra|W82R6N0GM9]].
In category-theoretic terms, the substructures of $\mathcal{M}$ are the subobjects of $\mathcal{M}$ given by embeddings.
In model theory, the substructure generated by $A$ is the base of the method of diagrams, and the condition that a substructure be elementary leads to the [[Tarski-Vaught test|60SA99BER7]] in the sub-area on [[elementary extensions|2RE7GWWXM5]].
The [[Łoś-Tarski theorem|9RN0KYXNT1]] gives the converse of the passage of universal sentences: a theory whose models are closed under substructures has universal axioms.
