## Summary

Atmospheric refraction is the bending of light as it passes through the Earth's atmosphere, whose density and refractive index change with height.
It makes celestial objects appear higher above the horizon than they are.
The effect is zero at the zenith and largest at the horizon, where it is about half a degree.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light bends when it passes from thin air into denser air, as it bends entering water.
Starlight passes through denser and denser layers on its way down, so its path curves towards the ground.
The eye sees the star along the final direction of the ray, which points higher than the star.
When the Sun seems to sit on the horizon at sunset, its true position is already just below it.
