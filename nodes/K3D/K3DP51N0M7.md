## Statement

Let a system with energy function $H_\lambda$ evolve by Markovian dynamics coupled to a heat bath at temperature $T$, with $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$.
Assume that for each fixed $\lambda$ the dynamics satisfies detailed balance with respect to the canonical distribution of $H_\lambda$.
In the forward process, the system starts in canonical equilibrium at $\lambda = A$, and $\lambda$ follows a protocol $\lambda(t)$, $0 \le t \le \tau$, from $A$ to $B$.
In the reverse process, the system starts in canonical equilibrium at $\lambda = B$, and $\lambda$ follows the time-reversed protocol $\lambda(\tau - t)$.
Let $P_F$ and $P_R$ be the [[probability densities|0DVDCMA2DK]] of the [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] $W$ done on the system in the two processes.
Let $\Delta F = F_B - F_A$ be the difference of the [[Helmholtz free energies|JXKY6C8JTA]].
Then, for every real $W$,
$$\frac{P_F(W)}{P_R(-W)} = e^{\beta (W - \Delta F)}.$$

## Remarks

The proof compares the probability of each forward trajectory with that of its time reverse in the reverse process.
Detailed balance makes their ratio $e^{\beta Q}$, where $Q$ is the heat given to the bath, and the equilibrium initial states add the factor $e^{\beta(\Delta E - \Delta F)}$; with $W = Q + \Delta E$ this gives $e^{\beta(W - \Delta F)}$.
Multiplying by $e^{-\beta W} P_R(-W)$ and integrating over $W$ gives the [[Jarzynski equality|1VPYJ7R7DT]].
The two distributions cross at $W = \Delta F$, which gives a way to measure free energy differences in experiments.
It is a [[fluctuation theorem|75NZNXNN7D]] for the work, in the form for driven systems that start in equilibrium.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pull a molecule from one shape to another many times and record the work, then do the reverse pulls.
The theorem says that the chance of a work value in the forward pulls and of its negative in the reverse pulls differ by an exact exponential factor.
Where the two histograms cross, the work equals the free energy difference.

### L2 human

The theorem refines the Jarzynski equality: it gives a relation between whole distributions, not only one average.
It is used with optical tweezers in single-molecule biophysics, where it has been checked in experiments on RNA hairpins.
Its hypotheses are the same as for detailed balance: an equilibrium bath and microscopically reversible dynamics.
