## Summary

The phase response curve of an oscillator records how much a brief stimulus advances or delays its rhythm.
It is a function of the phase at which the stimulus arrives.
It is defined for systems with a stable [[limit cycle|0AAQ73WRFB]].
Examples are a firing neuron, a beating heart cell and a circadian clock.
Phase response curves predict how oscillators lock to periodic forcing and how coupled oscillators synchronise.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a child on a swing and a friend who gives one quick push.
A push at one moment of the swing makes the next return come sooner; at another moment it makes it come later; at some moments it barely matters.
The phase response curve is the graph of this shift against the moment of the push.
For a circadian clock, light early in the subjective night delays the clock and light late in the night advances it, and the curve shows exactly this pattern.
