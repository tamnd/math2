## Summary

Light carries angular momentum in two forms: spin, tied to circular polarisation, and orbital angular momentum, tied to the spatial shape of the wavefront.
A beam whose phase varies as $e^{i\ell\varphi}$ around its axis carries orbital angular momentum $\ell\hbar$ per photon, where $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant.
Such beams have helical wavefronts and an [[optical vortex|23MDMTZT5X]] on the axis, as in the [[Laguerre–Gaussian modes|8YGCTX64MT]].
Allen and coworkers showed in 1992 that Laguerre–Gaussian laser modes carry this angular momentum.
It can be transferred to small particles and make them rotate.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light can push on things, and it can also twist them.
One kind of twist comes from polarisation: circularly polarised light makes a small absorbing particle spin about its own axis.
Another kind comes from the shape of the beam: if the wavefront winds around the axis like a corkscrew, the light carries a twist of its own.
A particle caught in the bright ring of such a beam is driven around the ring.
The number of full turns of the phase around the axis sets how much twist each photon carries.
