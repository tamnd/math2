## Summary

When saturated vapour touches a wall colder than its saturation temperature, it condenses into a thin liquid film that runs down the wall.
Nusselt's theory of film condensation was published by Wilhelm Nusselt in 1916.
It computes the thickness of a laminar film on a vertical wall and the resulting [[heat transfer coefficient|J821AD6YTE]].
It treats the film as a slow viscous flow driven by gravity, with heat crossing the film by conduction only.
Its closed-form result is still the starting point for the design of condensers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Steam meeting a cold window turns into water that runs down the glass in a thin sheet.
The heat released by the condensing steam must pass through this water sheet to reach the glass.
Lower down, more water has collected, the sheet is thicker, and heat passes less easily.
Nusselt computed how the thickness grows down the wall: it grows like the fourth root of the distance from the top.
