## Summary

Boiling heat transfer is heat transfer from a hot surface to a liquid whose saturation temperature is below that of the surface.
Then vapour forms at the surface.
The [[latent heat|FN6Z9QKAH5]] carried off by the vapour and the stirring by bubbles make it one of the most efficient modes of heat transfer.
Its dependence on the wall superheat is shown by the boiling curve, with the regimes of natural convection, nucleate boiling, transition boiling and film boiling.
The peak of the nucleate regime, the critical heat flux, is a key safety limit for boilers and nuclear reactors.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put a pot of water on a stove.
When the bottom gets a few degrees hotter than $100$ °C, bubbles start to form at small pits on the metal and rise through the water.
Each bubble takes a lot of heat with it, so the pot bottom is cooled very well.
If the bottom gets much hotter still, a layer of vapour can cover it and insulate it, and the heat transfer drops.
A drop of water dancing on a very hot pan shows this vapour layer.
