## Definition

Use geocentric spherical coordinates: radius $r$, geocentric latitude $\phi$ and longitude $\lambda$.
Let $GM$ be the mass parameter of the Earth and $a$ a reference radius.
Let $\bar P_{nm}$ be the fully normalised [[associated Legendre functions|VPJCSVRY6S]] of degree $n$ and order $m$.
A *geopotential model* of degree $N$ is the function
$$V(r, \phi, \lambda) = \frac{GM}{r}\left(1 + \sum_{n=2}^{N} \left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^{n} \sum_{m=0}^{n} \bar P_{nm}(\sin\phi)\big(\bar C_{nm}\cos m\lambda + \bar S_{nm}\sin m\lambda\big)\right),$$
given by the real numbers $\bar C_{nm}$ and $\bar S_{nm}$, its Stokes coefficients.
It models the gravitational part of the [[geopotential|5Y2BWNY6MF]] outside a sphere that contains all the masses of the Earth.

## Remarks

Example: the model with $N = 2$ and only $\bar C_{20} \ne 0$ describes an axially symmetric flattened Earth; with $J_2 = -\sqrt{5}\,\bar C_{20}$ it reads $V = \frac{GM}{r}\big(1 - J_2 (a/r)^2 P_2(\sin\phi)\big)$, where $P_2(t) = (3t^2 - 1)/2$; for the Earth, $J_2 \approx 1.083 \times 10^{-3}$.
Non-example: the function $GM/r + c\,r^2$ with $c \ne 0$ is not of this form, since $r^2$ terms grow at infinity; it is not the potential of bounded masses.
Each term $r^{-n-1}\bar P_{nm}(\sin\phi)\cos m\lambda$ is a solution of [[Laplace's equation|S8RG6N1PRT]], since $\bar P_{nm}(\sin\phi)\cos m\lambda$ is a [[spherical harmonic|3F32Y21HP4]] of degree $n$.
The degree $1$ terms are absent because the origin is the centre of mass.
The series for $N = \infty$ converges outside a sphere around the origin that contains all masses.
