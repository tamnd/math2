## Summary

This topic is a part of [[model theory|F3AXVABC35]].
It covers complete types and their spaces, the realization and omission of types, and the saturated, homogeneous, universal and special models in which types are realized.
Types record what first-order formulas can say about tuples, and the models of a theory are compared by the types that they realize and omit.
The topic ends with the large saturated models that serve as fixed universes, and with the link between types and automorphisms.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Type|9V71Q3SD1G]]: the set of formulas that a tuple satisfies over a set of parameters.
2. [[Stone space of types|VDY4PG5WHN]]: the compact totally disconnected space of complete types.
3. [[Realizing types|8R31F7BQGY]]: every type is realized in an elementary extension.
4. [[Isolated type|A0VNTN725W]]: a type implied by one of its formulas, realized in every model.
5. [[Omitting types theorem|7MFGBVDVG9]]: countably many non-isolated types are omitted in some countable model.
6. [[Algebraic and definable closure|DKAGC639W9]]: the elements pinned down by a set of parameters.
7. [[Saturated model|RMTZ5J64FX]]: a model that realizes all types over sets smaller than itself.
8. [[Existence of saturated models|GSQ833HAZT]]: saturated extensions always exist, and saturated models under cardinal arithmetic or stability.
9. [[Homogeneous model|4NJS7W57VK]]: partial elementary maps extend to automorphisms.
10. [[Universal model|F30ZVR4AD5]]: every smaller model of the theory embeds elementarily.
11. [[Special model|CBNPB0QZGQ]]: unions of saturated chains, a substitute for saturated models in ZFC.
12. [[Recursively saturated model|ZGEXNRHC9M]]: realization of computable types, and resplendence.
13. [[Monster model|EYTTQZ0E1S]]: one large saturated and homogeneous model as a fixed universe.
14. [[Rigid structure|EPNZKJQHWJ]]: structures whose only automorphism is the identity.
15. [[Types as orbits|7WH24MZZQ8]]: in homogeneous models, tuples of the same type are conjugate by automorphisms.
