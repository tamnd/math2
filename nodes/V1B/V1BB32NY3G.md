## Summary

Motivic cohomology is a cohomology theory for algebraic varieties, with groups $H^p(X, \Z(q))$ indexed by two integers.
It contains the [[Chow groups|EBEF6PEK4N]] of algebraic cycles and the [[Milnor K-groups|2YXDQ7Q445]] of fields as special cases.
It plays for algebraic [[K-theory|5GVFZWMWC8]] the role that ordinary cohomology plays for topological K-theory.
Its comparison with étale cohomology, with finite coefficients, is the content of the [[norm residue isomorphism theorem|5Q5VN56QH4]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary cohomology of a space can be computed from cycles and boundaries in the space.
Motivic cohomology does the same for an algebraic variety, but it only allows cycles cut out by polynomial equations.
It has two indices: a degree and a weight.
For example, the group in degree $2q$ and weight $q$ is the group of codimension $q$ cycles up to rational equivalence.
For a field $F$, the group in degree $1$ and weight $1$ is the group $F^\times$ of nonzero elements.
