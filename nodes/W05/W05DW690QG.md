## Example

Floyd Burton Jones, a student of Robert Lee Moore, described Moore's graduate course in topology at the University of Texas.
We check his description against the features of the [[Moore method|WKM6535VHG]].

1. Selection.
   Moore chose the members of the class, excluded students who had already studied topology, and told the class not to read about the subject.
2. Axioms.
   He stated undefined terms, such as "point" and "region", and axioms that govern them, in the style of the [[axiomatic method|GD2JBXJV5Z]].
3. The list.
   He read out definitions and statements of theorems, the students copied them, and he gave no proofs.
4. Examples.
   He asked the students to construct examples showing that the hypotheses of the theorems could not be weakened or dropped.
5. Presentation.
   At the next meeting he asked a student to prove Theorem 1 at the board, and once he knew the class he called on the weaker students first.
6. Checking.
   The other students, especially those without a proof of their own, checked that the proof was correct and convincing.
7. Gaps.
   When a flaw appeared, the class waited for the presenter to repair it, and if the presenter could not, the theorem stayed open or another student tried.

Steps 3 to 7 are the defining features of the method, and all of them are present.
So the course is an instance of the method.

## Remarks

The original course has two features that later forms usually drop: the selection of the class and the ban on reading.
These belong to Moore's practice, not to the method itself, since modern courses that allow reading after a theorem is proved are still Moore courses.
Moore's subject was the part of [[general topology|DQ77PZJBHV]] built on points and regions, where a few axioms give many theorems.
It suits the method because its first theorems need few tools from outside, so students can start at once.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Moore taught topology without a textbook and without proofs on the board.
He gave the students the basic rules and a list of theorems.
The students had to find the proofs alone and show them to the class.
The class checked each proof, and a theorem stayed open until someone proved it.
Several of his students became well-known topologists.

### L2 human

To check that the course is an instance, first list the features of the method: a list of theorems, proofs by students, presentation, checking by the class and guidance only through the list.
Then match each feature with a part of Jones's description, as in steps 3 to 7.
The extra features in steps 1 and 2 do not affect the verdict.
Watch out: it is easy to treat the ban on reading as part of the method, but then most modern Moore-style courses would not count, and they do.
