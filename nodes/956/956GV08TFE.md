## Definition

Work in the untyped [[lambda calculus|BD756KR8FM]] with [[beta conversion|Y9B9CVY6SJ]] $=_\beta$.
For terms $F, X$ define $F^0\, X = X$ and $F^{n+1}\, X = F\, (F^n\, X)$.
The *Church numeral* of $n \in \N$ is the term
$$\overline{n} = \lambda f.\, \lambda x.\, f^n\, x.$$
The *Church booleans* are $\mathsf{T} = \lambda x.\, \lambda y.\, x$ and $\mathsf{F} = \lambda x.\, \lambda y.\, y$.

A function $\varphi \colon \N^k \to \N$ is *$\lambda$-definable* if some term $F$ satisfies the following.
For all $n_1, \ldots, n_k \in \N$,
$$F\, \overline{n_1} \cdots \overline{n_k} =_\beta \overline{\varphi(n_1, \ldots, n_k)}.$$

## Remarks

Example: the successor function is $\lambda$-definable by $\mathsf{S} = \lambda n.\, \lambda f.\, \lambda x.\, f\, (n\, f\, x)$.
Indeed, $\mathsf{S}\, \overline{n} \to_\beta \lambda f.\, \lambda x.\, f\, (\overline{n}\, f\, x)$, and $\overline{n}\, f\, x \twoheadrightarrow_\beta f^n\, x$, so the result is $\lambda f.\, \lambda x.\, f^{n+1}\, x = \overline{n+1}$.
Addition is defined by $\lambda m.\, \lambda n.\, \lambda f.\, \lambda x.\, m\, f\, (n\, f\, x)$ and multiplication by $\lambda m.\, \lambda n.\, \lambda f.\, m\, (n\, f)$.

Non-example: $\lambda f.\, \lambda x.\, x\, f$ is not a Church numeral.
Its body applies $x$ to $f$, not $f$ some number of times to $x$.

The conditional "if $b$ then $P$ else $Q$" is just $b\, P\, Q$, since $\mathsf{T}\, P\, Q \twoheadrightarrow_\beta P$ and $\mathsf{F}\, P\, Q \twoheadrightarrow_\beta Q$.
By results of Church and Kleene, the $\lambda$-definable functions are exactly the total computable functions.
