## Statement

Let $\ell$ be a prime and $\nu \ge 1$ an integer.
Let $X$ be a separated [[regular|ESPG0BJERZ]] [[Noetherian scheme|ND6XEY68P4]] of finite [[Krull dimension|MVGBS3J1P7]] on which $\ell$ is invertible.
Assume that the étale $\ell$-cohomological dimensions of the [[residue fields|NJ3APQ8BHH]] of $X$ are bounded by a common constant, and, if $\ell = 2$, that $\sqrt{-1} \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$.
Let $K/\ell^\nu(X)[\beta^{-1}]$ be the [[mod $\ell^\nu$ K-theory|ANJ5719F7F]] spectrum of $X$ with the Bott element $\beta$ inverted.
Then:

1. The presheaf $U \mapsto K/\ell^\nu(U)[\beta^{-1}]$ satisfies étale descent on $X$: the natural map $K/\ell^\nu(X)[\beta^{-1}] \to \mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{et}}(X; K/\ell^\nu[\beta^{-1}])$ is a weak equivalence.
2. There is a strongly convergent spectral sequence
$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p_{\mathrm{et}}(X; \Z/\ell^\nu(q/2)) \Longrightarrow \pi_{q-p} \big( K/\ell^\nu(X)[\beta^{-1}] \big),$$
where $\Z/\ell^\nu(q/2)$ is the Tate twist $\mu_{\ell^\nu}^{\otimes q/2}$ for even $q \in \Z$ and $0$ for odd $q$.

## Remarks

Assume that $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ contains a primitive $\ell^\nu$-th [[root of unity|0YV2CKP2AV]] $\zeta$.
Then the Bott element is a class $\beta \in K_2(X; \Z/\ell^\nu)$ that maps to $\zeta$ in the $\ell^\nu$-torsion of $K_1(X)$.
In general only a suitable power of $\beta$ is defined, and that power is inverted.
Inverting $\beta$ makes mod $\ell^\nu$ K-theory periodic of period $2$, like topological K-theory.
Thomason first proved the theorem for schemes with an ample family of line bundles; the [[Thomason–Trobaugh K-theory|ECFW7KP0SX]] of perfect complexes removed this assumption.

At work: for $X = \operatorname{Spec} k$ with $k$ separably closed, the $E_2$ term is concentrated in $p = 0$, and the theorem gives $\pi_n(K/\ell^\nu(k)[\beta^{-1}]) \cong \Z/\ell^\nu$ for even $n$ and $0$ for odd $n$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Algebraic K-theory modulo $\ell^\nu$ is hard to compute directly.
After one inverts a special element, the Bott element, it becomes a theory that can be glued from étale pieces.
Then it is computed by étale cohomology, which is much better understood.

### L2 human

The theorem identifies Bott-inverted K-theory with [[étale K-theory|CC2HWX9X2Y]] in the sense of Dwyer and Friedlander, and gives its spectral sequence from étale cohomology.
The remaining question is how far the map from $K/\ell^\nu$ to its Bott localization is an isomorphism.
The Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture predicts an isomorphism in degrees above the étale cohomological dimension, and it follows from the Bloch–Kato conjecture proved by Voevodsky and Rost.
