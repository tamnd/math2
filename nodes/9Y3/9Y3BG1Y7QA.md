## Example

The hexagon $O_6$ has six elements $0, a, b, a^\perp, b^\perp, 1$.
Its order is generated by $0 < a < b < 1$ and $0 < b^\perp < a^\perp < 1$; elements of different chains, other than $0$ and $1$, are incomparable.
The map $\perp$ sends $0 \mapsto 1$, $1 \mapsto 0$, $a \mapsto a^\perp$, $a^\perp \mapsto a$, $b \mapsto b^\perp$ and $b^\perp \mapsto b$.

Claim 1: $O_6$ is an [[orthocomplemented lattice|J7KN14DYV3]].

1. Lattice. Two elements of one chain have their minimum and maximum as meet and join. For $x \in \set{a, b}$ and $y \in \set{a^\perp, b^\perp}$, the only common lower bound is $0$ and the only common upper bound is $1$. So $x \wedge y = 0$ and $x \vee y = 1$.
2. Complement. By step 1, $a \wedge a^\perp = 0$, $a \vee a^\perp = 1$, $b \wedge b^\perp = 0$ and $b \vee b^\perp = 1$. The pair $0, 1$ is complementary as well.
3. Involution. Applying $\perp$ twice gives the identity, by the table.
4. Order reversal. The relation $a \leq b$ goes to $b^\perp \leq a^\perp$, and $b^\perp \leq a^\perp$ goes back to $a \leq b$. The relations $0 \leq x \leq 1$ also reverse, because $\perp$ swaps $0$ and $1$.

Claim 2: $O_6$ is not an [[orthomodular lattice|6FPKEFX0X4]].

5. The orthomodular law says: if $x \leq y$, then $y = x \vee (x^\perp \wedge y)$.
6. Take $x = a$ and $y = b$, so $x \leq y$.
7. By step 1, $a^\perp \wedge b = 0$. So $a \vee (a^\perp \wedge b) = a \vee 0 = a$.
8. But $a \neq b$. So the law fails.

So $O_6$ is not a [[quantum logic|TWEYQ0XAY4]] in the abstract sense of [[quantum logic|2E9DMB7A6Q]].

## Remarks

In a closed subspace lattice the orthomodular law holds: if $M \subseteq N$, then $N = M \vee (M^\perp \cap N)$.
In $O_6$ the element $b$ has no part orthogonal to $a$ that fills the gap between $a$ and $b$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The hexagon is a small picture with two chains from bottom to top, each with two middle points.
"Not" swaps the two chains and reverses each.
It obeys all the rules of an orthocomplement, but it fails the rule that a larger proposition splits into a smaller one and its orthogonal remainder.

### L2 human

In a Hilbert space, a subspace $N$ that contains $M$ is $M$ plus the vectors of $N$ orthogonal to $M$.
In $O_6$, the meet of $a^\perp$ and $b$ is $0$, so this splitting gives back only $a$.
A trap is to think that every orthocomplemented lattice can model quantum propositions; the orthomodular law is an extra condition.
