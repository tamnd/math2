## Statement

Deciding whether an LTL formula can be met at all, and whether a finite system meets it, takes polynomial space, and no less in the worst case.

Consider formulas of [[linear temporal logic|RMJ56ZPA63]] with $X$ and $U$, and finite [[Kripke structures|RFQ8DJZNQT]] $M$ with a total transition relation.
A state $s$ satisfies $\varphi$ in the universal sense of [[the definition|8S64VZ6A1S]]: every infinite path from $s$ satisfies $\varphi$.

1. The satisfiability problem, "given $\varphi$, is there an infinite word $w$ with $w \models \varphi$?", is [[PSPACE-complete|JWC9CE07Q2]].
2. The model checking problem, "given $M$, a state $s$ and $\varphi$, does $s$ satisfy $\varphi$?", is PSPACE-complete.
3. Model checking can be done in time $\lvert M \rvert \cdot 2^{O(\lvert\varphi\rvert)}$, where $\lvert M \rvert$ is the number of states plus the number of edges.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The operator $X$ carries the lower bound.
The proof uses $X$ iterated polynomially often to compare a tape cell with the same cell of the next configuration.
For the fragment with only $F$ and its dual $G$, Sistla and Clarke showed that satisfiability is NP-complete, so the full strength of the theorem needs a richer language.

The transition relation is total so that every state has an infinite path.
Otherwise a dead-end state satisfies every formula vacuously, even $\bot$, and the problem changes.
The structure is finite so that it can be an input, and its size enters the time bound of part 3.

### What it gives you

Validity of LTL formulas is also PSPACE-complete, because $\varphi$ is valid exactly when $\neg\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, and PSPACE is closed under complement.
For a fixed formula, model checking is linear in the size of the structure.
This is why LTL model checking works on large systems with short specifications.
On the other side, an algorithm polynomial in both $\lvert M \rvert$ and $\lvert\varphi\rvert$ would give $\mathrm{P} = \mathrm{PSPACE}$, by part 2.

### Generalizations

Adding past operators keeps both problems PSPACE-complete.
For [[CTL*|Y4XZ94GRXV]], model checking is still PSPACE-complete, but satisfiability becomes 2EXPTIME-complete.
For [[CTL|F6JA5C5BJX]], model checking takes polynomial time, by [[the CTL model checking theorem|1A990HC0FJ]].

## History

Sistla and Clarke proved that satisfiability and model checking are PSPACE-complete in "The complexity of propositional linear temporal logics", Journal of the ACM, 1985.
Lichtenstein and Pnueli observed in 1985 that the time is linear in the size of the structure.
The automata-theoretic proof of the upper bound is due to Vardi and Wolper.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A computer can decide whether an LTL requirement can be met at all, and whether a finite system meets it.
The memory it needs grows only polynomially with the formula.
The time can grow exponentially with the formula, but only linearly with the system.
So long formulas are costly, while large systems are manageable.

### L2 human

Let us see where the two sizes enter, for a structure $M$ with $10^6$ states and the formula $\varphi = G(\mathit{req} \to F\,\mathit{grant})$.
The negation is $\neg\varphi \equiv F(\mathit{req} \wedge G\neg\mathit{grant})$, and an automaton for it needs only a few states, whatever $M$ is.
The product of $M$ with this automaton has a few million states.
A search for a reachable accepting cycle in the product takes time linear in its size, so the check is fast.
Watch out: PSPACE-hardness does not mean that every formula is hard; it is a statement about the worst case over all formulas.

### L3 human

The upper bound comes from [[the translation into Büchi automata|BYMYN6GN8E]] and an on-the-fly search that never stores the whole automaton.
The lower bound encodes a Turing machine with polynomial space, with $X$ iterated to reach the same cell in the next configuration.
For CTL the structure and the formula are processed together, subformula by subformula, and that explains its polynomial bound.
