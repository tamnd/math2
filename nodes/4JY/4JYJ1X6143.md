## Statement

Let a system undergo a [[thermodynamic cycle|AGRGCDWQF8]].
Suppose that it takes in small amounts of [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] $\delta Q$ from reservoirs at [[thermodynamic temperature|AJ8M9MGZGF]] $T$, with $\delta Q < 0$ when heat is given out.
Then
$$\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} \le 0.$$
If the cycle is [[reversible|F3DHZ45H6A]], then equality holds.

## Remarks

Split the cycle into small steps, where step $i$ takes heat $\delta Q_i$ at temperature $T_i$.
Supply each $\delta Q_i$ through a reversible [[Carnot engine|2DF8JBNKNG]] that runs between one fixed reservoir at temperature $T_0$ and temperature $T_i$.
That engine draws heat $T_0 \, \delta Q_i / T_i$ from the fixed reservoir.
The system and the engines together run in a cycle whose only effect is to take heat $T_0 \sum_i \delta Q_i / T_i$ from one reservoir and turn it into work.
The [[Kelvin–Planck statement|RMNJ00XP8X]] forces this to be at most $0$; for a reversible cycle, running it backwards gives the opposite inequality as well.
As a consequence, $\int \delta Q / T$ along a reversible path depends only on its end points; this is how entropy is defined.
For a Carnot cycle that takes $Q_h > 0$ at $T_h$ and gives out $Q_c > 0$ at $T_c$, equality reads $Q_h / T_h = Q_c / T_c$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Over any closed cycle, add up each bit of heat taken in divided by the temperature at which it came in.
The total is never positive.
It is exactly zero when the cycle could just as well be run backwards.

### L2 human

The theorem is the bridge from the classical second law to entropy.
Equality for reversible cycles shows that $\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}} / T$ is the differential of a state function, the entropy $S$.
The inequality for irreversible processes then gives the Clausius inequality $dS \ge \delta Q / T$.
