## Summary

The planetary boundary layer is the lowest part of the atmosphere, in which the flow is directly affected by contact with the Earth's surface.
It is usually [[turbulent|VY307F8Z0S]], and it exchanges momentum, heat and moisture between the surface and the free atmosphere above.
Its depth varies from about a hundred metres on calm nights to one or two kilometres or more on sunny days.
It is the atmospheric example of a [[boundary layer|YCFPD0GE5G]], with rotation and stratification added.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The air close to the ground feels friction and the heating or cooling of the ground; the air higher up hardly does.
The planetary boundary layer is the lowest layer, in which these surface effects are felt within an hour or so.
On a sunny day, warm bubbles rising from the ground stir this layer well, and smoke from a chimney spreads through it quickly.
On a clear night the ground cools, the layer becomes shallow and calm, and fog or pollution can be trapped near the ground.
Above it, the wind blows more freely and is close to geostrophic.
