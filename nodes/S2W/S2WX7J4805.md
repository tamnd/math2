## Statement

Consider a closed network of $M$ queues, numbered $1, \ldots, M$, in which a fixed number $K \ge 1$ of customers circulate and no customer enters or leaves.
Each queue $i$ has one server, first-come first-served discipline, and [[exponential|0E99F51GVB]] service times of rate $\mu_i > 0$, all independent.
After service at queue $i$, a customer moves to queue $j$ with probability $p_{ij}$, independently of everything else, where $P = (p_{ij})$ is an irreducible stochastic matrix.
Let $(e_1, \ldots, e_M)$ be a solution of $e_j = \sum_{i=1}^{M} e_i p_{ij}$ with all $e_i > 0$.
Let $S_K = \set{(n_1, \ldots, n_M) : n_i \ge 0, \; n_1 + \cdots + n_M = K}$.
Then the vector $(n_1, \ldots, n_M)$ of queue lengths is a [[continuous-time Markov chain|TWQFE0FZZS]] on $S_K$, with [[stationary distribution|0KRH5157WP]]
$$\pi(n_1, \ldots, n_M) = \frac{1}{G(K)} \prod_{i=1}^{M} \left( \frac{e_i}{\mu_i} \right)^{n_i},$$
where the normalising constant is
$$G(K) = \sum_{(n_1, \ldots, n_M) \in S_K} \prod_{i=1}^{M} \left( \frac{e_i}{\mu_i} \right)^{n_i}.$$

## Remarks

The vector $e$ is unique up to a positive factor, since $P$ is irreducible, and the formula does not depend on this factor.
The proof checks that $\pi$ satisfies the global balance equations, which reduce to the equations $e = eP$ for the visit ratios.
For two queues in a cycle, $p_{12} = p_{21} = 1$, one may take $e_1 = e_2 = 1$; then $\pi(n, K - n)$ is proportional to $(\mu_2 / \mu_1)^n$ for $n = 0, \ldots, K$.
Unlike in an open [[Jackson network|NG6FXQB0Y5]], the queue lengths are not independent, because they sum to $K$.
The constant $G(K)$ has a number of terms that grows fast with $M$ and $K$; Buzen's algorithm computes it by a recursion.
No stability condition is needed, because the state space $S_K$ is finite.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a fixed number of jobs moving forever between machines, such as a fixed pool of pallets in a factory.
The theorem says that the long-run chance of each arrangement of jobs is a simple product of one factor per machine.
Each factor measures how loaded the machine is: how often jobs visit it, divided by how fast it works.
The only complication is the normalising constant that makes all probabilities sum to $1$.

### L2 human

The theorem is the closed counterpart of Jackson's theorem for open networks.
The product form is the same, but the constraint $n_1 + \cdots + n_M = K$ couples the queues through $G(K)$.
Throughputs follow from ratios of normalising constants, for example the throughput of queue $i$ is $e_i \, G(K - 1) / G(K)$.
Mean value analysis gives the same performance measures without computing $G(K)$.
The theorem is a special case of the [[BCMP network|WVNP7RQQ9D]] theorem.
