## Definition

Let $P$ be a station at height $h \ge 0$ above sea level, with [[free-air anomaly|WDMHD6BW8Y]] $\Delta g_{\mathrm{FA}}$.
Let $G$ be the gravitational constant and let $\rho$ be an assumed constant density of the rock between $P$ and sea level.
The *simple Bouguer anomaly* at $P$ is
$$\Delta g_{\mathrm{B}} = \Delta g_{\mathrm{FA}} - 2\pi G \rho h.$$
The term $2\pi G\rho h$ is the Bouguer correction.
It is the attraction of an infinite horizontal slab of thickness $h$ and density $\rho$ on a point of its upper face.
The *complete Bouguer anomaly* also includes a terrain correction for the departure of the real topography from the slab.

## Remarks

Example: with the usual density $\rho = 2670\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $2\pi G\rho \approx 1.12 \cdot 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{s^{-2}}$, that is $0.1119$ mGal per metre.
A station at $h = 1000$ m with $\Delta g_{\mathrm{FA}} = 20$ mGal has $\Delta g_{\mathrm{B}} \approx 20 - 111.9 = -91.9$ mGal.
Non-example: subtracting the attraction of a ball of rock instead of a slab gives a different reduction, not the Bouguer correction.
At a land station at sea level, $h = 0$, the Bouguer and free-air anomalies agree.
The Bouguer anomaly is a [[gravity anomaly|J6V1B7SP0Q]] in the loose geophysical sense.
