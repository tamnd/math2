## Summary

The event horizon of a [[black hole|YHJ0PDNY8T]] is the boundary between the events that can send signals to far-away observers and the events that cannot.
It is a null hypersurface: it is made of light rays that hover at the edge, neither escaping nor falling in.
For the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]] it is the sphere $r = 2m$; for the [[Kerr metric|X3FZC85THY]] it is $r = r_+$.
Assuming the null energy condition, its area never decreases, by the [[Hawking area theorem|42EQ4G6978]], which links black holes with thermodynamics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The event horizon is the point of no return around a black hole.
Anything that crosses it, including light, can never come back out or send a message out.
Nothing special happens locally when a falling observer crosses it; it is not a surface of matter but a boundary in spacetime.
A light ray pointed straight outwards exactly on the horizon stays on it forever, like a swimmer going upstream at exactly the speed of the current.
Because it is defined by what can reach infinity in the far future, one cannot locate the horizon without knowing the whole future history.
