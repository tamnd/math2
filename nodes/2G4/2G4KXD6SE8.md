## Summary

The electrostatic energy of a system of [[charges|6XPSV47R56]] is the work needed to assemble it from charges that start infinitely far apart.
For point charges it is a sum over pairs of Coulomb terms.
For a continuous distribution it can be written through the [[electric potential|SAEDXNM3Y1]] or as an integral of the square of the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]].
Equilibria of charges are critical points of this energy, as in the [[Thomson problem|3C7V5X5FY8]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pushing two like charges together takes work, because they repel.
That work is stored as energy, which comes back if the charges are let go and fly apart.
The electrostatic energy is the total stored work for the whole arrangement.
One can also think of the energy as living in the field itself, with energy density proportional to the square of the field strength.
