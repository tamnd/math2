## Statement

Let $k$ be a field and let $X$ be a smooth [[variety|HV6YBS0YDH]] over $k$.
Write $H^i(X, \Z(j))$ for the [[motivic cohomology|V1BB32NY3G]] of $X$ and $K_n(X)$ for its [[algebraic K-groups|Y2467453Q5]].
Then there is a strongly convergent [[spectral sequence|GG8AYV847V]]
$$E_2^{p, q} = H^{p - q}(X, \Z(-q)) \Longrightarrow K_{-p-q}(X).$$

## Remarks

The spectral sequence is the motivic analogue of the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence from ordinary cohomology to topological K-theory.
For a field it was constructed by Bloch and Lichtenbaum, as the [[Bloch–Lichtenbaum spectral sequence|6E0DCB1NF1]]; Friedlander and Suslin extended it to smooth varieties, and Levine gave another construction.
Rationally the spectral sequence degenerates, and $K_n(X) \otimes \Q \cong \bigoplus_q H^{2q - n}(X, \Q(q))$, where the summands are eigenspaces of Adams operations.
Example: the term $E_2^{q, -q} = H^{2q}(X, \Z(q)) = CH^q(X)$ contributes to $K_0(X)$, which links K-theory to the [[Chow groups|EBEF6PEK4N]].
For $X = \operatorname{Spec} F$, the term $E_2^{0, -n} = H^n(F, \Z(n)) \cong K^M_n(F)$ contributes to $K_n(F)$, which gives the map from Milnor K-theory to Quillen K-theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

K-groups of a variety are hard to compute, while motivic cohomology groups are closer to geometry: they are built from algebraic cycles.
The spectral sequence is a machine that computes K-theory from motivic cohomology, one layer at a time.
Up to some correction steps, each K-group is assembled from motivic cohomology groups of different weights.
After tensoring with the rational numbers, no correction is needed at all.

### L2 human

The spectral sequence is the main bridge between algebraic K-theory and arithmetic.
With finite coefficients and the norm residue isomorphism theorem, it gives the Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture, and so a computation of K-groups of number rings in terms of étale cohomology.
Its rational degeneration gives $K_0(X) \otimes \Q \cong \bigoplus_q CH^q(X) \otimes \Q$, a result going back to Grothendieck's Riemann–Roch theory.
