## Summary

Quicksort is a [[divide-and-conquer|G9NFJ55KCX]] [[sorting algorithm|MGKDQAM7K3]].
It picks a pivot element, partitions the other elements into those smaller and those not smaller than the pivot, and sorts the two parts recursively.
Its worst case is $\Theta(n^2)$ comparisons, but with a uniformly random pivot the expected number is about $2 n \ln n \approx 1.39\, n \log_2 n$, and it is very fast in practice.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pick one item, the pivot, and split the rest into a pile of smaller items and a pile of larger items.
Now the pivot is already in its final place, between the two piles, and each pile can be sorted the same way.
To sort $(3, 5, 1, 4, 2)$ with pivot $3$: the piles are $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 4)$, which sort to $(1, 2)$ and $(4, 5)$, giving $(1, 2, 3, 4, 5)$.
If the pivot is always the smallest item, one pile is empty each time and the method becomes slow.
