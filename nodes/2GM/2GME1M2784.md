## Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $X = I_2$.
We test the four Penrose conditions of the [[Moore-Penrose inverse|0PSRCMCM5X]] one at a time.

1. Condition 1 holds. Since $X = I_2$, we get $A X A = A^2$, and $A^2 = A$ because the diagonal entries $1$ and $0$ are equal to their squares.
2. Condition 3 holds, because $A X = A$ is real and symmetric.
3. Condition 4 holds, because $X A = A$ is real and symmetric.
4. Condition 2 fails, because $X A X = A$, and $A \ne I_2 = X$ since the entries in position $(2, 2)$ are $0$ and $1$.
5. So $X = I_2$ is not the Moore-Penrose inverse of $A$.
6. The pseudoinverse is $A^+ = A$. Check: with $X = A$, both $A X A$ and $X A X$ equal $A^3 = A$, which gives conditions 1 and 2, and $A X = X A = A^2 = A$ is symmetric, which gives conditions 3 and 4.

## Remarks

The example shows that the second condition carries real information.
Since $A X A = A$, the identity is a generalized inverse of $A$, and so is every matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix}$, because $A X A$ only sees the entry in position $(1, 1)$.
Such matrices are far from unique, and conditions 3 and 4 alone do not cut them down to one: they force $s = t = 0$ but leave $u$ free.
Condition 2 then forces $u = 0$, because $X A X = \operatorname{diag}(1, 0)$ for every such $X$ with $s = t = 0$.

The meaning is geometric.
The matrix $A$ keeps the first coordinate and kills the second, so the second coordinate axis is the orthogonal complement of the column space.
The identity acts as the identity on that axis, while $A^+$ must send it to zero.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The matrix $A$ keeps the first coordinate of a vector and turns the second coordinate into zero.
The identity matrix undoes the first part, but it leaves the second coordinate alone.
The pseudoinverse also turns the second coordinate into zero, so it is $A$ itself.

### L2 human

To test a candidate pseudoinverse, you must check all four conditions, and here three of them hold while one fails.
The quickest test is to compute $A X$ and $X A$ first, since all four conditions use them.
Here both products are $A$, so conditions 3 and 4 hold, and $A X A = A$ holds, but $X A X = A$ is not $X$.
Watch out: a matrix that inverts $A$ where it can is not yet the pseudoinverse; it must also be zero on the orthogonal complement of the column space of $A$.
