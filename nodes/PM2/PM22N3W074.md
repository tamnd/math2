## Definition

*Computational fluid dynamics* (CFD) is the part of [[numerical analysis|B6E54YTR64]] that approximates solutions of the equations of fluid mechanics.
It treats a problem, such as one for the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]], by the following steps:

1. choose a model: a system of equations with boundary and initial conditions on a domain $\Omega$ and a time interval $[0, T]$;
2. discretise it: replace $\Omega$ by a mesh or a set of particles with size $h > 0$, and $[0, T]$ by time steps $\Delta t$.
   Replace the equations by a finite algebraic system, for example by [[finite differences|X7HYEBA5SA]], [[finite volumes|HGKN8E09RS]] or [[finite elements|98GZ9DVHTA]];
3. solve the algebraic system on a computer;
4. check that the discrete solution converges to the solution of the model as $h, \Delta t \to 0$, or estimate its error.

## Remarks

Example: for the advection equation $\partial_t u + a \, \partial_x u = 0$ with $a > 0$, the upwind scheme on a grid $x_j = j h$, $t_n = n \Delta t$ is
$$u_j^{n+1} = u_j^n - \frac{a \Delta t}{h} (u_j^n - u_{j-1}^n).$$
When $0 \le a \Delta t / h \le 1$, the new value is a convex combination of $u_j^n$ and $u_{j-1}^n$, so $\max_j \abs{u_j^n}$ does not grow; this is the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition, and the scheme converges.
If $a \Delta t / h > 1$, the coefficient of $u_j^n$ is negative and errors grow, so the scheme is unstable.
Non-example: the exact parabolic profile of Poiseuille flow, found by hand, is analytic fluid dynamics, not CFD, since nothing is discretised.
By the Lax equivalence theorem, a consistent linear scheme for a well-posed linear problem converges if and only if it is stable.
