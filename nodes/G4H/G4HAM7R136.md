## Example

Take the planar [[three-body problem|JE1Z920QKT]] with three equal masses.
There are a period $T > 0$ and a closed curve $x \colon \R \to \R^2$ of period $T$, shaped like the figure eight, with this property.
The motion $x_1(t) = x(t)$, $x_2(t) = x(t + T/3)$, $x_3(t) = x(t + 2T/3)$ is a solution.
It is a [[periodic orbit|0AA97XA70K]].
So the three bodies chase each other along the same curve, each one third of a period behind the next.
The [[centre of mass|0HNK6KT05K]] stays at the crossing point of the eight, and the total [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] is zero.
At some instants the three bodies are collinear, with one body at the midpoint of the other two.

Check of the period: since $x$ has period $T$, each $x_i$ has period $T$, so the whole motion repeats after time $T$.

## Remarks

Moore found this orbit numerically in 1993.
Chenciner and Montgomery proved its existence in 2000.
They minimised the action among loops with the symmetries of the eight and showed that the minimiser has no collisions.
A solution in which all bodies follow one curve with equal time shifts is called a choreography.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Three equal stars can dance along one figure-eight path forever.
They follow each other around the curve, evenly spaced in time.
Unlike the older periodic solutions of Euler and Lagrange, the triangle formed by the three bodies keeps changing its shape.
