## Example

Let $K_{1,3}$ be the [[star|NJB31J8DZT]] with a center $c$, three leaves $a_1, a_2, a_3$, and the three edges $c a_1$, $c a_2$ and $c a_3$.
We test two false conclusions that students often draw from an average, and the one true conclusion.

1. The [[degrees|0KS0KRDV4Y]] are $d(c) = 3$ and $d(a_1) = d(a_2) = d(a_3) = 1$.
2. The degree sum is $3 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6 = 2 \cdot 3$, as the [[handshaking lemma|0XAP8WHNES]] says for $3$ edges.
3. The average degree is
   $$\frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}.$$
4. False conclusion 1 is "every vertex has degree at least $3/2$".
   The leaf $a_1$ has degree $1 < 3/2$.
5. False conclusion 2 is "some vertex has degree exactly $3/2$".
   Every degree is an integer, and $3/2$ is not.
6. The true conclusions of the [[averaging argument|HXP9VMXV8S]] hold.
   The center has degree $3 \ge 3/2$, and each leaf has degree $1 \le 3/2$.

## Remarks

The star shows the exact content of an averaging argument.
The average gives one element on each side of it, and nothing about the other elements.
An argument that claims more is not an averaging argument, even when it starts with an average.

The picture is the same for every star.
The star $K_{1,r}$ with $r \ge 2$ leaves has degree sum $2r$ and average degree
$$\frac{2r}{r + 1} = 2 - \frac{2}{r + 1},$$
which lies strictly between $1$ and $2$.
So no vertex has the average degree, and all $r$ leaves are below it.
For $r = 1$ the star is a single edge, both degrees are $1$, and the average $1$ is attained.
In general the average is attained by every vertex only when all the values are equal, as in a regular graph.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A star has one center joined to three outer points.
The center has three lines and each outer point has one line, so the average is one and a half.
No point has one and a half lines, and three of the four points are below the average.
The average only tells you that some point is at least as large as it, here the center, and that some point is at most as large, here any outer point.

### L2 human

Here is a wrong proof with a hidden false step.
The claim is "in a graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, every vertex has degree at least $2m/n$".
The proof says: "the average degree is $2m/n$, so each vertex has degree at least $2m/n$."
The step from "the average is $2m/n$" to "each value is at least $2m/n$" has no reason.
The star refutes the claim, because for $K_{1,3}$
$$\frac{2m}{n} = \frac{2 \cdot 3}{4} = \frac{3}{2},$$
and a leaf has degree $1$.

The correct statement is "some vertex has degree at least $2m/n$".
Watch out for the minimum degree: it can be far below the average, and a lower bound on it needs a different argument.
