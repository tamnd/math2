## Example

A *Stokes wave* is a periodic travelling wave of permanent form that solves the full nonlinear [[water wave problem|R9WC0Z7Z69]].
Stokes constructed it as a power series in the wave steepness $ka$, where $a$ is the amplitude and $k$ the wavenumber.
In deep water, with $\theta = kx - \omega t$, the surface elevation to second order is
$$\eta = a\cos\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}ka^2\cos(2\theta) + O(k^2a^3),$$
and the angular frequency to third order is
$$\omega = \sqrt{gk}\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{2}k^2a^2\right) + O(k^3a^3).$$
The first term of $\eta$ is the linear wave of [[Airy wave theory|CNCGSCVQB8]].
The second harmonic makes the crests sharper and the troughs flatter.
The frequency now depends on the amplitude, so steeper waves travel faster than the [[linear dispersion relation|JT5EGBG2ZV]] predicts.

## Remarks

The existence of small-amplitude Stokes waves as exact solutions was proved in the 1920s, by Nekrasov and by Levi-Civita for deep water and by Struik for finite depth.
As the height grows, the family ends at a wave of greatest height whose crest is a corner of interior angle $120^\circ$, as Stokes predicted.
Amick, Fraenkel and Toland proved this Stokes conjecture in 1982.
Stokes waves on deep water are unstable to slow modulations of their amplitude, the Benjamin–Feir instability.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Real waves at sea are not perfect sine curves: their crests are pointed and their troughs are broad and flat.
Stokes found how to correct the simple sine wave, step by step, to account for this.
He also found that bigger waves of the same length travel a little faster.
There is a limit to how steep such a wave can be; the steepest one has a sharp crest with an angle of $120$ degrees.
