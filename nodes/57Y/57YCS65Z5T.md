## Statement

Let a simple system have internal energy $U(S, V, N_1, \dots, N_r)$, a differentiable and [[extensive|H9F9CNPBSN]] function of its [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]] $S$, volume $V$ and amounts of substance $N_1, \dots, N_r$.
Let $T$, $p$ and $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_r$ be its temperature, pressure and [[chemical potentials|WVH4PVW64P]].
Then for any change between neighbouring equilibrium states,
$$S \, dT - V \, dp + \sum_{i=1}^{r} N_i \, d\mu_i = 0.$$

## Remarks

Differentiate the [[Euler relation|B6DK7G8K3G]] $U = TS - pV + \sum_i \mu_i N_i$.
This gives $dU = T \, dS + S \, dT - p \, dV - V \, dp + \sum_i (\mu_i \, dN_i + N_i \, d\mu_i)$.
Subtract the [[fundamental relation|41NHDR6MH6]] $dU = T \, dS - p \, dV + \sum_i \mu_i \, dN_i$ to get the equation.
Example: for one substance, dividing by $N$ gives $d\mu = -s \, dT + v \, dp$, where $s = S/N$ and $v = V/N$.
So at constant temperature, $(\partial \mu / \partial p)_T = v$.
For a two-component mixture at constant $T$ and $p$, it gives $N_1 \, d\mu_1 + N_2 \, d\mu_2 = 0$, so the two chemical potentials cannot change independently.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The intensive quantities of a system, such as temperature, pressure and chemical potentials, cannot all be changed freely.
Once all but one are fixed, the last one is fixed too.

### L2 human

For a system with $r$ substances there are $r + 2$ intensive variables, but only $r + 1$ of them are independent.
This count underlies the Gibbs phase rule.
In chemistry the equation relates the activities of the components of a mixture, so one can be computed from measurements of the others.
