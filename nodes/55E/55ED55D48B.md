## Example

Take the sentence "if $n$ is even and $n$ is a square, then $n$ is divisible by $4$".
With $p$ for "$n$ is even", $q$ for "$n$ is a square" and $r$ for "$n$ is divisible by $4$", its form is the formula $\varphi = (p \land q) \to r$.
We use the [[principle of bivalence|5DYNDW9526]] to list every case for $\varphi$ and then compute its [[truth table|05RSK4Q657]].

1. By bivalence, each of $p$, $q$ and $r$ has exactly one of the values T and F.
2. The three choices are independent, so there are $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 2^3 = 8$ valuations.
   In the standard order they are TTT, TTF, TFT, TFF, FTT, FTF, FFT and FFF.
3. In each row we compute the [[conjunction|025YZN3VJW]] $p \land q$ first.
   Then we compute the [[implication|0NMG7W7Q11]] $(p \land q) \to r$, which is F only when $p \land q$ is T and $r$ is F.

| $p$ | $q$ | $r$ | $p \land q$ | $(p \land q) \to r$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | T | T | T |
| T | T | F | T | F |
| T | F | T | F | T |
| T | F | F | F | T |
| F | T | T | F | T |
| F | T | F | F | T |
| F | F | T | F | T |
| F | F | F | F | T |

4. The formula is F in exactly one row, the row TTF.
5. The $8$ rows cover every case, so $\varphi$ is false exactly when $p$ and $q$ are true and $r$ is false.

## Remarks

Bivalence fixes the list of cases in the truth-table method.
Without it, step 5 would not follow, because a case outside the table could exist.
For the sentence about $n$, the bad row TTF never happens: an even square is divisible by $4$, so the implication is true for every whole number $n$.

With $n$ variables the same count gives $2^n$ rows: $16$ rows for $4$ variables and $1024$ rows for $10$ variables.
With three truth values, three variables would give $3^3 = 27$ rows, and every connective would need a new table for the third value.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each of three light switches can be on or off.
The first switch has $2$ positions, each of them goes with $2$ positions of the second, and each of those with $2$ of the third.
So there are $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$ ways to set the three switches.
A truth table with three letters lists these $8$ ways, because each letter is either true or false.

### L2 human

Count the rows for $(p \lor q) \land \neg(r \land s)$ and check one of them.
The formula has the $4$ variables $p$, $q$, $r$ and $s$, so it needs $2^4 = 16$ rows.
Take the row where $p$ is F and $q$, $r$, $s$ are T.
Then $p \lor q$ is T because $q$ is T, and $r \land s$ is T, so $\neg(r \land s)$ is F.
The whole formula is F in this row, because its second part is F.

Count the rows before you read a result off a table, so that you know the table is complete.
Watch out: a variable counts once however often it occurs.
The formula $(p \land q) \lor (\neg q \land p)$ has $2$ variables and needs $4$ rows, not $16$.
