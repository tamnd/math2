## Summary

The cable equation is a linear [[partial differential equation|GC5BDZ6QBC]] for the membrane potential along a thin cylindrical nerve fibre.
It applies to fibres with a passive membrane, such as many dendrites.
It combines current flow along the core of the fibre with leakage and charging through the membrane.
Its two constants, the length constant and the time constant, say how far and how fast a voltage signal spreads before it fades.
It is closely related to the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]] and was first derived for telegraph cables.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a long leaky garden hose: water pushed in at one end flows along it but escapes through small holes in the wall.
A dendrite is like that for electric current: current flows along the inside and leaks out through the membrane.
So a voltage made at one point gets weaker with distance.
For a steady input in a long fibre the voltage falls off exponentially, dropping by a factor $e$ over each length constant $\lambda$.
