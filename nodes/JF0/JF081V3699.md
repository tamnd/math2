## Example

Forbid a block of ones in longer and longer blocks, and the forbidden parts shrink fast enough that more than half of all strings survive.
We give this tree explicitly and compute its levels, to check that it has positive measure in the sense of [[WWKL0|KPHM3025GW]].
Split $\mathbb{N}$ into consecutive blocks $B_1, B_2, B_3, \dots$, where $B_i$ has $i + 1$ elements and starts at $(i - 1)(i + 2)/2$.
So $B_1 = \{0, 1\}$, $B_2 = \{2, 3, 4\}$, $B_3 = \{5, 6, 7, 8\}$, and $B_4$ starts at $9$.
Let $T$ be the set of strings $\sigma$ such that no block $B_i \subseteq \{0, \dots, \lvert \sigma \rvert - 1\}$ has $\sigma(j) = 1$ for all $j \in B_i$.

### The tree and its levels

1. $T$ is computable, since for a given $\sigma$ only the finitely many blocks inside $\{0, \dots, \lvert \sigma \rvert - 1\}$ need checking.
2. $T$ is a tree.
   If $\rho \preceq \sigma$, every block inside $\{0, \dots, \lvert \rho \rvert - 1\}$ is also inside $\{0, \dots, \lvert \sigma \rvert - 1\}$, and $\rho$ and $\sigma$ agree on it.
   So a block of ones in $\rho$ is also a block of ones in $\sigma$.
3. Fix $n$ and let $B_1, \dots, B_m$ be the blocks inside $\{0, \dots, n - 1\}$.
   A string of length $n$ is in $T$ if and only if, on each $B_i$ with $i \le m$, it takes one of the $2^{i+1} - 1$ values other than all ones.
   The other positions are free.
4. So $\lvert T_n \rvert / 2^n = \prod_{i=1}^{m} (1 - 2^{-(i+1)})$.
5. For example, $\lvert T_2 \rvert = 3$, $\lvert T_5 \rvert = 3 \cdot 7 = 21$, and $\lvert T_9 \rvert = 3 \cdot 7 \cdot 15 = 315$.
   The ratios are $\tfrac{3}{4}$, $\tfrac{21}{32}$ and $\tfrac{315}{512} \approx 0.615$.

### A lower bound for every level

6. For numbers $a_1, \dots, a_m$ in $[0, 1]$, induction on $m$ gives $\prod_{i=1}^{m} (1 - a_i) \ge 1 - \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i$.
7. With $a_i = 2^{-(i+1)}$, the sum is less than $\sum_{i \ge 1} 2^{-(i+1)} = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
   So $\lvert T_n \rvert / 2^n > \tfrac{1}{2}$ for every $n$.
8. So $T$ has positive measure with $q = \tfrac{1}{2}$, and it meets the hypothesis of [[weak weak König's lemma|BJQ77Y2QFX]].
9. The sequence $0, 0, 0, \dots$ is a path, since no block of it is all ones.

## Remarks

The measure of the set of paths is the infinite product $\prod_{k \ge 2} (1 - 2^{-k}) \approx 0.5776$.
Here $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ already proves that $T$ has a path, because the path of step 9 is computable.
The tree is a model case for the reversals of measure theory: each block is a small open set to avoid, and the sum of their measures stays below $1$.
Positive measure follows from the bound on the sum alone, in step 7, without the exact product.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Flip a coin forever, and group the flips into blocks of $2$, $3$, $4$, and so on.
A sequence loses if some block comes up all heads.
The chance to lose on block $i$ is $2^{-(i+1)}$, and these chances add up to $\tfrac{1}{2}$.
So the chance to survive is at least one half, and the tree of surviving starts has positive measure.
The sequence of all tails survives.

### L2 human

We check step 3 at length $n = 5$.

1. The blocks inside $\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ are $B_1 = \{0, 1\}$ and $B_2 = \{2, 3, 4\}$.
2. On $B_1$ the string may take $00$, $01$ or $10$, which is $3$ choices.
3. On $B_2$ it may take any of the $8$ values except $111$, which is $7$ choices.
4. So $\lvert T_5 \rvert = 21$ and $\lvert T_5 \rvert / 2^5 = \tfrac{21}{32} \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$.

The typical use: trees of this kind come from a sequence of intervals of small total length, as in the reversal of countable additivity.
The trap: if every block had length $1$, the losing chances would add up to infinity, and the ratio $\lvert T_n \rvert / 2^n$ would tend to $0$.

### L3 human

The set of paths is a closed subset of [[Cantor space|W9VYJRBF9N]].
It is the complement of the union of the open sets $\{X : X(j) = 1 \text{ for all } j \in B_i\}$.
Steps 6 and 7 are the subadditivity of measure in a finite form, which $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ proves.
The reversals that reach $\mathsf{WWKL}_0$ run this argument backwards: a tree of positive measure is turned into a sequence of open sets of small total measure.
