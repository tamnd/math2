## Proof

We show that a left inverse forces $Ax = 0$ to have only the trivial solution, and then elimination makes $A$ invertible.

**Part 1.** Assume $BA = I_n$.

1. Let $x \in F^n$ with $Ax = 0$, a solution of the [[homogeneous system|04KGXJG1A2]] with matrix $A$.
2. By [[associativity|073E4XC016]], $x = I_n x = (BA)x = B(Ax) = B0 = 0$.
3. So $Ax = 0$ has only the trivial solution.
4. By [[invertibility and row reduction|1ZK55FS2BF]], a square matrix over a field for which $Ax = 0$ has only the trivial solution is invertible, so $A$ is invertible.
5. Here $B$ is a left inverse and $A^{-1}$ is a right inverse of $A$, so $B = A^{-1}$ by the [[uniqueness of the inverse|FMCX4D7RD4]].
6. Hence $AB = AA^{-1} = I_n$.

**Part 2.** Assume $AB = I_n$.

7. Read with the roles exchanged, this equation says that $A$ is a left inverse of $B$.
8. Part 1 applied to the pair $(B, A)$ shows that $B$ is invertible and $BA = I_n$.
9. So $AB = BA = I_n$, and by the definition of the [[inverse|0TWTS88TWN]], $A$ is invertible with $A^{-1} = B$.

## Idea

A left inverse kills every nonzero solution of $Ax = 0$, because it can recover $x$ from $Ax$.
With no free variables, elimination finds a pivot in every column of the square matrix $A$.
So the reduced row echelon form is $I_n$, and $A$ is invertible.
Part 2 is part 1 read from the other side.

## Variants

The [[rank-nullity proof|W4WRRCVKRT]] makes the same argument in the language of linear maps: injective implies surjective in finite dimension.
Over a commutative ring, the determinant gives a third route, since $\det B \det A = 1$ makes $\det A$ a unit.
