## Summary

A rigid body is an idealised solid body in which the distance between any two of its points never changes.
Its position at each time is given by a [[rotation|0HFREYRSZP]] and a [[translation|087VJM5Q77]], that is, by an element of the [[Euclidean group|0H3YY8CPKH]].
This needs six numbers in space and three in the plane.
The model ignores deformation, and it is accurate when deformations are small compared with the motion.
Rigid bodies are the basic objects of rigid body kinematics and dynamics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A stone, a wheel or a hammer can be treated as rigid: they move and turn, but they do not stretch or bend noticeably.
Knowing where three points of such a body are, not all on one line, tells you where every other point is.
A rubber ball squeezed in the hand is not rigid, since the distances between its points change.
