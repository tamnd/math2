## Example

Let $P$ be an atomic formula other than $\bot$, and let $A$ be the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]] $P \vee \neg P$, where $\neg P$ is $P \to \bot$.
We compute the Gödel-Gentzen [[negative translation|QEZR7PZ2DD]] $A^N$ and derive it in intuitionistic [[natural deduction|PAQ4ZX2TX1]] NJ.

1. The clause for atoms gives $P^N = \neg \neg P$.
2. The clauses for $\to$ and $\bot$ give the translation of the second disjunct:
   $$(\neg P)^N = (P \to \bot)^N = \neg \neg P \to \bot = \neg \neg \neg P.$$
3. The clause for $\vee$ gives the whole translation:
   $$A^N = \neg (\neg \neg \neg P \wedge \neg \neg \neg \neg P).$$
4. In NJ, assume $\neg \neg \neg P \wedge \neg \neg \neg \neg P$ with the label $u$.
5. The rule ${\wedge}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\neg \neg \neg P$, and a second ${\wedge}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\neg \neg \neg \neg P$.
6. The formula $\neg \neg \neg \neg P$ is $\neg \neg \neg P \to \bot$, so ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ with the first conjunct gives $\bot$.
7. The rule ${\to}\mathrm{I}^u$ discharges $u$ and gives $\neg (\neg \neg \neg P \wedge \neg \neg \neg \neg P)$ with no open assumptions.

So $\vdash_i A^N$, although $A$ itself is not provable in [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]].

## Remarks

The translation of a classical axiom here has a four-line intuitionistic proof, and the proof uses only the rules for $\wedge$ and $\to$.
That is no accident: the [[double-negation translation|HGJVM49PA4]] removes $\vee$, so the troublesome rule for disjunction never appears.

The same four steps work for any formula $B$ in place of $P$.
The translation $(B \vee \neg B)^N$ is $\neg (\neg B^N \wedge \neg \neg B^N)$, and the second conjunct applied to the first gives $\bot$.
This is the case of the excluded middle in the [[proof of the Gödel-Gentzen theorem|3WH3N4GY7W]].

With the Kolmogorov translation the result is $\neg \neg (\neg \neg P \vee \neg \neg \neg \neg \neg P)$, and its proof needs the rules for $\vee$ as well.
In arithmetic, where $P$ is an equation and HA decides it, the translation can keep $P$ as it is, and then $A^N$ is $\neg (\neg P \wedge \neg \neg P)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The law of excluded middle says "a statement is true or it is false".
Intuitionistic logic does not accept this law in general, because it wants to know which of the two holds.
It does accept a weaker claim: "the statement cannot be both not true and not not true".
The negative translation produces exactly this weaker claim.
Its proof is short: assume both parts, notice that the second part denies the first, and conclude that the assumption was impossible.

### L2 human

We repeat the derivation for $B = \forall x\, Q(x)$ in place of $P$, to see that nothing depends on $P$ being atomic.
The translation of $B$ is $B^N = \forall x\, \neg \neg Q(x)$, and the clause for $\vee$ gives
$$(B \vee \neg B)^N = \neg (\neg B^N \wedge \neg \neg B^N).$$
Assume $\neg B^N \wedge \neg \neg B^N$.
The rule ${\wedge}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\neg B^N$ and $\neg \neg B^N$.
The formula $\neg \neg B^N$ is $\neg B^N \to \bot$, so ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\bot$, and ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ gives $(B \vee \neg B)^N$.

Watch out: if you translate only the atoms and keep $\vee$, you get $\neg \neg P \vee \neg \neg \neg P$.
That formula is not intuitionistically provable, so the clause for $\vee$ is essential.
