## Summary

Social choice theory is the mathematical study of how to combine individual preferences or judgments into a collective decision.
Its basic objects are rules that map individual preferences to a social ranking or a social choice.
An example is a [[social welfare function|KH8ACT22FR]].
It studies which properties such rules can have together, and its central results are impossibility theorems such as Arrow's theorem.
It underlies the analysis of voting systems, welfare economics and collective decisions in general.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A group of people must decide together, but each person has their own ranking of the options.
Social choice theory asks which rules for turning many rankings into one decision are fair and sensible.
It writes down fairness conditions as precise axioms and then asks which rules satisfy them.
For example, with three voters ranking three options in a cycle, majority vote cannot pick a clear winner.
Results of this kind show that every voting rule must give up something.
