## Summary

The K-theory spectrum of a ring, an exact category or a [[Waldhausen category|X7JG1QEB9Q]] is a [[spectrum|1GHF4EKBXS]] whose homotopy groups are its algebraic K-groups.
Its spaces are obtained by iterating Waldhausen's [[S-construction|ZPNXVVZY1J]].
Seeing K-theory as a spectrum makes the tools of stable homotopy theory available.
Cofibre sequences give long exact sequences, and smashing with Moore spectra gives K-theory with finite coefficients.
It is also the form in which K-theory is compared with other cohomology theories.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The K-groups $K_0, K_1, K_2, \ldots$ of a ring are not a loose list: they come from a single object.
That object is a spectrum, a sequence of spaces in which each space is the loop space of the next.
One builds the next space from the previous one by applying the S-construction once more.
Working with the spectrum instead of the groups makes it possible to take cofibres, products and coefficients in a clean way.
