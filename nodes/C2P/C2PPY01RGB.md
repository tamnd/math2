## Summary

The Selfridge–Conway procedure is a finite procedure that divides a cake among three agents so that the result is [[envy-free|P3GPD0S0J7]].
It was found independently by John Selfridge and John Horton Conway.
It uses at most five cuts and was the first finite envy-free procedure for three agents in [[fair cake-cutting|KCVV28DH6R]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The first agent cuts the cake into three pieces she finds equal.
The second agent may trim the piece she likes best so that it ties with her second favorite, and the trimmings are set aside.
The agents then choose pieces in the order third, second, first, with a rule that the trimmed piece must be taken by the second or the third agent.
Finally the trimmings are divided in a similar way, in an order designed so that nobody ends up envious.
