## Example

Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$$
over $\mathbb{Q}$.
A $2 \times 3$ matrix has no determinant, but it has $\binom{2}{2} \binom{3}{2} = 3$ [[minors|0SD528P0EK]] of order $2$, one for each pair of columns.
We compute them, read off the rank, and check them with the Cauchy-Binet formula.

1. Columns $1$ and $2$ give
$$\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 5 \end{pmatrix} = 5 - 8 = -3.$$
2. Columns $1$ and $3$ give
$$\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 6 \end{pmatrix} = 6 - 12 = -6.$$
3. Columns $2$ and $3$ give
$$\det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix} = 12 - 15 = -3.$$
4. At least one minor of order $2$ is nonzero, so the two rows are independent and the rank of $A$ is $2$.
5. For the check, compute $A A^{\mathsf{T}}$, whose entries are dot products of rows:
$$A A^{\mathsf{T}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + 4 + 9 & 4 + 10 + 18 \\ 4 + 10 + 18 & 16 + 25 + 36 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 14 & 32 \\ 32 & 77 \end{pmatrix}.$$
   Its determinant is $14 \cdot 77 - 32 \cdot 32 = 1078 - 1024 = 54$.
6. The [[Cauchy-Binet formula|08WE2W7DYS]] says that $\det(A A^{\mathsf{T}})$ is the sum of the squares of the minors of order $2$ of $A$:
$$(-3)^2 + (-6)^2 + (-3)^2 = 9 + 36 + 9 = 54.$$
   The two numbers agree.

## Remarks

The three minors are, up to sign, the coordinates of the cross product of the rows:
$$(1, 2, 3) \times (4, 5, 6) = (2 \cdot 6 - 3 \cdot 5, \ 3 \cdot 4 - 1 \cdot 6, \ 1 \cdot 5 - 2 \cdot 4) = (-3, 6, -3).$$
So step 6 is the identity $\|u \times v\|^2 = \|u\|^2 \|v\|^2 - (u \cdot v)^2$, here $54 = 14 \cdot 77 - 32^2$.

If the second row is changed to $(2, 4, 6)$, twice the first, then every minor of order $2$ is $0$.
For example, columns $1$ and $2$ give $1 \cdot 4 - 2 \cdot 2 = 0$.
The rank drops to $1$, and the minors of order $1$, the entries, still show that $A \neq 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A table with $2$ rows and $3$ columns is not square, so it has no determinant.
But each choice of $2$ of its $3$ columns gives a square piece, and each piece has a determinant.
Here the three pieces give $-3$, $-6$ and $-3$.
Since not all of them are $0$, the two rows point in different directions.

### L2 human

The minors of a rectangular matrix measure its rank, and here one nonzero minor of order $2$ already shows that the rank is $2$.
Computing $\det(A A^{\mathsf{T}})$ in two ways checks all three minors at once.
Geometrically, $54$ is the square of the area of the parallelogram spanned by the two rows in space, and the three minors are, up to sign, the areas of its shadows on the three coordinate planes.
Watch out: a minor keeps the columns in their original order, and columns $3$ and $1$ in that order would give $3 \cdot 4 - 1 \cdot 6 = 6$ instead of $-6$.
