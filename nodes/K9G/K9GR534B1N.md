## Summary

The Rayleigh criterion is a conventional rule that says when two point sources seen through an optical instrument count as resolved.
They are just resolved when the centre of the diffraction pattern of one falls on the first dark ring of the other.
For a circular aperture of diameter $D$ and wavelength $\lambda$, this gives the angular separation $\theta$ with $\sin \theta \approx 1.22 \, \lambda / D$.
It is the usual measure of the [[diffraction limit|GAM6ADR3ZJ]] of telescopes and microscopes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two stars very close together in the sky can blur into one blob in a telescope.
Each star makes a bright disc with faint rings around it.
Lord Rayleigh proposed calling the two stars separate when the bright centre of one sits on the first dark ring of the other.
At that distance the eye still sees a dip in brightness between the two centres.
A wider telescope makes smaller discs, so it can separate closer stars.
