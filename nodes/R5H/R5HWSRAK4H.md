## Summary

Bose–Einstein statistics describes how identical bosons, such as photons or helium-4 atoms, fill the single-particle states of a system in thermal equilibrium.
Any number of bosons may share one state, and the mean occupation of a state of energy $\varepsilon$ is $1/(e^{(\varepsilon - \mu)/k_B T} - 1)$.
It gives Planck's law for black-body radiation, the heat capacity of crystal vibrations, and [[Bose–Einstein condensation|YYBRA846TR]].
At high temperature or low density it reduces to [[Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics|B60C1S87XB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Bosons are sociable: a state that already holds many bosons is no harder to enter than an empty one.
Swapping two identical bosons gives back the same state, so only the number in each state matters.
As a result, low-energy states can hold huge numbers of particles at low temperature.
For light in a box the chemical potential is $0$, and a mode of frequency $\nu$ holds on average $1/(e^{h\nu/k_B T} - 1)$ photons, where $h$ is the Planck constant.
