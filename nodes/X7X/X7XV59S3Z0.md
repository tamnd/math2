## Statement

Let $\varepsilon_0, \mu_0 > 0$ be the vacuum permittivity and permeability.
Let $E$ and $B$ be $C^1$ fields on an open set of $\R^3 \times \R$ that solve [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] in vacuum with current density $J$.
Let $u = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2} \abs{E}^2 + \frac{1}{2 \mu_0} \abs{B}^2$ be the energy density and $S = \frac{1}{\mu_0} E \times B$ the [[Poynting vector|P3PK0Q02CZ]].
Then
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot S = - J \cdot E.$$
Consequently, for every bounded region $V$ with smooth boundary and outward unit normal $n$, fixed in time, inside the domain of the fields,
$$\frac{d}{dt} \int_V u \, dV = - \int_{\partial V} S \cdot n \, dA - \int_V J \cdot E \, dV.$$

## Remarks

The proof is a direct computation.
By the product rule $\nabla \cdot (E \times B) = B \cdot (\nabla \times E) - E \cdot (\nabla \times B)$, one of the [[vector calculus identities|0JC5X4HWR2]].
Faraday's law replaces $\nabla \times E$ by $-\partial_t B$, and the Ampère–Maxwell law replaces $\nabla \times B$ by $\mu_0 J + \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \partial_t E$.
Dividing by $\mu_0$ gives $\nabla \cdot S = -\partial_t u - J \cdot E$.
The integral form follows from the [[divergence theorem|0JX86PSSA9]].

The term $J \cdot E$ is the power per unit volume that the field does on the charges, by the Lorentz force.
In a resistor with $J = \sigma E$ it is the Joule heating $\sigma \abs{E}^2$.
In a linear, non-dispersive medium with symmetric permittivity and permeability, the same computation gives $\partial_t u + \nabla \cdot (E \times H) = -J_f \cdot E$ with $u = \frac{1}{2} (E \cdot D + B \cdot H)$.

Example: a long straight resistive wire carrying a steady current has an axial $E$ and a circular $B$, so $S$ points radially into the wire.
The flux of $S$ into a segment of the wire equals the Joule heat produced in it, $I^2 R$ for a segment of resistance $R$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Poynting's theorem is the bookkeeping of energy for electric and magnetic fields.
The energy stored in a region can change for two reasons only.
Energy flows in or out through the walls, or the field gives energy to charges inside, for example by heating a wire.
The Poynting vector measures the flow through the walls.

### L2 human

The theorem is the local conservation law of energy for Maxwell's equations, a continuity equation with a source term.
For a solution in vacuum with $J = 0$ and enough decay at infinity, it gives conservation of the total energy $\int u \, dV$.
This is a basic a priori estimate in the analysis of the Maxwell system.
In the covariant formulation it is the time component of the conservation law of the electromagnetic stress–energy tensor, whose space components give the momentum balance with the Maxwell stress tensor.
