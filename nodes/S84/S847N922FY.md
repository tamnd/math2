## Summary

Supersymmetry is a symmetry of spacetime that exchanges bosons and fermions.
Its generators, the supercharges, are odd elements of a [[Lie superalgebra|7CZ4Q5H78N]] that extends the Lie algebra of the [[Poincaré group|8ZQ9TA4Q2Q]].
The anticommutator of two supercharges is a translation.
The [[Haag–Łopuszański–Sohnius theorem|WKMW5H0GJZ]] concerns relativistic quantum field theories with a mass gap.
It shows that in them, such superalgebras are the only way to mix spacetime and internal symmetries.
No supersymmetric partner of a known particle has been observed.
Still, supersymmetry is a basic tool in [[string theory|GM58M1HFY0]], in [[Seiberg–Witten theory|BKKX6C8HR9]] and in many applications of physics to geometry.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary particles come in two families: bosons, like photons, which like to share a state, and fermions, like electrons, which never do.
Supersymmetry is a hypothetical symmetry that turns each boson into a fermion and each fermion into a boson.
If supersymmetry is unbroken, every particle has a partner of the other family with the same mass.
Doing two supersymmetry transformations in a row amounts to a shift in space and time, so supersymmetry is a kind of square root of a translation.
