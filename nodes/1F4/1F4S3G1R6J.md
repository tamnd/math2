## Problem

The geodetic boundary value problem asks for the gravity potential outside the Earth from gravity data given on its boundary.
Its classical linear form, the spherical Stokes problem, is the following.
Let $\sigma$ be the sphere of radius $R > 0$ centred at $0$ in $\R^3$, and let $\Delta g \colon \sigma \to \R$ be a given continuous function, the [[gravity anomaly|J6V1B7SP0Q]].
Find a function $T$, [[harmonic|0DEB0JMN0W]] in $\set{x : \norm{x} > R}$ and continuously differentiable up to $\sigma$, with $T(x) = O(1/\norm{x})$ as $\norm{x} \to \infty$, such that
$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial r} + \frac{2}{R}\, T = -\Delta g \quad \text{on } \sigma,$$
where $r = \norm{x}$.

## Remarks

Expand in [[spherical harmonics|3F32Y21HP4]]: the degree $n$ part of the solution has the form $(R/r)^{n+1} T_n$, and the boundary condition reads $(n-1) T_n = R\,\Delta g_n$.
So a solution exists only if $\Delta g$ has no degree $1$ component, and then the degree $1$ part of $T$ is arbitrary.
Once the degree $1$ part is fixed, for example by putting the origin at the centre of mass, the solution is unique.
It is given by [[Stokes' formula|MNK0GW8ZM6]] together with [[Bruns formula|8GF20Z4KDC]].
The boundary condition is of [[Robin type|581AGFCCRX]], and the free degree $1$ part shows that, unlike the exterior Dirichlet problem, it is not uniquely solvable without an extra condition.
Another form is the fixed gravimetric problem.
There the boundary is known from satellite positioning, and the condition becomes $\partial T/\partial r = -\delta g$ with the gravity disturbance $\delta g$.
A third form is the free boundary problem of [[Molodensky|PHW0E57MH3]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gravity can be measured only on or near the surface of the Earth.
The geodetic boundary value problem asks how to recover the whole external gravity field, and with it the geoid, from those surface measurements.
Mathematically, it asks for a harmonic function outside a closed surface with a prescribed combination of its value and its normal derivative on the surface.
