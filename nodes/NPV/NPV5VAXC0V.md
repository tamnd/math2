## Definition

A *shape-memory alloy* is a metal alloy with these properties.

1. It has a high-temperature crystal phase, called austenite, and a low-temperature crystal phase of lower symmetry, called martensite.
2. The two phases are related by a reversible [[martensitic transformation|EZRAMFGRZT]], a diffusionless change of the crystal lattice.
3. A specimen deformed in the martensite phase recovers its original shape when it is heated into the austenite phase.

Property 3 is called the *shape-memory effect*.

## Remarks

Example: nickel–titanium (nitinol) satisfies the definition.
Its martensite can be deformed by rearranging its variants, and heating above the austenite finish temperature restores the original shape.

Non-example: ordinary steel can form martensite by quenching, but a deformed piece of steel does not recover its shape on heating, so clause 3 fails.

The temperatures $M_s$, $M_f$, $A_s$, $A_f$ denote the start and finish of the martensite and austenite transformations.
The transformation shows hysteresis: in general $A_s$ and $M_s$ differ.
