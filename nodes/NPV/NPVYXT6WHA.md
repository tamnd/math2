## Summary

The solar dynamo is the process that generates the magnetic field of the Sun from the motions of its plasma.
It is a case of [[dynamo theory|AFVDBT0K5C]].
Differential rotation stretches a north–south poloidal field into an east–west toroidal field.
Twisting motions or the decay of active regions turn the toroidal field back into a poloidal field of reversed sign.
The cycle of these two steps produces the 11-year sunspot cycle and the 22-year magnetic cycle of the Sun.
Mean-field models describe it by the induction equation for the averaged field, with an $\alpha$-effect and a turbulent diffusivity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Sun's surface rotates faster at the equator than near the poles.
This stretches magnetic field lines around the Sun like rubber bands wound on a spool, making a strong east–west field below the surface.
Pieces of that field float up, break through the surface as sunspots, and are twisted as they rise.
Their remains drift toward the poles and slowly reverse the Sun's overall north–south field.
After about 11 years the field has flipped, and after about 22 years it is back where it started.
