## Summary

The scale factor is the function $a(t)$ of cosmic time that gives the size of the universe relative to a reference time.
In a [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker|ECJMS4VDTF]] model, the physical distance between any two galaxies at rest in comoving coordinates is proportional to $a(t)$.
Its logarithmic rate of change is the Hubble parameter.
Redshifts, distances and horizons in cosmology are all computed from $a(t)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a rubber sheet with dots drawn on it, stretched uniformly.
The dots keep their grid positions, but every distance between them is multiplied by the same factor.
That factor, as a function of time, is the scale factor.
If $a$ doubles between two moments, then every distance between galaxies at rest has doubled.
Today it is usually set to $a = 1$.
