## Summary

The electric potential is a real function on space whose negative gradient is the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] of a static charge distribution.
It is the [[scalar potential|0H7C3T1GJY]] of the electrostatic field.
Its value at a point is the work per unit charge needed to bring a test charge there from far away.
It turns the vector problems of electrostatics into problems for one scalar function.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of the potential as the height of a landscape, and of positive charges as balls that roll downhill.
The electric field points in the direction of steepest descent, and it is strong where the slope is steep.
A positive point charge makes a hill around itself, and a negative one makes a pit.
The difference of potential between two points is the voltage between them.
For a point charge $q$, the potential at distance $r$ is proportional to $q / r$.
