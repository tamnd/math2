## Definition

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be an $L$-[[structure|983GZBQYT4]] with domain $M$.
An [[automorphism|DSQ17TNW1C]] of $\mathcal{M}$ is an isomorphism $\sigma : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{M}$.
The structure $\mathcal{M}$ is *rigid* if the identity map of $M$ is its only automorphism.

## Notation

Rigidity says that the [[automorphism group|0K1SCPBNKJ]] $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{M})$ is the trivial group.

## Remarks

The main page of the idea is [[rigid structure|EPNZKJQHWJ]].

Example: the field $\Q$ is rigid, since an automorphism $\sigma$ satisfies $\sigma(1) = 1$, hence $\sigma(n) = n$ for integers $n$ and $\sigma(m/n) = m/n$.

Non-example: the field $\Q(\sqrt{2})$ is not rigid, since $a + b\sqrt{2} \mapsto a - b\sqrt{2}$ is an automorphism.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rigid structure has no symmetries other than the identity.

### L2 human

To show that $\mathcal{M}$ is rigid, show that every automorphism fixes a generating set.

1. An automorphism fixes the interpretation of every constant symbol.
2. It commutes with the functions of the structure, so it fixes everything built from fixed elements.
3. If the constants generate $\mathcal{M}$, then every automorphism is the identity.

The trap is that rigid structures need not be generated by constants, as the field $\R$ shows.
