## Definition

A [[pushdown automaton|8YEY51Q3B5]] $M = (Q, \Sigma, \Gamma, \delta, q_0, Z_0, F)$ is *deterministic* if for all $q \in Q$, $a \in \Sigma$ and $X \in \Gamma$:

1. $\delta(q, a, X)$ has at most one element;
2. $\delta(q, \varepsilon, X)$ has at most one element;
3. if $\delta(q, \varepsilon, X)$ is nonempty, then $\delta(q, a, X)$ is empty.

A language $L \subseteq \Sigma^*$ is *deterministic context-free* if $L = L(M)$, acceptance by final state, for some deterministic pushdown automaton $M$.

## Remarks

Example: $\set{w c w^R : w \in \set{a, b}^*}$, where $w^R$ is the reverse of $w$, is deterministic context-free.
A machine pushes each letter until it reads $c$, then pops and compares; in each situation exactly one move applies.
Non-example: the even palindromes $\set{w w^R : w \in \set{a, b}^*}$ form a [[context-free language|Q56EWEQYE2]] that is not deterministic context-free.
Every [[regular language|18WVBDCS38]] is deterministic context-free, and every deterministic context-free language is generated by a grammar that is not [[ambiguous|HFD162F9PH]].
The class is closed under complement but not under union or intersection.
The deterministic context-free languages are exactly the languages generated by LR(1) grammars.
