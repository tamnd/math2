## Example

The theory $\mathsf{FS}$ is $\mathsf{PA}$ in $\mathcal{L}_T$, with induction for all formulas, plus the following axioms and rules.

1. $\forall A\, (\mathrm{AtomSent}_{\mathsf{PA}}(A) \to (TA \leftrightarrow \mathrm{Tr}_0(A)))$.
2. $\forall A\, (T[\neg A] \leftrightarrow \neg TA)$.
3. $\forall A\, \forall B\, (T[A \wedge B] \leftrightarrow (TA \wedge TB))$ and $\forall A\, \forall B\, (T[A \vee B] \leftrightarrow (TA \vee TB))$.
4. $T[\forall v A(v)] \leftrightarrow \forall x\, T[A(\dot x)]$ and $T[\exists v A(v)] \leftrightarrow \exists x\, T[A(\dot x)]$, for all $A(v)$ with at most $v$ free.
5. *NEC*: from a proof of $\varphi$, infer $T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner)$. *CONEC*: from a proof of $T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner)$, infer $\varphi$.

Here $A$ and $B$ range over all sentences of $\mathcal{L}_T$, so $\mathsf{FS}$ is a type-free [[axiomatic theory of truth|KE8ZX8KVA3]].
We check that $\mathsf{FS}$ is not a theory of the [[Kripke-Feferman|VQG258JQAD]] kind.
Let $\lambda$ be the liar from the [[proof of the diagonal lemma|SFPSB6JPTC]], with $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.

6. Axiom 2 gives $\mathsf{FS} \vdash T(\ulcorner \neg \lambda \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
7. So $\mathsf{FS} \vdash \neg (T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \leftrightarrow T(\ulcorner \neg \lambda \urcorner))$, by propositional logic.
8. But $\mathsf{KF} \vdash T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \leftrightarrow T(\ulcorner \neg \lambda \urcorner)$, by [[this example|DN71AAR1R1]].
9. So $\mathsf{FS} \cup \mathsf{KF}$ is inconsistent. In particular, $\mathsf{FS}$ does not contain $\mathsf{KF}$.
10. *The iteration axiom.* Suppose $\mathsf{FS}$ also had $\forall A\, (T[TA] \leftrightarrow TA)$, the axiom that $\mathsf{KF}$ keeps.
11. NEC applied to $\lambda \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$ gives $T(\ulcorner \lambda \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \urcorner)$.
12. Axioms 2 and 3 move $T$ inside the connectives. They give $T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \leftrightarrow T(\ulcorner \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \urcorner)$.
13. Axiom 2 gives $T(\ulcorner \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \urcorner)$.
14. The iteration axiom gives $\neg T(\ulcorner T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
15. Steps 12 to 14 give $T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$, a contradiction.

## Remarks

The SEP entry states that the iteration axiom and the negation axiom are inconsistent over weak theories of syntax, even without NEC.
$\mathsf{FS}$ keeps the negation axiom, so truth in $\mathsf{FS}$ is classical: for each sentence, exactly one of it and its negation is true.
The cost is [[McGee's theorem|GQW8RQVZJ4]]: $\mathsf{FS}$ is $\omega$-inconsistent, although its arithmetical theorems are true.
The SEP entry reports Halbach's result that $\mathsf{FS}$ has the strength of ramified analysis for all finite levels.
It also describes $\mathsf{FS}$ as an axiomatization of the [[revision semantics|ZQ4TWM7K6E]] for all finite levels.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theory $\mathsf{FS}$ treats truth as fully classical.
A negation is true exactly when the sentence is not true.
The price is that "it is true that $\varphi$ is true" cannot always agree with "$\varphi$ is true".
The Kripke-Feferman theory makes the opposite choice.

### L2 human

1. In $\mathsf{FS}$, $T(\ulcorner \neg \lambda \urcorner)$ is equivalent to $\neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
2. In $\mathsf{KF}$, $T(\ulcorner \neg \lambda \urcorner)$ is equivalent to $T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.
3. Both together give $T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$.

The typical use is to compare classical and non-classical axiomatic truth.
The trap is to think that NEC and CONEC give the T-schema; they give only the rule form.
