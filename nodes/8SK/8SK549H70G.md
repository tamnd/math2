## Proof

1. Let $G$ be a finite [[group|G5S1M9]] with identity $e$, and let $g \in G$.
2. If $g$ had infinite [[order|0JPYSD5E0B]], then $\langle g \rangle$ would be infinite, by [[the order of a cyclic subgroup|031M8HX7DA]]. This is impossible, since $\langle g \rangle \subseteq G$ and $G$ is finite.
3. So $g$ has a finite order $m$. By [[this consequence of Lagrange's theorem|08V7JHS3QZ]], $m$ divides $\abs{G}$.
4. By [[the divisibility criterion for powers|0F5QC101Q7]], $g^{\abs{G}} = e$.

## Idea

The order of $g$ divides the order of the group, by Lagrange's theorem. A power of $g$ is the identity whenever its exponent is a multiple of the order of $g$.
