## Summary

Birefringence is the property of a material whose [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] depends on the [[polarization|PJ8ZSJ0QDW]] and the direction of the light.
It comes from an anisotropic [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]] tensor, as in crystals such as calcite and quartz.
A beam that enters a birefringent material generally splits into two beams with orthogonal polarizations.
Birefringence is the basis of wave plates, polarizing prisms and phase matching in nonlinear optics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put a clear calcite crystal on a printed word, and you see the word twice.
The crystal splits light into two beams that vibrate in perpendicular directions and travel at different speeds.
Each beam bends by its own amount when it enters, so the two images separate.
In calcite the two indices are about $1.66$ and $1.49$, a large difference.
Glass has the same index for every polarization, so it shows no doubling.
