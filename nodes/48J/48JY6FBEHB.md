## Summary

The tidal force of an external body, such as the Moon, on an extended body, such as the Earth, is a difference of two pulls.
It is the pull of the external body at a point minus its pull at the centre of mass.
It stretches the body along the line towards the external body and squeezes it across that line.
It falls off with the cube of the distance, and it drives the ocean tides, the [[Earth tide|JFX02GA88H]] and tidal heating of moons.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Moon pulls harder on the side of the Earth that faces it and more weakly on the far side.
The Earth as a whole falls towards the Moon with the pull felt at its centre.
Relative to the centre, the near side is pulled towards the Moon and the far side is left behind, so the Earth is stretched along the Earth–Moon line.
This is why there are two tidal bulges, one facing the Moon and one on the opposite side.
Doubling the distance to the Moon would make the stretching eight times weaker.
