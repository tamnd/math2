## Summary

Phase matching is the condition that the waves in a nonlinear optical process keep a fixed phase relation as they travel.
Then the new light made at each point adds up in phase.
For a three-wave process it asks that the wave vectors satisfy $\mathbf{k}_3 = \mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2$, which is conservation of momentum for photons.
Without it, the generated wave grows only over a short coherence length and then decreases again.
It is achieved with [[birefringence|8CWCYQJ44T]] or by periodic poling of the crystal, called quasi-phase matching.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In second-harmonic generation, each thin slice of the crystal makes a little new light.
The new light and the light that drives it usually travel at slightly different speeds, because of dispersion.
So the contributions from different slices get out of step and cancel.
Phase matching means choosing the crystal and its angle so that the speeds agree, and then all slices add up.
