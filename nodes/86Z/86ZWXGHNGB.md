## Summary

Auction theory studies the rules by which goods are sold to bidders with private values, and how bidders behave under these rules.
It compares auction formats, such as first-price and second-price sealed-bid auctions, by their equilibria, their revenue and their efficiency.
It is a main application of [[mechanism design|DTXJQKEHDP]] and of Bayesian games.
Its results guide the design of real auctions for radio spectrum, online advertising and government bonds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an auction, each buyer knows what the item is worth to her, but not what it is worth to the others.
The rules decide who wins and what everyone pays, and buyers bid strategically given these rules.
For example, if the winner pays her own bid, buyers bid less than their values; if the winner pays the second-highest bid, buyers can safely bid their values.
Auction theory works out such behaviour and compares the outcomes of different rules.
