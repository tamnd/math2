## Summary

A premixed flame is a thin reaction zone that travels into a mixture in which fuel and oxidiser are already mixed.
The flame moves because heat from the reaction diffuses ahead and ignites the fresh gas.
For a planar flame this is a travelling wave of a [[reaction–diffusion system|TAAHWE917M]].
Its speed, the laminar burning velocity, is a property of the mixture.
It is one of the two basic flame types of [[combustion theory|SXSCYXH068]]; the other is the diffusion flame, such as the [[Burke–Schumann flame|TVH1P5VTSP]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a gas stove: gas and air are mixed before they reach the burner, and the blue flame sits where the mixture burns.
The flame is a thin sheet, much less than a millimetre thick.
In a tube filled with a mixture, a flame lit at one end crawls through the tube at a fixed speed.
For a methane–air mixture this speed is a few tens of centimetres per second.
The speed is set by a balance: heat must diffuse ahead fast enough to warm the next layer to burning temperature.
