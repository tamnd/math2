## Example

Let $P = \set{x^2 - 2, \; x - 1}$, polynomials in one variable over the [[real numbers|08J2M1JYAG]].
We find the CAD of $\R$ adapted to $P$ and use it to eliminate a quantifier.

1. The real roots of $x^2 - 2$ are $-\sqrt 2$ and $\sqrt 2$, and the root of $x - 1$ is $1$. In order: $-\sqrt 2 < 1 < \sqrt 2$.
2. The cells are the three points and the four open intervals $(-\infty, -\sqrt 2)$, $(-\sqrt 2, 1)$, $(1, \sqrt 2)$, $(\sqrt 2, \infty)$. Each is a [[semialgebraic set|BX5WSK8ZCT]] and a point or an interval, so this is a CAD of $\R$.
3. Sample points: $-2$, $-\sqrt 2$, $0$, $1$, $\frac 5 4$, $\sqrt 2$, $2$.
4. Signs of $(x^2 - 2, x - 1)$ at the samples: $(+, -)$, $(0, -)$, $(-, -)$, $(-, 0)$, $(-, +)$, $(0, +)$, $(+, +)$. For example, $(\frac 5 4)^2 = \frac{25}{16} < 2$.
5. Each polynomial has constant sign on each cell, since its roots are among the cell points. So the CAD is adapted to $P$.
6. The sentence $\exists x \, (x^2 - 2 < 0 \land x - 1 > 0)$ is true, since the cell $(1, \sqrt 2)$ has the sign pair $(-, +)$.

## Remarks

The example shows the base phase of Collins's algorithm: in one variable, a CAD is given by the real roots, and sample points decide every sign condition.
In the algorithm, the roots are represented exactly, for example by isolating intervals with rational endpoints, and Sturm sequences count roots in each interval.

When the polynomials change, the decomposition changes.
For $P = \set{x^2 + 1}$ there is no real root, and the CAD has the single cell $\R$, on which $x^2 + 1 > 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The two polynomials $x^2 - 2$ and $x - 1$ change sign only at $-\sqrt 2$, $1$ and $\sqrt 2$.
Cutting the line at these three points gives seven pieces, and on each piece the signs are fixed.
To know whether some $x$ makes $x^2 < 2$ and $x > 1$, we look at one point from each piece.

### L2 human

We decide $\forall x \, (x - 1 > 0 \to x^2 - 2 > 0)$ with the same CAD.

1. The implication must hold at every sample point with $x - 1 > 0$: these are $\frac 5 4$, $\sqrt 2$ and $2$.
2. At $\frac 5 4$, the value $x^2 - 2 = -\frac{7}{16}$ is negative.
3. So the sentence is false, and the cell $(1, \sqrt 2)$ contains all counterexamples.

The typical use is the last step of CAD-based quantifier elimination, where the truth of a formula is read off the signs on the cells.
A trap is to sample only the open intervals: the points themselves are cells too, and conditions with $= 0$ or $\ge 0$ need them.
