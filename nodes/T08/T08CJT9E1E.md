## Summary

Kinematics is the part of [[classical mechanics|68W1NWECX8]] that describes motion without asking what causes it.
It studies positions as functions of time and their derivatives, such as [[velocity|K160D2Z0CS]], [[acceleration|Q8D2JWY8G6]] and [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]].
It also describes how these quantities change between frames of reference and how the parts of linkages and mechanisms move together.
Dynamics then adds masses and forces, and uses the kinematic description to state the laws of motion.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Kinematics answers questions such as: where is the ball after two seconds, and how fast is it going?
It does not ask why the ball moves, only how.
A ball thrown with known starting speed and constant downward acceleration follows a parabola; finding that parabola is a kinematics problem.
Working out how fast the piston of an engine moves when the crank turns at a given rate is another.
