## Summary

Entropy production is the entropy created inside a system by [[irreversible processes|D36GZB2766]], as opposed to entropy carried in or out across its boundary.
The [[second law of thermodynamics|T11MGWEKJ3]] says that it is never negative, and that it is zero exactly for reversible processes.
In a continuous medium it has a local density, which is a sum of products of fluxes and thermodynamic forces.
It measures how far a process is from reversibility and how much useful work is lost.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When heat leaks from a hot body to a cold one, the cold body gains more entropy than the hot body loses.
The difference has been created, not moved, and this is entropy production.
For example, let $100$ J of heat flow from a body at $400$ kelvin to a body at $300$ kelvin.
The entropy changes are $-0.25$ J/K and $+0.333$ J/K, so about $0.083$ J/K is produced.
Friction, mixing and electric resistance all produce entropy in the same way.
