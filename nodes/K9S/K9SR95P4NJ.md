## Summary

A propagator is a [[Green's function|SH6JY9RN2E]] of the wave operator of a free field, chosen with boundary conditions that fit quantum theory.
For the Feynman propagator, it equals the vacuum expectation value of the time-ordered product of two free fields.
Propagators are the lines of [[Feynman diagrams|BS6HVNMTWC]]: every term of perturbation theory is built from them.
In Euclidean form, the propagator is the covariance of the [[Gaussian free field|EGCDMWYKQB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A propagator measures how a disturbance of a field at one point is felt at another point.
In particle language, it is the amplitude for a particle created at $y$ to be found at $x$.
For a massive scalar field in Euclidean space, it decays like $e^{-m\abs{x-y}}$ at large distance, so heavy particles have a short range.
For a massless field in three dimensions it is $1/(4\pi\abs{x-y})$, the Coulomb potential.
