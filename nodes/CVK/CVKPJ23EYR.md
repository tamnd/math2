## Summary

Rayleigh scattering is the scattering of electromagnetic waves by particles much smaller than the wavelength.
Each particle becomes a small oscillating electric dipole driven by the incident field and radiates in all directions.
The scattered power grows like the fourth power of the frequency, so short wavelengths are scattered much more than long ones.
It explains why the clear sky is blue and why sunsets are red, and it limits the transparency of optical fibers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air molecules are thousands of times smaller than the wavelength of visible light.
Sunlight shakes their electrons, and each shaken molecule sends out a little light of its own in all directions.
Blue light shakes them faster and is scattered about $5$ to $6$ times more than red light.
So the sky glows blue from scattered sunlight, while the direct sunlight at sunset, after a long path through air, has lost its blue and looks red.
