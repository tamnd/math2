## Summary

The quantum Fourier transform is the [[discrete Fourier transform|TST0GH66A5]] applied to the amplitudes of a quantum state.
On $n$ qubits it can be implemented by a quantum circuit of $O(n^2)$ gates.
The classical fast Fourier transform on $2^n$ numbers needs about $n 2^n$ operations.
It is the key step of phase estimation and of [[Shor's algorithm|83G4EPJ3EJ]] for factoring.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A quantum state of $n$ qubits is a list of $2^n$ complex amplitudes.
The quantum Fourier transform replaces this list by its discrete Fourier transform, as one operation on the quantum state.
It turns a state whose amplitudes repeat with some period into a state concentrated near multiples of the inverse period.
Measuring the result then reveals the period, which is how Shor's algorithm finds factors.
The amplitudes themselves cannot be read out, so the transform helps only inside such algorithms.
