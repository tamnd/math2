## Summary

A wormhole is a spacetime geometry in which a narrow tunnel, the throat, joins two distant regions or two separate universes.
Some exact solutions of general relativity contain wormholes, such as the bridge in the [[Kruskal–Szekeres|2CCXEC0VR7]] extension of the Schwarzschild spacetime.
That bridge pinches off before anything can cross it.
A traversable wormhole, which can be crossed, requires matter that violates the null [[energy condition|ENWD1YR20P]] at the throat.
No wormhole has been observed, and their existence in nature is an open question.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a sheet of paper folded over, with a tube connecting a point on the top layer to a point right below it on the bottom layer.
Walking through the tube is much shorter than walking along the sheet.
A wormhole is the spacetime version of that tube: a short cut through space.
The narrowest part of the tube is called the throat; on each side the tube flares out into a large, nearly flat region.
To keep the throat open, general relativity requires an exotic kind of matter with negative energy density for some observers, so wormholes are a theoretical possibility rather than known objects.
