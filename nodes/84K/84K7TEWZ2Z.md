## Summary

A potential flow is a flow whose velocity field is the [[gradient|0ABK9V9Y64]] of a scalar function, the [[velocity potential|470A1RJWBP]].
Such a flow is irrotational, and if it is also incompressible, the potential satisfies [[Laplace's equation|S8RG6N1PRT]].
This turns many problems of [[inviscid flow|ZJH90RWYQ5]] into problems about [[harmonic functions|0DEB0JMN0W]], with pressure then given by [[Bernoulli's principle|7XFWQVWPG1]].
It is the classical model for flow around wings, for water waves and for flow far from walls.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a potential flow the fluid always moves "downhill" on a landscape given by a single function, the potential.
Such a flow has no swirl: a tiny paddle wheel carried along does not turn.
For example, with potential $\varphi = (x^2 - y^2)/2$ the velocity is $(x, -y)$: fluid comes in along the $y$-axis and leaves along the $x$-axis, as near the front of a blunt body.
Because the potential solves Laplace's equation, many such flows can be found and superposed by hand.
