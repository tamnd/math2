## Summary

A knowledge space is a mathematical model of what a learner may know in a field.
The field is a finite set of problems.
The feasible knowledge states are the subsets of problems that a learner could master at one time.
In a knowledge space the empty set and the whole set are feasible.
The union of feasible states is again feasible.
Jean-Paul Doignon and Jean-Claude Falmagne introduced the notion in 1985, and it underlies adaptive assessment systems that find a learner's state with few questions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Not every collection of skills is a sensible state of knowledge: nobody can solve quadratic equations without being able to add.
A knowledge space lists exactly the sensible states.
If two students pool what they know, the result is again a sensible state, which is why the states are closed under union.
For example, with problems "add" and "multiply", the states might be: nothing, "add" only, and both, but not "multiply" only.
