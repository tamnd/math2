## Summary

The Edgeworth box is a diagram of all ways to divide fixed totals of two goods between two people.
Each point of the rectangle is one allocation.
It is measured from the lower left corner for the first person and from the upper right corner for the second.
Drawing both people's indifference curves in the box shows at a glance the gains from trade, the [[Pareto efficient|JREVYHFY4T]] allocations, and the [[competitive equilibria|YTBPB52K27]].
It is the basic picture of exchange in [[general equilibrium theory|HGMHZSM0XQ]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a box whose width is the total amount of apples and whose height is the total amount of oranges.
A point in the box says how many apples and oranges the first person gets, read from the bottom left.
The second person gets the rest, read from the top right.
Moving the point toward the upper right helps the first person and hurts the second.
Where the two people's indifference curves just touch, no trade can help both, and these points form the contract curve.
