## Statement

Let a body or a system of particles have total [[four-momentum|67N6B8MJ3X]] $P$ and [[invariant mass|H5BXV0V62D]] $M > 0$, and let $c$ be the speed of light.
Then in its rest frame, the inertial frame in which its total momentum is zero, its total energy is
$$E_0 = M c^2.$$

## Remarks

In the rest frame $P = (E_0/c, \vec 0)$, so $-\eta(P, P) = E_0^2/c^2$, and the definition of the invariant mass gives $E_0 = Mc^2$.
So any change $\Delta E_0$ of the energy of a body at rest, such as heating it or emitting light, changes its mass by $\Delta M = \Delta E_0 / c^2$.
Example: the mass of a helium-4 nucleus is smaller than the sum of the masses of two free protons and two free neutrons.
The difference times $c^2$ is its binding energy.
The kinetic energies of the parts count in $E_0$, so a box of gas becomes heavier when the gas is heated.
Einstein derived the relation in 1905.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Mass is a form of energy: a body at rest with mass $M$ holds the energy $Mc^2$.
Since $c^2$ is huge, a small mass corresponds to a very large energy.
Conversely, energy put into a body, for example as heat, makes it slightly heavier.

### L2 human

The statement follows from the [[energy–momentum relation|GBEY10W50T]] in the frame where the momentum vanishes.
It explains the energy released in nuclear reactions through the mass defect, and the creation of particle pairs when the available energy exceeds the sum of their rest energies.
In relativistic collisions energy and momentum are conserved, while the sum of the rest masses of the particles in general is not.
