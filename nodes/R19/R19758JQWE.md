## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over $F$, and let $f \colon V \to W$ be a [[linear map|0RJF668P1T]].
Let $\operatorname{ev}_V \colon V \to V^{**}$ and $\operatorname{ev}_W \colon W \to W^{**}$ be the [[evaluation maps|0ZJM9D7NPS]].
Let $f^{**} = (f^*)^* \colon V^{**} \to W^{**}$ be the [[dual map|0C2TQMW5A1]] of the dual map $f^* \colon W^* \to V^*$.
Then
$$f^{**} \circ \operatorname{ev}_V = \operatorname{ev}_W \circ f,$$
that is, the following square commutes:
$$\begin{array}{ccc} V & \xrightarrow{\ \ f\ \ } & W \\ {\scriptstyle \operatorname{ev}_V} \big\downarrow & & \big\downarrow {\scriptstyle \operatorname{ev}_W} \\ V^{**} & \xrightarrow{\ f^{**}\ } & W^{**} \end{array}$$

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- $f$ must be linear, so that $f^*$ and $f^{**}$ are defined.
- No finite dimension is needed. The square commutes for all spaces; finite dimension only makes the vertical maps isomorphisms.

### Consequences

- In finite dimension, $f^{**} = \operatorname{ev}_W \circ f \circ \operatorname{ev}_V^{-1}$. So after the identification of $V$ with $V^{**}$, the double dual map $f^{**}$ is $f$ itself.
- In matrices: the matrix of $f^{**}$ is $(A^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}} = A$, by the theorem on the [[matrix of the dual map|0ZPB3180AY]] applied twice.

### Converse

There is no analogous family of maps $V \to V^*$.
For $f \colon V \to W$, the maps go $V \to W$ and $W^* \to V^*$, in opposite directions, so the square cannot be formed.
The isomorphisms $V \cong V^*$ given by bases depend on the basis, as the [[basis-dependent isomorphism of R^2 and its dual|A3CP855PNV]] shows.

### Generalizations

In the language of category theory, the lemma says that $\operatorname{ev}$ is a [[natural transformation|KYCPCC57FT]] from the identity functor to the double dual functor on vector spaces over $F$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

There are two ways to go from a vector $v$ of $V$ to the double dual of $W$.
One can first apply $f$ and then evaluate, or first evaluate and then apply $f^{**}$.
The lemma says that both ways give the same result.
This is what "natural" means for the evaluation map.

### L2 human

We check the square on one vector and one form.

1. Let $f \colon \R^2 \to \R$ be $f(x, y) = x + 2y$, let $v = (1, 3)$, and let $\varphi \in \R^*$ be $\varphi(t) = 5t$.
2. Path through $W$: $f(v) = 7$, and $\operatorname{ev}_W(7)(\varphi) = \varphi(7) = 35$.
3. Path through $V^{**}$: $f^*(\varphi) = \varphi \circ f$, so $f^{**}(\operatorname{ev}_V(v))(\varphi) = \operatorname{ev}_V(v)(\varphi \circ f) = 5 f(1, 3) = 35$.
4. Both paths give $35$.

The typical use is to justify that identifying $V$ with $V^{**}$ is compatible with all linear maps.
A trap: the analogous claim for maps $V \to V^*$ makes no sense, since no square of this shape exists.
