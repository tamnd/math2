## Summary

Motivic homotopy theory, also called $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy theory, is a homotopy theory for smooth schemes.
In it the affine line $\mathbb{A}^1$ plays the role of the unit interval.
It was founded by Morel and Voevodsky.
Algebraic K-theory, [[motivic cohomology|V1BB32NY3G]] and algebraic cobordism become representable objects in it.
Voevodsky used it in his proof of the Milnor conjecture.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In topology two maps are homotopic when one can be deformed into the other along an interval.
Algebraic varieties have no interval, but they have the affine line, so one declares every product $X \times \mathbb{A}^1$ to be equivalent to $X$.
Glueing is done with a topology finer than the Zariski topology, the Nisnevich topology.
The result is a world where the methods of homotopy theory apply to varieties.
For example the projective line becomes equivalent to the smash product of a circle with the punctured affine line, so there are two kinds of spheres.
