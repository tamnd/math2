## Definition

Let $\Omega$ be the region occupied by the Earth's mantle, under gravity of strength $g$ pointing locally along $-\hat{\mathbf{z}}$.
Let the rock have reference density $\rho_0$, thermal expansion coefficient $\alpha$, thermal diffusivity $\kappa$ and [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] $\mu$.
Let $H$ be the rate of internal heating per unit mass divided by the specific heat, so that $H$ has units of temperature per time.
*Mantle convection* is the buoyancy-driven flow of the rock in $\Omega$.
In the [[Boussinesq approximation|TXQQQG9T2S]] with infinite [[Prandtl number|C4KSBMR413]], its velocity $\mathbf{u}$, dynamic pressure $p$ and temperature $T$ satisfy:

1. $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$;
2. $0 = -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u} + \rho_0 \alpha g (T - T_0) \hat{\mathbf{z}}$, a [[Stokes flow|13CB7V2YM4]] with buoyancy, where $T_0$ is a reference temperature;
3. $\partial_t T + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla T = \kappa \nabla^2 T + H$, an [[advection–diffusion equation|DG49VBBT91]] with a source.

## Remarks

The vigour of the flow is measured by the [[Rayleigh number|CEKBV27MP0]] $Ra = \rho_0 \alpha g \Delta T d^3/(\kappa \mu)$, with $d$ the depth and $\Delta T$ the temperature contrast.
Example: take $\rho_0 = 4000$ kg/m³, $\alpha = 3 \times 10^{-5}$ K⁻¹, $g = 10$ m/s² and $\Delta T = 3000$ K.
With $d = 3 \times 10^6$ m, $\kappa = 10^{-6}$ m²/s and $\mu = 10^{21}$ Pa s, one gets $Ra \approx 10^8$.
This is far above the critical value of order $10^3$, so the mantle convects.
Non-example: a conductive layer with $Ra$ below the critical value has the solution $\mathbf{u} = 0$ with a stable temperature profile; nothing flows.
The infinite Prandtl number means inertia is dropped: the mantle's Prandtl number $\mu/(\rho_0 \kappa)$ is of order $10^{23}$.
In real models $\mu$ depends strongly on temperature and pressure, and the top layer forms rigid plates.
