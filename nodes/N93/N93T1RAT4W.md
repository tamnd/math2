## Proof

For a frame $\mathfrak{F} = (W, R)$, its complex algebra $\mathfrak{F}^+$ is the modal algebra $(\mathcal{P}(W), \cup, W \setminus \cdot, \emptyset, m_R)$.
For a modal algebra $\mathfrak{B}$, its dual frame $\mathfrak{B}_*$ is defined in [[general and descriptive frames|6W9MEGN982]]; we use only its underlying Kripke frame.
So $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{F} = (\mathfrak{F}^+)_*$.
Three results are cited without proof.

- (L1) [[Birkhoff's HSP theorem|5083DHSK82]]: a variety is closed under homomorphic images, subalgebras and products, and the variety generated by a class $\mathsf{C}$ is $\mathbf{HSP}(\mathsf{C})$.
- (L2) Jónsson-Tarski duality for morphisms: if $e \colon \mathfrak{A} \to \mathfrak{B}$ is an embedding of modal algebras, then $u \mapsto e^{-1}[u]$ is a surjective bounded morphism from $\mathfrak{B}_*$ onto $\mathfrak{A}_*$. If $h \colon \mathfrak{A} \to \mathfrak{B}$ is a surjective homomorphism, then $u \mapsto h^{-1}[u]$ is an isomorphism of $\mathfrak{B}_*$ onto a generated subframe of $\mathfrak{A}_*$.
- (L3) For every frame $\mathfrak{G}$, the frame $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{G}$ is a bounded morphic image of some ultrapower of $\mathfrak{G}$.

Part A: necessity of conditions 1 to 4.

1. Let $\mathsf{K}$ be defined by $\Sigma$. Conditions 1 to 3 hold by the frame versions of [[bisimulation invariance|TKFFTAX30E]]: validity is preserved by bounded morphic images and generated subframes, and validity on every summand gives validity on a disjoint union.
2. For condition 4, let $\mathfrak{M} = (W, R, V)$ be a model, and let $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{M}$ be $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{F}$ with the valuation $V^{ue}(p) = \set{u : V(p) \in u}$. Claim: for every formula $\varphi$ and ultrafilter $u$, $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{M}, u \models \varphi$ if and only if $V(\varphi) \in u$, where $V(\varphi)$ is the truth set of $\varphi$.
3. The claim holds for variables by definition. The cases $\neg$ and $\wedge$ hold because an ultrafilter contains exactly one of $X$ and $W \setminus X$, and contains $X \cap Y$ exactly when it contains $X$ and $Y$.
4. Case $\lozenge \psi$, from left to right: if $u R^{ue} v$ and $V(\psi) \in v$, then $m_R(V(\psi)) = V(\lozenge \psi) \in u$ by the definition of $R^{ue}$.
5. Case $\lozenge \psi$, from right to left: let $V(\lozenge \psi) \in u$. Let $l_R(Y) = W \setminus m_R(W \setminus Y)$. The family $\set{V(\psi)} \cup \set{Y : l_R(Y) \in u}$ has the finite intersection property.
6. Indeed, $l_R$ preserves finite intersections, so it suffices to check $V(\psi) \cap Y \neq \emptyset$ when $l_R(Y) \in u$. Otherwise $V(\psi) \subseteq W \setminus Y$, so $m_R(V(\psi)) \subseteq W \setminus l_R(Y)$, and $u$ would contain two disjoint sets.
7. Extend the family to an ultrafilter $v$. If $X \in v$ and $m_R(X) \notin u$, then $l_R(W \setminus X) \in u$, so $W \setminus X \in v$, which is impossible. So $u R^{ue} v$ and $V(\psi) \in v$, and the claim follows by induction.
8. Now let $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{F} \in \mathsf{K}$ and $\varphi \in \Sigma$. For each valuation $V$ on $\mathfrak{F}$ and each $w$, the principal ultrafilter $\pi_w = \set{X : w \in X}$ satisfies $\varphi$ in $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{M}$, so $V(\varphi) \in \pi_w$, that is, $w \in V(\varphi)$. So $\mathfrak{F} \models \Sigma$ and $\mathfrak{F} \in \mathsf{K}$.

Part B: sufficiency.
Assume conditions 1 to 4, and let $\Sigma$ be the set of formulas valid on every frame of $\mathsf{K}$.

9. Let $\mathfrak{F} \models \Sigma$. We show $\mathfrak{F} \in \mathsf{K}$. Then $\Sigma$ defines $\mathsf{K}$, since every frame of $\mathsf{K}$ validates $\Sigma$.
10. A formula $\varphi$ is valid on a frame $\mathfrak{G}$ exactly when the equation $\varphi = 1$ holds in $\mathfrak{G}^+$, with variables read as elements. Every equation $s = t$ of modal algebras is equivalent to $s \leftrightarrow t = 1$.
11. So $\mathfrak{F}^+$ satisfies every equation that holds in all $\mathfrak{G}^+$ with $\mathfrak{G} \in \mathsf{K}$. By (L1), $\mathfrak{F}^+ \in \mathbf{HSP}\set{\mathfrak{G}^+ : \mathfrak{G} \in \mathsf{K}}$.
12. So there are frames $\mathfrak{G}_i \in \mathsf{K}$ for $i \in I$, a subalgebra $\mathfrak{B}$ of $\prod_i \mathfrak{G}_i^+$, and a surjective homomorphism $h \colon \mathfrak{B} \to \mathfrak{F}^+$.
13. Let $\mathfrak{G} = \biguplus_i \mathfrak{G}_i$, which is in $\mathsf{K}$ by condition 3. The map $X \mapsto (X \cap W_i)_{i \in I}$ is an isomorphism $\mathfrak{G}^+ \cong \prod_i \mathfrak{G}_i^+$, because $m_R$ on a disjoint union acts on each summand separately.
14. So $\mathfrak{B}$ embeds into $\mathfrak{G}^+$. By (L2), $\mathfrak{B}_*$ is a bounded morphic image of $(\mathfrak{G}^+)_* = \mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{G}$.
15. By (L2) for $h$, the frame $(\mathfrak{F}^+)_* = \mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{F}$ is isomorphic to a generated subframe of $\mathfrak{B}_*$.
16. By (L3), $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{G}$ is a bounded morphic image of an ultrapower of $\mathfrak{G}$. The ultrapower is in $\mathsf{K}$ by the ultraproduct hypothesis, so $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{G} \in \mathsf{K}$ by condition 1.
17. By step 14 and condition 1, $\mathfrak{B}_* \in \mathsf{K}$. By step 15 and condition 2, $\mathfrak{ue}\,\mathfrak{F} \in \mathsf{K}$, since classes closed under generated subframes and bounded morphic images are closed under isomorphism.
18. By condition 4, $\mathfrak{F} \in \mathsf{K}$. This proves the theorem.

## Idea

Validity of formulas on frames is the same as validity of equations on complex algebras.
So a frame that validates the logic of $\mathsf{K}$ has a complex algebra in the variety generated by $\mathsf{K}$, and Birkhoff's theorem writes it with products, subalgebras and homomorphic images.
Duality turns these three operations into disjoint unions, bounded morphic images and generated subframes, at the price of passing to ultrafilter extensions.
The ultraproduct hypothesis brings ultrafilter extensions back into $\mathsf{K}$, and reflection of ultrafilter extensions finishes the proof.

## Variants

Goldblatt and Thomason gave the algebraic proof outlined here.
A model-theoretic proof replaces (L3) by the fact that a suitably saturated elementary extension of a frame has the ultrafilter extension as a bounded morphic image, which is the same tool as in the [[van Benthem characterization theorem|Y89MGMWE4H]].
For a single formula one can also use (L3) directly to show that modally definable elementary classes are closed under ultrafilter extensions.
