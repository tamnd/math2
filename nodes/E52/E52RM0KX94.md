## Example

Let $\mathrm{DLO}$ be the [[theory of dense linear orders without endpoints|2W4S2H4VYA]] in the signature $\set{<}$.
We show that $\mathrm{DLO}$ has [[quantifier elimination|RE5TFTDP4D]].
The signature has no constant symbols, so the quantifier-free sentences are built from $\top$ and $\bot$.

### Reduction to a system of inequalities

1. Reduction. By the [[one-quantifier test|Q1CGE4DWXQ]], it suffices to treat $\exists y \, \varphi(\bar x, y)$, where $\varphi$ is a conjunction of atomic and negated atomic formulas.
2. Negations. In a [[linear order|0FRXBVVR03]], $\neg(u < v)$ is equivalent to $v < u \vee u = v$, and $\neg(u = v)$ to $u < v \vee v < u$. We replace each negated atom in this way.
3. Disjunctions. We bring $\varphi$ to disjunctive normal form and use $\exists y \, (\alpha \vee \beta) \leftrightarrow \exists y \, \alpha \vee \exists y \, \beta$. So $\varphi$ is now a conjunction of atoms.
4. Atoms without $y$ do not depend on $y$, so we move them outside the quantifier. We delete the atoms $y = y$.
5. If $y < y$ occurs, the formula is equivalent to $\bot$, by irreflexivity. If $y = x_i$ occurs, then $\exists y \, \varphi$ is equivalent to $\varphi(\bar x, x_i)$, which is quantifier-free.

### The main case

6. In the remaining case the formula is $\exists y \, \bigl(\bigwedge_{i \in I} x_i < y \wedge \bigwedge_{j \in J} y < x_j\bigr)$. We claim that it is equivalent to $\bigwedge_{i \in I, \, j \in J} x_i < x_j$.
7. Left to right. If $x_i < y$ and $y < x_j$, then $x_i < x_j$ by transitivity.
8. Right to left, with $I$ and $J$ nonempty. Let $a$ be the largest $x_i$ with $i \in I$ and $b$ the smallest $x_j$ with $j \in J$. Then $a < b$, and density gives $y$ with $a < y < b$.
9. Right to left, with $J$ empty. The empty conjunction is $\top$, and the axiom "no greatest element" gives $y$ above the largest $x_i$. The case $I$ empty uses "no least element".
10. If $I$ and $J$ are both empty, the formula is $\exists y \, \top$. It is true in every model, since a model is nonempty.

So every formula is equivalent modulo $\mathrm{DLO}$ to a quantifier-free formula.

## Remarks

The example shows the standard pattern: reduce to one existential quantifier, then solve a system of conditions on one unknown.
Each sentence becomes $\top$ or $\bot$, so $\mathrm{DLO}$ is complete and decidable.
A subset of $\Q$ defined with parameters is a finite union of points and open intervals.
This is the first case of [[o-minimality|DTWHAW1N0V]].

When a hypothesis changes, the result changes in this way.

- With endpoints, the theory loses quantifier elimination. In $[0, 1] \cap \Q$ the elements $0$ and $1$ satisfy the same quantifier-free formulas in one variable, but only $0$ satisfies $\exists y \, (x < y)$.
- For the discrete order $(\Z, <)$, the pairs $(0, 1)$ and $(0, 2)$ satisfy the same quantifier-free formulas, but only $(0, 2)$ satisfies $\exists y \, (x < y \wedge y < z)$. So $\operatorname{Th}(\Z, <)$ has no quantifier elimination in the signature $\set{<}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of the fractions on a number line.
The question "is there a fraction strictly between $1/3$ and $1/2$?" has a quantifier, "there is".
The answer only depends on whether $1/3$ is smaller than $1/2$.
So the question can be replaced by the comparison "$1/3 < 1/2$", which has no quantifier.
Quantifier elimination says that every question about fractions and order can be replaced in this way.

### L2 human

We eliminate the quantifier in $\exists y \, (x_1 < y \wedge x_2 < y \wedge y < x_3)$.

1. The lower bounds of $y$ are $x_1$ and $x_2$. The upper bound is $x_3$.
2. By step 6 of the example, the formula is equivalent to $x_1 < x_3 \wedge x_2 < x_3$.
3. Check with numbers: for $x_1 = 0$, $x_2 = 1$, $x_3 = 2$, both sides are true, and $y = 3/2$ is a witness.
4. For $x_1 = 0$, $x_2 = 2$, $x_3 = 1$, the right side is false, and no $y$ exists since $2 < y < 1$ is impossible.

The typical use is to read off completeness and the form of the definable sets.
A trap is to forget the case of an equation $y = x_i$, where the witness is forced and density plays no role.
