## Summary

The Lorentz oscillator model describes how a dielectric responds to light.
It treats each bound electron as a damped harmonic oscillator driven by the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]].
It gives the [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]] as an explicit function of frequency, with a resonance at the natural frequency of the oscillators.
It explains normal dispersion, anomalous dispersion and absorption near a resonance, and it is a basic example of a causal response function.
With the restoring force removed it becomes the Drude model of metals.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of each electron in a material as a ball tied to its atom by a spring, with some friction.
A light wave shakes the ball back and forth at its own frequency.
If the light frequency is far from the natural frequency of the spring, the ball barely moves; near it, the ball swings strongly and absorbs energy.
The moving electrons radiate in turn, which changes the speed of light in the material, and this makes the speed depend on the colour.
