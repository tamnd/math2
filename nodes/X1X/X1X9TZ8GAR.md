## Summary

Fair division is the problem of dividing resources among several agents who value them differently.
The aim is that each agent gets a fair share by her own standards.
The resources may be divisible, like a cake or land, or indivisible, like a house and a car, and may include chores that nobody wants.
Fairness is made precise by criteria such as proportionality and [[envy-freeness|P3GPD0S0J7]], often together with efficiency.
The theory asks which fair allocations exist and gives procedures that find them using only the agents' own reports.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two children share a cake, heirs share an estate, roommates share rent: each is a fair division problem.
The difficulty is that people value the parts differently, and nobody can see another person's values.
A fair procedure gives each person a share that she herself judges good enough, for example at least $1/n$ of the whole.
The classic example is "I cut, you choose" for two people.
