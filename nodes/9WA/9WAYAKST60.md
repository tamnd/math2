## Summary

Brans–Dicke theory is the [[scalar–tensor theory|MWX9XPYEYY]] proposed by Carl Brans and Robert Dicke in 1961.
The gravitational constant is replaced by the inverse of a scalar field, and a single dimensionless constant $\omega$ fixes how strongly the field couples.
For large $\omega$ its predictions approach those of general relativity, and Solar System experiments require $\omega$ to be very large.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Brans and Dicke wanted a theory in which the strength of gravity depends on all the matter in the universe, an idea inspired by Mach.
So they let Newton's constant become a field that matter can change.
The theory has one adjustable number, $\omega$.
The larger $\omega$ is, the harder the field is to change, and the closer the theory comes to general relativity.
Radio tracking of the Cassini spacecraft showed that $\omega$ must exceed about $40000$.
