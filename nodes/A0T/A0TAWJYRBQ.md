## Summary

Silk damping, or diffusion damping, is the erasure of small-scale fluctuations in the early universe by the random walk of photons through the plasma.
It acts on scales smaller than the distance a photon diffuses before [[recombination|EH021K5438]].
It explains the fall of the [[CMB angular power spectrum|SJY21N1SNZ]] at small angular scales, and it was described by J. Silk in 1968.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Before recombination, photons bounce from electron to electron like a ball in a pinball machine.
Over time each photon wanders a certain distance in a random zigzag.
Hot and cold spots smaller than that distance get mixed by the wandering photons and smoothed out.
So the early universe kept its large ripples but lost its smallest ones.
This shows up as a damping tail at fine angular scales in the microwave background.
