## Summary

The finite-difference time-domain method (FDTD) solves the time-dependent [[Maxwell equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] by [[finite differences|X7HYEBA5SA]].
It places the electric and magnetic field components on the staggered [[Yee lattice|7JX95QG3CZ]] and updates them in turn, half a time step apart.
It was introduced by Kane Yee in 1966.
It is simple, explicit and needs no matrix inversion, and one run with a short pulse gives the response over a wide band of frequencies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cut space into small boxes and time into small steps.
Store the electric field on the edges of the boxes and the magnetic field on their faces.
At each step, a change of the magnetic field around an edge updates the electric field there, and the other way round, as in Faraday's and Ampère's laws.
Repeating this many times lets a computer watch a wave travel, reflect and scatter, like a film.
