## Summary

The carrying capacity of an environment is the population size that it can sustain in the long run.
In a single-species [[population model|1YEMFQ1H32]] with density dependence, it is the positive [[equilibrium point|0N8NGKDZDZ]] at which births balance deaths.
In the [[logistic equation|0CC4VC7KR3]] it is the parameter $K$, and every positive solution tends to it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A pond has only so much food for its fish.
When the fish are few, they grow in number; when they are too many, they starve and their number falls.
The size where these two effects balance is the carrying capacity.
For example, in the logistic model $N' = 0.5 \, N (1 - N/1000)$ the population grows when $N < 1000$, shrinks when $N > 1000$, and stays put at $N = 1000$.
So $1000$ is the carrying capacity of this model.
