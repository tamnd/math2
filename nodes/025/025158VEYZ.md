## Definition

Let $V$ be a real [[inner product space|01EK923SKP]], and let $u \in V$ with $u \neq 0$.
The *reflection* in the hyperplane $u^\perp = \set{x \in V : \ip{x}{u} = 0}$ is the map
$$s_u \colon V \to V, \qquad s_u(x) = x - 2\frac{\ip{x}{u}}{\ip{u}{u}}u.$$
A linear map $V \to V$ is a *reflection* when it equals $s_u$ for some $u \neq 0$.

## Notation

- The hyperplane $u^\perp$ is the *mirror*, and $u$ is a normal vector of the mirror.
- For a unit vector $u \in \R^n$, the matrix of $s_u$ is $I - 2uu^T$.

## Remarks

- For $x \in u^\perp$, $s_u(x) = x$. Also $s_u(u) = u - 2u = -u$.
- More generally, let $W$ be a finite-dimensional subspace of $V$ with [[orthogonal projection|0Q8HZTJC7G]] $P_W$. The reflection in $W$ is $R_W = 2P_W - \id_V$. It fixes $W$ and negates the [[orthogonal complement|079D68S3AW]] $W^\perp$.
- For the hyperplane $W = u^\perp$, the projection is $P_W(x) = x - \frac{\ip{x}{u}}{\ip{u}{u}}u$, and $2P_W - \id_V = s_u$. So the two definitions agree on hyperplanes.
- The same formula works over $\C$ with a Hermitian inner product. Then $s_u$ is unitary, and $\det s_u = -1$ still holds.
- Examples and non-examples are in the node on the [[concept|06BRDF3F0E]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A reflection is a mirror image in a hyperplane through the origin.
Vectors in the mirror stay where they are, and the vector $u$ perpendicular to the mirror changes direction.
Reflecting twice brings every vector back.

### L2 human

Worked check for $u = (0, 1)$ in $\R^2$:

1. $\ip{u}{u} = 1$, so $s_u(x, y) = (x, y) - 2y(0, 1) = (x, -y)$.
2. The mirror $u^\perp$ is the $x$-axis, and $s_u$ fixes it.
3. The matrix is $I - 2uu^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$.

A trap: the reflection in a subspace $W$ of dimension less than $n - 1$ is not a reflection in this sense.
For example, $2P_W - \id$ for the $z$-axis $W$ in $\R^3$ is the half turn $\operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, 1)$.
