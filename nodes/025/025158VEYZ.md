## Definition

A reflection is the linear map that fixes a hyperplane through the origin and sends the normal direction of that hyperplane to its negative.

Let $V$ be a real [[inner product space|01EK923SKP]], and let $u \in V$ with $u \neq 0$.
The *reflection* in the hyperplane $u^\perp = \{ x \in V : \ip{x}{u} = 0 \}$ is the map
$$s_u \colon V \to V, \qquad s_u(x) = x - 2\frac{\ip{x}{u}}{\ip{u}{u}}u.$$
A linear map $V \to V$ is a *reflection* when it equals $s_u$ for some $u \neq 0$.

## Notation

The hyperplane $u^\perp$ is the *mirror*, and $u$ is a *normal vector* of the mirror.
For a unit vector $u \in \R^n$, the matrix of $s_u$ in the standard basis is $I_n - 2uu^{\mathsf{T}}$.

## Remarks

### Reading the formula

The vector $\frac{\ip{x}{u}}{\ip{u}{u}}u$ is the component of $x$ along $u$, the orthogonal projection of $x$ onto the line $\R u$.
The formula subtracts this component twice: once to land on the mirror, and once more to go the same distance past it.
So a vector $x$ of the mirror has $\ip{x}{u} = 0$ and stays fixed, while $s_u(u) = u - 2u = -u$.
The division by $\ip{u}{u}$ is there so that the result does not depend on the length of $u$; it needs $u \neq 0$.
The inner product must be real and positive definite, so that $\ip{u}{u} > 0$ for every $u \neq 0$.

### A small case

Take $u = (0, 1)$ in $\R^2$.
Then $\ip{u}{u} = 1$ and $\ip{(x, y)}{u} = y$, so
$$s_u(x, y) = (x, y) - 2y(0, 1) = (x, -y).$$
The mirror is the $x$-axis, and the matrix is $I - 2uu^{\mathsf{T}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$.

### Reflections in other subspaces

For a finite-dimensional subspace $W$ of $V$ with [[orthogonal projection|0Q8HZTJC7G]] $P_W$, the map $R_W = 2P_W - \id_V$ fixes $W$ and negates the [[orthogonal complement|079D68S3AW]] $W^\perp$.
For the hyperplane $W = u^\perp$, the projection is $P_W(x) = x - \frac{\ip{x}{u}}{\ip{u}{u}}u$, and $2P_W - \id_V = s_u$, so the two formulas agree on hyperplanes.
For a smaller subspace they do not give a reflection in our sense.
For example, with $W$ the $z$-axis of $\R^3$, the map $2P_W - \id$ is the half turn $\operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, 1)$, which reverses a plane and has determinant $+1$.

The same formula works in a complex inner product space, and there $s_u$ is unitary with $\det s_u = -1$.
The examples, the non-examples and the main properties are on the [[concept of a reflection|06BRDF3F0E]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A reflection is a mirror image in a mirror through the origin.
Vectors that lie in the mirror stay where they are.
The vector $u$ that sticks straight out of the mirror is turned to point the opposite way.
Reflecting twice in the same mirror brings every vector back.

### L2 human

Here is a check of the definition for $u = (1, 1, 1)$ in $\R^3$.
We have $\ip{u}{u} = 3$, so $s_u(x) = x - \frac{2}{3}(x_1 + x_2 + x_3)(1, 1, 1)$.
For $x = (1, 0, 0)$ this gives $(1, 0, 0) - \frac{2}{3}(1, 1, 1) = \frac{1}{3}(1, -2, -2)$.
Check: this image has length $\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = 1$, the same as $x$.
The vector $(1, -1, 0)$ lies in the mirror, since its entries add to $0$, and the formula leaves it fixed.
Watch out: the reflection in a line of $\R^3$, in the sense $2P_W - \id$, is a half turn and not a reflection.
