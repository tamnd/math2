## Definition

Let $R$ be a [[commutative ring|0GYGHKX834]] and let $n \ge 1$.
Two $n \times n$ [[square matrices|0CP7YY6NAG]] $A$ and $B$ over $R$ *commute* if
$$AB = BA,$$
where the products are [[matrix products|076JGP6R3T]].
A family of $n \times n$ matrices is *commuting* if each two of its members commute.

## Notation

- The condition is often written $[A, B] = 0$, where $[A, B] = AB - BA$ is the [[commutator|0PXW0BN7DW]] of $A$ and $B$.
- In Mathlib, `Commute A B` is the statement `A * B = B * A`.

## Remarks

- Example: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ commute, since $AB = BA = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 11 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$. [[This example|~5ce7b775]] checks each entry.
- Non-example: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ do not commute, since $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ but $BA = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
- The matrices that commute with a fixed matrix $A$ form the [[centraliser|0Q0Z1BEXTF]] of $A$ in the matrix ring. It contains every polynomial in $A$.
- Over an algebraically closed field, a commuting family of matrices has a common [[eigenvector|0N6960W0BV]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two matrices commute when the order of the product does not matter.

### L2 human

The definition is one matrix equation, so it is $n^2$ equations between entries.
For $n = 1$ every two matrices commute, because $R$ is commutative.
