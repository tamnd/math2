## Definition

Let $(N, v)$ be a [[cooperative game with transferable utility|39HGM44CFZ]], and for $x \in \R^N$ and $S \subseteq N$ write $x(S) = \sum_{i \in S} x_i$.
The *core* of $(N, v)$ is the set of vectors $x \in \R^N$ such that:

1. $x(N) = v(N)$;
2. $x(S) \ge v(S)$ for every coalition $S \subseteq N$.

## Remarks

Example: in the glove game with $N = \set{1, 2, 3}$, where $v(S) = 1$ if $S$ contains $1$ and at least one of $2, 3$, and $v(S) = 0$ otherwise, the core is $\set{(1, 0, 0)}$.
Indeed $(1, 0, 0)$ satisfies both clauses, and any core vector has $x_1 + x_2 \ge 1$, $x_1 + x_3 \ge 1$ and $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 1$, which forces $x_3 \le 0$ and $x_2 \le 0$, hence $x_2 = x_3 = 0$ and $x_1 = 1$.
Non-example: in the three-player majority game, where $v(S) = 1$ if $\abs{S} \ge 2$ and $v(S) = 0$ otherwise, the vector $(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$ fails clause 2 for $S = \set{1, 2}$, since $2/3 < 1$.
In fact this game has an empty core: adding the three inequalities $x(S) \ge 1$ for the pairs $S$ gives $2 x(N) \ge 3$, which contradicts $x(N) = 1$.
The core is a convex polytope, possibly empty, cut out by finitely many linear inequalities.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The core collects the splits of the total that no group of players can object to.
Clause 1 says the whole worth is shared out, and clause 2 says every group gets at least what it could earn by itself.
