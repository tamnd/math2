## Summary

A brane, or $p$-brane, is an extended object with $p$ spatial dimensions that moves through spacetime.
Its history is a $(p+1)$-dimensional submanifold called its worldvolume.
A $0$-brane is a point particle, a $1$-brane is a string and a $2$-brane is a membrane.
Branes are basic objects of [[string theory|GM58M1HFY0]] and [[M-theory|FEC8C1595Y]]; D-branes are branes on which open strings can end.
Brane-world models such as the [[Randall–Sundrum model|HCJJ0VE2BB]] suppose that our universe is a brane in a higher-dimensional spacetime.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A point moving in time traces a line, its world line.
A string moving in time sweeps out a surface, and a membrane sweeps out a three-dimensional volume.
The word brane, from membrane, names all such objects at once, with $p$ telling how many dimensions the object has at one moment.
The simplest law of motion for a brane says that its worldvolume has stationary volume, just as a free particle moves along a path of stationary length.
For example, a soap film stretched on a wire frame is a picture of a static $2$-brane in space that minimizes its area.
