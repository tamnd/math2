## Summary

A jump diffusion model describes an asset price that moves continuously most of the time but can also jump at random moments.
The continuous part is driven by a [[Brownian motion|22735FGJV6]], and the jumps arrive according to a Poisson process.
Robert Merton proposed such a model for stock prices in 1976.
Jumps give return distributions with fat tails and produce a steep [[volatility smile|2TYE23YVS5]] for short maturities.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A stock price usually wiggles a little from minute to minute, but sometimes news makes it jump at once.
A jump diffusion adds such sudden jumps to the smooth random wiggles of the Black–Scholes model.
The jumps come at random times, on average $\lambda$ per year, and each has a random size.
For example, with $\lambda = 0.5$ the price jumps on average once every two years.
