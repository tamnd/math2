## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a [[thermodynamic system|GYNNJBMTCP]].
$\Sigma$ is in *thermodynamic equilibrium* when all of the following hold:

1. Its macroscopic [[state|TD4C13V3J6]] does not change in time.
2. There is no net macroscopic flow of matter or of energy inside $\Sigma$ or across its boundary.
3. It is in thermal, mechanical and chemical equilibrium, so that temperature and pressure have no gradients and no net chemical reaction occurs.

Two systems in contact through a wall that lets heat pass are in *thermal equilibrium* with each other when no net heat flows between them.

## Remarks

A gas at rest in a closed rigid box with insulating walls, after it has settled, is in thermodynamic equilibrium.
Its temperature and pressure are the same everywhere, and nothing changes in time.
A metal bar whose ends are held at two different temperatures can reach a steady state, but it is not in equilibrium.
It fails clause 2, because heat flows through the bar all the time.
