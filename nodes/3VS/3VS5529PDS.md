## Definition

Let a medium at temperature $T$ have opacity $\kappa_\nu > 0$ per unit mass at each frequency $\nu > 0$.
Let $B_\nu(T)$ be the specific intensity of [[black-body|A2RMYX8A65]] radiation at frequency $\nu$ and temperature $T$, given by [[Planck's law|QKQ0V5S04R]].
The *Rosseland mean opacity* $\kappa_R$ is defined by
$$\frac{1}{\kappa_R} = \frac{\int_0^\infty \kappa_\nu^{-1} \, \frac{\partial B_\nu}{\partial T}(T) \, d\nu}{\int_0^\infty \frac{\partial B_\nu}{\partial T}(T) \, d\nu}.$$

## Remarks

Example: if $\kappa_\nu = \kappa_0$ does not depend on $\nu$ (a grey medium), then both integrals differ by the factor $1/\kappa_0$, so $\kappa_R = \kappa_0$.
Non-example: the Planck mean $\kappa_P = \int_0^\infty \kappa_\nu B_\nu \, d\nu \big/ \int_0^\infty B_\nu \, d\nu$ is an arithmetic weighted mean, not a harmonic one, and it is not the Rosseland mean when $\kappa_\nu$ varies with $\nu$.
The denominator equals $4 \sigma T^3 / \pi$, where $\sigma$ is the Stefan–Boltzmann constant.
In the diffusion approximation, valid at large [[optical depth|TWF69TQH3S]], the radiative flux in a star is $F = -\frac{16 \sigma T^3}{3 \kappa_R \rho} \frac{dT}{dr}$, where $\rho$ is the density and $r$ the radius.
