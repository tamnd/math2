## Definition

Let a [[biaxial|8CWCYQJ44T]] transparent medium be given, with principal speeds $v_x, v_y, v_z$ all distinct.
Its [[Fresnel wave surface|9ZXST6JR11]] $W$ has four planes that touch $W$ along a whole circle, and four conical singular points.
1. *Internal conical refraction*: let $\mathbf{s}$ be a wave normal orthogonal to one of these tangent planes.
The energy of the waves with normal $\mathbf{s}$ flows along all the rays from the centre of $W$ to the circle of contact.
So a narrow beam with wave normal $\mathbf{s}$ spreads into a hollow cone inside the medium.
2. *External conical refraction*: let $\mathbf{t}$ be a ray direction through one of the singular points.
The waves whose energy flows along $\mathbf{t}$ have a whole cone of wave normals.
So a ray along $\mathbf{t}$ leaves the medium as a hollow cone.

## Remarks

The directions $\mathbf{s}$ of clause 1 are the two optic axes, the normals to the circular sections of the [[index ellipsoid|NRF1ESCGMR]].
The directions $\mathbf{t}$ of clause 2 are the two optic ray axes.
In internal conical refraction a beam that leaves the crystal through a parallel face emerges as a hollow cylinder of light, seen as a ring on a screen.

Example: a biaxial crystal such as aragonite shows both effects along its axes.

Non-example: a uniaxial crystal shows no conical refraction.
There the two sheets of the wave surface touch only at two points on the optic axis, with a common tangent plane.
So no circle of contact and no cone appear.
