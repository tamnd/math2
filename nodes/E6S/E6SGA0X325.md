## Example

Let $R = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, so that $Re_1 = e_2$, $Re_2 = e_3$ and $Re_3 = e_1$.
We find its axis and its angle with [[the classification of O(3)|2ZVG43GSKA]].

1. The columns $e_2$, $e_3$, $e_1$ are orthonormal, so $R$ is orthogonal.
2. Expansion along the first row gives $\det R = 1 \cdot \det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 1$, with the sign $(-1)^{1 + 3} = 1$. So $R \in SO(3)$.
3. The [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]] is $\tr R = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0$. So $1 + 2\cos\theta = 0$, $\cos\theta = -\frac{1}{2}$ and $\theta = \frac{2\pi}{3}$.
4. $R - R^T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
5. The entries $(3, 2)$, $(1, 3)$ and $(2, 1)$ are $1$, $1$ and $1$.
6. $2\sin\theta = 2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}$. So $u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, 1, 1)$.
7. Check: $R(1, 1, 1) = (1, 1, 1)$, and $\norm{u} = 1$.
8. So $R$ is the rotation by $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ about the axis through $(1, 1, 1)$, counterclockwise when $u$ points toward the viewer.

## Remarks

- The example illustrates parts 1, 3 and 4 of [[the classification of O(3)|2ZVG43GSKA]].
- $R^3 = I_3$, which fits $3 \cdot \frac{2\pi}{3} = 2\pi$.
- $R$ permutes the three coordinate axes, so it is a symmetry of the cube with vertices $(\pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1)$. The axis passes through the two opposite vertices $\pm(1, 1, 1)$.
- $R = R_z(\pi/2)R_x(\pi/2)$, see [[the basic rotations|F1KZ5GE4TC]], and it is the rotation of the unit quaternion $\frac{1}{2}(1 + i + j + k)$, see [[this example|Z7AEM725B9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The matrix moves the $x$-axis to the $y$-axis, the $y$-axis to the $z$-axis, and the $z$-axis back to the $x$-axis.
Look at a cube along its long diagonal through $(1, 1, 1)$.
The three axes then look like three spokes of a wheel, and the matrix turns the wheel by one third of a full turn.

### L2 human

The worked computation is in the Example.
The typical use: these steps find the axis and the angle of any rotation matrix with $0 < \theta < \pi$.
A trap: $R^T$ is the rotation by $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ about $-u$, which is the rotation by $-\frac{2\pi}{3}$ about $u$.
