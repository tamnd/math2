## Summary

A Strömgren sphere is the region of ionised hydrogen around a hot star in a uniform cloud of hydrogen gas.
Its radius is set by a balance of two rates.
One is the number of ionising photons the star emits per second; the other is the number of [[recombinations|EH021K5438]] per second inside the sphere.
Inside the sphere the gas is almost fully ionised, and the transition to neutral gas outside is sharp.
It is the simplest model of an H II region such as the Orion Nebula.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A hot young star emits ultraviolet light that strips electrons from hydrogen atoms around it.
Electrons and protons keep meeting again and forming atoms, and each new atom must be ionised again.
The star can only keep a limited volume ionised, because it has a limited supply of ultraviolet photons per second.
The ionised bubble grows until the recombinations inside it use up all those photons.
A denser cloud recombines faster, so the bubble is smaller: its radius scales as density to the power $-2/3$.
