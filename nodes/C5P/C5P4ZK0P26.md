## Statement

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] with inner product $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$.
Let $T \colon \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{H}$ be an antiunitary operator, that is, a bijective antilinear map with $\ip{T\varphi}{T\psi} = \overline{\ip{\varphi}{\psi}}$ for all $\varphi, \psi \in \mathcal{H}$.
Assume that $T^2 = -I$.
Let $H$ be a [[self-adjoint operator|0NEGN5CRSC]] on $\mathcal{H}$ that commutes with $T$, meaning $T$ maps the domain of $H$ into itself and $HT = TH$ there.
Then for every eigenvector $\psi$ of $H$ with eigenvalue $E$, the vector $T\psi$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $E$.
Moreover $T\psi$ is orthogonal to $\psi$.
In particular, every finite-dimensional [[eigenspace|0G0RFPG8HR]] of $H$ has even dimension.

## Remarks

Since $E$ is real and $T$ is antilinear, $HT\psi = TH\psi = T(E\psi) = E\, T\psi$.
Apply the antiunitary property to $\varphi = T\psi$: it gives $\ip{T^2\psi}{T\psi} = \overline{\ip{T\psi}{\psi}} = \ip{\psi}{T\psi}$.
The left side equals $-\ip{\psi}{T\psi}$ because $T^2 = -I$, so $\ip{\psi}{T\psi} = 0$.
On a finite-dimensional eigenspace $V$, the map $T$ is an antilinear map with $T^2 = -I$, so it makes $V$ a vector space over the quaternions, and $\dim_\C V$ is even.

Example: for one particle of spin $1/2$ the [[time reversal|N8P02NZGZS]] operator on $\C^2$ can be taken as $T = \sigma_y K$.
Here $K$ is complex conjugation of the coordinates and $\sigma_y$ is the Pauli matrix with rows $(0, -i)$ and $(i, 0)$.
Then $T^2 = \sigma_y \overline{\sigma_y} = -\sigma_y^2 = -I$.
For $N$ such particles $T^2 = (-1)^N I$, so the theorem applies when $N$ is odd.

The hypothesis $T^2 = -I$ is necessary.
For a spinless particle on $L^2(\R)$ time reversal is $T\psi = \overline{\psi}$, with $T^2 = I$, and the harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian commutes with it while all its eigenvalues are simple.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Time reversal runs the motion of a quantum system backwards.
For particles with half-integer spin, doing time reversal twice gives minus the identity rather than the identity.
Kramers' theorem says that then every energy level of a time-reversal symmetric system is shared by at least two different states.
This happens for an odd number of electrons in any electric field, however irregular.

### L2 human

The proof only uses that an antiunitary $T$ with $T^2 = -I$ cannot fix the line through any vector: $T\psi$ is always orthogonal to $\psi$.
The pairs $\{\psi, T\psi\}$ are the Kramers doublets.
A magnetic field breaks time reversal symmetry, and then the doublets can split, as in the Zeeman effect.
The degeneracy can be larger than two, but each eigenspace splits into orthogonal Kramers doublets, so its dimension is even.
