## Summary

An adiabatic invariant is a quantity of a mechanical system that stays nearly constant under a very slow change of a parameter.
Here slow means slow compared with the motion itself.
It is not exactly conserved, but its total change over the long time of the slow variation tends to zero as the variation becomes slower.
For a periodic motion with one degree of freedom, the action $\frac{1}{2\pi} \oint p \, dq$ is the basic adiabatic invariant; see [[action-angle coordinates|M3T81YNSKC]].
Adiabatic invariants explain, for example, the trapping of charged particles in magnetic mirrors and appear in the old quantum theory.
See the [[definition|2N90MRJWMA]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a pendulum whose string is shortened very slowly while it swings.
Its energy changes, and its frequency changes, but the ratio of energy to frequency hardly changes at all.
This ratio is an adiabatic invariant.
If the string is shortened so that the frequency doubles, the energy also roughly doubles.
A sudden change of length, by contrast, can change this ratio by a lot.
