## Summary

The ergosphere of a rotating [[black hole|YHJ0PDNY8T]] is the region outside the [[event horizon|D8NZ32Z3SQ]] where nothing can stay at rest relative to distant observers.
There the time-translation [[Killing vector field|5AKNNW3W4Z]] becomes spacelike, so every observer is dragged around with the rotation of the hole.
In the [[Kerr metric|X3FZC85THY]] it is a flattened region that touches the horizon at the poles and reaches out to $r = 2M$ at the equator.
Particles inside it can have negative energy, which makes the [[Penrose process|TW2587J15Z]] possible.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A spinning black hole drags space around with it, like a whirlpool drags water.
Close enough to the hole, the drag is so strong that no rocket can hover in place; everything must circle in the same sense as the hole.
That zone, just outside the horizon, is the ergosphere.
Unlike the horizon, it can be left again, so it is possible to enter it, take energy from the rotation, and come back out.
The name comes from the Greek word ergon, meaning work.
