## Statement

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over a field $F$.

1. If $F$ is infinite, then $\mathfrak{g}$ has a [[Cartan subalgebra|BB098JPWGA]], that is, a [[nilpotent|MEJ9PGMJ9H]] subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ that equals its own normalizer.
2. If $F$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $0$, then any two Cartan subalgebras $\mathfrak{h}_1, \mathfrak{h}_2$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ are conjugate.
That is, $\varphi(\mathfrak{h}_1) = \mathfrak{h}_2$ for some automorphism $\varphi$ of $\mathfrak{g}$.
Moreover, $\varphi$ can be taken in the group generated by the $\exp(\operatorname{ad} x)$ with $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ and $\operatorname{ad} x$ nilpotent.

## Remarks

For existence, call $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ regular if the generalized $0$-eigenspace of $\operatorname{ad} x$ has the least possible dimension.
Regular elements exist, and for a regular $x$ the [[Engel subalgebra|PM39E26ZG6]] $\{y \in \mathfrak{g} : (\operatorname{ad} x)^n y = 0 \text{ for some } n\}$ is a Cartan subalgebra.
For conjugacy, one shows that the conjugates of the regular elements of a Cartan subalgebra fill a Zariski-dense constructible subset of $\mathfrak{g}$.
Two such subsets meet, which gives conjugacy.
In $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\C)$, the diagonal matrices of trace $0$ form a Cartan subalgebra, and by the theorem every Cartan subalgebra is conjugate to it.
Over $\R$ the conjugacy part fails.
The algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\R)$ has the diagonal Cartan subalgebra and the Cartan subalgebra spanned by $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
They are not conjugate, since the first contains elements with real nonzero eigenvalues of $\operatorname{ad}$ and the second does not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Cartan subalgebra is a kind of maximal "diagonal part" of a Lie algebra.
The theorem says that such a part always exists, and over the complex numbers it is unique up to a symmetry of the Lie algebra.
So any choice of it gives the same picture, and invariants built from it, such as the roots, do not depend on the choice.

### L2 human

For a semisimple Lie algebra over $\C$, a Cartan subalgebra is the same as a maximal toral subalgebra, and it is abelian.
The conjugacy theorem is what makes the rank and the root system invariants of the Lie algebra.
It is the Lie algebra analogue of the conjugacy of maximal tori in a compact connected Lie group.
