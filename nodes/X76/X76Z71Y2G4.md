## Definition

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $\Lambda \subset \R^n$ be a set of points.
$\Lambda$ is a *quasicrystal* (in the mathematical sense) if the following hold.

1. $\Lambda$ is uniformly discrete: there is $r > 0$ with $\abs{x - y} \ge r$ for all distinct $x, y \in \Lambda$.
2. $\Lambda$ is relatively dense: there is $R > 0$ such that every closed ball of radius $R$ meets $\Lambda$.
3. $\Lambda$ has pure point diffraction: the autocorrelation measure $\gamma = \lim_{s \to \infty} \frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(B_s)} \sum_{x, y \in \Lambda \cap B_s} \delta_{x - y}$ exists, and its Fourier transform $\hat\gamma$, the diffraction measure, is a pure point measure.
4. $\Lambda$ is not crystallographic: there is no [[Bravais lattice|2ASPT0SP6Z]] $L \subset \R^n$ with $\Lambda + L = \Lambda$.

Here $B_s$ is the ball of radius $s$ centred at $0$, and the limit is in the vague topology of measures.
A set with clauses 1 and 2 is a *Delone set*.

## Remarks

The definition is not uniform in the literature.
The International Union of Crystallography calls a crystal any solid with an essentially discrete diffraction diagram, and a quasicrystal one that is moreover aperiodic.
Some authors also require finite local complexity.

Example: the vertex set of a [[Penrose tiling|MBE83FTRBG]] is a Delone set, since the tiles are two rhombi of fixed size.
It is a model set (a cut-and-project set from $\Z^5$), so its diffraction is pure point, and it has no period, because the tiling is [[aperiodic|Z3FCJZF6WK]].
Its diffraction pattern has ten-fold rotational symmetry.

Non-example: $\Z^n$ satisfies clauses 1 to 3, with diffraction supported on $\Z^n$, but it fails clause 4 with $L = \Z^n$.
