## Statement

Let $(\Sigma, h, K)$ be an asymptotically flat [[initial data set|SB7BVCZJ3F]] for the Einstein equations whose matter satisfies the dominant [[energy condition|ENWD1YR20P]].
Let $m$ be its [[ADM mass|XQNV97Z79Q]].
Let $S \subseteq \Sigma$ be an outermost apparent horizon, a closed marginally [[trapped surface|26AVAT8306]].
Let $A$ be the least area of a closed surface in $\Sigma$ that encloses $S$.
Conjecture: then
$$m \ge \sqrt{\frac{A}{16\pi}},$$
with equality only for data in the Schwarzschild spacetime.

## Remarks

Penrose derived the inequality by a heuristic argument that combines weak cosmic censorship with the Hawking area theorem.
A counterexample would therefore be evidence against cosmic censorship.
For time-symmetric data, $K = 0$, the dominant energy condition becomes nonnegative scalar curvature and marginally trapped surfaces become minimal surfaces.
In this case the conjecture is the [[Riemannian Penrose inequality|Q5EWVTA2RW]], proved in 2001 by Huisken and Ilmanen for a connected horizon and by Bray in general.
The general case is open, and the precise choice of the surface $S$ and of the area $A$ in the general formulation is itself discussed in the literature.
For the Schwarzschild spacetime of mass $m$, the horizon has area $16\pi m^2$, so equality holds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A black hole of a given size must contain at least a certain amount of mass.
The area of its horizon gives a lower bound for the total mass of spacetime.
The conjecture is proved when space is momentarily at rest, and it is still open in general.
