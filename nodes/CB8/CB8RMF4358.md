## Definition

A *perpetual motion machine* is a hypothetical device that operates in a [[thermodynamic cycle|AGRGCDWQF8]] and is of one of the following kinds.

1. Of the first kind: in each cycle it does positive net [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] but takes in no net energy, as [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] or otherwise.
2. Of the second kind: in each cycle it turns all the heat it takes from one reservoir into work, with no other effect.

*Perpetual motion* is the running of such a machine.

## Remarks

A machine of the first kind would violate the [[first law of thermodynamics|V14W2V4JSR]], since over a cycle the net work equals the net heat taken in.
A machine of the second kind would violate the [[Kelvin–Planck statement|RMNJ00XP8X]] of the second law.
Example: a ship engine that cools the sea water and uses all the extracted heat to drive the propeller would be of the second kind.
Non-example: a real steam engine is not of the second kind, because it gives part of the heat from the boiler to the colder condenser.
A frictionless pendulum in vacuum moves forever, but it does no net work, so it is not a perpetual motion machine of either kind.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A perpetual motion machine would give useful work forever without anything paying for it.
The two laws of thermodynamics forbid the two kinds of such machines.
