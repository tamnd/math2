## Definition

Let $\hbar > 0$, and let $\mathcal{H}$ be the complex Hilbert space of a quantum system.
The *Hamiltonian* of the system is a self-adjoint operator $H$ on $\mathcal{H}$, the [[observable|DF5RN297GE]] of total energy, that generates the time evolution.
A state vector $\psi$ at time $0$ becomes
$$\psi(t) = e^{-itH/\hbar}\, \psi$$
at time $t \in \R$.

## Remarks

By [[Stone's theorem|DVGQB92HPJ]], $U(t) = e^{-itH/\hbar}$ is a strongly continuous one-parameter group of [[unitary operators|MM0EJ2GDE4]], and every such group arises from exactly one self-adjoint $H$.
For $\psi \in D(H)$, the curve $\psi(t)$ is differentiable and solves the abstract Schrödinger equation $i\hbar\, \psi'(t) = H\psi(t)$.

Consider a particle of mass $m > 0$ in $\R^n$ with a real potential $V$.
Its Hamiltonian is built from the [[momentum operators|5WG6YTAA6J]] and the [[position operators|7XAKGQQWTP]]:
$$H = \frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j=1}^n P_j^2 + V(X) = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta + V.$$
It is the [[Schrödinger operator|89AA6XJY5K]] of the [[Schrödinger equation|EPZR6H2WJN]].
One must check that it is self-adjoint on a suitable domain; for a bounded measurable $V$ this holds on the domain of the Laplacian, the Sobolev space $H^2(\R^n)$.

Example: for a free particle on $\R$, $H = P^2/2m$.
The Fourier transform turns it into multiplication by $\hbar^2 k^2/2m$, a real function, so $H$ is self-adjoint with spectrum $[0, \infty)$.

Non-example: the operator $iI$ on $\C^2$ is not self-adjoint.
It would give $e^{-it(iI)/\hbar} = e^{t/\hbar} I$, which is not unitary and makes the norm of a state vector grow.
