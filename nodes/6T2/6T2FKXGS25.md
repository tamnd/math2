## Summary

The Stanton number is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] that compares the heat transferred into a fluid with the heat the flow can carry along.
It equals $h / (\rho c_p U)$, where $h$ is the [[heat transfer coefficient|J821AD6YTE]] and $U$ the flow speed.
It also equals $\mathrm{Nu} / (\mathrm{Re} \, \mathrm{Pr})$, built from the [[Nusselt|THH3V9AHDN]], [[Reynolds|JH9EJ0HA0R]] and [[Prandtl|C4KSBMR413]] numbers.
It appears in the [[Reynolds analogy|RJ2CK83N11]] between heat transfer and friction.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A fluid flowing past a hot wall picks up heat from the wall and carries it downstream.
The Stanton number compares how fast the wall hands heat to the fluid with how fast the stream can take it away.
A small Stanton number means that the fluid warms only a little as it passes the wall.
