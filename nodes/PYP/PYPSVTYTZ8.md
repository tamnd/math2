## Statement

Let $K$ be an [[algebraic number field|MWBDFS2KJ5]] with at least one real embedding, let $\mathcal{O}$ be its [[ring of integers|0BXA4Q0JHW]], and let $n \ge 3$.
For a nonzero [[ideal|096Z9MP2ME]] $\mathfrak{q}$ of $\mathcal{O}$, let $\Gamma(\mathfrak{q})$ be the kernel of the reduction map $SL_n(\mathcal{O}) \to SL_n(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{q})$ on the [[special linear group|0V9PMN0RBH]].
Then every subgroup of finite [[index|0DTXBQPA2S]] in $SL_n(\mathcal{O})$ contains $\Gamma(\mathfrak{q})$ for some nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{q}$, that is, it is a [[congruence subgroup|1H6R856P3C]].

## Remarks

The idea of the proof: every subgroup of finite index contains a normal subgroup $N$ of finite index $m$.
Then $N$ contains the powers $e_{ij}(r)^m = e_{ij}(mr)$ of the elementary matrices.
Hence $N$ contains the normal subgroup $E_n(\mathcal{O}, \mathfrak{q})$ generated by the $e_{ij}(q)$ with $q \in \mathfrak{q}$, for $\mathfrak{q} = m\mathcal{O}$.
The quotient $\Gamma(\mathfrak{q})/E_n(\mathcal{O}, \mathfrak{q})$ is a relative $SK_1$ group, which Bass, Milnor and Serre computed with Mennicke symbols and reciprocity laws for power residue symbols.
When $K$ has a real embedding this quotient vanishes, so $N \supseteq \Gamma(\mathfrak{q})$.
Example: for $K = \Q$, every finite-index subgroup of $SL_3(\Z)$ contains the kernel of reduction modulo some integer $m \ge 1$.
The hypothesis $n \ge 3$ is necessary: $SL_2(\Z)$ has subgroups of finite index that are not congruence subgroups.
When $K$ is totally imaginary the conclusion fails, but the obstruction is small: it is a finite cyclic group whose order is the number of roots of unity in $K$.
The same paper treats the symplectic groups $Sp_{2n}(\mathcal{O})$ for $n \ge 2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A congruence subgroup is a group of integer matrices described by congruences of the entries modulo some number.
For $2 \times 2$ matrices there are many finite-index subgroups that cannot be described this way.
For matrices of size at least $3$, the theorem says every finite-index subgroup is described by congruences.
So in higher rank, the finite quotients of these groups all come from arithmetic modulo $m$.

### L2 human

The theorem solved the congruence subgroup problem for $SL_n$, $n \ge 3$, over rings of integers, and the solution is phrased in algebraic K-theory.
It also gives $SK_1(\mathcal{O}) = 0$ for the ring of integers of every number field, so $K_1(\mathcal{O}) \cong \mathcal{O}^\times$.
The method, via relative $K_1$ groups and Mennicke symbols, became a model for later work on other arithmetic groups.
In terms of profinite completions, it says the congruence kernel of $SL_n(\mathcal{O})$ is trivial when $K$ has a real place.
