## Definition

Let a star be at distance $d$ from the Sun.
Let $a$ be one astronomical unit, the mean distance from the Earth to the Sun.
Place a segment of length $a$ at the Sun, perpendicular to the line from the Sun to the star.
The *annual parallax* of the star is the angle $p$ that this segment subtends at the star, so $\tan p = a / d$.
The *parsec* is the distance $d$ at which $p = 1''$.

## Remarks

Since $p$ is tiny, $p \approx a/d$ in radians, so the distance in parsecs is $d = 1/p$ with $p$ in arcseconds.
One parsec is about $206\,265$ astronomical units, or about $3.26$ light years.
Example: Proxima Centauri has $p \approx 0.768''$, so $d \approx 1.30$ pc.
Over a year the direction of a star traces on the [[celestial sphere|W7GD4WR497]] an ellipse with semi-major axis $p$.
Non-example: the yearly shift of the stars by aberration is not parallax; it has size about $20.5''$ for every star, whatever its distance.
The shift by the rotation of the Earth, with the Earth's radius as base, is diurnal parallax, which matters for the Moon and planets.
