## Summary

Geometrothermodynamics is a geometric formalism for equilibrium thermodynamics proposed by Hernando Quevedo.
It adds to the [[contact geometry of thermodynamics|ZTH9SSWCDG]] a metric on the thermodynamic phase space that is invariant under Legendre transformations.
The metric induces a metric on the equilibrium manifold of each system, so the result does not depend on which thermodynamic potential is used.
Its curvature is interpreted as a measure of thermodynamic interaction, and curvature singularities are compared with phase transitions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The equilibrium states of a material form a surface, and one can ask how curved that surface is.
To measure curvature one needs a way to measure distances, that is a metric.
Earlier proposals used metrics built from one particular potential, such as the entropy or the energy, and the answer could change with the choice.
Geometrothermodynamics picks metrics that do not change when one switches potential.
For the ideal gas the resulting curvature is zero, which matches the idea that its particles do not interact.
