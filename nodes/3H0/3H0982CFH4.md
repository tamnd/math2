## Definition

Let $n \ge 1$, and let $\set{-1, 1}^n$ be the Boolean cube, a group under coordinatewise multiplication, isomorphic to $(\Z/2\Z)^n$.
For a subset $S \subseteq \set{1, \dots, n}$, the *parity function* $\chi_S \colon \set{-1, 1}^n \to \set{-1, 1}$ is $\chi_S(x) = \prod_{i \in S} x_i$, with $\chi_\emptyset = 1$.
For a function $f \colon \set{-1, 1}^n \to \R$, its *Fourier coefficient* at $S$ is
$$\widehat{f}(S) = \frac{1}{2^n} \sum_{x \in \set{-1, 1}^n} f(x)\, \chi_S(x).$$
The *Fourier expansion* of $f$ is $f = \sum_{S \subseteq \set{1, \dots, n}} \widehat{f}(S)\, \chi_S$.

## Remarks

The parity functions are exactly the [[characters|H1WNVN5V7F]] of the group $\set{-1, 1}^n$, and they form an orthonormal basis for $\ip{f}{g} = 2^{-n}\sum_x f(x) g(x)$, by the [[basis theorem for characters|6K90GWZJ7K]].
So the expansion holds and is unique, and Parseval's identity $2^{-n}\sum_x f(x)^2 = \sum_S \widehat{f}(S)^2$ holds.
Up to the factor $2^{-n}$ and a change from $\set{0,1}$ to $\set{-1,1}$, the vector of coefficients is the [[Walsh–Hadamard transform|K784BA2WHV]] of the table of values of $f$.

Example: for majority on three inputs, $\mathrm{Maj}(x) = \frac{1}{2}(x_1 + x_2 + x_3) - \frac{1}{2}x_1x_2x_3$.
Check: at $(1, 1, 1)$ the right side is $\frac{3}{2} - \frac{1}{2} = 1$; at $(1, 1, -1)$ it is $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1$; at $(1, -1, -1)$ it is $-\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2} = -1$; the other points follow by symmetry and by $\mathrm{Maj}(-x) = -\mathrm{Maj}(x)$.

Non-example: the polynomial $x_1^2 x_2$ is not multilinear, so it is not a Fourier expansion in this sense.
On the cube $x_1^2 = 1$, so as a function it equals $\chi_{\set{2}}$; the expansion is unique only among multilinear polynomials.

The *degree* of $f$ is the largest $\abs{S}$ with $\widehat{f}(S) \ne 0$, and the *influence* of coordinate $i$ on $f$ with values in $\set{-1,1}$ is $\sum_{S \ni i} \widehat{f}(S)^2$.
