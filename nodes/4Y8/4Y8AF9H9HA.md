## Summary

An extensive-form game describes a game as a tree of moves.
It records who moves at each point and what that player can do.
It also records what the player knows about earlier moves, the chances of random events, and the final payoffs.
It models sequential games such as chess, poker and bargaining.
It reduces to a [[normal-form game|5MZTZRC3CZ]] when each player chooses a complete plan of action in advance.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a tree drawn from the top down.
At the root one player chooses a branch.
Then another player chooses a branch below it, and so on until a leaf, where each player receives a payoff.
If a player cannot see an earlier move, the nodes she cannot tell apart are grouped into one information set, often drawn with a dashed line.
Example: a firm decides whether to enter a market, and then the incumbent firm, having seen this, decides whether to fight a price war or to share the market.
