## Definition

A *population* is a group of organisms of one species that live in the same region.
*Population dynamics* is the branch of [[mathematical biology|YA4XXZZ6P8]] that studies the change in time of one or more populations.
It follows their sizes or densities, and often their structure by age or stage.
Its models are [[dynamical systems|BBZP1R872N]] whose state is a vector of nonnegative numbers, one for each population or class.
Their rule of change comes from births, deaths, immigration, emigration and interactions.

## Remarks

Example: the rule $N_{t+1} = 1.1 \, N_t$ for the number $N_t$ of deer in year $t$ is a model of population dynamics, with a constant growth of $10\%$ per year.
Non-example: the study of the shape of a single animal's bones is not population dynamics, because it has no population size that changes in time.
The field is often split into single-species models, structured models, and models of interacting species such as predators and prey.
