## Definition

Let $m > 0$, $k > 0$ and $c \ge 0$ be constants, and consider the linear oscillator with viscous damping
$$m \ddot x + c \dot x + k x = 0.$$
The term $c \dot x$ is the *damping* force, $c$ is the *damping coefficient*, and the *damping ratio* is
$$\zeta = \frac{c}{2 \sqrt{m k}}.$$
The oscillator is

1. *undamped* when $\zeta = 0$;
2. *underdamped* when $0 < \zeta < 1$;
3. *critically damped* when $\zeta = 1$;
4. *overdamped* when $\zeta > 1$.

## Remarks

With $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$ the equation reads $\ddot x + 2 \zeta \omega_0 \dot x + \omega_0^2 x = 0$, whose characteristic roots are $\omega_0 \big(-\zeta \pm \sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1}\big)$; the four cases correspond to imaginary, complex, double real and distinct real roots; see the [[damped harmonic oscillator|0RNXVVTJTJ]].
The energy $E = \frac{1}{2} m \dot x^2 + \frac{1}{2} k x^2$ satisfies $\dot E = -c \dot x^2 \le 0$.

Example: $m = 1$, $c = 2$, $k = 5$ gives $\zeta = 2 / (2 \sqrt 5) = 1/\sqrt 5 < 1$, so the oscillator is [[underdamped|0JJ6GDDE7Z]]; its solutions are $e^{-t}(c_1 \cos 2t + c_2 \sin 2t)$.

Non-example: a negative coefficient, $c = -2$ with $m = 1$, $k = 5$, is not damping: the solutions $e^{t}(c_1 \cos 2t + c_2 \sin 2t)$ grow, and the energy increases.

Other kinds of damping, such as dry (Coulomb) friction or quadratic drag, are not linear in $\dot x$ and are not covered by this definition.
