## Summary

Lie algebra cohomology is a sequence of vector spaces $H^n(\mathfrak{g}, M)$ attached to a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] $\mathfrak{g}$ and a $\mathfrak{g}$-[[module|TZAZ163QGK]] $M$.
It measures obstructions in Lie theory: $H^1$ classifies derivations up to inner ones, and $H^2$ classifies abelian extensions.
For a compact connected Lie group, the cohomology of its Lie algebra with real coefficients is the de Rham [[cohomology of the group|HKZABKRPN6]].
It is the Lie algebra analogue of [[group cohomology|YXJXA0Q52R]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cohomology turns a question of the form "can this be corrected?" into linear algebra.
For a Lie algebra, one looks at multilinear functions of Lie algebra elements, and at a rule $d$ that builds a new function from an old one, with $d \circ d = 0$.
The cohomology counts the functions killed by $d$, modulo those of the form $d$ of something.
For semisimple Lie algebras in characteristic $0$, the groups $H^1$ and $H^2$ with finite-dimensional coefficients vanish, and this explains many rigidity results.
