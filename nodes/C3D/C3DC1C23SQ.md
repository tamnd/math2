## Statement

A bridge theorem says that a logic has a syntactic property iff its algebras have a matching algebraic property.
Let $\vdash$ be a finitary, finitely [[algebraizable logic|XADWEW9TNP]] with equivalent [[quasivariety|WRXEKT4NQS]] semantics $K$.

**Theorem.**

1. (Maksimova) A superintuitionistic logic $L$ has the Craig interpolation property iff the variety of [[Heyting algebras|NG943XNQWH]] that validate $L$ has the [[amalgamation property|XSCSX39GEZ]].
2. (Beth property) $\vdash$ has the infinite [[Beth definability property|RJBDSA2ETP]] iff every [[epimorphism|RVXDSY04KG]] between members of $K$ is surjective.
3. (Deduction theorem) $\vdash$ has a deduction-detachment theorem, that is, a finite set $I(x, y)$ of formulas with $\Gamma, \varphi \vdash \psi$ iff $\Gamma \vdash I(\varphi, \psi)$, iff the principal relative congruences of $K$ are equationally definable: there is a finite set of equations $\tau(x, y, z, w)$ such that, for all $A \in K$, $(c, d)$ lies in the least $K$-congruence of $A$ containing $(a, b)$ iff $\tau(a, b, c, d)$ holds in $A$.
4. For [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]] and Heyting algebras, part 3 takes the form: $(c, d)$ lies in the congruence generated by $(a, b)$ iff $a \leftrightarrow b \le c \leftrightarrow d$.

Part 1 is due to Maksimova, part 3 to Blok and Pigozzi, and part 2 in this form to Blok and Hoogland, after Németi's work on cylindric algebras.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. Parts 2 and 3 need algebraizability: without an equivalent algebraic semantics there is no class $K$ in which to state the algebraic side.
2. In part 3 the set $I$ must be one finite set for all formulas; local versions of the deduction theorem correspond to weaker algebraic properties.
3. In part 1 the logic and the variety match by the [[isomorphism theorem|8ZFJ67HTWR]], and the interpolant is for $\to$; for normal modal logics the corresponding algebraic property is superamalgamation.

### Consequences

1. By the classification of [[Maksimova's interpolation theorem|M3RTNGG3J5]], exactly seven consistent intermediate logics have a variety with the amalgamation property.
2. Intuitionistic logic has the Beth property, so every epimorphism of Heyting algebras is surjective.
3. Part 4 gives the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] of intuitionistic logic an algebraic proof, with $I(x, y) = \set{x \to y}$.

### Converse

Each part is an equivalence, so each direction is a separate result: the syntactic property gives the algebraic one, and conversely.

### Generalizations

1. Interpolation, Beth properties and deduction theorems each come in several variants, and each variant has its own algebraic counterpart.
2. For algebraizable logics, a local deduction theorem, with a family of sets $I$ in place of one, corresponds to the relative congruence extension property of $K$.

## History

Maksimova proved part 1 in 1977 and its modal analog shortly after, with a complete list of the superintuitionistic logics that have interpolation.
Blok and Pigozzi proved part 3 as part of their theory of algebraizable logics.
Németi related the Beth property to surjectivity of epimorphisms for cylindric algebras, and Blok and Hoogland gave the general form.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some properties of a logic are hard to study by looking at proofs.
For algebraizable logics, each such property often matches a simple property of the algebras.
For example, a logic has interpolants iff its algebras can be glued together along common parts.

### L2 human

1. In a Heyting algebra, let $a = p$ and $b = 1$; the congruence generated by $(a, b)$ makes $p$ equal to $1$.
2. Since $p \leftrightarrow 1 = p$, the theorem says that $(c, d)$ is identified iff $p \le c \leftrightarrow d$.
3. On the logic side, $p \vdash \gamma \leftrightarrow \delta$ iff $\vdash p \to (\gamma \leftrightarrow \delta)$, the deduction theorem.

The typical use: prove a logical property by checking the algebraic one in a small family of algebras, or refute it by a counterexample algebra.
The trap: interpolation for implication and interpolation for consequence can differ, and for modal logics they match different amalgamation properties.

### L3 human

Bridge theorems are what make abstract algebraic logic a working method rather than a classification.
Amalgamation and superamalgamation are categorical properties of the variety, and epimorphism surjectivity is the categorical form of definability.
