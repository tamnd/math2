## Summary

The Babcock–Leighton model is a model of the [[solar dynamo|NPVYXT6WHA]].
In it, the poloidal magnetic field of the Sun is regenerated at the surface, from the decay of tilted bipolar sunspot groups.
Differential rotation winds the poloidal field into a toroidal field below the surface.
The toroidal field emerges as bipolar active regions, and their systematic tilt gives a poloidal field of reversed sign.
Modern versions, called flux-transport dynamos, add a meridional circulation.
It carries the field toward the poles at the surface and back toward the equator deep inside.
Observations support the model.
The strength of the polar field at the end of a cycle is a good predictor of the strength of the next cycle.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Sunspots come in pairs of opposite magnetic polarity, and the pair is slightly tilted: the leading spot is closer to the equator.
When the spots break up, the leading parts from both hemispheres cancel across the equator, while the trailing parts drift to the poles.
There they cancel the old polar field and build a new one of opposite sign.
So the sunspots themselves are the machine that flips the Sun's field every cycle.
