## Summary

The ADM formalism, named after Arnowitt, Deser and Misner, is the Hamiltonian form of general relativity.
It slices spacetime into spacelike hypersurfaces and describes the metric by the metric of each slice, a lapse function and a shift vector.
The [[Einstein–Hilbert action|JPE3A1SN7V]] then becomes the action of a constrained [[Hamiltonian system|0EDKGA2FAK]], with the lapse and the shift as Lagrange multipliers.
It is the starting point of canonical quantum gravity, of the definition of the ADM energy and of most methods of numerical relativity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Spacetime can be cut into a stack of slices, each slice being all of space at one moment.
The ADM formalism describes how the shape of the slices changes from one to the next, as in a film made of frames.
The lapse says how much proper time passes between two slices, and the shift says how the spatial coordinates slide from one slice to the next.
For Minkowski space sliced by $t = \text{const}$, every slice is flat $\R^3$, the lapse is $1$ and the shift is $0$.
