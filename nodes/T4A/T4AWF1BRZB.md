## Summary

A special Jordan algebra is a [[Jordan algebra|6C2MCVB8C7]] that can be realized inside an associative algebra, with the symmetrized product $x \circ y = \frac{1}{2}(xy + yx)$.
Jordan algebras that are not special are called exceptional.
The algebras of Hermitian matrices over the reals, complex numbers and quaternions are special, while the [[Albert algebra|1MSMVDRAVD]] is exceptional.
The [[Shirshov–Cohn theorem|H4DT2XM8GJ]] and [[Macdonald's theorem|BWYX23HJMF]] show that identities in few variables can be tested in special Jordan algebras.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every associative algebra, such as an algebra of matrices, gives a Jordan algebra by averaging $xy$ and $yx$.
A Jordan algebra is special when it sits inside one of these, so that its product really is an averaged matrix product.
Most Jordan algebras met in practice are special, for example the real symmetric matrices.
The surprise found by Albert is that a few Jordan algebras are not special, and the $27$-dimensional Albert algebra is the basic one.
