## Definition

Let $C$ be the [[elasticity tensor|SX32X1D537]] of a linear elastic material, and let $e$ be a unit vector.
For a rotation $Q$, let $(Q \star C)_{ijkl} = Q_{ip} Q_{jq} Q_{kr} Q_{ls} C_{pqrs}$.
The material is *transversely isotropic* with axis $e$ if $Q \star C = C$ for every rotation $Q$ about the axis $e$.

## Remarks

Example: with axis $e = e_3$ and Voigt notation, the stiffness matrix of a transversely isotropic material has the form
$$\begin{pmatrix} C_{11} & C_{12} & C_{13} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ C_{12} & C_{11} & C_{13} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ C_{13} & C_{13} & C_{33} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & C_{44} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & C_{44} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & C_{66} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad C_{66} = \tfrac{1}{2}(C_{11} - C_{12}),$$
with the $5$ independent constants $C_{11}, C_{12}, C_{13}, C_{33}, C_{44}$.
Non-example: a cubic material has $C_{11} = C_{33}$, $C_{12} = C_{13}$ and $C_{66} = C_{44}$.
If $C_{44} \ne \tfrac{1}{2}(C_{11} - C_{12})$, the rotation by $\pi/4$ about $e_3$ changes $C$.
So such a cubic material is not transversely isotropic.
An isotropic material is transversely isotropic for every axis; many authors exclude this case.
The plane normal to $e$ is the plane of isotropy.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The elasticity tensor stays the same when the material is turned by any angle about one fixed axis.
So all directions in the plane across that axis behave alike.
