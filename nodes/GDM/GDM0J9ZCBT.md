## Summary

A beam theory is a one-dimensional model of a slender elastic body that carries loads mainly by bending.
Examples of such bodies are girders, shafts and rulers.
It replaces the three-dimensional equations of [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] by functions of the position along the axis.
These are the deflection, the rotation of the cross-sections, the [[bending moment|Q6XAPXMGH0]] and the shear force.
An assumption on how cross-sections deform turns the three-dimensional problem into ordinary differential equations.
The main examples are the [[Euler–Bernoulli beam equation|13FGKM7NQ0]] and [[Timoshenko–Ehrenfest beam theory|K4W0JQ1K6Z]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A plank across a stream sags when you stand on it.
To compute the sag you do not need to know what happens at every point inside the wood.
It is enough to follow the centre line of the plank and to know how stiff each slice is against bending.
Beam theory makes this idea exact: it replaces a long solid by a line with a bending stiffness.
For example, it says that a plank sags eight times more if it is twice as long and carries the same load at the middle.
