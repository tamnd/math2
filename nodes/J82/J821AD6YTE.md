## Summary

The heat transfer coefficient $h$ is the [[heat flux|1Y36434YTE]] from a surface into a fluid, divided by the temperature difference.
This difference is the surface temperature minus the fluid temperature.
It packs the whole effect of [[convection|RK24AYNG4Z]] near the surface into one number.
Unlike conductivity, it depends on the flow, the geometry and the fluid, not only on a material.
It enters [[Newton's law of cooling|02HB5F40VR]] and the [[Biot|ZYJRK1RVP5]] and [[Nusselt|THH3V9AHDN]] numbers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a windy day you feel colder than on a calm day at the same air temperature.
The wind raises the heat transfer coefficient of your skin, so more heat leaves for each degree of difference.
Typical values are a few to about $25$ watts per square metre per kelvin for still air.
They reach thousands for water flowing fast or boiling.
