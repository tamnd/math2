## Summary

The initial value formulation treats the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]] as an evolution problem, like the Cauchy problem for the wave equation.
One prescribes the geometry of space at one instant: a Riemannian metric and a second fundamental form on a 3-manifold.
One then asks for a spacetime that develops from these data.
The data cannot be arbitrary: they must satisfy the constraint equations.
Once they do, a solution exists, is unique up to diffeomorphism, and depends continuously on the data, so general relativity is a well-posed deterministic theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To predict the future in Newtonian mechanics, one needs positions and velocities now.
In general relativity the role of the position is played by the shape of space now, and the role of the velocity by how this shape is changing.
These two pieces of information are not free: four of the ten Einstein equations are conditions that they must satisfy at each instant.
The other six equations then tell how space evolves in time.
For example, flat space $\R^3$ that is not changing is valid initial data, and it develops into Minkowski spacetime.
