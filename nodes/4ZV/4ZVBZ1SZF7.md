## Summary

The Rosseland mean opacity is a weighted harmonic mean of the opacity over all frequencies.
It is the single number that governs the flow of radiation through an optically thick medium near local thermodynamic equilibrium.
The interior of a star is the main example.
With it, the radiative energy flux is a diffusion flux proportional to the temperature gradient.
It enters the equations of stellar structure for radiative energy transport.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The opacity of a gas depends strongly on the frequency of the light.
Radiation leaks out of a star mainly through the frequencies where the gas is most transparent, like water through the biggest holes of a sieve.
So the right average gives most weight to small opacities, which is what a harmonic mean does.
The Rosseland mean weights each frequency by how much the black-body intensity there changes with temperature.
For example, if half of the relevant spectrum has opacity $1$ and the other half has opacity $100$, the Rosseland mean is about $2$, far from the arithmetic mean $50.5$.
