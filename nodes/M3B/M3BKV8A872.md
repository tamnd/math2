## Definition

Let $\mathfrak g$ be a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over a field $k$.
For subspaces $\mathfrak a, \mathfrak b \subseteq \mathfrak g$, let $[\mathfrak a, \mathfrak b]$ be the span of all $[a, b]$ with $a \in \mathfrak a$ and $b \in \mathfrak b$.
The *derived series* of $\mathfrak g$ is given by $\mathfrak g^{(0)} = \mathfrak g$ and $\mathfrak g^{(i+1)} = [\mathfrak g^{(i)}, \mathfrak g^{(i)}]$ for $i \ge 0$.
The Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is *solvable* if $\mathfrak g^{(n)} = 0$ for some $n \ge 0$.

## Remarks

Each $\mathfrak g^{(i)}$ is a [[Lie ideal|JWMTXQ8A3A]] of $\mathfrak g$, and $\mathfrak g^{(i)} / \mathfrak g^{(i+1)}$ is abelian.
An equivalent condition: there is a chain of [[subalgebras|WKSSTQ69AK]] $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak a_0 \supseteq \mathfrak a_1 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq \mathfrak a_n = 0$ in which each $\mathfrak a_{i+1}$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak a_i$ and each $\mathfrak a_i / \mathfrak a_{i+1}$ is abelian.
Subalgebras and quotients of solvable Lie algebras are solvable; if an ideal $\mathfrak a$ and the quotient $\mathfrak g / \mathfrak a$ are solvable, then $\mathfrak g$ is solvable.

Example: the two-dimensional Lie algebra with basis $x, y$ and $[x, y] = y$.
Its derived algebra $\mathfrak g^{(1)}$ is spanned by $y$, and $\mathfrak g^{(2)} = [ky, ky] = 0$.

Non-example: the [[special linear Lie algebra|1ZAT1Z967A]] $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ over a field of characteristic not $2$, with basis $e, h, f$ and $[h, e] = 2e$, $[h, f] = -2f$, $[e, f] = h$.
The brackets $e = \tfrac12 [h, e]$, $f = -\tfrac12 [h, f]$ and $h = [e, f]$ lie in $\mathfrak g^{(1)}$, so $\mathfrak g^{(1)} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ and the derived series never reaches $0$.
