## Summary

A Feynman diagram is a [[graph|00C0BR7D7H]] that labels one term in the perturbation series of a quantum field theory.
Its edges carry [[propagators|K9SR95P4NJ]], and its internal vertices carry the coupling constants of the interaction.
Its value is an integral over the positions or momenta of the internal vertices.
By [[Wick's theorem|FZ0SKX44NF]], each order of perturbation theory is a finite sum over diagrams.
Diagrams organize the computation of scattering amplitudes and make divergences and [[renormalization|1X0W3AEBEJ]] visible graph by graph.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Feynman diagram is a drawing of particles moving and interacting.
Lines are particles travelling between points, and the points where lines meet are interactions.
Each picture stands for a definite integral, and the sum of the pictures gives the probability amplitude of a process.
For example, in the $\phi^4$ model two particles can scatter through one vertex where four lines meet; this is the simplest diagram for that process.
