## Summary

General equilibrium theory studies an economy as a whole, with many consumers, firms and goods.
It looks for prices at which all markets clear at the same time.
It asks whether such prices exist, whether they are unique and stable, and whether the resulting allocation is efficient.
Its central model is the [[Arrow–Debreu model|SVGN7R3RDY]], and its main results include the existence of [[competitive equilibrium|YTBPB52K27]] and the [[fundamental theorems of welfare economics|J6KA8R1H5Z]].
It contrasts with partial equilibrium analysis, which looks at one market and holds the other prices fixed.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A change in one market spills over into others: if bread gets dearer, people buy less butter and bakers hire more workers.
General equilibrium theory keeps track of all these links together.
It looks for a list of prices, one per good, at which for every good the total amount people want equals the total amount available.
For example, take two people who swap apples and oranges.
The theory asks for the price ratio at which the apples one person wants to sell are exactly the apples the other wants to buy.
