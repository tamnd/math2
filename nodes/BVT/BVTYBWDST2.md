## Proof

1. Let $H$ be a [[subgroup|0N0PXCTYA7]] of the [[additive group of integers|0BSCJA5G53]] $(\Z, +)$. The group operation is $+$, the identity is $0$, and the inverse of $a$ is $-a$.
2. In additive notation, the $k$-th power of $a \in \Z$ is the integer $ka$. Indeed, the $0$-th power is $0 = 0a$, and the $(k+1)$-th power is $ka + a = (k+1)a$.
3. For $k > 0$, the $(-k)$-th power of $a$ is the $k$-th power of $-a$, which is $k(-a) = (-k)a$ by step 2.
4. So the [[cyclic subgroup|0YT586TKXF]] generated by $a$ is $\set{ka : k \in \Z} = a\Z$. In particular $\Z = 1\Z$ is a [[cyclic group|0B85Q41RP6]].
5. Since [[subgroups of cyclic groups are cyclic|0KB1ND7XDS]], $H = a\Z$ for some integer $a$.
6. Put $n = \abs{a} \geq 0$. Since $ka = (-k)(-a)$ for all $k$, we have $a\Z = (-a)\Z$. So $H = n\Z$, which proves existence.
7. For uniqueness, let $n, n' \geq 0$ with $n\Z = n'\Z$. If $n = 0$, then $n\Z = \set{0}$, so $n' = n' \cdot 1 \in \set{0}$ and $n' = 0$. Likewise $n' = 0$ forces $n = 0$.
8. Suppose $n, n' > 0$. Since $n \in n\Z = n'\Z$, we have $n = n'c$ for an integer $c$. As $n, n' > 0$, $c \geq 1$, so $n \geq n'$.
9. By the same argument with the roles exchanged, $n' \geq n$. So $n = n'$.
10. Hence there is exactly one integer $n \geq 0$ with $H = n\Z$.

## Idea

The integers form a cyclic group generated by $1$, so each subgroup is generated by one integer $a$. The subgroup generated by $a$ is the set of multiples of $a$. The sign of $a$ does not matter, and a nonnegative generator is unique because two positive integers that divide each other are equal.
