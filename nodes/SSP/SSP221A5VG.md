## Summary

The Coleman–Noll procedure is a method of continuum mechanics that turns the second law into restrictions on [[constitutive equations|Q4C55V47A8]].
One requires the [[Clausius–Duhem inequality|MKXA9E1Z2T]] to hold in every process that the balance laws allow.
Since the rates of the state variables can be chosen freely at a given instant, the coefficients of these rates must vanish.
It is the central tool of [[rational thermodynamics|3450K0RK9F]] and gives, for example, the free energy as a potential for stress and entropy.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose one proposes a law for how a material responds to stretching and heating.
The Coleman–Noll procedure checks this law against the second law in every imaginable process, not just in the ones that happen to be tested.
Because one can stretch or heat a sample at any rate one likes, terms that could change sign with the rate must disappear.
For a rubber band, this forces the force to be the derivative of a single energy function.
