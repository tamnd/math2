## Statement

Let $\beta \ge 0$ and $h \in \R$.
For $N \ge 2$, let $(g_{ij})_{1 \le i < j \le N}$ be independent standard Gaussian random variables, and let
$$F_N = \frac{1}{N} \mathbb{E} \log \sum_{\sigma \in \set{-1,+1}^N} \exp\Big( \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{i < j} g_{ij} \sigma_i \sigma_j + h \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i \Big)$$
be the free energy of the [[Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model|MMSZK6CYRX]].
For a [[probability measure|059TJN0NZ1]] $\zeta$ on $[0, 1]$, let $\alpha(q) = \zeta([0, q])$, and let $\Phi_\zeta \colon [0, 1] \times \R \to \R$ solve the Parisi equation
$$\partial_t \Phi_\zeta = -\frac{\beta^2}{2} \Big( \partial_x^2 \Phi_\zeta + \alpha(t) \, (\partial_x \Phi_\zeta)^2 \Big), \qquad \Phi_\zeta(1, x) = \log \cosh x.$$
Define the Parisi functional
$$\mathcal{P}(\zeta) = \log 2 + \Phi_\zeta(0, h) - \frac{\beta^2}{2} \int_0^1 q \, \alpha(q) \, dq.$$
Then $F_N$ converges as $N \to \infty$, and
$$\lim_{N \to \infty} F_N = \inf_{\zeta} \mathcal{P}(\zeta),$$
where the infimum runs over all probability measures $\zeta$ on $[0, 1]$.

## Remarks

For $\zeta$ with finite support, $\alpha$ is a step function, and the Parisi equation is solved step by step with the Cole–Hopf transform.
The general case follows by continuity in $\zeta$.
Parisi found the formula around 1980 with the non-rigorous [[replica trick|4Y5V47G9NH]].
Guerra proved in 2003 the upper bound $F_N \le \mathcal{P}(\zeta) + o(1)$ for every $\zeta$ by an interpolation argument.
Talagrand proved the matching lower bound in 2006, which completed the proof.

Example: take $h = 0$ and $\zeta = \delta_0$, so that $\alpha \equiv 1$.
The Cole–Hopf transform gives $\Phi_\zeta(0, x) = \log \mathbb{E} \cosh(x + \beta Z) = \log \cosh x + \beta^2 / 2$ with $Z$ standard Gaussian.
Then $\mathcal{P}(\delta_0) = \log 2 + \beta^2/2 - \beta^2/4 = \log 2 + \beta^2/4$, the annealed free energy.
This value is the true limit for $\beta < 1$, while for $\beta > 1$ the infimum is strictly smaller.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model is a magnet with random interactions between all pairs of spins.
Its free energy per spin tends to a limit, and the Parisi formula says what this limit is.
The answer is a minimum over all ways to spread a unit of mass on the interval $[0, 1]$.
The minimizing mass describes how similar two typical low-energy states are.

### L2 human

The infimum is attained at a unique measure $\zeta$, the Parisi measure.
In the physics picture, and in rigorous form for generic mixed $p$-spin models, it is the limiting law of the overlap $\frac{1}{N} \sum_i \sigma^1_i \sigma^2_i$ of two independent samples from the Gibbs measure.
At high temperature it is a single point mass, which is called replica symmetry.
At low temperature it is expected to have a continuous part, which is called full replica symmetry breaking.
Panchenko later extended the formula to mixed $p$-spin models, with a proof based on the ultrametric structure of the overlaps.
The same variational form appears in many problems on random constraint satisfaction and on high-dimensional statistics.
