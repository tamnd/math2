## Definition

Let $f \colon \R^n \to \R$ be a [[differentiable function|0K57YG3BB8]] with [[gradient|0ABK9V9Y64]] $\nabla f$.
Let $x_0 \in \R^n$ be a starting point and let $(\eta_k)_{k \ge 0}$ be positive numbers, the *step sizes*.
*Gradient descent* is the method that produces the sequence $(x_k)$ defined by
$$x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta_k \nabla f(x_k) \qquad (k \ge 0).$$

## Remarks

The step sizes may be constant, decreasing, or chosen at each step by a line search.

Example: $f(x) = \tfrac12 x^T A x$ with $A$ symmetric positive definite and constant step $\eta = 1/\lambda_{\max}$, where $\lambda_{\min}$ and $\lambda_{\max}$ are the smallest and largest eigenvalues of $A$.
Then $x_{k+1} = (I - \eta A) x_k$, and the eigenvalues of $I - \eta A$ lie in $[0, 1 - \lambda_{\min}/\lambda_{\max}]$, so $\norm{x_k} \le (1 - \lambda_{\min}/\lambda_{\max})^k \norm{x_0} \to 0$.

Non-example: [[Newton's method|TNQZVR6TFN]] $x_{k+1} = x_k - (\nabla^2 f(x_k))^{-1} \nabla f(x_k)$, with $\nabla^2 f$ the Hessian matrix, is not gradient descent, since its step is not a positive multiple of $-\nabla f(x_k)$ in general.

If $f$ is [[convex|07GR3TNW4H]], has a minimiser $x^*$, and $\nabla f$ is $L$-[[Lipschitz|0AW1E6YTJY]], then with $\eta_k = 1/L$ one has
$$f(x_k) - f(x^*) \le \frac{L \norm{x_0 - x^*}^2}{2k} \qquad (k \ge 1).$$
