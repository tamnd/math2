## Definition

Let a photon of energy $\varepsilon$ scatter off a free electron of energy $\gamma m_e c^2$.
Here $m_e$ is the electron mass, $c$ the speed of light and $\gamma$ the [[Lorentz factor|2E9AJVZS6E]] of the electron.
Let $\varepsilon_1$ be the energy of the photon after the scattering.
The scattering is *inverse Compton scattering* when the electron transfers energy to the photon, so $\varepsilon_1 > \varepsilon$.
The term is used mainly for relativistic electrons, $\gamma \gg 1$, and photons with $\varepsilon \ll \gamma m_e c^2$.

## Remarks

Example: let $\gamma \gg 1$ and let the photons be isotropic, in the Thomson regime $\gamma \varepsilon \ll m_e c^2$.
Then the mean energy after scattering is about $\frac{4}{3} \gamma^2 \varepsilon$, so $\varepsilon_1 > \varepsilon$ on average.
The net power transferred to the photons is $P = \frac{4}{3} \sigma_T c \gamma^2 \beta^2 U_{\text{rad}}$.
Here $\beta = v/c$ with $v$ the speed of the electron, $\sigma_T$ is the [[Thomson|Z7BN231HNA]] cross-section and $U_{\text{rad}}$ is the energy density of the radiation.
Non-example: an X-ray photon scattering off an electron at rest loses energy; this is ordinary Compton scattering.
The loss is given by the Compton formula $\lambda_1 - \lambda = \frac{h}{m_e c}(1 - \cos\theta)$.
Here $\lambda, \lambda_1$ are the wavelengths before and after, $h$ is the Planck constant and $\theta$ the scattering angle.
The ratio of inverse Compton to [[synchrotron|TXJMA0HPG7]] power of the same electron is $U_{\text{rad}} / U_B$, where $U_B$ is the energy density of the magnetic field.
