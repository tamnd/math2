## Summary

Alternatives to general relativity are theories of gravitation that differ from Einstein's [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]].
They change the field content, the field equations, the dimension or the geometry of spacetime, as [[Lovelock's theorem|DW4CF8A42M]] shows they must.
They serve as foils for experimental tests and as attempts to explain dark energy or dark matter.
Some arise as low-energy limits of candidate quantum theories of gravity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

General relativity has passed every test so far, but physicists still ask whether a different theory could do as well.
Proposed alternatives add extra fields to the metric, change Einstein's equations, or allow extra dimensions.
For example, in Brans–Dicke theory the strength of gravity is set by a field that can vary in space and time.
Each alternative predicts small departures from general relativity, and experiments then rule it out or limit its parameters.
