## Example

Let $G = S_3$ be the [[symmetric group on three letters|09H9Y0Q11W]].
We compose permutations from right to left, so in $\sigma\tau$ we apply $\tau$ first.
Let $H = \langle (1\,2) \rangle = \{e, (1\,2)\}$ and $K = \langle (1\,3) \rangle = \{e, (1\,3)\}$.
These are [[subgroups|0X1JXT47GA]] of order $2$.
We show that their [[product|0PT52XQZD7]] $HK$ is not a subgroup of $S_3$.

1. The product $(1\,2)(1\,3)$ sends $1 \mapsto 3$, $3 \mapsto 2$ and $2 \mapsto 1$. Thus $(1\,2)(1\,3) = (1\,3\,2)$.
2. Thus $HK = \{e, (1\,3), (1\,2), (1\,3\,2)\}$, a set with $4$ elements.
3. The [[product formula|08PY4GNAPK]] agrees: $H \cap K = \{e\}$, so $|HK| = 2 \cdot 2 / 1 = 4$.
4. By [[Lagrange's theorem|3C53TD]], the order of a subgroup of $S_3$ divides $6$.
5. The number $4$ does not divide $6$. Thus $HK$ is not a subgroup.
6. A direct check agrees: $(1\,3\,2) \in HK$, but $(1\,3\,2)^2 = (1\,2\,3)$ is not in $HK$.

## Remarks

The product $(1\,3)(1\,2)$ sends $1 \mapsto 2$, $2 \mapsto 3$ and $3 \mapsto 1$, so it is $(1\,2\,3)$.
Thus $KH = \{e, (1\,2), (1\,3), (1\,2\,3)\}$, and $HK \ne KH$.
This agrees with the criterion that $HK$ is a subgroup if and only if $HK = KH$.
Neither $H$ nor $K$ is [[normal|0CB0DX5BVV]] in $S_3$, so the theorem that a [[product with a normal subgroup is a subgroup|0TXWV1JGG1]] does not apply.
The [[subgroup generated by|0RP7VWQ0YN]] $H \cup K$ is all of $S_3$, which is larger than $HK$.
In $S_4$, the same two subgroups give a product with $4$ elements, and $4$ divides $24$.
This product is still not a subgroup, by step 6, so divisibility alone does not prove that a product is a subgroup.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the swap of $1$ and $2$, and the swap of $1$ and $3$.
Multiply each of $e$ and the first swap by each of $e$ and the second swap.
You get only $4$ permutations.
A subgroup of a group with $6$ elements cannot have $4$ elements.
Thus the products do not form a subgroup.

### L2 human

The product formula shows the failure without a long computation.
Two subgroups of order $2$ that meet only in $e$ give $4$ products, and $4$ does not divide $6$.

The typical use is as the standard first example of a product of subgroups that is not a subgroup.
A trap is to think that $HK$ is the subgroup generated by $H$ and $K$.
Here that subgroup is $S_3$, but $HK$ has only $4$ elements.
A second trap is the order of the factors: $(1\,2)(1\,3)$ and $(1\,3)(1\,2)$ are different permutations.
