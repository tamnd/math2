## Statement

Let $f \colon [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be a [[continuous function|0X3WGX95FE]].
Suppose that $f$ is of [[exponential order|TKZAV3KR86]] with a constant $c$. That is, there is $M > 0$ with $|f(t)| \le M e^{ct}$ for all $t \ge 0$.
Then the [[Laplace transform|0V49JEZW06]] $F(s) = \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\}(s) = \int_0^\infty f(t) e^{-st}\, dt$ is defined for every real $s > c$. That is, the [[Laplace integral|05XT5AVJBK]] converges for each such $s$.

## Remarks

The reason is a comparison. For $s > c$ we have $|f(t) e^{-st}| \le M e^{-(s-c)t}$, and the integral of the right side over $[0, \infty)$ equals $M/(s-c)$.
So the integral of $f(t) e^{-st}$ converges absolutely, and $|F(s)| \le M/(s-c)$.
