## Summary

The capital asset pricing model (CAPM) says that the expected excess return of an asset is proportional to its beta.
Beta measures how much the asset's return moves with the return of the market portfolio.
The model follows from [[modern portfolio theory|ZBDA8W9D6V]] when all investors hold mean–variance [[efficient|SCTRT97C2R]] portfolios and agree on means and covariances.
It was developed in the 1960s by William Sharpe, John Lintner and others.
Only risk that cannot be diversified away is rewarded.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some risk can be removed by spreading money across many assets, and some cannot, because the whole market goes up and down together.
CAPM says that investors are paid only for the second kind.
The beta of an asset counts how strongly it follows the market.
If the safe rate is $2\%$, the market is expected to earn $8\%$, and an asset has beta $1.5$, then CAPM predicts $2\% + 1.5 \cdot 6\% = 11\%$.
