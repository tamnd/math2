## Summary

Munk's model is a linear model of the wind-driven circulation of an ocean basin.
In it, the [[Sverdrup balance|VQ5Z44PXXR]] of the interior is closed by lateral friction.
It was proposed by Walter Munk in 1950.
It predicts a western boundary current of width $(A_H / \beta)^{1/3}$.
Here $A_H$ is a lateral eddy [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] and $\beta$ is the northward gradient of the Coriolis parameter.
With realistic winds it gave the first quantitative account of the subtropical and subpolar gyres and of currents such as the Gulf Stream.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Winds over an ocean basin set the water turning in huge loops called gyres.
In the open ocean the flow is slow and set by the winds alone.
Near the western coast the water must return in a narrow, fast current, and something must slow that current down.
In Munk's model it is the sideways rubbing of fast water against slower water around it, which is lateral friction.
For example, with $A_H = 10^3 \ \mathrm{m^2/s}$ and $\beta = 2 \times 10^{-11} \ \mathrm{m^{-1} s^{-1}}$, the boundary current is about $(5 \times 10^{13})^{1/3} \approx 37$ km wide.
