## Statement

Consider a dry atmosphere that is an ideal gas with specific gas constant $R$, described in pressure coordinates $(x, y, p)$ with $x$ eastward and $y$ northward.
Let $f \ne 0$ be a constant Coriolis parameter, $\Phi$ the geopotential and $T$ the temperature.
Assume hydrostatic balance $\partial \Phi / \partial p = -R T / p$.
Assume that the horizontal wind is the geostrophic wind $\mathbf{v}_g = (u_g, v_g)$ with $u_g = -f^{-1} \, \partial \Phi / \partial y$ and $v_g = f^{-1} \, \partial \Phi / \partial x$, derivatives taken at constant $p$.
Then
$$\frac{\partial u_g}{\partial \ln p} = \frac{R}{f} \frac{\partial T}{\partial y}, \qquad \frac{\partial v_g}{\partial \ln p} = -\frac{R}{f} \frac{\partial T}{\partial x}.$$
Consequently, for pressures $p_1 < p_0$, let $\mathbf{v}_T = \mathbf{v}_g(p_1) - \mathbf{v}_g(p_0)$ be the *thermal wind*.
Then
$$\mathbf{v}_T = \frac{R}{f} \ln\left(\frac{p_0}{p_1}\right) \mathbf{k} \times \nabla_p \bar T.$$
Here $\mathbf{k}$ is the upward unit vector and $\bar T$ is the mean of $T$ over $\ln p$ between $p_1$ and $p_0$.

## Remarks

The proof differentiates the [[geostrophic|CZ1S1P465Y]] relations with respect to $p$ and exchanges the order of derivatives: $\partial u_g / \partial p = -f^{-1} \partial_y (\partial \Phi / \partial p) = (R / (f p)) \, \partial T / \partial y$.
Integration in $\ln p$ from $\ln p_0$ to $\ln p_1$ gives the second formula.

So the vertical shear of the geostrophic wind is parallel to the isotherms of the layer, with cold air to the left in the northern hemisphere ($f > 0$).
Example: in mid-latitudes of the northern hemisphere the temperature decreases northward, $\partial T / \partial y < 0$.
Since $p$ decreases upward, $u_g$ increases with height, which explains the westerly jet streams.
With $f = 10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$, $R = 287$ J/(kg K), $\partial T / \partial y = -5$ K per $1000$ km and $p_0 / p_1 = 4$, the shear is $u_g(p_1) - u_g(p_0) \approx 287 \cdot 5 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 1.39 / 10^{-4} \approx 20$ m/s.

In a Boussinesq fluid with buoyancy $b$ and height $z$, the same argument gives $f \, \partial u_g / \partial z = -\partial b / \partial y$ and $f \, \partial v_g / \partial z = \partial b / \partial x$.
The relation fails near the equator, where $f \to 0$ and geostrophic balance does not hold.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When the air to the north is colder than the air to the south, the wind from the west gets stronger as one goes up.
This happens because cold air is denser, so pressure falls faster with height in the cold air, which tilts the pressure surfaces.
The rotation of the Earth then turns this growing pressure difference into a growing wind.
This is why the strongest winds, the jet streams, sit high in the atmosphere above sharp temperature contrasts.

### L2 human

The thermal wind relation combines the two leading balances of large-scale flow: geostrophic balance in the horizontal and hydrostatic balance in the vertical.
It links the wind field and the temperature field, so a forecaster can infer the vertical shear from a temperature map.
A wind that turns clockwise with height in the northern hemisphere (veering) indicates warm advection, and a wind that turns anticlockwise (backing) indicates cold advection.
In quasi-geostrophic theory the relation holds automatically, since the wind and the buoyancy both come from one stream function.
A flow with vertical shear in thermal wind balance stores available potential energy that baroclinic instability can release.
