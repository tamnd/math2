## Summary

The Born–Oppenheimer approximation separates the motion of the electrons and of the nuclei in a molecule.
One first solves the electronic problem with the nuclei held fixed, which gives an energy $E(R)$ for each nuclear configuration $R$, the potential energy surface.
Then the nuclei move as quantum particles in the potential $E(R)$.
It works because nuclei are thousands of times heavier than electrons, and it is the basis of almost all of quantum chemistry.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a molecule, the light electrons move much faster than the heavy nuclei.
So the electrons adjust almost instantly to wherever the nuclei are, as if the nuclei stood still.
One computes the electron energy for each frozen arrangement of nuclei and treats it as a landscape on which the nuclei then move.
For the hydrogen molecule, this landscape is a curve in the distance between the two protons, with a minimum at the bond length.
