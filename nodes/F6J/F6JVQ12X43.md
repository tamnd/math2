## Summary

The internal energy of a thermodynamic system is the energy contained in it.
It excludes the kinetic energy of the motion of the system as a whole and its potential energy in external fields.
It is a [[state function|T8J5XRYBZZ]], defined up to an additive constant.
The first law of thermodynamics says that it changes by the heat absorbed plus the [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] done on the system.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A gas is made of molecules that move and push on each other.
The internal energy is the total energy of all this hidden motion and interaction.
You can raise it by heating the gas or by compressing it.
For a simple gas such as helium at low density, the internal energy, counted from a suitable zero, is proportional to the temperature.
Double the absolute temperature and you double this energy.
