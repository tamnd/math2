## Statement

Let $G$ be a [[compact group|EVHCE5VR6Y]] with [[Haar measure|VY0HXD3GC4]] $dg$ of total mass $1$.
Let $\widehat{G}$ be a set of representatives of the classes of continuous [[irreducible|FX3NEFYFFN]] [[unitary representations|74BPYJNZTM]] of $G$, where $\pi$ has dimension $d_\pi$.
For $f \in L^2(G)$ let $\hat f(\pi) = \int_G f(g) \pi(g)^* \, dg$ be its [[Fourier coefficients|2V97NSWT49]], and let $\norm{A}_{\mathrm{HS}} = \tr(A^* A)^{1/2}$ be the [[Hilbert–Schmidt norm|W3BX66Y47G]].

1. For every $f \in L^2(G)$,
$$\int_G \abs{f(g)}^2 \, dg = \sum_{\pi \in \widehat{G}} d_\pi \, \norm{\hat f(\pi)}_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.$$
2. The map $f \mapsto (\hat f(\pi))_{\pi}$ is a unitary isomorphism from $L^2(G)$ onto the Hilbert space of families $(A_\pi)_{\pi \in \widehat{G}}$ with $A_\pi \in \End(\C^{d_\pi})$ and $\sum_\pi d_\pi \norm{A_\pi}_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 < \infty$, normed by this sum.

## Remarks

Fix orthonormal bases and write $\pi_{ij}$ for the matrix entries of $\pi$.
By the [[Peter–Weyl theorem|6VC9QE39XH]] the functions $\sqrt{d_\pi} \, \pi_{ij}$ form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(G)$.
The $(j, i)$ entry of $\hat f(\pi)$ is $\int_G f \, \overline{\pi_{ij}} \, dg = d_\pi^{-1/2} \ip{f}{\sqrt{d_\pi} \pi_{ij}}$, so Parseval's identity for this basis is exactly the formula in 1, and surjectivity in 2 is the completeness of the basis.
In the language of the abstract [[Plancherel theorem for groups|DH3PYQJXH0]], the Plancherel measure of $G$ gives the point $\pi$ the mass $d_\pi$.

Example: for the circle group $\R / 2\pi\Z$, all $d_\pi = 1$ and the formula is Parseval's identity $\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \abs{f}^2 \, d\theta = \sum_{n \in \Z} \abs{c_n}^2$ for the Fourier coefficients $c_n$.
For a finite group $G$ with normalized counting measure, applying 1 to the indicator function of $\set{e}$ gives $\abs{G} = \sum_\pi d_\pi^2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The energy of a function on a compact group, its integral of $\abs{f}^2$, equals the total energy of its Fourier coefficients.
Each irreducible representation is counted with a weight equal to its dimension.
So no information is lost in passing from a function to its Fourier coefficients, and none is created.

### L2 human

This is the Peter–Weyl theorem read as a statement about the Fourier transform.
It shows that $L^2(G)$ is the Hilbert direct sum of the spaces of matrix coefficients, each of dimension $d_\pi^2$.
It is the model case of the Plancherel theorem for locally compact groups, where $\widehat{G}$ need not be discrete and the sum becomes an integral against the [[Plancherel measure|QESEAKKABM]].
