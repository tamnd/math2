## Summary

The Rasch model is a probabilistic model of answers to test items, introduced by the Danish mathematician Georg Rasch in 1960.
Each person has an ability and each item has a difficulty, both on one common scale.
The chance of a correct answer depends only on their difference, through the logistic function.
Its main property is that a person's total score carries all the information about ability.
This allows comparisons of persons that do not depend on which items were used.
It is the simplest model of item response theory and is widely used in educational testing.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put people and questions on one ruler.
A person standing far above a question will almost surely answer it correctly; a person far below will almost surely fail; a person level with it has a fifty-fifty chance.
The Rasch model turns the gap between person and question into a probability with an S-shaped curve.
For example, a gap of $\ln 3 \approx 1.1$ in favour of the person gives a chance of $3/4$ of a correct answer.
