## Summary

The Laguerre–Gaussian modes are a family of beam solutions of the [[paraxial wave equation|53DCTAEM5M]] in cylindrical coordinates.
Each one is a [[Gaussian beam|Y0QE726BE3]] multiplied by a generalised [[Laguerre polynomial|WAMRHWW82R]] in the radius and by a phase factor $e^{i\ell\varphi}$ in the angle.
Like the [[Hermite–Gaussian modes|9NKZHQSC8A]] they keep their shape while they propagate and form a complete orthogonal family.
The modes with $\ell \ne 0$ have a dark centre and a helical wavefront, and they carry orbital angular momentum.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some laser beams look like a ring of light, or several rings inside each other, instead of a single spot.
These are Laguerre–Gaussian modes.
The number $p$ counts the dark rings, and the number $\ell$ tells how many full turns the phase of the light makes as one goes once around the axis.
When $\ell$ is not zero, the twist forces the light to vanish at the centre, so the beam looks like a doughnut.
Such beams are used to trap and spin tiny particles.
