## Definition

Let $u$ be a direction on the celestial sphere other than the two celestial poles.
The *hour circle* of $u$ is the half great circle from the north celestial pole through $u$ to the south celestial pole.
The *right ascension* $\alpha$ of $u$ is the angle along the celestial equator from the March equinox to the hour circle of $u$, measured eastward.
It is the longitude of $u$ in the [[equatorial coordinate system|QBEJJPMWV1]], and it takes values in $[0^{\mathrm h}, 24^{\mathrm h})$.

## Remarks

The conversion is $1^{\mathrm h} = 15^\circ$, $1^{\mathrm m} = 15'$ and $1^{\mathrm s} = 15''$, where the minutes and seconds are of time.
Example: the unit vector $(0, 1, 0)$ of the equatorial frame lies on the equator a quarter turn east of the equinox, so its right ascension is $6^{\mathrm h}$.
Non-example: the angle measured westward from the observer's meridian is the hour angle, not the right ascension; it depends on the time and the place.
The right ascension of the Sun is $0^{\mathrm h}$ at the March equinox and grows by about $4$ minutes of time per day.
