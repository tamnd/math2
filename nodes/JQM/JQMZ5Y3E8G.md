## Summary

The Binet equation describes the shape of an orbit in a [[central force|APR7XN00N3]] field, with the polar angle as the variable instead of time.
It is written for $u = 1/r$, the reciprocal of the distance to the centre, and it is a second-order [[differential equation|0NT64JV96A]] in the angle.
For the inverse-square force it becomes a harmonic oscillator equation, whose solutions are exactly the conic sections.
It also answers the inverse question: which central force produces a given orbit.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To find the path of a planet one usually solves for its position as a function of time.
Binet's trick drops the time and describes the path directly, as distance against angle.
Using one over the distance instead of the distance makes the equation for gravity surprisingly simple.
For example, the orbit $1/r = 1 + \frac{1}{2} \cos \theta$ is an ellipse, and the Binet equation shows that an inverse-square force produces it.
