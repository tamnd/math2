## Statement

For each odd prime $p$, let $\partial_p \colon K_2(\Q) \to \F_p^\times$ be the homomorphism induced by the [[tame symbol|NPAX5HQFFQ]] of the $p$-adic valuation.
Let $h_\infty \colon K_2(\Q) \to \set{\pm 1}$ be the homomorphism induced by the real [[Hilbert symbol|WFG4YMBYVQ]].
Then the map
$$K_2(\Q) \longrightarrow \set{\pm 1} \oplus \bigoplus_{p \text{ odd prime}} \F_p^\times, \qquad x \mapsto \bigl(h_\infty(x), (\partial_p(x))_p\bigr),$$
is an isomorphism of groups, where $K_2(\Q)$ is [[K2|W7T56A6YF8]] of $\Q$ and $\F_p$ is the [[finite field|0ZXZ82FTX2]] with $p$ elements.

## Remarks

By [[Matsumoto's theorem|E0W8X5Y5SE]], $K_2(\Q)$ is generated by symbols $\set{a, b}$, and by bimultiplicativity one may take $a, b \in \set{-1} \cup \set{\text{primes}}$.
Tate's proof filters $K_2(\Q)$ by the subgroups $L_m$ generated by the symbols $\set{a, b}$ with $a, b$ integers of absolute value at most $m$.
Induction on $m$ shows that $L_p / L_{p-1} \cong \F_p^\times$ through $\partial_p$ for each odd prime $p$, that $L_m = L_{m-1}$ when $m$ is not prime, and that $L_2 = L_1$ has order $2$, generated by $\set{-1, -1}$.
The argument is modelled on Gauss's first proof of quadratic reciprocity.

The tame symbol at $2$ takes values in $\F_2^\times = 1$, which is why $p = 2$ does not appear.
Equivalently, there is a split exact sequence $0 \to K_2(\Z) \to K_2(\Q) \to \bigoplus_p \F_p^\times \to 0$ with $K_2(\Z) \cong \Z/2\Z$.

Example: $\set{3, 5}$ has $\partial_3(\set{3, 5}) = 5^{-1} \bmod 3 = 2$, $\partial_5(\set{3, 5}) = 3 \bmod 5$, all other $\partial_p$ trivial, and $h_\infty(\set{3, 5}) = 1$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Tate described $K_2$ of the rational numbers completely.
One piece is a sign, coming from the real numbers.
The other pieces are one copy of the nonzero residues modulo $p$ for each odd prime $p$, read off by tame symbols.

### L2 human

The theorem is the model case of the localization sequence $K_2(\Z) \to K_2(\Q) \to \bigoplus_p K_1(\F_p)$ of Quillen.
Combined with Hilbert symbols at all places, it leads to a proof of quadratic reciprocity through K-theory, and its generalization to number fields is the Moore reciprocity sequence.
The same method applies to a rational function field $k(t)$ over a field $k$: tame symbols at the monic irreducible polynomials $\pi$ give $K_2(k(t)) \cong K_2(k) \oplus \bigoplus_\pi (k[t]/(\pi))^\times$.
