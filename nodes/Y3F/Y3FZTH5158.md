## Definition

We define, by recursion on terms and formulas, which variables of a formula are free and which are bound.
Let formulas over a signature $\sigma$ be as in the [[definition of formulas|2CHENV4DCC]], and let $\ast$ range over the binary connectives.
The sets below are defined by [[recursion|AM4GQQMZ3C]].

1. The set $\operatorname{Var}(t)$ of variables of a [[term|6D3WA7N2W4]] $t$ is given by $\operatorname{Var}(x) = \{ x \}$ for a variable $x$, $\operatorname{Var}(c) = \emptyset$ for a constant symbol $c$, and $\operatorname{Var}(f(t_1, \ldots, t_n)) = \operatorname{Var}(t_1) \cup \cdots \cup \operatorname{Var}(t_n)$.
2. The set $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi)$ of *free variables* of a [[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]] $\varphi$ is given by
   $$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{FV}(t_1 = t_2) &= \operatorname{Var}(t_1) \cup \operatorname{Var}(t_2), \\ \operatorname{FV}(R(t_1, \ldots, t_n)) &= \operatorname{Var}(t_1) \cup \cdots \cup \operatorname{Var}(t_n), \\ \operatorname{FV}(\neg \varphi) &= \operatorname{FV}(\varphi), \\ \operatorname{FV}((\varphi \ast \psi)) &= \operatorname{FV}(\varphi) \cup \operatorname{FV}(\psi), \\ \operatorname{FV}(\forall x \, \varphi) = \operatorname{FV}(\exists x \, \varphi) &= \operatorname{FV}(\varphi) \setminus \{ x \}. \end{aligned}$$
3. The set $\operatorname{BV}(\varphi)$ of *bound variables* is given by $\operatorname{BV}(\varphi) = \emptyset$ for atomic $\varphi$, $\operatorname{BV}(\neg \varphi) = \operatorname{BV}(\varphi)$, $\operatorname{BV}((\varphi \ast \psi)) = \operatorname{BV}(\varphi) \cup \operatorname{BV}(\psi)$, and $\operatorname{BV}(\forall x \, \varphi) = \operatorname{BV}(\exists x \, \varphi) = \operatorname{BV}(\varphi) \cup \{ x \}$.
4. An *occurrence* of $x$ in $\varphi$ is a position of $x$ in the string $\varphi$ that does not come directly after $\forall$ or $\exists$.
   In a subformula $\forall x \, \psi$ or $\exists x \, \psi$ of $\varphi$, the formula $\psi$ is the *scope* of that quantifier.
5. An occurrence of $x$ is *bound* when it lies in the scope of a quantifier $\forall x$ or $\exists x$ on the same variable $x$, and *free* otherwise.
   A bound occurrence is bound by the innermost such quantifier.

Let $\forall x \, \psi$ be a subformula of $\varphi$, and let $y$ be a variable that does not appear anywhere in $\psi$.
A *renaming of a bound variable* replaces this subformula by $\forall y \, \psi'$, where $\psi'$ comes from $\psi$ by replacing each free occurrence of $x$ by $y$, and likewise with $\exists$ in place of $\forall$.
Two formulas are *alpha-equivalent* when one comes from the other by finitely many renamings and their reverses.

## Notation

Enderton defines "$x$ occurs free in $\varphi$" by the same recursion as item 2, without the set notation.
A formula with $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi) = \emptyset$ is a [[sentence|DKFGQTRYZE]], or closed formula.
A variable in $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi) \cap \operatorname{BV}(\varphi)$ is said to clash, and a formula with no clashes in which no two quantifiers bind the same variable is sometimes called rectified.
In Mathlib, `FirstOrder.Language.BoundedFormula L α n` takes free variables from `α` and bound variables from `Fin n`, so alpha-equivalent formulas are the same term.

## Remarks

Items 2 and 5 describe the same thing in two ways, a set computed by recursion and a status for each position.
They agree: $x \in \operatorname{FV}(\varphi)$ exactly when some occurrence of $x$ in $\varphi$ is free.
The proof is an induction on $\varphi$.
For atomic $\varphi$ every occurrence is free, and $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi)$ is the set of variables that occur.
The connective cases add no quantifier, so the free occurrences are those of the parts.
In $\forall y \, \psi$ the free occurrences of $x$ are those of $\psi$ when $x \neq y$, and there are none when $x = y$, which matches $\operatorname{FV}(\psi) \setminus \{ y \}$.

Item 5 insists on a quantifier on the same variable.
That is why, in $\forall y \, (x < y)$, the $x$ is [[free|AN4VTVRMTA]] although it sits in the scope of $\forall y$.

For a full example, let $\varphi = \forall y \, (x < y \land \exists x \, (y = x))$.
The inner formula $x < y \land \exists x \, (y = x)$ has $\operatorname{FV} = \{ x, y \} \cup \{ y \} = \{ x, y \}$, and $\forall y$ removes $y$, so $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi) = \{ x \}$.
Also $\operatorname{BV}(\varphi) = \{ x \} \cup \{ y \} = \{ x, y \}$.
The $x$ in $x < y$ is free, and the $x$ in $y = x$ is bound by $\exists x$.

The renaming needs the condition that $y$ is fresh.
Renaming the bound $y$ to $x$ in $\exists y \, (x < y)$ gives $\exists x \, (x < x)$, where the free $x$ has been captured, and the meaning has changed.
The concept page [[free and bound variables|9CNGDRAMGC]] has more examples.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rules count which letters in a formula are inputs.
A letter is an input when no "for all" or "there exists" on that same letter covers it.
In "for every $y$, $x$ is less than $y$" the letter $x$ is an input and $y$ is not.
So the claim is about one number $x$.
In the natural numbers with $x = 0$ it is false, because $y = 0$ is not larger than $0$.

### L2 human

We compute both sets for $\varphi = \exists x \, (P(x) \to \forall y \, R(x, y, z))$.
The atomic formula $R(x, y, z)$ has $\operatorname{FV} = \{ x, y, z \}$, and $\forall y$ removes $y$, giving $\{ x, z \}$.
The implication adds $\operatorname{FV}(P(x)) = \{ x \}$, which gives $\{ x, z \}$ again.
Then $\exists x$ removes $x$, so $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi) = \{ z \}$, and the quantifiers give $\operatorname{BV}(\varphi) = \{ x, y \}$.
The quantifier clause removes $x$ only from its own scope, and a free $x$ outside that scope stays free.
