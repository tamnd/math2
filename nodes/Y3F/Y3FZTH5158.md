## Definition

Let formulas over a signature $\sigma$ be as in the [[definition of formulas|2CHENV4DCC]].
The sets below are defined by [[recursion|AM4GQQMZ3C]].

1. The set $\operatorname{Var}(t)$ of variables of a [[term|6D3WA7N2W4]]: $\operatorname{Var}(x) = \set{x}$ for a variable $x$, $\operatorname{Var}(c) = \emptyset$ for a constant symbol $c$, and $\operatorname{Var}(f(t_1, \ldots, t_n)) = \operatorname{Var}(t_1) \cup \cdots \cup \operatorname{Var}(t_n)$.
2. The set $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi)$ of *free variables* of a [[formula|HYJ10AF6RT]]:
   - $\operatorname{FV}(t_1 = t_2) = \operatorname{Var}(t_1) \cup \operatorname{Var}(t_2)$ and $\operatorname{FV}(R(t_1, \ldots, t_n)) = \operatorname{Var}(t_1) \cup \cdots \cup \operatorname{Var}(t_n)$;
   - $\operatorname{FV}(\neg \varphi) = \operatorname{FV}(\varphi)$, and $\operatorname{FV}((\varphi \ast \psi)) = \operatorname{FV}(\varphi) \cup \operatorname{FV}(\psi)$ for each binary connective $\ast$;
   - $\operatorname{FV}(\forall x \, \varphi) = \operatorname{FV}(\exists x \, \varphi) = \operatorname{FV}(\varphi) \setminus \set{x}$.
3. The set $\operatorname{BV}(\varphi)$ of *bound variables*:
   - $\operatorname{BV}(\varphi) = \emptyset$ for an atomic formula $\varphi$;
   - $\operatorname{BV}(\neg \varphi) = \operatorname{BV}(\varphi)$, and $\operatorname{BV}((\varphi \ast \psi)) = \operatorname{BV}(\varphi) \cup \operatorname{BV}(\psi)$;
   - $\operatorname{BV}(\forall x \, \varphi) = \operatorname{BV}(\exists x \, \varphi) = \operatorname{BV}(\varphi) \cup \set{x}$.
4. An *occurrence* of $x$ in $\varphi$ is a position of $x$ in the string $\varphi$, not directly after $\forall$ or $\exists$. In a subformula $\forall x \, \psi$ or $\exists x \, \psi$, the formula $\psi$ is the *scope* of the quantifier.
5. An occurrence of $x$ is *bound* when it lies in the scope of a quantifier $\forall x$ or $\exists x$, and *free* otherwise. A bound occurrence is bound by the innermost such quantifier.

## Notation

1. Enderton defines "$x$ occurs free in $\varphi$" by the same recursion as item 2, without the set notation.
2. A formula $\varphi$ with $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi) = \emptyset$ is a [[sentence|DKFGQTRYZE]], or a closed formula.
3. A variable that lies in $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi) \cap \operatorname{BV}(\varphi)$ is said to clash in $\varphi$. A formula without clashes, in which no two quantifiers bind the same variable, is sometimes called rectified.
4. In Mathlib, `FirstOrder.Language.BoundedFormula L α n` has free variables from `α` and bound variables from `Fin n`, so alpha-equivalent formulas are the same term.

## Remarks

Example: let $\varphi = \forall y \, (x < y \land \exists x \, (y = x))$.
Then $\operatorname{FV}(x < y \land \exists x \, (y = x)) = \set{x, y} \cup \set{y} = \set{x, y}$, and $\forall y$ removes $y$, so $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi) = \set{x}$.
Also $\operatorname{BV}(\varphi) = \set{x} \cup \set{y} = \set{x, y}$.
The occurrence of $x$ in $x < y$ is free, and the occurrence of $x$ in $y = x$ is bound by $\exists x$.

Non-example: in $\forall y \, (x < y)$ the occurrence of $x$ is not bound.
It lies in the scope of $\forall y$, but item 5 asks for a quantifier on $x$ itself, and there is none.

Item 2 and item 5 agree: $x \in \operatorname{FV}(\varphi)$ exactly when some occurrence of $x$ in $\varphi$ is free.
The proof is an induction on $\varphi$.
For atomic $\varphi$ every occurrence is free, and $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi)$ is the set of variables that occur.
The cases of $\neg$ and of the binary connectives add no quantifier, so the free occurrences are those of the parts.
In $\forall y \, \psi$ the free occurrences of $x$ are those of $\psi$ when $x \neq y$, and there are none when $x = y$.
This matches $\operatorname{FV}(\psi) \setminus \set{y}$.

Renaming and alpha-equivalence.
Let $\forall x \, \psi$ be a subformula of $\varphi$, and let $y$ be a variable that does not appear anywhere in $\psi$, not even after a quantifier.
Replacing this subformula by $\forall y \, \psi'$, where $\psi'$ comes from $\psi$ by replacing each free occurrence of $x$ by $y$, is a *renaming of a bound variable*.
The same holds with $\exists$ in place of $\forall$.
Two formulas are *alpha-equivalent* when one comes from the other by a finite sequence of renamings and their reverses.
The condition on $y$ is needed.
Renaming $y$ to $x$ in $\exists y \, (x < y)$ gives $\exists x \, (x < x)$, whose free variable $x$ has been captured.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rules count which letters in a formula are inputs.
A letter is an input when no "for all" or "there exists" on that same letter covers it.
In "for every $y$, $x$ is less than $y$" the letter $x$ is an input and $y$ is not.
So the claim is about one number $x$.
For the natural numbers and $x = 0$ it is false, because $y = 0$ is not larger than $0$.

### L2 human

We compute $\operatorname{FV}$ and $\operatorname{BV}$ for $\varphi = \exists x \, (P(x) \to \forall y \, R(x, y, z))$.

1. $\operatorname{FV}(R(x, y, z)) = \set{x, y, z}$, and $\forall y$ removes $y$, giving $\set{x, z}$.
2. $\operatorname{FV}(P(x)) = \set{x}$, and the implication gives $\set{x} \cup \set{x, z} = \set{x, z}$.
3. $\exists x$ removes $x$, so $\operatorname{FV}(\varphi) = \set{z}$.
4. $\operatorname{BV}(\varphi) = \set{y} \cup \set{x} = \set{x, y}$.

The typical use of the recursion is a proof by induction on formulas about free variables, for example that renaming bound variables keeps $\operatorname{FV}$ the same.
The trap is the quantifier clause: it removes $x$ from the free variables of its scope only, and leaves a free $x$ outside the scope untouched.
