## Summary

Frank-Kamenetskii theory models a thermal explosion in a reacting body in which heat leaves only by conduction.
Unlike [[Semenov's theory|7ZES3H0JJG]], the temperature varies in space, and steady states solve a nonlinear elliptic equation $\Delta \theta + \delta e^{\theta} = 0$ with $\theta = 0$ on the boundary.
This is the [[Gelfand–Bratu problem|Q3M9H7YSYZ]].
A steady state exists only when the Frank-Kamenetskii parameter $\delta$ is at most a critical value $\delta_c$, which depends on the shape of the body.
Above it, [[thermal runaway|1BSB9NDCWF]] occurs.
See the [[definition|N9Q81KMYV2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a large heap of coal or a tank of a reactive chemical whose surface is kept cool by the air.
Heat produced deep inside must travel by conduction to the surface, so the centre gets hottest.
A bigger body has a longer way out for its heat, while its production grows with its volume.
So for each shape there is a critical size: below it the temperature settles to a steady hump, above it the centre heats up without limit.
For an infinite slab of half-thickness $a$ the critical value of the parameter is about $0.88$, for a long cylinder it is $2$, and for a sphere about $3.32$.
