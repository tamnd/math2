## Summary

Synchrotron radiation is the [[electromagnetic radiation|AC6R5A3M1C]] emitted by charged particles moving at relativistic speeds in a [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]].
The particles spiral around the field lines, and relativistic beaming concentrates the radiation into a narrow cone along the velocity.
A population of electrons with a power-law energy distribution produces a power-law spectrum with strong linear polarisation.
It explains the radio emission of supernova remnants, radio galaxies and jets, and is produced on purpose in synchrotron light sources.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A magnetic field bends the path of a moving electron into a circle or a spiral.
An electron that changes direction radiates, and a very fast electron radiates mostly forward, like a searchlight sweeping around.
An observer sees a short flash each time the beam points their way, and short flashes contain high frequencies.
The faster the electron, the higher the typical frequency: it grows like the square of the energy.
Much of the radio sky, such as the Crab Nebula, shines by this process.
