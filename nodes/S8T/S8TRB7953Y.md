## Example

Let $U = \R(1, 0)$, the $x$-axis in $\R^2$.
We find all [[complements|04C3BAHV03]] of $U$.

1. Let $W$ be a complement. Then $\dim W = \dim \R^2 - \dim U = 1$, by the [[dimension|05G2G0P224]] count of a direct sum. So $W = \R(a, b)$ for some $(a, b) \ne (0, 0)$.
2. Intersection. If $b = 0$, then $W = \R(a, 0) = U$, and $U \cap W = U \ne \{0\}$. So $b \ne 0$.
3. Dividing the spanning vector by $b$, we get $W = \R(m, 1)$ with $m = a / b$.
4. Conversely, let $W_m = \R(m, 1)$. A vector $t(m, 1)$ lies in $U$ only when $t = 0$, so the [[intersection|0V33SPH7RR]] $U \cap W_m$ is $\{0\}$.
5. And $\dim U + \dim W_m = 2 = \dim \R^2$. By the [[dimension criterion|P4JG9DJ2HH]], $\R^2 = U \oplus W_m$.
6. Explicitly, $(x, y) = (x - m y, 0) + y(m, 1)$.
7. Different $m$ give different lines, since $(m, 1)$ and $(m', 1)$ are proportional only when $m = m'$.

So the complements of the $x$-axis are the lines $\R(m, 1)$, one for each real number $m$.

## Remarks

The vertical line $\R(0, 1)$, for $m = 0$, is the [[orthogonal complement|0XJXTBCB2T]] for the standard inner product.
It is only one complement among infinitely many.
Each $W_m$ is isomorphic to $\R^2 / U$ by the map $y(m, 1) \mapsto (0, y) + U$, as the [[theorem on complements|244KTXDJHW]] predicts.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A complement of the horizontal axis is any line through the origin that is not horizontal.
Such a line has some slope, and it passes through a point $(m, 1)$ at height $1$.

### L2 human

The decomposition in step 6 depends on $m$.
For $(3, 2)$ and $m = 0$, we get $(3, 0) + (0, 2)$.
For $m = 1$, we get $(1, 0) + (2, 2)$.
The $U$-component changes with the choice of complement, so "the component of $v$ along $U$" makes sense only after a complement is chosen.
