## Summary

Many-body localization (MBL) is a proposed phase of isolated, interacting quantum systems with strong disorder in which the system never reaches thermal equilibrium.
It extends [[Anderson localization|MXEZM0QJ1K]] of a single particle to many interacting particles.
An MBL system keeps a memory of its initial state forever, in local observables, and so violates the [[eigenstate thermalization hypothesis|RAPF0XSNX9]].
A common characterization is the existence of a complete set of quasi-local conserved quantities.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Usually an isolated system left alone spreads its energy around and ends up looking the same everywhere, with a temperature.
In a strongly disordered material, a single quantum particle can get stuck in place; this is Anderson localization.
Many-body localization is the claim that this getting stuck can survive even when the particles interact with each other.
For example, in a chain of spins in strong random magnetic fields, a spin that starts pointing up stays mostly up for all times, instead of forgetting its direction.
Such systems behave like insulators for both charge and heat.
