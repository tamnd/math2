## Statement

A square matrix with a row of zeros always has determinant $0$, whatever its other rows are.
Let $K$ be a commutative ring, let $n \ge 1$, and let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $K$.
If one row of $A$ is zero, then $\det A = 0$, where $\det$ is the [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]].

## Remarks

### Why it holds

The determinant is linear in each row when the other rows stay fixed, by [[multilinearity in rows|0PPG793642]].
A zero row equals $0$ times itself, so the determinant equals $0$ times itself as well.
The [[proof by scaling a row|8T5B6HSHW7]] writes this out.
The statement is also part 2 of the theorem on [[equal rows or a zero row|0K4VQB7HWR]].

### An example and a column version

For instance,
$$\det \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 & 7 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 5 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = 0.$$
Check by the rule of Sarrus: each of the six products takes one entry from the middle row, so each product is $0$.
A zero column also gives $0$, by the [[determinant of the transpose|0ZZQ2E1MTE]].

### What it does not say

The converse is false: a determinant can be $0$ without any zero row.
For example, $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = 4 - 4 = 0$, although no entry is $0$.
Over a field, the right condition for $\det A = 0$ is that the rows are dependent, and a zero row is only the simplest case of that.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of the rows of a $3 \times 3$ table as three arrows in space, and of the determinant as the volume of the box they span.
A zero row is an arrow of length zero.
A box with one edge of length zero is flat, so its volume is zero.
That is why the determinant is $0$.

### L2 human

Here is a typical use: in row reduction, a row can turn into zeros.
Start from
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$$
and subtract $3$ times row $1$ from row $2$, which does not change the determinant.
The result is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, which has a zero row, so $\det A = 0$.
Check: $1 \cdot 6 - 2 \cdot 3 = 0$.
So a zero row that appears during elimination is a signal that the matrix is singular.
