## Summary

The Holstein–Primakoff transformation writes the operators of a quantum spin of size $s$ in terms of one bosonic mode.
The number of bosons counts how far the spin has been lowered from its maximal state.
Expanding the square root that appears in the formulas in powers of $1/s$ gives spin-wave theory, the standard description of magnons in magnets.
It was introduced by Theodore Holstein and Henry Primakoff in 1940.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A spin of size $s$ has $2s + 1$ states, from fully up to fully down.
Count the steps down from the top state: this count $n$ runs from $0$ to $2s$ and behaves like a number of particles.
The transformation makes this precise with a boson creation operator that adds one step down.
For a large spin and states near the top, the spin looks almost like a harmonic oscillator, and the bosons are the spin waves of a ferromagnet.
For $s = 1/2$ there are only two states, $n = 0$ and $n = 1$.
