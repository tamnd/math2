## Summary

A Verma module is the largest module of a complex [[semisimple Lie algebra|EY9DN7QAX1]] generated by a vector of a given [[highest weight|C5W8BE1DRY]].
It is built from the [[universal enveloping algebra|SCZ8PRVY00]] by induction from a Borel subalgebra.
Every irreducible highest weight module, in particular every finite-dimensional irreducible module, is a quotient of a Verma module.
Verma modules are the standard objects of [[category O|NM3KHQNYDB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

To build a representation with a given highest weight, start with one vector $v$ and declare how the "raising" elements act on it: they kill it.
Then apply the "lowering" elements to $v$ in every possible way and impose no further relations.
The result is the Verma module, which is usually infinite-dimensional.
For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ it has basis $v, fv, f^2 v, \dots$, one vector for each weight $\lambda, \lambda - 2, \lambda - 4, \dots$.
The finite-dimensional irreducible modules appear as its quotients.
