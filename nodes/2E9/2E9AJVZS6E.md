## Summary

The Lorentz factor of a speed $v$ below the speed of light $c$ is $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$.
It is the factor by which moving clocks run slow and moving rods shrink.
It appears in the Lorentz transformation and in relativistic energy and momentum.
It equals $1$ at rest and grows without bound as $v$ approaches $c$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Lorentz factor measures how strongly relativity matters at a given speed.
At the speed of a jet plane it differs from $1$ by less than one part in a billion, so relativistic effects go unnoticed.
At $v = 0.6 c$ it equals $1/\sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 1/0.8 = 1.25$, so a moving clock shows $8$ seconds while $10$ seconds pass in the lab.
Near the speed of light it becomes huge, which is why no massive object can reach $c$.
