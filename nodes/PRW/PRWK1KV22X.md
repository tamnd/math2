## Summary

A **Pythagorean quadruple** is a [[tuple|9HFER6N708]] of [[integers|0C4JS9RF5M]] $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$, such that $a^{2} + b^{2} + c^{2} = d^{2}$. They are solutions of a [[Diophantine equation|08B1RTW7VZ]] and often only positive integer values are considered. However, to provide a more complete geometric interpretation, the integer values can be allowed to be negative and zero (thus allowing [[Pythagorean triples|01G1WY1GVX]] to be included) with the only condition being that $d > 0$. In this setting, a Pythagorean quadruple $(a, b, c, d)$ defines a cuboid with integer side lengths $\lvert a\rvert$, $\lvert b\rvert$, and $\lvert c\rvert$, whose space diagonal has integer length $d$; with this interpretation, Pythagorean quadruples are thus also called *Pythagorean boxes*. In this article we will assume, unless otherwise stated, that the values of a Pythagorean quadruple are all positive integers.
