## Definition

Let $\Lambda$ be a set of modal formulas.

1. $\Lambda$ is closed under the necessitation rule when $\varphi \in \Lambda$ implies $\square\varphi \in \Lambda$, for every formula $\varphi$.
2. Let $S$ be a Hilbert system with theorem set $\operatorname{Thm}(S)$. A [[rule of inference|QPD3X471Q1]] $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n \,/\, \psi$ is admissible in $S$ when every substitution instance with premises in $\operatorname{Thm}(S)$ has its conclusion in $\operatorname{Thm}(S)$.
3. The rule is derivable in $S$ when $S$ derives $\psi$ from the hypotheses $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n$, using its rules on hypotheses as well.
4. For a normal logic $\Lambda$, the local consequence relation is $\Gamma \vdash_\Lambda \psi$ iff $\Lambda \vdash (\gamma_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \gamma_n) \to \psi$ for some $\gamma_i \in \Gamma$.
5. The global consequence relation $\Gamma \vdash^g_\Lambda \psi$ holds iff $\psi$ is in the smallest set that contains $\Lambda \cup \Gamma$ and is closed under modus ponens and necessitation.

## Notation

The rule is written $\varphi \,/\, \square\varphi$ and named Nec, RN or N.
Local consequence uses necessitation on theorems only, while global consequence uses it on hypotheses too.

## Remarks

1. In the usual Hilbert system of [[normal modal logic K|JS8W7PA6JK]], necessitation is a primitive rule, so it is derivable in the sense of item 3.
2. For local consequence it is not derivable: $p \nvdash_{\mathsf{K}} \square p$, since $\mathsf{K} \nvdash p \to \square p$, see [[the non-theorem p to box p|R2WWD2R5NV]].
3. For global consequence it is derivable, $p \vdash^g_{\mathsf{K}} \square p$, and the [[deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]] fails for $\vdash^g$, see [[the global consequence example|R6MA6TFN5K]].
4. Uniform substitution shows the same pattern of admissible versus derivable, see [[uniform substitution is admissible but not derivable|DE0GD8BM4M]].
5. Local consequence satisfies the deduction theorem by its definition: $\Gamma \cup \set{\varphi} \vdash_\Lambda \psi$ iff $\Gamma \vdash_\Lambda \varphi \to \psi$.
6. The two relations have the same theorems: $\emptyset \vdash_\Lambda \psi$ iff $\emptyset \vdash^g_\Lambda \psi$ iff $\psi \in \Lambda$, since $\Lambda$ is closed under the two rules.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rule of a logic says which new laws we may write down from old ones.
Necessitation says: from a proved law, write down "necessarily" in front of it.
The rule works for proved laws, but not for statements that we only assume.

### L2 human

We compare the two consequence relations on the hypothesis $p$.

1. Globally, $p$ is a hypothesis, so necessitation gives $\square p$; thus $p \vdash^g_{\mathsf{K}} \square p$.
2. Locally, $p \vdash_{\mathsf{K}} \square p$ would need $\mathsf{K} \vdash p \to \square p$.
3. The model $W = \set{1, 2}$, $R = \set{(1,2)}$, $V(p) = \set{1}$ falsifies $p \to \square p$ at 1.
4. By soundness, $\mathsf{K} \nvdash p \to \square p$, so $p \nvdash_{\mathsf{K}} \square p$.

The typical use of local consequence is completeness by [[canonical models|TVE4F04KME]], which concerns truth at a world.
A trap: a proof from hypotheses that uses necessitation on a hypothesis does not give an implication theorem.

### L3 human

The admissible rules of a logic include its derivable rules.
For some modal logics, such as $\mathsf{S4}$ and $\mathsf{GL}$, the admissible rules are not all derivable; Rybakov studied their decidability.
The local and global relations correspond to truth at a world and validity in a model.
The global one is the consequence relation of the variety of modal algebras.
