## Summary

Turbulence is the irregular, chaotic state of fluid motion that occurs at high [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]].
A turbulent flow has eddies over a wide range of sizes, and it mixes and dissipates energy far faster than a smooth flow.
Its details are unpredictable, while its averages are reproducible.
It is described by the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]], but no general theory derives its statistics from them.
Most flows in nature and engineering, from rivers to jet engines, are turbulent.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Watch a fast river behind a rock, or smoke from a chimney on a windy day.
The motion is a tangle of swirls inside swirls, constantly changing, and never exactly repeating.
Two nearly identical starts soon give completely different patterns, yet the average behaviour, such as the mean speed or how fast dye spreads, is the same each time.
That mix of unpredictable detail and reliable averages is the hallmark of turbulence.
