## Summary

The adiabatic flame temperature is the temperature that the products of a combustion reach when no heat is lost to the surroundings.
It follows from an energy balance at constant pressure.
The [[enthalpy|722RZK72P5]] of the products at that temperature equals the enthalpy of the reactants at the start.
It is an upper bound for the temperature of a real flame of the same mixture.
It is a basic number for the design of burners and engines and a key parameter in [[combustion theory|SXSCYXH068]].
See the [[definition|X9XJ6RH3DB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Burning a fuel releases chemical energy.
If none of this energy escapes as heat, all of it goes into warming the hot gases that the fire produces.
The temperature these gases end up at is the adiabatic flame temperature.
In a simple model with a heat release $Q$ per kilogram of mixture and a specific heat $c_p$ of the products, the gases warm up by $Q / c_p$.
A real flame loses some heat by radiation and conduction, so it is a little cooler.
