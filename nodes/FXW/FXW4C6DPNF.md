## Summary

The Hückel method is a simple model of the $\pi$-electrons of planar conjugated hydrocarbons, proposed by Erich Hückel in the 1930s.
In it the energy levels of the $\pi$-orbitals are affine functions of the [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]] of the [[adjacency matrix|08B9CX109G]] of the carbon skeleton.
It explains the stability of benzene and of aromatic rings, and it is a historical source of [[spectral graph theory|5JESM2ZN48]] and of [[chemical graph theory|AE724Y8CQX]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In molecules such as benzene, some electrons are not tied to one bond but spread over the whole ring.
The Hückel method models this with a single matrix: the table that records which carbon atoms are bonded.
The eigenvalues of this table give the energy levels of the spread-out electrons, and the eigenvectors give their shapes.
For benzene, the electrons end up lower in energy than in three separate double bonds, which is why benzene is so stable.
