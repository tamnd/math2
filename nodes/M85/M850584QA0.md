## Proof

We find the foot of the perpendicular from $X$ to the [[plane|07SDJ5DNE4]] and compare every other point of the plane with it.

1. Let $n = (a, b, c)$, a [[normal vector|0WQJJCCTC8]] of $P$. Its [[length|0XKBKGTCPC]] $\|n\|$ is positive, since $n \neq 0$.
2. With the [[dot product|0S52TNNTH9]], $P = \{Q \in \mathbb{R}^3 : n \cdot Q = d\}$, by the definition of $P$.
3. Let $k = n \cdot X - d = a x_0 + b y_0 + c z_0 - d$, by the definition of the dot product.
4. Let $t = k / \|n\|^2$ and $Q_0 = X - t n$.
5. $Q_0 \in P$, since $n \cdot Q_0 = n \cdot X - t \, (n \cdot n) = (k + d) - k = d$.
6. Let $Q \in P$. Then $n \cdot Q = d = n \cdot Q_0$ by step 5, so $n \cdot (Q_0 - Q) = 0$.
7. Write $X - Q = t n + (Q_0 - Q)$, by step 4. The two parts are orthogonal by step 6.
8. By the [[Pythagorean theorem for orthogonal vectors|0BVTDM872V]], $\|X - Q\|^2 = t^2 \|n\|^2 + \|Q_0 - Q\|^2 \geq t^2 \|n\|^2$.
9. The [[Euclidean distance|0NPPGHQNXQ]] is $d(X, Q) = \|X - Q\|$. By step 8, $d(X, Q) \geq |t| \, \|n\|$ for each $Q \in P$.
10. For $Q = Q_0$, $d(X, Q_0) = \|t n\| = |t| \, \|n\|$. So the bound of step 9 is reached.
11. Hence the least distance exists and equals $|t| \, \|n\| = |k| / \|n\|$, which is the formula. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Drop a perpendicular from $X$ to the plane along the normal vector $(a, b, c)$.
Every other point of the plane is farther away, by the Pythagorean theorem in the right triangle that the perpendicular makes.
The length of the perpendicular is the value of the equation at $X$, divided by the length of the normal vector.

## Variants

- Cauchy-Schwarz: for $Q \in P$, $|k| = |n \cdot (X - Q)| \leq \|n\| \, \|X - Q\|$. This gives the lower bound at once, and $Q_0$ reaches it.
- Projection: for any $A \in P$, the distance is the length of the [[projection|0JW13B48YP]] of $X - A$ along $n$, which is $|n \cdot (X - A)| / \|n\| = |k| / \|n\|$.
