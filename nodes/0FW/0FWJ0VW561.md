## Definition

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional [[inner product space|01EK923SKP]] over $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, of dimension $n$.
An orthonormal basis of $V$ is a family $(e_1, \dots, e_n)$ of vectors of $V$ such that:

1. $(e_1, \dots, e_n)$ is a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $V$;
2. $(e_1, \dots, e_n)$ is an [[orthonormal family|09VYHEF2VT]], that is, $\ip{e_i}{e_j} = \delta_{ij}$ for all $i, j$.

## Notation

- Mathlib writes `OrthonormalBasis ι 𝕜 E` for a finite type `ι`. It stores a linear isometry from `E` onto `EuclideanSpace 𝕜 ι`, the space $\mathbb{K}^\iota$ with the standard inner product. This is a variant of our definition.
- For a square matrix, "orthogonal" over $\mathbb{R}$ and "unitary" over $\mathbb{C}$ mean that the columns form an orthonormal basis.

## Remarks

An orthonormal family is linearly independent, so condition 1 can be replaced by "the family spans $V$" or by "the family has $n$ vectors".
The definition depends on the inner product: the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is orthonormal for the dot product and not for $2x_1 y_1 + x_2 y_2$.
The Mathlib form and ours agree: the isometry sends $e_i$ to the $i$-th standard vector, and an isometry preserves the values $\ip{e_i}{e_j}$.
In infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, "orthonormal basis" usually means a maximal orthonormal family, which is in general not a basis in the algebraic sense.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An orthonormal basis is a basis whose vectors have length $1$ and meet at right angles.
The axes $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ of the plane are one.
Each coordinate of a vector is its inner product with one basis vector.

### L2 human

To check the definition for $n$ vectors in a space of dimension $n$, it is enough to check orthonormality.
The vectors $\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(1, 1)$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(1, -1)$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ have squared norms $\frac{1 + 1}{2} = 1$ and dot product $\frac{1 - 1}{2} = 0$.
So they are orthonormal, and since there are $2 = \dim \mathbb{R}^2$ of them, they form an orthonormal basis.

### L3 human

An orthonormal basis is the same as a linear isometry $V \to \mathbb{K}^n$, where $\mathbb{K}^n$ has the standard inner product.
This is the form that Mathlib uses, and it shows that every inner product space of dimension $n$ is isometric to $\mathbb{K}^n$.
The two conditions of the definition separate the linear part, a basis, from the metric part, orthonormality.
