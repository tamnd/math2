## Summary

Mueller calculus is a matrix method for tracing the polarization of light through optical elements.
Light is described by its [[Stokes vector|1WA9ASC6NF]], and each element by a real $4 \times 4$ matrix, its Mueller matrix.
A chain of elements acts by the product of their matrices.
Unlike [[Jones calculus|D3XTQ5Z2QA]], it handles partially polarized light and elements that depolarize.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of each optical element, such as a polarizer or a wave plate, as a machine that takes four numbers in and gives four numbers out.
The four numbers are the Stokes parameters, which record intensity and polarization.
The machine is linear, so it is a $4 \times 4$ matrix.
To follow light through three elements, multiply their three matrices, the last element on the left.
For example, an ideal polarizer halves the intensity of unpolarized light and makes it fully polarized.
