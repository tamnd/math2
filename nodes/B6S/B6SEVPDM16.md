## Summary

The Arrow–Pratt measures quantify [[risk aversion|RJG35DZXTH]] at a given level of wealth from the curvature of the utility function.
The coefficient of absolute risk aversion is $-u''/u'$, and the coefficient of relative risk aversion is the same quantity times wealth.
Dividing by $u'$ makes the measures independent of the scale of the utility, so they compare the risk attitudes of different agents.
For small risks, the risk premium is approximately half the variance times the absolute coefficient.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A concave utility says "more money helps less and less", but it does not say how strongly.
The Arrow–Pratt coefficient measures how fast the extra value of money falls, relative to its current level.
For example, the utility $u(w) = -e^{-2w}$ has absolute risk aversion $2$ at every wealth level.
So a person with this utility treats a given bet the same way whether they are poor or rich.
