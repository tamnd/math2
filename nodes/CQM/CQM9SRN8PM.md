## Summary

The box of [[modal logic|C3RZY20ETK]] can be read in many ways, and each reading gives a logic with its own axioms, frames and questions.
This sub-area of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]] starts with time: Prior's tense operators, then linear and branching time as used in the verification of programs, Kamp's expressive completeness theorem, and logics of intervals.
It then reads the box as knowledge, where $\mathsf{S5}$, common knowledge as a fixed point and the muddy children puzzle show how agents reason about each other.
Belief drops the truth axiom and gives $\mathsf{KD45}$, and dynamic epistemic logic describes how announcements and private events change what agents know.
Obligation gives deontic logic, the logic $\mathsf{KD}$ and its paradoxes, and conditionals and agency give sphere semantics and the logic of seeing to it that.
Justification logic replaces the box by explicit proofs, and belief revision, Gärdenfors's triviality theorem and Fitch's paradox test how far these readings can be pushed.
The sub-area ends with questions, description logics and the logic of value, where the same Kripke methods reach knowledge representation and ethics.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Temporal logic|8XB117FKKV]]: you learn Prior's tense operators, the minimal tense logic $\mathsf{Kt}$, and how axioms match properties of time.
2. [[Linear temporal logic|RMJ56ZPA63]]: you learn next and until over the natural numbers, and why satisfiability and model checking are PSPACE-complete.
3. [[Computation tree logic|F6JA5C5BJX]]: you learn path quantifiers over branching time, and the model checking algorithm that is linear in the model and the formula.
4. [[CTL*|Y4XZ94GRXV]]: you learn the full branching-time logic that contains both LTL and CTL.
5. [[Kamp's theorem|CQ0QCQK5JT]]: you learn that since and until express every first-order property of Dedekind-complete linear orders.
6. [[Interval temporal logics|991B5RR6X4]]: you learn Allen's thirteen relations and why the Halpern-Shoham logic is undecidable.
7. [[Epistemic logic|EA34W4ERRE]]: you learn knowledge as a box over indistinguishable worlds, and the introspection axioms of $\mathsf{S5}$.
8. [[Common knowledge|7BCMKF2P7X]]: you learn common knowledge as reachability and as a greatest fixed point.
9. [[Muddy children puzzle|BG9TYCJ7HP]]: you see why the father's public statement matters, and why the muddy children step forward in round $k$.

### Reading order, continued

10. [[Doxastic logic|RTP4HTZYK3]]: you learn belief as $\mathsf{KD45}$, and why a Moore sentence can be true but not believed.
11. [[Dynamic epistemic logic|9465T2PWZT]]: you learn public announcements as model restriction, the reduction axioms, and action models for private events.
12. [[Deontic logic|7B74J6J5JV]]: you learn obligation as the logic $\mathsf{KD}$, and the paradoxes of Ross and Chisholm.
13. [[Conditional logic|ZGYG7WN7EC]]: you learn counterfactuals in Lewis's sphere semantics and Stalnaker's selection functions.
14. [[STIT logic|WYY2JXWMYY]]: you learn agency as choice in branching time.
15. [[Justification logic|5HKWCXEFG1]]: you learn explicit proof terms, and how the realization theorem recovers $\mathsf{S4}$.
16. [[AGM belief revision|Y05YQN7HGQ]]: you learn the postulates for rational revision and Grove's spheres that represent them.
17. [[Gärdenfors's triviality theorem|WMZ1QP7KQY]]: you learn why the Ramsey test and the preservation postulate cannot both hold.
18. [[Fitch's paradox of knowability|HVCW7QYXSW]]: you learn the short argument that if every truth is knowable, every truth is known.
19. [[Inquisitive logic|EX0ND8DCTN]]: you learn to treat questions as formulas in support semantics.
20. [[Description logic|TXQ80B8ZSA]]: you learn that $\mathcal{ALC}$ is multimodal $\mathsf{K}$, and the complexity of concept satisfiability.
21. [[Axiological logic|4BRXSBXXWG]]: you learn the logic of good, bad and better that Halldén and von Wright started.

### Prerequisites

1. [[Syntax, Kripke semantics and completeness of modal logic|SKY7AQFG1V]]: [[modal logic|C3RZY20ETK]], [[Kripke semantics|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]], the logics [[S4|N60RVXSA5W]] and [[S5|VC3A2VWXND]], and [[multimodal logic|ERG3KAQWYF]].
2. [[Provability and interpretability logics|3E7M66WW6Q]]: provability readings of the box, used in justification logic.
