## Summary

The elasticity tensor, or stiffness tensor, is the fourth-order tensor that maps the [[strain tensor|8QS6QMJF5Z]] to the [[Cauchy stress tensor|KG2XWEMJ30]] in a linear elastic material.
It encodes all the elastic properties of the material, including its anisotropy.
Its symmetries reduce its $81$ components to at most $21$ independent ones, and material symmetry reduces the number further, down to $2$ for isotropic materials.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In one dimension, the stiffness of a bar is a single number: stress equals that number times strain.
In three dimensions both stress and strain are symmetric $3 \times 3$ matrices, so the stiffness becomes a linear map from matrices to matrices.
That linear map is the elasticity tensor.
A wooden board is stiffer along the grain than across it, and its elasticity tensor shows it.
A glass plate has an elasticity tensor that looks the same in all directions.
