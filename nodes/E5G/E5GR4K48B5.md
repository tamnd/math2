## Summary

The Shakura–Sunyaev model describes a thin [[accretion disk|6ADKW56QGV]] of gas that orbits a central mass on nearly Keplerian circles.
It replaces the unknown turbulent friction in the disk by a [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] of the form $\nu = \alpha c_s H$.
Here $c_s$ is the [[speed of sound|SN4J6KP03C]], $H$ the thickness of the disk and $\alpha$ a dimensionless constant.
With this single parameter the disk equations close.
They give the mass flow, the heating and the temperature of the disk as functions of radius.
It is the standard reference model for disks around young stars, white dwarfs, neutron stars and black holes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gas around a star moves on circles, the inner rings faster than the outer rings.
Neighbouring rings rub against each other, so the inner gas slowly loses angular momentum and spirals inward while the disk heats up and shines.
Nobody knows the friction in detail.
So the model just says that it is a fixed fraction $\alpha$ of the largest friction that sound-speed eddies of the disk's thickness could give.
For example, with $\alpha = 0.1$ the eddies are about one tenth as effective as this upper bound.
The prediction that the disk temperature falls like $R^{-3/4}$ with the radius $R$ does not depend on $\alpha$ at all.
