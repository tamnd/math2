## Definition

Let $S$ be a set and let $\ast$ be a [[binary operation|0M418FE7BB]] on $S$.
The operation $\ast$ is commutative when $a \ast b = b \ast a$ for all $a, b \in S$.

## Notation

The symbol $+$ is used by convention only for commutative operations.
In Mathlib, the class `Std.Commutative op` states that the function `op` satisfies `op a b = op b a` for all `a` and `b`.

## Remarks

Addition and multiplication of real numbers are commutative.
Subtraction is not commutative: $1 - 2 \neq 2 - 1$.
Multiplication of $2 \times 2$ real matrices is associative but not commutative.
A [[group|G5S1M9]] with a commutative operation is an [[abelian group|082GHNPJ53]].
The concept page [[commutativity|099E72KTF8]] gives more examples, the non-examples and the first properties.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Commutativity says that the order of the two inputs does not matter.
For example, $3 \times 5$ and $5 \times 3$ are both $15$.
Many operations do not have this property, for example subtraction and the composition of rotations in space.

### L2 human

To check that an operation is commutative, take two arbitrary elements $a$ and $b$ and show that $a \ast b = b \ast a$.
To show that an operation is not commutative, one pair with $a \ast b \neq b \ast a$ is enough.
A trap is to test only pairs that commute by chance, such as a pair $a, a$ or a pair that contains an identity element.
