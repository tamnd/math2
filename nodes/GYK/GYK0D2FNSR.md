## Summary

The Redlich–Kwong equation of state is a cubic [[equation of state|YPT3SJSS5T]] for gases and liquids.
Otto Redlich and Joseph Neng Shun Kwong proposed it in 1949.
It modifies the attraction term of the [[van der Waals equation|X576BQTPQ5]].
The new term depends on the temperature and on the molar volume in a different way.
It is much more accurate than the van der Waals equation for gases above the critical temperature.
It was the starting point of later cubic equations used in chemical engineering.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The van der Waals equation corrects the ideal gas law for the size of molecules and their attraction, but its numbers are often off.
The Redlich–Kwong equation keeps the same idea and changes the attraction term so that the attraction matters less at high temperature.
Its two constants are computed from the critical temperature and pressure of the gas, so it needs only two measured numbers.
Engineers use it, and its improved versions, to compute densities of gases in pipelines and reactors.
