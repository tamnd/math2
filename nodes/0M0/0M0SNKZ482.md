## Definition

A block diagonal matrix is a block matrix with square blocks on the diagonal and zero blocks everywhere else.
Let $R$ be a ring and let $A_1, \dots, A_k$ be square matrices over $R$, where $A_i$ has order $n_i$.
The *block diagonal matrix* $\operatorname{diag}(A_1, \dots, A_k)$ is the [[block matrix|01AXG9KZQF]] of order $n_1 + \cdots + n_k$
$$\begin{pmatrix} A_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & A_2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & A_k \end{pmatrix}$$
with row partition and column partition both equal to $(n_1, \dots, n_k)$.
Its block in position $(i, i)$ is $A_i$, and its block in position $(i, j)$ with $i \neq j$ is the $n_i \times n_j$ zero matrix.
A square matrix is *block diagonal* for the partition $(n_1, \dots, n_k)$ if all of its blocks off the diagonal are zero.

## Notation

This matrix is also written $A_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus A_k$ and called the *direct sum* of the blocks.
In Mathlib, `Matrix.blockDiagonal'` builds it from blocks of any sizes, and `Matrix.blockDiagonal` builds it from blocks of one shape.
Both index the rows by sigma or product types, not by `Fin n`.

## Remarks

The row partition and the column partition are the same on purpose.
That makes each diagonal block square, so it sits on the main diagonal of the whole matrix.
For example, with $A_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A_2 = (5)$,
$$\operatorname{diag}(A_1, A_2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 3 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Every square matrix is block diagonal for the trivial partition with one block, so the notion says something only for two or more blocks.
With all $n_i = 1$ the definition gives the [[diagonal matrices|0Q9PGXD9E4]].
By the [[block multiplication rule|WR3RCF5PSQ]], products of block diagonal matrices with the same partition are computed block by block.
The [[concept page|001T5FJDC1]] lists the other rules and the link with invariant subspaces.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A block diagonal matrix has square blocks along the diagonal and zeros everywhere else.
Each block acts on its own group of coordinates.
A $5 \times 5$ example has a $2 \times 2$ block followed by a $3 \times 3$ block, with a $2 \times 3$ zero block and a $3 \times 2$ zero block off the diagonal.

### L2 human

The partition is the same for the rows and the columns, so the $i$-th group of coordinates is sent into itself.
In a basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$ this says that the span of each group of basis vectors is an [[invariant subspace|0ACX5QT8TE]].
Being block diagonal depends on the order of the basis, since the groups must be consecutive.
