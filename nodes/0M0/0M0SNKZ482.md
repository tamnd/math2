## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring and let $A_1, \dots, A_k$ be square matrices over $R$, where $A_i$ has order $n_i$.
The *block diagonal matrix* $\operatorname{diag}(A_1, \dots, A_k)$ is the [[block matrix|01AXG9KZQF]] of order $n_1 + \cdots + n_k$
$$\begin{pmatrix} A_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & A_2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & A_k \end{pmatrix},$$
with row and column partition $(n_1, \dots, n_k)$.
Its block in position $(i, i)$ is $A_i$, and its other blocks are zero matrices.

## Notation

- This matrix is also called the *direct sum* $A_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus A_k$.
- In Mathlib, `Matrix.blockDiagonal'` builds it from blocks of any sizes, and `Matrix.blockDiagonal` from blocks of one shape. Both use product or sigma index types, not `Fin n`.

## Remarks

- Products of block diagonal matrices with matching block sizes are computed block by block.
- Over a commutative ring, $\det \operatorname{diag}(A_1, \dots, A_k) = \det A_1 \cdots \det A_k$.
- The [[Jordan normal form|085HQ5G5TE]] is a block diagonal matrix of [[Jordan blocks|07PH4RX0X5]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A block diagonal matrix has square blocks along the diagonal and zeros everywhere else.
Each block acts on its own group of coordinates.

### L2 human

The partition is the same for the rows and the columns, so each diagonal block is square.
With all $n_i = 1$ we get the [[diagonal matrices|0Q9PGXD9E4]].
