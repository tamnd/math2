## Definition

Let $\Delta\sigma$ be the change of the [[Cauchy stress tensor|KG2XWEMJ30]] at a point of the crust caused by a source earthquake, with tension counted positive.
Let a receiver fault through that point have unit normal $n$ and unit slip direction $s$, with $s \perp n$.
The shear stress change is $\Delta\tau = s \cdot (\Delta\sigma\, n)$, and the normal stress change is $\Delta\sigma_n = n \cdot (\Delta\sigma\, n)$.
For an effective friction coefficient $\mu' \ge 0$, the *Coulomb failure stress change* is
$$\Delta \mathrm{CFS} = \Delta\tau + \mu' \Delta\sigma_n.$$
*Coulomb stress transfer* is the change of $\mathrm{CFS}$ on receiver faults produced by the source earthquake; a fault with $\Delta \mathrm{CFS} > 0$ is brought closer to failure.

## Remarks

Example: $\Delta\tau = 0.1$ MPa, $\Delta\sigma_n = -0.2$ MPa (more compression) and $\mu' = 0.4$ give $\Delta \mathrm{CFS} = 0.1 - 0.08 = 0.02$ MPa, a small loading.
Non-example: the change of the full magnitude of the shear traction is not $\Delta\tau$, because only the component along the slip direction $s$ of the receiver fault promotes its slip.
The coefficient $\mu'$ includes the effect of pore fluid pressure, which reduces the normal stress that clamps the fault.
The definition is the change of the Coulomb (Mohr–Coulomb) failure function $\tau + \mu' \sigma_n$, so it depends on the orientation and slip direction of the receiver fault.
In practice $\Delta\sigma$ is computed from a model of the source slip in a linear elastic half-space.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Coulomb stress change adds the extra push along the slip direction to the change in clamping, weighted by friction.
A positive number means the fault has been pushed towards slipping.
