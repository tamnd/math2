## Summary

Stochastic programming studies optimization problems in which some data are random variables with a known distribution.
The typical goal is to minimize an expected cost, possibly with constraints that must hold with high probability.
In two-stage problems, a decision is made now, the randomness is revealed, and then a recourse decision corrects the first one.
Applications include energy planning, finance, inventory and supply chain design.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A shop must order goods before it knows how many customers will come.
Ordering too much wastes money, and ordering too little loses sales.
If the shop knows the probabilities of the possible demands, it can choose the order that costs least on average.
Stochastic programming is the theory of such decisions under known random uncertainty.
