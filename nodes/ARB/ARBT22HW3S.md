## Summary

The alpha effect is the generation of a mean electric current parallel to the mean magnetic field by small-scale turbulent motions that lack mirror symmetry.
In mean-field electrodynamics it appears as a term $\alpha \overline{\mathbf{B}}$ in the averaged induction equation.
It lets a large-scale field regenerate itself from small-scale helical flow, a step that the antidynamo theorems forbid for simple symmetric mean flows.
It is a basic ingredient of models of the solar dynamo and of the [[geodynamo|19AESAFHF9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a rotating, convecting fluid, rising and sinking blobs twist as they move, like corkscrews.
A twisting blob carries a loop of magnetic field with it and turns the loop over.
Many such small twisted loops add up to a large-scale electric current along the original field.
This large current makes a new large-scale field, closing the dynamo cycle.
The strength of this effect is measured by the coefficient $\alpha$, which is roughly minus the average helicity of the flow times a correlation time.
