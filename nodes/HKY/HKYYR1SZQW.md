## Summary

A modular Lie algebra is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over a field of positive [[characteristic|1ZB13N78XW]].
Many basic theorems of characteristic zero fail for them, such as [[Lie's theorem|11K6SVNXMH]] and [[Weyl's theorem on complete reducibility|92EXQ237Q6]].
Their study is needed for algebraic groups over finite fields and for questions on finite groups such as the restricted Burnside problem.
The simple ones over algebraically closed fields of characteristic $p > 3$ have been classified.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Lie algebra can be defined over any field, including a field where $p \cdot 1 = 0$ for a prime $p$.
In that setting there are new phenomena, because numbers like $p$ that one would divide by are zero.
For example, the derivative $d/dt$ and multiplication by $t$ both act on polynomials in $t$ taken modulo $t^p$.
This works because the derivative of $t^p$ is $p t^{p-1} = 0$.
Such phenomena give small irreducible representations and new simple Lie algebras that have no counterpart over the complex numbers.
