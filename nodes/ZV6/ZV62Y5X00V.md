## Example

We look at the four Belnap values twice, first as a set with two orders and then as pairs of worlds.

### The bilattice FOUR

On $\mathbf{4} = \{\mathbf{N}, \mathbf{F}, \mathbf{T}, \mathbf{B}\}$ take two orders.

1. The information order is $x \le_i y$ iff $x \subseteq y$, so $\mathbf{N}$ is the bottom, $\mathbf{B}$ is the top, and $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{F}$ are incomparable.
2. The truth order is $x \le_t y$ iff $T \in x$ implies $T \in y$ and $F \in y$ implies $F \in x$, so $\mathbf{F}$ is the bottom, $\mathbf{T}$ is the top, and $\mathbf{N}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are incomparable.
3. Both orders are [[lattices|WH4SFAACAS]], and $\land$ and $\lor$ of [[FDE|AWEG5PR3GF]] are the meet and join for $\le_t$.
4. For example $\mathbf{N} \land \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{F}$, the only common lower bound of $\mathbf{N}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ for $\le_t$.
5. Negation reverses $\le_t$ and keeps $\le_i$, since it swaps $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{F}$ and fixes $\mathbf{N}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.

### The Routley star

A *star model* is a set $W$ of worlds, a map $* \colon W \to W$ with $a^{**} = a$, and a set $V(p) \subseteq W$ for each variable $p$.
A variable $p$ is true at a world $a$ iff $a \in V(p)$, and $A \land B$ and $A \lor B$ follow the classical clauses at $a$.
The new clause is for negation: $\neg A$ is true at $a$ iff $A$ is not true at $a^*$.
Fix a world $a$ and put $v(A) = \{T : A \text{ true at } a\} \cup \{F : A \text{ not true at } a^*\}$.
We check that $v$ obeys the Belnap-Dunn tables.

For negation, $T \in v(\neg A)$ iff $\neg A$ is true at $a$ iff $A$ is not true at $a^*$ iff $F \in v(A)$.
Also $F \in v(\neg A)$ iff $\neg A$ is not true at $a^*$ iff $A$ is true at $a^{**} = a$ iff $T \in v(A)$.
For conjunction, $T \in v(A \land B)$ iff both parts are true at $a$.
And $F \in v(A \land B)$ iff one part is not true at $a^*$, iff $F \in v(A)$ or $F \in v(B)$.
Disjunction is dual.
So $v$ is a valuation in the Belnap-Dunn tables, and "$A$ is true at $a$" means "$T \in v(A)$".

For a concrete case, let $W = \{a, b\}$ with $a^* = b$ and $b^* = a$, and let $V(p) = \{a\}$ and $V(q) = \emptyset$.
Then $p$ and $\neg p$ are true at $a$ and $q$ is not, so $v(p) = \mathbf{B}$ and explosion fails at $a$.

## Remarks

Conversely, every valuation $v$ in $\mathbf{4}$ arises from a two-world model.
Take $a^* = b$, put $a \in V(p)$ iff $T \in v(p)$, and put $b \in V(p)$ iff $F \notin v(p)$.
So validity at all worlds of all star models is the same relation as $\models_{\mathsf{FDE}}$.
A world with $a^* = a$ is classical, while worlds with $a^* \ne a$ produce gluts or gaps.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The four values can be ordered by how true they are, or by how much we were told.
In the first order, true is at the top and false at the bottom.
In the second, "both" is at the top and "nothing" at the bottom.
The star trick checks "not $A$" at a partner world, which can make $A$ and "not $A$" true together.

### L2 human

Build the truth order from two rules: more $T$ is better, and less $F$ is better.
Read $\land$ and $\lor$ as meet and join in this order.
For the star semantics, compute truth at $a$ and at $a^*$, and pair them as in the definition of $v$.
The typical use is to move between algebraic and world-based counter-models.
Watch out: the star map must satisfy $a^{**} = a$, because otherwise double negation fails and the semantics is no longer that of $\mathsf{FDE}$.
