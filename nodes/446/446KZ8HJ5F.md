## Statement

Let $\Sigma$ and $\Gamma$ be alphabets, and let $K, L \subseteq \Sigma^*$ be [[regular languages|18WVBDCS38]].
Then the following languages are regular:

1. the union $K \cup L$, the intersection $K \cap L$, the difference $K \setminus L$ and the complement $\Sigma^* \setminus L$;
2. the concatenation $KL = \set{uv : u \in K,\ v \in L}$;
3. the [[Kleene star|ZW1M7XG8V4]] $L^*$;
4. the reversal $L^R = \set{a_n \cdots a_1 : a_1 \cdots a_n \in L}$;
5. the image $h(L)$ and, for a regular $M \subseteq \Gamma^*$, the preimage $g^{-1}(M)$, where $h \colon \Sigma^* \to \Gamma^*$ and $g \colon \Sigma^* \to \Gamma^*$ are monoid [[homomorphisms|KBXSZC6BV1]].

## Remarks

Complement: swap accepting and non-accepting states of a complete [[DFA|W65B6BKZRY]] for $L$.
Intersection: run DFAs for $K$ and $L$ in parallel on the product of their state sets, accepting when both accept; union and difference work the same way.
Concatenation, star and reversal are easiest with nondeterministic automata: glue automata with $\varepsilon$-moves, or reverse all arrows and swap initial and accepting states.
For the preimage, a DFA for $g^{-1}(M)$ moves on the letter $a$ the way a DFA for $M$ moves on the word $g(a)$.
For example, the words over $\set{a, b}$ with an even number of $a$'s and an odd number of $b$'s form a regular language, as the intersection of two regular languages.
The class is not closed under infinite unions: every language is a union of singletons, and $\set{a^n b^n : n \ge 0}$ is not regular.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If two sets of words can each be checked by a finite machine, then so can their union, their common part, and the words missing from one of them.
The same holds for gluing words together, repeating, and reading words backwards.
This means one can build complicated regular languages from simple ones and stay inside the class.

### L2 human

Closure properties are a main tool for showing that a language is not regular.
For instance, if $L = \set{w \in \set{a, b}^* : w \text{ has as many } a\text{'s as } b\text{'s}}$ were regular, then $L \cap a^* b^* = \set{a^n b^n}$ would be regular, which the [[pumping lemma|CBR9WAGZGZ]] rules out.
Context-free languages, in contrast, are not closed under intersection or complement.
The constructions are effective, so they also give algorithms, for example to decide whether two DFAs accept the same language by testing the symmetric difference for emptiness.
