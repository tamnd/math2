## Summary

A Lie superalgebra is a vector space split into an even part and an odd part, with a bracket.
The bracket obeys the rules of a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] up to signs given by the parity of the elements.
The even part is an ordinary Lie algebra, and the odd part is a module over it.
Lie superalgebras describe supersymmetry in physics and appear in deformation theory and algebraic topology.
They form a particular kind of [[superalgebra|6JZW4JC68Z]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a Lie superalgebra each element is even, odd, or a sum of the two.
Brackets of two even elements, or of an even and an odd element, follow the usual rules of a Lie algebra.
The bracket of two odd elements is symmetric instead of antisymmetric.
For example, in the Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)$ of $2 \times 2$ matrices, the off-diagonal matrices $x = E_{12}$ and $y = E_{21}$ are odd.
Their bracket is the anticommutator $xy + yx$, the identity matrix.
