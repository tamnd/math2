## Example

Let $U = \{(x, y, 0)\}$ be the $xy$-plane and $W = \{(0, y, z)\}$ the $yz$-plane in $\R^3$.
We check the [[second isomorphism theorem|0APN65D6Y6]] step by step.

1. The [[intersection|0V33SPH7RR]] $U \cap W$ is $\{(0, y, 0)\}$, the $y$-axis.
2. The [[sum|0RMKABS2S5]] $U + W$ is $\R^3$, since $(x, y, z) = (x, y, 0) + (0, 0, z)$.
3. In $U / (U \cap W)$, the coset of $(a, b, 0)$ is the line $\{(a, t, 0) : t \in \R\}$. Two vectors of $U$ have the same coset exactly when they have the same first entry.
4. In $(U + W)/W = \R^3 / W$, the coset of $(a, b, c)$ is the plane $x = a$.
5. The map of the theorem sends $(a, b, 0) + (U \cap W)$ to $(a, b, 0) + W$, the plane $x = a$.
6. It is well defined, since the result depends only on $a$. It is injective, since different $a$ give different planes. It is surjective, since every plane $x = a$ is hit.
7. So $U / (U \cap W) \cong (U + W)/W$, and both are isomorphic to $\R$ through the label $a$.

## Remarks

The dimensions are $\dim U = \dim W = 2$, $\dim(U \cap W) = 1$ and $\dim(U + W) = 3$.
They satisfy [[Grassmann's formula|06HTJNCMPA]]: $3 + 1 = 2 + 2$.
The sum $U + W$ is not direct, since $U \cap W \ne \{0\}$, but the theorem still applies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The $xy$-plane adds one direction to the $yz$-plane, namely the $x$-direction.
Both quotients in the theorem measure this one direction.

### L2 human

Each side of the theorem is computed from a different space.
The left side lives inside $U$, and the right side lives inside $U + W$.
The theorem says that they are the same up to isomorphism, here the line of values of $x$.
