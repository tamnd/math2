## Summary

Young's modulus $E$ measures the stiffness of a material in tension or compression along one axis.
It is the ratio of axial stress to axial strain in a bar pulled along its length with free sides.
It is the most common elastic constant in engineering, and together with [[Poisson's ratio|NWYZ9QBA84]] it determines an isotropic linear elastic material.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pull on a rod and it gets a little longer.
The stress is the force per unit area of the cross-section, and the strain is the relative change of length.
Young's modulus is stress divided by strain.
Rubber has a Young's modulus of a few megapascals and steel of about $200$ gigapascals, so steel is roughly $100\,000$ times stiffer.
A steel rod with stress $200$ MPa has strain $200 \text{ MPa} / 200 \text{ GPa} = 0.001$, so a $1$ m rod lengthens by $1$ mm.
