## Definition

Let $L \ge 0$ be the concentration of a free ligand, let $K > 0$ be the concentration that gives half occupancy, and let $n > 0$ be the *Hill coefficient*.
The *Hill equation* gives the fraction $\theta$ of occupied binding sites as
$$\theta(L) = \frac{L^n}{K^n + L^n}.$$
The function $\theta$ is called a (activating) *Hill function*, and $1 - \theta(L) = K^n/(K^n + L^n)$ is the repressing Hill function.

## Remarks

The function is [[increasing|0A7KV2WB7A]] from $\theta(0) = 0$ to the limit $1$, with $\theta(K) = 1/2$.
Taking logarithms gives the Hill plot: $\ln(\theta / (1 - \theta)) = n \ln L - n \ln K$, a straight line of slope $n$.
In terms of $x = \ln(L/K)$, $\theta = \frac{1}{2}\big(1 + \tanh(n x / 2)\big)$, a [[hyperbolic tangent|0X6C0MAF84]] curve.
Example: with $n = 1$ the equation is $\theta = L/(K + L)$, the same shape as the Michaelis–Menten rate law; one checks $\theta(K) = K/(2K) = 1/2$.
Non-example: $\theta(L) = 1 - e^{-L/K}$ is increasing from $0$ to $1$ but is not of Hill form, since its Hill plot is not a straight line.
The Hill coefficient fitted from data is at most the number of binding sites, and it is often not an integer.
Some authors write the equation as $L^n/(K_d + L^n)$ with $K_d = K^n$.
