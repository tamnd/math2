## Summary

A repeated game is a game in which the same [[normal-form game|5MZTZRC3CZ]], the stage game, is played many times by the same players.
After each round the players see what everyone did, and they can condition later choices on this history.
Repetition makes rewards and punishments possible, so cooperation can be sustained even when it is not an equilibrium of the stage game.
The [[folk theorem|DECWPTFMJ6]] describes the payoffs that equilibria of repeated games can reach.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two shops on the same street set their prices every week.
In a single week, each shop is tempted to undercut the other.
But because they meet again next week, a shop that undercuts today can be punished tomorrow.
Thinking of the whole sequence of weeks as one big game is the idea of a repeated game.
In the repeated [[prisoner's dilemma|1YER21VF5Q]], the threat "I cooperate as long as you do" can keep both players cooperating forever.
