## Summary

The virial mass of a dark matter halo, galaxy or cluster is the mass inside its virial radius.
Inside that radius the system is roughly in equilibrium.
The radius is chosen so that the mean density inside it is a fixed multiple of the critical density of the universe.
It gives a well-defined size and mass to objects that have no sharp edge.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A halo of dark matter thins out slowly and has no edge, so "the mass of the halo" needs a convention.
Cosmologists draw a sphere around the centre and grow it until the average density inside drops to, say, $200$ times the critical density of the universe.
The mass inside that sphere is the virial mass, and its radius is the virial radius.
The choice comes from the [[spherical collapse model|A8YJ7EKMV7]], in which a collapsed and settled object has roughly this density contrast.
