## Summary

Simple harmonic motion is periodic motion along a line in which the position is a sine function of time, $x(t) = A \cos(\omega t + \varphi)$.
It is the motion of the [[harmonic oscillator|0YG0ZJPTWG]], driven by a restoring force proportional to the displacement.
Its period does not depend on the amplitude.
Small vibrations near a stable equilibrium are approximately of this kind, which makes it the basic model of all oscillations.
See the [[definition|1GRHMHWK7R]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A mass on a spring, pulled and released, moves back and forth in a smooth, regular way.
Its position, plotted against time, is a sine wave.
The spring pulls harder the farther the mass is from rest, and this exact proportionality makes every swing take the same time, large or small.
A mass of $1$ kg on a spring of stiffness $4$ N/m oscillates with angular frequency $2$ per second, so one full swing takes $\pi$ seconds.
The shadow of a point moving at constant speed around a circle performs the same motion.
