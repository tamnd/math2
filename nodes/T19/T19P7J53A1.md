## Summary

A holomorphic map between [[Riemann surfaces|5K0N8QMV6Q]] is a continuous map that is holomorphic when written in local coordinates on both sides.
These maps are the morphisms of the category of Riemann surfaces; the bijective ones are the biholomorphisms, which identify two Riemann surfaces.
Meromorphic functions on a Riemann surface are the same as holomorphic maps to the [[Riemann sphere|0CDMXGPSES]] that are not constantly $\infty$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a Riemann surface you cannot differentiate directly, but you can always look through a chart, which is a piece of the complex plane.
A map between two surfaces is holomorphic if every such view of it is an ordinary holomorphic function.
For instance $z \mapsto z^2$ on the Riemann sphere, with $\infty \mapsto \infty$, is holomorphic: near $\infty$ in the coordinate $w = 1/z$ it reads $w \mapsto w^2$.
Near any point where it is not constant, a holomorphic map looks like $z \mapsto z^k$ for some $k \ge 1$ in suitable coordinates.
