## Summary

Stokes drift is the average velocity of a fluid particle in an oscillating flow, minus the average velocity at a fixed point.
In a wave the two averages differ, because a particle spends slightly longer in the region where the flow carries it forward.
Under [[water waves|R9WC0Z7Z69]] the result is a slow net transport of water in the direction of the waves.
Stokes drift matters for the transport of floating debris, oil and plankton by ocean waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A floating cork on a wave seems to bob up and down in place, but it slowly creeps forward with the waves.
Under the crest the water moves forward, and under the trough it moves back.
The cork moves forward with the crest and so stays a little longer in the forward-moving water.
Over each wave period these small gains add up to a steady drift.
