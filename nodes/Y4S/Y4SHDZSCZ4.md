## Summary

Massive gravity is a family of theories of gravity in which the graviton, the quantum of the gravitational field, has a nonzero mass.
At linear order around flat spacetime the unique consistent theory is the one of Fierz and Pauli from 1939.
Making the theory nonlinear without an extra unstable mode, the Boulware–Deser ghost, was achieved only around 2010 by de Rham, Gabadadze and Tolley.
Massive gravity is studied as an [[alternative to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]] that weakens gravity at very large distances.
This might explain the accelerated expansion of the universe.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In general relativity gravity reaches out to any distance, weakening like the inverse square of the distance, which corresponds to a massless graviton.
If the graviton had a tiny mass, gravity would fade away much faster beyond some huge distance.
Building such a theory turns out to be hard: naive versions either disagree with Solar System tests or contain unstable modes.
Observations of gravitational waves put a very small upper bound on the graviton mass.
