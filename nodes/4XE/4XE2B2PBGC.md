## Summary

Friction is the force that resists the sliding of two surfaces in contact.
In [[classical mechanics|68W1NWECX8]] it is usually modelled by Coulomb's law.
Static friction can hold a body at rest, up to a limit proportional to the normal force.
Kinetic friction opposes sliding, with a size proportional to the normal force.
Friction is not conservative: it turns mechanical energy into heat.
Its set-valued, non-smooth form leads to differential inclusions and to paradoxes in rigid-body dynamics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Push a heavy box across the floor: at first it does not move, then it suddenly slides, and you must keep pushing to keep it sliding.
The floor's grip is friction.
A heavier box presses harder on the floor and needs a harder push.
If the floor grips with coefficient $0.5$ and the box weighs $100$ newtons, you need more than $50$ newtons to start it moving.
