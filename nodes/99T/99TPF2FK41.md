## Statement

Let a system exchange heat with an environment at fixed temperature $T_0 > 0$, and possibly with further heat reservoirs.
Let it go from a given initial state to a given final state.
Let $W_{\mathrm{rev}}$ be the useful [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] it would deliver in a reversible process between these states.
This reversible process exchanges the same heat with each further reservoir as the actual process.
Let $W$ be the useful work it delivers in the actual process.
Let $S_{\mathrm{gen}} \ge 0$ be the [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]] generated in the actual process, that is the total entropy change of the system and of all the reservoirs.
Then the lost work is
$$W_{\mathrm{rev}} - W = T_0\, S_{\mathrm{gen}}.$$

## Remarks

The proof combines the first law with the entropy balance.
Heat exchanged with the environment enters both balances, and eliminating it between them gives $W = W_{\mathrm{rev}} - T_0 S_{\mathrm{gen}}$, where $W_{\mathrm{rev}}$ is the value with $S_{\mathrm{gen}} = 0$.
In terms of [[exergy|AEFN5F79X0]], the right side $T_0 S_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is the exergy destroyed in the process.
By the [[second law|T11MGWEKJ3]], $S_{\mathrm{gen}} \ge 0$, so the actual work never exceeds the reversible work, with equality only for a reversible process.

Example: let heat $Q$ flow directly from a reservoir at $T_1$ to the environment at $T_0 < T_1$, with no work done.
Then $W = 0$ and $S_{\mathrm{gen}} = Q/T_0 - Q/T_1$, so the lost work is $Q(1 - T_0/T_1)$, the work a Carnot engine would have produced from $Q$.
The same balances are often written as rates for steady processes, with $\dot W_{\mathrm{lost}} = T_0 \dot S_{\mathrm{gen}}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every real process wastes some ability to do work, through friction, mixing or heat flowing across a temperature difference.
The Gouy–Stodola theorem measures this waste.
It says the work lost equals the temperature of the surroundings times the entropy created.
So to find where a machine loses the most, find where it creates the most entropy.

### L2 human

The theorem is the bridge between entropy generation and exergy destruction in engineering thermodynamics.
An exergy balance of a plant splits the input exergy into useful output, exergy carried away with waste streams, and the destroyed part $T_0 S_{\mathrm{gen}}$ of each component.
Minimising entropy generation is then the same as maximising the work output for given end states, which is the basis of entropy generation minimisation in design.
The temperature in the formula is that of the environment, not of the system, because the environment is the final sink for all rejected heat.
