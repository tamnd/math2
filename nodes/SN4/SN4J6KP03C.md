## Summary

The speed of sound is the speed at which small pressure disturbances travel through a fluid at rest.
It is fixed by how much the pressure rises when the fluid is compressed without exchange of heat, through $c^2 = (\partial p / \partial \rho)_s$.
It sets the scale for all effects of compressibility, and the ratio of the flow speed to it is the Mach number.
For an ideal gas it depends only on the temperature, $c = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When you clap, you push the air a little, and the push is passed on from one layer of air to the next.
How fast it travels depends on how stiff the air is against squeezing and how heavy it is.
A stiffer or lighter medium carries sound faster.
In air at $20$ degrees Celsius sound travels about $343$ metres per second, so thunder from a lightning strike $1$ kilometre away arrives about $3$ seconds after the flash.
