## Summary

Grzegorczyk logic $\mathsf{Grz}$ is the normal [[modal logic|C3RZY20ETK]] $\mathsf{K}$ with the extra axiom $\square(\square(p \to \square p) \to p) \to p$, with modus ponens, the [[necessitation rule|G989VP2NGP]] and uniform substitution as rules.
It contains $\mathsf{S4}$, and it is the logic of the finite [[partial orders|0QV2JS9RJ3]] in [[Kripke semantics|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]].
Under the translation $(\square \psi)^t = \psi^t \wedge \square \psi^t$, $\mathsf{Grz} \vdash \varphi$ holds exactly when $\mathsf{GL} \vdash \varphi^t$, so $\mathsf{Grz}$ is the logic of "true and provable" in $\mathsf{PA}$.
It is also the largest modal companion of [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]].
So one logic links [[provability logic|WJK0YA4GP2]], the order semantics of $\mathsf{S4}$, and intuitionistic logic.

## Motivation

The box of $\mathsf{GL}$ is not reflexive: $\square p \to p$ is not a law of provability.
A natural reflexive operator is strong provability, "$p$ is true and provable", written $p \wedge \square p$.
Its laws are those of $\mathsf{S4}$ and one more, the Grzegorczyk axiom, which plays the role of Löb's axiom for reflexive frames.
The same logic appears as the strongest modal logic into which intuitionistic logic embeds by the Gödel translation.

## Notation

1. The axiom $\square(\square(p \to \square p) \to p) \to p$ is called $\mathrm{Grz}$, and the logic is written $\mathsf{Grz}$ or $\mathsf{S4Grz}$.
2. For a formula $\varphi$, $\varphi^t$ is the translation that commutes with $\bot$, variables and $\to$, and has $(\square \psi)^t = \psi^t \wedge \square \psi^t$.
3. The Gödel translation $T$ of intuitionistic formulas into modal formulas puts $\square$ in front of variables and implications; see the [[modal logic|C3RZY20ETK]] foundations.

## Remarks

### Examples

1. [[Grz proves the reflexivity axiom|THNYAWYPM5]]: $\mathsf{Grz} \vdash \square p \to p$. Axiom 4, $\square p \to \square \square p$, is also a theorem of $\mathsf{Grz}$, so $\mathsf{S4} \subseteq \mathsf{Grz}$.
2. [[The two-element chain is a frame of Grz|F1BNDBCF7W]]: the frame $a \leq b$ validates $\mathrm{Grz}$, and the same argument works for every finite partial order.
3. The translation of $\square p \to p$ is $p \wedge \square p \to p$, a tautology, so it is in $\mathsf{GL}$, as the translation theorem requires.

### Non-examples

1. [[The two-element cluster is not a frame of Grz|GA3VNQQBPB]]: two worlds that see each other and themselves form an $\mathsf{S4}$ frame that refutes $\mathrm{Grz}$. So $\mathsf{S4} \neq \mathsf{Grz}$.
2. The partial order $(\N, \leq)$ is not a frame of $\mathsf{Grz}$. Let $p$ be true exactly at the even numbers. Every world sees an odd number above it, so $p \to \square p$ fails at every even number, and $\square(p \to \square p)$ fails everywhere. Then $\square(\square(p \to \square p) \to p)$ holds everywhere, while $p$ fails at $1$.
3. $\mathsf{GL}$ and $\mathsf{Grz}$ are incomparable: $\square p \to p$ is in $\mathsf{Grz}$ only, and Löb's axiom is in $\mathsf{GL}$ only, since it fails on the one-world reflexive frame, a finite partial order.

### Basic properties

1. Frames: $\mathrm{Grz}$ is valid on a frame exactly when the frame is a partial order with no infinite strictly ascending chain $w_0 < w_1 < \cdots$. $\mathsf{Grz}$ is complete for finite partial orders, so it is decidable. We cite these facts.
2. Translation theorem: $\mathsf{Grz} \vdash \varphi$ if and only if $\mathsf{GL} \vdash \varphi^t$. We cite it. With [[Solovay's theorem|1ZRWVDYPX0]], $\mathsf{Grz} \vdash \varphi$ if and only if $\mathsf{PA} \vdash (\varphi^t)^*$ for every realization $*$.
3. Modal companion: an intuitionistic formula $\varphi$ is provable in intuitionistic logic if and only if $\mathsf{Grz} \vdash T(\varphi)$. $\mathsf{Grz}$ is the largest normal extension of $\mathsf{S4}$ with this property.
4. Blok-Esakia: the extensions of intuitionistic logic correspond one to one, preserving order, to the normal extensions of $\mathsf{Grz}$. Algebraically, [[Heyting algebras|NG943XNQWH]] correspond to the [[interior algebras|BDEX7HET71]] that satisfy $\mathrm{Grz}$ and are generated by their open elements.

### History

The logic is named after Andrzej Grzegorczyk.
Wim Blok and Leo Esakia proved independently that the lattice of extensions of intuitionistic logic is isomorphic to the lattice of normal extensions of $\mathsf{Grz}$.

### Common errors

1. Taking $\mathsf{Grz}$ to be $\mathsf{S4}$ with antisymmetry. $\mathsf{S4}$ is also complete for the class of all partial orders, by unravelling its models into trees. The Grz axiom excludes infinite strictly ascending chains too, as in Non-example 2.
2. Reading the translation as $(\square \psi)^t = \square \psi^t$. Without the conjunct $\psi^t$ the translation of $\square p \to p$ is not a theorem of $\mathsf{GL}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Grzegorczyk logic is a modal logic where $\square p$ means "$p$ is true and provable".
Unlike plain provability, this box satisfies "if $\square p$, then $p$".
Its pictures are finite orderings of points, where each point sees itself and the points above it.
The same logic is the strongest one that matches intuitionistic logic through Gödel's translation.

### L2 human

We check that $\mathrm{Grz}$ holds at the bottom of the chain $a \leq b$ when $p$ is false at $a$ and true at $b$.

1. At $b$, $p$ is true and $b$ sees only $b$, so $b \models \square p$ and $b \models p \to \square p$.
2. At $a$, $p$ is false, so $a \models p \to \square p$.
3. Hence $a \models \square(p \to \square p)$, and $a \not\models p$, so $a \not\models \square(p \to \square p) \to p$.
4. Since $a \leq a$, $a \not\models \square(\square(p \to \square p) \to p)$, and the axiom $\mathrm{Grz}$ is true at $a$.

The typical use is to show that a formula is not in $\mathsf{Grz}$ by a finite partial order that refutes it.
A trap: an $\mathsf{S4}$ countermodel with a cluster is not enough, since clusters are not $\mathsf{Grz}$ frames.

### L3 human

$\mathsf{Grz}$ sits at the meeting point of three theories: the reflexive companion of $\mathsf{GL}$, the order semantics of $\mathsf{S4}$, and the modal companions of intuitionistic logic.
The map $\varphi \mapsto \varphi^t$ is the syntactic form of the passage from a strict order to its reflexive closure: on a finite strict partial order, $\varphi^t$ is true at a world exactly when $\varphi$ is true there in the reflexive closure.
The Blok-Esakia theorem makes the link with intuitionistic logic algebraic, through the interior algebras generated by their open elements.
