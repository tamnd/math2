## Summary

The trace distance is a metric on the set of quantum states, defined as half the trace norm of their difference.
It generalises the total variation distance between probability distributions.
Its operational meaning is the largest difference of the probabilities that the two states give to one measurement outcome.
For this reason it controls how well two states can be told apart.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of two quantum states as two coins that may be biased.
The trace distance is the best advantage a single measurement can have in telling which one was handed over.
If the trace distance is $0$, the states are the same and no test can tell them apart.
If it is $1$, one measurement tells them apart with certainty, as for $\lvert 0 \rangle$ and $\lvert 1 \rangle$.
For a qubit, the trace distance is half the Euclidean distance between the two points on the Bloch ball.
