## Definition

A *thermodynamic system* is a macroscopic body or region of space, separated from the rest of the world, its *surroundings*, by a boundary of walls.
A system is:

1. *isolated* if its walls let through neither matter nor energy;
2. *closed* if its walls let through energy, as heat or work, but no matter;
3. *open* if its walls let through both matter and energy.

## Remarks

A gas in a rigid, sealed metal container is a closed system: heat passes through the metal, but no gas does.
It is not isolated, because it fails clause 1: heat passes through the walls.
A wall that lets no heat through is called *adiabatic*; a wall that lets heat through is called *diathermal*.
