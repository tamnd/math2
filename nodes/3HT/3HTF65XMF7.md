## Summary

An asymptotically flat spacetime is a spacetime whose geometry approaches that of flat [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]] far away from its sources.
It is the model of an isolated system, such as a star, a black hole or a binary, in an otherwise empty universe.
The notion is made precise in two ways.
One uses fall-off conditions on the metric at spatial infinity; the other, due to Penrose, uses a [[conformal compactification|E4TDQZ7DDV]] with a null boundary at infinity.
It is the setting in which total mass, momentum and outgoing gravitational radiation are well defined.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Far from the Sun, its gravity becomes weaker and weaker, and spacetime looks almost flat.
An asymptotically flat spacetime is one where this happens in every direction.
This lets one speak of the total mass of the system, as seen by a distant observer, and of the waves it sends out to infinity.
The Schwarzschild black hole is an example, since its metric tends to the flat one as $r \to \infty$.
An expanding universe filled with matter is not, because it never becomes empty far away.
