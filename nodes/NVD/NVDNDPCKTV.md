## Summary

The Standard Model of particle physics is a [[gauge theory|2GKJQNQMVN]] on Minkowski space with gauge group $SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)$.
Its mathematical formulation lists the representations in which the matter fields transform and the Lagrangian density that couples them.
It combines [[quantum chromodynamics|SEE8XD0APW]] with the electroweak theory, in which the [[Higgs mechanism|F985ZCF9SZ]] breaks $SU(2) \times U(1)$ down to the $U(1)$ of electromagnetism.
It describes all known elementary particles and every known force except gravity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Standard Model is the list of the basic particles of nature together with the rules for how they interact.
Mathematically, each particle type is a field that transforms in a definite way under three symmetry groups, one for each force other than gravity.
The forces themselves are carried by gauge fields, one for each direction in these groups: eight gluons, and the $W$, $Z$ and photon after symmetry breaking.
Once the groups and the transformation rules are fixed, the possible interactions are almost forced, up to a few numbers measured in experiments.
