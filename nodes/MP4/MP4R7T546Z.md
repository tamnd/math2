## Statement

Let $p$ be a propositional variable.
The formula
$$\neg(p \land \neg p)$$
is a [[tautology|05006AJ7DZ]] of classical propositional logic, and it is also provable in [[intuitionistic logic|Q11N8X99FK]].
Here $\neg$ is [[negation|029JBBF693]] and $\land$ is [[conjunction|025YZN3VJW]].
Equivalently, $p \land \neg p$ is a contradiction: it is false under every valuation, so $p \land \neg p \equiv \bot$.
By substitution, $\neg(\varphi \land \neg\varphi)$ is a tautology for every formula $\varphi$.
In words: no [[proposition|0ZGC7ZESYN]] is true together with its negation.

There are two proofs: the [[proof by a truth table|82TG5066FF]] and the [[proof by natural deduction|RN3Y6804V7]], which uses only intuitionistic rules.
In Lean, where `Not p` is defined as `p → False`, a proof of `¬(p ∧ ¬p)` is the function `fun h => (And.right h) (And.left h)`.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The law has no hypotheses on $p$.
We show instead which close variants fail, and how the law depends on the semantics.

1. The same variable is needed twice. The formula $\neg(p \land q)$ is not a tautology: in the row $p = \mathrm{T}$, $q = \mathrm{T}$ it is F.
2. The inner negation is needed. The formula $\neg(p \land p)$ is equivalent to $\neg p$, and it is F in the row $p = \mathrm{T}$.
3. Two truth values are used in the truth-table proof. In Kleene's three-valued logic, where the third value U satisfies $\neg \mathrm{U} = \mathrm{U}$ and only T counts as true, the formula $\neg(p \land \neg p)$ takes the value U when $p$ does. So the law is not a tautology of that logic.
4. In the Logic of Paradox of Graham Priest, a [[paraconsistent logic|C7YWCR87QJ]], the third value B means "both true and false", and B counts as true. With $p = \mathrm{B}$, the formula $\neg(p \land \neg p)$ has the value B, so the law still holds, but $p \land \neg p$ also holds. The law no longer excludes a contradiction there.

### Consequences

1. All contradictions are equivalent to $p \land \neg p$, since all of them have a column with only F.
2. Refutation by contradiction. If an assumption $r$ leads to $q$ and to $\neg q$, then $r$ is false, because $q \land \neg q$ is never true. This is the logical basis of [[proof by contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]].
3. The formula $(p \land \neg p) \to q$ is a tautology, since its hypothesis is false in every row. This is the principle of [[explosion|TTKZGJEEK7]].
4. A set of formulas that contains $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ has no valuation that makes all its members true.
5. By [[De Morgan's laws (logic)|09PKJKA6J0]] and the [[double negation law|0NFAGWH84K]], classically $\neg(p \land \neg p) \equiv \neg p \lor \neg\neg p \equiv \neg p \lor p$. So the law of noncontradiction and the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]] are classically equivalent. Intuitionistically the first step uses the direction of De Morgan's law that fails, and only the law of noncontradiction holds.
6. For sets, $A \cap A^c = \varnothing$ for each subset $A$ of a universal set, where $A^c$ is the [[complement|0RKF1ZT5JJ]] of $A$.

### Converse

The law is a single formula, not an implication, so it has no converse in the strict sense.
Read as "$p \land \neg p$ is a contradiction", it has a true converse: every contradiction is equivalent to $p \land \neg p$, by consequence 1.

### Generalizations

1. In a [[Boolean algebra|0XADJKM699]] and in a [[Heyting algebra|NG943XNQWH]], each element $a$ satisfies $a \land \neg a = 0$. In a Heyting algebra, $\neg a$ is defined as the largest element $b$ with $a \land b = 0$.
2. For a predicate $P(x)$ on a domain $D$, the law gives $\neg \exists x \, (P(x) \land \neg P(x))$, and also $\forall x \, \neg(P(x) \land \neg P(x))$.
3. The natural deduction proof uses no rule about the false statement, so the law holds even in minimal logic, the part of intuitionistic logic without explosion.

## History

Aristotle stated the law in Book IV (Gamma) of the Metaphysics.
His formulation says that one property cannot belong and also not belong to the same thing, at the same time and in the same respect.
He called it the most certain of all principles, and he held that it cannot be proved from anything more basic: it can only be defended against an opponent who says something.
In the twentieth century, paraconsistent logics gave semantics in which a contradiction does not imply every statement.
Dialetheism, defended above all by Graham Priest, goes further and holds that some contradictions are true.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The law says that a statement and its opposite cannot both be true.
"The number $7$ is even and the number $7$ is not even" is false, whatever number we put in place of $7$.
If a calculation shows that a number is even and also not even, then the calculation contains an error, or one of its assumptions is false.
Most of logic and mathematics depends on this rule.

### L2 human

We see the law at work in the proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

1. Assume that $\sqrt{2} = a/b$ with integers $a$, $b$, $b \neq 0$, and with the fraction in lowest terms. Let $q$ be "$a$ and $b$ are both even".
2. Lowest terms means that $\neg q$ holds.
3. From $a^2 = 2b^2$, the number $a^2$ is even, so $a$ is even, $a = 2c$.
4. Then $4c^2 = 2b^2$, so $b^2 = 2c^2$ is even, and $b$ is even. So $q$ holds.
5. Now $q \land \neg q$ holds, and the law of noncontradiction says that this is impossible. So the assumption in step 1 is false.

The typical use is the last step of a proof by contradiction.
A trap is to call two contrary statements a contradiction.
"$x > 0$" and "$x < 0$" cannot both be true, but they can both be false, for $x = 0$, so neither is the negation of the other.

### L3 human

In the [[Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov interpretation|7B53ZGXXEQ]], a proof of $\neg(p \land \neg p)$ is a method that turns a pair (a proof $a$ of $p$, a method $f$ from proofs of $p$ to absurdity) into an absurdity, and $f(a)$ does this.
Through the [[Curry–Howard correspondence|A77FER1Q5H]], this is the Lean term above: function application.
For this reason the law holds in intuitionistic and in minimal logic, while its classical twin, the law of excluded middle, does not.

The dialetheist challenge does not deny the formula $\neg(p \land \neg p)$ in every logic.
It denies that the formula excludes the truth of $p \land \neg p$, and it gives candidates such as the [[liar sentence|KBYY281V8T]], "this sentence is false".
A dialetheist must use a [[paraconsistent logic|C7YWCR87QJ]], since in classical logic a single true contradiction would make every statement true by explosion.
