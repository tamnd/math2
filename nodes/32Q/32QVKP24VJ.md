## Summary

Linkage disequilibrium is the non-random association of alleles at two or more loci in a population.
It is measured by how far the frequency of a combination of alleles differs from the product of the single allele frequencies.
It decays under recombination, and it is used to map genes and to detect natural selection and population history.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose each person carries one version at gene 1 and one version at gene 2.
If the versions were independent, the share of people with both $A$ and $B$ would be the share with $A$ times the share with $B$.
When the actual share is different, the two genes are in linkage disequilibrium.
For example, suppose half the chromosomes carry $A$ and $B$ together and the other half carry neither.
Then the shares of $A$ and of $B$ are $1/2$, but $A$ and $B$ always come together.
