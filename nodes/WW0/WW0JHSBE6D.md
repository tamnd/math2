## Summary

An orbital perturbation is a departure of a motion from a Kepler orbit, caused by forces other than the inverse-square attraction of one central body.
Typical causes are the pull of other bodies, the flattening of the central body, atmospheric drag and radiation pressure.
Perturbations explain the slow turning of planetary orbits and the drift of satellite orbits, and they are studied through the change of the osculating elements.
See the [[definition|2F6JGZ24K0]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

If only the Sun pulled on a planet, the planet would trace the same ellipse forever.
The other planets also pull a little, so the ellipse slowly turns and changes shape.
These small extra effects are perturbations.
The discovery of Neptune came from perturbations of the orbit of Uranus that the known planets could not explain.
