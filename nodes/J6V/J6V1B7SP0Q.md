## Summary

A gravity anomaly is the difference between the actual gravity of the Earth at a point and the gravity of a smooth reference model there.
In geodesy the model is the [[normal gravity|Y35VMCD5K0]] of a [[reference ellipsoid|R9XX7TNJCH]].
Gravity anomalies are the main data for computing the [[geoid|999BYZY0ZG]], and in geophysics they reveal density contrasts below the surface.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If the Earth were a perfect ellipsoid of layered rock, gravity would change smoothly from the equator to the poles.
The real Earth has mountains, ocean trenches, dense ore bodies and light sediment basins.
Each of these pulls a little more or a little less than the smooth model predicts.
The gravity anomaly is this small excess or deficit.
It is measured in milligal, where $1$ mGal $= 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, about a millionth of ordinary gravity.
A dense buried body gives a positive anomaly above it, and a light one gives a negative anomaly.
