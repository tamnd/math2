## Summary

A Tollmien–Schlichting wave is an unstable travelling wave that grows in a viscous wall-bounded shear flow, such as a [[boundary layer|YCFPD0GE5G]] or a channel flow.
It is a two-dimensional eigenmode of the [[Orr–Sommerfeld equation|G0ADVS0K99]] that grows because of viscosity, in flows that would be stable without viscosity.
Tollmien and Schlichting computed these waves around 1930, and the experiments of Schubauer and Skramstad, published in 1947, confirmed them.
They are the first stage of the classical route from a laminar boundary layer to turbulence.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air flowing over a smooth wing forms a thin layer near the surface where its speed drops to zero.
Far enough along the wing, tiny ripples in this layer start to grow as they travel downstream, as long, slow waves.
These are Tollmien–Schlichting waves.
Oddly, friction is what makes them grow: in a frictionless model of the same layer they would not grow at all.
