## Summary

A competitive equilibrium, also called a Walrasian equilibrium, is a list of prices together with an allocation of goods.
At it, every agent chooses optimally while taking prices as given, and all markets clear.
It is the central solution concept of [[general equilibrium theory|HGMHZSM0XQ]].
Under mild conditions it exists.
When preferences are locally nonsatiated, the [[fundamental theorems of welfare economics|J6KA8R1H5Z]] show that its allocations are [[Pareto efficient|JREVYHFY4T]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a market where nobody bargains: a price is posted for every good, and everyone buys and sells what suits them best at those prices.
A competitive equilibrium is a set of prices at which these separate plans fit together, so that no good is left over and no one is turned away.
For instance, say Ann owns an apple, Bob owns an orange, and both would like half of each.
At equal prices they trade half an apple for half an orange, and both markets clear.
