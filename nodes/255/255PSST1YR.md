## Summary

Quantum tunnelling is the passage of a quantum particle through a potential barrier that is higher than its energy.
A classical particle with that energy would be reflected with certainty.
It follows from the [[Schrödinger equation|EPZR6H2WJN]], whose solutions decay but do not vanish inside the barrier.
It explains alpha decay and is the working principle of the scanning tunnelling microscope.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A ball rolled at a hill too high for it always rolls back.
A quantum particle is described by a [[wave function|E9WK6KJ4SM]], and inside the hill this wave does not stop sharply but fades exponentially.
If the hill is thin enough, a little of the wave survives on the other side, so the particle has a small chance to appear there.
The chance falls very fast as the barrier gets wider or higher, roughly like $e^{-2 a \sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}/\hbar}$ for a barrier of width $a$ and height $V_0$.
