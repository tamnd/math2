## Statement

Let $\nu > 0$ and let $D \subseteq \R^3 \times \R$ be an open set of space-time points $(x, t)$.
Let $u \colon D \to \R^3$ and $p \colon D \to \R$ be a *suitable weak solution* of the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] in $D$ with no external force.
This means the following.

1. $u$ is in $L^\infty_t L^2_x$, $\nabla u$ is in $L^2$ and $p$ is in $L^{5/4}$ on every compact subset of $D$.
2. The pair $(u, p)$ satisfies $\partial_t u + (u \cdot \nabla) u = \nu \Delta u - \nabla p$ and $\nabla \cdot u = 0$ in $D$ in the sense of distributions.
3. The local energy inequality holds: for every smooth function $\varphi \ge 0$ with compact support in $D$,
$$2 \nu \iint \abs{\nabla u}^2 \varphi \, dx \, dt \le \iint \Big( \abs{u}^2 (\partial_t \varphi + \nu \Delta \varphi) + (\abs{u}^2 + 2p) \, u \cdot \nabla \varphi \Big) dx \, dt.$$

A point of $D$ is *regular* if $u$ is essentially bounded on some neighbourhood of it, and *singular* otherwise.
Let $S$ be the set of singular points.
Then $\mathcal{P}^1(S) = 0$.
Here $\mathcal{P}^1$ is the one-dimensional parabolic [[Hausdorff measure|Q1TGZK8RN6]], built like the Hausdorff measure but with covers by parabolic cylinders $B_r(x) \times (t - r^2, t + r^2)$ of size $r$.

## Remarks

The proof rests on an $\varepsilon$-regularity criterion.
There is an absolute constant $\varepsilon > 0$ such that if $\limsup_{r \to 0} r^{-1} \iint_{Q_r(x, t)} \abs{\nabla u}^2 \, dy \, ds < \varepsilon$, with $Q_r(x, t)$ the parabolic cylinder of size $r$ around $(x, t)$, then $(x, t)$ is regular.
A covering argument then shows that the set where the criterion fails has $\mathcal{P}^1$-measure zero, since $\abs{\nabla u}^2$ is integrable.
Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg also proved that suitable weak solutions exist for every divergence-free initial velocity of finite energy, so the theorem applies to a class of Leray–Hopf weak solutions.
In particular, the singular set of such a solution cannot contain a curve in space-time.
Its projection on the time axis has $\tfrac{1}{2}$-dimensional Hausdorff measure zero, since a parabolic cylinder of size $r$ projects to a time interval of length $2r^2$.
The local energy inequality is needed: the argument controls $u$ near a point only through it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Nobody knows whether three-dimensional viscous flows stay smooth forever.
This theorem says that if they break down, the breakdown is very thin.
The bad points cannot fill a surface or even a curve in space and time.
At almost every place and moment, a suitable weak solution is smooth.

### L2 human

The theorem is the best known partial result on the regularity of [[weak solutions|SJZX81A783]] of the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations.
It uses the natural scaling $u_\lambda(x, t) = \lambda u(\lambda x, \lambda^2 t)$ of the equations, under which the quantity $r^{-1} \iint_{Q_r} \abs{\nabla u}^2$ is invariant.
This is why the parabolic dimension $1$ appears.
Later work simplified the proof and adapted the $\varepsilon$-regularity method to other equations.
Full regularity would settle the [[Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness|46Z1TVS2K6]] problem.
