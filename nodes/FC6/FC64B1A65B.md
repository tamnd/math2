## Summary

Poisson's equation in electrostatics is the equation $\Delta \varphi = -\rho / \varepsilon_0$ for the [[electric potential|SAEDXNM3Y1]] $\varphi$ of a charge density $\rho$.
It is the case of [[Poisson's equation|DWK5N5SKTK]] that comes from physics.
It packs Coulomb's law and Gauss's law into one second-order partial differential equation.
With boundary conditions, it is the main tool to find potentials near conductors.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The equation says that the potential curves most strongly where there is charge.
Where there is no charge, the potential is perfectly balanced.
There its value at a point is the average of its values on any small sphere around that point.
A positive charge makes the potential bulge upward, like a tent pushed up by a pole.
For a uniformly charged ball, the potential inside is a downward-opening paraboloid in the radius.
