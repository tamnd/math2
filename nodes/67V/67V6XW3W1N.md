## Summary

Batcher odd–even mergesort is a recursive construction of a [[sorting network|G854V12EC7]] for $n = 2^k$ inputs, due to Kenneth Batcher.
It sorts the two halves and then merges them with an odd–even merging network.
This network merges the odd-indexed and the even-indexed elements separately and ends with one layer of comparators.
The resulting network has depth $k(k+1)/2$ and uses $O(n \log^2 n)$ comparators.
It is one of the classical practical sorting networks, used for parallel sorting in hardware and on GPUs.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The idea follows [[merge sort|GNGVP9EE8Y]]: sort each half, then merge.
The merging step is itself done by a fixed network, so no decision depends on the data.
To merge two sorted lists, one merges the elements in odd positions and the elements in even positions separately.
After that, the result is almost sorted, and one round of neighbour comparisons fixes it.
For $4$ inputs the network uses $5$ comparators in $3$ rounds, and for $8$ inputs it uses $19$ comparators in $6$ rounds.
