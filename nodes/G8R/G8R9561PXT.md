## Summary

The stable general linear group $GL(R)$ of a ring $R$ is the union of all the [[general linear groups|0FCHE902D7]] $GL_n(R)$.
Each $GL_n(R)$ is placed inside the next by adding a $1$ on the diagonal.
It is the group of invertible infinite matrices that differ from the identity in only finitely many entries.
Algebraic K-theory starts from it: [[K1 of a ring|SXVNJY4XFY]] is its abelianisation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An invertible $n \times n$ matrix can be seen as an $(n+1) \times (n+1)$ matrix by adding one more row and column with a $1$ in the corner.
Doing this for all sizes at once puts all invertible matrices of all sizes into one big group.
In this group the size of a matrix no longer matters, only its essential part.
For example, the $1 \times 1$ matrix $(2)$ over $\Q$ and the $2 \times 2$ matrix $\mathrm{diag}(2, 1)$ are the same element of $GL(\Q)$.
Working in the big group makes many questions simpler, because there is always room to move things around.
