## Summary

The Dupuit–Forchheimer assumption is an approximation for groundwater flow in an unconfined aquifer.
When the water table slopes gently, the flow is taken to be horizontal and uniform over the depth.
It reduces the three-dimensional free-boundary problem to a two-dimensional equation for the height of the water table, the Boussinesq equation.
It is named after Jules Dupuit and Philipp Forchheimer.
It is accurate when the aquifer is much wider than it is thick.
It fails near wells and seepage faces, where the flow has strong vertical components.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Under a field, water fills the ground up to the water table, and it slowly flows sideways toward rivers or wells.
If the water table is nearly flat, the water at every depth moves nearly the same way, horizontally and at the same speed.
The Dupuit–Forchheimer assumption says exactly this, which makes the problem much easier: one only needs the height of the water table at each place.
For example, the steady flow from a high lake to a low river through a strip of sand can then be computed in one line.
