## Example

We prove that there are no integers $x$ and $y$ with $x^2 - 3y^2 = 2$.
The proof is a [[proof of impossibility|6F55216R53]] by a remainder obstruction.

1. Suppose that $x$ and $y$ are integers with $x^2 - 3y^2 = 2$, and aim for a [[contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]].
2. Then $x^2 = 3y^2 + 2$, so $x^2$ leaves remainder $2$ on division by $3$.
3. But the square of an integer leaves remainder $0$ or $1$ on division by $3$, by [[the case split on n modulo 3|N70XWKTVMC]]:
   $$(3k)^2 = 3 \cdot 3k^2, \qquad (3k + 1)^2 = 3(3k^2 + 2k) + 1, \qquad (3k + 2)^2 = 3(3k^2 + 4k + 1) + 1.$$
4. Steps 2 and 3 contradict each other, so no integers $x$ and $y$ satisfy the equation.

The proof assumes a solution and refutes it, so it proves the negative statement directly, with no appeal to excluded middle.

## Remarks

The obstruction is arithmetic, not a matter of size.
Over the real numbers the equation has many solutions, for example $x = 2$ and $y = \sqrt{2/3}$, since $4 - 3 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = 2$.
So the proof must use the fact that $x$ is an integer, and step 3 is where it does.

A change of the right side changes the answer.
The equation $x^2 - 3y^2 = 1$ has the integer solution $x = 2$, $y = 1$, and $x^2 - 3y^2 = -2$ has the solution $x = 1$, $y = 1$.
For these right sides the test modulo $3$ finds no obstruction, because $x^2 \equiv 1$ is a possible remainder of a square.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Divide the square numbers $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36$ by $3$, and you get the remainders $1, 1, 0, 1, 1, 0$.
A remainder of $2$ never occurs.
If $x^2 - 3y^2$ were $2$, then $x^2$ would be $2$ more than a multiple of $3$, so it would leave remainder $2$.
That cannot happen, so the equation has no solution in whole numbers, and no search is needed.

### L2 human

The same method handles $x^2 + y^2 = 4z + 3$ in integers $x$, $y$ and $z$.
A square leaves remainder $0$ or $1$ on division by $4$, because
$$(2k)^2 = 4k^2, \qquad (2k + 1)^2 = 4(k^2 + k) + 1.$$
So $x^2 + y^2$ leaves remainder $0$, $1$ or $2$, while $4z + 3$ leaves remainder $3$, and the equation has no integer solution.
The typical use is to reduce both sides modulo a small number and compare the possible remainders.
Watch out: the modulus must be chosen with care.
Modulo $2$, the equation $x^2 - 3y^2 = 2$ becomes $x^2 + y^2 \equiv 0$, which $x = y = 1$ satisfies, so that test proves nothing.
