## Statement

Let $\vdash$ be an [[algebraizable logic|XADWEW9TNP]] with defining equations $E(x)$, equivalence formulas $\Delta(x, y)$ and an equivalent algebraic semantics $K$ that is a [[quasivariety|WRXEKT4NQS]].
A *$K$-congruence* of $\mathrm{Fm}$ is a [[congruence|DCSXXT4SER]] $\theta$ with $\mathrm{Fm}/\theta \in K$.

**Theorem.**

1. For each theory $T$ of $\vdash$, the relation $\theta_T = \set{(\varphi, \psi) : \Delta(\varphi, \psi) \subseteq T}$ is a $K$-congruence. The map $T \mapsto \theta_T$ is a lattice isomorphism from the theories of $\vdash$ onto the $K$-congruences of $\mathrm{Fm}$, with inverse $\theta \mapsto \set{\varphi : E(\varphi) \subseteq \theta}$.
2. Any two equivalent algebraic semantics of $\vdash$ generate the same quasivariety.
3. Suppose $\vdash$ is finitary and $E$ is finite. For a subquasivariety $K'$ of $K$, let $\Gamma \vdash_{K'} \varphi$ iff $E[\Gamma] \models_{K'} E(\varphi)$. Then $K' \mapsto {\vdash_{K'}}$ is a dual lattice isomorphism from the subquasivarieties of $K$ onto the finitary extensions of $\vdash$.
4. Under the map of part 3, the axiomatic extensions of $\vdash$ correspond to the subclasses of $K$ defined relative to $K$ by equations.

All four parts are due to Blok and Pigozzi.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

1. In part 1, $K$ must be closed under subalgebras and products: the quotient $\mathrm{Fm}/\theta_T$ is built as a subalgebra of a product of members of $K$.
2. In part 3, finitarity and finiteness of $E$ are needed to turn each rule $\Gamma \vdash' \varphi$ of an extension into finitely many quasi-identities.
3. Part 2 is about the generated quasivariety: $\models_K$ only depends on the quasivariety generated by $K$, so any class that generates it is again an equivalent algebraic semantics.

### Consequences

1. Classical logic has only two finitary extensions, itself and the inconsistent logic. Every nontrivial Boolean algebra contains the two-element one, which generates all Boolean algebras as a quasivariety, so the only proper subquasivariety is the trivial one.
2. The axiomatic extensions of intuitionistic logic, the intermediate logics, correspond to the varieties of Heyting algebras.
3. The axiomatic extensions of Łukasiewicz logic correspond to the varieties of MV-algebras.

### Converse

Blok and Pigozzi also proved a converse of part 1: a logic is algebraizable iff its Leibniz operator is injective and order-preserving on theories and commutes with inverse substitutions; see [[the Leibniz hierarchy|6JT3CMJT8F]].

### Generalizations

1. The theorems extend to deductive systems whose statements are $k$-tuples of formulas; equational logic is such a system, with pairs.
2. For any logic, the Leibniz operator maps theories to congruences, and the levels of the Leibniz hierarchy measure how close it comes to the isomorphism of part 1.

## History

Blok and Pigozzi proved these theorems in their memoir "Algebraizable logics" of 1989.
The correspondence between intermediate logics and varieties of Heyting algebras, and between normal modal logics and varieties of modal algebras, was known before and became special cases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For an algebraizable logic, every theory is the same as a way to identify formulas, and every way to strengthen the logic is the same as a way to restrict its algebras.
Stronger logics correspond to smaller classes of algebras.

### L2 human

1. Intuitionistic logic $\vdash$ is algebraizable with $K$ the Heyting algebras.
2. Add the axiom $p \lor \lnot p$; the result is classical logic.
3. On the algebraic side, the corresponding class is the Heyting algebras satisfying $x \vee \lnot x \approx 1$, which are the Boolean algebras.
4. So classical logic, as an axiomatic extension of intuitionistic logic, corresponds to the subvariety of Boolean algebras.

The typical use: to show that a logic has continuum many extensions, find continuum many subquasivarieties of its algebras.
The trap: the correspondence reverses the order: more theorems means fewer algebras.

### L3 human

Part 1 says that the Leibniz operator on theories coincides with $T \mapsto \theta_T$ and is a lattice isomorphism; in other words, the logic and the equational consequence of $K$ are equivalent deductive systems.
Parts 3 and 4 reduce the study of extensions to the lattice of subquasivarieties of $K$, a question of universal algebra.
