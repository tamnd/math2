## Example

Consider the [[paraxial wave equation|53DCTAEM5M]] in one transverse dimension, $\partial_x^2 A + 2ik \, \partial_z A = 0$.
Fix a length $x_0 > 0$ and use the variables $s = x / x_0$ and $\xi = z / (k x_0^2)$.
Then the equation becomes $i \, \partial_\xi \phi + \frac12 \partial_s^2 \phi = 0$ for $\phi(s, \xi) = A(x, z)$.
The *Airy beam* is the solution
$$\phi(s, \xi) = \operatorname{Ai}\!\left(s - \frac{\xi^2}{4}\right) \exp\!\left(i\left(\frac{s\xi}{2} - \frac{\xi^3}{12}\right)\right),$$
where $\operatorname{Ai}$ is the [[Airy function|1N5D4PN92W]].

Check: put $u = s - \xi^2/4$ and $\theta = s\xi/2 - \xi^3/12$, so $\phi = \operatorname{Ai}(u) e^{i\theta}$.
Then $\frac12 \partial_s^2 \phi = \bigl(\frac12 \operatorname{Ai}'' + \frac{i\xi}{2} \operatorname{Ai}' - \frac{\xi^2}{8} \operatorname{Ai}\bigr) e^{i\theta}$.
Also $i \, \partial_\xi \phi = \bigl(-\frac{i\xi}{2} \operatorname{Ai}' - (\frac{s}{2} - \frac{\xi^2}{4}) \operatorname{Ai}\bigr) e^{i\theta}$.
The sum is $\bigl(\frac12 \operatorname{Ai}''(u) - \frac{u}{2}\operatorname{Ai}(u)\bigr) e^{i\theta}$, which is $0$ by the Airy equation $\operatorname{Ai}'' = u \operatorname{Ai}$.

The intensity $\abs{\phi}^2 = \operatorname{Ai}(s - \xi^2/4)^2$ has the same shape for every $\xi$, shifted sideways by $\xi^2/4$.
So the beam does not spread, and its main lobe follows the parabola $s = \xi^2/4$: the beam bends while it travels in a straight medium.
Its total power is infinite, because $\operatorname{Ai}(u)^2$ decays only like $\abs{u}^{-1/2}$ as $u \to -\infty$.
In practice one uses the truncated profile $\operatorname{Ai}(s) e^{as}$ with a small $a > 0$, which has finite power and keeps these features over a finite distance.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light in empty space travels in straight lines, but the bright part of an Airy beam follows a curve.
Its profile has one main bright lobe and a tail of weaker lobes on one side.
As the beam moves forward, the whole pattern slides sideways along a parabola without changing shape.
No energy moves along a curved path; the waves from the tail keep rebuilding the main lobe at a new place.
Like the Bessel beam, a perfect Airy beam would need infinite energy, so real ones bend only over a limited distance.
