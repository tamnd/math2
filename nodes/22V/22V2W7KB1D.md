## Summary

The Knudsen number is the ratio of the mean free path of the molecules of a gas to a length scale of the flow.
It tells whether a gas can be treated as a continuum, described by the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]], or must be described by [[kinetic theory|HHTG8JVGKQ]].
Small Knudsen numbers mean frequent collisions and continuum behaviour, and large ones mean rarefied gas, as in the upper atmosphere or in microdevices.
It is named after Martin Knudsen.
See the [[definition|9XCA2BB7KW]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A molecule of air travels a short distance, the mean free path, before it hits another molecule.
At sea level this distance is less than a tenth of a micrometre, tiny compared with a car or a wing.
Then the air behaves like a smooth fluid.
In a channel a few hundred nanometres wide, or high in the atmosphere where the air is thin, molecules hit the walls about as often as each other.
The Knudsen number, the ratio of these two lengths, tells which picture applies.
