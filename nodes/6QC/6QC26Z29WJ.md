## Summary

A threshold model of collective behavior gives each individual a threshold: the share of others who must already act before the individual joins in.
Mark Granovetter introduced it in 1978 to study riots, strikes and the spread of innovations.
The aggregate outcome depends on the whole distribution of thresholds, so two groups with nearly the same average preferences can behave in completely different ways.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a crowd where one person will throw a stone no matter what, another will join once one person has, a third once two have, and so on.
Then the first person starts, the second follows, and a chain reaction pulls in everyone.
Now remove the person with threshold one and replace them by someone with threshold two.
The first person still throws a stone, but nobody else joins, and the cascade stops at once.
A tiny change in individuals makes a huge change in the group.
