## Summary

A phase diagram is a map that shows which phase of a substance is stable for each value of the control variables.
The usual control variables are temperature and pressure.
Its regions are the phases, and its curves are where two phases coexist and a [[phase transition|6WJ0NADBT4]] happens.
Special points are the [[triple point|QENKZP7W4Y]], where three phases coexist, and the critical point, where a coexistence curve ends.
The slopes of the curves are given by the [[Clausius–Clapeyron relation|4XEQ5FA5P9]], and the [[Gibbs phase rule|5AYW64A7J3]] limits how many phases can meet.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a map with temperature along one side and pressure along the other.
The map is split into countries labelled solid, liquid and gas.
If you know the temperature and the pressure, the map tells you whether the substance is ice, water or steam.
The borders between the countries are where melting, boiling or sublimation take place.
For water, the border between ice and liquid leans slightly to the left, which is why pressure can melt ice.
