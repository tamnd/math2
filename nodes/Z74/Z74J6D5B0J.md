## Statement

Let $h^*$ be a [[generalized cohomology theory|H28PSZR7XG]] and let $X$ be a finite [[CW complex|5W95QFHXPR]].
Then there is a [[spectral sequence|GG8AYV847V]] $(E_r^{p,q}, d_r)_{r \ge 2}$ with differentials $d_r \colon E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}$ and
$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X; h^q(\text{point})),$$
the ordinary [[cohomology|6N7BC70CX0]] of $X$ with coefficients in the group $h^q(\text{point})$.
It converges to $h^{p+q}(X)$: for each $n$, the group $h^n(X)$ has a finite filtration whose successive quotients are the groups $E_\infty^{p,q}$ with $p + q = n$.

## Remarks

The spectral sequence comes from the filtration of $X$ by its skeleta $X^{(p)}$.
The $E_1$ term is $h^{p+q}(X^{(p)}, X^{(p-1)})$, which is a sum of copies of $h^q(\text{point})$, one for each $p$-cell, and $d_1$ is the cellular coboundary.

For [[complex K-theory|9E4JE4902G]], $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X; \Z)$ for $q$ even and $0$ for $q$ odd.
So $d_2 = 0$, and the first possible nonzero differential $d_3 \colon H^p(X; \Z) \to H^{p+3}(X; \Z)$ is the integral lift of the Steenrod square $Sq^3$.
All differentials are torsion-valued, so the spectral sequence collapses after tensoring with $\Q$, which recovers the Chern character isomorphism.

Example: for $X = S^n$, the $E_2$ page has nonzero columns only at $p = 0$ and $p = n$, and for $K$-theory no differential can be nonzero when $n$ is even.
This gives $\tilde{K}(S^n) \cong H^n(S^n; \Z) = \Z$ for even $n$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A generalized cohomology theory, such as K-theory, is hard to compute directly.
The spectral sequence starts from ordinary cohomology, which is easy to compute, with coefficients in the groups that the theory gives to a point.
Then it corrects this first guess in a series of steps, and the final answer determines the groups of the theory up to extensions.

### L2 human

The spectral sequence is the main computational tool for K-theory and cobordism of CW complexes.
When the cohomology of $X$ is free and concentrated in even degrees, all differentials vanish for K-theory.
Then $K^0(X)$ is free of rank equal to the sum of the Betti numbers; this applies to complex projective spaces and Grassmannians.
The same method, applied to a Serre fibration and a generalized cohomology theory, gives a version of the Serre spectral sequence for that theory.
