## Summary

The absolute magnitude of a star or galaxy is the [[apparent magnitude|R8BRMEQ4DN]] it would have if it were placed at a distance of $10$ parsecs.
It measures how luminous the object really is, independently of its distance.
Comparing absolute and apparent magnitudes gives distances.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bright-looking star may be a weak lamp nearby or a powerful one far away.
To compare the lamps themselves, imagine moving every star to the same distance, $10$ parsecs, about $32.6$ light years.
The magnitude it would then show is its absolute magnitude.
The Sun, so bright in our sky, would be a faint star of magnitude about $4.8$ at that distance.
