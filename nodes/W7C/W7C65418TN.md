## Statement

Let $a, k, L > 0$ and $T_0 > 0$.
Consider the one-phase [[Stefan problem|3B0HB3DHW1]] with diffusivity $a$, conductivity $k$, latent heat $L$ per unit volume and constant boundary temperature $g = T_0$.
Put $\mathrm{Ste} = k T_0 / (a L)$, the [[Stefan number|FGZDVJPJ5M]].

1. The equation $\lambda \, e^{\lambda^2} \operatorname{erf}(\lambda) = \mathrm{Ste} / \sqrt{\pi}$ has exactly one root $\lambda > 0$.
2. With this $\lambda$, the functions
$$s(t) = 2 \lambda \sqrt{a t}, \qquad u(x, t) = T_0 \left(1 - \frac{\operatorname{erf}\left(x / (2 \sqrt{a t})\right)}{\operatorname{erf}(\lambda)}\right)$$
solve the problem for $t > 0$, where $\operatorname{erf}$ is the [[error function|7Y56QCZSZJ]].

## Remarks

The left side of the equation in part 1 is continuous and strictly increasing on $(0, \infty)$, from $0$ to $\infty$, so the root exists and is unique.
For part 2, $\operatorname{erf}(x / (2 \sqrt{a t}))$ solves the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]] $u_t = a u_{xx}$, so $u$ does too.
Also $u(0, t) = T_0$ and $u(s(t), t) = 0$, since $x = s(t)$ gives the argument $\lambda$.
The Stefan condition $L s' = -k u_x(s, t)$ becomes $L \lambda \sqrt{a / t} = k T_0 e^{-\lambda^2} / (\operatorname{erf}(\lambda) \sqrt{\pi a t})$, which is the equation in part 1.

The solution is a [[similarity solution|579J5K9K5Z]]: it depends on $x$ and $t$ only through $x / \sqrt{t}$.
For small $\mathrm{Ste}$, $\operatorname{erf}(\lambda) \approx 2 \lambda / \sqrt{\pi}$ gives $\lambda^2 \approx \mathrm{Ste} / 2$, so $s(t) \approx \sqrt{2 \, \mathrm{Ste} \, a t}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A block of ice at its melting point is warmed from one face, held at a fixed warm temperature.
The ice melts, and the water layer grows thicker.
The Neumann solution gives the exact temperature in the water and the exact position of the melting front.
The front moves like the square root of time, so melting slows down as the water layer insulates the ice.

### L2 human

The Stefan problem is nonlinear because the domain moves, so explicit solutions are rare.
The Neumann solution is the main exact one, and numerical methods for phase change are tested against it.
Its key step is the similarity variable $x / \sqrt{t}$, which turns the heat equation into an ordinary differential equation and fixes the form $s(t) = 2 \lambda \sqrt{a t}$.
A two-phase version, in which the solid is below its melting point, is solved in the same way.
