## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a 3-manifold with a Riemannian metric $h_{ij}$ and a symmetric tensor $K_{ij}$, and let $\pi^{ij} = \sqrt{h} \, (K^{ij} - K h^{ij})$ with $K = h^{ij} K_{ij}$, as in the [[ADM formalism|CFAFAPV7HB]].
In units with $16 \pi G = 1$, the *Hamiltonian constraint* of vacuum general relativity is the equation
$$\mathcal{H} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{h}} \Bigl( \pi^{ij} \pi_{ij} - \tfrac12 \pi^2 \Bigr) - \sqrt{h} \, R(h) = 0,$$
where $R(h)$ is the [[scalar curvature|24CP8Q56CV]] of $h$ and $\pi = h_{ij} \pi^{ij}$.
In terms of $K$ it reads
$$R(h) + K^2 - K_{ij} K^{ij} = 0.$$
With matter of energy density $\rho$ measured by the normal observer, and with $G = 1$, the right-hand side becomes $16 \pi \rho$.

## Remarks

The constraint is the normal-normal component of the Einstein equations: $2 G_{ab} n^a n^b = R(h) + K^2 - K_{ij} K^{ij}$ for the unit normal $n$, by the Gauss equation.
It is one of the [[constraint equations|SB7BVCZJ3F]] of the initial value problem; the other three form the momentum constraint.
Example: the spatial Schwarzschild data $h = (1 + m/(2r))^4 \delta$ on $\R^3 \setminus \set{0}$ with $K = 0$ satisfy it.
Indeed, for $h = u^4 \delta$ one has $R(h) = -8 u^{-5} \Delta u$, and $u = 1 + m/(2r)$ is harmonic.
Non-example: the round 3-sphere of radius $a$ with $K = 0$ fails it, since $R(h) = 6/a^2 \ne 0$.
The Hamiltonian constraint and the momentum constraint are [[first class constraints|SV6K39RSA6]] in the sense of Dirac.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Hamiltonian constraint is the condition that the curvature of a slice of spacetime, its bending, and the energy of matter on it balance each other.
