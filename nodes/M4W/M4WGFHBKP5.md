## Summary

A Lie bialgebra is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] that is also a [[Lie coalgebra|J48KPHCC2E]], such that the cobracket is a $1$-cocycle for the adjoint action.
Lie bialgebras were introduced by Drinfeld as the infinitesimal objects of Poisson–Lie groups and as the classical limits of quantum groups.
For a finite-dimensional Lie bialgebra $\mathfrak{g}$, the dual $\mathfrak{g}^*$ is again a Lie bialgebra, and the two combine into the Drinfeld double.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Lie bialgebra carries two operations that point in opposite directions: a bracket that combines two elements into one, and a cobracket that splits one element into pairs.
The two must fit together: the cobracket of a bracket is computed from the cobrackets of the pieces, by a rule like the product rule for derivatives.
This compatibility is what survives of a quantum group when one lets Planck's constant go to zero.
The simplest example is any Lie algebra with the zero cobracket.
