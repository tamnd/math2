## Summary

The Schechter luminosity function is a formula for the number of galaxies per unit volume and per unit luminosity.
It is a power law at low luminosity with an exponential cut-off above a characteristic luminosity $L^*$.
P. Schechter proposed it in 1976, and it fits galaxy counts well with only three parameters.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Faint galaxies are far more common than bright ones.
Counting galaxies by brightness in a large volume gives a curve that rises steadily towards the faint end.
At the bright end the curve falls off very sharply above a typical luminosity, close to that of the Milky Way.
The Schechter formula joins these two behaviours: a power law for the faint galaxies times an exponential decay for the bright ones.
It is one particular model of a luminosity function, the distribution of luminosities in a population.
