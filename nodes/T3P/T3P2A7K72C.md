## Definition

Work in units with $c = \hbar = 1$ and with metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$.
Let $g$ be a spacetime metric with scalar curvature $R$, let $G$ be Newton's constant, and let $V \colon \R \to \R$ be a smooth function, called the potential.
A *quintessence field* is a real scalar field $\phi$ with the following action, which couples it minimally to gravity:
$$S = \int \left[ \frac{R}{16 \pi G} - \frac{1}{2} g^{\mu\nu} \partial_\mu \phi\, \partial_\nu \phi - V(\phi) \right] \sqrt{-\det g}\; d^4x + S_{\mathrm{m}}.$$
Here $S_{\mathrm{m}}$ is the action of the other matter, and it does not depend on $\phi$.
Take a spatially flat [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker|ECJMS4VDTF]] background with scale factor $a(t)$ and Hubble rate $H = \dot a / a$.
A homogeneous field $\phi(t)$ on it has energy density and pressure
$$\rho_\phi = \tfrac{1}{2} \dot\phi^2 + V(\phi), \qquad p_\phi = \tfrac{1}{2} \dot\phi^2 - V(\phi),$$
and it obeys
$$\ddot \phi + 3 H \dot \phi + V'(\phi) = 0.$$
Its [[equation of state parameter|6A915X05CK]] is $w_\phi = p_\phi / \rho_\phi$.
Quintessence is the use of such a field as the dark energy of the universe.

## Remarks

The equation for $\phi$ is the [[Klein–Gordon equation|AYCCDQWCJF]] with a potential and with the friction term $3H\dot\phi$ from the expansion.
If $V \ge 0$ and $\rho_\phi > 0$, then $-1 \le w_\phi \le 1$, with $w_\phi = -1$ exactly when $\dot\phi = 0$.
Example: for a constant potential $V = V_0 > 0$ and a field at rest, $\rho_\phi = V_0$ and $p_\phi = -V_0$, so $w_\phi = -1$ and the field acts as a [[cosmological constant|WVKKDY9V61]] $\Lambda = 8 \pi G V_0$.
Non-example: a field with $V = 0$ has $\rho_\phi = p_\phi = \tfrac{1}{2}\dot\phi^2$, so $w_\phi = 1$; by the [[Friedmann equations|PPHFS2P4G4]] it decelerates the expansion and cannot serve as dark energy, which needs $w < -1/3$.
Common choices of potential are inverse powers $V(\phi) = M^{4 + \alpha} \phi^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha > 0$ and exponentials $V(\phi) = V_0 e^{-\lambda \phi}$, where $M$, $V_0$ and $\lambda$ are positive constants.
