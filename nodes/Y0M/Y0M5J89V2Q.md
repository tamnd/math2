## Problem

Let $n \ge 1$ be the number of goods and let $u \colon \R^n_{\ge 0} \to \R$ be a [[continuous|0X3WGX95FE]] [[utility function|GZZS4MTDG9]].
Let $p \in \R^n$ be a price vector with all entries positive.
Let $\bar{u}$ be a utility level that is reached: $u(\bar{x}) \ge \bar{u}$ for some $\bar{x} \in \R^n_{\ge 0}$.
The problem asks for a bundle $x \in \R^n_{\ge 0}$ with $u(x) \ge \bar{u}$ that minimizes the expenditure $p \cdot x$ among all such bundles.

## Remarks

A solution exists: one may restrict to the set of bundles $x$ with $u(x) \ge \bar{u}$ and $p \cdot x \le p \cdot \bar{x}$, which is closed and bounded, hence [[compact|0TVJZYB6SH]], and nonempty.
The minimal value is the expenditure function $e(p, \bar{u})$, and a minimizer is a Hicksian demand $h(p, \bar{u})$.
If $\bar{u} > u(0)$, every solution $x$ satisfies $u(x) = \bar{u}$.
Indeed $x \ne 0$, and if $u(x) > \bar{u}$, continuity gives $u(tx) > \bar{u}$ for some $t < 1$, while $tx$ costs less than $x$.
The problem is dual to the utility maximization problem: under local nonsatiation, $e(p, v(p, w)) = w$ and $v(p, e(p, \bar{u})) = \bar{u}$, where $v$ is the indirect utility.
Example: for $u(x_1, x_2) = \sqrt{x_1 x_2}$ and $\bar{u} > 0$, the solution is $x_1 = \bar{u}\sqrt{p_2/p_1}$, $x_2 = \bar{u}\sqrt{p_1/p_2}$, with $e(p, \bar{u}) = 2\bar{u}\sqrt{p_1 p_2}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Instead of asking what is the best basket for a given budget, one asks what is the cheapest basket that makes the consumer a given amount happy.
The answer shows how purchases change with prices when the consumer is kept equally well off.
It is the mirror image of utility maximization, and the two problems together give the main identities of demand theory.
