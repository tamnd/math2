## Definition

Consider a straight fin of length $L$ with constant cross-section area $A_c$ and perimeter $P$, attached at $x = 0$ to a base at temperature $T_b$.
The fin has [[thermal conductivity|XBQBT9E7JT]] $k$ and lies in a fluid at temperature $T_\infty$ with [[heat transfer coefficient|J821AD6YTE]] $h$.
Assume [[steady state|RFWGC57K4H]], temperature $T(x)$ uniform over each cross-section, and constant $k$ and $h$.
Put $\theta(x) = T(x) - T_\infty$ and $m = \sqrt{h P / (k A_c)}$.
The *fin equation* is
$$\theta''(x) - m^2 \theta(x) = 0, \qquad 0 < x < L, \qquad \theta(0) = T_b - T_\infty,$$
completed by a condition at the tip $x = L$.

## Remarks

The equation is the energy balance of a thin slice: conduction by [[Fourier's law|9YJZ1PP2Y8]] gives $k A_c \theta''$, and the loss from the sides is $h P \theta$.

Example: with an insulated tip, $\theta'(L) = 0$, the solution is $\theta(x) = \theta(0) \cosh(m (L - x)) / \cosh(m L)$, using [[hyperbolic functions|01PPDSDP2N]].
Check: $\theta'' = m^2 \theta$, and $\theta'(L) = -m \theta(0) \sinh(0) / \cosh(m L) = 0$.
The heat removed is $Q = -k A_c \theta'(0) = \sqrt{h P k A_c} \, \theta(0) \tanh(m L)$.

Non-example: a fin whose cross-section shrinks towards the tip does not satisfy this equation, since $A_c$ and $P$ depend on $x$.
Its equation has variable coefficients.

For a very long fin, $\theta(x) = \theta(0) e^{-m x}$ and $Q = \sqrt{h P k A_c} \, \theta(0)$.
The fin efficiency with insulated tip is $\eta = \tanh(m L) / (m L)$.
