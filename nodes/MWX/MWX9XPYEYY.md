## Summary

A scalar–tensor theory is a theory of gravity in which a scalar field acts on gravity together with the spacetime metric.
The scalar field typically sets the effective strength of the gravitational coupling, which may then vary in space and time.
Scalar–tensor theories are the most studied [[alternatives to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]].
They appear in models of inflation and dark energy and in low-energy limits of string theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In general relativity gravity is described by one object, the metric, which tells how to measure distances and times.
A scalar–tensor theory adds a second object: a single number at each point of spacetime, a scalar field.
This field can make the gravitational constant effectively change from place to place or over cosmic time.
Brans–Dicke theory is the simplest example.
Experiments in the Solar System put tight limits on how strongly such a field may act.
