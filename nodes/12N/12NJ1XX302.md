## Definition

Let $U \subseteq \R^n$ be open and let $g_1, \dots, g_m, h_1, \dots, h_p \colon U \to \R$ be [[continuously differentiable|0VW90BBSJ4]].
Let $S = \set{x \in U : g_i(x) \le 0 \text{ for all } i,\ h_j(x) = 0 \text{ for all } j}$, let $x^* \in S$, and let $I = \set{i : g_i(x^*) = 0}$ be the active set.
A *constraint qualification* at $x^*$ is a condition on the $g_i$, $h_j$ and $x^*$ with the following property.
Let $f \colon U \to \R$ be any function that is differentiable at $x^*$ and has a local minimum on $S$ at $x^*$.
Then there are $\mu \in [0, \infty)^m$ and $\lambda \in \R^p$ with $\mu_i = 0$ for $i \notin I$ and
$$\nabla f(x^*) + \sum_{i \in I} \mu_i \nabla g_i(x^*) + \sum_{j=1}^p \lambda_j \nabla h_j(x^*) = 0.$$
The standard constraint qualifications are:

1. *LICQ*: the [[gradients|0ABK9V9Y64]] $\nabla g_i(x^*)$ for $i \in I$ and $\nabla h_j(x^*)$ for all $j$ are linearly independent.
2. *MFCQ* (Mangasarian–Fromovitz): the $\nabla h_j(x^*)$ are linearly independent, and some $d \in \R^n$ has $\nabla h_j(x^*) \cdot d = 0$ for all $j$ and $\nabla g_i(x^*) \cdot d < 0$ for all $i \in I$.
3. *Linearity*: all $g_i$ and $h_j$ are affine.
4. *Slater*: $U$ is convex, all $g_i$ are [[convex|07Q7CNM5WF]], all $h_j$ are affine, and some $\tilde x \in U$ has $g_i(\tilde x) < 0$ for all $i$ and $h_j(\tilde x) = 0$ for all $j$.

## Remarks

The displayed system is the set of [[Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions|HDWEPH4YG4]] at $x^*$.
LICQ implies MFCQ, and under LICQ the multipliers are unique.
Under MFCQ the set of multipliers is nonempty and bounded.
The Abadie and Guignard conditions are weaker constraint qualifications, stated with the tangent cone of $S$.

Example: $g_1(x) = -x_1$ and $g_2(x) = -x_2$ on $\R^2$ at $x^* = 0$.
Both are active with gradients $(-1, 0)$ and $(0, -1)$, which are linearly independent, so LICQ holds.

Non-example: $g_1(x) = x^2$ on $\R$ at $x^* = 0$.
The gradient $\nabla g_1(0) = 0$ is not linearly independent, and no $d$ gives $\nabla g_1(0) \cdot d < 0$, so LICQ and MFCQ fail.
Slater fails too, since $x^2 < 0$ has no solution.
Indeed $f(x) = x$ has its minimum on $S = \set{0}$ at $0$, but $1 + \mu \cdot 0 = 0$ has no solution.
