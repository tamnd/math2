## Summary

The Buckley–Leverett equation models the displacement of one fluid by another in a porous rock, such as oil pushed out by injected water.
It is a scalar [[hyperbolic conservation law|XN081NCPF5]] for the water saturation.
Its flux, the fractional flow function, is usually S-shaped and not convex, so solutions combine a [[shock wave|JSDMGGTG7G]] with a [[rarefaction wave|MFN4Q5SH8H]].
It is a classic model in petroleum engineering and a standard example in the theory of conservation laws.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pump water into one end of a rock filled with oil.
The water does not push the oil out like a piston; it moves through the pores alongside the oil.
Behind a sharp water front the rock holds a mixture whose water content rises slowly over time.
The Buckley–Leverett equation predicts where the front is and how much oil is left behind it.
