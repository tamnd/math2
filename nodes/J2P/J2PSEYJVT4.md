## Summary

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime studies [[quantum fields|W031YNVRE3]] that propagate on a fixed curved [[Lorentzian manifold|BY4QYYS788]], treated as a classical background.
The geometry acts on the fields, but the fields do not change the geometry.
Without the symmetries of Minkowski space there is in general no preferred vacuum state and no unique notion of particle.
This framework predicts particle creation by expanding universes, the [[Unruh effect|HM260RP02P]] and Hawking radiation from black holes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ordinary quantum field theory lives in flat spacetime, where all observers moving at constant velocity agree on what the empty state, the vacuum, looks like.
In curved spacetime, for example near a black hole or in an expanding universe, this agreement breaks down.
One keeps gravity classical, as a given curved background, and puts quantum fields on top of it.
The typical example is a free scalar field obeying the [[Klein–Gordon equation|AYCCDQWCJF]] written with the curved metric.
What one observer calls empty space can then contain particles for another observer, and this is how a black hole can radiate.
