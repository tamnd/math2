## Definition

Let $(M, g)$ be a [[Lorentzian manifold|BY4QYYS788]] of dimension $D \ge 2$, with [[Riemann curvature tensor|JA29ETCM02]] $R^{ab}{}_{cd}$.
Let $\delta^{a_1 \cdots a_n}_{b_1 \cdots b_n} = \det\left[\delta^{a_i}_{b_j}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}$ be the generalized Kronecker delta.
For each integer $k \ge 0$, the *$k$-th Lovelock density* is
$$\mathcal{L}_k = \frac{1}{2^k}\, \delta^{a_1 b_1 \cdots a_k b_k}_{c_1 d_1 \cdots c_k d_k} \, R^{c_1 d_1}{}_{a_1 b_1} \cdots R^{c_k d_k}{}_{a_k b_k},$$
with $\mathcal{L}_0 = 1$.
*Lovelock gravity* is the [[alternative to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]] with action
$$S = \int \sum_{k=0}^{t} \alpha_k \mathcal{L}_k \sqrt{-g}\, d^D x + S_m,$$
where $t$ is the largest integer with $2t < D$ and the $\alpha_k$ are real constants.

## Remarks

The first terms are $\mathcal{L}_1 = R$, the [[scalar curvature|ZHX2VYQDZ3]], and $\mathcal{L}_2 = R^2 - 4R_{ab}R^{ab} + R_{abcd}R^{abcd}$, the Gauss–Bonnet term.
Check for $\mathcal{L}_1$: $\frac12 \delta^{ab}_{cd} R^{cd}{}_{ab} = \frac12 (R^{ab}{}_{ab} - R^{ba}{}_{ab}) = R$.
The field equations obtained by varying $g$ contain no derivatives of $g$ of order higher than two.
For $2k = D$ the term $\mathcal{L}_k\sqrt{-g}$ is locally a total divergence.
It is the Euler density, whose integral over a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension $2k$ is a constant multiple of its [[Euler characteristic|RNDDWDAFJ5]].
For $2k > D$ the term vanishes identically, which is why the sum stops at $t$.
Example: for $D = 4$, $t = 1$, and the action is $\int (\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 R)\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$, which is general relativity with a cosmological constant, in agreement with [[Lovelock's theorem|DW4CF8A42M]].
Non-example: the action $\int R^2 \sqrt{-g}\,d^Dx$ is not a Lovelock action, and its field equations are of fourth order.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Lovelock action adds up the curvature, then special products of two curvatures, then of three, and so on.
The special combinations are the ones whose equations stay of second order, like Einstein's.
