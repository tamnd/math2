## Definition

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a vector space over $F$, and let $T \colon V \to V$ be an endomorphism.
A polynomial $p \in F[x]$ *annihilates* $T$ when $p(T) = 0$, where $p(T)$ is the [[value of p at T|0BNEXSKNRV]].
Such a polynomial is an *annihilating polynomial* of $T$.
The set of all annihilating polynomials of $T$ is written $I_T$.
For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$, $p$ annihilates $A$ when $p(A) = 0$.

## Notation

Mathlib has no separate name for $I_T$.
It is the kernel `RingHom.ker (Polynomial.aeval T)`, and $p \in I_T$ is written `Polynomial.aeval T p = 0`.

## Remarks

The zero polynomial annihilates every $T$.
The theorem on [[annihilating polynomials|~540651d7]] shows that $I_T$ is an ideal, and that it is nonzero when $V$ is finite-dimensional.
For a [[projection|033DA1WE8Y]] $P$, the polynomial $x^2 - x$ annihilates $P$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An annihilating polynomial is a polynomial that becomes the zero map when we put $T$ into it.
For example, a reflection $R$ satisfies $R^2 = I$, so $x^2 - 1$ annihilates it.

### L2 human

We check that $x^2 - 1$ annihilates $R = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. $R^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 0 \end{pmatrix} = I$.
2. So $R^2 - I = 0$.

A trap: $x - 1$ does not annihilate $R$, because $R \ne I$, although $1$ is an eigenvalue of $R$.
