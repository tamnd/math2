## Definition

Consider a self-gravitating ball of gas in [[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]] that radiates energy from its surface.
Let $\Omega < 0$ be its gravitational potential energy, $U$ its thermal energy and $L$ its luminosity.
The *Kelvin–Helmholtz mechanism* is the supply of the luminosity by contraction alone, without nuclear or other energy sources.
The total energy $E = U + \Omega$ decreases at the rate $dE/dt = -L$, and the ball contracts so that $\Omega$ decreases.

## Remarks

For a monatomic ideal gas the [[virial theorem|6T0BTVNMRF]] gives $2U + \Omega = 0$, so $E = \Omega / 2 = -U$.
Hence $d\Omega/dt = -2L$ and $dU/dt = L$: half of the released gravitational energy is radiated and half heats the gas.
Example: for a uniform ball of mass $M$ and radius $R$, $\Omega = -\frac{3}{5} G M^2 / R$, with $G$ the gravitational constant, so radiating $L$ needs $dR/dt = -\frac{10}{3} L R^2 / (G M^2)$, a contraction.
Non-example: a main-sequence star that burns hydrogen at the rate that matches its luminosity keeps a nearly constant radius, so its luminosity is not supplied by the Kelvin–Helmholtz mechanism.
The time over which the mechanism can supply the present luminosity is the [[Kelvin–Helmholtz timescale|KR6BPP5WHY]].
