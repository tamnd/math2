## Definition

Work in [[linearized gravity|AH62KSWA6Y]] on $\R^4$ with coordinates $(ct, x)$, $x \in \R^3$, in the harmonic gauge.
Let the source be stationary matter with mass density $\rho(x)$ and velocity field $u(x)$ with $\abs{u} \ll c$, and neglect its stresses.
Define the gravitoelectric potential $\Phi$ and the gravitomagnetic vector potential $A$ as the decaying solutions of
$$\nabla^2 \Phi = 4\pi G \rho, \qquad \nabla^2 A = \frac{4\pi G}{c} \rho u.$$
Then the metric is, to first order,
$$ds^2 = -\Big(1 + \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\Big) c^2 dt^2 + \frac{8}{c} A \cdot dx \, dt + \Big(1 - \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\Big) \abs{dx}^2.$$
The *gravitoelectric field* is $E_g = -\nabla \Phi$ and the *gravitomagnetic field* is $B_g = \nabla \times A$.
*Gravitoelectromagnetism* is the description of weak stationary gravity by the pair $(E_g, B_g)$.

## Remarks

In this normalization the fields satisfy the Maxwell-like equations
$$\nabla \cdot E_g = -4\pi G \rho, \quad \nabla \times E_g = 0, \quad \nabla \cdot B_g = 0, \quad \nabla \times B_g = -\frac{4\pi G}{c} \rho u,$$
where the last one uses the gauge condition $\nabla \cdot A = 0$.
A test particle with velocity $v$ moving along a [[geodesic|RNRTS8WWNM]] has, to first order in $v/c$,
$$\frac{d^2 x}{dt^2} = E_g + \frac{4}{c}\, v \times B_g.$$
The factor $4$ and the minus signs differ from electromagnetism ([[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]]); other authors rescale $A$ and $B_g$, so the numerical factors in the literature vary.

Example: a mass $M$ at rest at the origin gives $\Phi = -GM/r$, $E_g = -GM x/r^3$, and $A = 0$, so $B_g = 0$; this is Newtonian gravity.

Non-example: the strong field near a neutron star is outside the definition, because the linear approximation fails.
So is a source moving close to the speed of light, because the slow-motion approximation fails.
