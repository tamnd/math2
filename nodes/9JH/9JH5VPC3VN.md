## Definition

*M-theory* is the name of the conjectured quantum theory, in eleven spacetime dimensions, characterized by the following properties.

1. Its low-energy limit is [[eleven-dimensional supergravity|CRHHG45BVB]].
2. Compactified on a circle of radius $R$, it is equivalent to type IIA [[superstring theory|N993FCEJY2]] with string coupling $g_s = R / \ell_s$, where $\ell_s$ is the string length.
3. Compactified on the interval $S^1 / \Z_2$, it is equivalent to the $E_8 \times E_8$ heterotic string theory.
4. It contains [[branes|DJAATX710N]] of spatial dimension $2$ and $5$, the M2-brane and the M5-brane.
They couple electrically and magnetically to the three-form field of eleven-dimensional supergravity.

These properties do not determine a mathematical definition: no complete formulation of M-theory is known.

## Remarks

Example of the circle limit at work: as $g_s \to \infty$ in type IIA string theory, property 2 gives $R \to \infty$, so the strongly coupled type IIA theory becomes eleven-dimensional.
Through further dualities, the other superstring theories are also limits of M-theory, so it unifies all five.
Non-example: eleven-dimensional supergravity alone is not M-theory; it is only the low-energy limit and is not a consistent quantum theory by itself.
The letter M was left without a fixed meaning by Witten.
The BFSS matrix model is a proposal for a nonperturbative formulation in a special frame.
