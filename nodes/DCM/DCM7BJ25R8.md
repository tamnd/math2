## Summary

A closed timelike curve is a closed loop in a spacetime along which an observer always moves into the future, slower than light.
Yet the observer returns to the starting event.
Such curves are allowed by some solutions of the Einstein equations, such as Gödel's rotating universe and the inner region of the Kerr black hole.
Their presence breaks ordinary causality, so their absence is the weakest of the causality conditions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An observer's history in spacetime is a curve, their [[world line|Z788G6MJ9E]].
Normally this curve goes forever forward in time and never comes back.
A closed timelike curve is a world line that loops back to its own past, so it describes time travel.
A simple example: take flat spacetime and declare that time $t$ and time $t + 1$ are the same moment, so time is a circle.
Then an observer sitting still returns to the same event after one unit of time.
