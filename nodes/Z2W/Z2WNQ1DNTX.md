## Summary

This topic is a part of [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].
It covers the written conventions of mathematics, the conflicts between them, and the informal words that mathematicians use in proofs and talks.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Mathematical notation|QKEX4PD029]]: the symbols, the layout rules and the letter conventions.
2. [[Conflicting conventions|CSFEP56A7D]]: the symbols with two meanings, and the choice of this site for each.
3. [[Abuse of notation|JV68YRB5YZ]]: the standard shortcuts and the check that makes them safe.
4. [[Equality, definition and equivalence signs|BCGR3NPPXC]]: the signs $=$, $:=$, $\equiv$, $\approx$, $\cong$, $\sim$ and the one-way $=$ of big O.
5. [[Glossary of mathematical symbols|2PTCAGCWZE]]: a reference list of the common symbols.
6. [[Mathematical abbreviations|4ZNYMYRRY3]]: the short forms of words in proofs.
7. [[Mathematical jargon|4M1FGQ0HNZ]]: the informal words of mathematical English.
8. [[Trivial, obvious and clearly|Y0HXCJRB9B]]: what these words claim, and when they hide a gap.
9. [[Canonical and natural|F8WNADZFMM]]: maps that need no choice.
10. [[Almost all, generic and in general|N1XSAXCQY3]]: statements that hold outside a small set.
11. [[Sufficiently large, eventually and frequently|FEQ7WATMHF]]: statements about all large values or about infinitely many values.
12. [[Stronger and weaker statements|KCK4AQ469Q]]: how to compare two results by implication.
13. [[Sharp and best possible results|KWQV0G0VJ3]]: bounds that cannot be improved.
14. [[Elementary proof|84SZZBACP3]]: proofs that avoid a given set of methods.
15. [[Pathological objects|WMBZPYPN2D]]: counterexamples that break a natural guess.
16. [[Elegant and deep|STHWD1K9AN]]: words for the value of a proof or a theorem.
17. [[Q.E.D. and end of proof marks|ET9J6R9T5C]]: the signs that close a proof.

### Prerequisites

This topic assumes the basic language of [[sets|0BSJ7RQ9BW]] and [[functions|0EPAT51DXQ]].
It also assumes the topic [[the language of mathematical statements|WMY13MXY3W]], which treats quantifiers, conditionals and negation.
The proofs that use this notation are treated in the topic [[proof methods|G9JFJGY4TQ]].
