## Summary

A fictitious force, or inertial force, is an apparent force in a frame of reference that is not [[inertial|Q05G0NZDNB]].
It must be added to the real forces to make [[Newton's second law|KNBP19FG6K]] hold in that frame.
It is proportional to the mass of the body, and no other body exerts it.
In a frame moving without rotation it is $-m$ times the acceleration of the frame.
In a [[rotating frame|HNXK6CJ5B2]] it consists of the Coriolis, centrifugal and Euler forces.
Fictitious forces make computations in Earth-bound and other natural frames convenient.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a bus brakes, the passengers lurch forward as if pushed.
Nobody pushes them: they keep moving while the bus slows down under them.
Seen from inside the bus, it looks like a force pushing forward, and calling it a force lets one use Newton's laws inside the bus.
Such pushes, which come only from the motion of the frame, are fictitious forces.
