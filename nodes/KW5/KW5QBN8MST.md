## Definition

Let $R$ be a [[ring|0B1261B5ED]] with identity.
Let $GL(R) = \bigcup_{n \ge 1} GL_n(R)$, where $GL_n(R)$ is the [[general linear group|0FCHE902D7]] and $g \mapsto \operatorname{diag}(g, 1)$ embeds $GL_n(R)$ in $GL_{n+1}(R)$.
Let $E(R) \subseteq GL(R)$ be the subgroup generated by the elementary matrices $e_{ij}(r)$, $i \ne j$, $r \in R$.
The matrix $e_{ij}(r)$ has $1$ on the diagonal, $r$ in position $(i, j)$ and $0$ elsewhere.
The *lower K-groups* of $R$ are the following three abelian groups.

1. [[$K_0(R)$|Q14JS2MA1C]], the Grothendieck group of finitely generated projective left $R$-modules under direct sum.
2. [[$K_1(R)$|SXVNJY4XFY]] $= GL(R)/E(R)$.
3. [[$K_2(R)$|W7T56A6YF8]] $= \ker(\mathrm{St}(R) \to E(R))$.
Here the [[Steinberg group|590VZSQ83X]] $\mathrm{St}(R)$ has generators $x_{ij}(r)$, $i \ne j$ positive integers, $r \in R$, and the following relations.
They are $x_{ij}(r) x_{ij}(s) = x_{ij}(r + s)$, $[x_{ij}(r), x_{jk}(s)] = x_{ik}(rs)$ for $i \ne k$, and $[x_{ij}(r), x_{kl}(s)] = 1$ for $j \ne k$ and $i \ne l$.
The map sends $x_{ij}(r)$ to $e_{ij}(r)$.

## Remarks

By Whitehead's lemma, $E(R)$ is the commutator subgroup of $GL(R)$, so $K_1(R)$ is abelian.
The group $K_2(R)$ is the center of $\mathrm{St}(R)$, so it is abelian too.

Example: for a field $F$, $K_0(F) \cong \Z$ by the dimension, and the determinant gives $K_1(F) \cong F^\times$, because $E(F)$ is the group of matrices of determinant $1$.
For a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$, $K_2(\mathbb{F}_q) = 0$.

Non-example: $GL_n(R)/E_n(R)$ for a fixed $n$ is not $K_1(R)$ in general, and for $n = 2$ it need not even be a group.
The passage to the limit $GL(R)$ is part of the definition.
