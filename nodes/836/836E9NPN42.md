## Summary

An information inequality is a linear inequality among the joint entropies of a finite family of random variables that holds for every joint law.
Basic examples are the non-negativity of [[mutual information|A83CYGDXFG]] and of [[conditional mutual information|PW6PXZ2WV4]].
The inequalities that follow from these by adding with non-negative coefficients are called Shannon-type.
The [[Zhang–Yeung inequality|Y39WB3892W]] showed in 1998 that, for four or more random variables, there are information inequalities that are not of Shannon type.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Entropies of random variables can not take arbitrary values: they must obey certain rules.
For example, two random variables together never carry more information than the sum of what each carries alone, so $H(X, Y) \le H(X) + H(Y)$.
Rules of this kind are information inequalities.
Most of them follow from a few basic ones, the Shannon inequalities.
For four or more variables there are surprising extra rules that do not follow from the basic ones.
