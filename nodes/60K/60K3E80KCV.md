## Definition

Work in Minkowski space with coordinates $x^0 = ct, x^1, x^2, x^3$, where $c$ is the speed of light, and metric $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$.
Let $E = (E_1, E_2, E_3)$ be the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] and $B = (B_1, B_2, B_3)$ the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]].
The *electromagnetic tensor* is the antisymmetric [[tensor field|WX302T93TW]] $F^{\mu\nu}$, $\mu, \nu \in \set{0, 1, 2, 3}$, given by the [[skew-symmetric matrix|0PBY4E9GQF]]
$$F^{\mu\nu} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -E_1/c & -E_2/c & -E_3/c \\ E_1/c & 0 & -B_3 & B_2 \\ E_2/c & B_3 & 0 & -B_1 \\ E_3/c & -B_2 & B_1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Indices are lowered with $\eta$, so $F_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\alpha} \eta_{\nu\beta} F^{\alpha\beta}$; this gives $F_{0i} = E_i / c$ and $F_{ij} = F^{ij}$.

## Remarks

With the four-current $J^\nu = (c \rho, J)$ and the vacuum permeability $\mu_0$, [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] become
$$\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0 J^\nu, \qquad \partial_\lambda F_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu F_{\nu\lambda} + \partial_\nu F_{\lambda\mu} = 0.$$
For $\nu = 0$ the first equation reads $\frac{1}{c} \nabla \cdot E = \mu_0 c \rho$, which is Gauss's law since $\varepsilon_0 \mu_0 c^2 = 1$.
The second set contains $\nabla \cdot B = 0$ and Faraday's law.

The two Lorentz invariants of the field are $F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu} = 2 \big( \abs{B}^2 - \abs{E}^2 / c^2 \big)$ and $E \cdot B$, up to a constant factor for the second.

Example: a point charge $q$ at rest at the origin has $B = 0$, so only the entries $F^{i0} = -F^{0i} = E_i / c$ with $E = q x / (4 \pi \varepsilon_0 \abs{x}^3)$ are nonzero.

Non-example: a matrix with a nonzero diagonal entry, such as $F^{00} = 1$, is not an electromagnetic tensor, because it is not antisymmetric.

Sign conventions differ between books, depending on the signature of the metric and the order of the indices.
