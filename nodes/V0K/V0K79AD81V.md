## Summary

A polynomial-time reduction turns instances of one decision problem into instances of another, with the same answers, by an algorithm that runs in polynomial time.
It is the [[many-one reduction|JTAH37JTHD]] of computability theory with an added bound on running time.
If a problem $A$ reduces to a problem $B$ and $B$ is in [[P|GVT2NKN37K]], then $A$ is in P as well.
These reductions are the tool for comparing the difficulty of problems in NP, and they define NP-hardness and NP-completeness.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A reduction is a fast translator from one kind of question to another.
Every question of the first kind becomes a question of the second kind with the same yes-or-no answer.
So a fast solver for the second kind gives a fast solver for the first.
For example, the question "does this graph have $k$ vertices that are pairwise not joined?" becomes "does the complement graph have $k$ vertices that are pairwise joined?".
Building the complement graph is quick, and the answers agree.
