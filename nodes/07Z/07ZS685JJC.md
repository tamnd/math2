## Statement

Let $a$ and $b$ be vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$, and let $\times$ be the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]].
Then
$$b \times a = -(a \times b).$$
In particular, $a \times a = 0$ for each $a \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The statement has no hypothesis beyond $a, b \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
It uses the coordinate formula of the cross product, which exists only for vectors with three components.

### Consequences

- With $b = a$ the statement gives $a \times a = -(a \times a)$, so $2(a \times a) = 0$ and $a \times a = 0$.
- The cross product is not [[commutative|099E72KTF8]]: $\mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{j} = \mathbf{k}$ but $\mathbf{j} \times \mathbf{i} = -\mathbf{k}$.
- The vectors $a \times b$ and $b \times a$ have the same length and opposite directions.
- If $a$ is parallel to $b$, say $b = sa$, then $a \times b = s(a \times a) = 0$ by bilinearity.

### Converse

For a bilinear product, the two properties "$b \times a = -(a \times b)$ for all $a, b$" and "$a \times a = 0$ for all $a$" are equivalent over $\mathbb{R}$.
From $a \times a = 0$ for all $a$, expand $(a + b) \times (a + b) = 0$ to get $a \times b + b \times a = 0$.

### Generalizations

Every Lie bracket is anticommutative; the commutator $AB - BA$ of matrices is an example.
The exterior product of vectors satisfies $b \wedge a = -(a \wedge b)$ in every dimension.

## History

Anticommutativity appears in the multiplication rules $ij = k$ and $ji = -k$ that Hamilton found for quaternions in 1843.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If we swap the two factors of a cross product, the result points the other way.
Its length stays the same.
The right-hand rule shows this: swapping the factors turns the hand over.

### L2 human

Worked example with $a = (1, 2, 3)$ and $b = (4, 5, 6)$.

1. $a \times b = (2 \cdot 6 - 3 \cdot 5, \; 3 \cdot 4 - 1 \cdot 6, \; 1 \cdot 5 - 2 \cdot 4) = (-3, 6, -3)$.
2. $b \times a = (5 \cdot 3 - 6 \cdot 2, \; 6 \cdot 1 - 4 \cdot 3, \; 4 \cdot 2 - 5 \cdot 1) = (3, -6, 3)$.
3. So $b \times a = -(a \times b)$.

The typical use is to simplify expressions: $a \times b + b \times a = 0$.
A trap: in a longer product, moving a factor changes the sign each time two neighbours are swapped, and regrouping is not allowed at all.

### L3 human

Anticommutativity says that the cross product is an alternating bilinear map $\mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$.
Alternating bilinear maps on $V$ correspond to linear maps on $\Lambda^2 V$, which is the link to the [[exterior algebra|0MDMZ965VE]] and to the alternating forms of the sub-area on [[bilinear forms|DYAK7YMK7B]].
