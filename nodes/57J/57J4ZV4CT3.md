## Definition

Let $n > 0$, $I_e > 0$ and $R_e > 0$.
Let $b_n > 0$ be the unique solution of $\gamma(2n, b_n) = \frac{1}{2} \Gamma(2n)$, where $\Gamma$ is the [[gamma function|0H5SYBSED9]] and $\gamma$ is the lower [[incomplete gamma function|YB7ZV0HG7K]].
The *Sérsic profile* with index $n$, effective radius $R_e$ and effective surface brightness $I_e$ is the function of the projected radius $R \ge 0$
$$I(R) = I_e \exp\left( -b_n \left[ \left( \frac{R}{R_e} \right)^{1/n} - 1 \right] \right).$$

## Remarks

The total luminosity is
$$L = \int_0^\infty 2\pi R \, I(R) \, dR = 2 \pi n \, I_e R_e^2 \, \frac{e^{b_n}}{b_n^{2n}} \, \Gamma(2n),$$
by the substitution $u = b_n (R/R_e)^{1/n}$.
The same substitution shows that the light inside $R_e$ is $L \, \gamma(2n, b_n)/\Gamma(2n)$, so the choice of $b_n$ makes $R_e$ the radius that contains half of the light, and $I_e = I(R_e)$.
A good approximation is $b_n \approx 2n - 1/3$ for $n$ not too small.
In magnitudes per unit area, the profile is a straight line in $R^{1/n}$.

Example: for $n = 1$ the profile is the exponential disk $I(R) = I_0 e^{-R/h}$ with $I_0 = I_e e^{b_1}$ and $h = R_e / b_1$.
The condition is $\gamma(2, b_1) = 1 - (1 + b_1) e^{-b_1} = \frac{1}{2}$, which gives $b_1 \approx 1.678$.

Non-example: a power law $I(R) = I_e (R/R_e)^{-2}$ is not a Sérsic profile for any $n$, since its integral $\int_0^\infty 2\pi R \, I(R) \, dR$ diverges, while every Sérsic profile has finite total light.
