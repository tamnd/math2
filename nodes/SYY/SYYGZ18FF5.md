## Summary

The heat capacity of a body is the heat it must receive to raise its temperature by one unit.
It depends on the process, so one distinguishes the heat capacity at constant volume $C_V$ and at constant pressure $C_p$.
Heat capacities connect the measurable heat with the internal energy and the enthalpy, and they enter most thermodynamic identities.
Divided by the mass or by the amount of substance, they give the specific and the molar heat capacity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Some things warm up fast and some slowly.
A pot of water needs much more heat than an iron pan of the same mass to get one degree warmer.
The heat capacity measures this: it is the heat needed per degree of temperature rise.
Liquid water needs about $4.2$ joules per gram per kelvin, which is why the sea warms and cools slowly.
If the body may expand while it is heated, part of the heat becomes work, so heating at constant pressure costs more than heating at constant volume.
