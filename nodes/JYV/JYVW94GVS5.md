## Summary

Electrohydrodynamics, or EHD, is the study of fluids moved by electric fields.
Free charges and polarisation in the fluid feel electric forces, and these forces drive or deform the flow.
It is the electric counterpart of [[magnetohydrodynamics|T8JWCBK9M6]]: magnetic effects are neglected and the electric field is electrostatic.
It explains electrospray, the Taylor cone, ionic wind and the deformation of drops in electric fields.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A charged comb can bend a thin stream of water from a tap.
That is electrohydrodynamics: electric forces act on charges in a liquid and make it move.
A strong field can pull a drop into a sharp cone that sprays tiny charged droplets, the principle of electrospray.
Ionised air pushed by a high voltage can even create a breeze, called ionic wind.
