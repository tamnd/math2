## Statement

Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a vector space over $F$ of finite [[dimension|05G2G0P224]] $n$.
Let $v_1, \dots, v_n \in V$ be $n$ vectors.

1. If $v_1, \dots, v_n$ are [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]], then they form a [[basis|0VTQTPQGAV]] of $V$.
2. If $v_1, \dots, v_n$ span $V$, then they form a basis of $V$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The number of vectors must be exactly $n$.
With fewer: $(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0)$ are independent in $\R^3$, but they fail to span, since $(0, 0, 1)$ is not a combination of them.
With more: $(1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1)$ span $\R^2$, but they fail independence.
Finite dimension is needed.
In $F[x]$, the family $x, x^2, x^3, \dots$ is independent and has as many vectors as the basis $1, x, x^2, \dots$.
But it fails to span, because $1$ is missing.

### Consequences

1. For exactly $\dim V$ vectors, independence and spanning are equivalent.
2. Three vectors of $\R^3$ form a basis if and only if the $3 \times 3$ matrix with these columns is invertible; see [[the square matrix criterion|~fab8b33f]].
3. A family $v_1, \dots, v_k$ is independent if and only if its span has dimension $k$.
4. A linear map between spaces of the same finite dimension is injective if and only if it is surjective.

### Converse

Each basis of $V$ has exactly $n$ vectors, by the [[dimension theorem|0WK0Y8RH1M]].
So a family of $n$ vectors is a basis if and only if it is independent, and if and only if it spans.

### Generalizations

Over a commutative ring $R$, $n$ elements that generate $R^n$ form a basis.
But $n$ independent elements need not: $2$ is independent in $\Z$ and does not generate $\Z$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When we have exactly as many vectors as the dimension, we only need to check one of the two basis conditions.
Independence gives spanning for free, and spanning gives independence for free.
This halves the work of checking a basis.

### L2 human

Example: is $(1, 2), (3, 4)$ a basis of $\R^2$?
Check only independence: if $a(1, 2) + b(3, 4) = 0$, then $a + 3b = 0$ and $2a + 4b = 0$.
The first equation gives $a = -3b$, and then $-6b + 4b = -2b = 0$, so $b = 0$ and $a = 0$.
The two vectors are independent, and $\dim \R^2 = 2$, so they form a basis.
