## Summary

Axial precession is the slow turning of the Earth's rotation axis around the pole of the ecliptic, with a period of about $26\,000$ years.
It is a [[precession|CFE9SNEHMP]] caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun on the Earth's equatorial bulge.
It makes the equinoxes drift westward along the ecliptic, so the equatorial coordinates of all stars change slowly.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A spinning top whose axis is tilted does not fall over; its axis slowly sweeps a cone.
The Earth does the same, because the Moon and the Sun tug on the bulge around its equator.
The axis now points near Polaris, but in a few thousand years it will point elsewhere, and other stars will serve as pole star.
This is why star catalogues must say for which year their coordinates hold.
