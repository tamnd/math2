## Summary

The Schwarzschild radius of a mass $M$ is the length $r_s = 2GM/c^2$.
It is the radius of the event horizon of the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]] of mass $M$.
A spherical, non-rotating, uncharged body that is compressed inside its Schwarzschild radius becomes a [[black hole|YHJ0PDNY8T]].
It sets the natural length scale of strong gravity for a given mass.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every mass has a Schwarzschild radius, and it is proportional to the mass.
If all the mass sat inside a ball of that radius, not even light could escape from the ball.
For the Sun the radius is about $3$ kilometres, and for the Earth it is about $9$ millimetres.
Ordinary stars and planets are far larger than their Schwarzschild radius, so they are not black holes.
