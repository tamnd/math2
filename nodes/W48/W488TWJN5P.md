## Summary

The Elliott classification program aims to classify separable, simple, [[nuclear C*-algebras|D2M4VXJ7PA]] up to isomorphism.
The invariant, called the Elliott invariant, is built from K-theory and traces.
It grew out of [[Elliott's classification of AF algebras|Y9KSMY8A1Q]] by their ordered $K_0$-groups.
Counterexamples of Rørdam and Toms show that the invariant does not classify all simple separable nuclear C*-algebras, so a regularity hypothesis is needed.
The classification is now a theorem, due to many authors, for unital, simple, separable C*-algebras of finite nuclear dimension in the UCT class.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The program asks whether a large family of operator algebras can be told apart by a small amount of computable data.
The data are the K-groups $K_0$ and $K_1$, the order on $K_0$ and the class of the unit, the traces, and how traces evaluate on $K_0$.
The first success was for limits of matrix algebras, where $K_0$ alone with its order suffices.
The [[Kirchberg–Phillips classification theorem|XF19H09NC9]] settles the purely infinite case in the UCT class, where the traces play no role.
