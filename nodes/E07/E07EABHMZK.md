## Statement

Let a body of mass $M$ be compressed into a region whose circumference $C$ in every direction satisfies
$$C \le \frac{4\pi G M}{c^2}.$$
Then a [[black hole|YHJ0PDNY8T]] forms, with an [[event horizon|D8NZ32Z3SQ]] around the body.
Conversely, a horizon forms only when such a compression occurs.
The bound is the circumference $2\pi r_s$, where $r_s$ is the [[Schwarzschild radius|G48AGJAZNG]] of $M$.
So the body must fit inside a hoop of that circumference, turned in every direction.

## Remarks

The conjecture is a heuristic criterion and not a precise mathematical statement.
The notions of mass and of circumference "in every direction" are not uniquely defined in a general curved spacetime, and different choices give different precise versions.
For spherically symmetric collapse the criterion agrees with the Schwarzschild picture, since the horizon of mass $M$ has circumference $2\pi r_s = 4\pi G M / c^2$.
Its main content is that collapse in one or two directions only, for example to a long thin spindle, need not produce a horizon.

## Explanations

### L1 human

How small must a body be to become a black hole?
For a round body the answer is its Schwarzschild radius.
Thorne proposed that the same holds for any shape: a black hole forms when the body fits through a hoop of the right size, turned in every direction.
A very long needle of the same mass would not fit through the hoop lengthwise, and the conjecture says that it would not form a horizon.
