## Summary

A module is stably free if it becomes [[free|0DZ9DKAKZR]] after adding a free module of finite rank.
Every free module of finite rank is stably free, but the converse fails in general.
A finitely generated projective module is stably free exactly when its class in [[reduced $K_0$|GBJ1S3M5QV]] is zero.
So $K_0$ cannot tell a stably free module from a free one.
The question of when stably free modules are free is a central theme of the theory of projective modules.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A stably free module is one that is "free up to a correction": add a few free coordinates and it becomes free.
The standard example comes from the $2$-sphere.
Its tangent bundle, plus the normal line bundle, is the trivial bundle of rank $3$ of the surrounding space.
But the tangent bundle itself is not trivial, because by the hairy ball theorem it has no nowhere-zero section.
The algebraic version of this picture gives a stably free module that is not free.
