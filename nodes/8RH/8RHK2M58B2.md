## Statement

Consider a network with traffic rates as in the definition of a [[Wardrop equilibrium|F2SMTMBDP0]].
Assume that every latency function is affine, $\ell_e(x) = a_e x + b_e$ with $a_e, b_e \ge 0$.
For a feasible flow $g$ with edge flows $g_e$, let $C(g) = \sum_{e} g_e \ell_e(g_e)$ be its total travel time.
If $f$ is a Wardrop equilibrium and $f^*$ is any feasible flow, then
$$C(f) \le \tfrac{4}{3}\, C(f^*).$$

## Remarks

The bound is due to Tim Roughgarden and Éva Tardos (2002).
At an equilibrium every used path is a shortest path for the latencies $\ell_e(f_e)$.
This gives the variational inequality $\sum_e \ell_e(f_e) f_e \le \sum_e \ell_e(f_e) f^*_e$.
For affine latencies, $y\,\ell_e(x) \le y\,\ell_e(y) + \tfrac14 x\,\ell_e(x)$ for all $x, y \ge 0$.
Summing gives $C(f) \le C(f^*) + \tfrac14 C(f)$, which is the bound.

The constant $4/3$ is sharp.
In Pigou's network, two parallel edges with $\ell_1(x) = 1$ and $\ell_2(x) = x$ and rate $1$, the equilibrium puts all traffic on the second edge and $C(f) = 1$.
The split $1/2, 1/2$ has cost $\tfrac12 \cdot 1 + \tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac12 = \tfrac34$, and the ratio is $4/3$.
[[Braess's paradox|VTPMBJAHD7]] also reaches the ratio $4/3$.

The hypothesis on latencies is needed.
With $\ell_2(x) = x^d$ in Pigou's network the ratio grows without bound as $d \to \infty$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When every driver picks the fastest route for themselves, total travel time is usually worse than under a central plan.
The price of anarchy measures how much worse.
For roads whose delay grows linearly with traffic, selfish routing costs at most a third more than the best plan.

### L2 human

The price of anarchy of a game is the worst ratio between the cost of an equilibrium and the optimal cost.
In routing, equilibria minimize a potential function, while optimal flows minimize the total cost.
For affine latencies these two functions differ by at most a factor $2$, which already bounds the ratio by $2$.
The sharper bound $4/3$ uses the variational inequality.
Roughgarden showed that, for many classes of latency functions, networks of two parallel edges already give the worst ratio.
