## Summary

Linear algebra is the study of vector spaces, of the linear maps between them, and of the matrices that represent these maps.
A vector space over a field is a set with an addition and a multiplication by scalars that obey the usual rules of arithmetic, and a linear map is a map that keeps these two operations.
Linear algebra is used in almost every part of mathematics and in physics, statistics, engineering and computer science, because many problems become linear in a first approximation.
The Chinese text The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art solved systems of linear equations by elimination, and Peano gave the axioms of a vector space in 1888.
This topic collects the linear algebra pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Matrix theory and multilinear algebra, numerical analysis, rings and modules, and functional analysis have their own topics.
