## Summary

The subgradient method minimises a convex function that need not be differentiable by stepping against a [[subgradient|9C5WXYYQEK]] at each point.
It is the analogue of [[gradient descent|12317R8RTJ]] for nonsmooth convex functions, such as maxima of affine functions or the $\ell_1$ norm.
A step need not decrease the function, so one tracks the best value found so far.
With suitable step sizes this best value converges to the minimum, at the slow but unimprovable rate of order $1/\sqrt{k}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

At a kink of a convex function there is no single slope, but there are many lines that touch the graph from below; their slopes are the subgradients.
The method picks one of them and steps against it, as gradient descent would.
Such a step may go uphill for a moment, but with shrinking steps it gets closer to the minimiser on average.
For $f(x) = \abs{x}$, a subgradient at $x \ne 0$ is the sign of $x$, so each step moves toward $0$ by the step size.
