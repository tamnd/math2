## Definition

Let $n, k \ge 1$, let $q = (q_1, \ldots, q_n)$ and $s = (s_1, \ldots, s_k)$ be [[generalized coordinates|BCF1EX9W2M]], and let $L(q, s, \dot q, \dot s, t)$ be a Lagrangian of class $C^2$.
Assume that each $s_j$ is a [[cyclic coordinate|TGC5C62HYE]], that is, $\partial L / \partial s_j = 0$, so that $L = L(q, \dot q, \dot s, t)$.
Let $p_j = \partial L / \partial \dot s_j$ for $j = 1, \ldots, k$.
Assume that for fixed $(q, \dot q, t)$ the map $\dot s \mapsto p$ is invertible, with inverse $\dot s = u(q, \dot q, p, t)$ of class $C^1$.
The *Routhian* is the function
$$R(q, \dot q, p, t) = \sum_{j=1}^k p_j \, u_j(q, \dot q, p, t) - L\big(q, \dot q, u(q, \dot q, p, t), t\big).$$
*Routhian mechanics* describes the motion by the [[Lagrange equations|BEM454AYTT]] of $R$ in the variables $q$, with $p$ held constant:
$$\frac{d}{dt} \frac{\partial R}{\partial \dot q_i} - \frac{\partial R}{\partial q_i} = 0, \qquad i = 1, \ldots, n,$$
together with $\dot p_j = 0$ and $\dot s_j = \partial R / \partial p_j$.

## Remarks

Since $R$ is a partial Legendre transform of $L$ in $\dot s$ only, $\partial R / \partial q_i = -\partial L / \partial q_i$ and $\partial R / \partial \dot q_i = -\partial L / \partial \dot q_i$, so the equations for $q$ are the Lagrange equations of $L$.
Some authors use the opposite sign, $R = L - \sum_j p_j \dot s_j$; the equations of motion for $q$ do not change.

Example: a particle of mass $m$ in the plane with a central potential, $L = \frac{1}{2} m (\dot r^2 + r^2 \dot \varphi^2) - V(r)$.
The angle $\varphi$ is cyclic, $p = m r^2 \dot \varphi$, and
$$R = \frac{p^2}{2 m r^2} - \frac{1}{2} m \dot r^2 + V(r).$$
The Lagrange equation of $R$ in $r$ is $m \ddot r = p^2/(m r^3) - V'(r)$, a problem with one degree of freedom.

Non-example: for $L = \frac{1}{2} \dot s^2 - \frac{1}{2} s^2$ the coordinate $s$ is not cyclic, since $\partial L / \partial s = -s \ne 0$, so its momentum $\dot s$ is not conserved and cannot be treated as a constant.
