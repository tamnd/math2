## Definition

Let $W$ be a discrete memoryless [[channel|B9C5RBFATB]].
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a block code of length $n$ for $W$, with a decoder.
Let $P_e(\mathcal{C})$ be its average probability of decoding error, with messages chosen uniformly.

1. Let $(\mathcal{C}_n)$ be a sequence of such codes, where $\mathcal{C}_n$ has length $n$. Its *error exponent* is
$$E = \lim_{n \to \infty} -\frac{1}{n} \log_2 P_e(\mathcal{C}_n),$$
when this limit exists in $[0, \infty]$.
2. For a rate $R \ge 0$, let $P_e^*(n, R)$ be the smallest value of $P_e(\mathcal{C})$ over all codes of length $n$ with at least $2^{nR}$ codewords. The *reliability function* of $W$ is
$$E(R) = \limsup_{n \to \infty} -\frac{1}{n} \log_2 P_e^*(n, R).$$

## Remarks

Example: let $W$ be the binary symmetric channel with crossover probability $p \in (0, \frac12)$.
For odd $n$, let $\mathcal{C}_n$ be the repetition code with the two codewords $0 \cdots 0$ and $1 \cdots 1$ and majority decoding.
An error occurs when more than $n/2$ of the $n$ bits flip.
By Cramér's theorem on large deviations this probability decays with exponent
$$E = \tfrac12 \log_2 \frac{1}{2p} + \tfrac12 \log_2 \frac{1}{2(1-p)} = -\tfrac12 \log_2\big(4p(1-p)\big) > 0.$$

Non-example: suppose $\mathcal{C}_n$ has error probability $2^{-n}$ for even $n$ and $2^{-2n}$ for odd $n$.
Then $-\frac1n \log_2 P_e(\mathcal{C}_n)$ oscillates between $1$ and $2$.
The limit in clause 1 does not exist, so the sequence has no error exponent in this sense.

Some authors use the $\liminf$ in clause 2, or the maximal error probability instead of the average.
The reliability function is positive for $0 \le R < C$, where $C$ is the [[capacity|J7MZ2XQP9M]], and it is $0$ for $R > C$.
For rates between a critical rate and $C$ it is known exactly: there the random coding lower bound of Gallager meets the sphere-packing upper bound.
