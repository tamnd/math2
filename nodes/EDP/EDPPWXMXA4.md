## Statement

Let $\hbar > 0$ and let $\mathcal{P}_{\le k}$ be the space of real polynomials of degree at most $k$ in two variables $q, p$, with the [[Poisson bracket|2S0MXDT4YH]] $\{f, g\} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial q}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial p}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q}$.
Let $V$ be the space of functions $x \mapsto h(x) e^{-x^2/2}$ on $\R$ with $h$ a complex polynomial, which is the span of the [[Hermite functions|52VS07H6GK]].
Let $X$ and $P$ be the operators on $V$ given by $(X\psi)(x) = x\psi(x)$ and $P\psi = -i\hbar\, \psi'$.
Then there is no linear map $Q \colon \mathcal{P}_{\le 4} \to \End(V)$ such that:

1. $Q(1) = I$, $Q(q) = X$ and $Q(p) = P$;
2. $[Q(f), Q(g)] = i\hbar\, Q(\{f, g\})$ for all $f, g \in \mathcal{P}_{\le 3}$.

## Remarks

The idea of the proof: since $V$ is an irreducible module over the algebra generated by $X$ and $P$, an operator on $V$ that commutes with both is a scalar.
Using this and clause 2 with $f$ or $g$ of degree $1$, the map $Q$ is forced, step by step.
It must agree with [[Weyl quantization|RFNGJ09WDR]] on all polynomials of degree at most $3$.
The polynomial $q^2 p^2$ then can be reached in two ways, since $\{q^3, p^3\} = 9 q^2 p^2$ and $\{q^2 p, q p^2\} = 3 q^2 p^2$.
The two commutators $\frac{1}{9}[Q(q^3), Q(p^3)]$ and $\frac13[Q(q^2 p), Q(q p^2)]$, computed with the Weyl operators, differ by a nonzero multiple of $\hbar^3 I$, which contradicts the linearity of $Q$.

Example of what does work: on $\mathcal{P}_{\le 2}$ alone, Weyl quantization satisfies clause 2 exactly, since the bracket of two quadratic polynomials is again quadratic.
So the obstruction appears only at degree $3$ and $4$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Dirac hoped that quantization could turn every classical Poisson bracket into a quantum commutator, divided by $i\hbar$.
Groenewold's theorem says that this is impossible, even for polynomials of degree four in one position and one momentum variable.
So any quantization rule must accept errors in the bracket rule, of order $\hbar^2$ and higher.

### L2 human

The theorem explains why [[canonical quantization|7NW2C10YSR]] needs extra choices beyond the canonical commutation relations.
Weyl quantization satisfies the bracket rule only up to terms of order $\hbar^2$: the Moyal bracket replaces the Poisson bracket.
Deformation quantization accepts this and builds a star product whose commutator agrees with the Poisson bracket at first order in $\hbar$.
Van Hove later extended the result to more general phase spaces and quantization settings.
