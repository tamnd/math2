## Definition

Let $n$ be a positive integer, let $a$ be a vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$, and let $b$ be a nonzero vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
Write $a \cdot b = a_1 b_1 + \dots + a_n b_n$ for the dot product.
The vector projection of $a$ onto $b$ is the vector
$$\operatorname{proj}_b a = \frac{a \cdot b}{b \cdot b} \, b.$$
The scalar projection, or scalar component, of $a$ onto $b$ is the real number $\dfrac{a \cdot b}{\|b\|}$, where $\|b\|$ is the [[length|0XKBKGTCPC]] of $b$.
The vector rejection of $a$ from $b$ is the vector $a - \operatorname{proj}_b a$.

## Notation

Other notations are $P_b a$ and $a_{\parallel b}$ for the vector projection, and $\operatorname{comp}_b a$ for the scalar projection.

## Remarks

The hypothesis $b \neq 0$ makes $b \cdot b$ positive, so the quotient in the definition exists.
The coefficient $\frac{a \cdot b}{b \cdot b}$ is chosen so that the rejection has dot product $0$ with $b$, which means it is [[perpendicular to $b$|03NK6RSRQ1]].
Among all multiples $cb$, the projection is also the one that makes $\|a - cb\|$ smallest.
The two projections are tied together: the vector projection is the scalar projection times the unit vector $b / \|b\|$.
If $\theta$ is the angle between $a \neq 0$ and $b$, the scalar projection equals $\|a\| \cos \theta$, so its sign tells whether the angle is acute or obtuse.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The projection answers the question "how much of $a$ points along $b$?"
For $a = (3, 4)$ and $b = (1, 0)$ the projection is $(3, 0)$.
The rest, $(0, 4)$, is perpendicular to $b$.

### L2 human

For $a = (1, 2, 3)$ and $b = (1, 1, 1)$ the dot products are $a \cdot b = 6$ and $b \cdot b = 3$, so
$$\operatorname{proj}_b a = \tfrac{6}{3}\, b = (2, 2, 2).$$
The scalar projection is $6 / \sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3}$, which is the length of $(2, 2, 2)$.
Watch out: the vector projection divides by $b \cdot b = \|b\|^2$, while the scalar projection divides by $\|b\|$.
