## Summary

A quantum state is the mathematical description of a quantum system at one moment, from which the probabilities of all measurement results follow.
In the Hilbert space formulation it is a [[density operator|V1JTB52E8C]] on the [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] of the system.
The pure states are the unit vectors up to a phase factor, the points of the [[projective Hilbert space|RC1C7WXX1H]]; the others are [[mixed states|CKCMBP5S2P]].
The states form a convex set, whose extreme points are the pure states.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A quantum state is everything one can know about a quantum system before measuring it.
It does not fix the result of every measurement; it fixes the probabilities of the results.
For a spin, the state "up" is the vector $(1, 0)$ in $\C^2$, "down" is $(0, 1)$, and $(1, 1)/\sqrt{2}$ is an equal superposition of both.
A measurement of the vertical spin in the last state gives "up" or "down" with probability $1/2$ each.
Multiplying a state vector by a complex number of modulus $1$ does not change any probability, so it gives the same state.
