## Summary

The centrifugal force is a [[fictitious force|ZN2Z2HJY9Z]] that appears when motion is described in a [[rotating reference frame|HNXK6CJ5B2]].
For a particle of mass $m$ at position $r$ in a frame that turns with [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]] $\omega$, it equals $-m\,\omega \times (\omega \times r)$.
It points away from the axis of rotation and has size $m \abs{\omega}^2 d$, where $d$ is the distance to the axis.
With the Coriolis force and the Euler force, it lets Newton's second law keep its form in a rotating frame.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a merry-go-round you feel pushed outward, away from the centre.
Seen from the ground, nothing pushes you outward: your body simply tends to go straight while the platform turns under you.
The centrifugal force is the bookkeeping term that explains this push for an observer who turns with the platform.
For example, a mass of $2$ kg held $3$ m from the axis of a platform turning at $1$ radian per second feels an outward force of $2 \cdot 1^2 \cdot 3 = 6$ newtons.
