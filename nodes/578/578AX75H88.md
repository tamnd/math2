## Summary

Simulated annealing is a randomized method for global optimization, especially over large finite sets such as the tours of a travelling salesman.
It is a random walk that always accepts moves that lower the cost.
It accepts moves that raise the cost with a probability that falls as a temperature parameter decreases.
At a fixed temperature it is the [[Metropolis–Hastings algorithm|MKHF449E22]] for a Boltzmann distribution, which favours low costs more strongly at low temperatures.
It was proposed for optimization by Kirkpatrick, Gelatt and Vecchi in 1983, by analogy with the slow cooling of metals.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A pure downhill search gets stuck in the first valley it finds.
Simulated annealing sometimes accepts an uphill move, so it can climb out of a valley and find a deeper one.
Early on, when the "temperature" is high, it accepts uphill moves often and explores widely.
As the temperature is lowered, it becomes more and more like a downhill search and settles in a deep valley.
