## Summary

Continuum damage mechanics describes the gradual loss of stiffness and strength of a material caused by the growth of microcracks and voids.
Instead of tracking each defect, it adds an internal variable, the damage, to the [[constitutive equations|Q4C55V47A8]].
In the simplest models the damage is a scalar $D$ between $0$ for the intact material and $1$ for complete failure.
The damage reduces the effective load-carrying area, and its evolution is governed by a law that respects the second law of thermodynamics.
The theory, started by Kachanov and Rabotnov for creep rupture, is used to predict the onset of macroscopic cracks in creep, fatigue and brittle failure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A concrete beam that has been overloaded looks whole, but it is full of tiny cracks and has become softer.
Damage mechanics does not try to describe these cracks one by one.
It gives each point of the material a number $D$ that says how much of it is already broken.
If $D = 0.3$, the material behaves as if only $70$ percent of its cross-section were still carrying load, so it is $30$ percent less stiff.
