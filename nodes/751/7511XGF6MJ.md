## Summary

The direct stiffness method computes the displacements and member forces of a framed structure, such as a truss or a frame.
It assembles the stiffness of each member into one global [[stiffness matrix|G8ZTW9JVX1]].
The unknowns are the displacements of the joints, and the equations are the force balances at the joints.
It is the matrix method used in structural analysis programs and the simplest case of the [[finite element method in structural mechanics|CGYM9VZC39]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every bar in a structure acts like a spring: pushing its ends together or apart needs a force proportional to the change of length.
The method writes down this spring law for every bar, in the coordinates of the whole structure.
At each joint, the forces from all bars meeting there must balance the outside load.
Adding the contributions of all bars gives one system of linear equations for the joint movements.
For two springs of stiffness $k_1$ and $k_2$ in a row, fixed at one end and pulled by $P$ at the other, the method gives the end displacement $P/k_1 + P/k_2$.
