## Summary

Elastic wave scattering happens when a wave in an elastic solid meets an obstacle, a cavity or an inclusion.
The obstacle then sends out new waves in many directions.
In the time-harmonic setting it is a boundary value problem for the Navier equation in an exterior domain with a radiation condition at infinity.
A special feature of solids is mode conversion: an incident pressure wave produces both pressure and shear waves.
Scattering is the basis of ultrasonic testing of materials and of the seismic imaging of the Earth.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Throw a stone into a pond with a post sticking out of the water, and the ripples bend around the post and spread out from it.
The same happens inside a solid when a sound or seismic wave meets a hole, a crack or a stone of another material.
In a solid there are two kinds of waves, push waves and shake waves, and a scatterer turns some of each kind into the other.
By listening to the scattered waves one can find hidden flaws, which is how ultrasonic inspection of metal parts works.
