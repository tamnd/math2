## Definition

Let $f \colon \R^n \times \R^m \to \R^n$ be continuously differentiable, and consider the [[control system|ZPDTVZQN5K]] $x' = f(x, u)$.
Let $P \subseteq \R^q$ be a set of parameters and let $\rho \mapsto (x_\rho, u_\rho)$ be a continuous family of [[equilibrium|0N8NGKDZDZ]] pairs, that is, $f(x_\rho, u_\rho) = 0$ for each $\rho \in P$.
Let $t \mapsto \rho(t) \in P$ be a measured *scheduling variable*, for example a reference set point or an external condition.
A *gain-scheduled controller* is obtained in three steps.

1. For each $\rho \in P$, form the [[linearisation|09ZWYRQ41J]] $\delta x' = A(\rho)\, \delta x + B(\rho)\, \delta u$, with $A(\rho)$ and $B(\rho)$ the [[Jacobian matrices|047NDTEPCV]] of $f$ with respect to $x$ and $u$ at $(x_\rho, u_\rho)$.
2. For each $\rho$ in a finite grid of $P$, design a gain $K(\rho)$ such that all eigenvalues of $A(\rho) + B(\rho) K(\rho)$ have negative real part. Extend $K$ to all of $P$ by interpolation.
3. Apply the feedback $u(t) = u_{\rho(t)} + K(\rho(t))\, \big(x(t) - x_{\rho(t)}\big)$.

*Gain scheduling* is the design of controllers by this procedure, and of its variants with dynamic linear controllers in step 2.

## Remarks

Example: for $x' = -x^3 + u$ take $P = \R$, $x_\rho = \rho$ and $u_\rho = \rho^3$; the linearisation is $\delta x' = -3\rho^2\, \delta x + \delta u$.
The gain $K(\rho) = 3\rho^2 - 1$ gives $A(\rho) + B(\rho) K(\rho) = -1$ for every $\rho$.
The scheduled feedback is $u = \rho^3 + (3\rho^2 - 1)(x - \rho)$.
For a constant set point $\rho$, the closed loop $x' = -x^3 + \rho^3 + (3\rho^2 - 1)(x - \rho)$ has the equilibrium $x = \rho$ with linearisation $\delta x' = -\delta x$, so it is locally exponentially stable.
Non-example: one fixed gain $K(0) = -1$ used for all set points is not gain scheduling; at $\rho = 1$ it gives $A + B K = -4$, a different closed-loop behaviour from the design at $\rho = 0$.
Stability of each frozen linear closed loop does not imply stability of the nonlinear closed loop when $\rho(t)$ varies; standard results give stability when $\rho$ varies slowly enough.
