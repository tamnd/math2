## Summary

An equation of state is a relation between state variables, such as pressure, volume and temperature.
It holds in every equilibrium state of a given substance.
It encodes the material properties of the substance, while the laws of thermodynamics hold for all substances.
The ideal gas law, the van der Waals equation and the virial expansion are standard examples.
Equations of state are needed to close the equations of fluid dynamics and to compute properties of gases, liquids and stars.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The laws of thermodynamics are the same for air, water and steel, but these materials behave very differently.
An equation of state is the formula that describes one particular material.
For example, for a dilute gas the pressure times the volume is proportional to the temperature.
Knowing two of pressure, volume and temperature, the equation of state gives the third.
