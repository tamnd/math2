## Definition

Let a monochromatic scalar field of wavelength $\lambda$ and wavenumber $k = 2\pi/\lambda$ have complex amplitude $u_0(\xi, \eta)$ in the plane $z = 0$, supported in an aperture.
Consider the [[Rayleigh–Sommerfeld formula|3B5D567BEK]] for the field at $(x, y, z)$ with $z > 0$.
In its phase, replace the distance $r$ by the expansion $z + \frac{(x - \xi)^2 + (y - \eta)^2}{2z}$.
In its amplitude, replace $r$ by $z$.
The result is the *Fresnel diffraction integral*
$$u(x, y, z) = \frac{e^{i k z}}{i \lambda z} \iint u_0(\xi, \eta) \exp\left( \frac{i k}{2 z} \left[ (x - \xi)^2 + (y - \eta)^2 \right] \right) d\xi \, d\eta.$$
*Fresnel diffraction* is the diffraction pattern given by this integral, in the regime where the [[Fresnel number|AK3TF7B3ZW]] of the aperture is not small.

## Remarks

The approximation is [[paraxial|N111BHFQ2H]]: it needs the transverse distances to be small compared with $z$.
A sufficient condition is that the next term of the phase expansion is small, $z^3 \gg \frac{\pi}{4\lambda} \max \left[ (x - \xi)^2 + (y - \eta)^2 \right]^2$.
Example: if the aperture is the whole plane and $u_0 \equiv 1$, the integral is a Gaussian integral and gives $u = e^{i k z}$, the plane wave, as it should.
Non-example: if $z \gg a^2 / \lambda$ for an aperture of size $a$, the quadratic phase in $\xi, \eta$ is negligible.
The pattern is then Fraunhofer diffraction, a limiting case rather than a typical Fresnel pattern.
The integral is a convolution of $u_0$ with the kernel $\frac{e^{ikz}}{i\lambda z} e^{i k (x^2 + y^2)/(2z)}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Fresnel integral adds the waves from all points of the aperture, keeping the exact phase up to second order in the transverse offsets.
This is the right approximation when the screen is far compared with the aperture, but not so far that the aperture looks like a point.
