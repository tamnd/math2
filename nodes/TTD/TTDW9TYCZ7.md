## Example

Let $F$ be a field and let $M = F[x]$ be the [[polynomial ring|0SDC934R52]], as a module over itself.
We show that $M$ is not the [[module of an endomorphism|KGVTFBZX59]] of a finite-dimensional space, although it has a single generator.

1. $M$ is generated by $1$, so it is finitely generated, and it is [[free|0DZ9DKAKZR]] of rank $1$.
2. The polynomials $1, x, x^2, \dots$ are [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]] over $F$, so $\dim_F M$ is infinite.
3. If $f \ne 0$, then $f \cdot 1 = f \ne 0$. So no nonzero polynomial kills $M$, and $M$ is not torsion.
4. For a finite-dimensional $V$ and a linear map $T$, the module $V_T$ has finite $F$-dimension and is killed by the [[minimal polynomial|03NDVCSHSB]] $m_T$.
5. By step 2, or by step 3, $M$ is not isomorphic to any $V_T$ with $V$ finite-dimensional.

## Remarks

### The free part of the structure theorem

A finitely generated module over $F[x]$ is a free part $F[x]^r$ plus a torsion part.
The example shows that $r = 0$ for every module that comes from a matrix, so only the torsion part matters in linear algebra.

### What does work

The module $M$ is still of the form $V_T$, with the infinite-dimensional space $V = F[x]$ and $T$ the multiplication by $x$.
Each quotient $F[x]/(f)$ with $f$ monic of degree $n \ge 1$ has dimension $n$, and it is the module of the [[companion matrix|0ZCWPNZTQ3]] $C(f)$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The polynomials form a space with infinitely many independent directions: $1$, $x$, $x$ squared, and so on.
A matrix acts on a space with only finitely many directions.
So the polynomials, with "multiply by $x$" as the map, cannot come from a matrix.

### L2 human

Here is the argument by dimension in a few lines.
Assume $F[x] \cong V_T$ with $\dim V = n$.
An isomorphism of $F[x]$-modules is in particular an $F$-linear bijection, so $\dim_F F[x] = n$.
But $1, x, \dots, x^n$ are $n + 1$ independent elements of $F[x]$, which is a contradiction.
The typical use is to see that every module $V_T$ is torsion.
Watch out: a finitely generated module need not be finite-dimensional over $F$, since $F[x]$ has one generator and infinite dimension.
