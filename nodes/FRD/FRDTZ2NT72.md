## Summary

Relativistic angular momentum is the form of [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] used in special relativity.
For a particle it is the antisymmetric tensor $M^{\mu\nu} = x^\mu p^\nu - x^\nu p^\mu$, built from its position four-vector $x$ and its [[four-momentum|67N6B8MJ3X]] $p$.
Its space-space components are the usual angular momentum $\vec x \times \vec p$, and its time-space components describe the motion of the centre of energy.
The total angular momentum tensor of an isolated system is conserved, as a consequence of the invariance of physics under Lorentz transformations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In Newtonian physics angular momentum is a vector $\vec x \times \vec p$ that measures how much a body turns about a point.
In relativity this vector is not enough, because a change of frame mixes it with other quantities.
The fix is to collect it, together with a second vector built from energy, time and position, into a $4 \times 4$ antisymmetric table.
For example, a particle moving along the $x$-axis through the origin has zero ordinary angular momentum about the origin.
