## Summary

The compressibility of a substance is the relative decrease of its volume per unit increase of pressure.
It depends on the process, and the two standard cases are the isothermal compressibility $\kappa_T$ and the isentropic (adiabatic) compressibility $\kappa_S$.
Its inverse is the bulk modulus.
Compressibility enters the thermodynamic stability conditions, the relation between heat capacities, and the speed of sound.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Squeeze a balloon and it gets smaller; squeeze a steel ball with the same force and it hardly changes.
Compressibility measures how much the volume shrinks, as a fraction of the volume, for each unit of added pressure.
Gases are very compressible, liquids much less, solids least.
For an ideal gas at constant temperature and pressure $p$, a small extra pressure $\Delta p$ shrinks the volume by about the fraction $\Delta p / p$, so the compressibility is $1/p$.
