## Proof

This is a cited outline.
Steps 1 to 3 are complete arguments.
Steps 4 to 9 summarize two series of papers, and each series ends with the solution.
The first is Zlil Sela, "Diophantine geometry over groups VI: The elementary theory of a free group", Geometric and Functional Analysis 16 (2006), pages 707 to 730.
The second is Olga Kharlampovich and Alexei Myasnikov, "Elementary theory of free non-abelian groups", Journal of Algebra 302 (2006), pages 451 to 552.
We use the notation of [[the solution of Tarski's problems|NCWBT0BZC3]].

1. Part 1 follows from part 2. By part 2 with $k = 2$, the standard embedding $F_2 \to F_n$ is elementary, so $F_2 \equiv F_n$ for each $n \ge 2$. Elementary equivalence is an equivalence relation, so $F_m \equiv F_n$ for all $m, n \ge 2$.
2. The groups $F_2$ and $F_n$ embed in each other. The standard inclusion gives $F_2 \le F_n$. In $F_2$ with basis $a, b$, the [[subgroup|0X1JXT47GA]] generated by $a, bab^{-1}, \ldots, b^{n-1} a b^{-(n-1)}$ is free with this basis, so $F_n$ embeds in $F_2$. The reason: the kernel of the homomorphism $F_2 \to \mathbb{Z}$ with $a \mapsto 0$ and $b \mapsto 1$ is free with basis $\{b^i a b^{-i} : i \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, by the Reidemeister-Schreier method, and a subset of a basis is a basis of the subgroup that it generates.
3. A universal sentence that is true in a group is true in each subgroup, by basic property 1 of the [[elementary theory of groups|RX9MB386C5]]. With step 2, $F_2$ and $F_n$ satisfy the same universal sentences, and so, by negation, the same existential sentences.
4. (Makanin; Razborov.) There is an algorithm that decides whether a finite system of equations, with constants from $F$, has a solution in a free group $F$. Razborov described the set of all solutions of such a system by a finite diagram of homomorphisms, now called a Makanin-Razborov diagram.
5. (Sela; Kharlampovich-Myasnikov.) The groups in these diagrams are limit groups, also called finitely generated fully residually free groups. Both series of papers develop a structure theory for them, and from it a description of the solutions of formulas with the quantifier pattern $\forall \exists$ over a free group.
6. (Sela; Kharlampovich-Myasnikov.) Each formula over a non-abelian free group is equivalent to a Boolean combination of $\forall \exists$ formulas. The reduction works in the same way in each $F_n$, and it is the main step of the proofs.
7. (Sela; Kharlampovich-Myasnikov.) For $2 \le k \le n$, a $\forall \exists$ formula with parameters from $F_k$ is true in $F_k$ if and only if it is true in $F_n$. This uses the description of step 5.
8. By steps 6 and 7, each formula with parameters from $F_k$ has the same truth value in $F_k$ and in $F_n$. This is the definition of an [[elementary embedding|CGH8C8EA9N]], so part 2 holds, and part 1 follows by step 1.
9. (Kharlampovich-Myasnikov.) The constructions of steps 4 to 6 are effective. So an algorithm reduces each sentence to a Boolean combination of $\forall \exists$ sentences and decides these. This proves part 3.

## Idea

The proof reduces first-order sentences over a free group to statements about systems of equations.
Makanin and Razborov describe the solutions of one system of equations.
Sela and Kharlampovich-Myasnikov extend this description to formulas with alternating quantifiers, and they show that every formula reduces to formulas with one alternation, which have the same truth value in all non-abelian free groups.

## Variants

Sela's proof is geometric: it studies actions of limit groups on real trees.
The proof of Kharlampovich and Myasnikov is algebraic and combinatorial, and it gives the decision algorithm directly.
The two proofs were found independently.
Step 3 alone gives the much easier fact that all non-abelian free groups of finite rank have the same universal theory.
