## Example

Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, the [[integers modulo $12$|03VFZ2WETT]] under addition.
We find all its [[maximal subgroups|RTTRNQ4JYF]].

1. By the [[fundamental theorem of cyclic groups|0423TXR9AN]], $G$ has exactly one subgroup of each order $d$ dividing $12$, and no other subgroups. The divisors are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$.
2. The six subgroups are $\{0\}$, $\langle 6 \rangle = \{0, 6\}$, $\langle 4 \rangle = \{0, 4, 8\}$, $\langle 3 \rangle = \{0, 3, 6, 9\}$, $\langle 2 \rangle = \{0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10\}$ and $G$, of orders $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$.
3. By the same theorem, the subgroup of order $d$ lies in the subgroup of order $d'$ exactly when $d \mid d'$.
4. So the proper subgroup of order $d$ is maximal exactly when $d$ divides no divisor of $12$ other than $d$ and $12$. The proper divisors of $12$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6$.
5. The order $6$ divides only $6$ and $12$, and the order $4$ divides only $4$ and $12$. The order $3$ divides $6$, the order $2$ divides $6$, and the order $1$ divides $6$. So $\langle 4 \rangle$, $\langle 6 \rangle$ and $\{0\}$ lie strictly inside $\langle 2 \rangle$, and they are not maximal.
6. So the maximal subgroups of $G$ are $\langle 2 \rangle$, of order $6$ and [[index|0DTXBQPA2S]] $2$, and $\langle 3 \rangle$, of order $4$ and index $3$.

## Remarks

The indices $2$ and $3$ are the primes that divide $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, as in the general rule for $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.
The two maximal subgroups have different orders, and neither contains the other.
Their intersection is $\{0, 6\} = \langle 6 \rangle$, which is the [[Frattini subgroup|KXJG5VV340]] of $G$.
An element $a$ generates $G$ exactly when it lies in neither maximal subgroup, that is, when $a$ is odd and not a multiple of $3$.
These are $1, 5, 7, 11$, the $\varphi(12) = 4$ generators.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a clock with $12$ hours.
If you move only by $2$ hours at a time, you reach the six even hours.
If you move only by $3$ hours at a time, you reach the four hours $0, 3, 6, 9$.
These two sets are as large as a proper part of the clock can be: if you add one more hour to either set and keep it closed under moves, you reach the whole clock.

### L2 human

We check that $\langle 3 \rangle$ is maximal directly.

1. Let $H$ be a subgroup with $\langle 3 \rangle \subsetneq H$, and choose $a \in H$ with $a \notin \langle 3 \rangle$.
2. Then $3 \nmid a$, so $\gcd(a, 3) = 1$, and there are integers $u, v$ with $ua + 3v = 1$.
3. Since $a \in H$ and $3 \in H$, we get $1 = ua + 3v \in H$, so $H = G$.

The typical use of this example is to show that maximal subgroups need not have the same order.
A trap: $\langle 4 \rangle$ has order $3$, a prime, but it is not maximal; the index, not the order, must be prime here.
