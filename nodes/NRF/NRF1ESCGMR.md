## Summary

The index ellipsoid, also called the optical indicatrix, is an ellipsoid built from the [[permittivity|F768YN0P2V]] tensor of an anisotropic medium.
Its semi-axes are the principal [[refractive indices|9VGK0SP6EQ]].
Cut it by the plane through the centre orthogonal to a direction of propagation.
The section is an ellipse whose semi-axes are the two refractive indices for that direction.
It turns the optics of crystals into the geometry of a [[quadric|Q3Q4FF23VG]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each crystal carries an invisible egg-shaped surface that encodes how fast light moves in it.
To find what happens to light going in some direction, slice the egg through its centre, at right angles to that direction.
The slice is an ellipse; its long and short half-axes are the two refractive indices, and they point along the two allowed polarizations.
For glass the egg is a ball, so every slice is a circle and there is only one index.
