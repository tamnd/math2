## Summary

Self-organized criticality is the property of some slowly driven dynamical systems to evolve by themselves into a critical state.
In that state they show scale invariance, such as avalanches of all sizes with a power-law size distribution.
No parameter needs to be tuned to a critical value.
Ordinary [[critical phenomena|57789VKKAY]] need a tuned parameter, such as the temperature of a magnet.
The idea was proposed by Per Bak, Chao Tang and Kurt Wiesenfeld in 1987, with the [[abelian sandpile model|N33S3095R5]] as the first example.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pour sand slowly onto a table.
The pile grows steeper until it reaches a certain slope.
From then on new grains cause landslides of all sizes, from a few grains to huge parts of the pile.
Nobody adjusts anything: the pile itself keeps its slope at the edge of stability.
This is the picture behind self-organized criticality.
It has been proposed as an explanation for power laws in earthquakes, forest fires and other natural systems, but how widely it applies is debated.
