## Definition

A process of a thermodynamic system is *isothermal* if the [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]] of the system is the same at all times of the process.
In the usual case the process is [[quasistatic|PVDWD7HGFG]], and the temperature is constant along its curve in the state space.

## Remarks

Let $n$ moles of [[ideal gas|N7R0H2VNDJ]] at temperature $T$ expand quasistatically from volume $V_1$ to $V_2$.
Then $pV = nRT$ is constant, and the work done by the gas is $nRT \ln(V_2 / V_1)$.
Since the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on $T$, the heat absorbed is also $nRT \ln(V_2 / V_1)$.
The expansion of a gas in an insulated cylinder is not isothermal: the gas does work without taking in heat, so it cools.
