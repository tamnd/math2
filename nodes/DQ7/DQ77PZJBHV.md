## Summary

General topology is the study of topological spaces and continuous maps, and of the properties of spaces that homeomorphisms preserve.
A topological space is a set with a family of open subsets that contains the empty set and the whole set and is closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
The subject gives the common language of continuity, convergence, compactness and connectedness that analysis, geometry and algebraic topology use.
Fréchet introduced metric spaces in 1906, and Hausdorff gave an axiomatic definition of a topological space by neighbourhoods in 1914.
This topic collects the general topology pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Algebraic topology, manifolds and differential topology, set theory and functional analysis have their own topics.
