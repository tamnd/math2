## Summary

Electromagnetic scattering is the process by which an object redirects part of an incident electromagnetic wave.
The object may also be a region where the material differs from its surroundings.
The total field is written as the incident field plus a scattered field that radiates outward from the object.
Far away, the scattered field is an outgoing spherical wave whose angular profile is the far-field pattern.
Scattering explains the colour of the sky, radar echoes and the imaging of small particles, and its inverse problems underlie many imaging methods.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When light or radio waves hit an object, part of the wave bounces off in many directions.
This bounced part is the scattered wave, and its strength in each direction depends on the size, shape and material of the object.
Radar sends out a wave and listens for the part scattered straight back.
Small particles in the air scatter blue light more than red, which is why the sky looks blue.
