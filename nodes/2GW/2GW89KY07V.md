## Definition

Let the Earth's surface be the sphere $S \subset \R^3$ centred at the origin, and let $P_1, \dots, P_k$ be the present-day plates of a [[plate tectonic|AYQQM6PBMR]] model.
Fix a reference frame, either one plate taken as fixed or an absolute frame such as the paleomagnetic frame.
A *plate reconstruction* at a past time $t$ assigns to each plate $P_i$ a rotation $A_i(t)$ in the [[special orthogonal group|0CGYNDCWRR]] $SO(3)$.
The rotation $A_i(t)$ must carry each point of $P_i$ from its present position to its position at time $t$ in the reference frame.
The rotation $A_i(t)$ is a *total reconstruction rotation*, and its pole is a *total reconstruction pole*.

## Remarks

Each $A_i(t)$ is a rotation about a finite rotation pole, by the [[Euler pole theorem|6SGQDKV15M]].
Rotations relative to different plates compose along a chain of plates: if $A_{i/j}(t)$ moves plate $i$ in the frame of plate $j$, then $A_{i/k}(t) = A_{j/k}(t) \, A_{i/j}(t)$.
Example: take two plates on the two sides of an ocean ridge.
The seafloor magnetic anomalies are dated by the record of [[geomagnetic reversals|ZR7WW7HP5W]].
The rotation that superimposes the anomalies of age $t$ reconstructs one plate relative to the other at time $t$.
Non-example: a map that moves a plate by a reflection or a stretch is not a reconstruction.
It is not in $SO(3)$, so it is not a rigid motion of a plate.
Paleomagnetic data fix past latitudes and orientations but not past longitudes, so absolute reconstructions need extra assumptions.
