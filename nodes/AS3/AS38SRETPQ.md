## Summary

Lovelock theory is the most general local metric theory of gravity in $D$ dimensions with second-order field equations.
Its Lagrangian is a sum of terms built from powers of the Riemann curvature tensor, each a dimensional continuation of an Euler density.
In four dimensions it reduces to general relativity with a cosmological constant, as [[Lovelock's theorem|DW4CF8A42M]] says.
In higher dimensions it allows new terms, the first of which is the Gauss–Bonnet term.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In four dimensions Einstein's equations are the only reasonable equations for a metric alone.
In five or more dimensions there is more room, and Lovelock found every other possibility.
The new terms are products of two, three or more curvature tensors, combined so carefully that the equations still contain only second derivatives of the metric.
These theories appear in string theory and in studies of black holes in higher dimensions.
