## Summary

The Wigner quasiprobability distribution, or Wigner function, of a quantum state is a real function $W(x, p)$ on phase space.
Its marginals are the probability densities of position and of momentum.
The expectation value of an observable is the integral of its [[Weyl symbol|RFNGJ09WDR]] against $W$.
It can take negative values, so it is not a probability density; negativity is a sign of non-classical behaviour.
It is the basic object of the [[phase space formulation|G742VWZ4PB]] of quantum mechanics, and the same transform is the [[Wigner distribution function|FCHW5QGG5V]] of signal analysis.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Classical mechanics describes uncertainty by a probability density on the plane of positions and momenta.
Quantum mechanics forbids a true joint density, because position and momentum cannot be sharp together.
The Wigner function is the best substitute: it gives the right distribution for position alone and for momentum alone.
For the ground state of a harmonic oscillator it is a positive Gaussian bump centred at the origin.
For the first excited state it is negative near the origin, which no classical density can be.
