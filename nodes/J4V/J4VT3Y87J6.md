## Definition

Let $n \ge 1$ be a number of inputs.
A *Cobb–Douglas production function* is a function $f \colon \R^n_+ \to \R_+$ of the form
$$f(x_1, \dots, x_n) = A \, x_1^{\alpha_1} x_2^{\alpha_2} \cdots x_n^{\alpha_n},$$
where $A > 0$ is a constant, called total factor productivity, and $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n > 0$ are constants.

## Remarks

Example: $f(L, K) = 2 L^{1/3} K^{2/3}$ has $A = 2$, $\alpha_1 = 1/3$ and $\alpha_2 = 2/3$, all positive.
For $t > 0$ one checks $f(tL, tK) = t^{1/3 + 2/3} f(L, K) = t f(L, K)$, so it has constant returns to scale.

Non-example: $f(L, K) = L^{-1/2} K$ has the product form but the exponent of $L$ is negative, so it fails the condition $\alpha_1 > 0$; its output falls when labour grows.

In general $f(t x) = t^{\alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n} f(x)$, so $f$ has constant, increasing or decreasing returns to scale as $\sum_i \alpha_i$ is equal to, greater than or less than $1$.
For $x_i > 0$, the output elasticity is $\frac{x_i}{f} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} = \alpha_i$.
With two inputs, the elasticity of substitution equals $1$; the function is the limit case $\rho \to 0$ of the [[constant elasticity of substitution|MYBPAD9TGZ]] family.
The two-input form $Q = A L^{\alpha} K^{1 - \alpha}$ is the one used most often.
