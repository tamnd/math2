## Definition

Let $N \ge 2$ and $m > 0$, and let $\phi \colon \R^3 \to \R$ be a smooth even pair potential.
Consider $N$ identical particles with positions $q_i \in \R^3$ and momenta $p_i \in \R^3$, write $z_i = (q_i, p_i)$, and let their motion be the [[Hamiltonian system|0EDKGA2FAK]] with Hamiltonian
$$H = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\abs{p_i}^2}{2m} + \sum_{1 \le i < j \le N} \phi(q_i - q_j).$$
Let $f_N(t, z_1, \dots, z_N)$ be a probability density on $(\R^6)^N$ that is symmetric in $z_1, \dots, z_N$, decays fast at infinity, and solves the Liouville equation of this dynamics.
For $1 \le s \le N$, the *$s$-particle marginal* is
$$f_s(t, z_1, \dots, z_s) = \int f_N(t, z_1, \dots, z_N) \, dz_{s+1} \cdots dz_N.$$
The *BBGKY hierarchy* is the system of [[partial differential equations|GC5BDZ6QBC]], for $s = 1, \dots, N$:
$$\partial_t f_s + \sum_{i=1}^s \frac{p_i}{m} \cdot \nabla_{q_i} f_s - \sum_{\substack{i, j \le s \\ i \ne j}} \nabla \phi(q_i - q_j) \cdot \nabla_{p_i} f_s = (N - s) \sum_{i=1}^s \int \nabla \phi(q_i - q_{s+1}) \cdot \nabla_{p_i} f_{s+1} \, dz_{s+1},$$
where $\nabla_{q_i}$ and $\nabla_{p_i}$ are the [[gradients|0ABK9V9Y64]] in $q_i$ and $p_i$.

## Remarks

Derivation: the Liouville equation is $\partial_t f_N + \sum_i \frac{p_i}{m} \cdot \nabla_{q_i} f_N - \sum_{i \ne j} \nabla \phi(q_i - q_j) \cdot \nabla_{p_i} f_N = 0$.
Integrate it in $z_{s+1}, \dots, z_N$.
Terms that are derivatives in a variable $z_k$ with $k > s$ integrate to $0$.
The forces exerted on particle $i \le s$ by the $N - s$ particles $j > s$ give equal contributions by symmetry, and they produce the right side.

Example: for $s = N$, the factor $N - s$ is $0$, and the equation is the Liouville equation itself.
For $s = 1$ the equation reads $\partial_t f_1 + \frac{p_1}{m} \cdot \nabla_{q_1} f_1 = (N - 1) \int \nabla \phi(q_1 - q_2) \cdot \nabla_{p_1} f_2 \, dz_2$, so the evolution of $f_1$ is not closed.

Non-example: replacing $f_2$ by $f_1 \otimes f_1$ in the equation for $s = 1$ gives a closed equation for $f_1$.
In the mean-field scaling this is the [[Vlasov equation|SJH7M2VCTE]], but this closure is an approximation and not part of the hierarchy.

Other normalizations, such as $\frac{N!}{(N - s)!} f_s$, are common and change the factor $N - s$.
