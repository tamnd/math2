## Example

Let $U$ be the plane $x + y = 0$ and $W$ the plane $y + z = 0$ in $\R^3$, as in [[the two-plane example|5ABEVQG5YQ]].
Their [[principal angles|YHVD3773AW]] are $0$ and $\pi/3$.
We pick one unit vector in each plane and show that the angle between them is neither of these.

1. The vectors $e = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(1, -1, 0)$ and $f = (1, 0, 0)$ have length $1$, and $e$ satisfies $x + y = 0$ while $f$ satisfies $y + z = 0$.
2. They are the first vectors of the [[orthonormal bases|0S9GGTR0EX]] used in the two-plane example.
3. Their dot product is $e \cdot f = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}$, so the [[angle|0ZEHZY85E6]] between them is $\pi/4$.
4. The value $\pi/4$ is neither $0$ nor $\pi/3$, so it is not a principal angle.
5. The pair $(e, f)$ fails the first search, because the pair $u_1 = w_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt 3}(1, -1, 1)$ gives $u_1 \cdot w_1 = 1 > \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}$.
6. The pair $(e, f)$ cannot be a second pair either, because $e \cdot (1, -1, 1) = \frac{2}{\sqrt 2} \ne 0$, so $e$ is not orthogonal to the first principal vector.
7. The other entries of $Q_1^{\mathsf{T}} Q_2$ in these bases are $-\frac{1}{2}$, $0$ and $-\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}$, the cosines of $2\pi/3$, $\pi/2$ and $3\pi/4$, and none of these is a principal angle.

## Remarks

Every pair of unit vectors $u \in U$ and $w \in W$ has $u \cdot w \le \cos \theta_1$.
So the angle between any two such vectors is at least $\theta_1$, but in general it equals no principal angle.
As $e$ and $f$ turn inside their planes, the angle between them sweeps a whole interval of values, here from $0$ up to $\pi$.
The principal angles are the few values that are fixed by the maximization, and they come from the singular values of $Q_1^{\mathsf{T}} Q_2$, not from its entries.

If you replace $f$ by $w_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt 3}(1, -1, 1)$, the angle with $e$ has cosine $\frac{2}{\sqrt 6} = \sqrt{2/3}$, about $35.26^\circ$.
That is again not a principal angle, which shows that even one principal vector paired with an arbitrary vector does not help.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pick one direction in each of two sheets of paper and measure the angle between the two directions.
The answer depends on the directions you picked, so it says little about the sheets themselves.
Here two choices give $45$ degrees, while the sheets share a fold line and open at $60$ degrees.
The principal angles are the special angles, $0$ and $60$ degrees here, that do not depend on such a choice.

### L2 human

A common error is to take the angle between the first basis vectors of two subspaces as the angle between the subspaces.
This example shows that this gives $\pi/4$ for two planes whose principal angles are $0$ and $\pi/3$.
The correct move is to compute the singular values of the whole matrix $Q_1^{\mathsf{T}} Q_2$, which here are $1$ and $\frac{1}{2}$.
