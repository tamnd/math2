## Statement

Consider a mixture of two components $A$ and $B$ at fixed temperature and pressure.
It has $n$ moles in total, of which a fraction $x_0$ is $B$.
Assume that the mixture is in [[thermodynamic equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]] as two coexisting phases $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
Let $x_\alpha$ and $x_\beta$ be their mole fractions of $B$, where $x_\alpha < x_0 < x_\beta$.
Let $n_\alpha$ and $n_\beta$ be the numbers of moles in the two phases.
Then
$$n_\alpha (x_0 - x_\alpha) = n_\beta (x_\beta - x_0), \qquad \frac{n_\beta}{n} = \frac{x_0 - x_\alpha}{x_\beta - x_\alpha}.$$

## Remarks

The proof is a mass balance: the total amount of $B$ gives $n x_0 = n_\alpha x_\alpha + n_\beta x_\beta$, and $n = n_\alpha + n_\beta$.
Substituting $n = n_\alpha + n_\beta$ and rearranging gives both formulas.
For example, if $x_\alpha = 0.2$, $x_\beta = 0.7$ and $x_0 = 0.4$, then $n_\beta / n = 0.2/0.5 = 0.4$, so $40$ percent of the material is in phase $\beta$.
The same rule holds with mass fractions and masses in place of mole fractions and moles.
The name comes from a picture in the [[phase diagram|R6XCQYXKRP]].
On the tie line from $x_\alpha$ to $x_\beta$, the amounts balance like weights on a lever with its fulcrum at $x_0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a mixture splits into two phases, the lever rule tells how much of each phase you get.
Mark the overall composition and the two phase compositions on a line.
The closer the overall composition is to one phase, the more of that phase there is, just like a seesaw that balances when the heavier child sits nearer the middle.

### L2 human

In a binary [[phase diagram|R6XCQYXKRP]] at fixed pressure, the [[Gibbs phase rule|5AYW64A7J3]] gives one degree of freedom in a two-phase region.
So fixing the temperature fixes $x_\alpha$ and $x_\beta$: they are the ends of the horizontal tie line.
The lever rule then fixes the amounts, which the phase rule does not.
It is used daily in metallurgy, for example to find the fractions of solid and liquid in a cooling alloy, and in distillation.
