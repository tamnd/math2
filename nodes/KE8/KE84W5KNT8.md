## Summary

The multipole expansion writes the [[electric potential|SAEDXNM3Y1]] of a bounded charge distribution, far from the charges, as a series in inverse powers of the distance.
The terms are the monopole, dipole, quadrupole and higher terms, with coefficients given by the moments of the charge density.
The $l$-th term decays like $r^{-(l+1)}$, so a few terms describe the field well at large distance.
The angular factors are [[Legendre polynomials|QFYGJVA996]] or, in general, [[spherical harmonics|3F32Y21HP4]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Seen from very far, any lump of charge looks like a single point charge equal to its total charge.
If the total charge is zero, the next thing one sees is the dipole: how the plus and minus charges are separated.
If that is zero too, one sees the quadrupole, and so on.
Each new term is weaker by one more factor of the distance.
For example, the potential of a neutral dipole falls like $1/r^2$, not like $1/r$.
