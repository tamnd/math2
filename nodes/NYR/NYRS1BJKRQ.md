## Summary

The Kolmogorov microscales are the length, time and velocity scales of the smallest eddies of a [[turbulent|VY307F8Z0S]] flow.
They are formed from the kinematic [[viscosity|NM5Q4845Z4]] $\nu$ and the mean rate $\varepsilon$ of energy dissipation per unit mass.
In Kolmogorov's 1941 theory these are the only quantities that matter at those scales.
At the Kolmogorov length scale, viscous dissipation turns the kinetic energy of the eddies into heat.
They set the grid resolution needed to compute a turbulent flow in full.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stirring a cup of coffee creates big swirls that break into smaller and smaller ones.
The breaking stops at a size where the liquid's internal friction is strong enough to smear the swirls out.
That size is the Kolmogorov length; in a stirred cup of coffee or in the wind it is typically a fraction of a millimetre.
It depends only on how sticky the fluid is and how fast energy is being pumped in and lost.
