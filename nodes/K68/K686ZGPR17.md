## Example

Statement: for every integer $n$, if $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd.
A student writes this argument.

1. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $n$ is odd and $n^2$ is even.
2. Since $n$ is odd, $n = 2k + 1$ for an integer $k$.
3. Then $n^2 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1$, so $n^2$ is odd.
4. This contradicts the assumption that $n^2$ is even. So $n^2$ is odd.

The argument is logically valid, but it is not a genuine [[proof by contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]].
We check the condition that fails.

1. A genuine proof by contradiction uses the assumed negation to reach the contradiction.
2. The assumed negation here is "$n^2$ is even". Steps 2 and 3 do not use it. They use only "$n$ is odd".
3. Step 3 already states the conclusion "$n^2$ is odd". The negation enters only in step 4, to say that step 3 disagrees with it.
4. Deleting the frame (the words "for a contradiction" in step 1 and the whole of step 4) leaves the [[direct proof that the square of an odd integer is odd|E330HD4J2D]].

## Remarks

The test for a disguised direct proof is simple: delete the assumed negation and see whether every step still holds.
If it does, the last step before the contradiction is already the conclusion, and the proof should be written directly.

The direct form is better for three reasons.
It is shorter.
It is constructive, because it does not use the [[law of excluded middle|0RYYK8PG4S]].
And it shows the reason for the result, here the formula $n^2 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1$, without a detour.

A genuine proof by contradiction of a similar statement is the proof that $\log_2 3$ is irrational, where the assumption gives the integers $a$ and $b$ that the rest of the proof uses.
See [[the logarithm of 3 to base 2 is irrational|8V5FDX9562]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a detective who says: "Suppose the butler is innocent; the camera shows the butler at the scene; so the butler is guilty, which contradicts my supposition."
The supposition did no work: the camera alone gave the answer.
The detective could just say: "The camera shows the butler at the scene, so the butler is guilty."
A proof by contradiction is honest only when the supposition helps to reach the impossible situation.

### L2 human

We rewrite a disguised proof as a direct proof.
Claim: if $x > 2$, then $x^2 > 4$.
Disguised proof: "Suppose $x > 2$ and $x^2 \leq 4$; since $x > 2 > 0$, multiplying $x > 2$ by $x$ and by $2$ gives $x^2 > 2x > 4$, which contradicts $x^2 \leq 4$."

1. Find the steps that use $x^2 \leq 4$. There are none before the last sentence.
2. Delete the assumption $x^2 \leq 4$ and the last sentence.
3. The direct proof is: since $x > 2 > 0$, we get $x^2 > 2x$ and $2x > 4$, so $x^2 > 4$.

The typical use of this check is in the revision of a proof: after the proof is found, the writer asks whether the contradiction frame is needed.

A trap: the opposite error also exists.
A proof that does need the assumed negation cannot be rewritten in this way, and deleting the frame breaks it.
