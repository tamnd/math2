## Example

Statement: for every integer $n$, if $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd.
A student writes this argument.

1. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $n$ is odd and $n^2$ is even.
2. Since $n$ is odd, $n = 2k + 1$ for an integer $k$.
3. Then $n^2 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1$, so $n^2$ is odd.
4. This contradicts the assumption that $n^2$ is even, so $n^2$ is odd.

The argument is valid, but it is not a genuine [[proof by contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]].
We find the condition that fails.

1. A genuine proof by contradiction uses the assumed negation on the way to the contradiction.
2. The assumed negation here is "$n^2$ is even".
   Steps 2 and 3 do not use it; they use only "$n$ is odd".
3. Step 3 already states the conclusion "$n^2$ is odd".
   The negation enters only in step 4, to say that step 3 disagrees with it.
4. Delete the frame, that is, the words "for a contradiction" and "$n^2$ is even" in step 1 and the whole of step 4.
   What is left is the [[direct proof that the square of an odd integer is odd|E330HD4J2D]].

## Remarks

The test for a disguised direct proof is short: delete the assumed negation and see whether every step still holds.
If it does, the last line before the contradiction is already the conclusion, and the proof should be written directly.

The direct form is better for three reasons.
It is shorter.
It needs no final step from "not even" to "odd", so it rests on nothing beyond the computation.
And it shows the reason for the result, the formula $n^2 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1$, with no detour.

A genuine proof by contradiction of a similar kind is the proof that [[the logarithm of 3 to base 2 is irrational|8V5FDX9562]].
There the assumption gives the integers $a$ and $b$ that the rest of the proof uses, and without it the proof cannot start.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a detective who says: "Suppose the butler is innocent; the camera shows the butler at the scene; so the butler is guilty, which contradicts my supposition."
The supposition did no work, because the camera alone gave the answer.
The detective could just say that the camera shows the butler at the scene, so the butler is guilty.
A proof by contradiction is honest only when the supposition helps to reach the impossible situation.

### L2 human

Here is a second disguised proof to rewrite.
The claim is that if $x > 2$, then $x^2 > 4$.
The disguised proof reads: "Suppose $x > 2$ and $x^2 \le 4$; since $x > 2 > 0$, multiplying $x > 2$ by $x$ and by $2$ gives $x^2 > 2x > 4$, which contradicts $x^2 \le 4$."
Look for a step that uses $x^2 \le 4$, and you find none before the last clause.
Delete that assumption and the last clause, and the direct proof remains:
$$x > 2 > 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x^2 > 2x \text{ and } 2x > 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x^2 > 4.$$
The typical use of this check is in revision: after a proof is found, the writer asks whether the contradiction frame is needed.
Watch out: a proof that does use the assumed negation cannot be rewritten in this way, and deleting its frame breaks it.
