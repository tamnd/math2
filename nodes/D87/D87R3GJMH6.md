## Summary

The Hamiltonian constraint is the equation $\mathcal{H} = 0$ that arises in a theory invariant under reparametrisation of time.
In such a theory the Hamiltonian itself must vanish on physical states.
In general relativity, in the [[ADM formalism|CFAFAPV7HB]], it is the component of the Einstein equations along the normal of a spatial slice.
It relates the scalar curvature of space to the extrinsic curvature and the energy density of matter.
In canonical quantum gravity, imposing it as an operator equation gives the [[Wheeler–DeWitt equation|TNKMDJB5FP]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In general relativity there is no external clock, and the choice of time is free.
As a consequence the total Hamiltonian is a sum of constraints, and one of them, the Hamiltonian constraint, generates the change of the time slicing.
On a spatial slice it says that intrinsic curvature, the bending of the slice inside spacetime, and the energy of matter must balance.
For instance, for a slice at a moment of time symmetry it says that the scalar curvature of space equals $16\pi$ times the energy density.
