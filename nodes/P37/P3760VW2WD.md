## Summary

Consider a body that orbits a much heavier body.
Its Hill sphere is the region around it in which its own gravity dominates the motion of small satellites.
For a body of mass $m$ on a circular orbit of radius $a$ around a body of mass $M$, its radius is about $a \sqrt[3]{m / (3M)}$.
This radius is the approximate distance from the small body to the [[Lagrange points|WDJNQVGTX0]] $L_1$ and $L_2$ of the [[circular restricted three-body problem|Y2R2QRPD1B]].
It gives a first estimate of where moons can orbit a planet and planets can orbit a star in a binary system.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Moon goes around the Earth even though the Sun pulls on it too.
Close to the Earth, the Earth's pull wins; far away, the Sun's pull wins and takes over any satellite.
The Hill sphere is the boundary of the region where the Earth wins.
For the Earth it has a radius of about $1.5$ million kilometres, roughly four times the distance to the Moon.
