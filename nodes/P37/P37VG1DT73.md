## Summary

Matsubara frequencies are the discrete frequencies that appear when quantum statistical mechanics at temperature $T$ is written in imaginary time.
In this formalism the time variable runs over an interval of length $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$, and bosonic quantities are periodic in it while fermionic quantities are antiperiodic.
Their [[Fourier series|G12KXDHPPS]] therefore use the frequencies $2\pi n / \beta$ for bosons and $(2n+1)\pi/\beta$ for fermions, $n \in \Z$.
Sums over these frequencies, computed by the [[residue theorem|032HKYS329]], produce the [[Bose–Einstein|R5HWSRAK4H]] and [[Fermi–Dirac|FM6XWC4BST]] occupation factors.

## Explanations

### L1 human

At temperature zero, quantum field theory uses ordinary time and all frequencies.
At positive temperature, a clever change of variables turns the thermal average into a problem in imaginary time, which lives on a circle of length $\beta$.
A function on a circle has a Fourier series instead of a Fourier integral, so only a discrete list of frequencies appears.
For bosons the list is $0, \pm 2\pi/\beta, \pm 4\pi/\beta, \dots$, and for fermions it is $\pm \pi/\beta, \pm 3\pi/\beta, \dots$.
As the temperature goes to zero, $\beta$ grows and the frequencies get closer, so the sums turn back into integrals.
