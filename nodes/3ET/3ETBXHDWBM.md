## Summary

The Rayleigh–Taylor instability is the instability of the interface between two fluids of different density.
It occurs when the heavier fluid lies above the lighter one in a gravity field.
More generally it occurs whenever the light fluid pushes or accelerates the heavy one.
Small ripples of the interface grow into fingers of heavy fluid falling and bubbles of light fluid rising.
It matters in [[hydrodynamic stability|H028A17Z55]], in inertial confinement fusion, in supernova explosions and in everyday mixing.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Turn a glass of water upside down with a card over it and pull the card away: the water falls out even though air pressure could in principle hold it.
The flat water surface is in balance, but the balance is unstable.
Any small bump lets some water drop and some air rise, and the bump grows.
Surface tension can hold very short ripples back, which is why water stays in a thin straw turned upside down.
