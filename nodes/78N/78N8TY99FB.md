## Summary

Atmospheric dynamics is the study of the motions of the Earth's atmosphere by the laws of [[fluid dynamics|F2JRWJNYN4]] and thermodynamics.
The atmosphere is a thin, stably stratified layer of gas on a rotating sphere, so the [[Coriolis force|6HZRX3WSQD]] and buoyancy shape its large-scale flow.
Its basic models are the [[primitive equations|QANHETP404]] and their simplifications, such as [[geostrophic balance|CZ1S1P465Y]].
It is the theoretical basis of weather forecasting and of climate models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air moves because the Sun heats it unevenly: warm air near the equator rises and cold air near the poles sinks.
On a spinning Earth, moving air is deflected sideways, so winds blow around highs and lows instead of straight into them.
Atmospheric dynamics explains with equations why we have jet streams, cyclones and trade winds.
For example, it predicts that far from the ground the wind blows almost parallel to the lines of equal pressure on a weather map.
