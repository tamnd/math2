## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a [[thermodynamic system|GYNNJBMTCP]].
The *work* $W$ done on $\Sigma$ in a process is the energy transferred to $\Sigma$ by macroscopic forces of the surroundings.
These forces act through changes of external parameters of $\Sigma$, such as its volume.
Transfers of energy as heat or with matter are not work.
For a [[quasistatic process|PVDWD7HGFG]] $\gamma$ of a fluid with pressure $p$ and volume $V$,
$$W = -\int_\gamma p \, dV.$$

## Remarks

Compress $n$ moles of ideal gas quasistatically at constant temperature $T$ from volume $V_1$ to $V_2 < V_1$.
Then $p = nRT/V$, and $W = -\int_{V_1}^{V_2} nRT \, dV / V = nRT \ln(V_1 / V_2) > 0$.
Heating a gas in a rigid container is not work: the volume does not change, and the energy enters as heat.
Some texts write $W$ for the work done by the system, which changes the sign.
