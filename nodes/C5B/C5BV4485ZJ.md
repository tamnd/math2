## Definition

Let $\varphi \colon \R^d \to \R \cup \set{+\infty}$ be an even pair potential, so that two particles at positions $x$ and $y$ interact with energy $\varphi(x - y)$.
Let $\beta > 0$ be the inverse temperature.
The *Mayer $f$-function* is
$$f(x) = e^{-\beta \varphi(x)} - 1, \qquad x \in \R^d,$$
with the [[exponential|0J0FCWV32D]] convention $e^{-\infty} = 0$.
One writes $f_{ij} = f(x_i - x_j)$ for particles $i$ and $j$.

## Remarks

Always $f \ge -1$, and $f(x) \to 0$ as $\abs{x} \to \infty$ when $\varphi(x) \to 0$.
Example: the hard-sphere potential in $\R^3$, with $\varphi(x) = +\infty$ for $\abs{x} < \sigma$ and $\varphi(x) = 0$ otherwise, gives $f = -\mathbf{1}_{\set{\abs{x} < \sigma}}$.
Its second virial coefficient is $B_2 = -\frac{1}{2} \int_{\R^3} f(x) \, dx = \frac{2 \pi \sigma^3}{3}$.
Non-example: the Boltzmann factor $e^{-\beta \varphi}$ itself is not the Mayer function; it tends to $1$ rather than $0$ at large distances.
The identity $\prod_{i < j} e^{-\beta \varphi(x_i - x_j)} = \prod_{i < j} (1 + f_{ij})$ expands into a sum over graphs on the particles.
