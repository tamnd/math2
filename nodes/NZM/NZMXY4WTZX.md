## Summary

A symmetric cone is an open [[convex cone|6VE4KA12VD]] in a finite-dimensional real inner product space with two properties.
It equals its own dual cone, and its linear symmetries act transitively on it.
The positive orthant, the Lorentz cone and the cone of positive definite symmetric matrices are the basic examples.
By the [[Koecher–Vinberg theorem|PXXEWA9V2S]], symmetric cones are exactly the cones of squares of [[Euclidean Jordan algebras|N184N4TVZ2]].
They are the natural domains of semidefinite and second-order cone programming and of harmonic analysis on cones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of the first quadrant of the plane: it is an open wedge, and it looks the same from every point inside it.
A stretch of the two axes by positive factors moves any point of the quadrant to any other, and maps the quadrant onto itself.
The quadrant is also its own dual: a vector has positive inner product with every nonzero point of the closed quadrant exactly when it lies in the open quadrant.
A symmetric cone is a cone with these two properties in any dimension.
The set of positive definite $n \times n$ symmetric matrices is a more interesting example of the same kind.
