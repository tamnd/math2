## Summary

Stress is force per unit area acting across an imagined surface inside a [[continuum body|X01DZTS224]].
At each point and for each orientation of the surface it gives a vector, the traction, with a normal part and a shear part.
Stress is how internal forces are described in continuum mechanics, and the condition that it stays below a limit governs the design of structures.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cut a loaded bar in your mind: the two halves push or pull on each other across the cut.
Stress is that force divided by the area of the cut.
A rod of cross-section $2\ \text{cm}^2$ carrying a weight of $1000$ N has a tensile stress of $500$ N per $\text{cm}^2$.
If the cut is slanted, the force across it has a part perpendicular to the cut, the normal stress, and a part along it, the shear stress.
