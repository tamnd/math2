## Summary

Quantum field theory (QFT) is the framework that combines quantum mechanics with special relativity by quantizing fields instead of particles.
Particles appear as excitations of the fields, and their number can change.
The [[Fock space|1TJ8TNH331]] of free particles is the simplest example of its state spaces.
It is the language of the Standard Model of particle physics and of much of condensed matter physics.
Its computations are organized by [[path integrals|1NYTFT5NPE]], Feynman diagrams and renormalization.
Mathematically, axiom systems such as the [[Wightman axioms|5QTZQV6TNG]] and the [[Haag–Kastler axioms|MGBCX7FH2G]] make the notion precise.
No interacting theory in four spacetime dimensions is yet known to satisfy them.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In ordinary quantum mechanics the number of particles is fixed, but in high-energy collisions particles are created and destroyed.
Quantum field theory explains this by treating fields, such as the electromagnetic field, as the basic objects.
A field is like an infinite collection of coupled oscillators, one at every point of space.
Quantizing these oscillators gives discrete packets of energy, and these packets are the particles, such as photons.
Its predictions, for instance of the magnetic moment of the electron, agree with experiment to many digits.
