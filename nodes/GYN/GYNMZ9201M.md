## Summary

The Boltzmann distribution gives a state of energy $\varepsilon$ a probability proportional to $e^{-\varepsilon / (k_B T)}$, where $T$ is the temperature and $k_B$ the Boltzmann constant.
Low-energy states are favoured, and the more so the lower the temperature.
It is the distribution of a system in equilibrium with a heat bath, and applied to whole systems it is the [[canonical ensemble|G1P1A0N7NZ]].
Among all distributions with a given mean energy it is the one of largest entropy.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Air is thinner at the top of a mountain.
A molecule high up has more potential energy, and the Boltzmann factor $e^{-\varepsilon / (k_B T)}$ makes such states less likely.
The ratio of the probabilities of two states depends only on the difference of their energies.
For example, if a level lies $k_B T$ above another, it is $e \approx 2.7$ times less likely.
The [[Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution|3QSVWQJX11]] of molecular speeds is one instance.
