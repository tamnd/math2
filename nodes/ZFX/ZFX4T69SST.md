## Summary

The Hicksian demand function gives the cheapest bundle of goods that reaches a fixed level of utility at given prices.
It is the solution of the [[expenditure minimization problem|Y0M5J89V2Q]], seen as a function of the prices and the utility level.
It isolates the substitution effect of a price change, because the consumer is kept equally well off.
It is also called compensated demand, and it is the gradient of the expenditure function with respect to prices.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose the price of coffee rises, and the consumer is given just enough extra money to be as happy as before.
The basket they then buy is the Hicksian demand at the new prices.
Since they are equally happy, the change in their basket comes only from coffee being dearer relative to tea, not from feeling poorer.
For this reason the Hicksian demand for a good never rises when its own price rises.
