## Summary

This topic is a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]].
It covers classical propositional logic as a formal system, with its formal language, valuations, normal forms, functional completeness, deductive systems, and the theorems of soundness, completeness and compactness.
It also treats the proof systems for automated reasoning on clauses, resolution and the Tseitin transformation, and the interpolation theorem.
The connectives, truth tables and the elementary laws of logic at school level are in the topic [[propositions, connectives and quantifiers|PM1AYWABA7]].

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Propositional calculus|09K9MWD2QQ]]: the formal system as a whole, its language, semantics and proof theory.
2. [[Propositional formula|~7139c138]]: the formulas, defined by induction from variables and connectives.
3. [[Unique readability theorem|~26f00b05]]: each formula is built in exactly one way.
4. [[Truth valuation|~41f42e3d]]: the unique extension of a truth assignment to all formulas.
5. [[Logical consequence|1HK0VYWTV7]]: the relation $\Gamma \models \varphi$.
6. [[Satisfiability|QN7M1JCGT9]]: sets of formulas that some assignment makes true.
7. [[Equisatisfiability|B37TYWXS74]]: formulas that are satisfiable together or not at all.
8. [[Conjunctive normal form|0B3D4C8YF2]]: conjunctions of clauses, and the conversion to them.
9. [[Disjunctive normal form|0GVDFAY4KW]]: disjunctions of conjunctions of literals, read off a truth table.
10. [[Clause|T3SXVH3ZZC]]: finite sets of literals, read as disjunctions.
11. [[Horn clause|QR92EZDTKF]]: clauses with at most one positive literal, and their fast satisfiability test.
12. [[Functional completeness|TDZVCWYJ9F]]: sets of connectives that express every truth function.
13. [[Post's functional completeness theorem|WGBR7JHBV4]]: the five classes that decide functional completeness.
14. [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]]: the axiom system L with modus ponens.
15. [[Deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]]: from $\Gamma, \varphi \vdash \psi$ to $\Gamma \vdash \varphi \to \psi$.
16. [[Peirce's law|~70d00c19]]: a classical tautology in implication alone.
17. [[Implicational propositional calculus|ZMVBAY7ZZD]]: the fragment with implication as the only connective.
18. [[Single axioms for propositional logic|~f2d74783]]: one axiom schema with one rule.
19. [[Independence of axioms|~2d77c15c]]: the method of many-valued truth tables.
20. [[Soundness theorem for propositional logic|~7a1817da]]: every derivable formula is a consequence.
21. [[Completeness theorem for propositional logic|~e697b103]]: every consequence is derivable.
22. [[Compactness theorem for propositional logic|~205aa33d]]: satisfiability is decided by finite subsets.
23. [[Applications of propositional compactness|~ec805984]]: colourings, orders and other infinite combinatorics.
24. [[Resolution|~99ab3fe3]]: a proof system with one rule on clauses.
25. [[Tseitin transformation|~f66d5ee5]]: a linear-size equisatisfiable CNF.
26. [[Craig interpolation for propositional logic|~03163f49]]: an implication explained in the shared variables.

### Prerequisites

- The connectives, truth tables, tautologies and logical equivalence, from the topic [[propositions, connectives and quantifiers|PM1AYWABA7]].
- Formal languages, formal systems and rules of inference, from the topic [[logic, formal systems and foundations|H8QZY0RZXZ]].
- Proofs by induction, in particular [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]], and the habits of proof from the area [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].
- For the compactness theorem on uncountable sets of variables: [[Zorn's lemma|04RXTQA5ZA]] or [[Tychonoff's theorem|0JTRSJKCFB]].
