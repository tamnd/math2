## Summary

Write down $p \to q$, give $p$ and $q$ every pair of truth values, and you get four rows; the row with $p = 1$ and $q = 0$ is the only false one.
The propositional calculus starts from tables like this one and turns them into a complete theory of reasoning with "not", "and", "or" and "if ... then", as a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]].

The story has three parts.
First, formulas become exact objects, built by induction and read in only one way, and a truth assignment gives each formula one value.
From this come logical consequence, satisfiability and the tests that decide them.

Second, every formula can be put into a normal form, such as a conjunction of clauses, and a small set of connectives, even the single connective NAND, can express every truth function.
New variables give short equisatisfiable clause forms, and this is how SAT solvers receive their input.

Third, a proof system with three axiom schemas and one rule derives exactly the tautologies.
The soundness and completeness theorems say this, compactness carries it to infinite sets of premises, and resolution gives a second proof system that works by refutation on clauses.

The connectives, truth tables and elementary laws at school level are in the topic [[propositions, connectives and quantifiers|PM1AYWABA7]].

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Propositional calculus|09K9MWD2QQ]]: you see the whole system, its language, its semantics and its proofs, in one place.
2. [[Propositional formula|TT7WT5HPN9]]: you learn to build formulas by induction and to prove facts about all of them.
3. [[Unique readability theorem|6KBJX91N7G]]: you learn why each formula has one main connective, so that definitions by recursion make sense.
4. [[Truth valuation|H31CS3HK5P]]: you learn to compute the value of any formula from the values of its variables.
5. [[Logical consequence|1HK0VYWTV7]]: you learn to test whether a conclusion follows by hunting for a countermodel.
6. [[Satisfiability|QN7M1JCGT9]]: you learn to reduce consequence, validity and equivalence to one question.
7. [[Equisatisfiability|B37TYWXS74]]: you learn what a clause conversion keeps and what it loses.
8. [[Conjunctive normal form|0B3D4C8YF2]]: you learn to convert any formula into a conjunction of clauses step by step.
9. [[Disjunctive normal form|0GVDFAY4KW]]: you learn to read a formula off its truth table.
10. [[Clause|T3SXVH3ZZC]]: you get the set notation that resolution and SAT solvers use.
11. [[Horn clause|QR92EZDTKF]]: you get a class of clause sets with a fast satisfiability test.
12. [[Functional completeness|TDZVCWYJ9F]]: you learn to show that a set of connectives, such as NAND alone, expresses every truth function.
13. [[Post's functional completeness theorem|WGBR7JHBV4]]: you get five tests that decide functional completeness for any set of connectives.
14. [[Hilbert system|1MGMQX43B8]]: you learn to write a formal derivation, starting with the five lines that derive $p \to p$.
15. [[Deduction theorem|9X08XK5RV2]]: you learn to shorten derivations by assuming the antecedent.
16. [[Peirce's law|GMSZ75D5WE]]: you see a classical tautology in implication alone that intuitionistic logic rejects.
17. [[Implicational propositional calculus|ZMVBAY7ZZD]]: you see how much logic implication alone can carry.
18. [[Single axioms for propositional logic|644XCQAC8C]]: you see that one axiom schema with one rule can be enough.
19. [[Independence of axioms|729RCBPAM3]]: you learn to show that no axiom follows from the others, with many-valued tables.
20. [[Soundness theorem for propositional logic|XD629S54CS]]: you learn why every derivable formula is a tautology.
21. [[Completeness theorem for propositional logic|81ZNY1XKYJ]]: you learn why every tautology is derivable.
22. [[Compactness theorem for propositional logic|4X9126SEMM]]: you learn to pass from finite sets of formulas to infinite ones.
23. [[Applications of propositional compactness|S7SW0CZZ9V]]: you see compactness prove facts about colorings and orders of infinite sets.
24. [[Resolution|ZDW6N1PGJ7]]: you learn to refute a clause set, such as a set of three clauses, with one rule.
25. [[Tseitin transformation|S367PDDP61]]: you learn to give any formula to a SAT solver at linear size.
26. [[Craig interpolation for propositional logic|TRS2HY57EG]]: you learn to explain an implication by a formula in the shared variables.

### Prerequisites

- The connectives, truth tables, tautologies and logical equivalence, from the topic [[propositions, connectives and quantifiers|PM1AYWABA7]].
- Formal languages, formal systems and rules of inference, from the topic [[logic, formal systems and foundations|H8QZY0RZXZ]].
- Proofs by induction, in particular [[structural induction|12MQ2NJ29A]], and the habits of proof from the area [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].
- For the compactness theorem on uncountable sets of variables, [[Zorn's lemma|04RXTQA5ZA]] or [[Tychonoff's theorem|0JTRSJKCFB]].
