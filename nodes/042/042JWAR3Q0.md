## Statement

Let $F$ be a field and let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $F$ with $n \ge 1$.
Then there are unique monic nonconstant polynomials $d_1, \dots, d_r \in F[x]$ with $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$ such that $A$ is [[similar|027KDW2K72]] to the [[block diagonal matrix|0M0SNKZ482]]
$$R = C(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus C(d_r) = \begin{pmatrix} C(d_1) & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & C(d_r) \end{pmatrix},$$
where $C(d_i)$ is the [[companion matrix|0VH1TC1PWF]] of $d_i$.
The matrix $R$ is the *rational canonical form*, or *Frobenius normal form*, of $A$.
The $d_i$ are the [[invariant factors|~bcfda459]] of $A$; $d_r$ is the minimal polynomial and $d_1 \cdots d_r$ the characteristic polynomial of $A$.
Two matrices in $M_n(F)$ are similar if and only if they have the same rational canonical form.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The divisibility $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$ is needed for uniqueness: $C(x - 1) \oplus C(x - 2) = \operatorname{diag}(1, 2)$ and $C((x - 1)(x - 2)) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ are similar block matrices of companion matrices, and only the second meets the condition.
The $d_i$ must be nonconstant, or blocks of size $0$ would appear.
No hypothesis on $F$ is needed: the theorem holds over every field, and no roots of polynomials are used.

### Consequences

- Similarity in $M_n(F)$ is decided by a finite computation over $F$: compute the invariant factors, for example by the [[Smith normal form of $xI - A$|~57adc122]].
- If $A, B \in M_n(K)$ are similar over a field $L \supseteq K$, they are [[similar over $K$|~a2ce4e28]].
- Every matrix is [[similar to its transpose|~762fdc0e]].
- A matrix is cyclic if and only if $r = 1$.

### Converse

Every block matrix $C(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus C(d_r)$ with monic nonconstant $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$ is the rational canonical form of itself.
So every such list with $\sum \deg d_i = n$ occurs as the list of invariant factors of some $n \times n$ matrix.

### Generalizations

The elementary divisors give a second normal form, $\bigoplus C(p^e)$ over the prime powers $p^e$; it is unique up to the order of the blocks.
A further variant, the primary rational canonical form or generalized Jordan form, replaces $C(p^e)$ by a block matrix with $C(p)$ on the diagonal and a matrix unit above it.
This form generalizes the Jordan form to fields where $\chi_A$ does not split.
Over a principal ideal domain other than $F[x]$, the same module theory classifies finitely generated torsion modules.

## History

The form is named after the German mathematician Ferdinand Georg Frobenius.
The word "rational" refers to the fact that the form is found without operations that change under an extension of the field, in particular without factoring polynomials.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rational canonical form is a standard representative of each similarity class, over any field.
It is built from companion matrices, one for each invariant factor.
It needs no roots of polynomials, only division of polynomials.
Two matrices are the same map in different coordinates exactly when their rational canonical forms are equal.

### L2 human

Find the rational canonical form of $A = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 2)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

1. The elementary divisors are $x - 1$, $x - 1$, $x - 2$.
2. The invariant factors are $d_1 = x - 1$ and $d_2 = (x - 1)(x - 2) = x^2 - 3x + 2$.
3. $C(d_1) = (1)$ and $C(d_2) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. So $R = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
5. A transition matrix: take $v_1 = e_1$ and $v_2 = e_2 + e_3$; then $P$ has columns $v_1$, $v_2 = (0, 1, 1)$, $A v_2 = (0, 1, 2)$, and $AP = PR$.

The typical use is to decide similarity over a field where the characteristic polynomial does not split, such as $\mathbb{Q}$.
The trap is to build a block for each eigenvalue: $C(x - 1) \oplus C(x - 1) \oplus C(x - 2)$ is diagonal, but it fails the divisibility rule and is not the rational canonical form.

### L3 human

The rational canonical form is the matrix form of the invariant factor decomposition $F^n_A \cong \bigoplus F[x]/(d_i)$ of the [[$F[x]$-module of $A$|~772e5994]].
It is canonical in the strict sense: one matrix per similarity class, chosen with no choices, so it can serve as a normal form in computer algebra.
Because it is defined over the field of the entries, it gives the invariance of similarity under field extension and the classification of [[conjugacy classes of $GL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$|~82afa2e3]].
