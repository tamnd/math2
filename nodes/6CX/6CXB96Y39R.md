## Summary

The Richardson number is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] that compares the stabilising effect of a stable density stratification with the destabilising effect of velocity shear.
In its gradient form it is the square of the [[Brunt–Väisälä frequency|XQ82TE42FH]] divided by the square of the shear.
When it is large, buoyancy suppresses overturning and turbulence; when it is small, shear can roll the layers up into [[Kelvin–Helmholtz billows|7PJ97MHYTP]].
The [[Miles–Howard theorem|PAFD50PFXX]] makes the threshold $1/4$ precise for inviscid parallel flows.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Wind blowing over a layer of cold, heavy air tries to stir it up; the heaviness of the cold air resists being lifted.
The Richardson number compares these two tendencies.
When it is large, the layers slide past each other smoothly; when it is small, the shear wins and the boundary curls into waves and turbulence.
Pilots meet the result as clear-air turbulence where strong shear meets weak stratification.
