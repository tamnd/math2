## Example

Consider an infinite thin plate of homogeneous isotropic linear elastic material in [[plane stress|AS7WQTM99W]], with a circular hole of radius $a > 0$ centred at the origin.
The edge of the hole is free of traction, and far from the hole the plate is under uniform tension $s$ in the $x$ direction.
In [[polar coordinates|0BSW4T7THS]] $(r, \theta)$, $r \ge a$, the Kirsch solution for the [[stress|KG2XWEMJ30]] is
$$\sigma_{rr} = \frac{s}{2} \left( 1 - \frac{a^2}{r^2} \right) + \frac{s}{2} \left( 1 - \frac{4a^2}{r^2} + \frac{3a^4}{r^4} \right) \cos 2\theta,$$
$$\sigma_{\theta\theta} = \frac{s}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{a^2}{r^2} \right) - \frac{s}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{3a^4}{r^4} \right) \cos 2\theta,$$
$$\sigma_{r\theta} = -\frac{s}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{2a^2}{r^2} - \frac{3a^4}{r^4} \right) \sin 2\theta.$$

Check of the boundary conditions: at $r = a$, $\sigma_{rr} = \frac{s}{2} \cdot 0 + \frac{s}{2}(1 - 4 + 3) \cos 2\theta = 0$ and $\sigma_{r\theta} = -\frac{s}{2}(1 + 2 - 3) \sin 2\theta = 0$, so the hole is traction-free.
As $r \to \infty$, $\sigma_{rr} \to \frac{s}{2}(1 + \cos 2\theta) = s \cos^2 \theta$, $\sigma_{\theta\theta} \to s \sin^2 \theta$ and $\sigma_{r\theta} \to -s \sin \theta \cos \theta$, which are the polar components of the uniform tension $\sigma_{xx} = s$.
The fields come from an [[Airy stress function|7ZGSJRYBGK]] that is biharmonic, so equilibrium and compatibility hold.

At the edge of the hole, $\sigma_{\theta\theta}(a, \theta) = s (1 - 2 \cos 2\theta)$.
Its maximum is $3s$, at $\theta = \pm \pi/2$, the points of the hole on the axis across the load.
At $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$ it equals $-s$, a compression.
So the stress concentration factor of a circular hole is $3$, whatever its radius.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Drill a small round hole in a metal strip and pull on the strip.
The stress flows around the hole like water around a stone, and it crowds at the two sides of the hole.
There the stress is three times the stress far away.
This is why cracks in pulled plates often start at the sides of holes, and why engineers round off corners and avoid sharp notches.
