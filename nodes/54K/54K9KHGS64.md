## Definition

Let $\mathfrak n$ and $\mathfrak h$ be [[Lie algebras|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over a field $k$.
Let $\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak n)$ be the Lie algebra of [[derivations|57J7MHA8CN]] of $\mathfrak n$.
Let $\varphi \colon \mathfrak h \to \operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak n)$ be a [[Lie algebra homomorphism|H5D71JE8TA]].
The *semidirect product* $\mathfrak n \rtimes_\varphi \mathfrak h$ is the vector space $\mathfrak n \oplus \mathfrak h$ with the bracket
$$[(n_1, h_1), (n_2, h_2)] = \bigl([n_1, n_2] + \varphi(h_1) n_2 - \varphi(h_2) n_1,\ [h_1, h_2]\bigr)$$
for $n_1, n_2 \in \mathfrak n$ and $h_1, h_2 \in \mathfrak h$.

## Remarks

This bracket is bilinear and alternating, and the Jacobi identity holds because $\varphi$ is a homomorphism into derivations.
The subspace $\mathfrak n \oplus 0$ is an ideal, $0 \oplus \mathfrak h$ is a subalgebra, and $[(0, h), (n, 0)] = (\varphi(h) n, 0)$.

Example: let $\mathfrak n = \mathfrak h = k$ with zero brackets and $\varphi(t)(s) = ts$.
Put $x = (0, 1)$ and $y = (1, 0)$.
Then $[x, y] = (\varphi(1) 1, 0) = y$, so $k \rtimes_\varphi k$ is the nonabelian $2$-dimensional Lie algebra.

Non-example: if $\varphi \colon \mathfrak h \to \operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak n)$ is linear but not a homomorphism, the formula still defines an alternating bilinear bracket, but the Jacobi identity fails for some triple $(0, h_1), (0, h_2), (n, 0)$.

If $\varphi = 0$, the semidirect product is the direct sum $\mathfrak n \oplus \mathfrak h$.
Other notations are $\mathfrak h \ltimes_\varphi \mathfrak n$ and $\mathfrak n \rtimes \mathfrak h$.
