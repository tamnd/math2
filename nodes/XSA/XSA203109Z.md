## Summary

An order parameter is a quantity that vanishes in the disordered, symmetric phase of a system and is nonzero in its ordered phase.
It is usually the average of an observable that changes under a symmetry of the Hamiltonian.
The magnetization per site of the [[Ising model|R0KGM32QN6]] is the standard example.
Order parameters classify phases, and their behaviour near a transition defines several [[critical exponents|SQ41AZGZ2P]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Water and ice have the same molecules, but ice is ordered and water is not.
An order parameter is a number that measures how much order there is: $0$ in the disordered phase, and not $0$ in the ordered phase.
For a magnet, it is the average direction of the tiny atomic magnets.
Above the Curie temperature they point every way and the average is $0$.
Below it most of them line up, and the average becomes a nonzero vector.
