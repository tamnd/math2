## Definition

A particle of charge $q$ and mass $m$ moves in a uniform [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] $B$ at speed $v$ near the speed of light $c$.
So its [[Lorentz factor|2E9AJVZS6E]] $\gamma = (1 - v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ satisfies $\gamma \gg 1$.
Under the Lorentz force the particle moves on a helix around the field direction.
Its $\gamma$ and its pitch angle $\alpha$ between velocity and field stay constant.
*Synchrotron radiation* is the [[electromagnetic radiation|AC6R5A3M1C]] emitted by the particle because of this acceleration.

## Remarks

Example: for an electron of charge $-e$ and mass $m_e$, the radiated power averaged over isotropic pitch angles is $P = \frac{4}{3} \sigma_T c \beta^2 \gamma^2 U_B$.
Here $\beta = v/c$, $\sigma_T$ is the Thomson cross-section and $U_B = B^2/(2\mu_0)$ is the energy density of the field in SI units.
The spectrum of one electron peaks near the critical frequency $\nu_c = \frac{3}{2} \gamma^2 \nu_B \sin\alpha$, where $\nu_B = eB/(2\pi m_e)$ is the non-relativistic gyrofrequency.
If the electrons have an energy distribution $N(E) \propto E^{-p}$, the emitted spectrum in the optically thin regime is $F_\nu \propto \nu^{-(p-1)/2}$.
Non-example: radiation of slow electrons with $\gamma \approx 1$ in a magnetic field is called cyclotron radiation; it is emitted at the gyrofrequency and its harmonics, not over a broad band.
