## Summary

The competitive Lotka–Volterra equations model species that compete for common resources.
Each species grows logistically up to its own [[carrying capacity|X23CRWHRCH]], and the other species use up part of that capacity, in proportion to their numbers.
They are a special case of the [[generalized Lotka–Volterra equation|4SFBZ8FJF1]], and they explain when competing species coexist and when one of them excludes the others.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two kinds of grass grow on the same field.
Each would fill the field alone, but they share the water and the light.
In the model, each plant of the second kind takes up the room of $\alpha_{12}$ plants of the first kind, and conversely.
If each kind hurts itself more than the other, they can live together; if not, one of them usually takes over.
