## Example

Let $V = C[-1, 1]$ be the space of real continuous functions on $[-1, 1]$, an [[inner product space|0QVNCJXRY3]] with
$$\ip{f}{g} = \int_{-1}^{1} f(x) g(x) \, dx.$$
Let $U_d$ be the subspace of polynomials of degree at most $d$.
By the [[best approximation theorem|0MCDDSCB9Z]], the polynomial $p \in U_d$ that makes $\int_{-1}^{1} (f - p)^2 \, dx$ as small as possible is the [[orthogonal projection|0B8KZRT28M]] of $f$ onto $U_d$.
To compute it, we first need an orthogonal basis of $U_d$.

Orthogonal polynomials by Gram-Schmidt.
Odd powers times even powers integrate to $0$ over $[-1, 1]$, and $\int_{-1}^{1} x^{m} \, dx = \frac{2}{m + 1}$ for even $m$.

1. The [[Gram-Schmidt process|0VT6P0GNQR]] starts with $q_0 = 1$, and $\ip{x}{1} = 0$ gives $q_1 = x$ unchanged.
2. For $x^2$ we remove its parts along $1$ and $x$, using $\ip{x^2}{1} = \frac{2}{3}$, $\ip{1}{1} = 2$ and $\ip{x^2}{x} = 0$:
$$q_2 = x^2 - \frac{2/3}{2} \cdot 1 = x^2 - \frac{1}{3}.$$
3. For $x^3$ only the part along $x$ survives, with $\ip{x^3}{x} = \frac{2}{5}$ and $\ip{x}{x} = \frac{2}{3}$:
$$q_3 = x^3 - \frac{2/5}{2/3} \, x = x^3 - \frac{3}{5} x.$$
4. Scaling each $q_n$ so that its value at $1$ is $1$ gives the [[Legendre polynomials|QFYGJVA996]]
$$P_0 = 1, \quad P_1 = x, \quad P_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(3x^2 - 1), \quad P_3 = \tfrac{1}{2}(5x^3 - 3x).$$
5. Their squared norms are $\norm{P_n}^2 = \frac{2}{2n + 1}$; for example $\norm{P_2}^2 = \frac{1}{4} \int_{-1}^{1} (9x^4 - 6x^2 + 1) \, dx = \frac{1}{4} \left( \frac{18}{5} - 4 + 2 \right) = \frac{2}{5}$.

So $P_0, \dots, P_d$ is an orthogonal basis of $U_d$, and the orthogonal-basis formula of the [[projection formulas|AK3JJ5HHG2]] gives
$$p = \sum_{n=0}^{d} c_n P_n, \qquad c_n = \frac{\ip{f}{P_n}}{\ip{P_n}{P_n}}, \qquad \norm{f - p}^2 = \norm{f}^2 - \sum_{n=0}^{d} c_n^2 \norm{P_n}^2.$$

The best quadratic for $\abs{x}$.

6. Take $f(x) = \abs{x}$ and $d = 2$; then $\ip{f}{P_0} = \int_{-1}^{1} \abs{x} \, dx = 1$, and $\ip{f}{P_1} = 0$ because $\abs{x} \cdot x$ is odd.
7. Since $\abs{x} P_2$ is even, $\ip{f}{P_2} = 2 \int_0^1 x \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}(3x^2 - 1) \, dx = \int_0^1 (3x^3 - x) \, dx = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$.
8. So $c_0 = \frac{1}{2}$, $c_1 = 0$ and $c_2 = \frac{1/4}{2/5} = \frac{5}{8}$, and
$$p(x) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{3x^2 - 1}{2} = \frac{3}{16} + \frac{15}{16} x^2.$$
9. With $\norm{f}^2 = \int_{-1}^{1} x^2 \, dx = \frac{2}{3}$, the squared error is
$$\frac{2}{3} - \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 2 - \left( \frac{5}{8} \right)^2 \cdot \frac{2}{5} = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{2} - \frac{5}{32} = \frac{64 - 48 - 15}{96} = \frac{1}{96}.$$
10. For comparison, the quadratic $x^2$ has $\int_{-1}^{1} (\abs{x} - x^2)^2 \, dx = 2 \left( \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{5} \right) = \frac{1}{15}$, which is more than six times as large.

Two more projections.

11. Take $f(x) = x^3$ and $d = 2$; since $x^3 = \frac{2}{5} P_3 + \frac{3}{5} P_1$ and $P_3 \perp U_2$, the projection is $p(x) = \frac{3}{5} x$, with squared error $\norm{\frac{2}{5} P_3}^2 = \frac{4}{25} \cdot \frac{2}{7} = \frac{8}{175}$.
12. Take $f(x) = e^x$ and $d = 1$; then $\ip{f}{P_0} = e - e^{-1}$ and $\ip{f}{P_1} = \bigl[(x - 1) e^x\bigr]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{2}{e}$, so $c_0 = \sinh 1 \approx 1.1752$ and $c_1 = \frac{2/e}{2/3} = \frac{3}{e} \approx 1.1036$.
13. With $\norm{e^x}^2 = \frac{e^2 - e^{-2}}{2}$ and $2 \sinh^2 1 = \frac{e^2 - 2 + e^{-2}}{2}$, the squared error of the line $p(x) = \sinh 1 + \frac{3}{e} x$ is
$$\frac{e^2 - e^{-2}}{2} - \frac{e^2 - 2 + e^{-2}}{2} - \frac{9}{e^2} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = 1 - \frac{1}{e^2} - \frac{6}{e^2} = 1 - \frac{7}{e^2} \approx 0.0527.$$
14. The Taylor line $1 + x$ has squared error $\int_{-1}^{1} (e^x - 1 - x)^2 \, dx = \sinh 2 + \frac{8}{3} - 2(e + e^{-1}) \approx 0.1212$, more than twice as large.

Fourier coefficients as projections.

15. Let $W = C[-\pi, \pi]$ with $\ip{f}{g} = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f g \, dx$; the functions $1, \cos x, \sin x, \dots, \cos Nx, \sin Nx$ are pairwise orthogonal, with $\norm{1}^2 = 2\pi$ and $\norm{\cos kx}^2 = \norm{\sin kx}^2 = \pi$ for $k \ge 1$.
16. The same formula projects $f$ onto the [[trigonometric polynomials|J3NJQ5FZJ5]] of degree at most $N$, and gives $\frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^{N} (a_k \cos kx + b_k \sin kx)$ with $a_k = \frac{1}{\pi} \ip{f}{\cos kx}$ and $b_k = \frac{1}{\pi} \ip{f}{\sin kx}$, the [[Fourier coefficients|5NN2YZZR3V]].
17. For $f(x) = x$, which is odd, every $a_k$ is $0$, and integration by parts gives $b_k = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} x \sin kx \, dx = \frac{2(-1)^{k+1}}{k}$, so $b_1 = 2$ and $b_2 = -1$.
18. With $\norm{x}^2 = \frac{2\pi^3}{3}$, the squared error is $\frac{2\pi^3}{3} - \pi \sum_{k=1}^{N} b_k^2$, which is $\frac{2\pi^3}{3} - 4\pi \approx 8.1045$ for $N = 1$ and $\frac{2\pi^3}{3} - 5\pi \approx 4.9629$ for $N = 2$.

## Remarks

The example shows why an orthogonal basis is worth the work of Gram-Schmidt.
Each coefficient $c_n$ comes from one integral, on its own.
To pass from degree $d$ to degree $d + 1$ we add one term and keep the old coefficients, while with the basis $1, x, x^2, \dots$ we would have to solve a new linear system each time.
The squared error can only go down as $d$ grows, because $U_d \subseteq U_{d+1}$ and the projection onto a larger subspace is at least as close.

The best approximation on the whole interval is not the Taylor polynomial.
Taylor polynomials are best near $0$, while the projection balances the error over all of $[-1, 1]$, which is why the line in step 13 beats $1 + x$.
For $\abs{x}$ the projection even misses at $0$: $p(0) = \frac{3}{16}$ while $\abs{0} = 0$.

In step 18, $\pi \sum_k b_k^2 \le \norm{f}^2$ is [[Bessel's inequality|0RBT5PB74K]].
For $f(x) = x$ the errors tend to $0$ by the [[mean-square convergence of Fourier series|3DGHC142FT]], so equality holds in the limit, which is [[Parseval's identity|047RFCNVSK]]:
$$4\pi \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{1}{k^2} = \frac{2\pi^3}{3}, \qquad \text{so} \qquad \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{1}{k^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}.$$
The general theory of [[Fourier series|G12KXDHPPS]] and of [[orthogonal polynomials|8TXASARQN1]] belongs to analysis.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a space of functions, the integral of a product plays the role of the dot product.
With it, the best approximation of a function by a polynomial, in the sense of the average squared error, is an orthogonal projection.
For example, the best parabola for $\abs{x}$ on $[-1, 1]$ is $\frac{3}{16} + \frac{15}{16} x^2$, and with an orthogonal basis each of its coefficients comes from one integral.

### L2 human

The method has three steps: choose an orthogonal basis of the approximating space, compute $c_n = \ip{f}{P_n} / \ip{P_n}{P_n}$, and add up.
The error formula $\norm{f}^2 - \sum_n c_n^2 \norm{P_n}^2$ then checks the result without one more integral of $(f - p)^2$.
This is how functions are approximated on a whole interval in signal processing and in numerical analysis.
Watch out: the Legendre polynomials are orthogonal but not orthonormal, so the division by $\ip{P_n}{P_n} = \frac{2}{2n + 1}$ is needed.
Without it, the coefficient of $P_2$ for $\abs{x}$ would be $\frac{1}{4}$ instead of $\frac{5}{8}$.
