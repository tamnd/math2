## Problem

Let $(V, E)$ be a finite [[directed graph|0KFYGSHHF5]].
Each edge $e \in E$ has a capacity $u_e \in [0, \infty)$ and a cost per unit of flow $a_e \in \R$.
Each vertex $v$ has a supply $b_v \in \R$, with $\sum_{v \in V} b_v = 0$; a negative supply is a demand.
A *feasible flow* is a function $f \colon E \to \R$ such that:

1. $0 \le f_e \le u_e$ for every edge $e$.
2. $\sum_{e \text{ leaves } v} f_e - \sum_{e \text{ enters } v} f_e = b_v$ for every vertex $v$.

Find a feasible flow that minimizes the total cost $\sum_{e \in E} a_e f_e$, or decide that no feasible flow exists.

## Remarks

The problem is a linear program, and its constraint matrix is the incidence matrix of a directed graph, which is totally unimodular.
Hence, if all $u_e$ and $b_v$ are integers and an optimal flow exists, there is an integral optimal flow.
A feasible flow is optimal exactly when its residual network has no directed cycle of negative cost.
This gives the cycle-canceling algorithm; the [[network simplex algorithm|7NT7MRVZSQ]] and successive shortest path methods also solve it, and Éva Tardos gave the first strongly polynomial algorithm in 1985.

Many problems are special cases.
The [[shortest path problem|WE864PFF2M]] is the case of one unit of supply at $s$, one unit of demand at $t$ and unbounded capacities, when no cycle has negative cost.
The [[maximum flow problem|EXMR7RYRTP]], the [[transportation problem|2B9QWY4APJ]] and the [[assignment problem|K65YS73SBW]] are also special cases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Goods must be shipped from factories to shops through a road network.
Each road can carry only so much and charges a price for each unit sent along it.
The task is to meet every shop's demand at the lowest total price.
For example, if one road costs $1$ per unit but carries only $5$ units, the rest must go by a dearer road.
