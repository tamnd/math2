## Definition

Let a hollow [[waveguide|EWHJ19ND64]] with perfectly conducting walls be filled with a homogeneous lossless medium of permittivity $\varepsilon$ and permeability $\mu$.
Let a TE or TM [[transverse mode|1T9MVT21YB]] of the guide have transverse eigenvalue $k_c^2 > 0$, so that its propagation constant at angular frequency $\omega$ satisfies $\beta^2 = \omega^2 \mu\varepsilon - k_c^2$.
The *cutoff angular frequency* of the mode is
$$\omega_c = \frac{k_c}{\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}},$$
and its *cutoff frequency* is $f_c = \omega_c / (2\pi)$.

## Remarks

For $\omega > \omega_c$, $\beta$ is real and the mode propagates; for $\omega < \omega_c$, $\beta = i\sqrt{k_c^2 - \omega^2\mu\varepsilon}$ is imaginary and the mode is an [[evanescent field|VT8YK4WC0H]] that decays along the guide.
The guide wavelength $2\pi/\beta$ tends to infinity and the group velocity tends to $0$ as $\omega \to \omega_c^+$.
Example: in an air-filled rectangular guide of width $a$ and height $b < a$, the $\mathrm{TE}_{10}$ mode has $k_c = \pi/a$ and $f_c = c/(2a)$.
For the standard WR-90 guide, $a = 22.86$ mm, so $f_c \approx 6.56$ GHz.
Non-example: a TEM mode, as in a coaxial cable, has $k_c = 0$ and propagates at every frequency, so it has no cutoff in this sense.
