## Summary

Bertrand competition is a model of an industry in which firms selling the same good set their prices at the same time.
Buyers purchase from the cheapest firm.
Its solution is a [[Nash equilibrium|745WYP9YJ0]] of this price-setting game.
With two firms of equal constant marginal cost, the only equilibrium has both firms pricing at marginal cost.
So two firms already give the competitive outcome, a result known as the Bertrand paradox.
It is named after Joseph Bertrand, who criticized [[Cournot's model|S5HGX1PNZR]] in 1883.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two petrol stations stand side by side and sell the same fuel.
Drivers always fill up at the cheaper one.
If one station charges more than its cost, the other can undercut it slightly and take every customer.
This undercutting stops only when both stations charge their cost, so they make no profit even though there are just two of them.
