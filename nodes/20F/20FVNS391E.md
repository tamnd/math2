## Description

The *Preliminary reference Earth model* (PREM) is a one-dimensional model of the Earth, published by Dziewonski and Anderson in 1981.
It gives the density $\rho$, the [[P-wave|EEPJJHC5JZ]] and [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]] speeds, and the seismic attenuation as functions of the radius $r$ only.
The Earth is cut into shells by discontinuities, such as the core–mantle boundary at $r = 3480$ km and the inner core boundary at $r = 1221.5$ km.
In each shell, each quantity is a polynomial of degree at most $3$ in the normalised radius $x = r/a$, with $a = 6371$ km.
In the upper mantle the model is transversely isotropic, an instance of [[seismic anisotropy|XXB2TQX6KW]].

## Remarks

The model was fitted at once to several kinds of data.
These are the frequencies of the [[normal modes of the Earth|NKN1ZDMTG6]] and the [[travel-time curves|1ED8R29JZR]] of body waves.
They also include the [[dispersion of surface waves|FCNW57HD54]] and the mass and moment of inertia of the Earth.
Check of one formula: in the inner core PREM takes $\rho(x) = 13.0885 - 8.8381\, x^2$ in g/cm³.
At the centre this gives $13.09$ g/cm³.
At the inner core boundary, $x = 1221.5/6371 \approx 0.1917$, so $x^2 \approx 0.0368$ and $\rho \approx 13.0885 - 0.325 \approx 12.76$ g/cm³.
Just above the boundary, in the liquid outer core, the density is lower, about $12.17$ g/cm³.

## Explanations

### L1 human

PREM is a standard table of what the inside of the Earth is like, depth by depth.
It says how dense the rock is and how fast seismic waves travel at each depth, assuming the Earth is the same in every direction.
Seismologists use it as a common starting point: they compute predicted waves in PREM and study how real data differ from them.
Despite the word "preliminary" in its name, it is still widely used as a reference model.
