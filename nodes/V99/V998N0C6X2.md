## Statement

Let $n \ge 3$ and let $m_1, \ldots, m_n > 0$ be masses.
Then the number of planar [[central configurations|HQSVS8HX7F]] of these masses, counted up to translation, rotation and scaling, is finite.

## Remarks

This is the sixth problem of Smale's 1998 list, where it is stated for relative equilibria of the [[n-body problem|62SNWC5E45]].
The question was asked earlier by Chazy and by Wintner.
For $n = 3$ it is classical: there are exactly five classes, the equilateral triangle of Lagrange and the three collinear configurations of Euler.
Moulton showed that for every $n$ there are exactly $n!/2$ collinear classes.
Hampton and Moeckel proved finiteness for $n = 4$ in 2006, with a computer-assisted algebraic argument.
Albouy and Kaloshin proved it for $n = 5$ in 2012, except possibly for masses in a codimension-two subvariety of the mass space.
The question is open for $n \ge 6$.
The positivity of the masses matters: Roberts found a continuum of relative equilibria in a five-body problem with one negative mass.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For given masses, there are special shapes in which the bodies can rotate rigidly like a single object.
The conjecture says that for any choice of positive masses there are only finitely many such shapes.
This is known for up to four bodies and, for most masses, for five bodies.
