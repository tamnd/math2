## Summary

Loop-shaping is a method of feedback design.
One chooses the controller so that the open-loop gain, the product of plant and controller, has a desired shape as a function of frequency.
High gain at low frequencies gives good tracking and disturbance rejection.
Low gain at high frequencies gives robustness and noise rejection.
The transition between them must keep enough [[phase margin|J39ZSFW0YF]].
The classical version works with [[Bode plots|EMVW7N54FE]]; H-infinity loop-shaping, due to McFarlane and Glover, combines the shaping with an [[H-infinity|QVREFP7BH7]] robust stabilization step.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A feedback loop is good at following slow commands if it amplifies slow errors strongly.
It is robust against model errors and sensor noise if it does not amplify fast signals.
Loop-shaping designs the controller by drawing the desired gain curve: high on the left of the Bode plot, low on the right.
The art is to make the curve fall from high to low without losing stability where it crosses gain one.
