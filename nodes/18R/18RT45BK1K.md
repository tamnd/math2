## Statement

Model the oceanic lithosphere as the half-space $z > 0$, with $z$ the depth.
Let it have constant thermal conductivity $k$ and thermal diffusivity $\kappa$, and no internal heat sources.
Assume heat is carried only by vertical conduction.
Let the rock have the uniform mantle temperature $T_m$ at time $t = 0$, when it leaves the ridge.
Let the surface $z = 0$ be held at temperature $T_0$ for $t > 0$.
Then the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]] $\partial_t T = \kappa \, \partial_z^2 T$ with these data has the bounded solution
$$T(z, t) = T_0 + (T_m - T_0) \, \operatorname{erf}\!\left( \frac{z}{2\sqrt{\kappa t}} \right),$$
where $\operatorname{erf}$ is the [[error function|7Y56QCZSZJ]].
The surface heat flow is
$$q(t) = k \, \partial_z T(0, t) = \frac{k (T_m - T_0)}{\sqrt{\pi \kappa t}}.$$

## Remarks

Proof idea: the problem has no length scale, so the solution depends only on the [[similarity variable|579J5K9K5Z]] $\eta = z/(2\sqrt{\kappa t})$.
The heat equation becomes $f'' + 2\eta f' = 0$, whose solution with $f(0) = 0$ and $f(\infty) = 1$ is $\operatorname{erf}$.
If the plate spreads from the ridge at constant speed $u$, then $t = x/u$ at distance $x$ from the ridge.
Let the rock have density $\rho_m$ at $T_m$ and thermal expansion coefficient $\alpha$, and let water have density $\rho_w$.
If the cooled column is in [[isostatic|7CJTGB6M0V]] balance, the seafloor sinks below the ridge depth by
$$w(t) = \frac{2 \rho_m \alpha (T_m - T_0)}{\rho_m - \rho_w} \sqrt{\frac{\kappa t}{\pi}},$$
using the [[complementary error function|TZ3CT1YA9B]] integral $\int_0^\infty \operatorname{erfc}(s) \, ds = 1/\sqrt{\pi}$.
So depth and heat flow vary as $\sqrt{t}$ and $1/\sqrt{t}$, which matches observations for young seafloor; older seafloor flattens, which suggests a plate of finite thickness.
The surface value of the [[geothermal gradient|N9RFPKXE2Z]] is $q(t)/k$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

New ocean floor is born hot at a mid-ocean ridge and cools as it moves away.
The model says it cools like a hot slab whose top touches cold water: the cold creeps down a distance proportional to the square root of the age.
Cooling rock shrinks, so the ocean floor gets deeper with age, again like the square root of the age.
This simple law fits the depth of much of the world's ocean floor.

### L2 human

The model is the basic link between [[plate tectonics|AYQQM6PBMR]] and heat flow.
It is the classical solution of the heat equation in a half-space with a step change of surface temperature.
The same solution appears in Kelvin's estimate of the age of the Earth.
Fitting the $\sqrt{t}$ law to bathymetry gives an estimate of the product $\alpha (T_m - T_0)$.
The departure of old seafloor from the law is explained by the plate model, which adds a bottom boundary at fixed temperature.
