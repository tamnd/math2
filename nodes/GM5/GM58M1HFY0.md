## Summary

String theory is a framework of physics in which the point particles of quantum field theory are replaced by one-dimensional strings.
A string moving in spacetime sweeps out a surface, its worldsheet.
In perturbation theory the dynamics of the string is a two-dimensional [[conformal field theory|837V89WJKS]] on this surface.
The vibration modes of a string appear as particles.
One of them always has the properties of a graviton, so the theory includes quantum gravity.
Consistency fixes the dimension of spacetime: $26$ for the bosonic string and $10$ for the [[superstring|N993FCEJY2]].
String theory has had a large influence on mathematics, for example through mirror symmetry, [[vertex operator algebras|GHRPQAJY43]] and the [[AdS/CFT correspondence|92T9TPF13Y]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In string theory the smallest pieces of matter are not dots but tiny loops or segments of string.
Just as a guitar string can vibrate in many ways and play different notes, a fundamental string can vibrate in many ways, and each way looks like a different particle.
As a string moves through time it traces out a surface, much as a point traces out a line.
The rules for these surfaces are so rigid that the theory only works in a spacetime with a specific number of dimensions.
The extra dimensions are supposed to be curled up too small to see.
