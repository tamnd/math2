## Statement

Let $G$ be a [[topological group|92700QAB5Y]], and let $AP(G)$ be the space of [[almost periodic functions|N9WKQ0MSEK]] $G \to \C$.
For $f \in AP(G)$ and $a \in G$, let $(L_a f)(x) = f(a x)$, $(R_a f)(x) = f(x a)$ and $\check f(x) = f(x^{-1})$.
There is exactly one linear map $M \colon AP(G) \to \C$ such that:

1. $M(\mathbf{1}) = 1$, where $\mathbf{1}$ is the constant function $1$;
2. $M(f) \ge 0$ whenever $f \ge 0$;
3. $M(L_a f) = M(f)$ for all $a \in G$ and $f \in AP(G)$.

This map also satisfies $M(R_a f) = M(f)$ and $M(\check f) = M(f)$, and $M(f) > 0$ whenever $f \ge 0$ and $f \ne 0$.

## Remarks

Von Neumann's proof shows that the closed convex hull of the left translates of $f$ contains exactly one constant function, and $M(f)$ is that constant.
A shorter proof uses the [[Bohr compactification|G852FHZ3W1]] $\beta \colon G \to bG$.
Every $f \in AP(G)$ is $F \circ \beta$ for one continuous $F$ on $bG$, and $M(f)$ is the integral of $F$ against the normalized [[Haar measure|VY0HXD3GC4]] of $bG$.
For $G = \R$ the mean is
$$M(f) = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{2T} \int_{-T}^{T} f(x) \, dx.$$
For instance, $M(e^{i\lambda x}) = 0$ for $\lambda \ne 0$, and $M(\abs{\cos x + \cos(\sqrt{2} x)}^2) = 1$.
For a compact group $G$, $M$ is the integral against normalized Haar measure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Almost periodic functions can be averaged over the whole group, even when the group is not compact.
The theorem says that there is one and only one sensible average, one that is positive, gives $1$ for the constant $1$, and ignores shifts.
On the real line this average is the long-run mean over larger and larger intervals.

### L2 human

The mean $M$ is an [[invariant mean|ZYNY2VFECQ]] on the subspace $AP(G)$ of bounded functions, and it exists for every topological group, amenable or not.
It turns $AP(G)$ into a pre-Hilbert space with inner product $M(f \bar g)$.
In that space the coefficients of inequivalent irreducible finite-dimensional unitary representations satisfy Schur orthogonality relations, which leads to von Neumann's Fourier theory of almost periodic functions.
The same uniqueness holds for weakly almost periodic functions, by the Ryll-Nardzewski fixed-point theorem.
