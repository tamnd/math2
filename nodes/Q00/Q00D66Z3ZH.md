## Summary

The Dieudonné determinant extends the determinant to square matrices over a [[division ring|0EA5BTA0CX]] $D$.
Its values lie in the [[abelianisation|0AQZV0905N]] $D^\times/[D^\times, D^\times]$ of the multiplicative group, not in $D$ itself.
It is multiplicative, and for matrices of size at least $2$ it is trivial exactly on the [[elementary subgroup|E5JXB0E324]].
It gives the isomorphism $K_1(D) \cong D^\times/[D^\times, D^\times]$ for [[K1 of a ring|SXVNJY4XFY]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Over a noncommutative ring, the usual formula for the determinant breaks, because the order of the factors matters.
Dieudonné's fix is to forget the order: the determinant is only defined up to commutators.
Row reduction still brings an invertible matrix to the form $\mathrm{diag}(1, \dots, 1, a)$, and the class of $a$ is the determinant.
For a field, commutators are trivial and this is the usual determinant.
For the quaternions, the Dieudonné determinant is the class of a quaternion modulo commutators, and the quaternion norm records this class.
