## Summary

A symmetry of a quantum system is a transformation of its states that preserves all transition probabilities.
A symmetry of the dynamics also commutes with the time evolution.
By [[Wigner's theorem|2KZCM5V7YN]], every such transformation is given by a unitary or an antiunitary operator on the Hilbert space.
A group of symmetries therefore acts by a projective [[unitary representation|74BPYJNZTM]].
Representation theory then organises the states: degenerate energy levels carry representations of the symmetry group.
Continuous symmetries give conserved quantities, such as momentum from translations and [[angular momentum|VKWHPCHH6K]] from rotations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A symmetry is a change of point of view that does not change the physics.
Rotating an isolated atom, or shifting a free particle in space, gives a system that behaves exactly as before.
In quantum mechanics such a change acts on the state vectors by a unitary operator.
If it commutes with the Hamiltonian, it maps states of a given energy to states of the same energy.
For example, the rotation symmetry of the hydrogen atom is why its energy levels come in groups of $2\ell + 1$ states with the same energy.
