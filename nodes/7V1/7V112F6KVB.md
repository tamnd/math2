## Summary

The Komar mass is a definition of the mass of a stationary spacetime, given by Komar in 1959.
It is the integral over a closed 2-surface of the derivative of the timelike [[Killing vector field|5AKNNW3W4Z]] of the spacetime.
In a vacuum region the integral does not depend on the surface, so the mass can be measured on any sphere enclosing the sources.
It agrees with the ADM mass for stationary asymptotically flat vacuum spacetimes.
The same construction with a rotational Killing field gives the Komar angular momentum.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a spacetime that does not change in time there is a symmetry, the time translation, described by a Killing vector field.
The Komar mass measures the mass inside a surface by how fast this field changes across the surface.
In the same way, Gauss's law measures the charge inside a surface by the flux of the electric field.
Like Gauss's law, it gives the same answer for every surface that encloses the same matter.
For the Schwarzschild black hole of mass $M$, every sphere $r = \text{const}$ gives the Komar mass $M$.
