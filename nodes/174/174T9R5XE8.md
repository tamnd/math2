## Definition

Let $B \subseteq \R^3$ be a nonempty open set, the reference configuration of a [[continuum body|X01DZTS224]].
A *deformation* of $B$ is a map $\varphi \colon B \to \R^3$ such that:

1. $\varphi$ is continuously differentiable;
2. $\varphi$ is injective;
3. $\det \nabla \varphi(X) > 0$ for every $X \in B$, where $\nabla \varphi$ is the [[Jacobian matrix|047NDTEPCV]] of $\varphi$.

The *displacement* of the deformation is the map $u \colon B \to \R^3$, $u(X) = \varphi(X) - X$.
The set $\varphi(B)$ is the *deformed* or *current configuration*.

## Remarks

The uniform stretch $\varphi(X) = (2X_1, X_2, X_3)$ is a deformation: it is smooth, injective, and $\det \nabla \varphi = 2 > 0$; its displacement is $u(X) = (X_1, 0, 0)$.
The reflection $\varphi(X) = (-X_1, X_2, X_3)$ is smooth and injective but fails clause 3, since $\det \nabla \varphi = -1$: it would turn the body inside out.
Clause 3 together with the inverse function theorem makes $\varphi$ a diffeomorphism of $B$ onto the open set $\varphi(B)$.
A deformation is called *rigid* when $\varphi(X) = c + QX$ with $Q$ a rotation matrix and $c \in \R^3$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A deformation tells where every point of a body goes.
Two points may not land on the same place, and small regions may not be flipped like a mirror image.
The displacement is the arrow from the old position to the new one.
