## Summary

Creep is the slow growth of the deformation of a solid that is held under a constant load.
It matters at high temperatures for metals, as in turbine blades, and at room temperature for polymers, concrete, ice and rock.
Creep under constant stress is the counterpart of stress relaxation under constant strain, and both are signatures of [[viscoelasticity|GCX1B3DAJN]] and viscoplasticity.
In engineering it limits the service life of parts, which can fail by creep rupture well below their short-term strength.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Hang a weight on a plastic strip and come back a week later: the strip is longer than it was right after you hung the weight.
The load never changed, yet the material kept stretching slowly.
That slow stretching is creep.
A glacier flowing downhill and a bookshelf that sags over the years are also creep.
