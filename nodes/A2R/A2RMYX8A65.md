## Summary

A black body is an idealised body that absorbs all electromagnetic radiation that falls on it, at every wavelength and from every direction.
In equilibrium at a given temperature, no body emits more [[thermal radiation|S6XR2K4N8G]] than it does.
Its spectrum is given by [[Planck's law|QKQ0V5S04R]].
Its total emitted power follows the [[Stefan–Boltzmann law|6JQVMS9Y7R]], and real surfaces are described by comparing them with it through their [[emissivity|VETMCF1NQ5]].
The study of black-body radiation led Planck to the quantum hypothesis.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A perfectly black object reflects nothing: all light that hits it is swallowed.
The best real example is a small hole in the wall of a large hollow box.
Light that enters the hole bounces around inside and is almost surely absorbed before it can find its way out.
If the box is heated, the light coming out of the hole has a colour that depends only on the temperature, not on what the box is made of.
