## Summary

A central force is a force field that always points towards or away from a fixed centre.
Its strength depends only on the distance to the centre.
Gravity of a point mass and the Coulomb force are the main examples.
A central force exerts no torque about its centre, so [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] about the centre is conserved.
When its strength depends continuously on the distance, it is also [[conservative|0PKG97PX9Y]], so energy is conserved.
This reduces the motion to a planar problem with one effective degree of freedom, the basis of the Kepler problem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a ball tied to a post by an elastic cord: the cord always pulls the ball straight towards the post.
How hard it pulls depends on how far the ball is, not on the direction.
Because the pull never pushes sideways, the ball keeps turning around the post in a fixed plane.
The Sun's gravity on a planet acts in the same way, with strength proportional to $1/r^2$ at distance $r$.
