## Statement

Let $\varphi$ be a modal formula with variables $p_1, \dots, p_n$, and let $\mathrm{ST}_x$ be the [[standard translation|YW6YGBMVQY]].
Read a [[Kripke model|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]] $\mathfrak{M} = (W, R, V)$ as the first-order structure with domain $W$.
In this structure, $R$ interprets the relation symbol $R$, and $V(p_i)$ interprets the predicate $P_i$.
Then the following four facts hold.

1. Variables: $\mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$ has no free variable other than $x$ and contains no variable other than $x$ and $y$, and the same holds for $\mathrm{ST}_y(\varphi)$ with $x$ and $y$ exchanged.
2. Local truth: for every $w \in W$, $\mathfrak{M}, w \models \varphi$ if and only if $\mathfrak{M} \models \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)[x \mapsto w]$.
3. Global truth: $\mathfrak{M} \models \varphi$ if and only if $\mathfrak{M} \models \forall x\, \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$.
4. Frame validity: for a [[frame|6BQ71KQ6QT]] $\mathfrak{F} = (W, R)$, $\mathfrak{F} \models \varphi$ if and only if $\mathfrak{F} \models \forall P_1 \dots \forall P_n\, \forall x\, \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$, a sentence of monadic [[second-order logic|G6X7SZSNC5]].

The proof is in [[the proof of correctness|EAK51TNH1M]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The structure must interpret $P_i$ as $V(p_i)$.
With another interpretation, part 2 compares $\varphi$ with a different valuation, and it fails in general.

Part 4 needs the second-order quantifiers.
Without them, $\forall x\, \mathrm{ST}_x(\varphi)$ only expresses global truth in one model.
For $\varphi = \square p \to \square\square p$, global truth can hold in a model on a frame that is not transitive, as [[the axiom 4 example|NA6H3978PW]] shows.

Part 1 depends on the reuse of variables in the definition.
With a new variable for each box, the translation of a formula of modal depth $d$ uses $d + 1$ variables.

### What it gives you

Modal logic over all models inherits compactness and the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem from [[first-order logic|086QKPF0A6]].
Each modal formula is equivalent to a formula of the two-variable fragment, which is decidable, so satisfiability over all models is decidable.
Frame validity of a modal formula is a $\Pi^1_1$ property, and for many formulas it is first-order, as in [[the second-order translation of T|KM2BKWZKW8]] and [[common modal axiom schemata|FDFQFWPM4X]].
A worked computation is [[the translation of a box and a diamond|ZWH6KH29YT]].

### The converse

Not every first-order formula in one free variable is equivalent to a translation, as [[the reflexive point|SB9HMACX5D]] shows.
Up to equivalence, the image is exactly the class of formulas invariant under bisimulation, by [[van Benthem's theorem|Y89MGMWE4H]].

### Generalizations

For several boxes, use one relation symbol $R_i$ for each box, and the same proof works, as in [[multimodal logic|ERG3KAQWYF]].
For the past operator of temporal logic, the clause uses the converse relation, $\mathrm{ST}_x(\mathrm{H}\varphi) = \forall y\,(R(y, x) \to \mathrm{ST}_y(\varphi))$.
For the universal modality, the clause drops the guard, $\mathrm{ST}_x(\mathrm{A}\varphi) = \forall y\, \mathrm{ST}_y(\varphi)$.

## History

Van Benthem made the translation systematic in his dissertation of 1976, where it became the basis of correspondence theory.
Mortimer proved in 1975 that the two-variable fragment of first-order logic has the finite model property.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The translation is faithful.
A modal statement is true at a world exactly when its translation, read in ordinary logic, is true of that world.
A statement holds on a whole frame, for every choice of facts, exactly when a second-order sentence holds.

### L2 human

We check part 2 for $\varphi = \lozenge p$ on a small model.

1. Take $W = \{ a, b \}$, $R = \{ (a, b) \}$ and $V(p) = \{ b \}$.
2. $\mathrm{ST}_x(\lozenge p)$ is equivalent to $\exists y\,(R(x, y) \wedge P(y))$.
3. At $x = a$, the value $y = b$ gives $R(a, b)$ and $P(b)$, so the translation holds, and modally $a$ sees $b$, where $p$ is true, so $\lozenge p$ is true at $a$.
4. At $x = b$, no $y$ has $R(b, y)$, so the translation fails, and modally $b$ has no successor, so $\lozenge p$ is false at $b$.

The typical use is to transfer a first-order theorem to modal logic.
Watch out: part 2 is about one model, while part 4 is about frames and quantifies over all valuations.

### L3 human

Part 4 places frame validity at the level of $\Pi^1_1$, and this is optimal in general.
Thomason showed that the consequence relation between modal formulas over frames is as complex as consequence in full second-order logic.
Part 1 places local truth in the two-variable fragment, whose satisfiability problem is NEXPTIME-complete.
But modal satisfiability is only PSPACE-complete, by [[the complexity theorem|1P2B0C7CQ4]].
The guarded fragment explains this gap better.
