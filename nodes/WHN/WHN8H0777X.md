## Example

Let
$$N = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
over $\mathbb{Q}$.
We follow the [[definition of condensation|F7D849A4RZ]] and find $\det N = -14$, with a negative divisor in the last step.

1. Step 1 takes the connected $2 \times 2$ minors.
   For example, the top left entry is $1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 3 = -5$, and the bottom right entry is $1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 1 = -1$.
   All nine entries together give
$$N^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} -5 & 4 & -2 \\ 3 & -1 & 4 \\ -2 & 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
2. Step 2 divides the connected $2 \times 2$ minors of $N^{(1)}$ by the interior of $N$, which is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$:
$$\frac{(-5)(-1) - 4 \cdot 3}{1} = -7, \quad \frac{4 \cdot 4 - (-2)(-1)}{2} = 7, \quad \frac{3 \cdot 1 - (-1)(-2)}{1} = 1, \quad \frac{(-1)(-1) - 4 \cdot 1}{1} = -3.$$
   So
$$N^{(2)} = \begin{pmatrix} -7 & 7 \\ 1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}.$$
3. Step 3 divides the determinant of $N^{(2)}$ by the interior of $N^{(1)}$, which is the entry $-1$:
$$N^{(3)} = \frac{(-7)(-3) - 7 \cdot 1}{-1} = \frac{14}{-1} = -14.$$
4. So condensation gives $\det N = -14$.

## Remarks

### A check by expansion

The [[Laplace expansion|0C08BQ3M05]] along row $1$ uses the four minors $-3$, $5$, $7$ and $1$ of the entries of row $1$, with alternating signs:
$$1 \cdot (-3) - 2 \cdot 5 + 0 \cdot 7 - 1 \cdot 1 = -14.$$
The two methods agree.

### What the example shows

Step 3 is one use of the [[Desnanot-Jacobi identity|KWW7519E58]] for the whole matrix.
The entries of $N^{(2)}$ are the four corner minors of order $3$, and the divisor $-1$ is the central minor $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
The division by $2$ in step 2 is exact too, because the quotient $7$ is the connected $3 \times 3$ minor of $N$ in rows $1$ to $3$ and columns $2$ to $4$.

### What changes with the sign

A negative divisor needs no special care, but its sign must be kept.
If we divided by $1$ instead of $-1$ in step 3, we would get $14$, the right size with the wrong sign.
If the interior entry $2$ in position $(2, 3)$ of $N$ were $0$, step 2 would divide by $0$, and the method would stop.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The method works in the same way with negative numbers.
Here the last division is by $-1$, and it only changes the sign of the result.
So a $4 \times 4$ determinant comes out of a few $2 \times 2$ determinants and three easy divisions.

### L2 human

The typical use is to compute or to check a determinant with small integers only.
The same matrix appears in the example of the Desnanot-Jacobi identity, which gets $-14$ from the same five minors.
Watch out: write each divisor with its sign before you divide, because a lost sign gives a result of the right size and the wrong sign.
