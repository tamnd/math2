## Definition

Let $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ be the vacuum permittivity.
Let $K \subset \R^3$ be a compact set with nonempty interior and smooth boundary, the conductor, whose complement $\R^3 \setminus K$ is connected.
Let $V > 0$, and let $\varphi$ be the [[harmonic function|0DEB0JMN0W]] on $\R^3 \setminus K$ with $\varphi = V$ on $\partial K$ and $\varphi(x) \to 0$ as $\abs{x} \to \infty$.
The [[charge|6XPSV47R56]] on the conductor is
$$Q = -\varepsilon_0 \int_{\partial K} \partial_n \varphi \, dA,$$
where $n$ is the unit normal pointing out of $K$.
The *capacitance* of $K$ is $C = Q / V$.

## Remarks

The potential $\varphi$ solves an exterior [[Dirichlet problem|8D56GQ4AG8]], and it is proportional to $V$, so $C$ does not depend on $V$.
Example: for the ball of radius $R$ centred at $0$, $\varphi(x) = V R / \abs{x}$, and $\partial_n \varphi = -V / R$ on the sphere, so $Q = 4 \pi \varepsilon_0 R V$ and $C = 4 \pi \varepsilon_0 R$.
Non-example: a single point is not a conductor in this sense; its Newtonian capacity is $0$, so it cannot hold charge at finite potential.
For two conductors with charges $+Q$ and $-Q$ and potential difference $\Delta V$, the capacitance is $C = Q / \Delta V$.
Parallel plates of area $A$ at a small distance $d$ have $C \approx \varepsilon_0 A / d$, neglecting edge effects.
Up to a constant factor, $C$ is the Newtonian [[capacity|4YZVFGJVSZ]] of $K$; in the normalisation where a ball of radius $R$ has capacity $4 \pi R$, the factor is $\varepsilon_0$.
The unit of capacitance is the farad, one coulomb per volt.
