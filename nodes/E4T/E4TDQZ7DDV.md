## Summary

A conformal compactification of a spacetime is a conformal embedding of it into a larger spacetime in which it has compact closure.
In this way infinity becomes a finite boundary.
Because the metric is only rescaled by a positive factor, light cones and hence the [[causal structure|KZWG5F0TSB]] are preserved.
A Penrose diagram is the two-dimensional picture of such a compactification, usually for a spherically symmetric spacetime, with light rays drawn at $45°$.
It shows at a glance the global structure of a spacetime: its infinities, horizons and singularities.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Spacetime is infinite, which makes it hard to draw.
The trick is to squeeze it, like a map of the whole Earth that shrinks distant regions, but in a way that keeps the directions of light rays.
After the squeeze, infinity becomes the edge of a finite picture.
For flat spacetime the picture is a diamond (a triangle when only $r \ge 0$ is drawn), whose edges are where light rays end up.
For a black hole, the picture shows which regions can still send signals to infinity and which cannot.
