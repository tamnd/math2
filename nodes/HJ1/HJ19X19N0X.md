## Definition

Let $\sigma$ be a [[Cauchy stress tensor|KG2XWEMJ30]] at a point, a real symmetric $3 \times 3$ matrix, and let $I$ be the identity matrix.
The *mean stress* is $\sigma_m = \tfrac13 \operatorname{tr} \sigma$, where $\operatorname{tr}$ is the [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]].
The *stress deviator tensor* is
$$s = \sigma - \sigma_m I,$$
and $\sigma_m I$ is the *hydrostatic* or *volumetric* part of $\sigma$.

## Remarks

The deviator is symmetric with $\operatorname{tr} s = 0$, and it has the same principal directions as $\sigma$, with principal values $\sigma_k - \sigma_m$.
Its invariant $J_2 = \tfrac12 \sum_{i,j} s_{ij}^2$ enters the von Mises yield criterion.
For uniaxial tension $\sigma = \operatorname{diag}(s_0, 0, 0)$, one gets $\sigma_m = s_0/3$, $s = \operatorname{diag}(2s_0/3, -s_0/3, -s_0/3)$ and $J_2 = s_0^2/3$.
For a hydrostatic stress $\sigma = -p I$, the deviator is $0$.
In fluid mechanics the pressure is often taken as $p = -\sigma_m$, so that $\sigma = -p I + s$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The stress deviator is stress minus its average normal part.
It is the part of stress that changes shape rather than size.
