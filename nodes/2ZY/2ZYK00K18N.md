## Summary

A tensegrity is a structure of rigid struts and cables in which the struts are held in place by the tension of the cables.
In the mathematical model, a tensegrity framework is a [[framework|V5YS9BZX0N]] with three kinds of members.
Cables may not lengthen, struts may not shorten, and bars keep a fixed length.
Its rigidity is studied with stresses that must be nonnegative on cables and nonpositive on struts.
Tensegrities are used in architecture, in deployable structures and as models of the cytoskeleton of cells.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture three wooden sticks that float in the air without touching each other, held only by strings that join their ends.
The strings pull, the sticks push back, and the whole thing keeps its shape.
That balance of pulls and pushes is a tensegrity.
Unlike a [[truss|E5ZHQSHB97]], where every member can both push and pull, a string can only pull, so the structure is stable only when the strings are under tension.
