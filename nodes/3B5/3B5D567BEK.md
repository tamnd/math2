## Statement

Let $k > 0$ and $\lambda = 2\pi/k$.
Let $u$ solve the [[Helmholtz equation|MNXKTXJ35G]] $\Delta u + k^2 u = 0$ in the half-space $z > 0$ of $\R^3$.
Assume $u$ is continuously differentiable up to the plane $z = 0$ and satisfies the Sommerfeld radiation condition.
Assume its boundary values $u(\cdot, \cdot, 0)$ and $\partial u / \partial z (\cdot, \cdot, 0)$ decay fast enough for the integrals below to converge absolutely.
For a point $P = (x, y, z)$ with $z > 0$ and a point $Q = (\xi, \eta, 0)$ of the plane, let $r = \abs{P - Q}$.
Then:

1. (First Rayleigh–Sommerfeld formula)
$$u(P) = -\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{\R^2} u(\xi, \eta, 0) \, \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \left( \frac{e^{i k r}}{r} \right) d\xi \, d\eta.$$
2. (Second Rayleigh–Sommerfeld formula)
$$u(P) = -\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{\R^2} \frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(\xi, \eta, 0) \, \frac{e^{i k r}}{r} \, d\xi \, d\eta.$$

## Remarks

Let $r'$ be the distance to the mirror image of $P$ in the plane.
The proof applies Green's representation formula on the half-space with the Dirichlet [[Green's function|SH6JY9RN2E]] $\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{e^{ikr}}{r} - \frac{e^{ikr'}}{r'}\right)$.
For the second formula it uses the Neumann Green's function, with $+$ instead of $-$.
Each Green's function kills one of the two boundary terms of [[Kirchhoff's integral theorem|W178NQAYS9]], so only $u$ (or only $\partial u / \partial z$) is needed on the plane.
Since $\frac{\partial}{\partial z} \frac{e^{ikr}}{r} = \left(i k - \frac{1}{r}\right) \frac{e^{ikr}}{r} \cos\theta$, where $\cos\theta = z/r$, the first formula becomes, for $r \gg \lambda$,
$$u(P) \approx \frac{1}{i\lambda} \int_{\R^2} u(\xi, \eta, 0) \, \frac{e^{ikr}}{r} \cos\theta \, d\xi \, d\eta,$$
the Huygens–Fresnel principle with obliquity factor $\cos\theta$.
In diffraction by an aperture $A$ in a screen, one sets $u$ equal to the incident field on $A$ and $0$ on the rest of the plane.
Unlike Kirchhoff's conditions, this prescribes only the boundary values, so it is consistent.
The radiation condition is needed: the standing wave $\sin(kz)$ solves the Helmholtz equation and vanishes on the plane $z = 0$, so without it the first formula would fail.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If you know the light field on a whole plane, and you know the light only travels away from the plane, then the field everywhere beyond the plane is determined.
The Rayleigh–Sommerfeld formula writes this field as a sum of waves emitted by the points of the plane.

### L2 human

These formulas are exact for outgoing waves in a half-space, and they are the rigorous base of scalar diffraction theory.
The first one, with the field given on an aperture, is the usual model for diffraction by a hole in a thin screen.
Taking the two-dimensional Fourier transform of the formula gives the angular spectrum method, in which propagation multiplies each plane-wave component by a phase.
