## Definition

A *heat engine* is a system that repeatedly performs a [[thermodynamic cycle|AGRGCDWQF8]] such that, in each cycle:

1. it absorbs a quantity of [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] $Q_H > 0$ from one or more bodies at higher temperature,
2. it gives a quantity of heat $Q_C \ge 0$ to one or more bodies at lower temperature, and
3. the net [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] $W$ it does on its surroundings is positive.

## Remarks

By the [[first law of thermodynamics|V14W2V4JSR]] applied to the cycle, $W = Q_H - Q_C$.
In the simplest case there are two reservoirs, a hot one at temperature $T_H$ and a cold one at $T_C < T_H$.
Example: an engine that per cycle takes $1000\,\mathrm{J}$ from a boiler, gives $600\,\mathrm{J}$ to a condenser and does $400\,\mathrm{J}$ of work meets all three clauses.
A non-example: a refrigerator also runs a cycle between two reservoirs, but work is done on it, $W < 0$, so clause 3 fails.
By the [[Kelvin–Planck statement|RMNJ00XP8X]] of the second law, every heat engine has $Q_C > 0$.
