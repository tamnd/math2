## Summary

The Reynolds equation is the elliptic partial differential equation for the pressure in a thin film of viscous fluid between two surfaces.
It is the central equation of [[lubrication theory|90FD7XNX72]], derived by Osborne Reynolds in 1886.
Given the shape of the gap and the motion of the surfaces, it determines the pressure, and so the load that the film can carry.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a shaft turns in an oil-filled bearing, it drags oil into a narrowing gap.
The oil piles up and its pressure rises, and this pressure lifts the shaft off the bearing surface.
The Reynolds equation computes this pressure from the gap height and the speed of the surfaces.
Its key feature is the factor $h^3$: a gap half as thick lets through only an eighth as much oil under the same pressure gradient.
