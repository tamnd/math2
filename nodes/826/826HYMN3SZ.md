## Summary

Atmospheric tides are global-scale oscillations of the atmosphere whose periods are a solar or lunar day divided by a whole number.
Unlike ocean tides, the largest atmospheric tides are forced by the daily cycle of solar heating, not by gravity.
The lunar gravitational tide is much weaker.
Classical tidal theory describes them as forced solutions of the [[Laplace tidal equations|9BF0QWFZA9]] for the atmosphere.
Their amplitude grows with height as the air density falls, and they dominate the winds of the upper mesosphere and lower thermosphere.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every day the sun heats the atmosphere on the day side, and the warmed air expands.
This daily push sets off huge waves in the air that circle the globe following the sun.
At the ground they show up as a tiny regular rise and fall of air pressure, about one hectopascal in the tropics, twice a day.
High up, around $100$ km, these waves become strong winds because the thin air there moves easily.
The Moon also pulls on the air, but its effect is much smaller than the sun's heating.
