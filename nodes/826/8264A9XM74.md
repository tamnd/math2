## Summary

An extension of a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] $\mathfrak g$ by a Lie algebra $\mathfrak a$ is a Lie algebra $\mathfrak e$ containing $\mathfrak a$ as an ideal with quotient $\mathfrak g$.
It is written as a short exact sequence $0 \to \mathfrak a \to \mathfrak e \to \mathfrak g \to 0$.
Extensions are the Lie algebra analogue of [[group extensions|MF4N3VGGFV]], and those with abelian kernel are classified by Lie algebra cohomology.
Central extensions, where $\mathfrak a$ lies in the center, give the Heisenberg algebra, affine Kac–Moody algebras and the Virasoro algebra.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An extension builds a bigger Lie algebra from two smaller ones, a part $\mathfrak a$ inside and a quotient $\mathfrak g$ on top.
Unlike a direct sum, the two parts can be glued in a twisted way.
For example, the Heisenberg algebra has the line spanned by $z$ inside and the abelian plane on top.
The plane is abelian, but the brackets in the extension are not zero: $[x, y] = z$.
