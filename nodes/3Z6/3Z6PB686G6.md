## Definition

Consider three particles with the [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] $\mathcal{H} = L^2(\R^6)$ of the relative motion, after removal of the center of mass.
Let $H_0$ be the [[self-adjoint|0NEGN5CRSC]] kinetic energy operator.
Let $V_1, V_2, V_3$ be the pair potentials, where $V_i$ is the interaction of the pair that does not contain particle $i$.
Let $H = H_0 + V_1 + V_2 + V_3$ and $V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3$.
For $z \in \C \setminus \R$, let $G_0(z) = (z - H_0)^{-1}$ be the free [[resolvent|J5YNNKSB9P]].
Let $T_i(z)$ be the two-body transition operator of the pair $i$ in the three-body space, defined by
$$T_i(z) = V_i + V_i\, G_0(z)\, T_i(z).$$
The *Faddeev equations* are the system for three operators $T^{(1)}, T^{(2)}, T^{(3)}$
$$T^{(i)}(z) = T_i(z) + T_i(z)\, G_0(z) \sum_{j \ne i} T^{(j)}(z), \qquad i = 1, 2, 3.$$
The sum $T(z) = T^{(1)}(z) + T^{(2)}(z) + T^{(3)}(z)$ then solves the three-body equation $T(z) = V + V\, G_0(z)\, T(z)$.
For a bound state $\psi$ of energy $E$ below the spectrum of $H_0$, the wave function form is $\psi = \psi_1 + \psi_2 + \psi_3$ with
$$\psi_i = G_0(E)\, T_i(E) \sum_{j \ne i} \psi_j, \qquad i = 1, 2, 3.$$

## Remarks

Check of the operator form: put $T^{(i)} = V_i + V_i G_0 T$; then $T^{(i)} - V_i G_0 T^{(i)} = V_i \big(1 + G_0 \sum_{j \ne i} T^{(j)}\big)$, and $(1 - V_i G_0)^{-1} V_i = T_i$ gives the Faddeev equation; summing over $i$ gives $T = V + V G_0 T$.
Check of the wave function form: if $H\psi = E\psi$, then $\psi = G_0(E) V \psi$, and $\psi_i = G_0(E) V_i \psi$ satisfies $(1 - G_0 V_i)\psi_i = G_0 V_i \sum_{j \ne i} \psi_j$, which is the stated equation since $(1 - G_0 V_i)^{-1} G_0 V_i = G_0 T_i$.
Non-example: the three-body Lippmann–Schwinger equation $T = V + V G_0 T$ itself has a kernel $V G_0$ that is not compact, even after iteration.
The reason is the terms in which one pair interacts while the third particle moves freely.
These terms carry a delta function in the momentum of the spectator particle.
The kernel of the Faddeev system has no such disconnected terms after one iteration, and for suitable short-range potentials a power of it is compact on suitable function spaces.
The equations were introduced by L. D. Faddeev in 1960.
