## Summary

The core of a [[cooperative game|39HGM44CFZ]] is the set of splits of the total worth that no coalition can improve on alone.
It is a notion of stability: an allocation outside the core is open to objection by some group of players.
The core may be empty, and the Bondareva–Shapley theorem says exactly when it is nonempty.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a group must split a prize, and every subgroup knows how much it could get by walking away.
A split is in the core if every subgroup already gets at least what it could get alone, so nobody has a reason to leave.
For two partners who earn $10$ together and $3$ each alone, the core is the set of splits $(a, 10 - a)$ with $3 \le a \le 7$.
In a majority vote among three people over one prize, every split leaves some pair wanting to take everything, so the core is empty.
