## Summary

Perturbation theory in quantum mechanics computes the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a Hamiltonian $H_0 + \lambda V$ as power series in a small parameter $\lambda$.
It starts from the known solution for $H_0$.
The time-independent version, the Rayleigh–Schrödinger series, gives energy shifts; the time-dependent version, the [[Dyson series|PNYD1NXC1M]], gives transition amplitudes.
It is the main tool for systems that are close to an exactly solvable one.
Its mathematical side is the [[perturbation theory of linear operators|JTZK8DZ257]], which says when the series converge.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Few quantum problems can be solved exactly, but many are close to one that can.
Perturbation theory starts from the exact answer and adds corrections one order at a time.
First comes the average effect of the small extra term, then its effect through the other states, and so on.
For example, a weak electric field shifts the energy levels of an atom a little, and the first few corrections already predict the shift well.
