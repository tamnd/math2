## Summary

Strong gravitational lensing is the regime of gravitational lensing in which a lens forms several images of one source, or strongly stretched arcs and rings.
It happens when the source lies close to the line of sight through a dense enough mass, within about the [[Einstein radius|4K4NMJCPD5]].
It is used to measure the masses of galaxies and clusters and, through time delays between images, the Hubble constant.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a massive galaxy sits almost exactly in front of a distant quasar, light from the quasar can reach us along several bent paths.
We then see two or four copies of the same quasar around the galaxy.
An extended source, such as a galaxy behind a cluster, is drawn out into long thin arcs, or into a full ring when the alignment is perfect.
The positions and shapes of these images tell how much mass the lens has and how it is spread.
