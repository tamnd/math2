## Summary

Orbital mechanics, or astrodynamics, is the study of the motion of spacecraft and natural bodies under gravity, with a view to practical problems.
It plans launches, orbit changes, rendezvous and interplanetary trajectories.
Its basic model is the Kepler orbit of the two-body problem, refined by perturbations and by the thrust of the engines.
See the [[definition|D4VE2QAHNJ]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Orbital mechanics answers questions such as how to put a satellite in orbit and how to send a probe to Mars.
A spacecraft with its engines off simply falls around a planet or the Sun along an ellipse.
Short engine burns change the ellipse into another one.
For example, a burn that speeds up a craft on a circular orbit makes the orbit stretch into an ellipse that reaches farther out.
