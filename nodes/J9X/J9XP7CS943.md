## Summary

The Q-construction turns an [[exact category|TFADSC9YPZ]] $\mathcal{C}$ into a new category $Q\mathcal{C}$ with the same objects.
A morphism of $Q\mathcal{C}$ is a way to see one object as a subquotient of another.
Quillen defined the K-groups of $\mathcal{C}$ as the homotopy groups of the loop space of the classifying space $BQ\mathcal{C}$.
This definition works for any exact category, not only for rings, and it gives the main structural theorems of [[algebraic K-theory|5GVFZWMWC8]]: resolution, dévissage and localization.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an exact category one can talk about subobjects and quotients, as with modules.
The Q-construction keeps the objects and declares a map from $M$ to $N$ to be a way of finding $M$ as a quotient of a subobject of $N$.
From this category one builds a space, and its loops carry the K-theory.
The first group of loops gives back the Grothendieck group $K_0$, where an object counts as the sum of its sub and its quotient.
For the category of finitely generated projective modules over a ring, the result agrees with the plus construction.
