## Summary

Scattering theory studies how a quantum system that is free in the far past interacts and becomes free again in the far future.
It compares the dynamics generated by a full [[Hamiltonian|VXDEQB46GV]] $H$ with the dynamics of a simpler free Hamiltonian $H_0$ for large positive and negative times.
Its main objects are the wave operators and the [[S-matrix|YDC9XBJDE1]], which map incoming free states to outgoing free states.
It is the mathematical basis of collision experiments in atomic, nuclear and particle physics, and has close analogues for classical waves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Fire a particle at a target and watch where it goes.
Long before and long after the collision, the particle is far from the target and moves freely.
Scattering theory describes only what matters for the experiment: which free motion goes in and which free motion comes out.
For example, an electron hitting an atom comes in as a plane wave and leaves partly undeflected and partly as a spherical wave spreading in all directions.
The rule that turns the incoming motion into the outgoing one is the S-matrix.
