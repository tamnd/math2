## Summary

The replicator equation is a system of [[ordinary differential equations|0NT64JV96A]] that describes how the shares of strategies in a large population change over time.
A strategy grows when its payoff is above the population average and shrinks when it is below.
It was introduced by Peter Taylor and Leo Jonker in 1978 and is the central dynamic of evolutionary game theory.
Its rest points and their stability are linked to Nash equilibria and [[evolutionarily stable strategies|9ECGX1RGGT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a population in which each individual plays one fixed strategy, and successful strategies spread by reproduction or imitation.
The replicator equation says that the growth rate of each strategy's share equals how much better than average it does.
If hawks currently earn more than the average animal, the share of hawks rises; if they earn less, it falls.
In the Hawk–Dove game with prize $2$ and cost $4$, the share of hawks moves towards one half from any starting mix of both types.
