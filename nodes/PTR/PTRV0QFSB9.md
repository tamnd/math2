## Summary

A nonholonomic system is a mechanical system with constraints on its [[velocities|K160D2Z0CS]] that cannot be reduced to constraints on its positions.
Rolling without slipping and the blade of a skate are the standard sources of such constraints.
At each instant only some directions of motion are allowed.
In typical examples the system can still reach every position of a region of its configuration space.
Lagrange's equations in their usual form do not apply directly, and the equations of motion need multipliers or other methods.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A car cannot move sideways: at each moment its wheels only let it go forward or backward while it turns.
Still, by going back and forth and turning, a driver can park the car in any spot and with any heading.
So the rule restricts how the car moves, not where it can end up.
Rules of this kind make a system nonholonomic, unlike a bead on a wire, which can never leave the wire.
