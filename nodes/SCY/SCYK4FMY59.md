## Summary

The Rossby number $\mathrm{Ro} = U / (f L)$ is the [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]] that compares the inertia of a flow with the Coriolis force due to rotation.
It is the main parameter of rotating flows, in particular of the atmosphere and the oceans.
When it is small, the flow is close to geostrophic balance, in which the pressure gradient balances the Coriolis force.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a rotating planet, moving air is deflected sideways by the rotation.
For a small flow, such as water draining from a bathtub, the effect is tiny compared with the motion itself, and the Rossby number is large.
For a weather system a thousand kilometres wide, the rotation matters a lot, and the Rossby number is small.
This is why winds blow around large highs and lows instead of straight into them.
