## Proof

We prove the explicit form: for $n \ge 20$, every resolution refutation of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has at least $(11/10)^{\lfloor n/20 \rfloor}$ clauses.
This proof is due to Beame and Pitassi.
For $N \ge 1$, write $[N] = \set{1, \dots, N}$ and use pigeons in $[N+1]$ and holes in $[N]$.

*Part 1: critical assignments and the monotone transform.*

1. An assignment $\alpha$ is *$i$-critical* if it matches the pigeons other than $i$ one to one onto the $N$ holes, and sends pigeon $i$ nowhere. So $x_{kj} = 1$ exactly when pigeon $k$ sits in hole $j$.
2. An $i$-critical assignment satisfies every hole clause, since no hole has two pigeons. It satisfies every pigeon clause except $P_i$, and it falsifies $P_i$.
3. For a clause $C$, let $M(C)$ be the clause with only positive literals obtained by replacing each literal $\neg x_{kj}$ by the literals $x_{k'j}$ for all pigeons $k' \ne k$. Under a critical assignment, every hole has exactly one pigeon, so $\neg x_{kj}$ is true exactly when some $x_{k'j}$ with $k' \ne k$ is true. Hence $C$ and $M(C)$ have the same value under every critical assignment.

*Part 2: every refutation contains a clause with a wide transform.*

4. Let $N \ge 3$ and let $\pi$ be a refutation of $\mathrm{PHP}^{N+1}_N$. For a clause $C$, let $\mu(C)$ be the number of pigeons $k$ such that some $k$-critical assignment falsifies $C$.
5. By step 2, $\mu(P_i) = 1$ and $\mu(H_{ii'j}) = 0$. Every assignment falsifies $\square$, so $\mu(\square) = N + 1$.
6. If $C$ is the resolvent of $A$ and $B$, then every assignment that falsifies $C$ falsifies $A$ or $B$, by the soundness of the rule. So $\mu(C) \le \mu(A) + \mu(B)$.
7. Let $C$ be the first clause of $\pi$ with $\mu(C) \ge (N+1)/3$. It exists by step 5. It is not an axiom, since $(N+1)/3 > 1$ for $N \ge 3$. Its premises come earlier, so each has $\mu < (N+1)/3$, and $\mu(C) < 2(N+1)/3$ by step 6.
8. Let $K$ be the set of the pigeons counted by $\mu(C)$, and $s = \abs{K}$. So $(N+1)/3 \le s < 2(N+1)/3$.
9. *Swap.* Let $i \in K$ and $j \notin K$. Choose an $i$-critical $\alpha$ that falsifies $C$, and let $l$ be the hole of pigeon $j$ under $\alpha$. Let $\alpha'$ be $\alpha$ with pigeon $j$ removed from hole $l$ and pigeon $i$ put into hole $l$. Then $\alpha'$ is $j$-critical, so it satisfies $C$, since $j \notin K$.
10. By step 3, $\alpha$ falsifies $M(C)$ and $\alpha'$ satisfies $M(C)$. The two assignments differ only in $x_{il}$, which changes from $0$ to $1$, and $x_{jl}$, which changes from $1$ to $0$. Since $M(C)$ has only positive literals, the literal made true is $x_{il}$. So $x_{il} \in M(C)$.
11. For a fixed $i$ and fixed $\alpha$, different pigeons $j \notin K$ sit in different holes $l$. So $M(C)$ contains at least $N + 1 - s$ variables $x_{il}$ for each $i \in K$, and these variables are different for different $i$.
12. So $\abs{M(C)} \ge s(N + 1 - s)$. The function $s \mapsto s(N + 1 - s)$ is concave, so on the interval of step 8 it is smallest at an end point. Hence $\abs{M(C)} \ge \tfrac{N+1}{3} \cdot \tfrac{2(N+1)}{3} = \tfrac{2(N+1)^2}{9}$.

*Part 3: random restrictions.*

13. Let $n \ge 20$, $m = \lfloor n/20 \rfloor$ and $t = 2(n - m)^2/9$. Call a clause $C$ *large* if $\abs{M(C)} \ge t$. Let $\pi$ be a refutation of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ with $L$ large clauses. We show $L \ge (11/10)^m$.
14. Choose a random partial matching $\rho$ of $m$ pigeons into $m$ holes, one pair at a time. At each step, choose a pigeon and a hole uniformly among the pairs of unused pigeons and unused holes. For each chosen pair $(i, j)$, set $x_{ij} = 1$, $x_{ij'} = 0$ for the other holes $j'$, and $x_{i'j} = 0$ for the other pigeons $i'$.
15. *Killing a large clause.* Suppose $\rho$ chooses a pair $(i, j)$ with $x_{ij} \in M(C)$. Then $x_{ij} \in C$, or $\neg x_{kj} \in C$ for some $k \ne i$. In the first case $\rho$ makes $x_{ij}$ true. In the second case $\rho$ sets $x_{kj} = 0$, since hole $j$ is taken by $i$. In both cases $C|_\rho = 1$.
16. Fix a large clause $C$, and suppose that the first $r < m$ chosen pairs did not kill it. The used pigeons and holes cover at most $r(2n + 1)$ variables. So at least $t - m(2n + 1)$ variables of $M(C)$ remain among the unused pairs, and there are at most $n(n+1)$ unused pairs.
17. Since $m \le n/20$, we have $t \ge \tfrac{2}{9} \cdot \tfrac{361}{400} n^2 = \tfrac{361}{1800} n^2$ and $m(2n + 1) \le \tfrac{n^2}{10} + \tfrac{n}{20}$. A computation gives $t - m(2n+1) \ge \tfrac{n(n+1)}{11}$ when $382 n^2 \ge 5580 n$, which holds for $n \ge 15$.
18. So each step kills $C$ with probability at least $1/11$, given the earlier steps. The probability that $C$ survives all $m$ steps is at most $(10/11)^m$.
19. If $L < (11/10)^m$, the probability that some large clause survives is at most $L (10/11)^m < 1$. So some $\rho$ kills every large clause.
20. *The restricted formula.* Under this $\rho$, the clauses of the chosen pigeons and holes are true, and the other clauses lose the literals of the used holes. So $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n|_\rho$ is the formula $\mathrm{PHP}^{n'+1}_{n'}$ of the remaining pigeons and holes, with $n' = n - m \ge 19$.
21. By the [[restriction lemma|7RX0Z6S1KZ]], $\pi$ gives a refutation $\pi'$ of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n'+1}_{n'}$, each clause $D$ of which satisfies $D \subseteq C|_\rho$ for some $C \in \pi$ with $C|_\rho \ne 1$.
22. By Part 2, with $N = n'$, some $D \in \pi'$ has a transform $M'(D)$, taken for the remaining pigeons, with $\abs{M'(D)} \ge 2(n'+1)^2/9 > t$.
23. Each literal of $M'(D)$ is a literal of $M(C)$: a positive literal of $D$ is in $C$, and a negative literal $\neg x_{kj}$ of $D$ is in $C$ and gives $M(C)$ all $x_{k'j}$ with $k' \ne k$. So $\abs{M(C)} \ge \abs{M'(D)} > t$, and $C$ is large.
24. But $C|_\rho \ne 1$, so $C$ is a large clause that survives $\rho$. This contradicts step 19.
25. Hence $L \ge (11/10)^m$, and $\pi$ has at least $(11/10)^{\lfloor n/20 \rfloor}$ clauses.

Finally we derive Haken's form with $c = (11/10)^{1/40} > 1$.

26. For $n \ge 20$, $\lfloor n/20 \rfloor \ge n/40$, so the bound of step 25 is at least $c^n$.
27. For $1 \le n < 20$, every refutation uses all $n + 1$ pigeon clauses, since without $P_i$ the formula is satisfied by a matching of the other pigeons. So it has at least $n + 1$ clauses, and $n + 1 \ge 2 > c^{19} \ge c^n$. $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Every refutation must, at some point, "know" something about a third of the pigeons, and such a clause has a wide monotone form.
A small refutation has few wide clauses, and a random partial matching makes all of them true.
What is left is a refutation of a smaller pigeonhole formula without wide clauses, which Part 2 forbids.

## Variants

- *Bottleneck counting.* Haken's original proof follows each critical assignment $\alpha$ from $\square$ back to an axiom, along clauses falsified by $\alpha$. It shows that every path passes a "complex" clause, and that each complex clause is passed by few assignments. So there are many complex clauses.
- *Width.* Ben-Sasson and Wigderson prove an exponential bound, by the [[size-width relation|GWDGRPAQSM]], for the pigeonhole formula restricted to a bipartite expander graph of bounded degree. That formula is $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ with $x_{ij} = 0$ for the non-edges, so by the [[restriction lemma|7RX0Z6S1KZ]] the bound also holds for $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$.
- The constants are not optimal; the method gives $2^{\Omega(n)}$ with better constants.
