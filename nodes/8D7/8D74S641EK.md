## Proof

We show that every refutation contains a clause that is wide in a monotone sense, and that a random partial matching kills all wide clauses of a small refutation, which leaves a smaller refutation with no wide clause.
This is the proof of Beame and Pitassi, and it gives the explicit form: for $n \ge 20$, every resolution refutation of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ has at least $(11/10)^{\lfloor n/20 \rfloor}$ clauses.
For $N \ge 1$ we write $[N] = \{1, \dots, N\}$, and $\mathrm{PHP}^{N+1}_N$ has pigeons in $[N+1]$ and holes in $[N]$.

### Part 1: critical assignments and the monotone transform

1. An assignment $\alpha$ is *$i$-critical* if it matches the pigeons other than $i$ one to one onto the $N$ holes, and sends pigeon $i$ nowhere.
   So $x_{kj} = 1$ exactly when pigeon $k$ sits in hole $j$.
2. An $i$-critical assignment satisfies every hole clause, since no hole has two pigeons.
   It satisfies every pigeon clause except $P_i$, and it falsifies $P_i$.
3. For a clause $C$, let $M(C)$ be the clause with only positive literals that we get by replacing each literal $\neg x_{kj}$ by the literals $x_{k'j}$ for all pigeons $k' \ne k$.
   Under a critical assignment every hole has exactly one pigeon, so $\neg x_{kj}$ is true exactly when some $x_{k'j}$ with $k' \ne k$ is true.
   Hence $C$ and $M(C)$ have the same value under every critical assignment.

### Part 2: every refutation has a clause with a wide transform

4. Let $N \ge 3$ and let $\pi$ be a refutation of $\mathrm{PHP}^{N+1}_N$.
   For a clause $C$, let $\mu(C)$ be the number of pigeons $k$ such that some $k$-critical assignment falsifies $C$.
5. By step 2, $\mu(P_i) = 1$ and $\mu(H_{ii'j}) = 0$.
   Every assignment falsifies $\square$, so $\mu(\square) = N + 1$.
6. If $C$ is the resolvent of $A$ and $B$, then every assignment that falsifies $C$ falsifies $A$ or $B$, by the soundness of the rule.
   So $\mu(C) \le \mu(A) + \mu(B)$.
7. Let $C$ be the first clause of $\pi$ with $\mu(C) \ge (N+1)/3$, which exists by step 5.
   It is not an axiom, since $(N+1)/3 > 1$ for $N \ge 3$.
   Its premises come earlier, so each has $\mu < (N+1)/3$, and $\mu(C) < 2(N+1)/3$ by step 6.
8. Let $K$ be the set of pigeons counted by $\mu(C)$, and let $s = \lvert K \rvert$.
   So $(N+1)/3 \le s < 2(N+1)/3$.

### Part 2, the swap

9. Let $i \in K$ and $j \notin K$.
   Choose an $i$-critical $\alpha$ that falsifies $C$, and let $l$ be the hole of pigeon $j$ under $\alpha$.
   Let $\alpha'$ be $\alpha$ with pigeon $j$ taken out of hole $l$ and pigeon $i$ put into hole $l$.
   Then $\alpha'$ is $j$-critical, so it satisfies $C$, because $j \notin K$.
10. By step 3, $\alpha$ falsifies $M(C)$ and $\alpha'$ satisfies $M(C)$.
    The two assignments differ only in $x_{il}$, which changes from $0$ to $1$, and in $x_{jl}$, which changes from $1$ to $0$.
    Since $M(C)$ has only positive literals, the literal that became true is $x_{il}$, so $x_{il} \in M(C)$.
11. For a fixed $i$ and $\alpha$, different pigeons $j \notin K$ sit in different holes $l$.
    So $M(C)$ contains at least $N + 1 - s$ variables $x_{il}$ for each $i \in K$, and these variables differ for different $i$.
12. So $\lvert M(C) \rvert \ge s(N + 1 - s)$.
    The function $s \mapsto s(N + 1 - s)$ is concave, so on the interval of step 8 it is smallest at an end point, and both end points give the same value:
    $$\lvert M(C) \rvert \ge \frac{N+1}{3} \cdot \frac{2(N+1)}{3} = \frac{2(N+1)^2}{9}.$$

### Part 3: random restrictions

13. Let $n \ge 20$, $m = \lfloor n/20 \rfloor$ and $t = 2(n - m)^2/9$.
    Call a clause $C$ *large* if $\lvert M(C) \rvert \ge t$.
    Let $\pi$ be a refutation of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ with $L$ large clauses; we show $L \ge (11/10)^m$.
14. Choose a random partial matching $\rho$ of $m$ pigeons into $m$ holes, one pair at a time, each time uniformly among the pairs of an unused pigeon and an unused hole.
    For each chosen pair $(i, j)$, set $x_{ij} = 1$, set $x_{ij'} = 0$ for the other holes $j'$, and set $x_{i'j} = 0$ for the other pigeons $i'$.
15. Suppose $\rho$ chooses a pair $(i, j)$ with $x_{ij} \in M(C)$.
    Then $x_{ij} \in C$, or $\neg x_{kj} \in C$ for some $k \ne i$.
    In the first case $\rho$ makes $x_{ij}$ true, and in the second case $\rho$ sets $x_{kj} = 0$, since hole $j$ is taken by $i$.
    In both cases $C|_\rho = 1$, and we say that $\rho$ *kills* $C$.
16. Fix a large clause $C$, and suppose that the first $r < m$ pairs did not kill it.
    The used pigeons and holes cover at most $r(2n + 1)$ variables, so at least $t - m(2n + 1)$ variables of $M(C)$ remain among the unused pairs, and there are at most $n(n+1)$ unused pairs.

### Part 3, the count

17. Since $m \le n/20$, we have $n - m \ge 19n/20$, and so
    $$t \ge \frac{2}{9} \cdot \frac{361}{400} n^2 = \frac{361}{1800} n^2, \qquad m(2n + 1) \le \frac{n^2}{10} + \frac{n}{20}.$$
    So $t - m(2n+1) \ge \tfrac{181}{1800} n^2 - \tfrac{n}{20}$, and this is at least $\tfrac{n(n+1)}{11}$ when $191 n^2 \ge 2790 n$, which holds for $n \ge 15$.
18. So each step kills $C$ with probability at least $1/11$, whatever the earlier steps were.
    The probability that $C$ survives all $m$ steps is at most $(10/11)^m$.
19. If $L < (11/10)^m$, the probability that some large clause survives is at most $L (10/11)^m < 1$, by the union bound.
    So some $\rho$ kills every large clause.
20. Under this $\rho$, the clauses of the chosen pigeons and holes are true, and the other clauses lose the literals of the used holes.
    So $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n|_\rho$ is the formula $\mathrm{PHP}^{n'+1}_{n'}$ of the remaining pigeons and holes, with $n' = n - m \ge 19$.

### Part 3, the contradiction

21. By the [[restriction lemma|7RX0Z6S1KZ]], $\pi$ gives a refutation $\pi'$ of $\mathrm{PHP}^{n'+1}_{n'}$.
    Each clause $D$ of $\pi'$ satisfies $D \subseteq C|_\rho$ for some $C \in \pi$ with $C|_\rho \ne 1$.
22. By Part 2 with $N = n'$, some $D \in \pi'$ has a transform $M'(D)$, taken for the remaining pigeons, with $\lvert M'(D) \rvert \ge 2(n'+1)^2/9 > t$.
23. Each literal of $M'(D)$ is a literal of $M(C)$.
    A positive literal of $D$ is in $C$, and a negative literal $\neg x_{kj}$ of $D$ is in $C$ and puts all $x_{k'j}$ with $k' \ne k$ into $M(C)$.
    So $\lvert M(C) \rvert \ge \lvert M'(D) \rvert > t$, and $C$ is large.
24. But $C|_\rho \ne 1$, so $C$ is a large clause that survives $\rho$, and this contradicts step 19.
25. Hence $L \ge (11/10)^m$, and $\pi$ has at least $(11/10)^{\lfloor n/20 \rfloor}$ clauses.

### Haken's form

We derive the form $c^n$ with $c = (11/10)^{1/40} > 1$.

26. For $n \ge 20$ we have $\lfloor n/20 \rfloor \ge n/40$, since $\lfloor y \rfloor \ge y/2$ for $y \ge 1$.
    So the bound of step 25 is at least $c^n$.
27. For $1 \le n < 20$, every refutation uses all $n + 1$ pigeon clauses, since without $P_i$ the formula is satisfied by a matching of the other pigeons.
    So it has at least $n + 1 \ge 2$ clauses, and $2 > c^{19} \ge c^n$ because $c^{19} = (1.1)^{19/40} < 1.05$.
    This proves the theorem.

$\blacksquare$

## Idea

At some point every refutation must "know" something about a third of the pigeons, and the swap argument shows that such a clause has a wide monotone form.
A small refutation has few wide clauses, so a random partial matching makes all of them true at once.
What is left is a refutation of a smaller pigeonhole formula with no wide clause, and Part 2 says that this cannot exist.
The whole proof is a counting argument: a probability below $1$ means that a good matching exists.

## Variants

Haken's original proof of 1985 is called *bottleneck counting*.
It follows each critical assignment from $\square$ back to an axiom, along clauses that the assignment falsifies.
Every such path passes a "complex" clause, and each complex clause is passed by few assignments, so there must be many complex clauses.

Ben-Sasson and Wigderson prove an exponential bound by the [[size-width relation|GWDGRPAQSM]] for the pigeonhole formula on a bipartite expander graph of bounded degree.
That formula is $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$ with $x_{ij} = 0$ for the non-edges, so the [[restriction lemma|7RX0Z6S1KZ]] transfers the bound to $\mathrm{PHP}^{n+1}_n$.
Both methods give $2^{\Omega(n)}$, and the constants above are not the best possible.
