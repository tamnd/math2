## Example

Take $F = \{x \vee y, \ x \vee \neg y, \ \neg x \vee y, \ \neg x \vee \neg y\}$ and the width bound $w = 2$.
A search that keeps only clauses with at most $2$ literals finds a [[resolution|6JSH907W5R]] refutation of $F$ with $7$ clauses in two rounds.

1. Round 1 resolves pairs of axioms.
   From $x \vee y$ and $x \vee \neg y$ on $y$ it gets $x$, and in the same way it gets $\neg x$, $y$ and $\neg y$.
   The pair $x \vee y$ and $\neg x \vee \neg y$ clashes on two variables, so it has no resolvent, and the same holds for $x \vee \neg y$ and $\neg x \vee y$.
2. Round 2 resolves $x$ with $\neg x$ and gets $\square$.
3. The recorded derivation of $\square$ has $7$ clauses: the four axioms, $x$, $\neg x$ and $\square$.

### The algorithm in general

In general, let $F$ be a CNF over $n$ variables and let $w \geq 1$.
The *width* of a clause is its number of literals, and $w(F \vdash \square)$ is the least $w$ such that $F$ has a refutation whose clauses all have width at most $w$.
The algorithm $\mathrm{Search}_w$ works as follows.

1. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the set of clauses of $F$ of width at most $w$.
2. For every pair of clauses in $\mathcal{C}$ that clash on exactly one variable, form the resolvent.
   Add it to $\mathcal{C}$, with its two premises recorded, if its width is at most $w$ and it is new.
3. Repeat step 2 until $\square \in \mathcal{C}$ or until a round adds no clause.
4. If $\square \in \mathcal{C}$, output the recorded derivation of $\square$, and otherwise output "no refutation of width $w$".

We check its size, time, correctness and completeness.

1. A clause of width at most $w$ fills at most $w$ places from the $2n$ literals, so there are at most $(2n + 1)^w$ such clauses, and $\mathcal{C}$ never holds more.
2. Each round adds a clause or stops, and checks at most $\lvert \mathcal{C} \rvert^2$ pairs.
   So the time is $\lvert F \rvert^{O(1)} \cdot n^{O(w)}$.
3. Every clause in $\mathcal{C}$ is derived by resolution from $F$, so an output is a refutation of width at most $w$.
4. If $F$ has a refutation with all clauses of width at most $w$, then each of its clauses enters $\mathcal{C}$, by induction along the refutation, so $\square$ enters $\mathcal{C}$.

## Remarks

Running $\mathrm{Search}_w$ for $w = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ in turn finds a refutation in time $n^{O(w(F \vdash \square))}$, up to a factor polynomial in $\lvert F \rvert$.

By the first part of the [[size-width relation|GWDGRPAQSM]], $w(F \vdash \square) \leq w(F) + \log_2 S_T(F \vdash \square)$, where $S_T$ is the least tree-like size.
So the search runs in time $n^{O(w(F) + \log S_T(F \vdash \square))}$.
This is quasi-polynomial in $n + S_T(F \vdash \square)$ when $w(F)$ is at most polylogarithmic in $n$.

The output may be a dag-like refutation, since a clause in $\mathcal{C}$ can be a premise many times.
So the algorithm automates tree-like resolution only in the weak sense, with general resolution as the output system.

For general resolution, the second part of the size-width relation gives only the time $n^{O(w(F) + \sqrt{n \log S(F \vdash \square)})}$, which is not quasi-polynomial in $S$.

The search is an instance of [[automatizability|WBECDG163W]] only for formulas of small width.
The [[Atserias-Müller theorem|W7FP9TZMVP]] shows that no polynomial-time version exists for general resolution, unless P = NP.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The program tries all short clauses that it can make from the formula.
If a proof that uses only short clauses exists, the program finds it.
There are not many short clauses, so the program is fast when the clauses of some proof are short.

### L2 human

The search is breadth-first over the clauses of width at most $w$.
The number $(2n + 1)^w$ counts the ways to fill $w$ places with a literal or a blank, and it is what makes the time $n^{O(w)}$.
A trap is to think that width search is fast for every formula with a short refutation.
The [[pigeonhole formulas|RJ571F7FF0]] have initial clauses of width $n$, so the search must start from width $n$.
For formulas whose refutations all need width close to $n$, the bound $n^{O(w)}$ is exponential.

### L3 human

Width search is the basic positive result about proof search in resolution.
Together with the size-width relation it gives a quasi-polynomial bound in terms of the tree-like size.
The Atserias-Müller theorem shows that nothing of this kind runs in polynomial time for general resolution, unless P = NP.
