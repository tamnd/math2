## Summary

The Johnson–Kendall–Roberts (JKR) model describes the contact of two elastic spheres whose surfaces attract each other.
It adds the surface energy of the contact to [[Hertz's theory|XNCND3KYNP]] and finds the contact radius by minimizing the total energy.
It predicts a finite contact patch at zero load and a definite pull-off force needed to separate the bodies.
K. L. Johnson, K. Kendall and A. D. Roberts published it in 1971, and it fits soft compliant solids with strong short-range adhesion.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two soft rubber balls stick together a little when they touch.
Even with no push, they form a small contact patch, held by the attraction of the surfaces.
To pull them apart takes a definite force, and the patch shrinks before it suddenly snaps off.
The JKR model computes this patch and this pull-off force from the stiffness of the rubber and the strength of the adhesion.
