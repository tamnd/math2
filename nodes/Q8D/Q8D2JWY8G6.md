## Summary

The acceleration of a moving point is the rate of change of its [[velocity|K160D2Z0CS]] with respect to time.
It is the [[second derivative|0Q4QJWEHW3]] of the position.
Newton's second law ties acceleration to force, so acceleration is the link between the description of motion and its causes.
A point accelerates when its speed changes and also when only its direction changes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a car speeds up from $0$ to $100$ km/h in $10$ seconds, its speed grows by $10$ km/h each second: that is its acceleration.
Braking is acceleration opposite to the motion.
A car going around a bend at constant speed also accelerates, because the direction of its velocity changes; passengers feel this as a push toward the outside of the bend.
