## Definition

Let $(M, g)$ be a [[spacetime|63ZKC7AFX2]] with signature $(-, +, +, +)$, in units where $c = 1$, and let $\nabla$ be its [[Levi-Civita connection|CAZYGE1T4E]].
A *stress–energy tensor* of a matter distribution on $M$ is a symmetric $(0, 2)$-[[tensor field|WX302T93TW]] $T$ with the following meaning.
Let $u$ be the four-velocity of an observer at a point, with $g(u, u) = -1$, and let $e$, $e'$ be unit vectors orthogonal to $u$.

1. $T(u, u)$ is the energy density measured by the observer.
2. $-T(u, e)$ is the density of momentum in the direction $e$, which equals the flux of energy in the direction $e$.
3. $T(e, e')$ is the flux in the direction $e'$ of the momentum in the direction $e$, that is, the stress.

For an isolated system the tensor satisfies the local conservation law $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$.

## Remarks

Example: dust of energy density $\rho \ge 0$ moving with four-velocity $U$ has $T = \rho \, U^\flat \otimes U^\flat$, where $U^\flat = g(U, \cdot)$.
For the observer $u = U$, $T(u, u) = \rho \, g(U, U)^2 = \rho$, and $T(u, e) = 0$ and $T(e, e') = 0$, since $g(U, e) = 0$: no momentum flow and no stress in the rest frame.
A perfect fluid with pressure $p$ has $T = (\rho + p) U^\flat \otimes U^\flat + p \, g$.
Non-example: an antisymmetric tensor such as the [[electromagnetic tensor|WRZJRJS44W]] $F$ is not a stress–energy tensor, since a stress–energy tensor must be symmetric.
The electromagnetic field has the [[stress–energy tensor|N22WEQ3GWH]] $F_{\mu\alpha}F_\nu{}^\alpha - \frac14 g_{\mu\nu} F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}$, up to a constant factor.
In an inertial frame of special relativity, the components $T^{00}$, $T^{0i}$ and $T^{ij}$ are the energy density, the energy flux and the stress tensor.
