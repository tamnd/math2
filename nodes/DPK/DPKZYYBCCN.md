## Statement

Let $U$ be a connected open subset of [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]] $(\R^4, \eta)$ with $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$, in units where $c = 1$.
Let $u$ be a smooth vector field on $U$ with $\eta(u, u) = -1$, the [[four-velocity|Y59V040THG]] field of a moving body.
Assume that the motion is [[Born rigid|J7FVATX80A]], that is, the expansion and the shear of $u$ vanish on $U$.
Let $\omega$ be the vorticity tensor of $u$.

1. If $\omega = 0$ on $U$, then every hypersurface orthogonal to $u$ is a piece of a spacelike hyperplane.
2. If $\omega \ne 0$ at every point of $U$, then there is a [[Killing vector field|5AKNNW3W4Z]] $K$ of Minkowski space such that $u = K / \sqrt{-\eta(K, K)}$ on $U$.

## Remarks

Born rigidity says that the distances between neighbouring particles of the body, measured in their momentary rest frames, do not change.
In the rotational case, the integrability conditions of the rigidity equations use the flatness of Minkowski space.
They force the flow of $u$ to be generated by an isometry, up to the normalisation of $u$.
So an irrotational rigid motion has the freedom of a family of moving hyperplanes.
A rotational rigid motion is one of the isometric motions, such as a uniform rotation about a fixed axis.
A body that rotates uniformly about the $z$-axis with angular speed $\Omega$ has $u$ proportional to the Killing field $\partial_t + \Omega(x \partial_y - y \partial_x)$, on the region where $\Omega^2(x^2 + y^2) < 1$.
A consequence is that a body at rest cannot be brought into rotation while staying Born rigid, which is the core of the Ehrenfest paradox.
The result is due to Gustav Herglotz and Fritz Noether.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In relativity a body cannot be perfectly rigid in the everyday sense, but it can move so that each small piece keeps its own shape and size.
The theorem says that this kind of rigid motion is very restricted.
Without rotation, the body is carried along by a family of flat slices of spacetime.
With rotation, the body can only turn at a constant rate in a fixed way, given by a symmetry of spacetime.
So a rigid wheel can spin steadily, but it cannot be spun up from rest while staying rigid.

### L2 human

Write $h = \eta + u^\flat \otimes u^\flat$ for the metric on the rest spaces; Born rigidity is the condition that the Lie derivative of $h$ along $u$ vanishes.
This condition removes the expansion and shear parts of the covariant derivative of $u$, so only the acceleration and the vorticity remain.
The Herglotz–Noether theorem then splits Born rigid motions into the two classes of the statement.
It covers the example of hyperbolic motion, which is irrotational, and of uniform rotation, which is a Killing motion.
