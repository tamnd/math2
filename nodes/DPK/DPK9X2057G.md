## Proof

We prove two inequalities: a nonzero minor of order $k$ forces $k$ independent columns, and $\rho$ independent columns always contain a nonzero minor of order $\rho$.

1. Let $A$ be a nonzero $m \times n$ matrix over the field $F$, with columns $a_1, \dots, a_n$.
   Let $\rho = \operatorname{rank} A$, the dimension of the [[column space|0F0RPPJ2TT]] of $A$.

*A nonzero minor gives independent columns.*

2. Let $S$ be a $k \times k$ submatrix of $A$ with $\det S \ne 0$, and let $I$ be its set of rows and $J$ its set of columns.
3. By the [[invertibility criterion|FBSAPTP1PR]], $S$ is invertible.
   So $S c = 0$ only for $c = 0$.
4. Suppose that $\sum_{j \in J} c_j a_j = 0$ for some scalars $c_j$.
   This is a vector equation with $m$ coordinates.
5. Keep only the coordinates with index in $I$.
   They form the equation $S c = 0$ for the vector $c = (c_j)_{j \in J}$, so $c = 0$ by step 3.
6. So the $k$ columns $a_j$ with $j \in J$ are [[linearly independent|0EVHJFTG9Z]].
   Independent vectors in a space of dimension $\rho$ number at most $\rho$, so $k \le \rho$.

*Independent columns give a nonzero minor.*

7. The columns of $A$ span its column space, so some $\rho$ of them form a basis of it, because [[every spanning set contains a basis|06MRNP4BNT]].
   Let $A'$ be the $m \times \rho$ matrix of these $\rho$ columns.
8. The column rank of $A'$ is $\rho$.
   By [[row rank equals column rank|0TZAG60ZYT]], $A'$ has $\rho$ linearly independent rows.
9. Let $S'$ be the $\rho \times \rho$ matrix formed by these rows of $A'$.
   Its rows are independent, so its row rank is $\rho$.
10. By row rank equals column rank again, the $\rho$ columns of $S'$ are independent, so they form a basis of $F^{\rho}$, and $S'$ is invertible.
11. By the invertibility criterion, $\det S' \ne 0$.
   The matrix $S'$ is a submatrix of $A$, so $\det S'$ is a nonzero [[minor|0SD528P0EK]] of $A$ of order $\rho$.

*Conclusion.*

12. By step 6, no nonzero minor has order larger than $\rho$, and by step 11 there is a nonzero minor of order $\rho$.
   So $\rho$ is the largest order of a nonzero minor of $A$.

## Idea

A nonzero minor is an invertible block, and an invertible block makes its columns independent even after we add back the other rows.
So the rank is at least the order of every nonzero minor.
Conversely, pick $\rho$ independent columns, and inside them pick $\rho$ independent rows.
Where these rows and columns cross, there is an invertible $\rho \times \rho$ block, and its determinant is the nonzero minor we want.

## Variants

A proof by elimination also works.
Row and column operations change each minor of order $r$ into a combination of minors of order $r$, and they can be undone, so they keep the largest order of a nonzero minor.
After elimination the matrix has the form $\begin{pmatrix} I_\rho & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and for that form the largest nonzero minor has order $\rho$ by inspection.
