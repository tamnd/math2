## Definition

Let $F$ be a field and let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $F$.
The matrix $A$ is *diagonalizable* over $F$ when there is an [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]] $n \times n$ matrix $P$ over $F$ such that $P^{-1} A P$ is a [[diagonal matrix|044JNC7ZAA]].
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$.
An [[endomorphism|0J7H0YBFDJ]] $T \colon V \to V$ is *diagonalizable* when there is a basis of $V$ in which the [[matrix of $T$|0YQSGNYE3K]] is diagonal.

## Notation

We write $P^{-1} A P = D$ or $A = P D P^{-1}$, with $D = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$.
The phrase "diagonalizable over $F$" names the field of the entries of $P$ and $D$.

## Remarks

So $A$ is diagonalizable if and only if it is [[similar|0E89HQATM8]] to a diagonal matrix.
An endomorphism is diagonalizable if and only if its matrix in one basis, and then in every basis, is a diagonalizable matrix, by the [[change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]].
The diagonal entries of $D$ are the eigenvalues of $A$, and the columns of $P$ are eigenvectors, by the [[characterization by eigenvectors|056SW1E0AC]].
The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is not diagonalizable over any field.
Over $\R$, the rotation matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is not diagonalizable, and over $\C$ it is.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A diagonalizable matrix becomes a diagonal matrix in a suitable basis.
In that basis, the map stretches each coordinate axis by a factor.
For example, a map may stretch one axis by $5$ and another by $2$.
This makes powers, exponentials and other functions of the matrix easy to compute.

### L2 human

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. $P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, since $P P^{-1} = I$.
2. $A P = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. $P^{-1}(A P) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, which is diagonal.
4. So $A$ is diagonalizable, with eigenvalues $2$ and $1$ and eigenvectors $(1, 1)$ and $(0, 1)$.

The trap is to think that a triangular matrix is already diagonal enough; a triangular matrix is diagonalizable only under the conditions of the [[test by multiplicities|~1ac265ba]].

### L3 human

Diagonalizability of an endomorphism means that $V$ is a direct sum of one-dimensional invariant subspaces.
As an $F[x]$-module, $V$ is then a direct sum of copies of modules $F[x]/(x - \lambda)$.
