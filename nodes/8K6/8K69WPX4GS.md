## Summary

Cosmological redshift is the stretching of the wavelength of light that travels through an expanding universe.
In a [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker|ECJMS4VDTF]] model, wavelengths grow in proportion to the [[scale factor|J70F32P86W]].
So the redshift of a source measures how much the universe has expanded since the light left it.
It is the main observable of cosmology.
It differs from both the [[relativistic Doppler effect|7PFB064X9P]] and [[gravitational redshift|TJ74QPATFP]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw a wave on a balloon and blow the balloon up: the wave stretches with it.
Light crossing the universe is stretched in the same way, so it arrives redder than it was sent.
If the universe has doubled in size while the light travelled, every wavelength has doubled, and the redshift is $z = 1$.
Distant galaxies have larger redshifts because their light has travelled longer.
