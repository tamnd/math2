## Summary

An optical parametric amplifier is a device in which a strong pump wave amplifies a weak signal wave in a medium with second-order [[nonlinear susceptibility|7N71T3FKZ6]].
A third wave, the idler, is created at the difference frequency $\omega_i = \omega_p - \omega_s$.
The medium only mediates the exchange of energy and stores none, which is why the process is called parametric.
Because the signal frequency can be tuned continuously, it is a common source of tunable short pulses.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A child on a swing can pump it higher by moving at the right rhythm.
In an optical parametric amplifier, a strong laser beam plays the role of the pumping child, and a weak beam is the swing.
Inside a special crystal, the strong beam gives energy to the weak beam and to a new third beam.
Each photon of the strong beam splits into a photon of the weak beam and a photon of the new beam.
