## Definition

Consider two-dimensional flow of a fluid of kinematic viscosity $\nu > 0$ and density $1$ above the wall $y = 0$, in the region $x \in \R$, $y > 0$, $t > 0$.
Let $U(x, t)$ be the tangential velocity of the outer inviscid flow at the wall.
Let the pressure gradient along the wall be given by $-\partial_x p = \partial_t U + U \, \partial_x U$.
The *Prandtl boundary layer equations* for the velocity $(u, v)$ are
$$\partial_t u + u \, \partial_x u + v \, \partial_y u = \partial_t U + U \, \partial_x U + \nu \, \partial_y^2 u, \qquad \partial_x u + \partial_y v = 0,$$
with the boundary conditions

1. $u = v = 0$ at $y = 0$,
2. $u \to U(x, t)$ as $y \to \infty$.

## Remarks

Example: for a constant outer speed $U$ the function $u = U \operatorname{erf}\big( y / (2 \sqrt{\nu t}) \big)$, $v = 0$ is a solution, where $\operatorname{erf} = 1 - \operatorname{erfc}$.
Indeed $u$ does not depend on $x$, so the equations reduce to $\partial_t u = \nu \, \partial_y^2 u$, which $u$ solves; and $u(0, t) = 0$, $u \to U$ as $y \to \infty$.

Non-example: the steady profile $u = U (1 - e^{-y})$, $v = 0$, with constant $U$, satisfies both boundary conditions but not the momentum equation, since the left side is $0$ and $\nu \, \partial_y^2 u = -\nu U e^{-y} \ne 0$.

The equations arise from the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] by rescaling $y$ and $v$ by $\mathrm{Re}^{-1/2}$ and letting the Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}$ tend to infinity.
The normal momentum equation becomes $\partial_y p = 0$, so the pressure is imposed by the outer flow of the [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]] through Bernoulli's relation.
For a steady flow the equations are parabolic in $x$, so one can march downstream from an initial profile.
Their solutions may break down where the flow separates from the wall.
