## Definition

Let $R$ be a [[ring|0B1261B5ED]] with identity, and let $t$ be an indeterminate that commutes with $R$.
Let $R[t]$ and $R[t^{-1}]$ be [[polynomial rings|0SDC934R52]], both contained in the [[Laurent polynomial ring|QMY5JTZZB8]] $R[t, t^{-1}]$.
Set $K_0$ to be [[K0 of a ring|Q14JS2MA1C]].
For $n \ge 0$, define $K_{-n-1}$ by induction on $n$.
The group $K_{-n-1}(R)$ is the cokernel of the homomorphism
$$K_{-n}(R[t]) \oplus K_{-n}(R[t^{-1}]) \to K_{-n}(R[t, t^{-1}]), \qquad (x, y) \mapsto x + y,$$
where each component is induced by the inclusion of rings.
The groups $K_{-n}(R)$ with $n \ge 1$ are the *negative K-groups* of $R$.

## Remarks

The definition is motivated by degree one.
There, the same cokernel, built from $K_1$ instead of $K_{-n}$, is isomorphic to $K_0(R)$, by the Bass–Heller–Swan decomposition of $K_1(R[t, t^{-1}])$.

Example: let $F$ be a field.
The rings $F[t]$ and $F[t, t^{-1}]$ are principal ideal domains, so both have $K_0 \cong \Z$, generated by the class of the free module of rank $1$.
The inclusion sends one generator to the other, so the map is onto, and $K_{-1}(F) = 0$.
More generally, $K_{-n}(R) = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$ when $R$ is a regular Noetherian ring.

Example with a nonzero group: let $k$ be a field and let $R = k[x, y]/(y^2 - x^3 - x^2)$, the coordinate ring of a nodal cubic.
The normalization $k[s]$, with $x = s^2 - 1$ and $y = s(s^2 - 1)$, gives a Milnor square, and its Mayer–Vietoris sequence shows $K_{-1}(R) \cong \Z$.

Non-example: the cokernel of the map from $K_{-n}(R[t])$ alone is not the right group.
It also contains the image of $K_{-n}(R[t^{-1}])$, which need not lie in the image of $K_{-n}(R[t])$; both polynomial subrings must be used.
