## Example

A world with no successors makes every box true and every diamond false, and a world that misses itself can make a box true while the formula inside is false.
These two small pictures separate three of the [[modal logics T, D and K4|XVYMEZKH7R]]: $\mathsf{K}$, $\mathsf{D} = \mathsf{KD}$ and $\mathsf{T} = \mathsf{KT}$.
We show the following chain, and then a second axiom for $\mathsf{D}$:
$$\mathsf{K} \subsetneq \mathsf{D} \subsetneq \mathsf{T}.$$

1. $\mathsf{T}$ proves $\mathrm{D}$.
   Axiom $\mathrm{T}$ gives $\square p \to p$, its dual gives $p \to \lozenge p$, and chaining gives $\square p \to \lozenge p$.
2. So $\mathsf{D} \subseteq \mathsf{T}$, since $\mathsf{T}$ is a normal logic that contains the axiom of $\mathsf{D}$.
3. $\mathsf{K}$ does not prove $\mathrm{D}$.
   On one world without successors, $\square p$ is true and $\lozenge p$ is false, so by [[soundness|E87G6ZTMTN]] the axiom $\mathrm{D}$ is not in $\mathsf{K}$.
4. $\mathsf{D}$ does not prove $\mathrm{T}$.
   Take $W = \{ 1, 2 \}$, $R = \{ (1, 2), (2, 2) \}$ and $V(p) = \{ 2 \}$.
5. Each world has a successor, so the frame is serial and validates $\mathsf{D}$, by the [[common modal axiom schemata|FDFQFWPM4X]] and soundness.
6. At $1$ the only successor $2$ has $p$, so $\square p$ is true, while $p$ is false.
   So $\mathrm{T}$ fails at $1$.
7. Steps 2, 3 and 6 give $\mathsf{K} \subsetneq \mathsf{D} \subsetneq \mathsf{T}$.

Next we show that $\mathsf{D} = \mathsf{K} \oplus \lozenge\top$.

8. $\mathsf{D}$ proves $\lozenge\top$.
   [[Necessitation|G989VP2NGP]] applied to the tautology $\top$ gives $\square\top$, and the instance $\square\top \to \lozenge\top$ of $\mathrm{D}$ gives $\lozenge\top$.
9. The tautology $p \to (\neg p \to \bot)$, necessitation and axiom $\mathrm{K}$ twice give $\square p \to (\square\neg p \to \square\bot)$ in $\mathsf{K}$.
10. The tautology $\neg\top \leftrightarrow \bot$ and the rule RE of [[normal modal logic K|JS8W7PA6JK]] give $\square\neg\top \leftrightarrow \square\bot$.
    So $\lozenge\top$, which is $\neg\square\neg\top$, is equivalent to $\neg\square\bot$.
11. From $\neg\square\bot$ and step 9, propositional logic gives $\square p \to \neg\square\neg p$, which is $\square p \to \lozenge p$.
12. So $\mathrm{D}$ is a theorem of $\mathsf{K} \oplus \lozenge\top$, and with step 8 the two logics are equal.

## Remarks

The example shows that the empty condition, seriality and reflexivity give three different logics.
The formula $\lozenge\top$ says "this world has a successor", so it states seriality directly at each world.

The frame of step 4 is also transitive, so the same model shows that $\mathsf{KD4}$ does not prove $\mathrm{T}$.
If you remove the loop $(2, 2)$, then $2$ has no successor, the frame is no longer serial, and $\mathrm{D}$ fails at $2$ with any valuation.

The logic $\mathsf{D}$ is the basic deontic logic.
Read $\square p$ as "$p$ is obligatory": then $\mathrm{D}$ says that obligations are consistent, since $\square p$ and $\square\neg p$ together would give $\square\bot$ by step 9.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Three rules for "box" are compared here.
Rule T says "box $p$ implies $p$", and rule D says "box $p$ implies diamond $p$".
Rule T is stronger: from "box $p$" it gives $p$, and from $p$ it gives "diamond $p$".
Rule D does not give T, because an obligation can be unmet while it is still possible to meet it.
The basic logic K proves neither rule, since a world with no successors makes every "box" true and every "diamond" false.

### L2 human

Read $\square p$ as "$p$ is obligatory" and test the model of step 4.
World $1$ is a world where the duty $p$ holds but is not met, since $p$ is false at $1$.
World $2$ is the ideal world where every duty is met, and $1$ points to it.
So the model is a reasonable picture of obligation, and it fails $\mathrm{T}$ but keeps $\mathrm{D}$.
This is how you choose between the two logics in an application: $\mathsf{T}$ for knowledge, $\mathsf{D}$ for obligation or belief.
Watch out: $\mathsf{K}$ proves $\square\top$, but this does not give $\lozenge\top$, because a world without successors makes $\square\top$ true and $\lozenge\top$ false.
