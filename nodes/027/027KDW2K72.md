## Definition

Two square matrices are similar when one is obtained from the other by conjugation with an invertible matrix.
Let $F$ be a field and let $A$ and $B$ be $n \times n$ matrices over $F$.
The matrix $B$ is *similar* to $A$ when there is an [[invertible|0TWTS88TWN]] $n \times n$ matrix $P$ over $F$ with
$$B = P^{-1} A P.$$

## Notation

We write $A \sim B$ when the meaning is clear from the context.
Some books write $B = PAP^{-1}$, which defines the same relation with $P^{-1}$ in place of $P$.
Friedberg, Insel and Spence write $B = Q^{-1}AQ$.

## Remarks

Each condition has a reason.
The matrices are square of one size, so that $P^{-1}AP$ is defined and has the size of $A$.
The matrix $P$ is invertible, since otherwise $P^{-1}$ does not exist.
The matrix $P$ has entries in $F$, so the definition does not depend on a larger field; that a larger field gives the same relation is a theorem, and the rational canonical form proves it.
The same $P$ appears on both sides of $A$, and this is what makes $A$ and $B$ the matrices of one map in two bases, by the [[change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]].

For example, with $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ gives $P^{-1}AP = \operatorname{diag}(1, 2)$, as the [[example of a diagonalization|X1CZSG8JEC]] computes.
If we drop the condition that the same matrix appears on both sides, we get matrix equivalence $B = QAP$, and then $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ become related although their traces are $1$ and $0$.

Similarity is an equivalence relation on $M_n(F)$, by the [[theorem on the equivalence relation|HWKZ8DM8P0]], so "$B$ is similar to $A$" and "$A$ and $B$ are similar" mean the same thing.
Similar matrices have the same [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]], [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]], [[characteristic polynomial|0VSSPAFM2C]], [[rank|0ZC9YTXZRY]], [[minimal polynomial|03NDVCSHSB]] and [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]], by the [[theorem on similarity invariants|191HBJ7PVZ]].
The matrices $I_2$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ have the same characteristic polynomial but [[are not similar|62XEH0NYE1]].
The [[concept page|0E89HQATM8]] gives more examples.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Similar matrices describe the same linear map, seen from two different bases.
Properties of the map itself, like the trace and the determinant, are the same for both matrices.
A main goal of linear algebra is to find a simple matrix, such as a diagonal one, in each similarity class.

### L2 human

The matrix $P$ is the change of basis matrix whose columns are the new basis vectors, written in the old basis.
Read $P^{-1}AP$ from right to left: $P$ turns new coordinates into old ones, $A$ applies the map, and $P^{-1}$ turns the result back into new coordinates.
Watch out: $PAP^{-1}$ uses the change in the other direction, so do not mix the two forms in one computation.
