## Definition

Let $F$ be a field and let $A$ and $B$ be $n \times n$ matrices over $F$.
The matrix $B$ is *similar* to $A$ when there is an [[invertible|0EB9N0S33C]] $n \times n$ matrix $P$ over $F$ with
$$B = P^{-1} A P.$$

## Notation

One writes $A \sim B$ when the meaning is clear from the context.
Some books write $B = P A P^{-1}$.
This defines the same relation, with $P^{-1}$ in place of $P$.
Friedberg, Insel and Spence write $B = Q^{-1} A Q$.

## Remarks

Similarity is an equivalence relation on $M_n(F)$, by the [[theorem on the equivalence relation|~e995afb1]].
So "$B$ is similar to $A$" and "$A$ and $B$ are similar" mean the same thing.
By the [[change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]], two matrices are similar if and only if they represent the same endomorphism of $F^n$ in two bases.
Similar matrices have the same [[trace|0XC1RDBY7A]], [[determinant|00KDZRW4C0]], [[characteristic polynomial|0VSSPAFM2C]], [[rank|0ZC9YTXZRY]], [[minimal polynomial|03NDVCSHSB]] and [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]], by the [[theorem on similarity invariants|~b354e4d1]].
The matrices $I_2$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ have the same characteristic polynomial but [[are not similar|~1ebbf521]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Similar matrices describe the same linear map, seen from two different bases.
Properties of the map itself, like trace and determinant, are the same for both matrices.
A main goal of linear algebra is to find a simple matrix in each similarity class.

### L2 human

The matrix $P$ is the change of basis matrix whose columns are the new basis vectors, written in the old basis.
The product $P^{-1} A P$ first converts new coordinates to old ones, then applies $A$, then converts back.
