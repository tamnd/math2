## Summary

Population genetics studies how the frequencies of genetic variants change in populations over time.
The forces it considers are mutation, natural selection, [[genetic drift|AFJ02FJ1KR]], migration and recombination.
Its mathematical models, such as the [[Wright–Fisher model|SR4F169EKF]] and the [[Moran process|A9M7VKRRV9]], are Markov chains and diffusions on allele frequencies.
It is the mathematical core of the theory of evolution.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A gene can come in several versions, called alleles, such as the versions that give different blood types.
Population genetics asks how common each version is in a population and how this changes from one generation to the next.
Some changes come from selection, when one version helps survival, and some from pure chance in who has children.
For example, if $30\%$ of the gene copies in a population carry version $A$, chance alone may push this to $28\%$ or $33\%$ in the next generation.
