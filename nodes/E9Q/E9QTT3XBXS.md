## Summary

A qubit is a quantum system whose state space is the two-dimensional complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] $\C^2$.
It is the basic unit of [[quantum information|MJC22KY2WW]], as the bit is the basic unit of classical information.
A pure state of a qubit is a [[superposition|2HMDSKEZFP]] of the two basis states $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$.
Systems of $n$ qubits live in $(\C^2)^{\otimes n}$, a space of dimension $2^n$, which is the setting of quantum computing.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A classical bit is either $0$ or $1$.
A qubit can be in a blend of both, written $\alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are complex numbers.
When you measure it, you get $0$ with probability $\abs{\alpha}^2$ and $1$ with probability $\abs{\beta}^2$.
For example, $(|0\rangle + |1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$ gives $0$ or $1$ with equal chance.
Physical qubits include the spin of an electron and the polarization of a photon.
