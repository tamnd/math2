## Summary

Non-equilibrium thermodynamics is the part of [[thermodynamics|HG1SSMA2GM]] that describes systems not in thermodynamic equilibrium.
Its classical form assumes [[local thermodynamic equilibrium|J027RJ924H]] and describes irreversible processes by fluxes, forces and the [[entropy production|STR73R5MX4]].
Near equilibrium, fluxes depend linearly on forces, and the [[Onsager reciprocal relations|9YACB2RQ80]] make the coefficient matrix symmetric.
It explains heat conduction, diffusion, viscosity, chemical reactions and their coupled effects, such as thermoelectricity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Equilibrium thermodynamics describes systems at rest, where nothing changes.
Most of the world is not at rest: heat flows, liquids mix, reactions run.
Non-equilibrium thermodynamics describes these flows and the entropy they create.
A metal bar heated at one end is a simple case: heat flows towards the cold end, and entropy is produced all along the bar.
