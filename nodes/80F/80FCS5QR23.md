## Problem

Two particles are prepared in an [[entangled|CHNXT8CCK7]] state and then separated.
For this state, measuring the position of the first particle predicts with certainty the position of the second one.
The same holds for momentum.
Assume locality: a measurement on the first particle does not change the physical situation of the distant second particle.
Assume the reality criterion: if the value of a quantity can be predicted with certainty without disturbing a system, the quantity has a definite value.
Then both the position and the momentum of the second particle have definite values.
Quantum mechanics assigns no state with definite values of both, since these [[observables|DF5RN297GE]] do not commute.
The question is whether quantum mechanics is therefore an incomplete description of physical reality.

## Remarks

Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen concluded that the quantum description is incomplete, which suggests a theory with additional variables.
Bohr replied in the same year that the reality criterion is ambiguous when the choice of measurement is part of the experimental arrangement.
Bohm reformulated the argument with two spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ particles in the singlet state, where spin components along any axis are perfectly anticorrelated.
[[Bell's theorem|7X9M1G56VP]] later showed that local [[hidden-variable theories|QRW6NE1VY7]] cannot reproduce all quantum predictions for this state.
Experiments find violations of Bell inequalities, as quantum mechanics predicts.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two particles that interacted in the past can stay linked so that measuring one tells you the result for the other, however far apart they are.
Einstein and his coauthors argued that the second particle must then have had its properties all along, because nothing done far away could have created them.
Since quantum mechanics does not describe these properties as definite in advance, they called it incomplete.
The later work of Bell turned this philosophical debate into a question that experiments can decide.
