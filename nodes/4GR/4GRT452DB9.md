## Example

In [[Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory|0HE5WDQ9FJ]] (ZF), the set $\mathbb{N}$ with its usual zero, successor, addition and multiplication is a model of [[Peano arithmetic|0QFT7Z76N5]].
By the [[soundness theorem|AVD7T7J063]], a theory with a model does not prove $0 = 1$.
So ZF proves $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$, a correct [[consistency|VM8TQ70CVJ]] proof.
We check it against the three results of an [[ordinal analysis|57PTMS2ADY]].

1. Notation system: the proof uses none.
2. Lower bound: the proof says nothing about which transfinite inductions PA proves.
3. Upper bound: the proof says nothing about which orders PA cannot prove well-founded.

All three results are missing, so this proof is not an ordinal analysis of PA.

## Remarks

The proof is correct, but it uses far more than PA.
It needs the infinite set $\mathbb{N}$, and it needs the truth of every arithmetical sentence in $\mathbb{N}$, which PA cannot define.
Gentzen's proof uses primitive recursive reasoning and one principle of quantifier-free induction along a primitive recursive order of type $\varepsilon_0$.
That principle is exactly what PA cannot prove, so Gentzen's proof measures what PA needs.
The same contrast holds for every model-theoretic consistency proof: it shows that a theory is consistent, but not how strong it is.

## Explanations

### L1 human

We can prove that arithmetic has no contradictions by pointing at the natural numbers: they satisfy all the axioms, so the axioms cannot contradict each other.
This argument is correct, but it rests on a much stronger theory of sets.
It does not tell us how strong arithmetic itself is, and that is what an ordinal analysis measures.

### L2 human

Compare what the two consistency proofs need.
The ZF proof needs a truth definition for all arithmetical formulas in $\mathbb{N}$, and PA cannot define one.
Gentzen's proof needs PRA, which is much weaker than PA, plus quantifier-free induction up to $\varepsilon_0$, which PA does not prove.
So Gentzen's proof locates the strength of PA between two known points, while the ZF proof only confirms consistency.
Watch out: both are called consistency proofs, but only one of them is an analysis.
