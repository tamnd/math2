## Summary

Model order reduction is the replacement of a large dynamical model by a much smaller one with nearly the same input-output behaviour.
Models from discretised partial differential equations, circuits or structures can have millions of states, which makes simulation and controller design expensive.
A reduced model keeps the main features, such as stability and the response at relevant frequencies, with far fewer states.
Standard methods are balanced truncation, based on [[Hankel singular values|VCPQ0B99MA]], and projection methods such as moment matching and proper orthogonal decomposition.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A detailed computer model of a bridge may track the motion of every small piece, which gives a huge system of equations.
For many purposes only a few vibration patterns matter.
Model order reduction finds a small model that reproduces the important behaviour, like a sketch that keeps the main lines of a picture.
For instance, a system with two decoupled states that decay like $e^{-t}$ and $e^{-100 t}$ can often be replaced by the slow state alone.
