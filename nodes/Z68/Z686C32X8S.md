## Summary

The thermal efficiency of a [[heat engine|S7XFW04T3V]] is the fraction of the heat it takes in that it turns into net work.
It is a number between $0$ and $1$, and by the second law it is always strictly less than $1$.
[[Carnot's theorem|4Y41F2C3P9]] shows that between reservoirs at temperatures $T_H$ and $T_C$ it can be at most $1 - T_C/T_H$.
For refrigerators and heat pumps the analogous measure is the [[coefficient of performance|W0Q5VTTFGX]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Efficiency answers the question: of all the heat I put in, how much came out as useful work?
If a car engine burns fuel that releases $100$ units of heat and the engine delivers $30$ units of work, its efficiency is $30$ percent.
The other $70$ units leave as warm exhaust and through the radiator.
No engine can reach $100$ percent, because some heat must always be passed on to the cold side.
