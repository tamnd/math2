## Summary

The equilibrium theory of tides assumes that the ocean surface adjusts at once to the [[tide-generating potential|2E2KCFNMHE]].
Then the surface is always a level surface of the total potential, gravity plus tidal.
It goes back to Newton's Principia.
It predicts two tidal bulges and the right periods and relative sizes of the main tides.
It does not predict the actual times and heights of tide at a coast, which depend on the dynamics in the [[Laplace tidal equations|9BF0QWFZA9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine an Earth covered by a deep ocean that responds instantly to the Moon's pull.
The water would pile up in two bulges, one under the Moon and one on the far side, about half a metre high.
As the Earth turns, each place would pass under both bulges and see two high tides a day.
Real oceans are too shallow and are blocked by continents, so the real tide lags behind and can be much larger or smaller.
The equilibrium tide is still the reference that tidal analysis starts from.
