## Summary

The Rescorla–Wagner model describes how the associative strength of cues changes during classical conditioning.
Robert Rescorla and Allan Wagner proposed it in 1972.
On each trial, every cue present changes its strength in proportion to the prediction error.
This error is the difference between what happened and what all present cues together predicted.
This shared error term explains effects such as blocking, which a cue-by-cue learning rule cannot explain.
The same error-correcting rule appears as the delta rule (least mean squares rule) for training linear models.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A dog hears a bell and then gets food.
At first the bell predicts nothing, so the food is a big surprise and the dog learns a lot.
As the bell comes to predict food, the surprise shrinks and learning slows down.
If a light that already predicts food is shown together with a new tone, there is no surprise left, so the tone learns almost nothing; this is blocking.
