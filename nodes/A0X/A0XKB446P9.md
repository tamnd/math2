## Definition

Let $k > 0$ and let a bounded scatterer lie inside a ball of radius $R$ centred at $0$.
For integers $n \ge 0$ and $\abs{m} \le n$, let $Y_n^m$ be the spherical harmonics.
Let $j_n$ be the spherical Bessel functions and $h_n^{(1)}$ the spherical Hankel functions of the first kind.
Set $\phi_{nm}(x) = j_n(kr) Y_n^m(\hat{x})$ and $\psi_{nm}(x) = h_n^{(1)}(kr) Y_n^m(\hat{x})$, with $r = \abs{x}$ and $\hat{x} = x / r$.
An incident solution of the [[Helmholtz equation|MNXKTXJ35G]] is expanded as $u_i = \sum_{n,m} a_{nm} \phi_{nm}$.
The scattered field is expanded for $r > R$ as $u_s = \sum_{n,m} p_{nm} \psi_{nm}$.
Since the scattering problem is linear, there is a linear map $T$ with
$$p_{nm} = \sum_{n', m'} T_{nm, n'm'} \, a_{n'm'}.$$
The matrix $T = (T_{nm, n'm'})$ is the *T-matrix* (transition matrix) of the scatterer.
The *T-matrix method* computes $T$, usually truncated at $n \le n_{\max}$, and obtains scattered fields from it.

## Remarks

For electromagnetic waves, the scalar functions are replaced by the vector spherical wave functions $M_{nm}$ and $N_{nm}$, and $T$ couples both families.

Example: for a sound-soft sphere of radius $a$ (with $u = 0$ on the sphere), $T$ is diagonal with $T_{nm,nm} = -j_n(ka) / h_n^{(1)}(ka)$.
Check: on $r = a$, the term $a_{nm} j_n(ka) Y_n^m + p_{nm} h_n^{(1)}(ka) Y_n^m$ must vanish for each $(n, m)$.

Non-example: for a non-spherical scatterer the T-matrix is in general not diagonal, because one incident spherical wave produces scattered waves of several orders.

A common way to compute $T$ for a non-spherical particle is the extended boundary condition method, also called the null-field method.
Rotating the particle conjugates $T$ by Wigner rotation matrices, which gives closed-form orientation averages.
