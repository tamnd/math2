## Definition

Let $E$ be an [[ellipse|0VKCCEXBJ1]] with semi-major axis $a > 0$ and semi-minor axis $b$, where $0 \le b \le a$.
The *flattening* of $E$ is
$$f = \frac{a - b}{a}.$$
The flattening of an ellipsoid of revolution is the flattening of its meridian ellipse, the section by a plane through the axis.

## Remarks

Example: the ellipse $x^2/25 + y^2/16 = 1$ has $a = 5$, $b = 4$, so $f = 1/5$.
Non-example: the formula is not used with $a < b$; for a prolate ellipse one first names the longer semi-axis $a$.
One always has $0 \le f \le 1$, with $f = 0$ exactly for a circle.
The flattening and the [[eccentricity|0HVX863SAJ]] $e = \sqrt{1 - b^2/a^2}$ are related by $e^2 = f(2 - f)$.
Geodesists often give the inverse flattening $1/f$, for example $1/f = 298.257223563$ for the WGS 84 ellipsoid.
