## Summary

A locally compact group is of type I when every factor representation generates a type I factor.
Then its unitary representations break up into irreducibles in an essentially unique way.
For such groups the [[unitary dual|95Q8WZMN0W]] is a well-behaved space, and the Plancherel theorem decomposes the regular representation as a direct integral of irreducibles.
Abelian groups, compact groups, connected semisimple Lie groups and connected nilpotent Lie groups are type I groups.
Non-abelian free groups are not, and for them the decomposition into irreducibles is far from unique.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a finite group, every representation is a sum of irreducible pieces, and the pieces are determined up to reordering.
For infinite groups this can fail badly: a representation can be split into irreducibles in two completely different ways.
Type I groups are those where this pathology does not happen.
They are the groups whose harmonic analysis looks like the classical one, with a nice space of basic harmonics.
