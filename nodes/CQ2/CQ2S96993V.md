## Statement

Let $u \colon \R^n_{\ge 0} \to \R$ be a continuous, locally nonsatiated and strictly quasiconcave utility function.
Let $x(p, w)$ be its [[Marshallian demand|EQPEPJ7QQZ]] and $v(p, w)$ its [[indirect utility function|N9S29DKCY8]].
Let $p \in \R^n$ have all entries positive and let $w > 0$.
Assume that $v$ is differentiable at $(p, w)$ and that $\partial v / \partial w\,(p, w) \ne 0$.
Then, for every good $\ell \in \set{1, \dots, n}$,
$$x_\ell(p, w) = -\frac{\partial v / \partial p_\ell\,(p, w)}{\partial v / \partial w\,(p, w)}.$$

## Remarks

Set $\bar{u} = v(p, w)$; the function $p' \mapsto v(p', e(p', \bar{u})) - \bar{u}$ is zero for all $p'$, where $e$ is the expenditure function.
Differentiating at $p' = p$ in $p_\ell$ gives $\partial v / \partial p_\ell + (\partial v / \partial w)\, \partial e / \partial p_\ell = 0$.
By [[Shephard's lemma|T968JGCJHC]], $\partial e / \partial p_\ell = h_\ell(p, \bar{u}) = x_\ell(p, w)$, where $h$ is the [[Hicksian demand|ZFX4T69SST]], and the identity follows.
Example: for $v(p, w) = w / (2\sqrt{p_1 p_2})$, $\partial v / \partial p_1 = -w / (4 p_1 \sqrt{p_1 p_2})$ and $\partial v / \partial w = 1 / (2\sqrt{p_1 p_2})$, and the formula gives $x_1 = w / (2 p_1)$.
The condition $\partial v / \partial w \ne 0$ is needed to divide; it holds when the marginal utility of wealth is positive.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If one knows how happy a consumer can be at each price and income, one can read off what the consumer buys.
Raising the price of a good by a little hurts the consumer in proportion to how much of the good they buy.
Roy's identity turns this into a formula: demand is the harm from a price rise divided by the benefit of extra money.

### L2 human

Roy's identity lets one start from an indirect utility function, which is often easier to write down, and derive the demand by differentiation.
It is the analogue for the Marshallian demand of Shephard's lemma for the Hicksian demand, and both are consequences of the envelope theorem.
Together with the Slutsky equation it is one of the basic duality identities of consumer theory.
