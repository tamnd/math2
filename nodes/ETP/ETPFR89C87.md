## Statement

Let $S$ be a finite set and $\Omega = S^{\Z^d}$ with the product topology and Borel $\sigma$-algebra.
Let $\Phi = (\Phi_A)$ be an interaction: $A$ runs over finite subsets of $\Z^d$, and $\Phi_A \colon \Omega \to \R$ depends only on the spins in $A$.
Assume $\sup_{x \in \Z^d} \sum_{A \ni x} \norm{\Phi_A}_\infty < \infty$, and let $\beta > 0$.
For finite $\Lambda \subset \Z^d$ and $\omega \in \Omega$, let $\sigma_\Lambda \omega_{\Lambda^c}$ be the configuration equal to $\sigma$ on $\Lambda$ and to $\omega$ off $\Lambda$.
Put $H_\Lambda^\omega(\sigma) = \sum_{A \cap \Lambda \ne \emptyset} \Phi_A(\sigma_\Lambda \omega_{\Lambda^c})$ and
$$\gamma_\Lambda(\sigma_\Lambda \mid \omega) = \frac{e^{-\beta H_\Lambda^\omega(\sigma)}}{\sum_{\eta \in S^\Lambda} e^{-\beta H_\Lambda^\omega(\eta)}}.$$
A [[probability measure|059TJN0NZ1]] $\mu$ on $\Omega$ satisfies the *DLR equations* if, for every finite $\Lambda$ and every $\sigma_\Lambda \in S^\Lambda$, $\mu(\sigma_\Lambda \mid \mathcal{F}_{\Lambda^c})(\omega) = \gamma_\Lambda(\sigma_\Lambda \mid \omega)$ for $\mu$-almost every $\omega$.
Here $\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda^c}$ is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the spins off $\Lambda$, and the left side is a [[conditional probability|0TTDGTEFMN]].
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be the set of these measures, the Gibbs measures for $\beta \Phi$.

1. $\mathcal{G}$ is nonempty, convex and compact for the [[weak topology|MBW9JCGZC1]].
2. For each $\omega \in \Omega$, every weak limit point of $\gamma_{\Lambda_n}(\cdot \mid \omega)$, along cubes $\Lambda_n \uparrow \Z^d$, lies in $\mathcal{G}$.
3. The extreme points of $\mathcal{G}$ are exactly the measures in $\mathcal{G}$ that are trivial on the tail $\sigma$-algebra.

## Remarks

Proof idea: summability makes $\omega \mapsto \gamma_\Lambda(\sigma_\Lambda \mid \omega)$ continuous, which lets the DLR equations pass to weak limits; compactness of $\Omega$ then gives limit points.
The kernels are consistent: $\gamma_\Delta \gamma_\Lambda = \gamma_\Delta$ for $\Lambda \subseteq \Delta$, and this is why finite-volume measures with boundary conditions converge to solutions.
Example: the [[Ising model|R0KGM32QN6]] on $\Z^2$ with $h = 0$ and large $\beta$ has two different limits, with all-plus and all-minus boundary conditions, so $\mathcal{G}$ has more than one element.
In dimension one with a translation-invariant finite-range interaction, $\mathcal{G}$ has exactly one element for every $\beta$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For an infinite system one cannot write the Boltzmann weight $e^{-\beta H}$ directly, because the total energy is infinite.
The DLR equations ask instead that, inside every finite window, the spins follow the Boltzmann rule given what is outside.
A measure with this local property is a Gibbs measure for the infinite system.

### L2 human

The DLR approach, due to Dobrushin and to Lanford and Ruelle in the late 1960s, separates local rules from global behaviour.
The local rules always have solutions, but there can be several, and their number is the mathematical meaning of phase coexistence.
Extreme Gibbs measures are the pure phases, and every Gibbs measure is a unique mixture of them.
The [[Dobrushin uniqueness criterion|8JH9AJ4P2A]] gives a condition for a unique solution, and the [[Gibbs variational principle|BKWBKNEAKA]] characterizes the translation-invariant ones.
