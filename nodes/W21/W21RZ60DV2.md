## Statement

Let $n \ge 1$ and $\alpha > 0$.
Let $f \colon \R^n \times [0, \infty) \to \R$ have compact support, two continuous derivatives in $x$ and one continuous derivative in $t$.
Let $\Phi(x, t) = (4 \pi \alpha t)^{-n/2} e^{-\abs{x}^2 / (4 \alpha t)}$ for $t > 0$ be the [[heat kernel|KD404MF042]].
For $0 \le s < t$, let $v(x, t; s) = \int_{\R^n} \Phi(x - y, t - s) \, f(y, s) \, dy$.
This solves $\partial_t v = \alpha \Delta v$ for $t > s$ with $v(\cdot, t; s) \to f(\cdot, s)$ as $t \to s$.
Define
$$u(x, t) = \int_0^t v(x, t; s) \, ds.$$
Then $u$ has two continuous derivatives in $x$ and one in $t$ on $\R^n \times (0, \infty)$.
It solves
$$\partial_t u - \alpha \Delta u = f \quad \text{in } \R^n \times (0, \infty),$$
and $u(x, t) \to 0$ as $(x, t) \to (x_0, 0)$, for every $x_0 \in \R^n$.

## Remarks

The idea is to view the source as a stream of initial data: at each time $s$, the slice $f(\cdot, s) \, ds$ starts a free evolution of the [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]].
Formally, $\partial_t u = v(x, t; t) + \int_0^t \partial_t v \, ds = f(x, t) + \alpha \Delta u$.
The rigorous proof moves derivatives onto $f$ by a change of variables, which is why $f$ is assumed smooth with compact support.

Example: let $n = 1$ and $f(x, t) = g(x) \psi(t)$, where $g$ and $\psi$ are smooth with compact support.
Then $u(x, t) = \int_0^t \psi(s) \, (\Phi(\cdot, t - s) * g)(x) \, ds$, where $*$ is convolution in $x$.
It is the temperature of an initially cold rod heated by a source of shape $g$ and strength $\psi(t)$.

With initial data $u(\cdot, 0) = g$, one adds the solution $\Phi(\cdot, t) * g$ of the homogeneous problem.
It is the same principle as [[Duhamel's principle|Q28DM6ZCMF]] for linear ordinary differential equations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heat sources that act over a time span are hard to handle directly.
Duhamel's principle cuts the time span into thin slices.
Each slice of source is treated as an instant burst of heat, which then spreads by the ordinary heat equation.
The temperature at the end is the sum of all these spread-out bursts.

### L2 human

The principle reduces the inhomogeneous [[heat equation|VP2HN86VAG]] to the homogeneous one, which the [[heat kernel|KD404MF042]] solves.
The solution operator $g \mapsto \Phi(\cdot, t) * g$ plays the role of the propagator $e^{(t - s) A}$ in the ODE version.
In heat transfer texts, the same idea also handles boundary temperatures that vary in time.
One superposes the responses to step changes of the boundary data, weighted by their rates of change.
