## Definition

A linear map is a function between two vector spaces over the same field that preserves sums and scalar multiples.
Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ and $W$ be [[vector spaces|000AYPA9AJ]] over $F$.
A function $T \colon V \to W$ is *linear* when, for all $u, v \in V$ and all $a \in F$,
$$T(u + v) = T(u) + T(v), \qquad T(av) = a\, T(v).$$
The first condition is *additivity*, and the second condition is *homogeneity*.
A linear function $T \colon V \to W$ is called a *linear map* from $V$ to $W$.

## Notation

The terms "linear map", "linear transformation", "linear operator" and "homomorphism of vector spaces" name the same object.
Some books use "operator" only for linear maps from a space to itself.
The set of linear maps from $V$ to $W$ is $\Hom_F(V, W)$, also written $\mathcal{L}(V, W)$.

## Remarks

Each condition says that one operation of the vector space can be done before or after $T$ with the same result.
Additivity matches the sum in $V$ with the sum in $W$, and homogeneity matches the scaling in $V$ with the scaling in $W$.
Both conditions are needed, because neither one implies the other: complex conjugation on $\C$ is additive but not homogeneous over $\C$, and $(x, y) \mapsto \sqrt[3]{x^3 + y^3}$ on $\R^2$ is homogeneous but not additive.

The scalars on both sides come from the same field $F$, and that is why $V$ and $W$ must be spaces over one field.
The two conditions together are equivalent to the single condition $T(au + v) = a\,T(u) + T(v)$ for all $u, v \in V$ and $a \in F$, by the [[lemma on linear combinations|STYEWH8R4E]].

For a small example, $T(x, y) = (3x - y, \; 2y)$ on $\R^2$ is linear.
For a failing case, $t(x, y) = (x + 1, \; y)$ is not, since $t(0 + 0) = (1, 0)$ while $t(0) + t(0) = (2, 0)$; this is the [[translation non-example|DTCM6N02A9]].
The [[concept page|0NRYHJPQG2]] gives more examples and the first properties.
Mathlib defines `LinearMap` for modules over semirings, with a ring homomorphism that twists the scalars, and the case here is the identity twist over a field.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A linear map respects the two operations of a vector space.
The image of a sum is the sum of the images, and the image of a scaled vector is the scaled image.
In the plane, a linear map sends each line through the origin to a line through the origin or to the origin itself.

### L2 human

To check the definition for $T(x, y) = (3x - y, \; 2y)$ on $\R^2$, take $u = (x_1, y_1)$ and $v = (x_2, y_2)$.
Apply $T$ to the sum and split each entry:
$$T(u + v) = (3x_1 + 3x_2 - y_1 - y_2, \; 2y_1 + 2y_2) = T(u) + T(v).$$
For a real $a$, the factor comes out of each entry: $T(au) = (3ax_1 - ay_1, \; 2ay_1) = a\,T(u)$.
So $T$ is linear.
Watch out: checking only one condition is not enough, as complex conjugation on $\C$ shows.
