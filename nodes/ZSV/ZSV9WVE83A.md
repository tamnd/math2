## Definition

*Forced convection* is [[convective heat transfer|RK24AYNG4Z]] between a surface and a fluid in which an external agent moves the fluid relative to the surface.
Such agents are a pump, a fan, a pressure difference, a free stream or the motion of the surface.
The flow exists whether or not there are temperature differences.

## Remarks

Example: water pumped through a heated pipe.
The pump sets the flow, and the water would move in the same way if the pipe were cold.

Non-example: still air around a hot radiator.
The air moves only because the heated air is lighter, so this is natural convection.

When buoyancy is negligible, dimensional analysis gives $\mathrm{Nu} = f(\mathrm{Re}, \Pr)$ for the [[Nusselt number|THH3V9AHDN]] in terms of the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]] and the [[Prandtl number|C4KSBMR413]].
When the ratio $\mathrm{Gr}/\mathrm{Re}^2$ of the Grashof number to the squared Reynolds number is of order $1$, both effects matter, and one speaks of mixed convection.
