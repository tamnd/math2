## Example

We derive $\Rightarrow \neg p \vee \neg\neg p$ without cut in Avron's calculus HLC from the [[definition of hypersequent calculus|3PR9PEMXJ4]].
Here $\neg A$ abbreviates $A \to \bot$.
The communication rule is the following:
$$\dfrac{G \mid \Gamma_1, \Delta_1 \Rightarrow \Pi_1 \qquad G \mid \Gamma_2, \Delta_2 \Rightarrow \Pi_2}{G \mid \Gamma_1, \Gamma_2 \Rightarrow \Pi_1 \mid \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \Rightarrow \Pi_2} \ (\mathsf{com})$$

### The derivation

1. The sequents $p \Rightarrow p$ and $\bot \Rightarrow$ are axioms.
2. Apply ${\to}$L to them, with $\Gamma = p$, $A = p$, $B = \bot$ and $\Delta$ empty.
   This gives $p \to \bot, p \Rightarrow$, that is, $\neg p, p \Rightarrow$.
3. Apply $\mathsf{com}$ to two copies of step 2, with $G$ empty, $\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2 = p$, $\Delta_1 = \Delta_2 = \neg p$ and $\Pi_1 = \Pi_2$ empty.
   Each premise $\Gamma_i, \Delta_i \Rightarrow \Pi_i$ is $p, \neg p \Rightarrow$, which is step 2.
4. The conclusion is $p, p \Rightarrow \; \mid \; \neg p, \neg p \Rightarrow$.
5. Internal contraction on $p$ in the first component and on $\neg p$ in the second gives $p \Rightarrow \; \mid \; \neg p \Rightarrow$.
6. Internal weakening on the right of both components gives $p \Rightarrow \bot \mid \neg p \Rightarrow \bot$.
7. ${\to}$R on the first component gives $\Rightarrow \neg p \mid \neg p \Rightarrow \bot$.
8. ${\to}$R on the second component gives $\Rightarrow \neg p \mid \Rightarrow \neg p \to \bot$, that is, $\Rightarrow \neg p \mid \Rightarrow \neg\neg p$.
9. ${\vee}$R on each component gives $\Rightarrow \neg p \vee \neg\neg p \mid \Rightarrow \neg p \vee \neg\neg p$.
10. External contraction gives $\Rightarrow \neg p \vee \neg\neg p$, and no step uses cut.

### The formula is not intuitionistic

We check that intuitionistic logic does not prove the formula, so the communication rule is necessary.

11. Take a Kripke model with a root $r$ and two incomparable worlds $a$ and $b$ above $r$, with $p$ true at $a$ only.
12. At $r$, $\neg p$ fails, because $a \ge r$ forces $p$.
13. At $r$, $\neg\neg p$ fails, because $b \ge r$ forces $\neg p$: no world above $b$ forces $p$.
14. So $r$ forces neither disjunct, and $\neg p \vee \neg\neg p$ fails at $r$.

## Remarks

The communication step uses the general form of the rule, in which each conclusion component keeps part of each premise.
The special form that only swaps succedents gives $p, \neg p \Rightarrow \; \mid \; p, \neg p \Rightarrow$ from two copies of step 2, and that does not help.
The general form separates $p$ from $\neg p$: one component keeps both copies of $p$, and the other keeps both copies of $\neg p$.

What changes on a model whose worlds form a chain?
There the formula holds at every world $w$.
If no world above $w$ forces $p$, then $w$ forces $\neg p$.
If some $v \ge w$ forces $p$, take any $u \ge w$; it is comparable with $v$, so $v$ is above $u$ or $u$ forces $p$ by persistence.
Either way some world above $u$ forces $p$, so no world above $w$ forces $\neg p$, and $w$ forces $\neg\neg p$.
The [[Kripke semantics|NGHWZ9Y7ZV]] check in steps 11 to 14 needs a model that is not a chain.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The weak excluded middle says: either $p$ is impossible, or the impossibility of $p$ is impossible.
Intuitionistic logic cannot prove it, because two possible futures can disagree about $p$.
The communication rule assumes that any two situations are comparable.
With that rule we start from the fact "$p$ and not $p$ give a contradiction" and split it into two alternatives.
One alternative says "$p$ gives a contradiction", and the other says "not $p$ gives a contradiction".

### L2 human

The derivation has four phases.
First, ${\to}$L builds $\neg p, p \Rightarrow$ from two axioms.
Second, communication on two copies sends both copies of $p$ to one component and both copies of $\neg p$ to the other.
Third, contraction, weakening with $\bot$ and ${\to}$R in each component give $\Rightarrow \neg p \mid \Rightarrow \neg\neg p$.
Fourth, ${\vee}$R in each component and external contraction finish.
The typical use is to show that HLC derives theorems of LC that are not instances of the prelinearity axiom.
Watch out: the special form of communication, which only swaps succedents, cannot separate $p$ from $\neg p$ in the second phase.
