## Summary

A unitary element of a unital $*$-algebra is an element whose adjoint is its inverse.
In the algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space these are the [[unitary operators|MM0EJ2GDE4]], and in a matrix algebra they are the [[unitary matrices|0AR68JTMDT]].
In a [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]], unitaries have norm $1$ and spectrum in the unit circle.
Their homotopy classes define the K-group [[K1|7NTVTHC7CG]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A unitary element is the algebra form of a rotation: it is invertible, and undoing it is the same as taking its adjoint.
For complex numbers the adjoint is the complex conjugate, so the unitaries of $\C$ are the numbers $z$ with $\abs{z} = 1$.
In the algebra $C(X)$ of continuous functions on a compact space, a unitary is a function with values on the unit circle.
The unitaries of an algebra form a group under multiplication.
