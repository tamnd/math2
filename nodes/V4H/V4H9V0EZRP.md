## Summary

The discrete dipole approximation is a numerical method for the [[scattering|8BQYB8H5CT]] of light by a particle of arbitrary shape.
The particle is replaced by a finite array of polarizable points, each of which becomes an oscillating dipole.
Each dipole responds to the incident field plus the fields of all other dipoles, which gives a linear system for the dipole moments.
It is widely used for dust grains, ice crystals and nanoparticles whose shape is too irregular for exact series solutions.
See the [[definition|BDHTRF8RMF]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cut a particle into many tiny cubes.
Each cube is small compared with the wavelength, so it acts like a tiny antenna: the light makes its charges swing back and forth.
Each tiny antenna feels the incoming light and also the light sent out by all the others.
Solving for how strongly every antenna swings gives the scattered light as the sum of their waves.
A snow crystal with a few thousand cubes is a typical case.
