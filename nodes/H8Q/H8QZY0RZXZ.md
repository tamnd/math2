## Summary

This topic is a part of [[mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]].
It covers what logic studies, the general notions that all logics share, such as formal systems, rules, consequence, consistency, soundness and completeness, and the programs for the foundations of mathematics that shaped the field.
The sub-area treats logic as a branch of mathematics; the use of logic in everyday proofs belongs to [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]]: the map of the area, its branches and its main theorems.
1. [[Foundations of mathematics|EFFRJFW67Y]]: what a foundation must supply, and the set-theoretic, type-theoretic and categorical answers.
1. [[History of mathematical logic|WP3602P9EH]]: from Aristotle and the Stoics to Gödel, Gentzen, Tarski, Church and Turing.
1. [[Aristotelian syllogistic|MZBNX2T6Q6]]: the first formal logic, with categorical propositions and valid syllogisms.
1. [[Square of opposition|VYNBSVZ2TH]]: the four relations between the categorical propositions, and existential import.
1. [[Syllogistic figures|S4BTTGFR6J]]: the four positions of the middle term.
1. [[Syllogistic moods|1TD6HRM5NG]]: the 256 forms and the 24 valid moods.
1. [[Formal system|439M8B4DY2]]: alphabet, formulas, axioms, rules of inference, derivations and theorems.
1. [[Object language and metalanguage|D9G8M42GKC]]: the two levels of language, use and mention, metatheorems.
1. [[Rule of inference|QPD3X471Q1]]: schematic rules, and derivable versus admissible rules.
1. [[Consequence relation|J78Y1M4GND]]: Tarski's axioms of reflexivity, monotonicity and cut.
1. [[Abstract logical systems|RQXV7PM2WT]]: logics as consequence plus semantics, institutions and logical frameworks.
1. [[Consistency|VM8TQ70CVJ]]: syntactic consistency, satisfiability, and the limits of consistency proofs.
1. [[Post consistency and Post completeness|WK2P0E82B4]]: absolute consistency, and the maximality of classical propositional logic.
1. [[Soundness|4A84VHDTHV]]: every provable formula is valid, by induction on derivations.
1. [[Conservative extension|N238J43XNE]]: a larger theory that proves no new sentences of the old language.
1. [[Extension by definitions|GCK0NN60E4]]: new symbols that can be eliminated and prove nothing new.
1. [[Relative consistency proof|X3G0RG2GPZ]]: consistency of one theory from consistency of another.
1. [[Hilbert's program|P1RW6JWX73]]: finitary consistency proofs, and the effect of the second incompleteness theorem.
1. [[Existential graphs|D3ZS6WHPM1]]: Peirce's diagrammatic logic.
1. [[Logical constants and logicality|2QQ7Q5SKP8]]: which notions are logical, by permutation invariance or by harmony.
