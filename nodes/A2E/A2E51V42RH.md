## Summary

A Love wave is a horizontally polarized shear wave trapped in a soft surface layer that lies on a stiffer half-space.
The ground moves sideways, parallel to the surface and across the direction of travel, and the motion decays with depth in the half-space.
Love waves are dispersive: their speed depends on the wavelength and lies between the [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]] speeds of the layer and of the half-space.
A. E. H. Love explained them in 1911; they do not exist on a homogeneous half-space, so they show that the Earth's crust is layered.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a slow soft layer of soil on top of hard rock.
A sideways shaking wave entering the soil keeps bouncing between the free surface and the rock below, and stays trapped in the layer.
The trapped wave runs along the surface, moving the ground from side to side.
Long waves reach deeper into the fast rock and so travel faster than short ones.
