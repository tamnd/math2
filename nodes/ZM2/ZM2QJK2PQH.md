## Statement

Let $A$ be a finite algebra, and let $V(A)$ be the [[variety|AJNZJD8442]] generated by $A$.
Then exactly one of the following holds.

1. There is a natural number $n$ such that every [[subdirectly irreducible|DASF939439]] algebra in $V(A)$ has at most $n$ elements.
2. $V(A)$ is residually large: for every cardinal $\kappa$, some subdirectly irreducible algebra in $V(A)$ has more than $\kappa$ elements.

## Remarks

The conjecture is also called the RS conjecture.
Hobby and McKenzie stated it in 1988 in their book "The Structure of Finite Algebras".
It says that a [[residually small|TH2XQGGFWH]] variety generated by a finite algebra always has a finite bound on the sizes of its subdirectly irreducible members.

The conjecture is false.
By 1993 McKenzie had built an eight-element algebra $A$ such that $V(A)$ has no finite bound on its subdirectly irreducible members and yet is not residually large.
With the same method he showed that no algorithm decides, for a finite algebra $A$ of finite signature, whether $V(A)$ has such a finite bound.

The conjecture holds for some classes of algebras.
Suppose that $V(A)$ is [[congruence-distributive|9GZ7F6ETC6]].
Then [[Jónsson's lemma|VT72G3BA99]] puts every subdirectly irreducible member of $V(A)$ in $HS(A)$, so alternative 1 holds with $n = \abs{A}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every algebra in a variety is built from irreducible pieces, the subdirectly irreducible algebras.
For a variety generated by one finite algebra, one can ask how large these pieces can get.
The conjecture said there are only two possibilities: either all pieces are smaller than some fixed number, or there are pieces of every infinite size.
McKenzie found a finite algebra for which neither happens, so the conjecture is false.
