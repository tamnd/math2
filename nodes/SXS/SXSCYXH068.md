## Summary

Combustion theory is the mathematical study of flows in which a fuel burns.
It couples a fast exothermic chemical reaction, whose rate depends strongly on temperature, with the transport of heat and species by convection and diffusion.
The resulting equations are nonlinear [[reaction–diffusion|TAAHWE917M]] systems coupled to [[fluid dynamics|F2JRWJNYN4]].
Their main solutions are flames, ignition and extinction events, and [[detonations|FBR0PYYJJ7]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A candle flame is a thin bright layer where fuel vapour meets air and burns.
The burning releases heat, the heat spreads to the cold gas nearby, and the gas there starts to burn too.
Combustion theory writes this loop as equations and asks how fast a flame moves, how thick it is, and when it goes out.
A key feature is that the reaction is almost off at room temperature and very fast at flame temperature.
This sharp switch makes flames thin, and it lets mathematicians treat a flame as a moving surface.
