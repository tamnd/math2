## Summary

A strategy is rationalizable if it survives repeated removal of strategies that are never a best response.
It is what a player may play when all players are rational, and this is common knowledge, but they need not agree on beliefs.
The concept was introduced by B. Douglas Bernheim and David Pearce in 1984.
Every strategy used in a Nash equilibrium is rationalizable, but not conversely.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A rational player only uses a strategy that is best against some guess about the others.
Knowing that the others are rational, she only guesses that they use such strategies.
Repeating this reasoning removes more and more strategies, and what is left is rationalizable.
In the prisoner's dilemma only "defect" survives, because "cooperate" is never best against any guess.
In matching pennies nothing is removed, so all strategies are rationalizable.
