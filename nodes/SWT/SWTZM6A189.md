## Statement

Let *n* be odd, square-free and greater than 3. Then

$$\Phi_n(z) = U_n^2(z) - (-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}nzV_n^2(z)$$

for certain polynomials *U<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) and *V<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) with integer coefficients, *U<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) of degree *φ*(*n*)/2, and *V<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) of degree *φ*(*n*)/2 − 1. This can also be written

$$\Phi_n \left ((-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}z \right ) = C_n^2(z) - nzD_n^2(z).$$

If *n* is even, square-free and greater than 2 (this forces *n*/2 to be odd),

$$\Phi_{\frac{n}{2}} (-z^2) = \Phi_{2n}(z)= C_n^2(z) - nzD_n^2(z)$$

for *C<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) and *D<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) with integer coefficients, *C<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) of degree *φ*(*n*), and *D<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) of degree *φ*(*n*) − 1. *C<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) and *D<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) are both palindromic.

The first few cases are:

$$\begin{aligned} \Phi_3(-z) &=\Phi_6(z) =z^2-z+1 \\ &= (z+1)^2 - 3z \\[6pt] \Phi_5(z) &=z^4+z^3+z^2+z+1 \\ &= (z^2+3z+1)^2 - 5z(z+1)^2 \\[6pt] \Phi_{6/2}(-z^2) &=\Phi_{12}(z)=z^4-z^2+1 \\ &= (z^2+3z+1)^2 - 6z(z+1)^2 \end{aligned}$$
