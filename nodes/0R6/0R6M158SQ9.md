## Definition

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a vector space over $F$, and let $U$ be a [[subspace|0EPTJA5QV4]] of $V$.
The *annihilator* of $U$ is the subset of the [[dual space|0M856Q1AMZ]]
$$U^0 = \set{f \in V^* : f(u) = 0 \text{ for all } u \in U}.$$

## Notation

Other notations are $U^\circ$, $\operatorname{Ann}(U)$ and $U^\perp$.
Mathlib writes `Submodule.dualAnnihilator`.

## Remarks

- The annihilator $U^0$ is a subspace of $V^*$: it contains $0$, and if $f(u) = g(u) = 0$, then $(af + bg)(u) = 0$.
- The same formula defines $S^0$ for any subset $S \subseteq V$, and $S^0 = (\operatorname{span} S)^0$.
- If $U_1 \subseteq U_2$, then $U_2^0 \subseteq U_1^0$.
- In finite dimension, $\dim U + \dim U^0 = \dim V$; see the [[dimension of the annihilator|06Q1EFVH0Q]].
- The annihilator of the line spanned by $(1, 1)$ in $F^2$ is spanned by the form $(x, y) \mapsto x - y$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The annihilator of a subspace collects the linear forms that are zero on all of it.
In $\R^3$, the forms that vanish on a plane through the origin are the multiples of one form, whose kernel is that plane.
A larger subspace has a smaller annihilator.
