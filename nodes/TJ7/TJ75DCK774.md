## Proof

We take complex Jordan chains, choose the chains of $\bar\mu$ as the conjugates of the chains of $\mu$, and replace each conjugate pair of chains by the real and imaginary parts of one of them.
Write $\bar v$ for the entrywise [[complex conjugate|0FTH2E9TAS]] of $v \in \mathbb{C}^n$, and $W_\mu = \ker_{\mathbb{C}} (A - \mu I)^n$ for $\mu \in \mathbb{C}$.

1. By the [[fundamental theorem of algebra|0JP0W07Y9A]], $\chi_A$ splits over $\mathbb{C}$. By the [[decomposition into generalized eigenspaces|0Z3GDY89T5]], $\mathbb{C}^n$ is the direct sum of the $W_\mu$ over the distinct roots $\mu$ of $\chi_A$.
2. The polynomial $\chi_A$ has real coefficients, so its nonreal roots come in pairs $\mu = a + ib$ and $\bar\mu = a - ib$ with $b > 0$.
3. Since $A$ is real, $\overline{(A - \mu I)^n v} = (A - \bar\mu I)^n \bar v$. So conjugation maps $W_\mu$ onto $W_{\bar\mu}$, and it maps a chain for $A - \mu I$ to a chain of the same length for $A - \bar\mu I$.
4. Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ be a real root, and let $U_\lambda = \ker_{\mathbb{R}} (A - \lambda I)^n \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$. Gaussian elimination on the real matrix $(A - \lambda I)^n$ gives a basis of its null space with real entries, and the same vectors span the complex null space, so $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} U_\lambda = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} W_\lambda$.
5. The space $U_\lambda$ is $A$-invariant, and $A - \lambda I$ is nilpotent on it. By the [[normal form of nilpotent endomorphisms|Z2RTKWZZAZ]] over $\mathbb{R}$, $U_\lambda$ has a real chain basis, in which $A|_{U_\lambda}$ has the matrix $\bigoplus J_k(\lambda)$.
6. Real vectors that are independent over $\mathbb{R}$ stay independent over $\mathbb{C}$, since the rank of a real matrix is the same over both fields. So by step 4 this real basis is also a $\mathbb{C}$-basis of $W_\lambda$.
7. Now fix $\mu = a + ib$ with $b > 0$. By the normal form over $\mathbb{C}$, $W_\mu$ has a chain basis for $A - \mu I$. Take one chain $z_1, \dots, z_k$, with $A z_s = \mu z_s + z_{s-1}$ and $z_0 = 0$.
8. Write $z_s = x_s + i y_s$ with $x_s, y_s \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Expanding $A(x_s + i y_s) = (a + ib)(x_s + i y_s) + x_{s-1} + i y_{s-1}$ and comparing real and imaginary parts gives
$$A y_s = a y_s + b x_s + y_{s-1}, \qquad A x_s = -b y_s + a x_s + x_{s-1}.$$
9. In the ordered list $y_1, x_1, y_2, x_2, \dots, y_k, x_k$, step 8 says that the column of $y_s$ has $a, b$ in the places of $y_s, x_s$ and $1$ in the place of $y_{s-1}$. The column of $x_s$ has $-b, a$ in the places of $y_s, x_s$ and $1$ in the place of $x_{s-1}$. So if these $2k$ vectors are independent, $A$ acts on their span by the matrix $C_k(a, b)$.
10. We have $x_s = (z_s + \bar z_s)/2$ and $y_s = (z_s - \bar z_s)/(2i)$, and conversely $z_s = x_s + i y_s$ and $\bar z_s = x_s - i y_s$. So by step 3, the complex span of all the $x_s, y_s$ from the chains of $W_\mu$ is $W_\mu \oplus W_{\bar\mu}$.
11. Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the family of the real chain vectors of step 5 for all real $\lambda$, together with the vectors $y_s, x_s$ of steps 7 to 9 for all chains and all $\mu$ with $b > 0$. By steps 1, 6 and 10, $\mathcal{B}$ spans $\mathbb{C}^n$ over $\mathbb{C}$.
12. The family $\mathcal{B}$ has $\sum_\lambda \dim W_\lambda + \sum_{b > 0} 2 \dim W_\mu = n$ members, since $\dim W_{\bar\mu} = \dim W_\mu$ by step 3. So $\mathcal{B}$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-basis of $\mathbb{C}^n$.
13. Real vectors that are independent over $\mathbb{C}$ are independent over $\mathbb{R}$. So $\mathcal{B}$ consists of $n$ independent vectors of $\mathbb{R}^n$, and it is a basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
14. Let $P$ be the real matrix with the vectors of $\mathcal{B}$ as columns. By steps 5 and 9 and the [[change of basis formula|0MQXVZEBMT]], $P^{-1} A P$ is block diagonal with the blocks $J_k(\lambda)$ and $C_k(a, b)$. This proves part 1.
15. For part 2, let $C = C_k(a, b)$ act on $\mathbb{R}^{2k}$ with the standard basis $f_1, \dots, f_{2k}$. Reading the columns as in step 9, $C f_{2s-1} = a f_{2s-1} + b f_{2s} + f_{2s-3}$ and $C f_{2s} = -b f_{2s-1} + a f_{2s} + f_{2s-2}$, with $f_{-1} = f_0 = 0$.
16. Let $w_s = f_{2s} + i f_{2s-1}$, which plays the part of $x_s + i y_s$. Then
$$C w_s = (a + ib) f_{2s} + (-b + ia) f_{2s-1} + w_{s-1} = \mu w_s + w_{s-1},$$
since $-b + ia = i(a + ib)$. Conjugating gives $C \bar w_s = \bar\mu \bar w_s + \bar w_{s-1}$.
17. The $2k$ vectors $w_s, \bar w_s$ span every $f_j$ over $\mathbb{C}$, so they form a basis. In the order $w_1, \dots, w_k, \bar w_1, \dots, \bar w_k$ the matrix of $C$ is $J_k(\mu) \oplus J_k(\bar\mu)$. This proves part 2.
18. For part 3, let $A$ be similar over $\mathbb{R}$ to a matrix $R'$ of the form in part 1. By part 2, $A$ is similar over $\mathbb{C}$ to the Jordan matrix whose blocks are the blocks $J_k(\lambda)$ of $R'$ and the pairs $J_k(a + ib) \oplus J_k(a - ib)$ for the blocks $C_k(a, b)$ of $R'$.
19. By the uniqueness part of the [[Jordan normal form|085HQ5G5TE]], this complex Jordan matrix is fixed by $A$ up to order. Since $b > 0$, the list of blocks $J_k(a + ib)$ with $b > 0$ fixes the list of blocks $C_k(a, b)$, so $R'$ is fixed by $A$ up to the order of the blocks. This proves part 3.

## Idea

Start from the complex Jordan form, which exists by the fundamental theorem of algebra.
Because $A$ is real, conjugation carries chains of $\mu$ to chains of $\bar\mu$, so the two conjugate eigenvalues can share one set of real vectors: the real and imaginary parts of the chains of $\mu$.
On these real vectors $A$ acts by the rotation-scaling blocks, while real eigenvalues keep their real chains.

## Variants

A proof without complex numbers uses the primary decomposition of $\mathbb{R}^n$ under the factors $(x - \lambda)^m$ and $((x - a)^2 + b^2)^m$ of $\chi_A$.
On a primary part of the second kind, a chain argument for the nilpotent map $(A - aI)^2 + b^2 I$ then builds the blocks $C_k(a, b)$.
A third route starts from the primary [[rational canonical form|042JWAR3Q0]] over $\mathbb{R}$ and changes the basis inside each cyclic block.
