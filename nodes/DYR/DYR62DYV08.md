## Summary

An amphidromic point is a point of the sea where the amplitude of a given tidal constituent is zero.
Around it the time of high water changes steadily, so that the crest of the tide wave rotates around the point once per tidal period.
Amphidromic points arise because the tide in an ocean basin is a wave deflected by the Earth's rotation, as described by the [[Laplace tidal equations|9BF0QWFZA9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a large basin, the tide does not simply go up and down everywhere at once.
The high water travels around the basin like the hand of a clock.
At the centre of the clock the water level hardly changes at all: this is the amphidromic point.
Lines of equal time of high water fan out from it like spokes, and the range of the tide grows with distance from it.
The North Sea is a well-known example with three such points for the main lunar tide.
