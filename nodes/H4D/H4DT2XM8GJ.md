## Statement

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic not $2$, and let $J$ be a [[Jordan algebra|6C2MCVB8C7]] over $K$.
Let $x, y \in J$.
Then the subalgebra of $J$ generated by $x$ and $y$ is [[special|T4AWF1BRZB]].
That is, it is isomorphic to a subalgebra of an algebra $B^+$ for some associative algebra $B$ over $K$.
Here $B^+$ is $B$ with the product $a \circ b = \frac{1}{2}(ab + ba)$.

## Remarks

The theorem says that any Jordan algebra generated by two elements is special.
It does not extend to three generators: the free Jordan algebra on three generators is not special.
The core of the proof is that the free Jordan algebra on two generators is special.
As a consequence, any identity in two variables that holds in all special Jordan algebras holds in all Jordan algebras.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Jordan algebras were invented to capture the product $\frac{1}{2}(ab + ba)$ of matrices.
Not every Jordan algebra comes from matrices or another associative algebra in this way.
The theorem says that the trouble never shows up with only two elements: whatever two elements generate looks like symmetrized products in an associative algebra.

### L2 human

The theorem is a two-variable analogue of [[Artin's theorem|PBRCDXKQFR]] for alternative algebras.
It lets one prove identities in two variables for all Jordan algebras by checking them in associative algebras.
[[Macdonald's theorem|BWYX23HJMF]] extends this to identities in three variables that are of degree at most one in the third variable.
