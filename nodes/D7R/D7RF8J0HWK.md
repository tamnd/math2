## Summary

The Hadley cell is the overturning circulation of the tropical atmosphere.
Air rises near the equator, flows poleward aloft, sinks in the subtropics and returns towards the equator near the surface.
The Held–Hou model is the simplest theory of this cell, for an axisymmetric atmosphere in which the poleward-moving air conserves its angular momentum.
It predicts the latitude at which the cell ends, the subtropical jet at its edge and the easterly trade winds below.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The sun heats the tropics more than higher latitudes, so tropical air rises and spreads towards the poles at high altitude.
As this air moves poleward it comes closer to the Earth's axis.
Like a spinning skater pulling in her arms, it spins faster, so it turns into a strong west wind.
This cannot go on forever, so the air sinks again at about $30^\circ$ latitude, creating the dry subtropical deserts.
Near the ground the air flows back towards the equator and is turned into the trade winds.
The Held–Hou model makes this picture quantitative with a few simple balances.
