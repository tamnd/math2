## Summary

Lubrication theory studies viscous flows in thin layers, where one dimension of the flow region is much smaller than the others.
In such a layer the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] simplify: the pressure is constant across the layer and the velocity profile across it is parabolic.
The theory explains how oil films carry the load in bearings, and it also models thin films, coating flows and [[Hele-Shaw flow|CM1R6CSHAA]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a thin film of oil between a shaft and its bearing.
Because the film is so thin, the oil moves almost parallel to the surfaces, and viscosity dominates inertia.
The pressure then depends only on the position along the film, and it can become large enough to keep the two metal surfaces apart.
The whole flow is reduced to one equation for the pressure, the [[Reynolds equation|K2W9427PJ2]].
