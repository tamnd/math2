## Example

Let $G = S_3$ be the [[symmetric group on three letters|09H9Y0Q11W]].
We compose permutations from right to left, so in $\sigma\tau$ we apply $\tau$ first.
Let $N = A_3 = \{e, (1\,2\,3), (1\,3\,2)\}$ be the [[alternating group|0Z5905Q73D]].
Let $H = \langle (1\,2) \rangle = \{e, (1\,2)\}$, a [[subgroup|0X1JXT47GA]] of order $2$.
We show that the [[product|0PT52XQZD7]] $NH$ is all of $S_3$.

1. The subgroup $N$ has index $6 / 3 = 2$ in $S_3$, so $N$ is [[normal|0CB0DX5BVV]], because [[subgroups of index two are normal|0V547YQRMV]].
2. The orders $3$ and $2$ are coprime, so $N \cap H = \{e\}$ by [[Lagrange's theorem|3C53TD]].
3. The [[product formula|08PY4GNAPK]] gives $|NH| = 3 \cdot 2 / 1 = 6$.
4. Thus $NH$ is a subset of $S_3$ with $6$ elements, so $NH = S_3$.
5. A direct check agrees. The products $ne$ give the $3$ elements of $N$.
6. The products $n(1\,2)$ give $(1\,2)$, $(1\,2\,3)(1\,2) = (1\,3)$ and $(1\,3\,2)(1\,2) = (2\,3)$.

## Remarks

The product $NH$ is a subgroup, as the theorem that the [[product with a normal subgroup is a subgroup|0TXWV1JGG1]] predicts.
Also $HN = S_3$, so $NH = HN$, in agreement with the criterion for a product to be a subgroup.
Each element of $S_3$ is a product $nh$ with $n \in N$ and $h \in H$ in exactly one way, because $|N| \cdot |H| = |S_3|$.
Thus each permutation of three letters is an even permutation, or an even permutation followed by the swap $(1\,2)$.
The same argument gives $S_n = A_n \langle (1\,2) \rangle$ for each $n \ge 2$, because $|A_n| = n!/2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The group $S_3$ has $3$ rotations of a triangle and $3$ reflections.
Take the rotations, and take one reflection together with the identity.
Each symmetry of the triangle is a rotation, or a rotation done after the reflection.
Thus the product of the two subgroups is the whole group.

### L2 human

We count with the product formula instead of listing products.
The two subgroups have orders $3$ and $2$, and they share only $e$.
Thus they give $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ different products, and $S_3$ has exactly $6$ elements.

The typical use is to split a group into a normal subgroup and a second subgroup that meets it only in $e$.
This is the first step to see $S_3$ as a [[semidirect product|0H3WZXCZNF]] of $A_3$ and $\langle (1\,2) \rangle$.
A trap is to conclude that $S_3$ is the direct product of $A_3$ and $\langle (1\,2) \rangle$.
This is false, because $\langle (1\,2) \rangle$ is not normal in $S_3$, and $S_3$ is not abelian.
