## Summary

A Feistel cipher is a block cipher built from several rounds.
Each round mixes one half of the block into the other through a round function.
Each round is invertible whatever the round function is, so the designer may use any function, even a non-invertible one.
The Data Encryption Standard (DES) is a Feistel cipher with $16$ rounds.
The [[Luby–Rackoff theorem|698HZWHH6F]] shows that a few rounds with pseudorandom round functions already give a pseudorandom permutation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cut the block of bits into a left half and a right half.
In one round, the right half moves to the left, and the new right half is the old left half plus a scrambled copy of the old right half.
To undo the round, scramble the left half again and add it back: since adding twice cancels, the old halves come back.
The scrambling itself never needs to be undone, which is why it can be any function.
Repeating the round many times with different keys spreads every input bit over the whole block.
