## Summary

Continuum mechanics is the part of mechanics that models a material body as a continuous medium filling a region of space.
It ignores the atomic structure of matter.
Mass, velocity, stress and temperature become fields, that is, functions of position and time.
The theory combines kinematics, universal balance laws and constitutive equations, and it is the common base of [[solid mechanics|JRD6BDKT1X]] and [[fluid mechanics|H27M44BV4M]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A steel beam or a glass of water is made of atoms, but on everyday scales it behaves as if matter were spread out smoothly.
Continuum mechanics takes this smooth picture seriously and describes the body by fields such as its density at each point.
A bent ruler is a good picture: each point of the ruler moves to a new place, and the theory tracks this map from old positions to new positions.
From such maps it computes stretching, from forces it computes stress, and balance laws tie the two together.
