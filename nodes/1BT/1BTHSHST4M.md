## Summary

This topic is a part of [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].
It covers what makes a proof rigorous, how the standards changed, and how mathematicians come to accept a proof, including pictures, computer proofs and very long proofs.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Mathematical rigour|E1PE9R8DBB]]: what makes an argument rigorous, and for which reader.
2. [[Formal and informal proofs|8VZ77R4R8S]]: derivations in a formal system and the proofs of daily practice.
3. [[History of proof and rigour|20F7K9RVT6]]: from Greek deductive proof to the computer age.
4. [[Proof without words|V6ESFEFKJC]]: pictures that make an identity visible, and when a picture is a proof.
5. [[Computer-assisted proof|MC18C7BMFT]]: the four colour theorem, the Kepler conjecture and SAT solvers.
6. [[Long proofs and surveyability|RM0S65BHWH]]: proofs too long for one reader to check.
7. [[Acceptance of proofs|MJ3EHQJJHX]]: refereeing, consensus, errors and contested claims.
8. [[Many proofs of one theorem|KNQDDN6PT2]]: why mathematicians prove known theorems again.
9. [[The Jaffe-Quinn and Thurston debate|K3M3TEX7SJ]]: speculation, proof and credit, 1993 and 1994.
10. [[Proofs from the Book|A4RJYP79A7]]: Erdős's ideal of the perfect proof.

### Prerequisites

- [[Proof, theorems and mathematical method|N4P3E53HM0]]: what a proof is and what it is for.
- [[Basic proof methods|958EF46EYJ]]: direct proof, contradiction, contrapositive and induction.
