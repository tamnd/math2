## Statement

When many objects go into a few boxes, some box receives at least the average number of objects, rounded up.

Let $A$ and $B$ be finite sets, let $f : A \to B$ be a function, and let $n$ be a natural number.
Assume that $n \cdot |B| < |A|$.
Then there is an element $b \in B$ whose fiber $f^{-1}(b) = \{ a \in A : f(a) = b \}$ has more than $n$ elements.

Two other forms are standard, and the [[proof|V64DJESEA6]] covers all three.

- Ceiling form (equivalent): if $N \geq 0$ objects go into $k \geq 1$ boxes, then some box receives at least $\lceil N / k \rceil$ objects, where $\lceil x \rceil$ is the [[ceiling|0EA4XFB0EY]] of $x$, the least integer $\geq x$.
- Weighted form (stronger): let $q_1, \dots, q_k$ be positive integers.
  If $q_1 + q_2 + \cdots + q_k - k + 1$ objects go into boxes $1, \dots, k$, then for some $i$ the box $i$ receives at least $q_i$ objects.

The weighted form with $q_1 = \cdots = q_k = n + 1$ is the main form, and the main form with $n = 1$ is the [[pigeonhole principle|0HGVCWQGY3]].

## Remarks

### A first use: one color at a vertex

Color each of the $15$ edges of the complete graph $K_6$ red or blue, and look at one vertex $v$.
Five edges leave $v$, and they go into two boxes, red and blue.
The ceiling form gives a color that appears on at least
$$\left\lceil \tfrac{5}{2} \right\rceil = 3$$
of these edges.
Check with the main form: $n = 2$ and $|B| = 2$ give $2 \cdot 2 = 4 < 5$, so some color has more than $2$ edges.
This is the first step of the [[theorem on friends and strangers|0NYM0ZGAK9]]: three edges of one color at $v$ lead to a triangle of one color.

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The inequality $n \cdot |B| < |A|$ must be strict.
Take $A = B \times \{1, \dots, n\}$ and $f(b, i) = b$, so that $|A| = n \cdot |B|$, and every fiber has exactly $n$ elements.

In the ceiling form, $k \geq 1$ is needed for $N / k$ to make sense, and with no boxes no object can be placed at all.
In the weighted form, $q_1 + \cdots + q_k - k$ objects are not enough: put exactly $q_i - 1$ objects into box $i$ for each $i$.
In every form each object must go into a box, which means that $f$ is defined on all of $A$.

### What it gives you

The first use is a lower bound on the largest class of a partition.
Among $25$ people, at least $3$ share a birth month, because $2 \cdot 12 = 24 < 25$.
The [[Erdős-Szekeres theorem|0SNJM8HSD3]] follows from the main form, applied to the length of the longest increasing subsequence that starts at each term.
For real numbers there is an averaging form: some term of a finite list is at least the mean of the list.
The [[averaging argument|HXP9VMXV8S]] is built on that form.

### The converse

The converse is true: if every function $f : A \to B$ has a fiber with more than $n$ elements, then $n \cdot |B| < |A|$.
Indeed, if $|A| \le n \cdot |B|$, deal the elements of $A$ to the elements of $B$ in turn, like cards to players, and each element of $B$ receives at most $n$ of them.
So the bound $\lceil N / k \rceil$ is sharp: dealing $N$ objects to $k$ boxes in turn gives no box more than $\lceil N / k \rceil$ objects.

### Generalizations

The [[infinite pigeonhole principle|0FS3RHF6KE]] replaces "more than $n$" by "infinitely many".
For weights, if real numbers $x_1, \dots, x_k$ have sum $s$, then $x_i \geq s / k$ for some $i$, since otherwise the sum would be less than $s$.
[[Ramsey's theorem|00TQV3F5S4]] uses the weighted form with different thresholds $q_i$ at each step of its induction.

### Watch out

The most common slip is to round the wrong way.
With $97$ people and $12$ months, $97 / 12 \approx 8.08$, and the bound is $\lceil 97/12 \rceil = 9$, not $\lfloor 97/12 \rfloor = 8$.
A second slip is to state the bound as $N / k$ when $k$ does not divide $N$.
No month can hold $8.08$ people, so the statement must be about the integer $9$.

### Try it

How many cards must you draw from a standard deck of $52$ to be sure of $5$ cards of one suit?
The boxes are the $4$ suits, and you need $\lceil N/4 \rceil \geq 5$, so $N = 17$; with $16$ cards you can hold exactly $4$ of each suit.

Show that two of the $500$ students of a school share a birthday, with February 29 counted as a day.
There are $366$ boxes and $500 > 366$, so $\lceil 500/366 \rceil = 2$ students share a day.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If $12$ boxes hold at most $2$ objects each, they hold at most $24$ objects.
So if we put $25$ objects into $12$ boxes, some box must hold at least $3$ objects.
The rule is to divide the number of objects by the number of boxes and to round up.
For $25$ objects and $12$ boxes, $25 / 12$ is about $2.08$, and rounding up gives $3$.

### L2 human

What is the least number of people in a room that forces $9$ people with the same birth month?
The boxes are the $12$ months, and each person goes into the box of the month of birth.
We want the least $N$ with $\lceil N / 12 \rceil \geq 9$, which means
$$\frac{N}{12} > 8, \qquad N > 96.$$
So $N = 97$ is enough: $8 \cdot 12 = 96 < 97$, and the main form with $n = 8$ gives a month with at least $9$ people.
The number $96$ is not enough, because $8$ people born in each month make $96$ people with no month holding $9$.
So the answer is $97$, and the example shows the typical shape of an answer: one count that forces the property and one arrangement that shows the count cannot be lowered.

### L3 human

The main form says that a function between finite sets has a large fiber, and the proof is a sum over the fibers.
In Mathlib it is `Fintype.exists_lt_card_fiber_of_mul_lt_card`, and there are variants with sums of weights in place of numbers of elements.
The weighted form with different thresholds $q_i$ is the shape that appears in the inductive proof of [[Ramsey's theorem|00TQV3F5S4]].

The averaging version for real numbers is the discrete case of the first moment method: a random variable takes a value at least its mean with positive probability.
This view connects the principle with the [[averaging argument|HXP9VMXV8S]] and with the [[probabilistic method|3B55HNS754]].
The [[infinite pigeonhole principle|0FS3RHF6KE]] is the limit form, and it leads to infinite Ramsey theory.
