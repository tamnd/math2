## Example

On $V = \{0, 1, 2\}$ let $w(x, y) = \max(x, y) + 1 \bmod 3$.
Here is its table, with a row for each $x$ and a column for each $y$.

| $w$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $0$ |
| $1$ | $2$ | $2$ | $0$ |
| $2$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |

We define the shift, Post's negation and $\max$ from $w$.

1. The diagonal gives the shift $S x = x + 1 \bmod 3$.
   We read $w(0, 0) = 1$, $w(1, 1) = 2$ and $w(2, 2) = 0$.
2. Post's negation is $S^2$, so $\neg x = w(w(x, x), w(x, x))$.
   This gives $\neg 0 = 2$, $\neg 1 = 0$ and $\neg 2 = 1$.
3. Since $S^3$ is the identity, $\max(x, y) = S^2(w(x, y))$.
   Check at $(1, 0)$: $w(1, 0) = 2$, then $S(2) = 0$ and $S(0) = 1$, and $\max(1, 0) = 1$.
4. Check at $(2, 1)$: $w(2, 1) = 0$, then $S(0) = 1$ and $S(1) = 2$, and $\max(2, 1) = 2$.
5. The constant $2$ is $\max(x, Sx, S^2 x)$, a term in $w$ by steps 1 and 3.

So $w$ defines Post's $\neg$ and $\max$, and by [[Webb's theorem|EPSBDVPKJP]] every three-valued function is a term in $w$.

## Remarks

The table shows two features of $w$.
It is symmetric, because $w(x, y)$ depends only on $\max(x, y)$.
And the row and the column of $2$ are constant $0$, since $\max(2, y) = 2$ and $2 + 1 \equiv 0$.

The example is the case $m = 3$ of a general pattern.
With $m$ values, the diagonal is still the shift, and $\max$ is $S^{m-1} \circ w$.
For $m = 2$ the table becomes NOR: $w(0, 0) = 1$ and $w$ is $0$ elsewhere.

Once $\neg$ and $\max$ are terms, every other function follows by [[Post's construction|FR9JC8MNBJ]].
For example, Łukasiewicz negation $2 - x$ is a term in $w$, by substituting into the formula of [[the three-valued Post example|FV0ZA5CEH6]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The table shows Webb's function on three values.
Putting the same value in twice moves it one step around the circle $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 0$.
With this step and the function itself, we get Post's "or" and "not", and those two build everything.

### L2 human

First read the diagonal of the table to get the shift.
Then compose the shift with itself to undo the step up inside $w$.
Finally check the composite on a few pairs, as in steps 3 and 4.
This is the typical use of a small case: it checks the general proof by hand.
Watch out: $S^2 \circ w$ is $\max$ only because $S^3$ is the identity on three values.
With four values you need $S^3 \circ w$, since $S^2(w(1, 0)) = S^2(2) = 0$ there, not $1$.
