## Example

Let $M$ be a countable [[non-standard model|1QWN5CV4CG]] of $\mathsf{PA}$, and let $\mathcal{K}$ be the class of the cuts $I$ of $M$ with $2x \in I$ for every $x \in I$.
For $1 \le a \le b$ let $Y(a, b) = \max\set{c : a \cdot 2^c \le b}$, and let $Y(a, b) = 0$ otherwise.
We check that $Y$ is an [[indicator|JWTZ8NVVGK]] for $\mathcal{K}$.

1. $Y$ is defined by a formula of arithmetic, because $x \mapsto 2^x$ is definable in $\mathsf{PA}$. The maximum exists, because $a \cdot 2^c \le b$ gives $c < b$.
2. So $Y(a, b) \le b$. Raising $b$ or lowering $a$ keeps every $c$ with $a \cdot 2^c \le b$, so $Y$ is monotone.
3. Let $1 \le a \le b$ with $c = Y(a, b)$ non-standard. Let $I$ be the set of the $x$ with $x \le a \cdot 2^k$ for some standard $k$.
4. $I$ is closed downward and under doubling, because $x \le a \cdot 2^k$ gives $2x \le a \cdot 2^{k+1}$. It is closed under $x \mapsto x + 1$, because $x + 1 \le 2x$ for $x \ge 1$ and $1 \le a$.
5. $a \in I$ with $k = 0$. For a standard $k$ we have $k < c$, so $a \cdot 2^k < a \cdot 2^c \le b$. So $b \notin I$.
6. So $I \in \mathcal{K}$ and $a \in I < b$.
7. Conversely, let $I \in \mathcal{K}$ with $a \in I < b$, and $a \ge 1$. Then $a \cdot 2^k \in I$ for each standard $k$, by doubling $k$ times.
8. So $a \cdot 2^k < b$ for each standard $k$, and $Y(a, b) \ge k$ for each standard $k$. So $Y(a, b)$ is non-standard.

A numerical check of the formula: $Y(3, 100) = 5$, because $3 \cdot 2^5 = 96 \le 100 < 192 = 3 \cdot 2^6$.

## Remarks

The [[difference indicator|1T6K132VVA]] measures the distance from $a$ to $b$ by addition, and this one by doubling.
Faster operations give indicators for smaller classes of cuts, and an indicator for the models of $\mathsf{PA}$ needs a combinatorial principle.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Here two infinite numbers are separated when one can double $a$ any finite number of times and still stay below $b$.
The number of doublings that fit below $b$ is $Y(a, b)$.
It is infinite exactly when a cut closed under doubling lies between $a$ and $b$.

### L2 human

The cut in step 3 is the set of numbers below $a$ times some standard power of $2$.
The typical use of this example is to see how the closure condition on the cuts changes the indicator.
The trap: closure under doubling is much weaker than being a model of $\mathsf{PA}$, as the [[non-example|Q1WSA5TPC2]] shows.
