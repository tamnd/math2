## Statement

The laws of thermodynamics are the following four postulates about [[thermodynamic systems|GYNNJBMTCP]].

0. *Zeroth law.* If two systems are each in thermal [[equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]] with a third system, then they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
1. *First law.* For a closed system, the change of [[internal energy|F6JVQ12X43]] in a process is $\Delta U = Q + W$. Here $Q$ is the [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] absorbed and $W$ is the [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] done on the system. In particular, the internal energy of an isolated system is constant.
2. *Second law.* The [[entropy|SPY89W0RJ7]] of an isolated system never decreases. It stays constant exactly in a [[reversible process|F3DHZ45H6A]].
3. *Third law.* As the [[thermodynamic temperature|AJ8M9MGZGF]] tends to $0$, the entropy of a system in equilibrium tends to a constant value. This value does not depend on the other state variables, such as pressure.

## Remarks

The laws are not proved inside thermodynamics: they are postulates drawn from experience.
Statistical mechanics derives them, in a suitable sense, from the microscopic laws of motion.
The zeroth law makes thermal equilibrium an equivalence relation and so allows a [[temperature|KK46Q2YV1D]] to be defined.
The first law rules out a machine that produces work without using energy.
The second law rules out a cyclic engine whose only effect is to turn heat from one reservoir fully into work.
For example, it gives the Carnot bound $1 - T_C / T_H$ on the efficiency of an engine between reservoirs at $T_H > T_C$.
The third law implies that absolute zero cannot be reached in a finite number of steps.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The four laws are the ground rules for heat and energy.
The zeroth law says that temperature makes sense: things in balance with the same thermometer are in balance with each other.
The first law says that energy is never created or destroyed, only moved as heat or work.
The second law says that heat flows by itself only from hot to cold, and that disorder in an isolated system tends to grow.
The third law says that absolute zero is a limit that cannot be reached.

### L2 human

Each law introduces or constrains a state function.
The zeroth law gives empirical temperature, the first law gives the internal energy $U$, and the second law gives the entropy $S$ and the thermodynamic temperature $T$.
Together, the first and second laws give $dU = T \, dS - p \, dV$ for a simple fluid in reversible processes.
From this relation come the thermodynamic potentials, the Maxwell relations, and the limits on the efficiency of engines and refrigerators.
The third law fixes the additive constant in the entropy and governs behaviour near absolute zero, for example that heat capacities vanish there.
