## Summary

The cardinal points of a rotationally symmetric optical system are six points on its axis: two focal points, two principal points and two nodal points.
In the [[paraxial approximation|N111BHFQ2H]], they determine how the system forms images.
Any such system, however many lenses it has, then acts like a single thin lens, once distances are measured from its two principal planes.
They reduce the design and analysis of compound lenses to a few numbers.
See the [[definition|PGA5QGTCJ5]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A camera lens may contain ten pieces of glass, but for many purposes it behaves like one simple lens.
The cardinal points say where that simple lens should be imagined.
Parallel rays from far away meet at the rear focal point.
The principal planes are where the bending of the rays seems to happen.
A ray aimed at the front nodal point leaves as if from the rear nodal point, with its direction unchanged.
