## Summary

The Talbot effect is the self-imaging of a periodic object.
When a plane wave passes through a grating, copies of the grating pattern reappear at regular distances behind it.
In the [[Fresnel|M8669NXXJS]] approximation the copies occur at multiples of the Talbot length $z_T = 2 d^2 / \lambda$, where $d$ is the period and $\lambda$ the wavelength.
Halfway, the image is shifted by half a period, and at rational fractions of $z_T$ superpositions of shifted copies appear, forming the Talbot carpet.
The effect rests on a number-theoretic fact: the phases $e^{-i\pi n^2 \lambda z / d^2}$ of the diffraction orders all return to $1$ together.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light a fine comb of slits with a laser and look at the light at various distances behind it.
At a certain distance you see a perfect picture of the slits again, without any lens.
At half that distance you see the picture again, moved sideways by half a slit spacing.
In between, the light forms an intricate repeating pattern, like a carpet.
