## Summary

Viscosity is the resistance of a fluid to deformation at a given rate; it is a measure of internal friction.
In a simple shear flow, the dynamic viscosity $\mu$ is the ratio of the shear stress to the rate of shear.
It dissipates kinetic energy into heat and sets the scale at which a flow is smooth, through the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Honey is thick and water is thin: honey has a much larger viscosity.
Picture a deck of cards sliding over each other when you push the top card: viscosity is the friction between layers of fluid that slide past each other.
To slide a plate at constant speed over a thin film of fluid, you must keep pushing, and the force needed is proportional to the viscosity.
Water at room temperature has a viscosity of about $10^{-3}\ \mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$, air about $1.8 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$.
