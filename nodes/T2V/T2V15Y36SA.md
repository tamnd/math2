## Example

*Chua's circuit* consists of two capacitors $C_1, C_2$, an inductor $L$, a linear resistor $R$ and a nonlinear resistor, the Chua diode.
The current of the Chua diode is a piecewise linear function of its voltage.
In dimensionless form its state $(x, y, z)$ satisfies the system of [[ordinary differential equations|0NT64JV96A]]
$$x' = \alpha \big(y - x - f(x)\big), \qquad y' = x - y + z, \qquad z' = -\beta y.$$
Here $x, y$ are scaled capacitor voltages, $z$ is the scaled inductor current, and $\alpha, \beta > 0$.
The characteristic of the diode is
$$f(x) = m_1 x + \tfrac{1}{2}(m_0 - m_1)\big(\abs{x + 1} - \abs{x - 1}\big),$$
with slope $m_0$ for $\abs{x} < 1$ and slope $m_1$ for $\abs{x} > 1$.
Take the parameters $\alpha \approx 15.6$, $\beta = 28$, $m_0 = -8/7$ and $m_1 = -5/7$.
Then numerical solutions settle on a [[strange attractor|9WKCZGG906]], the double scroll, made of two sheets wound around two unstable equilibria.
The system is autonomous, nonlinear and three-dimensional.
Three is the smallest dimension in which an autonomous system of differential equations can be chaotic.

## Remarks

In each of the three regions $x < -1$, $\abs{x} \le 1$, $x > 1$ the system is affine, so all its nonlinearity comes from the two corners of $f$.
This makes the circuit easy to build from standard parts and easy to analyse piece by piece.
The equilibria are the origin and two symmetric points in the outer regions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Chua's circuit is a small electronic circuit, built from a handful of ordinary parts, that behaves chaotically.
Its voltages swing around one centre for a while, then jump unpredictably to circle another centre, and never repeat.
The same equations can be solved on a computer, and the pictures agree with what an oscilloscope shows on the real circuit.
