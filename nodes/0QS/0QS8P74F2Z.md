## Definition

A semisimple endomorphism is one for which every invariant subspace has an invariant complement.
Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and let $T \colon V \to V$ be an [[endomorphism|0J7H0YBFDJ]].
We call $T$ *semisimple* when for each $T$-[[invariant subspace|0ACX5QT8TE]] $W$ of $V$ there is a $T$-invariant subspace $W'$ of $V$ with
$$V = W \oplus W',$$
a [[direct sum|0XYYT5KNTX]] of subspaces.

## Notation

In Mathlib, `Module.End.IsSemisimple T` states that $V$ is a semisimple module over $F[t]$, with $t$ acting as $T$.
The $F[t]$-submodules of $V$ are exactly the $T$-invariant subspaces, so this is the same condition.

## Remarks

The definition asks for an invariant complement, and both words matter.
Every subspace has some complement, so the condition says nothing until we ask that the complement be invariant too.
And the condition is asked for every invariant $W$, including $W = 0$ and $W = V$, which are always fine because each is a complement of the other.

A small example: the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1, 2)$ on $F^2$ has the invariant subspaces $0$, $F e_1$, $F e_2$ and $F^2$, and the two lines are complements of each other.
A case that fails: the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ keeps the line $F e_1$, and no invariant line complements it, as the explanation below shows.

The [[semisimple endomorphism|08VRX3Q4PB]] page gives the equivalent test by the minimal polynomial, which has no repeated irreducible factor exactly when $T$ is semisimple.
When the characteristic polynomial splits, semisimple means [[diagonalizable|07SQCNV233]].
The rotation of $\R^2$ by a right angle is semisimple but not diagonalizable over $\R$, and over the imperfect field $\mathbb{F}_2(s)$ the [[companion matrix of x² + s|C720K6S84D]] is semisimple but not diagonalizable over an algebraic closure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A map is semisimple when every piece of the space that it keeps in place has a partner piece that it also keeps in place, and the two pieces together fill the space.
For a stretch by $1$ along one axis and by $2$ along the other, each axis is the partner of the other.

### L2 human

We show that $N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is not semisimple.

1. Since $N e_1 = 0$, the line $W = F e_1$ is invariant.
2. A complement of $W$ in $F^2$ is a line $F v$ with $v \notin F e_1$, and it is invariant only when $N v = \mu v$ for some $\mu \in F$.
3. Then $0 = N^2 v = \mu^2 v$, so $\mu = 0$ and $v \in \ker N = F e_1$, which contradicts step 2.
4. So $W$ has no invariant complement, and $N$ is not semisimple.

Watch out: the line $F e_2$ is a complement of $W$, but $N e_2 = e_1$ does not lie on it, so it is not invariant.
