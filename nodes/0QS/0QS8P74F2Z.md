## Definition

Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and let $T \colon V \to V$ be an [[endomorphism|0J7H0YBFDJ]].
The map $T$ is *semisimple* when, for each $T$-[[invariant subspace|0ACX5QT8TE]] $W$ of $V$, there is a $T$-invariant subspace $W'$ with $V = W \oplus W'$, a [[direct sum|0XYYT5KNTX]].

## Notation

In Mathlib, `Module.End.IsSemisimple T` states that $V$ is a semisimple module over $F[t]$, with $t$ acting as $T$.
The $F[t]$-submodules are exactly the $T$-invariant subspaces, so the two definitions agree.

## Remarks

The map $T$ is semisimple exactly when its minimal polynomial has no repeated irreducible factor.
If the characteristic polynomial of $T$ splits, $T$ is semisimple exactly when it is [[diagonalizable|07SQCNV233]].
Over a perfect field, $T$ is semisimple exactly when it is diagonalizable over the algebraic closure.
Over an imperfect field this fails, as the [[companion matrix of x² + s over F₂(s)|C720K6S84D]] shows.
The rotation of $\R^2$ by $90$ degrees is semisimple, but it is not diagonalizable over $\R$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Semisimple maps generalize diagonalizable maps to fields where eigenvalues can be missing.
They have no hidden nilpotent part.
Over $\C$, semisimple simply means diagonalizable.

### L2 human

Show that $N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is not semisimple.

1. $N e_1 = 0$, so the line $W = F e_1$ is invariant.
2. An invariant complement would be a line $F v$ with $v \notin F e_1$ and $N v \in F v$.
3. $N v = \mu v$ forces $\mu = 0$, since $N^2 = 0$. So $v \in \ker N = F e_1$, which is false.
4. So $W$ has no invariant complement.

The typical use is to find an invariant subspace without an invariant complement, which proves that $T$ is not semisimple.
The trap is to look for any complement: $F e_2$ is a complement of $W$, but it is not invariant.

### L3 human

The definition is the special case of a semisimple module, for the ring $F[t]$ acting through $T$.
For several commuting maps, one uses the ring generated by them, and the same definition applies.
