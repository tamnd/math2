## Summary

Entropy is a [[state function|T8J5XRYBZZ]] of a thermodynamic system, introduced by Clausius.
Its change in a reversible process is the heat absorbed divided by the temperature.
The second law of thermodynamics says that the entropy of an isolated system never decreases.
In statistical mechanics, entropy measures the number of microscopic states compatible with the macroscopic state.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Entropy tells which changes can happen by themselves and which cannot.
Ice melting in a warm room, perfume spreading in the air, and a hot cup cooling down all raise the total entropy.
The reverse changes would lower it, and they never happen on their own.
Loosely, entropy counts how many ways the molecules can be arranged without changing what we see.
Spread-out perfume can be arranged in far more ways than perfume in a bottle.
