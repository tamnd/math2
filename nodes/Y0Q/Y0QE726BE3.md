## Summary

A Gaussian beam is a light beam whose transverse intensity profile is a Gaussian function at every point along its axis.
It is the simplest solution of the [[paraxial wave equation|53DCTAEM5M]] of finite power, and it models the output of most lasers.
The beam has a narrowest place, the waist, and widens on both sides of it, slowly at first and then linearly with distance.
Its width, wavefront curvature and phase are given by simple closed formulas in terms of the waist radius and the wavelength.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A laser pointer makes a spot that is brightest in the middle and fades smoothly towards the edge, like a bell curve.
That is a Gaussian beam.
Near its narrowest point, the waist, the beam stays almost the same width for a while.
Far away it spreads out like a cone, with an angle about the wavelength divided by the waist size.
So a wider waist gives a beam that stays narrow over a longer distance.
