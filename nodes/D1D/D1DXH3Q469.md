## Example

Work in classical [[propositional logic|09K9MWD2QQ]] with formulas that may contain $\neg$ above compound formulas.
The switch rule of [[deep inference|4ZW0SHW2RG]] rewrites $A \wedge (B \vee C)$ into $(A \wedge B) \vee C$.
Apply it inside the context $S\{\ \} = \neg\{\ \}$:
$$\neg\bigl(A \wedge (B \vee C)\bigr) \quad \text{to} \quad \neg\bigl((A \wedge B) \vee C\bigr) .$$
We show that this step is not sound.

1. Take $A$ false, $B$ false and $C$ true. Any formulas with these values will do, for instance the atoms $a$, $b$, $c$.
2. $B \vee C$ is true, and $A \wedge (B \vee C)$ is false, because $A$ is false.
3. So the premise $\neg(A \wedge (B \vee C))$ is true.
4. $A \wedge B$ is false, and $C$ is true, so $(A \wedge B) \vee C$ is true.
5. So the conclusion $\neg((A \wedge B) \vee C)$ is false.
6. The step goes from a true formula to a false one, so it is not sound.

We also check that switch itself is sound, so the fault lies in the context.

7. If $A \wedge (B \vee C)$ is true, then $A$ is true and $B \vee C$ is true.
8. If $B$ is true, then $A \wedge B$ is true. If $C$ is true, then $C$ is true. In both cases $(A \wedge B) \vee C$ is true.
9. So $A \wedge (B \vee C) \to (A \wedge B) \vee C$ is valid, but $\neg$ reverses the direction of the implication.

## Remarks

The condition that fails is the positivity of the context.
In a positive context, built from $\wedge$ and $\vee$ only, the hole is monotone: if $A \to B$ is valid, then $S\{A\} \to S\{B\}$ is valid.
The context $\neg\{\ \}$ is antitone: a valid $A \to B$ gives a valid $\neg B \to \neg A$, the reverse direction.

SKS avoids the problem by its formula language: negation occurs only on atoms, and $\neg$ of a compound formula is defined by the De Morgan laws.
Then $\neg(A \wedge (B \vee C))$ is the formula $\bar{A} \vee (\bar{B} \wedge \bar{C})$, and switch does not apply to it in the way above.
In the context $\neg\{\ \}$, the sound step goes the other way: from $\neg((A \wedge B) \vee C)$ to $\neg(A \wedge (B \vee C))$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The switch rule turns a stronger statement into a weaker one.
Putting "not" in front of both statements reverses this: the negation of the weaker one is the stronger one.
So inside a "not", the rule would turn a weaker statement into a stronger one, and that can turn a truth into a falsehood.
With $A$ false, $B$ false and $C$ true, the step goes from a true statement to a false one.

### L2 human

To refute a rule inside a negation, look for values that make the inner premise false and the inner conclusion true.
Here $A$ false and $C$ true do it, whatever $B$ is.
Then the negated premise is true and the negated conclusion is false, so the step is unsound, even though switch itself is valid.
This is why deep inference systems work in negation normal form, where every context is positive.
Watch out: a valid rule may be applied inside a context only when the hole is in a positive position.
In $\neg a \vee \{\ \}$ the hole is positive and switch is sound there, while in $\neg(a \vee \{\ \})$ it is negative.
