## Summary

Semenov's theory is the simplest model of a thermal explosion.
It treats a reacting mixture in a vessel as well stirred, so that the mixture has one temperature.
It compares the heat released by the reaction with the heat lost through the walls.
The heat release grows exponentially with temperature, while the loss grows linearly.
An explosion, that is [[thermal runaway|1BSB9NDCWF]], occurs when the two curves no longer meet, and the critical condition is that they touch.
In dimensionless form this gives the critical Semenov number $1/e$; the transition is a [[saddle-node bifurcation|MAP5J6HK0T]].
See the [[definition|T7WBVDBHKE]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw two curves against temperature: the heat made by the reaction, which bends upward like an exponential, and the heat lost to the cold walls, which is a straight line.
Where they cross, gain and loss balance and the temperature can stay put.
If the vessel is small or the walls are cold, the line is steep and crosses the curve, so the mixture warms slightly and then settles.
If the line is too shallow, it misses the curve entirely, gain always beats loss, and the mixture explodes.
The border case, where the line just touches the curve, is the critical condition for ignition.
