## Summary

The quantum Hall effect is the quantisation of the Hall conductance of a two-dimensional electron system in a strong perpendicular magnetic field at low temperature.
On plateaus of the field or density, the Hall conductance equals $\nu e^2/h$ and the longitudinal resistance vanishes.
Here $\nu$ is an integer, or in the fractional effect a simple fraction.
Klaus von Klitzing discovered the integer effect in 1980; Daniel Tsui, Horst Störmer and Arthur Gossard found the fractional effect in 1982.
The integer effect has a topological explanation: the conductance is a Chern number, the [[TKNN invariant|CX8GDHENTT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Pass a current through a thin sheet of metal in a magnetic field, and a voltage appears across the sheet: this is the Hall effect.
Classically the ratio of this voltage to the current grows steadily with the field.
In very clean two-dimensional samples at very low temperature it instead climbs in steps, with flat plateaus.
On each plateau the resistance equals $h/(\nu e^2)$ to better than one part in a billion, independent of the sample's shape or impurities.
This precision is so reliable that it is used to realise the standard unit of electrical resistance.
