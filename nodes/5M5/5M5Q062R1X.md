## Example

Statement: if $m$ and $n$ are even [[integers|0C4JS9RF5M]], then $m + n$ is even.
Definition: an integer $x$ is even when $x = 2k$ for some integer $k$.
We give the [[proof|0B94TKFQJX]], with the reason for each step.

1. Let $m$ and $n$ be even integers.
   This is the hypothesis.
2. Since $m$ is even, there is an integer $a$ with $m = 2a$.
   The reason is the definition of an even integer.
3. Since $n$ is even, there is an integer $b$ with $n = 2b$.
   The reason is the same definition, and we use a new letter because $n$ need not equal $m$.
4. Substitute steps 2 and 3, and use the distributive law:
   $$m + n = 2a + 2b = 2(a + b).$$
5. The number $c = a + b$ is an integer, because a sum of two integers is an integer.
6. So $m + n = 2c$ with $c$ an integer, and $m + n$ is even by the definition.
   This is the conclusion.

Check: for $m = 6$ and $n = 10$ we have $a = 3$, $b = 5$, $c = 8$, and $2c = 16 = 6 + 10$.

## Remarks

The proof is a [[direct proof|0NTF6DMVAP]]: it starts from the hypothesis and reaches the conclusion through a definition and some algebra.
It shows the three parts of every proof: the starting points (the hypothesis and the definition), the steps with their reasons, and the conclusion.
It covers every pair of even integers at once, because steps 2 to 6 use only the forms $2a$ and $2b$, never particular values.

Small changes give related results with the same proof.
With $m = 2a + 1$ and $n = 2b + 1$ the sum is
$$(2a + 1) + (2b + 1) = 2(a + b + 1),$$
so the sum of two odd integers is even.
With $m = 2a$ and $n = 2b + 1$ the sum is $2(a + b) + 1$, so the sum of an even and an odd integer is odd.

Watch out.
If step 3 reused the letter, as in "$n = 2a$", the argument would prove only the case $n = m$.
It would say nothing about the pair $2$ and $4$, so it would not prove the statement.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An even number is two times a whole number, such as $8 = 2 \times 4$.
Take two even numbers, two times $a$ and two times $b$.
Their sum is two times $a$ plus two times $b$, which is two times the number $a + b$.
So the sum is again two times a whole number, and it is even.
The argument does not depend on the values of $a$ and $b$, so it works for all even numbers.

### L2 human

The same method proves that the sum of three even integers is even.
Write them as $m = 2a$, $n = 2b$ and $p = 2c$ with integers $a$, $b$ and $c$.
Then
$$m + n + p = 2a + 2b + 2c = 2(a + b + c),$$
and $a + b + c$ is an integer, so the sum is even.
The method is always the same: unfold the definition in the hypothesis, compute, and fold the definition back in the conclusion.
Most first proofs about divisibility, such as "the sum of two multiples of $3$ is a multiple of $3$", follow this pattern word for word.
