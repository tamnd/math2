## Summary

A material is transversely isotropic when its properties are the same in every direction within a plane.
Along the axis normal to that plane they may differ.
In [[anisotropic elasticity|NMDDYDXT0V]] this means the [[elasticity tensor|SX32X1D537]] is unchanged by every rotation about one axis.
Such a material has $5$ independent elastic constants.
Unidirectional fibre composites, many layered rocks and hexagonal crystals are modelled this way.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a bundle of parallel straws glued together.
Seen from the end, all sideways directions look alike, so the bundle behaves the same whichever way you push it sideways.
Along the straws it is much stiffer.
This symmetry, round about one axis but different along it, is transverse isotropy.
