## Summary

The horizontal coordinate system is the [[celestial coordinate system|YT0G7RE2GM]] of a local observer, built on the horizon.
Its coordinates are the altitude, the angle above the horizon, and the azimuth, the compass direction along the horizon.
It describes what an observer actually sees, and it changes with the place and the time.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To tell a friend where to look, say how high above the horizon and in which compass direction.
These two numbers are the altitude and the azimuth.
A star rising due east has altitude $0^\circ$ and azimuth $90^\circ$.
The point straight overhead, the zenith, has altitude $90^\circ$.
Because the Earth turns, the altitude and azimuth of a star change all night.
