## Summary

The number of degrees of freedom of a mechanical system is the number of independent parameters needed to fix its position.
For a system with holonomic constraints it is the number of [[generalized coordinates|BCF1EX9W2M]], that is, the dimension of the [[configuration space|MGYKGP7PW3]].
Each independent holonomic constraint lowers the count by one.
The number of degrees of freedom is the number of second-order equations of motion that the system needs.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A free particle in space has $3$ degrees of freedom: left-right, front-back, up-down.
Two free particles have $6$; joining them by a rigid rod removes one, leaving $5$.
A rigid body moving freely in space has $6$: three for the position of its centre and three for its orientation.
A pendulum swinging in a plane has only $1$, its angle.
