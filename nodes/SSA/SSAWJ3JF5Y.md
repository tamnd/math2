## Summary

The magnetic dipole moment of a current distribution is the vector that measures its strength and orientation as a small magnet.
For a flat current loop it is the current times the area, along the normal to the loop.
Far from the currents, the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] is to leading order that of a magnetic dipole, decaying like the inverse cube of the distance.
It also gives the torque and the energy of the distribution in a uniform external field.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A small loop of wire carrying a current behaves like a tiny bar magnet.
Its magnetic moment is an arrow perpendicular to the loop, with length equal to the current times the area enclosed.
The direction is given by the right-hand rule: curl the fingers along the current, and the thumb points along the moment.
In a magnetic field the arrow turns to line up with the field, as a compass needle does.
Electrons and many atoms carry magnetic moments too.
