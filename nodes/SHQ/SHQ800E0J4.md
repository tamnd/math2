## Summary

Combinatorial game theory studies two-player games of perfect information without chance, such as Nim, Go endgames, Hackenbush and Domineering.
The players move alternately, and under the normal play convention the player who cannot move loses.
John Conway, Elwyn Berlekamp and Richard Guy built the modern theory, in which games are added together and compared like numbers.
A classical result of the subject, older than the general theory, is the Sprague–Grundy theorem for impartial games.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Many board games split into several separate parts, and on each turn a player moves in just one part.
Combinatorial game theory gives each part a value, and the value of the whole game is the sum of the values of its parts.
Some values are ordinary numbers that count spare moves for one player.
Others are new objects, such as the value $*$ of a single move that either player can take.
For example, a game in which Left has two free moves and Right has none has value $2$, and Left wins whoever starts.
