## Proof

The proof uses one theorem proved elsewhere and one cited lemma.

1. Lemma 1, the [[Jankov-de Jongh theorem|D25R70HQ7C]]: for each finite rooted frame $F$ there is a formula $\chi(F)$ such that, for every finite frame $G$, $G \nvDash \chi(F)$ iff $F \leq G$.
2. Lemma 2, cited (Jankov): there is an infinite sequence $(F_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ of finite rooted frames that is an [[antichain|03HF498P1J]] for $\leq$. That is, $F_m \nleq F_n$ for $m \neq n$.
3. For nonempty $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, let $L_S = \mathrm{Log}\set{F_n : n \in S}$.
4. Claim: $L_S$ is an [[intermediate logic|H5NSKEP3P2]]. It contains $\mathsf{IPC}$ by soundness of [[Kripke semantics|5GTQ69W47T]].
5. Validity on a frame is preserved by modus ponens. For a substitution $\sigma$ and a valuation $V$, let $V'(p)$ be the up-set of worlds that force $\sigma p$ under $V$.
6. By induction on $\varphi$, a world forces $\varphi$ under $V'$ iff it forces $\sigma \varphi$ under $V$. So validity is preserved by substitution, and $L_S$ is closed under both rules.
7. For $L_S \subseteq \mathsf{CPC}$, let $v$ be a classical valuation and $n \in S$. On $F_n$, make each $p$ true at all worlds if $v(p) = 1$, and at no world otherwise.
8. By induction on formulas, every world of $F_n$ then forces $\varphi$ iff $v(\varphi) = 1$. So every formula of $L_S$ is a classical tautology.
9. Claim: $S \neq T$ implies $L_S \neq L_T$. By symmetry, let $n \in S \setminus T$.
10. The identity map shows $F_n \leq F_n$. By Lemma 1, $F_n \nvDash \chi(F_n)$, so $\chi(F_n) \notin L_S$.
11. For $m \in T$, we have $m \neq n$, so $F_n \nleq F_m$ by Lemma 2. By Lemma 1, $F_m \vDash \chi(F_n)$.
12. So $\chi(F_n) \in L_T$, and $L_S \neq L_T$.
13. There are $2^{\aleph_0}$ nonempty subsets of $\mathbb{N}$. By step 12, there are at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ intermediate logics.
14. There are countably many formulas, so at most $2^{\aleph_0}$ sets of formulas. By the Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem, there are exactly $2^{\aleph_0}$ intermediate logics.

## Idea

The Jankov formula of $F$ is the weakest condition that excludes $F$ and everything above it in the order $\leq$.
On an antichain, these conditions are independent: excluding $F_n$ does not exclude any other $F_m$.
So every subset of the antichain gives its own logic.

## Variants

2. One can use the logics $\mathsf{IPC} + \set{\chi(F_n) : n \in S}$ instead. Then $F_m$, for $m \notin S$, validates the logic and refutes $\chi(F_m)$.
3. In algebraic terms, the proof shows that the variety of Heyting algebras has $2^{\aleph_0}$ subvarieties, generated by sets of finite subdirectly irreducible algebras.
