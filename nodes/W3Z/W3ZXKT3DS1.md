## Summary

Mathematical optimization is the part of mathematics that studies how to choose a best element from a set of allowed elements.
Its basic object is the [[optimization problem|NS0Q42Y3FX]]: minimize or maximize an objective function over a [[feasible region|1TTKMRW8PD]].
It studies when optimal solutions exist, how to recognize them through optimality conditions such as [[Lagrange multipliers|0VKKP8X6Q6]], and how to compute them with algorithms.
Its main branches include linear, nonlinear, convex, integer and combinatorial optimization, and it is the mathematical core of operations research.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Optimization is the mathematics of making the best choice under rules.
Examples are the cheapest diet that meets nutritional needs, the shortest route through a city, or the strongest bridge for a given amount of steel.
One describes the choices with variables, writes the rules as equations and inequalities, and writes the goal as a function to be made as small or as large as possible.
For instance, among rectangles with perimeter $4$, the one with the largest area is the square of side $1$.
