## Statement

There is no finite set $F$ of sentences of the [[language of arithmetic|D8SD54M5PN]] $L_A$ such that $F$ and [[Peano arithmetic|HACXBCM7SS]] $\mathsf{PA}$ have the same theorems.
Equivalently, no single sentence $\sigma$ of $L_A$ satisfies $\sigma \vdash \varphi$ for every axiom $\varphi$ of $\mathsf{PA}$ and $\mathsf{PA} \vdash \sigma$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- The language. The second-order [[Peano axioms|0QFT7Z76N5]] are finitely many axioms, with one induction axiom that quantifies over all sets. The theorem concerns first-order axioms in $L_A$.
- The language, again. $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ is finitely axiomatizable in the two-sorted language of second-order arithmetic, and its first-order theorems are exactly those of $\mathsf{PA}$, by [[this example|HPA6HX3114]]. So a finite axiomatization exists once set variables are allowed.
- Full induction. The [[fragments|T70PYM1F36]] $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n$ for $n \ge 1$ are finitely axiomatizable, and so is [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]] $\mathsf{Q}$. The theorem uses that $\mathsf{PA}$ has induction for formulas of every complexity.
- The same theorems. $\mathsf{PA}$ is axiomatized by a recursive infinite schema, so the content of the theorem is that no finite set of sentences, not necessarily axioms of $\mathsf{PA}$, generates the same theory.

### Consequences

- No consistent extension of $\mathsf{PA}$ in $L_A$ is finitely axiomatizable. This uses that $\mathsf{PA}$ is essentially reflexive: every extension $T$ of $\mathsf{PA}$ in $L_A$ proves the consistency of each finite subset of $T$.
- The fragments $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n$ do not collapse: for each $n$ there is $m > n$ with $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n \subsetneq \mathsf{I}\Sigma_m$.
- Every finite subset of $\mathsf{PA}$ has a model that is not a model of $\mathsf{PA}$.

### Converse

The converse fails: a theory that is not finitely axiomatizable need not contain $\mathsf{PA}$.
The [[theory of infinite sets|KFWXHKBHYT]] in the empty language is not finitely axiomatizable, by compactness, and it has no arithmetic at all.
The theory $\mathsf{R}$ of Tarski, Mostowski and Robinson, one of the [[weak theories of arithmetic|Q5MT8WY7JM]], is another example inside arithmetic.

### Generalizations

- [[Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory|0HE5WDQ9FJ]] is not finitely axiomatizable, if it is consistent, by the same reflection idea: $\mathsf{ZF}$ proves the consistency of each of its finite subsets.
- Any consistent theory $T$ in $L_A$ that extends $\mathsf{PA}$ is not finitely axiomatizable, as listed under consequences.

## History

Czesław Ryll-Nardzewski proved the theorem in 1952.
The proof given in the [[proof node|BBX212YCR4]] uses the fragments $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_n$, partial truth predicates and the [[second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]].
Feferman later pointed out that consistency statements depend on how the axioms of a theory are presented, which matters for statements such as "$T \vdash \mathrm{Con}(T)$".

## Explanations

### L1 human

Peano arithmetic has infinitely many axioms, one induction axiom for each property of numbers.
The theorem says that this cannot be avoided.
No finite list of axioms, written in the same language, proves exactly the same statements.
The reason is that any finite part of Peano arithmetic is weak enough that the full theory can prove the part is consistent.

### L2 human

We show how a finite subset of $\mathsf{PA}$ lies in a fragment.

1. Take the finite set $\set{\mathsf{Q}1, \ldots, \mathsf{Q}8, \iota_1, \iota_2}$, where $\iota_1$ is induction for $\exists y \, (y \cdot y = x)$ and $\iota_2$ is induction for $\forall y \, \exists z \, (x + y = z)$.
2. $\iota_1$ is an induction axiom for a $\Sigma_1$ formula, so it is an axiom of $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$.
3. $\iota_2$ is an induction axiom for a $\Pi_2$ formula, which is also $\Sigma_3$ after a vacuous $\exists$ in front, so it is an axiom of $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_3$, up to equivalence.
4. So the whole finite set is provable in $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_3$, and $\mathsf{PA}$ proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{I}\Sigma_3)$.

The typical use is the step "finitely many axioms of $\mathsf{PA}$ are proved in one fragment".
A trap is to think that the theorem says $\mathsf{PA}$ is undecidable or incomplete; those are different properties, and finite theories such as $\mathsf{Q}$ have them too.
