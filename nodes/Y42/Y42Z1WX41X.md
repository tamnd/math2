## Summary

The Weissenberg number $\mathrm{Wi}$ is a [[dimensionless number|T3R5FWV91P]].
It compares the relaxation time of a viscoelastic fluid with the time scale of the deformation in a flow.
It measures how strong elastic effects are compared with viscous effects.
When $\mathrm{Wi}$ is small the fluid behaves almost like a [[Newtonian fluid|T4ZV12BVHN]].
When it is large, elastic effects such as normal stresses and elastic instabilities dominate.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A viscoelastic fluid needs some time, its relaxation time, to forget that it has been stretched.
If the flow deforms it slowly compared with this time, the fluid relaxes fully and acts like an ordinary liquid.
If the flow deforms it fast, the fluid cannot relax and behaves partly like rubber.
The Weissenberg number is the product of the relaxation time and the rate of deformation.
For example, a fluid with relaxation time $0.1$ seconds sheared at $50$ per second has $\mathrm{Wi} = 5$, so elastic effects are strong.
