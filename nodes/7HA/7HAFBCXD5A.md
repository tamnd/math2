## Summary

The Rayleigh–Plesset equation is an ordinary differential equation for the radius of a spherical bubble in a large body of liquid.
It describes how the bubble grows, shrinks and oscillates when the pressure in the liquid changes.
It includes the inertia of the liquid, its viscosity and surface tension.
It is the basic model of bubble dynamics in [[cavitation|K544PYSW2A]] and in medical ultrasound.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When the pressure around a gas or vapour bubble drops, the bubble swells; when it rises, the bubble is crushed.
The liquid around the bubble has to move out of the way or rush in, and its inertia makes the collapse violent.
The Rayleigh–Plesset equation tracks the radius of the bubble over time.
It shows that an empty bubble collapsing under a steady pressure reaches zero radius in a finite time, with a wall speed that grows without bound.
