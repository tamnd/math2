## Summary

In general relativity, a geodesic is a [[geodesic|NSYMQMHPDN]] of the Lorentzian metric of [[spacetime|63ZKC7AFX2]], a curve whose tangent vector is parallel along itself.
Timelike geodesics are the world lines of freely falling test particles, and null geodesics are the paths of light rays.
In this way gravity is not a force in the theory.
Bodies on which no other force acts simply follow the geodesics of the curved spacetime.
Among nearby timelike curves between two events, a timelike geodesic locally has the greatest proper time.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a curved surface, the straightest possible paths are called geodesics, like great circles on a globe.
In general relativity spacetime is curved, and its straightest paths are the motions of free fall.
A planet orbiting the Sun, a falling apple and a ray of starlight passing the Sun all follow geodesics.
In flat spacetime without gravity, the geodesics are the straight lines of uniform motion.
