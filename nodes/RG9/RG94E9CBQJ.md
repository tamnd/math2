## Summary

A perfect Bayesian equilibrium is a solution concept for dynamic games with incomplete information.
It consists of strategies and beliefs.
Each player acts optimally at every information set given her beliefs, and beliefs follow Bayes' rule whenever possible.
It is the standard tool for analysing [[signaling games|DNVNS5HNYR]] such as job-market signalling.
Several versions exist in the literature, differing in what they require of beliefs after unexpected moves.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When you act after someone else, you often have to guess what they know from what they did.
In a perfect Bayesian equilibrium, every player makes such guesses in a consistent way, using the planned strategies and the rules of probability.
Then each player picks the best move given her guess, at every point of the game.
For example, an employer who sees a job applicant with a degree updates her belief about the applicant's ability, and offers a wage that is best given that belief.
