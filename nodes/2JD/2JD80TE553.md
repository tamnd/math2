## Summary

Elementary function arithmetic $\mathsf{EFA}$ is the first-order theory $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \mathsf{exp}$.
Here $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ is [[Robinson arithmetic|3K1AKGXF8A]] $\mathsf{Q}$ together with the [[induction schema|1QD0KDDGYJ]] for all [[$\Delta_0$ formulas|GE43CSZTS9]], and $\mathsf{exp}$ is the axiom $\forall x \, \exists y \, \mathrm{Exp}(x, y)$, where $\mathrm{Exp}(x, y)$ is a fixed $\Delta_0$ formula that defines the relation $y = 2^x$ in $\mathfrak{N}$.
An equivalent formulation adds a function symbol $x^y$ with the axioms $x^0 = S0$ and $x^{Sy} = x^y \cdot x$, and allows the new symbol in the $\Delta_0$ formulas of the induction schema; the [[definition node|SXYZP1DNJJ]] gives both.
The functions that $\mathsf{EFA}$ proves total are exactly the [[elementary recursive functions|RJ3MCAAZSV]].
$\mathsf{EFA}$ is a weak theory that seems to prove most theorems of ordinary number theory, and Friedman's grand conjecture, which is open, states this belief in a sharp form.

## Motivation

[[Peano arithmetic|HACXBCM7SS]] has far more induction than ordinary number theory uses.
A proof about primes, divisibility or polynomials usually needs induction only on bounded statements, such as "every $d \le n$ with $d \mid n$ has a property".
The theory $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ gives exactly this, but it cannot prove that $2^x$ exists for every $x$, so it cannot code a sequence of length $x$ whose entries are about as large as $x$.
Adding the single axiom that exponentiation is total repairs this: finite sequences, sets of numbers below $x$ and proofs of length $x$ can then be coded.
The result is a theory strong enough to carry out syntax and finite combinatorics, and weak enough to have a small class of provably total functions.

## Notation

We write $\mathsf{EFA}$ for elementary function arithmetic; other names are $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \mathsf{exp}$, $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0(\mathsf{exp})$, elementary arithmetic and exponential function arithmetic.
The formula $\mathrm{Exp}(x, y)$ is often written $2^x = y$, or $x^y = z$ for the binary version.
A different theory is elementary recursive arithmetic $\mathsf{ERA}$: the quantifier-free system like [[primitive recursive arithmetic|WCXH72T8D8]], but with function symbols only for the elementary functions.

## Remarks

### Examples

- $\mathsf{EFA}$ proves that $x \mapsto 2^{2^x}$ is total, by two uses of the axiom $\mathsf{exp}$; [[this example|FFAWTY9TBE]] checks it.
- $\mathsf{EFA}$ proves that there are infinitely many primes: for each $a$ there is a prime $p$ with $a < p \le a^a + 1$; [[this example|88PS05WWG7]] gives the proof.
- $\mathsf{EFA}$ proves the totality of $x \mapsto x!$, because $x! \le x^x$ and the graph of the factorial has a $\Delta_0$ definition with exponential bounds.
- $\mathsf{EFA}$ proves that every finite sequence of numbers has a code, in the form "for all $n$ and $b$ there is a code of every sequence of length $n$ with entries below $b$", because $(b+1)^{n+1}$ exists.
- $\mathsf{EFA}$ proves the basic facts of syntax: a formula has a unique parse, the set of proofs is $\Delta_0$-definable, and substitution is a total function.

### Non-examples

- The superexponential function, $2_0 = 1$ and $2_{x+1} = 2^{2_x}$, is not provably total in $\mathsf{EFA}$, because it is not elementary; [[this non-example|7T8J7ZM3RC]] checks the growth argument.
- $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ is not $\mathsf{EFA}$: it does not prove $\forall x \, \exists y \, \mathrm{Exp}(x, y)$, by Parikh's theorem below.
- $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$ is not $\mathsf{EFA}$: it proves that the superexponential function is total, while $\mathsf{EFA}$ does not.

### Basic properties

- Parikh's theorem. If $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ proves $\forall x \, \exists y \, \varphi(x, y)$ with $\varphi$ in $\Delta_0$, then there is a term $t(x)$ of $L_A$ such that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ proves $\forall x \, \exists y \le t(x) \, \varphi(x, y)$. A term of $L_A$ is a polynomial, and $2^x$ is not bounded by any polynomial, so $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ does not prove $\mathsf{exp}$.
- Provably total functions. A function $f$ is provably total in $\mathsf{EFA}$ when some $\Sigma_1$ formula $\varphi(x, y)$ defines its graph in $\mathfrak{N}$ and $\mathsf{EFA} \vdash \forall x \, \exists! y \, \varphi(x, y)$. The provably total functions of $\mathsf{EFA}$ are exactly the elementary recursive functions.
- Growth bound. For each elementary function $f$ there is a fixed height $k$ such that $f(x)$ is at most a tower of $k$ twos topped by $x$, for all large $x$. So no provably total function of $\mathsf{EFA}$ grows like the superexponential.
- Position. $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 \subsetneq \mathsf{EFA} \subsetneq \mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$, where each inclusion means "every theorem of the left theory is a theorem of the right theory". On the quantifier-free side, $\mathsf{ERA} \subsetneq \mathsf{PRA}$.
- Ordinal. The proof-theoretic ordinal of $\mathsf{EFA}$ is $\omega^3$, compared with $\omega^2$ for $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ and $\omega^\omega$ for $\mathsf{PRA}$ and $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$.
- Coding. In $\mathsf{EFA}$ the usual arithmetization of syntax works, so the [[second incompleteness theorem|4PZM5GJTR5]] holds for $\mathsf{EFA}$ and its extensions with a suitable formalization of $\mathrm{Con}(T)$.

### Friedman's grand conjecture

Harvey Friedman formulated the following conjecture.
Every theorem published in the Annals of Mathematics whose statement involves only finitary mathematical objects, that is, which is an arithmetical sentence, can be proved in $\mathsf{EFA}$.
The conjecture is open.
It is a claim about the practice of mathematics, not a precise mathematical statement, because "published in the Annals" is not a mathematical property.
It is known to fail for some arithmetical sentences in general: $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{EFA})$ is a true arithmetical sentence that $\mathsf{EFA}$ does not prove, and finite combinatorial statements such as the [[Paris-Harrington theorem|509ZWA0R26]] are not even provable in $\mathsf{PA}$.
The conjecture says that such statements do not occur among the published theorems of ordinary mathematics.

### History

László Kalmár introduced the elementary functions, and they give the theory its name.
The study of $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ and its extension by $\mathsf{exp}$ belongs to the theory of [[fragments of Peano arithmetic|T70PYM1F36]].
Friedman stated the grand conjecture in a post to a mailing list for the foundations of mathematics.

### Common errors

- To think that $\mathsf{EFA}$ has a symbol for every exponential tower. It proves the totality of each tower of fixed height, one at a time, but not of the tower of variable height $2_x$.
- To confuse provability of $\forall x \, \exists y \, \varphi$, for $\varphi$ in $\Sigma_1$, with the truth of each instance. Each instance $\exists y \, \varphi(\overline{n}, y)$ that is true is provable in $\mathsf{Q}$, by [[$\Sigma_1$-completeness|69234Z1V00]]; provable totality is a much stronger property.
- To read the grand conjecture as a theorem. It is open, and it may be false for some published theorem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Elementary function arithmetic is a small set of rules for whole numbers.
It allows addition, multiplication and powers such as $2^{10} = 1024$.
It allows proofs by induction, but only about statements that look at numbers below a given bound.
Its rules can show that each number $x$ has a power $2^x$, and so a power of a power such as $2^{2^3} = 256$.
They cannot show that every tower $2^{2^{\cdots}}$ of any height exists.
Many logicians believe that almost all of number theory can be done with these rules.

### L2 human

We show how $\mathsf{exp}$ gives codes of finite sets, a typical use.

1. A set $X \subseteq \set{0, 1, \ldots, n - 1}$ is coded by the number $c = \sum_{i \in X} 2^i$, so $c < 2^n$.
2. Membership $i \in X$ is the $\Delta_0$ statement "the $i$-th binary digit of $c$ is $1$", which uses only quotients and remainders of $c$ by numbers $\le c$.
3. In $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ alone, the code of the set $\set{0, \ldots, n - 1}$ is $2^n - 1$, and its existence is not provable.
4. In $\mathsf{EFA}$, the axiom $\mathsf{exp}$ gives $2^n$, so every subset of $\set{0, \ldots, n - 1}$ that a $\Delta_0$ formula defines has a code below $2^n$, by $\Delta_0$ induction on $n$.

So in $\mathsf{EFA}$ we can quantify over finite sets of numbers below $n$ with a bounded quantifier $\exists c < 2^n$.
A trap is to think that the bounded quantifier $\exists c < 2^n$ is available in $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$: without $\mathsf{exp}$, the bound $2^n$ need not exist.

### L3 human

$\mathsf{EFA}$ is the natural base theory of weak arithmetic: below it lie $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ and the theories of [[bounded arithmetic|RA380RWEC0]], which match feasible computation, and above it lie $\mathsf{PRA}$, $\mathsf{I}\Sigma_1$ and the [[fragments of Peano arithmetic|T70PYM1F36]].
In reverse mathematics, the systems $\mathsf{RCA}_0^*$ and $\mathsf{WKL}_0^*$ are conservative over $\mathsf{EFA}$ for $\Pi^0_2$ sentences, so they play for $\mathsf{EFA}$ the role that $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ and $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ play for $\mathsf{PRA}$.
The class of provably total functions, the elementary functions, is the level $\mathcal{E}^3$ of the Grzegorczyk hierarchy, and the step from $\mathsf{EFA}$ to $\mathsf{PRA}$ adds all the levels $\mathcal{E}^n$ for $n \ge 4$.
The grand conjecture is a statement about the logical strength of mathematical practice, in the same spirit as the observation in reverse mathematics that many theorems of analysis are provable in, or equivalent to, one of five systems.
