## Example

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite [[graph|00C0BR7D7H]], let $J \in \R$ be a coupling constant and let $\beta \ge 0$ be the inverse temperature.
A configuration assigns to each vertex $x$ a unit vector $s_x$ of $\R^3$, that is, a point of the sphere $S^2$.
Its energy is
$$H(s) = -J \sum_{\set{x, y} \in E} s_x \cdot s_y.$$
The *classical Heisenberg model* on $G$ is the probability measure on $(S^2)^V$ with density $e^{-\beta H(s)} / Z$ with respect to the product of the uniform measures.
Here $Z$ is the normalizing constant.
For $J > 0$ the model is ferromagnetic and for $J < 0$ it is antiferromagnetic.

## Remarks

The energy is invariant under every rotation of $\R^3$ applied to all spins at once, so the model has $O(3)$ symmetry.
It is the case $n = 3$ of the [[n-vector model|N0VMY0EF9G]].
It is the classical limit of the [[quantum Heisenberg model|5QG15H8P5J]], where the spins are operators.
On $\Z^2$ the [[Mermin–Wagner theorem|5C0QQFXSD7]] shows that there is no spontaneous magnetization at any positive temperature.
On $\Z^3$ with $J > 0$, the infrared bounds of Fröhlich, Simon and Spencer show long-range order at low temperature.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each site of a grid carries an arrow that can point in any direction in space.
Neighbouring arrows like to align when $J > 0$.
It is the basic model of a magnet whose magnetization can point anywhere, not only up or down.
