## Definition

Let $\mathcal{H}_A$ and $\mathcal{H}_B$ be complex [[Hilbert spaces|HM2HHE3J51]], and let $\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})$ denote the [[trace class operators|Z2CP530WDE]] on $\mathcal{H}$.
A *quantum operation* from $A$ to $B$ is a linear map $\mathcal{E} \colon \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_A) \to \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_B)$ such that:

1. $\mathcal{E}$ is [[completely positive|CGY299QMC3]]: for every $n \ge 1$, the map $\mathcal{E} \otimes \id_n$ on $\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \C^n)$ sends [[positive operators|77020CY5ZW]] to positive operators;
2. $\mathcal{E}$ does not increase the trace: $\tr \mathcal{E}(\rho) \le \tr \rho$ for every positive $\rho \in \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_A)$.

For a [[density operator|V1JTB52E8C]] $\rho$ with $\tr \mathcal{E}(\rho) > 0$, the number $\tr \mathcal{E}(\rho)$ is the probability that the process occurs, and the output state is $\mathcal{E}(\rho)/\tr \mathcal{E}(\rho)$.

## Remarks

Example: for a projection $P$ on $\mathcal{H}_A = \mathcal{H}_B$, the map $\mathcal{E}(\rho) = P\rho P$ is a quantum operation.
It is completely positive since $(P \otimes I)X(P \otimes I) \ge 0$ for $X \ge 0$, and $\tr(P\rho P) = \tr(P\rho) \le \tr \rho$ for $\rho \ge 0$.
Non-example: the transpose map $\rho \mapsto \rho^T$ on $\C^{2 \times 2}$ preserves positivity and the trace, but clause 1 fails for $n = 2$.
Indeed, applied to $\psi\psi^*$ with $\psi = e_0 \otimes e_0 + e_1 \otimes e_1$, it gives the swap operator, which has the eigenvalue $-1$.
In finite dimensions every quantum operation has the Kraus form $\mathcal{E}(\rho) = \sum_k K_k \rho K_k^*$ with $\sum_k K_k^* K_k \le I$.
