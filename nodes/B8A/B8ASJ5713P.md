## Definition

Let $\varepsilon_0, \mu_0 > 0$ be the vacuum permittivity and permeability, and let $c = 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_0 \mu_0}$.
The *electromagnetic wave equation* for a vector field $u \colon \R^3 \times \R \to \R^3$ is
$$\frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} - \Delta u = 0,$$
where the [[Laplacian|0E0B6RA8K0]] $\Delta$ in the space variable acts on each component.
Let $(E, B)$ be a twice differentiable solution of the source-free [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]].
Then the [[electric field|YZ7Q9DP8RT]] $u = E$ and the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] $u = B$ both satisfy this equation.

## Remarks

Derivation: from $\nabla \times E = -\partial_t B$ and $\nabla \times B = \frac{1}{c^2} \partial_t E$ one gets $\nabla \times (\nabla \times E) = -\frac{1}{c^2} \partial_t^2 E$, and $\nabla \times (\nabla \times E) = \nabla (\nabla \cdot E) - \Delta E = -\Delta E$ because $\nabla \cdot E = 0$.
The same steps give the equation for $B$.
With charges and currents one gets the [[inhomogeneous electromagnetic wave equations|1AE7YGNX2R]] instead.
The converse is false: a pair of solutions of the wave equation need not solve Maxwell's equations, which also require zero divergence and the coupling between $E$ and $B$.
In a linear, homogeneous and isotropic medium with permittivity $\varepsilon$ and permeability $\mu$, $c$ is replaced by $1/\sqrt{\varepsilon \mu}$.

Example: $u(x, y, z, t) = (0, \cos(kx - \omega t), 0)$ with $k > 0$ and $\omega = ck$ is a solution, since $\frac{1}{c^2} \partial_t^2 u_y = -\frac{\omega^2}{c^2} u_y = -k^2 u_y = \Delta u_y$.

Non-example: the same field with $\omega = 2ck$ is not a solution, since then $\frac{1}{c^2} \partial_t^2 u_y - \Delta u_y = -3 k^2 u_y \neq 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each component of the electric and magnetic field obeys the ordinary wave equation with speed $c$.
So in empty space the fields move as waves at the speed of light.
