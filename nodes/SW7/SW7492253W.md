## Definition

Let $U \subseteq \R^m$ be a set of input values and let $f \colon \R^n \times U \to \R^n$ be continuous, with $f(0, u_0) = 0$ for some $u_0 \in U$.
Consider the [[control system|ZPDTVZQN5K]] $x' = f(x, u)$.
A *control-Lyapunov function* for this system is a continuously differentiable function $V \colon \R^n \to \R$ such that:

1. $V(0) = 0$ and $V(x) > 0$ for every $x \ne 0$;
2. $V$ is proper, that is, $V(x) \to \infty$ as $\norm{x} \to \infty$;
3. for every $x \ne 0$ there is $u \in U$ with $\nabla V(x) \cdot f(x, u) < 0$.

Here $\nabla V$ is the [[gradient|0ABK9V9Y64]] of $V$.

## Remarks

Clause 3 says that at every nonzero state some input makes $V$ decrease along the trajectory, so the decrease of $V$ can be forced by the control.
Example: for $x_1' = x_2$, $x_2' = u$ with $U = \R$, take $V(x) = x_1^2 + (x_1 + x_2)^2$.
Then $\nabla V(x) \cdot f(x, u) = 2 x_1 x_2 + 2 (x_1 + x_2)(x_2 + u)$.
If $x_1 + x_2 \ne 0$, a suitable large $u$ of the right sign makes this negative; if $x_1 + x_2 = 0$ and $x \ne 0$, it equals $2 x_1 x_2 = -2 x_1^2 < 0$.
Clauses 1 and 2 hold, so $V$ is a control-Lyapunov function.
Non-example: for $x' = x$ with no effective input ($f(x, u) = x$), the function $V(x) = x^2$ fails clause 3, since $\nabla V(x) \cdot f(x, u) = 2x^2 > 0$ for $x \ne 0$.
For control-affine systems $x' = f(x) + \sum_{i=1}^m u_i g_i(x)$ with $U = \R^m$, clause 3 reads: for $x \ne 0$, if $\nabla V(x) \cdot g_i(x) = 0$ for all $i$, then $\nabla V(x) \cdot f(x) < 0$.
Some authors drop clause 2 and obtain a local notion, or require $V$ smooth instead of $C^1$.
Clause 2 is the [[proper map|15XEBGNGHC]] condition for $V$, also called radial unboundedness.
