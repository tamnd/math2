## Summary

The Randić index of a graph is a sum over its edges of $1/\sqrt{d(u) d(v)}$, where $d(u)$ and $d(v)$ are the [[degrees|0KS0KRDV4Y]] of the two ends.
Milan Randić introduced it in 1975 as a branching index for alkanes, and it is also called the connectivity index.
It is one of the most used degree-based topological indices of [[chemical graph theory|AE724Y8CQX]].
Its extremal values over classes of graphs are a topic of graph theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each bond of a molecule gets a weight that is small when its two atoms have many neighbours.
Adding the weights of all bonds gives the Randić index.
A branched molecule has more crowded atoms, so its index is smaller than that of a chain with the same number of atoms.
For the four-carbon chain the index is $1/\sqrt 2 + 1/2 + 1/\sqrt 2 \approx 1.91$, and for the branched star it is $3/\sqrt 3 \approx 1.73$.
