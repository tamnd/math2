## Summary

A Lie algebroid is a [[vector bundle|P4DZM975BV]] over a manifold whose sections carry a Lie bracket.
It comes with a bundle map to the [[tangent bundle|WY6K3P4YCG]], the anchor, that turns sections into vector fields.
It contains both the tangent bundle of a manifold and a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] as extreme cases.
Lie algebroids are the infinitesimal objects of [[Lie groupoids|HNVW0E732J]], as Lie algebras are the infinitesimal objects of Lie groups.
They appear in Poisson geometry, foliation theory and the study of group actions on manifolds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On a manifold, vector fields can be bracketed: the [[Lie bracket of vector fields|68FGQEBD6J]] measures how two flows fail to commute.
A Lie algebroid copies this structure on another vector bundle.
Its sections can be bracketed, and each section points in some direction on the manifold through the anchor map.
When the manifold is a single point, there are no directions, and what remains is a Lie algebra.
When the bundle is the tangent bundle itself and the anchor is the identity, the Lie algebroid is just the usual bracket of vector fields.
