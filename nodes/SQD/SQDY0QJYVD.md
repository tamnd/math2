## Summary

The conformal bootstrap is a method to determine or constrain [[conformal field theories|837V89WJKS]] from consistency conditions alone, without a Lagrangian.
It uses the [[operator product expansion|EN02ENDWWD]] to write a four-point function in two different ways and requires that the two agree, together with unitarity.
The idea goes back to the early 1970s.
Since 2008 numerical versions have produced rigorous bounds on scaling dimensions and precise critical exponents, for example for the three-dimensional Ising model.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a conformal field theory the fields come with a multiplication table, the operator product expansion.
A four-point correlation can be computed by first multiplying the fields in pairs one way, or the other way, and both answers must be equal, a kind of associativity.
This single requirement, together with the condition that probabilities are positive, is surprisingly restrictive.
By testing which tables can satisfy it, one can rule out large regions of possible theories and pin down the few that survive.
