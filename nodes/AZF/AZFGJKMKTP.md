## Summary

Seismic ray theory is the high-frequency approximation of elastic wave propagation in which energy travels along curves called rays.
The travel time solves an [[eikonal equation|HGMX1QGTAZ]], and the rays obey [[Fermat's principle|RKX9XJ0RVW]], as light rays do in geometric optics.
It is the basis of earthquake location, travel-time tables and travel-time tomography.
It is accurate when the wavelength is much shorter than the scale on which the medium changes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Instead of following a whole wave, follow one small piece of the wavefront as it moves through the Earth.
Its path is a ray, and the time it takes is the travel time.
Where the rock gets faster with depth, rays bend back up toward the surface, like light in a mirage.
In a uniform rock the rays are straight lines and the travel time is the distance divided by the speed.
Ray theory fails near sharp corners and caustics, where the full wave picture is needed.
