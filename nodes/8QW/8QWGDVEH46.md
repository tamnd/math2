## Summary

The moment magnitude scale measures the size of an earthquake by a logarithm of its [[seismic moment|88YRH7XQDX]].
It was introduced by Hanks and Kanamori in 1979 and is now the standard scale for medium and large earthquakes.
Unlike older scales read from wave amplitudes, it does not saturate for very large earthquakes.
Its constants were chosen so that it roughly agrees with the older magnitude scales where those work well.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Earthquakes range over enormous sizes, so their sizes are given on a logarithmic scale.
On the moment magnitude scale, one step up means about $32$ times more seismic moment, and two steps mean $1000$ times.
A magnitude $6$ earthquake might come from a fault about ten kilometres long; a magnitude $9$ one ruptures hundreds of kilometres.
Because it rests on the physical size of the fault slip, the scale keeps working for the very largest earthquakes.
