## Example

Model the hydrogen atom as one electron in the Coulomb field of a fixed proton.
Use atomic units, in which the electron mass, the elementary charge, $\hbar$ and $4\pi\varepsilon_0$ all equal $1$.
The Hamiltonian is the [[Schrödinger operator|89AA6XJY5K]] $H = -\frac12 \Delta - \frac{1}{\abs{x}}$ on $L^2(\R^3)$, where $\Delta$ is the Laplacian.
Its [[essential spectrum|FRZG0E15SK]] is $[0, \infty)$.
Below $0$ its spectrum consists of the eigenvalues
$$E_n = -\frac{1}{2n^2}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots,$$
and $E_n$ has multiplicity $n^2$.

Check for $n = 1$: let $\psi(x) = \pi^{-1/2} e^{-r}$ with $r = \abs{x}$.
For a radial function, $\Delta f = f'' + \frac{2}{r} f'$, so $\Delta \psi = \left( 1 - \frac{2}{r} \right) \psi$.
Then $H\psi = -\frac12 \psi + \frac1r \psi - \frac1r \psi = -\frac12 \psi$.
Also $\int_{\R^3} e^{-2r}\, dx = 4\pi \int_0^\infty r^2 e^{-2r}\, dr = \pi$, so $\psi$ has norm $1$.

## Remarks

The eigenfunctions separate in spherical coordinates as $R_{n\ell}(r)\, Y_\ell^m(\theta, \varphi)$, with $\ell = 0, \ldots, n - 1$ and $m = -\ell, \ldots, \ell$.
The angular parts are [[spherical harmonics|3F32Y21HP4]], and the radial parts involve associated [[Laguerre polynomials|WAMRHWW82R]].
Counting the pairs $(\ell, m)$ gives $\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1} (2\ell + 1) = n^2$, the multiplicity of $E_n$.
Rotation invariance alone only explains the degeneracy in $m$.
The extra degeneracy in $\ell$ comes from a hidden $SO(4)$ symmetry, generated by the angular momentum and the Runge–Lenz vector.
In physical units $E_n \approx -13.6\ \mathrm{eV}/n^2$, which matches the spectral lines of hydrogen up to small relativistic corrections.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The hydrogen atom is one electron held near one proton by electric attraction.
Quantum mechanics allows the bound electron only the energies $-1/(2n^2)$ in atomic units, for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$.
Jumps between these levels emit or absorb light of definite colours, the spectral lines of hydrogen.
It is one of the few realistic systems whose spectrum can be computed exactly, so it is the model for all of atomic physics.
