## Summary

The skin effect is the tendency of an alternating current in a conductor to flow mainly in a thin layer near the surface.
Inside a good conductor, a time-harmonic field decays exponentially with depth, with a length scale called the skin depth.
The skin depth shrinks like the inverse square root of the frequency.
It explains why the resistance of a wire grows with frequency and why thin metal sheets shield high-frequency fields.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a current changes direction quickly, it induces eddy currents inside the conductor that oppose the change deep inside.
So the current is pushed out toward the surface, and the middle of the wire carries almost nothing.
Picture a copper rod where only a thin outer shell is "used" at high frequency.
For copper the skin depth is about $8$ mm at $60$ Hz but only about $0.07$ mm at $1$ MHz.
This is why radio engineers use hollow tubes or many thin strands instead of thick solid wires.
