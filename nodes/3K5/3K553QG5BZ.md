## Summary

An orbital resonance occurs when bodies orbiting the same primary have periods in a ratio close to a ratio of small integers.
Their mutual pulls then act coherently.
Repeated encounters at the same points of their orbits then add up instead of averaging out.
Resonances can protect orbits, as for Pluto and Neptune, or destabilise them, as for the gaps in the asteroid belt.
See the [[definition|DMPYWAGHQ3]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Push a child on a swing at the right rhythm and the swing goes higher.
Planets and moons give each other small regular pushes too.
When their periods fit a simple ratio, such as two orbits of one for every orbit of the other, the pushes come at the same place each time.
Io, Europa and Ganymede, three moons of Jupiter, have periods in the ratio about $1 : 2 : 4$.
