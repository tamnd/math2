## Summary

A fixed point of the renormalization group is a Hamiltonian, or a set of coupling constants, that a [[renormalization group|5XTSCZCF7Z]] transformation maps to itself.
Since such a transformation divides the correlation length by the scale factor, a fixed point has correlation length $0$ or $\infty$.
Fixed points with infinite correlation length describe critical points, and the flow near them determines the [[critical exponents|SQ41AZGZ2P]].
All models that flow to the same fixed point form one [[universality class|GWXP0KSW9Y]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Zoom out from a system again and again, and record its effective parameters after each zoom.
A fixed point is a choice of parameters that looks exactly the same after zooming out.
A perfectly ordered magnet and a completely random one are trivial fixed points: they look the same at every scale.
A magnet exactly at its critical temperature is the interesting case, since it has fluctuations of all sizes and still looks the same at every scale.
The behaviour of the parameters near this fixed point explains why very different materials have the same critical exponents.
