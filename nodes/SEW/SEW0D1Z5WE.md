## Summary

The London equations are two equations that relate the supercurrent in a superconductor to the electric and magnetic fields.
Combined with [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]], they show that a magnetic field decays exponentially inside a superconductor over the London penetration depth.
This explains the Meissner effect, the expulsion of magnetic field from a superconductor.
They are the simplest phenomenological description of superconductivity, and they appear as a limit of Ginzburg–Landau theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In an ordinary metal, a current needs an electric field to keep flowing.
In a superconductor the electric field instead speeds the current up, as if the electrons felt no friction.
The second London equation says more: a magnetic field creates a current that cancels the field inside the material.
So a magnetic field applied at the surface dies out within a thin layer, typically tens to hundreds of nanometres thick.
For a flat superconductor filling the half-space $x > 0$, the field inside is $B_0 e^{-x/\lambda}$, where $B_0$ is the field at the surface and $\lambda$ is the penetration depth.
