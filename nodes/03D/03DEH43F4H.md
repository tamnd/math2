## Summary

Let $G$ be a [[group|G5S1M9]], let $H$ be a [[subgroup|0X1JXT47GA]] of $G$, and let $g \in G$.
The left coset of $H$ with representative $g$ is the set $gH = \{gh : h \in H\}$.
It is the set of all products of $g$ on the left with an element of $H$.
For $a, b \in G$, the left cosets $aH$ and $bH$ are equal if and only if $a^{-1}b \in H$, and they are disjoint otherwise.
The left cosets cut $G$ into pieces of equal size, and this gives [[Lagrange's theorem|3C53TD]].

## Motivation

A subgroup $H$ is often too small to see the whole group at once.
The left cosets move $H$ around $G$, one copy for each element.
Two elements $a$ and $b$ are in the same copy when $b$ is $a$ times an element of $H$.
This sorting of $G$ into copies of $H$ is the first tool to compare the size and the structure of $G$ and $H$.

## Notation

The [[left coset|05FD317V2W]] of $g$ is written $gH$, and in additive notation $g + H$.
The set of all left cosets of $H$ in $G$ is written $G/H$.
This notation does not mean that $G/H$ is a group: it is a group only when $H$ is a [[normal subgroup|0CB0DX5BVV]].
In this site "coset" means "left coset" when no side is given.

## Remarks

### Examples

- In $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$, the left cosets of $3\mathbb{Z}$ are $3\mathbb{Z}$, $1 + 3\mathbb{Z}$ and $2 + 3\mathbb{Z}$, as [[this example|BM4VXG92DT]] shows.
- In $(\mathbb{R}^2, +)$, the left cosets of a line through the origin are the parallel lines, as [[this example|3WTCVF5RP8]] shows.
- In $S_3$ with $H = \{e, (1\,2)\}$, the left cosets are $H$, $\{(1\,3), (1\,2\,3)\}$ and $\{(2\,3), (1\,3\,2)\}$, as [[this example|6VFF2TTXYZ]] shows.
- In the group $\mathbb{R}^\times$ of nonzero real numbers under multiplication, the subgroup $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ of positive numbers has two left cosets. They are $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ and $-1 \cdot \mathbb{R}_{>0}$, the negative numbers.

### Non-examples

- In $S_3$, the set $H(1\,3) = \{(1\,3), (1\,3\,2)\}$ is a right coset of $H = \{e, (1\,2)\}$, but it is not a left coset of $H$, as [[this example|M9F4BWH6EN]] shows.
- In $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, the set $\{1, 2\}$ is $1 + \{0, 1\}$, but it is not a left coset of any subgroup. The only subgroup of order $2$ is $\{0, 2\}$, with the cosets $\{0, 2\}$ and $\{1, 3\}$. [[This example|94SNG0EQQZ]] gives the details.

### Basic properties

Let $a, b, g \in G$.

- The element $g$ is in $gH$, because $g = ge$ and $e \in H$.
- The equality $gH = H$ holds if and only if $g \in H$.
- Criterion: $aH = bH$ if and only if $a^{-1}b \in H$. If $aH = bH$, then $b = be \in aH$, so $b = ah$ and $a^{-1}b = h \in H$. If $a^{-1}b = h \in H$, then $bH = ahH = aH$, because $hH = H$.
- Two left cosets are equal or disjoint. If $c \in aH \cap bH$, then $cH = aH$ and $cH = bH$ by the criterion.
- The left cosets form a [[partition|08QMKTWF9T]] of $G$. They are the [[equivalence classes|0W95W33CMP]] of the relation $a^{-1}b \in H$.
- [[Each left coset has the same cardinality as $H$|0DKMBWTD7P]].
- The left multiplication by $x \in G$ maps the left coset $gH$ to the left coset $xgH$. This is the [[action of $G$ on $G/H$|0AZG100DKH]].

### History

The left cosets of a subgroup of a permutation group are present in the work of Galois around 1830.
Most English textbooks now use left cosets as the standard, and some older books use right cosets.

### Common errors

- The criterion is $a^{-1}b \in H$, not $ab^{-1} \in H$. The condition $ab^{-1} \in H$ is the criterion for the right cosets $Ha$ and $Hb$.
- A left coset $gH$ with $g \notin H$ is not a subgroup, because it does not contain $e$.
- The representative of a coset is not unique: $gH = ghH$ for each $h \in H$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a subgroup as a stamp.
Each element of the group lets you press the stamp at a new place, by multiplying on the left.
Each print is a left coset, and each print has the same number of elements as the stamp.
Two prints are the same or they do not overlap at all.
For the even numbers inside the whole numbers, there are two prints: the even numbers and the odd numbers.

### L2 human

We find the left cosets of $H = \{1, -1\}$ in the group $G = \{1, -1, i, -i\}$ of fourth roots of unity, with multiplication.

1. The set $H$ is a subgroup, because $(-1)(-1) = 1$ and each element is its own inverse.
2. The left coset of $1$ is $1 \cdot H = \{1, -1\}$.
3. The left coset of $i$ is $iH = \{i, -i\}$.
4. The left coset of $-1$ is $\{-1, 1\} = H$. The criterion agrees: $1^{-1} \cdot (-1) = -1 \in H$.
5. The left coset of $-i$ is $\{-i, i\} = iH$. The criterion agrees: $i^{-1} \cdot (-i) = (-i)(-i) = -1 \in H$.
6. Thus there are two left cosets, $\{1, -1\}$ and $\{i, -i\}$, and they partition $G$.

The typical use of the criterion is to decide fast whether two elements give the same coset.
Compute $a^{-1}b$ and test whether it is in $H$.

A trap is to test $ab^{-1}$ instead of $a^{-1}b$.
In $S_3$ with $H = \{e, (1\,2)\}$, take $a = (1\,3)$ and $b = (1\,2\,3)$.
Then $a^{-1}b = (1\,3)(1\,2\,3) = (1\,2) \in H$, so $aH = bH$.
But $ab^{-1} = (1\,3)(1\,3\,2) = (2\,3) \notin H$, so $Ha \neq Hb$.

### L3 human

The left cosets of $H$ are the orbits of the right action of $H$ on $G$, $g \mapsto gh$.
The set $G/H$ is the standard transitive $G$-set: each transitive action of $G$ is isomorphic to the action on $G/H$ for a stabiliser $H$.
In topology and geometry, $G/H$ is a homogeneous space, for example a sphere or a projective space.
In representation theory, the functions on $G/H$ form the permutation representation induced from the trivial representation of $H$.
