## Summary

Topological cyclic homology, written $TC$, is a spectrum built from [[topological Hochschild homology|PFW77RAAY3]] using its circle action and its Frobenius maps.
It was introduced by Bökstedt, Hsiang and Madsen.
It receives the cyclotomic trace from [[algebraic K-theory|5GVFZWMWC8]], and for nilpotent extensions of rings the two theories change in the same way.
This makes $TC$ the main tool for computing K-theory of $p$-adic and local rings.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Topological Hochschild homology of a ring has a symmetry by rotating a circle.
It also has a Frobenius map for each prime $p$, related to winding the circle $p$ times around itself.
Topological cyclic homology keeps exactly the part of $THH$ on which the circle-invariant structure and the Frobenius maps agree.
This part is close enough to K-theory that the difference can often be controlled, yet it can be computed by homotopy theory.
For example, after completion at $p$, $TC(\mathbb{F}_p)$ has homotopy groups $\Z_p$ in degrees $0$ and $-1$ and zero elsewhere.
