## Definition

Let $K$ be a field.
An *octonion algebra* over $K$ is a [[composition algebra|1Q9G6AST10]] over $K$ that has a unit and has dimension $8$ over $K$.

## Remarks

Every octonion algebra is alternative and not associative.
If the characteristic of $K$ is not $2$, it is the Cayley–Dickson double $\mathrm{CD}(Q, \mu)$ of a quaternion algebra $Q$ over $K$ with some $\mu \neq 0$.
Two octonion algebras over $K$ are isomorphic if and only if their norms are isometric.
An octonion algebra is *split* if its norm has a nonzero zero, and then it has zero divisors; otherwise it is a division algebra.
Example: the [[octonions|1HRMT3DSX2]] $\mathbb{O}$ with $N(x) = x\bar{x}$; this norm is the sum of the squares of the eight coordinates, which is nondegenerate and multiplicative.
Non-example: the quaternions $\mathbb{H}$ are a unital composition algebra, but of dimension $4$.
Over $\C$ and over finite fields, every octonion algebra is split, so there is only one up to isomorphism.
