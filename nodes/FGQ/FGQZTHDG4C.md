## Example

A *rattleback* is a rigid body with a curved bottom, often close to half an ellipsoid, resting on a horizontal table.
Its [[principal axes of inertia|WVGTEH6P10]] are turned by a small angle with respect to the directions of greatest and least curvature of its bottom.
Spun about the vertical in one direction, it keeps spinning.
Spun in the other direction, it starts to rock, slows down, and then spins in the opposite direction.

It is a standard example of a [[nonholonomic system|PTRV0QFSB9]].
The usual model lets the body [[roll without slipping|PPW75DGKDR]] on the table, which is a constraint on velocities, not on positions.
In this model, with no dissipation of energy, a linear stability analysis of steady spinning already shows the asymmetry.
Steady spin is stable for one sense of rotation and unstable for the other, and the instability turns spin into rocking.

## Remarks

The misalignment is necessary.
If the principal axes of inertia are aligned with the directions of curvature, a mirror reflection exchanges the two senses of spin, so both behave alike.
Real rattlebacks also lose energy through friction, which is why the reversal is eventually followed by the body coming to rest.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The rattleback, or celt, is a curved piece of wood or plastic that seems to prefer spinning one way.
Spin it the wrong way, and it wobbles, stops spinning, and then turns around and spins the other way.
The trick is that its shape and its mass are slightly twisted with respect to each other.
