## Summary

Constant elasticity of substitution (CES) is a property of a production or utility function.
It says that the elasticity of substitution between the inputs is the same at every input bundle.
The CES functions $A (a K^{\rho} + (1 - a) L^{\rho})^{1/\rho}$ form a one-parameter family with elasticity $\sigma = 1/(1 - \rho)$.
Its limit cases are perfect substitutes as $\rho \to 1$, the [[Cobb–Douglas|XWMZSDDKTZ]] function as $\rho \to 0$ and the [[Leontief|KTTZ1J9SPZ]] function as $\rho \to -\infty$.
So a single parameter measures how easily inputs replace each other.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The elasticity of substitution says how strongly the mix of inputs reacts to their relative price.
If labour becomes $1\%$ more expensive relative to capital, a firm with elasticity $\sigma$ shifts its ratio of capital to labour by about $\sigma \%$.
For CES functions this number is a constant that does not depend on where the firm produces.
At $\sigma = 0$ the inputs must be used in fixed proportions, at $\sigma = 1$ one has Cobb–Douglas, and as $\sigma$ grows the inputs become close to interchangeable.
