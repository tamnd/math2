## Summary

The radical of a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] is its largest [[solvable|W7A2R0GR3T]] [[ideal|JWMTXQ8A3A]].
It exists because the sum of two solvable ideals is again a solvable ideal.
The quotient by the radical is semisimple, and the Lie algebras with zero radical are exactly the semisimple ones.
The radical is the solvable part in the Levi decomposition of a Lie algebra.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra contains a biggest ideal that is solvable, that is, built from commutative layers.
This ideal is the radical, the "messy" solvable part of the algebra.
Dividing it out leaves a semisimple algebra, which is much better understood.
For example, in the Lie algebra of all $n \times n$ complex matrices, the radical is the line of scalar matrices.
