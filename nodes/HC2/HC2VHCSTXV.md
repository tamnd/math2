## Summary

The Clohessy–Wiltshire equations describe the motion of a chaser spacecraft relative to a target on a circular orbit, when the two are close.
They are linear with constant coefficients, so they can be solved in closed form.
They are the standard model for rendezvous, docking and formation flying.
See the [[definition|HRR414Q2V2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Two spacecraft fly close to each other around the Earth.
Seen from one of them, the other drifts in strange ways: pushing forward makes it rise and fall behind.
The Clohessy–Wiltshire equations predict these relative motions.
For example, a craft placed slightly below the target and moving with the right speed drifts steadily ahead of it.
