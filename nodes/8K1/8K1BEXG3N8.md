## Summary

Mathematical finance and insurance is the study of the valuation of cash flows, securities and contingent claims, the choice of portfolios, the measurement of financial risk, and the actuarial models of life and non-life insurance.
Its central notion is the absence of arbitrage, and the fundamental theorem of asset pricing links this condition to the existence of an equivalent probability measure under which discounted prices are martingales.
Banks and insurers use the theory to price and hedge contracts, to manage risk and to set capital requirements.
Bachelier modelled prices by Brownian motion in 1900, Lundberg started the theory of ruin in 1903, and Black, Scholes and Merton published their option pricing theory in 1973.
This topic collects the mathematical finance and insurance pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Stochastic analysis, stochastic processes, mathematical economics and statistical models have their own topics.
