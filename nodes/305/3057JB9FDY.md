## Definition

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a space of Earth models, such as functions giving a physical property at each point of a region.
Let $\mathcal{D} = \R^N$ be the space of data.
Let $G \colon \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{D}$ be a forward operator given by the governing physics, and let $d \in \mathcal{D}$ be observed data, with $d = G(m_{\mathrm{true}}) + e$ for an unknown error $e$.
*Geophysical inversion* is the [[inverse problem|PCMW7KN5P4]] of finding models $m \in \mathcal{M}$ with $G(m)$ close to $d$ within the size of the errors.
The usual formulation is the regularised [[least squares|0BYTQ4X1HC]] problem
$$\min_{m \in \mathcal{M}} \norm{W_d (G(m) - d)}^2 + \lambda \norm{W_m (m - m_{\mathrm{ref}})}^2.$$
Here $W_d$ weights the data by their errors, and $W_m$ measures the roughness or size of the model.
The model $m_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a reference model, and $\lambda > 0$ is a trade-off parameter.

## Remarks

Example: in straight-ray travel-time inversion, $m$ is the slowness in $n$ cells and $G(m) = A m$, where $A_{ij}$ is the length of ray $i$ in cell $j$.
The problem is linear, and the minimiser solves
$$(A^T W_d^T W_d A + \lambda W_m^T W_m)\, m = A^T W_d^T W_d\, d + \lambda W_m^T W_m\, m_{\mathrm{ref}}.$$
Non-example: forward modelling, the computation of $G(m)$ for a given $m$, is not an inversion.
The penalty term is a form of [[Tikhonov regularization|M68V0M5A3R]], and $\lambda$ is often chosen so that the data misfit matches the expected size of the errors.
Bayesian inversion replaces the minimiser by a posterior probability distribution on $\mathcal{M}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The forward operator turns an Earth model into predicted measurements.
Inversion looks for a model whose predictions match the real measurements, and among such models it prefers a simple one.
