## Statement

Let $K$ be a commutative ring, let $n \ge 2$, and let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $K$.
Let $M_{ij}$ be the first [[minors|0SD528P0EK]] and $C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} M_{ij}$ the [[cofactors|0W2QAVJQ1W]] of $A$.
Then, for each row $i$,
$$\det A = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} C_{ij} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} (-1)^{i+j} a_{ij} M_{ij},$$
where $\det$ is the [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]].
The same holds for each column $j$: $\det A = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ij} C_{ij}$; this is the [[expansion along a column|06AMCJMMVM]].

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

The sign $(-1)^{i+j}$ is needed; without it the expansion is wrong, as the [[non-example on unsigned minors|4HC79H7TJR]] shows.
The entries and the cofactors must come from the same row: with row $k \neq i$ of entries and row $i$ of cofactors, the sum is $0$, by the [[adjugate identity|0528HKWZ1Z]].

### Consequences

- A determinant of order $n$ is a combination of $n$ determinants of order $n - 1$. With $\det(a) = a$ for $n = 1$, this is a recursive definition, the Laplace recursion of the [[equivalence theorem|W3JN0WPZ10]].
- The expansion is efficient along a row or column with many zeros.
- If row $i$ has only one nonzero entry $a_{ij}$, then $\det A = (-1)^{i+j} a_{ij} M_{ij}$.
- $\det A$ is a linear function of row $i$ with coefficients $C_{ij}$, so $C_{ij} = \partial \det A / \partial a_{ij}$.

### Converse

The cofactors are determined by the expansion: if $\det A = \sum_j a_{ij} D_j$ for all values of row $i$, with $D_j$ independent of row $i$, then $D_j = C_{ij}$.
Take row $i$ equal to $e_j$ to see this.

### Generalizations

The [[general Laplace expansion|541X51GQTV]] expands along $k$ rows at once, with products of complementary minors.
The expansion with cofactors of another row gives $0$, and this yields $A \operatorname{adj} A = (\det A) I_n$.

## History

Laplace gave the general expansion of a determinant in terms of its complementary minors in 1772.
Vandermonde had already given a special case.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To expand a determinant along a row, take each entry of that row.
Multiply it by its cofactor, which is the signed determinant of what is left after deleting its row and column.
Add these products.
The signs follow a checkerboard, starting with plus in the top left corner.

### L2 human

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 4 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$.

1. Column $2$ has only one nonzero entry, $a_{22} = 4$, with sign $(-1)^{2+2} = +1$.
2. So $\det A = 4 \det \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix} = 4 (15 - 4) = 44$.
3. Check along row $1$: $3 (20 - 0) - 0 + 2 (0 - 8) = 60 - 16 = 44$.

The typical use is a hand computation with a row or column full of zeros, and proofs by induction on $n$.
A trap: a full expansion of an $n \times n$ determinant has $n!$ products, so for large $n$ row reduction is much faster.

### L3 human

The expansion along row $i$ is the formula $\det(\dots, x, \dots) = \langle x, c_i \rangle$, where $c_i$ is row $i$ of the cofactor matrix.
In exterior algebra it is the pairing $\Lambda^1 \otimes \Lambda^{n-1} \to \Lambda^n$; the general Laplace expansion is the pairing $\Lambda^k \otimes \Lambda^{n-k} \to \Lambda^n$.
