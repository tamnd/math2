## Summary

Complex K-theory assigns to a compact space $X$ the group $K^0(X)$ built from complex [[vector bundles|P4DZM975BV]] over $X$.
It also assigns groups $K^{-n}(X)$ obtained from suspensions.
These groups form a generalized cohomology theory, denoted $KU$, and by [[Bott periodicity|WN2DCBSX1C]] they repeat with period $2$.
It is the first and most used example of [[topological K-theory|6XCFW7YGX8]].
It has applications to index theory and to the Hopf invariant one problem.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Vector bundles over a space can be added by taking direct sums, but not subtracted.
K-theory allows formal differences $[E] - [F]$, in the same way as the integers are built from the natural numbers.
Over a single point a complex vector bundle is just a vector space, so $K^0(\text{point}) = \Z$, counting dimensions.
Over a sphere $S^2$ there are line bundles that are not trivial, and $K^0(S^2)$ is $\Z^2$, one copy for the rank and one for the twisting.
The groups $K^{-n}(X)$ come from bundles on suspensions of $X$ and make K-theory behave like a cohomology theory.
