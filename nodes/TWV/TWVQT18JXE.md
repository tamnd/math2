## Summary

The Killing form of a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] $\mathfrak g$ is the symmetric bilinear form $\kappa(x, y) = \tr(\operatorname{ad} x \circ \operatorname{ad} y)$.
It is an [[invariant bilinear form|GMB9ANSTT1]] that every finite-dimensional Lie algebra carries.
By Cartan's criterion, in characteristic $0$ it detects solvability and semisimplicity: $\mathfrak g$ is semisimple exactly when $\kappa$ is nondegenerate.
For the Lie algebra of a compact Lie group it is negative semidefinite.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each element $x$ of a Lie algebra acts on the algebra by "bracket with $x$".
To compare two elements, multiply their actions and take the trace of the result.
This number is the Killing form.
For $2 \times 2$ trace zero matrices, it equals $4 \tr(xy)$, a multiple of the usual trace pairing of matrices.
