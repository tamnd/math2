## Summary

Stellar dynamics studies the motion of stars, or other point masses, under their mutual [[gravity|29WRN5J730]] in systems such as star clusters, galaxies and galaxy clusters.
These systems contain from thousands to hundreds of billions of stars, so they are described statistically.
The description uses a distribution function in phase space.
It obeys the [[Vlasov equation|SJH7M2VCTE]], also called the collisionless Boltzmann equation, coupled with [[Poisson's equation|DWK5N5SKTK]].
Close encounters between individual stars are rare and act slowly, so on short time scales a stellar system behaves as a collisionless fluid.
The field connects celestial mechanics with kinetic theory and supplies the models used to measure masses of galaxies and dark matter halos.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A galaxy is like a gas whose molecules are stars, held together by their own gravity.
Unlike gas molecules, stars almost never collide or even pass close to each other.
Each star moves in the smooth gravitational field of the whole galaxy, and the shape of the galaxy is in turn made by the stars.
Stellar dynamics finds the arrangements of stars and velocities that are consistent with this.
An example is a spherical cluster in which every star follows its own orbit but the cluster as a whole never changes.
