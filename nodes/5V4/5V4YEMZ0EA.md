## Summary

Magnetic helicity is a number that measures how much the field lines of a [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] twist around each other and link with each other.
It is the integral of the dot product of a [[vector potential|8PEMR2J7EF]] with the field.
For thin flux tubes it counts the [[linking number|SFJ329YB9F]] of the tubes, weighted by their fluxes, so it is a topological quantity.
It is nearly conserved in highly conducting plasmas, which makes it central in solar physics and fusion research.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine the field lines of a magnet as strands of rope.
If two closed strands are linked like two rings of a chain, they cannot be pulled apart without cutting one.
Helicity adds up how often the strands wind around each other, and how much each bundle of strands is twisted, with the strength of the field as a weight.
In a hot plasma the field lines move with the gas and cannot be cut easily, so this count stays almost the same over time.
