## Statement

Let a fixed amount of an [[ideal gas|N7R0H2VNDJ]] be kept at a fixed temperature.
Then its pressure $p$ and volume $V$ in equilibrium satisfy
$$pV = \text{const},$$
so that for any two such states $p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2$.

## Remarks

It is the special case $T$ and $n$ fixed of the [[ideal gas law|VXVGVDSJ0W]] $pV = nRT$.
The states of an [[isothermal process|C9MM7GFRWN]] of an ideal gas therefore lie on a hyperbola in the $(V, p)$ plane.
Example: if air in a syringe at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is slowly compressed from $30\,\mathrm{ml}$ to $10\,\mathrm{ml}$ at constant temperature, its pressure becomes $300\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
Both hypotheses matter.
If the compression is fast, the gas warms and the pressure rises faster than $1/V$.
For a real gas at high pressure, $pV$ varies at fixed temperature, as the [[virial expansion|QMJ04KSQ59]] shows.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Squeeze a gas into half the space without changing its temperature, and its pressure doubles.
Pressure and volume are inversely proportional.
This is why a sealed balloon taken up a mountain, where the outside pressure is lower, grows larger.

### L2 human

Boyle's law was one of the first quantitative laws of gases, found from experiments in the seventeenth century.
Together with Charles's law and Avogadro's law it gives the ideal gas law.
It is used to compute the work $W = nRT \ln(V_2/V_1)$ done by an ideal gas in a reversible isothermal expansion, obtained by integrating $p = nRT/V$.
