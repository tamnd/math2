## Statement

Let $K$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]] and let $L$ be a [[free Lie algebra|NNAXK7BM3H]] over $K$ on some set of generators.
Then every [[Lie subalgebra|WKSSTQ69AK]] of $L$ is itself a free Lie algebra over $K$, on some set of generators.

## Remarks

The theorem was proved by Shirshov in 1953 and independently by Witt in 1956.
The proofs are combinatorial.
They use a basis of $L$ made of bracketed words, such as a Hall or Lyndon basis, and a leading-word reduction that extracts free generators of the subalgebra.
Example: in the free Lie algebra on $x, y$, the ideal generated by $y$ is free on the elements $y, [x, y], [x, [x, y]], \ldots$, that is, on $(\operatorname{ad} x)^n (y)$ for $n \ge 0$.
This also shows that a subalgebra of a finitely generated free Lie algebra need not be finitely generated.
The analogue fails for free associative algebras: in $K[t]$, the free associative algebra on one generator, the subalgebra $K[t^2, t^3]$ is not free.
It is commutative, so it could only be free on one generator, but it is not generated by a single element.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A free Lie algebra is the most general Lie algebra built from some generators, with no relations beyond those that every Lie algebra must satisfy.
The theorem says that whatever piece of it one cuts out, as long as it is closed under the bracket, is again free.
So freeness can never be lost by passing to a Lie subalgebra.

### L2 human

The theorem is the Lie algebra counterpart of the Nielsen–Schreier theorem, which says that subgroups of free groups are free.
It does not hold for every type of algebra: free associative algebras and polynomial rings have subalgebras that are not free.
A variety of algebras in which every subalgebra of a free algebra is free is called a Schreier variety.
The theorem says that Lie algebras over a field form one.
Elimination, the passage from $L(x, y)$ to the ideal generated by $y$, is a basic tool in the study of free Lie algebras and their bases.
