## Proof

We replace each step of an $\mathcal F_1$-proof by a fixed $\mathcal F_2$-derivation of the same rule, under the substitution of that step, and we count lines and symbols.
The proof is complete when $\mathcal F_1$ and $\mathcal F_2$ use the same set $K$ of connectives, and the case of different sets is outlined at the end.
For a formula $\theta$ and a [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]] $\sigma$, write $\theta\sigma$ for the result of replacing each variable $p$ by $\sigma(p)$.

1. *Substitution lemma.*
   Let $D = \theta_1, \dots, \theta_r$ be an $\mathcal F_2$-derivation from hypotheses $\Gamma$, and let $\sigma$ be a substitution.
   Then $\theta_1\sigma, \dots, \theta_r\sigma$ is an $\mathcal F_2$-derivation from $\Gamma\sigma$.
   The reason is that if $\theta_i$ is the conclusion of a rule instance by a substitution $\rho$, then $\theta_i\sigma$ is the conclusion of the same rule by $p \mapsto \rho(p)\sigma$, with premises the images of earlier lines.
2. *Fixed derivations.*
   Let $R = \varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k \,/\, \psi$ be a rule of $\mathcal F_1$.
   It is sound, so $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k \models \psi$, and $\mathcal F_2$ is implicationally complete, so some $\mathcal F_2$-derivation $D_R$ of $\psi$ from $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k$ exists.
   We fix one such $D_R$ for each rule $R$.
3. *Constants.*
   $\mathcal F_1$ has finitely many rules.
   Let $c$ be the largest number of lines of a $D_R$, and let $c'$ be the largest size of a formula in some $D_R$.
4. *Translation.*
   Let $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_m$ be an $\mathcal F_1$-proof, and let $\chi_i$ be the conclusion of an instance of $R$ by $\sigma$, with premises among $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_{i-1}$.
   Extend $\sigma$ by $\sigma(q) = q$ for the variables $q$ of $D_R$ that are not in $R$.
   We replace $\chi_i$ by the list $D_R\sigma$, with its hypothesis lines removed.
5. *Correctness.*
   By step 1, $D_R\sigma$ derives $\psi\sigma = \chi_i$ from $\varphi_1\sigma, \dots, \varphi_k\sigma$.
   By induction on $i$, these hypotheses are already lines of the new list, so the removed lines are not needed, and the new list is an $\mathcal F_2$-proof of $\chi_m$.
6. *Lines.*
   Each $\chi_i$ is replaced by at most $c$ lines, so the new proof has at most $c m$ lines.
7. *Size of one line.*
   Let $S = \lvert \chi_1 \rvert + \dots + \lvert \chi_m \rvert$.
   For a variable $p$ of $R$, the formula $\sigma(p)$ is a subformula of a line of the old proof, so $\lvert \sigma(p) \rvert \le S$.
   A formula $\theta$ of $D_R$ has at most $c'$ symbols, each replaced by at most $S$ symbols, so $\lvert \theta\sigma \rvert \le c' S$.
8. *Total size.*
   The new proof has at most $c m$ lines of size at most $c' S$ each, and $m \le S$, so its size is at most $c c' S^2$.
9. *Polynomial time.*
   The translation finds a rule and a substitution for each line and writes $D_R\sigma$.
   Each part takes time polynomial in $S$, because the rules and the derivations $D_R$ are fixed.
10. *p-simulation.*
    Let $f_1$ and $f_2$ be the proof systems of $\mathcal F_1$ and $\mathcal F_2$, let $\tau_0$ be the tautology that $f_1$ outputs on strings that are not $\mathcal F_1$-proofs, and fix an $\mathcal F_2$-proof $w_0$ of $\tau_0$.
    Let $h(w)$ be the translation of $w$ when $w$ is an $\mathcal F_1$-proof, and $w_0$ otherwise.
    Then $h$ runs in polynomial time by step 9, and $f_2(h(w)) = f_1(w)$ for every string $w$ by step 5.

So $\mathcal F_2$ p-simulates $\mathcal F_1$, and the constant $\max(c, c c')$ gives the bounds of the statement.
Exchanging the roles of the two systems gives p-equivalence.
$\blacksquare$

*Different connectives, in outline.*
Reckhow's proof first rewrites each line into the connectives of $\mathcal F_2$.
A direct rewriting can be exponentially long, for example when $\leftrightarrow$ is written with $\land$, $\lor$ and $\neg$, since each $\leftrightarrow$ copies both sides.
So each formula is first rebalanced to depth $O(\log n)$, which a formula of size $n$ allows, and then translated with polynomial growth.
The argument above is then applied to the translated rules.

## Idea

Each rule of one system is a derived rule of the other, because the other is implicationally complete, and the derivation is fixed once and for all.
Replacing each step by a substituted copy of that fixed derivation multiplies the number of lines by a constant.
The size grows at most quadratically, because each substituted formula is no longer than the whole proof.
The whole difficulty of the general case lies in the translation of formulas between bases, not in the rules.

## Variants

The same proof shows that any two [[extended Frege systems|5YSE04SHFH]] over the same connectives are p-equivalent, since an extension step is copied as it is.
If every formula of every $D_R$ has depth at most $d_0$, the translation raises the depth of each line by at most $d_0$.
So the proof also works for [[bounded-depth Frege|41QJ1WEMJ3]], where the depth of lines is the measure kept bounded.
