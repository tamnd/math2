## Summary

Representation theory is the study of groups and algebras through their actions by linear maps on vector spaces.
A representation of a group is a homomorphism from the group to the group of invertible linear maps of a vector space.
Representations turn questions about groups into questions of linear algebra, and they are used in number theory, in combinatorics, in harmonic analysis and in quantum physics.
Frobenius created the character theory of finite groups in 1896, and Burnside, Schur, Weyl and Brauer extended the theory in the following decades.
This topic collects the representation theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Group theory, Lie theory and topological groups, harmonic analysis, rings and modules and automorphic forms have their own topics.
