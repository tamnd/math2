## Definition

Let particles with masses $m_1, \dots, m_N > 0$ and positions $x_1, \dots, x_N \in \R^3$ be given, and fix an opening angle $\theta > 0$.
The *Barnes–Hut algorithm* computes approximate gravitational accelerations as follows.

1. Build an octree. The root is a cube containing all particles. A cube with more than one particle is split into eight equal subcubes, recursively, until each leaf holds at most one particle.
2. For each cell $C$ of the [[tree|062D9KXJB1]], store its total mass $M_C$ and its centre of mass $X_C$.
3. To compute the acceleration of particle $i$, traverse the tree from the root. Let a cell $C$ have side $s_C$ and distance $d = \norm{x_i - X_C}$. If $s_C / d < \theta$ or $C$ is a leaf, add the term $-G M_C (x_i - X_C)/d^3$, omitting particle $i$ itself. Otherwise visit the children of $C$.

A *Barnes–Hut simulation* is an [[N-body simulation|DRCDVJS6PT]] whose accelerations at each time step are computed by this algorithm.

## Remarks

Example: with $\theta \to 0$, every cell is opened down to the leaves, and the method returns the exact sum of [[Newton's law of gravitation|29WRN5J730]] over all pairs.
With a typical value $\theta \approx 0.5$ to $1$, the number of cells visited per particle grows like $\log N$ for roughly uniform distributions, so the cost per step is of order $N \log N$.

Non-example: replacing every cell, near or far, by its centre of mass (no opening criterion) is not the Barnes–Hut algorithm; it gives large errors for nearby particles.

The approximation by the centre of mass is the monopole term of a multipole expansion; variants add the quadrupole term for better accuracy.
