## Statement

Rayleigh's theorem states that for two positive [[irrational numbers|080T2TXVQV]] $r$ and $s$, both greater than one, with $\tfrac1r+\tfrac1s=1$, the two sequences $\lfloor i\cdot r\rfloor$ and $\lfloor i\cdot s\rfloor$ for $i=1,2,3,\dots$, obtained by rounding down to an integer the multiples of $r$ and $s$, are complementary. It can be seen as an instance of the Lambek–Moser theorem with $f(n)=\lfloor rn\rfloor-n$ and $f^\ast(n)=\lfloor sn\rfloor-n$. The condition that $r$ and $s$ be greater than one implies these two functions are non-decreasing; the derived functions are $F(n)=\lfloor rn\rfloor$ and $F^*(n)=\lfloor sn\rfloor$. The sequences of values of $F$ and $F^*$ forming the derived partition are known as Beatty sequences, after Samuel Beatty's 1926 rediscovery of Rayleigh's theorem.
