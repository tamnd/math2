## Summary

BCS theory, due to John Bardeen, Leon Cooper and Robert Schrieffer in 1957, is the microscopic theory of conventional superconductivity.
A weak attraction between electrons near the [[Fermi surface|GK3SVTXXKX]], mediated by lattice vibrations, binds them into Cooper pairs.
The pairs condense into one coherent state.
The theory predicts an energy gap $\Delta$ in the excitation spectrum and a critical temperature $T_c$, with the universal ratio $2\Delta(0) \approx 3.53\, k_B T_c$.
Lev Gor'kov later derived the [[Ginzburg–Landau equations|713MRSZ9XA]] from it near $T_c$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a superconductor the electrical resistance drops to zero below a critical temperature.
BCS theory explains this by pairing: two electrons with opposite momenta and spins attract each other slightly through the vibrations of the crystal.
Near the Fermi surface even a tiny attraction forms bound pairs, as Cooper showed.
All pairs then share one quantum state, and breaking a pair costs a minimum energy, the gap.
Because small disturbances cannot pay this cost, the current flows without loss.
