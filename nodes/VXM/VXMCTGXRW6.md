## Summary

Structure formation is the process by which galaxies, clusters of galaxies and the cosmic web grew from tiny density fluctuations in the early universe.
Gravity amplifies regions that are slightly denser than average, and they eventually collapse into bound objects.
In the standard cold dark matter model small objects form first and merge into larger ones.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The early universe was almost perfectly smooth, with density differences of about one part in a hundred thousand.
A slightly denser region pulls in matter from around it and becomes denser still; this is the [[Jeans instability|S1PZ23YAXQ]] at work in an expanding universe.
Over billions of years these regions grow into dark matter halos, and gas cooling inside them forms galaxies.
Between them, matter collects in sheets and filaments around large empty voids.
