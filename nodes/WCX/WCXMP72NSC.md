## Summary

Geodesy is the science that measures and models the Earth as a whole.
It studies the [[shape and size of the Earth|4W7PGZ0B37]], its orientation in space and its [[gravity field|5Y2BWNY6MF]], and how these change in time.
Its mathematics includes the geometry of the [[reference ellipsoid|R9XX7TNJCH]], [[map projections|Y2C8D9VC7D]], potential theory and least-squares adjustment.
Geodesy supplies the coordinate frames used by maps, surveying and satellite navigation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Geodesy answers the question "where exactly is this point on the Earth?".
To answer it, one needs a precise model of the Earth's shape and a way to attach coordinates to points.
The Earth is close to a ball, slightly flattened at the poles, and its gravity varies from place to place.
For example, the equator is about $21$ km farther from the centre than the poles are.
Geodesists measure such facts with surveying instruments, gravity meters and satellites.
