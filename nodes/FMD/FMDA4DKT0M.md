## Summary

The phase velocity of a sinusoidal [[plane wave|NM024G378A]] is the speed at which its crests, the points of constant phase, move.
For a wave of angular frequency $\omega$ and wave number $k$ it equals $\omega / k$, and it is read off from the [[dispersion relation|R5P8M6SBG7]].
For light in a medium of refractive index $n$ it is $c / n$.
It can exceed the speed of light without any conflict with relativity, since a pure sinusoid carries no signal.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Watch one crest of a wave and follow it: its speed is the phase velocity.
A wave $\cos(kx - \omega t)$ has crests where $kx - \omega t$ is a multiple of $2\pi$, so the crests move at speed $\omega / k$.
For example $\cos(2x - 6t)$ has crests moving at speed $3$.
In glass light crests move at about two thirds of their speed in vacuum.
