## Example

Let $n \ge 2$ and let $\mathcal{O}_n$ be the [[Cuntz algebra|36Q7XH9P7W]], generated by isometries $s_1, \ldots, s_n$ with $\sum_{i=1}^n s_i s_i^* = 1$.
Its K-groups are
$$K_0(\mathcal{O}_n) \cong \Z/(n-1)\Z, \qquad K_1(\mathcal{O}_n) = 0,$$
and the class $[1]$ of the unit generates $K_0(\mathcal{O}_n)$.
For the Cuntz algebra $\mathcal{O}_\infty$, generated by a sequence of isometries with mutually orthogonal ranges, $K_0(\mathcal{O}_\infty) \cong \Z$ with generator $[1]$, and $K_1(\mathcal{O}_\infty) = 0$.

Check of the relation $(n - 1)[1] = 0$: each $s_i$ satisfies $s_i^* s_i = 1$, so the projection $s_i s_i^*$ is [[Murray–von Neumann equivalent|GT9S7APKG1]] to $1$.
The projections $s_i s_i^*$ are mutually orthogonal and add up to $1$, so in [[K0|BP86XQRZD2]],
$$[1] = \sum_{i=1}^n [s_i s_i^*] = n [1],$$
which gives $(n - 1)[1] = 0$.
That $[1]$ has order exactly $n - 1$, that it generates, and that [[K1|7NTVTHC7CG]] vanishes are the substance of Cuntz's computation.
For $n = 2$ this gives $K_0(\mathcal{O}_2) = 0$ and $K_1(\mathcal{O}_2) = 0$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In the Cuntz algebra $\mathcal{O}_n$, the whole space can be split into $n$ pieces, each of which is as large as the whole space.
In K-theory this says that the unit counts the same as $n$ copies of itself, so $n - 1$ copies of it count as nothing.
The K-groups of the different $\mathcal{O}_n$ are different, so these algebras are not isomorphic for different $n$.
