## Definition

Let $N \ge 2$ and $J \in \R$.
Consider the spin-$1/2$ Heisenberg XXX chain on $N$ sites with periodic boundary conditions.
It is the operator on $(\C^2)^{\otimes N}$ given by
$$H = J \sum_{j=1}^{N} \left( \mathbf{S}_j \cdot \mathbf{S}_{j+1} - \tfrac{1}{4} \right), \qquad \mathbf{S}_{N+1} = \mathbf{S}_1,$$
where $\mathbf{S}_j$ is one half of the vector of Pauli matrices acting on site $j$.
For $0 \le M \le N/2$, let $| x_1, \dots, x_M \rangle$ with $1 \le x_1 < \dots < x_M \le N$ be the basis vector with spins down at the sites $x_i$ and up elsewhere.
Let $S_M$ be the [[symmetric group|0515A6D2Y1]], let $k_1, \dots, k_M$ be complex numbers and let $A_P$, for $P \in S_M$, be complex amplitudes.
The *coordinate Bethe ansatz* is the trial vector $\sum \psi(x_1, \dots, x_M) | x_1, \dots, x_M \rangle$ with
$$\psi(x_1, \dots, x_M) = \sum_{P \in S_M} A_P \exp\left( i \sum_{j=1}^{M} k_{P(j)} x_j \right).$$
The ratios of the amplitudes are fixed by the two-body scattering of the momenta.
Write $e^{i k_j} = (\lambda_j + i/2)/(\lambda_j - i/2)$.
Suppose that the $\lambda_j$ are distinct and satisfy the *Bethe equations*
$$\left( \frac{\lambda_j + i/2}{\lambda_j - i/2} \right)^{N} = \prod_{l \ne j} \frac{\lambda_j - \lambda_l + i}{\lambda_j - \lambda_l - i}, \qquad j = 1, \dots, M.$$
Then the vector, when it is nonzero, is an [[eigenvector|0BQAA2BK3Q]] of $H$.
Its eigenvalue is then $E = -\frac{J}{2} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \frac{1}{\lambda_j^2 + 1/4}$.

## Remarks

More generally, a Bethe ansatz for a one-dimensional model is a trial eigenfunction of this form.
It is a sum over permutations of products of plane waves, with amplitudes that factor into two-body scattering terms.
Example: for $M = 1$ the ansatz is the plane wave $\psi(x) = e^{i k x}$.
Here $H$ equals $\frac{J}{2} \sum_j (P_{j, j+1} - 1)$, where $P_{j, j+1}$ swaps sites $j$ and $j+1$, so $H \psi$ has coefficients $\frac{J}{2}(\psi(x-1) + \psi(x+1) - 2\psi(x))$ and the eigenvalue is $J(\cos k - 1)$.
Periodicity gives $e^{i k N} = 1$, which is the Bethe equation for $M = 1$, and $J(\cos k - 1) = -\frac{J}{2} \frac{1}{\lambda^2 + 1/4}$.
Non-example: adding a next-nearest-neighbour coupling $\sum_j \mathbf{S}_j \cdot \mathbf{S}_{j+2}$ to $H$ spoils the factorization of many-body scattering into two-body terms, and the ansatz no longer gives eigenvectors.
The algebraic form of the method is the [[quantum inverse scattering method|ZQ3ZZ9AQR1]].
