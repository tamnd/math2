## Summary

The membrane theory of shells is the simplest [[shell theory|DF7RPJHHY1]].
It assumes that a thin shell carries its loads only by forces in its middle surface, without bending moments.
The equilibrium equations then involve only the membrane forces and the curvature of the surface.
For many smooth shells under smooth loads, such as tanks under pressure and domes under their own weight, this gives the main state of stress.
Bending becomes important near edges, supports and point loads, where the membrane state cannot satisfy all the conditions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A balloon has almost no stiffness against bending, yet it holds pressure.
It does so by pulling tight within its own surface, and its curvature turns this pull into a push outwards that balances the pressure.
Membrane theory treats a shell in this way, as a curved skin under tension or compression.
For a sphere of radius $R$ under pressure $p$, the skin carries a force $pR/2$ per unit length in every direction.
