## Summary

The Orr–Sommerfeld equation is the linear ordinary differential equation that governs small wave-like disturbances of a parallel shear flow of a viscous incompressible fluid.
It comes from the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] linearised about a flow $U(y)$ in one direction that depends only on the distance $y$ across the flow.
Together with boundary conditions at the walls it is an eigenvalue problem, and its eigenvalues decide the linear [[stability|H028A17Z55]] of the flow.
It is the basic tool of linear stability theory for channel flows and boundary layers.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine water flowing steadily in layers between two plates, with a speed that changes from one layer to the next.
Add a tiny ripple to this flow, shaped like a wave that travels downstream.
The ripple either dies out or grows, and the Orr–Sommerfeld equation tells which.
It does this by reducing the full flow equations to one equation for the shape of the ripple across the channel.
For water between two plates pushed by a pressure gradient, the equation predicts that ripples start to grow once the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]] passes about $5772$.
