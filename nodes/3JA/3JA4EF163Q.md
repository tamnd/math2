## Summary

Let $G$ be a [[locally compact group|AHV05ZBCYW]].
Its Fourier algebra $A(G)$ is the algebra of functions $x \mapsto \ip{\lambda(x)\xi}{\eta}$ on $G$, where $\lambda$ is the [[left regular representation|PR8C073VWY]] and $\xi, \eta \in L^2(G)$.
With pointwise operations and a suitable norm it is a commutative Banach algebra of continuous functions vanishing at infinity.
For abelian $G$ it is the image of $L^1$ of the dual group under the Fourier transform.
For general $G$ it serves as a replacement for $L^1$ of a dual group, which does not exist when $G$ is not abelian.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For the integers, the Fourier coefficients of an integrable function on the circle form a sequence on $\Z$.
These sequences form an algebra under termwise multiplication, and that algebra is the Fourier algebra of $\Z$.
For a general group there is no dual group, but one can still form the analogous functions from the way the group shifts square-integrable functions.
The result is an algebra of functions on the group itself, and it remembers the group completely: from $A(G)$ as a Banach algebra one can recover $G$.
