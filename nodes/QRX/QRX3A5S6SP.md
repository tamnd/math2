## Summary

The Kompaneets equation describes how repeated Compton scattering by a hot thermal gas of electrons changes the spectrum of a photon gas.
It is a nonlinear diffusion equation in photon energy, of [[Fokker–Planck|FVQWR7NS00]] type, for the photon occupation number.
It holds when the electrons are non-relativistic and each scattering changes the photon energy only a little.
It is the basic tool for the Comptonised spectra of X-ray sources and for the [[Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect|D4J2Z7J7K2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

When light bounces many times off hot electrons, each bounce nudges the photon energy up or down by a small amount.
On average, photons that are colder than the electrons gain energy and hotter ones lose it.
The Kompaneets equation tracks the whole spectrum under this random walk in energy, like a heat equation where energy plays the role of position.
If one waits long enough, the spectrum relaxes to the equilibrium form of photons at the temperature of the electrons.
