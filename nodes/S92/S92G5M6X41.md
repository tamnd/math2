## Summary

The Bethe ansatz is a method to find exact eigenvectors and eigenvalues of certain one-dimensional quantum many-body Hamiltonians.
It guesses that the eigenfunction is a sum of products of plane waves over all orderings of the momenta.
The amplitudes are given by two-body scattering factors.
Periodic boundary conditions then turn into a system of algebraic equations for the momenta, the Bethe equations.
Hans Bethe introduced it in 1931 for the spin-$1/2$ [[quantum Heisenberg model|5QG15H8P5J]] chain.
It later solved the [[Lieb–Liniger model|YVNNZD8F4N]], the [[six-vertex model|45HEVXM7RD]] and many other [[exactly solvable models|DVQ0PVHKY1]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a chain of quantum spins, a flipped spin moves like a wave.
With several flipped spins, the waves bump into each other.
For special models every collision of many waves breaks into collisions of two, so the whole state is fixed by how two waves scatter.
The Bethe ansatz writes the state this way, and the problem shrinks from a huge matrix to a few equations for the wave momenta.
