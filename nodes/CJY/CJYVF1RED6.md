## Statement

Consider the vacuum [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]], or the Einstein equations coupled to a suitable matter model.
Let the initial data be [[asymptotically flat|3HTF65XMF7]], complete and generic.
Then the maximal [[globally hyperbolic|PZGHVCQB13]] development of the data, given by the [[Choquet-Bruhat–Geroch theorem|626MENG0QH]], has a complete future null infinity.

## Remarks

Informally, singularities formed by gravitational collapse are hidden from distant observers inside an [[event horizon|D8NZ32Z3SQ]]: there are no naked [[singularities|45AE2YXWT3]].
The formulation with complete future null infinity is due to Christodoulou; Penrose's original 1969 statement was not given in precise mathematical form.
The word "generic" cannot be dropped.
Christodoulou constructed spherically symmetric solutions of the Einstein equations coupled to a scalar field in which a naked singularity forms.
He later proved that such solutions are unstable under perturbation of the data.
The conjecture is open in general.
It is a hypothesis behind several classical results, such as the [[Hawking area theorem|42EQ4G6978]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a massive star collapses, general relativity predicts a singularity, a place where the theory breaks down.
If such a place could be seen from far away, physics outside would lose its predictive power.
Penrose suggested that nature is protected from this: in realistic collapse the singularity is always cloaked by the horizon of a black hole.
Whether this is true for Einstein's equations is one of the central open problems of mathematical relativity.
