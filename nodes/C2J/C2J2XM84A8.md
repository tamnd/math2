## Definition

Fix the basis of gates AND and OR of fan-in $2$ and NOT of fan-in $1$.
Let $n \ge 1$ and let $f \colon \set{0,1}^n \to \set{0,1}$ be a [[Boolean function|0X05GS525E]].
The *size* of a [[Boolean circuit|SQXNFGVADX]] is its number of gates.
Its *depth* is the largest number of gates on a path from an input to the output.
The *circuit complexity* of $f$ is
$$\mathrm{size}(f) = \min \set{\mathrm{size}(C) : C \text{ is a circuit with } n \text{ inputs that computes } f}.$$
The *depth complexity* $\mathrm{depth}(f)$ is the least depth of a circuit computing $f$.
For a language $L \subseteq \set{0,1}^*$, its circuit complexity is the function $n \mapsto \mathrm{size}(f_n)$, where $f_n$ is the indicator function of $L \cap \set{0,1}^n$.

## Remarks

Example: the function $\mathrm{AND}_n(x) = x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n$ has $\mathrm{size}(\mathrm{AND}_n) = n - 1$.
A chain of $n - 1$ AND gates computes it.
Conversely, the function depends on all $n$ inputs, so all of them must be connected to the output.
A connected graph built from gates with at most two inputs needs at least $n - 1$ such gates to join $n$ inputs.

Non-example: the number of gates of one particular circuit for $f$ is only an upper bound for $\mathrm{size}(f)$.
For instance, a circuit for $\mathrm{AND}_2$ with four gates does not show $\mathrm{size}(\mathrm{AND}_2) = 4$; in fact $\mathrm{size}(\mathrm{AND}_2) = 1$.

Changing to another finite complete basis of fan-in-two gates changes $\mathrm{size}(f)$ by at most a constant factor.
Every $f$ on $n$ variables satisfies $\mathrm{size}(f) = O(2^n / n)$, a result of Lupanov, and most $f$ need size of that order by [[Shannon's counting argument|2R30KR3Z5G]].
