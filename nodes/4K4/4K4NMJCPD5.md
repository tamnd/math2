## Summary

The Einstein radius is the angular radius of the ring image that a gravitational lens forms when the source lies exactly behind it.
It sets the angular scale of every lensing effect of that lens: image separations, the size of arcs and the duration of microlensing events.
It depends on the mass of the lens and on the distances to the lens and to the source.

## Explanations

### L1 human

If a distant galaxy sits exactly behind a massive object, its light is bent around all sides equally, and we see a ring of light.
The angular size of this ring is the Einstein radius.
A heavier lens bends light more and gives a larger ring.
For a star in our galaxy lensing a more distant star, the Einstein radius is about a thousandth of an arcsecond, too small to see the ring.
For a cluster of galaxies it can reach tens of arcseconds.
