## Summary

Spin is the intrinsic angular momentum of a quantum particle, carried in addition to the orbital angular momentum of its motion.
A particle of spin $s$, with $s$ an integer or half-integer, has an internal state space of dimension $2s + 1$.
Rotations act on it through an irreducible representation of [[SU(2)|E5P8AG6CK7]].
Half-integer spins, such as $\frac12$ for the electron, have no classical analogue: a rotation by $2\pi$ multiplies the state by $-1$.
Spin decides the statistics of [[identical particles|Y64G7W4BEC]]: particles of half-integer spin are fermions and those of integer spin are bosons.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An electron behaves as if it were a tiny spinning top with a magnetic needle, although it is not literally spinning.
Measured along any axis, its spin takes only two values, up or down, $\pm\hbar/2$.
This two-valuedness explains the splitting of a beam of silver atoms in two by a non-uniform magnetic field, in the Stern–Gerlach experiment.
Spin is also what makes magnets magnetic and what the Pauli principle in chemistry is about.
