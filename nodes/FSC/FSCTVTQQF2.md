## Summary

Physical optics, or wave optics, treats light as a wave and explains the effects that ray optics cannot: interference, diffraction and polarisation.
In its scalar form, a monochromatic light field is a complex amplitude that solves the [[Helmholtz equation|MNXKTXJ35G]].
It sits between [[geometrical optics|90F3NWRHVD]], the limit of small wavelength, and the full electromagnetic theory of Maxwell's equations.
The name also refers to an approximation in scattering theory.
It replaces the currents on a large smooth scatterer by those of the tangent plane on its lit side.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ray optics pictures light as straight lines, which works well for lenses and mirrors.
But light passing through a narrow slit spreads out, and two beams can cancel each other to give dark fringes.
These effects show that light is a wave with a very short wavelength, about half a micrometre for visible light.
Physical optics is the theory that keeps track of the wave and its phase, so it can add waves and predict where they reinforce or cancel.
