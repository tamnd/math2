## Summary

Shell theory models thin elastic bodies whose middle surface is curved, such as domes, pipes, car bodies, egg shells and aircraft skins.
It reduces three-dimensional elasticity to equations on the middle [[surface|VGJJVBZ1MQ]], expressed through its [[first fundamental form|MAJ3DH7MZS]] and [[second fundamental form|9KHQR16P1B]].
A shell resists loads both by stretching of its middle surface and by bending, and the curvature couples the two.
This is why a thin curved shell can be much stiffer than a flat [[plate|782XMC21FH]] of the same thickness.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An egg shell is very thin, yet it is hard to crush by squeezing its ends.
Its curved shape lets it carry the load by stretching and compressing within its surface, which takes far more force than bending.
Shell theory describes such bodies by the shape of their middle surface and how that surface stretches and bends.
A flat plate is the special case of a shell with no curvature.
