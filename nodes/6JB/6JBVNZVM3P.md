## Summary

The tests of general relativity are the observations and experiments that compare the predictions of [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]] with nature.
They start with the three classical tests proposed by Einstein.
These are the perihelion advance of Mercury, the bending of light by the Sun, and the gravitational redshift.
Modern tests include the Shapiro time delay, frame-dragging, binary pulsars and the direct detection of gravitational waves.
They are usually phrased in the [[parameterized post-Newtonian formalism|2TAW8GAMM1]], which measures how far gravity departs from general relativity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A theory of gravity must agree with what we can measure.
In the Solar System, general relativity differs from Newton's theory only by tiny amounts, so the tests look for tiny effects.
For example, the orbit of Mercury turns by about $43$ arcseconds per century more than Newton's theory predicts, and general relativity explains exactly this amount.
Other tests time radar echoes from planets, watch starlight bend near the Sun, or measure the rate of clocks at different heights.
So far general relativity has passed every such test.
