## Summary

A C*-algebra is a complex [[Banach algebra|9KYWN94QDN]] with an involution $a \mapsto a^*$ whose norm satisfies the [[C* identity|KDV7W5EDTJ]] $\norm{a^* a} = \norm{a}^2$.
One basic example is the algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space.
Another is the algebra of continuous functions vanishing at infinity on a locally compact space.
By the Gelfand–Naimark theorems these examples are universal: commutative C*-algebras are function algebras, and every C*-algebra is a closed algebra of operators.
C*-algebras are the setting of operator K-theory and of noncommutative topology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Matrices with complex entries can be added, multiplied, and conjugate-transposed, and they have a size given by the operator norm.
A C*-algebra is any algebra with these operations in which the size and the conjugate transpose are linked by one rule: $\norm{a^* a} = \norm{a}^2$.
Continuous complex functions on a compact space, with complex conjugation and the maximum norm, form a commutative example.
Since every commutative example with a unit has this form, a general C*-algebra can be thought of as the functions on a "noncommutative space".
