## Summary

The Julian day is a continuous count of days used by astronomers, starting at noon on 1 January 4713 BC in the proleptic Julian calendar.
The Julian date adds the fraction of the day since the last noon, so one real number gives an instant of time.
Differences of Julian dates give time intervals directly, with no months, leap years or calendar reforms in the way.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Calendars are awkward for arithmetic: months have different lengths and leap years come and go.
Astronomers avoid this by numbering the days one after another from a very old starting point.
To find how many days lie between two events, subtract their Julian dates.
For example, noon on 1 January 2000 has Julian date $2\,451\,545.0$.
