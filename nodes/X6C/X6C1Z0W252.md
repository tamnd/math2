## Example

On $\R^2$ let $B(x, y) = x_1 y_1 - x_2 y_2$, with matrix $A = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1)$.

1. $\det A = -1 \neq 0$, so $B$ is nondegenerate by the [[criteria for nondegeneracy|3ND2FNJ38Y]].
2. For $v = (a, b)$, $B(v, v) = a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$.
3. So $B(v, v) = 0$ exactly when $b = a$ or $b = -a$. The isotropic vectors are the nonzero vectors on the two lines $b = a$ and $b = -a$.
4. Let $W$ be the line through $u = (1, 1)$. For $v = (v_1, v_2)$, $B(u, v) = v_1 - v_2$.
5. So the [[orthogonal complement|05Q4JD6XEH]] $W^\perp$ is the line $v_1 = v_2$, which is $W$ itself.
6. $\dim W + \dim W^\perp = 1 + 1 = 2$, as the dimension statement says. But $W \cap W^\perp = W \neq \{0\}$.
7. Let $v = \frac12 (1, -1)$. Then $B(u, v) = \frac12 (1 + 1) = 1$ and $B(v, v) = \frac14 (1 - 1) = 0$.
8. So in the basis $(u, v)$ the matrix of $B$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

## Remarks

The two lines of isotropic vectors form the light cone of the plane of special relativity with one space dimension, as in [[the Minkowski form on the plane|TFGECQ5C32]].
The isotropic vectors with $0$ do not form a subspace: $(1, 1) + (1, -1) = (2, 0)$, and $B((2, 0), (2, 0)) = 4$.
Step 8 shows that $B$ is a hyperbolic plane, with the hyperbolic pair $(u, v)$.
The form is nondegenerate, but its restriction to $W$ is the zero form, so $\R^2 \neq W \oplus W^\perp$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The form $x_1 y_1 - x_2 y_2$ gives $0$ on some nonzero vectors with themselves.
These vectors lie on the two diagonals of the plane.
Each diagonal is its own orthogonal complement.
This cannot happen for the dot product.

### L2 human

The recipe to find isotropic vectors of a form in two variables is the following.

1. Write $B(v, v)$ as a quadratic polynomial in the coordinates.
2. Factor it, or solve it for one coordinate.
3. The solutions other than $0$ are the isotropic vectors.

The typical use is to find a basis in which the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, as in step 8.
A trap: the form is not degenerate, although it vanishes on the line $W$.
Degenerate means that a vector pairs to $0$ with every vector.
