## Definition

*Physical oceanography* is the study of the ocean as a physical system.
Its objects of study are:

1. the distribution of temperature, salinity and density in the ocean;
2. the motions of sea water at all scales, from the general circulation and its currents to [[waves|R9WC0Z7Z69]], tides and turbulent mixing;
3. the exchanges of momentum, heat and fresh water between the ocean, the atmosphere and the sea ice.

The motions are modelled by the [[Navier–Stokes equations|18KXZHVF4M]] in a frame rotating with the Earth, with the [[Coriolis force|6HZRX3WSQD]] and gravity.
An equation of state gives the density of sea water.

## Remarks

Example: explaining why the strongest currents of the subtropical gyres lie at the western side of the ocean basins is a problem of physical oceanography, under clause 2.
Non-example: the study of plankton populations belongs to biological oceanography, since it concerns living organisms rather than the physical state of the water.
Physical oceanography is often divided into descriptive oceanography, which maps the observed state, and dynamical oceanography, which explains it with the equations of motion.
