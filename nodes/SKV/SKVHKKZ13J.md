## Definition

The *galactic coordinate system* is the [[celestial coordinate system|YT0G7RE2GM]] on the heliocentric [[celestial sphere|W7GD4WR497]] with these data:

1. the fundamental plane is a conventional approximation of the mid-plane of the Milky Way;
2. the pole is the north galactic pole, on the same side as the north celestial pole;
3. the primary direction points from the Sun approximately towards the centre of the Galaxy.

The latitude is the galactic latitude $b \in [-90^\circ, 90^\circ]$ and the longitude is the galactic longitude $l \in [0^\circ, 360^\circ)$.

## Remarks

The plane and the primary direction are fixed by convention of the International Astronomical Union, not by measurement at each epoch.
In J2000.0 [[equatorial coordinates|QBEJJPMWV1]] the north galactic pole lies near $\alpha \approx 192.86^\circ$, $\delta \approx +27.13^\circ$.
The direction $l = 0$, $b = 0$ lies in Sagittarius.
Example: a star in the direction of the north galactic pole has $b = +90^\circ$, and its longitude is undefined.
Non-example: coordinates with the ecliptic as fundamental plane are not galactic coordinates; the ecliptic is tilted by about $60^\circ$ against the galactic plane.
