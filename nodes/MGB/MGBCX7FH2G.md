## Summary

The Haag–Kastler axioms describe a relativistic quantum field theory by a net of [[C*-algebras|M2WFY1D3SZ]].
The net gives one algebra of observables for each bounded region of Minkowski space.
The axioms ask for isotony, locality, covariance under the [[Poincaré group|8ZQ9TA4Q2Q]] and a vacuum state with positive energy.
They are the base of algebraic quantum field theory, which studies [[quantum field theory|W031YNVRE3]] without fields, using only local observables.
Many structural results, such as the [[Reeh–Schlieder theorem|A0N6031YWZ]], can be stated and proved in this frame.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a laboratory one can only measure things inside a bounded region of space and time.
The Haag–Kastler approach attaches to each such region the algebra of all measurements that can be made there.
A bigger region gives a bigger algebra.
Two regions too far apart for light to travel between them give measurements that do not disturb each other, so their operators commute.
For the free scalar field, the algebra of a region is generated by the field smeared with test functions supported in that region.
