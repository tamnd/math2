## Example

A smooth curve has no jumps, and a curve without jumps on $[0, 1]$ has an area under it, so a smooth curve on $[0, 1]$ has an area under it.
Let $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function.
Let $p$ be "$f$ is [[differentiable|0K57YG3BB8]] at every point of $\mathbb{R}$", let $q$ be "$f$ is continuous at every point of $\mathbb{R}$", and let $r$ be "the restriction of $f$ to $[0, 1]$ is Riemann integrable".
We check that the argument "$p \to q$, $q \to r$, therefore $p \to r$" is an instance of [[hypothetical syllogism|7MT8CPCEM4]] with true premises.

1. The premise $p \to q$ is true, by the theorem that [[differentiable functions are continuous|07ZMX4ANJS]] applied at each point of $\mathbb{R}$.
2. The premise $q \to r$ is true.
   If $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, its restriction to $[0, 1]$ is continuous, and [[continuous functions on a closed bounded interval are Riemann integrable|06SESV7GQY]].
3. The consequent $q$ of the first premise is the antecedent of the second premise, word for word.
   So the form is $\varphi \to \psi$, $\psi \to \chi$ with $\varphi = p$, $\psi = q$ and $\chi = r$.
4. Hypothetical syllogism gives $p \to r$: every function differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ is Riemann integrable on $[0, 1]$.

Check: $f(x) = x\lvert x\rvert$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$, with $f'(x) = 2\lvert x\rvert$, so $\int_0^1 f$ exists, and its value is $\int_0^1 x^2\,dx = 1/3$.

## Remarks

### What the example shows

A theorem can be built from two known theorems, without any new estimate.
Neither link can be reversed.
The function $\lvert x\rvert$ is continuous but not differentiable at $0$.
The function equal to $0$ on $[0, 1/2]$ and $1$ elsewhere is integrable on $[0, 1]$ but not continuous.
So the conclusion $p \to r$ is not reversible either, and its [[converse|09A3N7ZN5G]] fails for the same step function.

### Watch out

The chain runs in one direction only: an integrable function need not be continuous, and a continuous function need not be differentiable.

### Try it

Does $\int_0^1 e^{x^2}\,dx$ exist?
Yes: $e^{x^2}$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ by the chain rule, so the chain gives that it is Riemann integrable on $[0, 1]$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A smooth curve has no corners, and a curve without corners has no jumps.
A curve without jumps on $[0, 1]$ has an area under it.
Putting the two steps together: a smooth curve on $[0, 1]$ has an area under it.

### L2 human

We show that $\int_0^1 e^{x^2}\,dx$ exists.
The function $e^{x^2}$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$, by the chain rule, which is $p$.
Link 1 gives $q$: $e^{x^2}$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.
Link 2 gives $r$: $e^{x^2}$ is Riemann integrable on $[0, 1]$.
Once both links are known theorems, the middle statement can be skipped.
