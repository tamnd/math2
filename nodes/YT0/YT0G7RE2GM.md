## Summary

A celestial coordinate system gives each point of the [[celestial sphere|W7GD4WR497]] two angles, a latitude and a longitude.
It is fixed by a fundamental great circle and a starting direction on that circle.
The usual systems are the horizontal, equatorial, ecliptic and galactic systems, and they differ by rotations of the sphere.

## Explanations

### L1 human

On the Earth a place is given by its latitude and longitude.
A celestial coordinate system does the same for directions in the sky.
One picks a great circle that plays the role of the equator and a point on it that plays the role of Greenwich.
For example, the equatorial system uses the projection of the Earth's equator, so a star straight above the North Pole has latitude $+90^\circ$.
