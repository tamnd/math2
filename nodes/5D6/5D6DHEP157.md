## Definition

Let $I \subseteq \R$ be an open interval and let $R \colon I \to SO(3)$ be a differentiable curve in the [[special orthogonal group|0CGYNDCWRR]].
The matrix $R(t)$ gives the orientation at time $t$ of a rotating body or frame relative to a fixed frame.
For each $t \in I$ the matrix $\dot R(t) R(t)^T$ is [[skew-symmetric|0PBY4E9GQF]].
The *angular velocity* at time $t$ is the unique vector $\omega(t) \in \R^3$ such that
$$\dot R(t) R(t)^T y = \omega(t) \times y \quad \text{for all } y \in \R^3,$$
where $\times$ is the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]].
Its length $\abs{\omega(t)}$ is the *angular speed*.

## Remarks

The matrix is skew-symmetric because differentiating $R R^T = I$ gives $\dot R R^T + (\dot R R^T)^T = 0$, and every skew-symmetric $3 \times 3$ matrix acts as $y \mapsto \omega \times y$ for exactly one vector $\omega$.
Example: rotation about the $z$-axis by the angle $\theta(t)$ gives $\dot R R^T y = \dot\theta\, (-y_2, y_1, 0)$, so $\omega = (0, 0, \dot\theta)$.
Non-example: the vector $(0, 0, \theta(t))$ is not the angular velocity; the angle is a position, and only its rate $\dot\theta$ enters.
A point fixed in the body at $R(t) X$ has velocity $\omega(t) \times R(t) X$.
For a single particle at position $r \ne 0$ with velocity $v$, its angular velocity about the origin is often defined as $r \times v / \abs{r}^2$.
