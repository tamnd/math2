## Proof

We prove the contrapositive: if $\varphi \notin \mathsf{ML}$ and $\psi \notin \mathsf{ML}$, then $\varphi \vee \psi \notin \mathsf{ML}$.

1. Since $\varphi \notin \mathsf{ML}$, there are $m \geq 1$, a valuation $V_1$ on $M_m$ and a world $I_1$ with $I_1 \nVdash \varphi$, by the definition of [[Medvedev's logic|R2FD01J3PC]].
2. In the same way there are $n \geq 1$, a valuation $V_2$ on $M_n$ and a world $I_2$ with $I_2 \nVdash \psi$.
3. Let $A = \set{1, \dots, m}$ and $B = \set{m + 1, \dots, m + n}$, so $A \cup B = \set{1, \dots, m + n}$ and $A \cap B = \emptyset$. Let $X = A \cup B$.
4. Identify $M_n$ with $M(B)$ by the shift $i \mapsto m + i$. Let $V_2'$ and $I_2'$ be the shifted valuation and world.
5. $M(A)$ is an up-set of $M(X)$: a world above $I \subseteq A$ is a nonempty $J \subseteq I$, so $J \subseteq A$. The same holds for $M(B)$.
6. $M(A)$ and $M(B)$ are disjoint, since a nonempty set cannot be a subset of both $A$ and $B$.
7. Define $V(p) = V_1(p) \cup V_2'(p)$ for each atom $p$. Each part is an up-set of $M(X)$, by step 5. So $V(p)$ is an up-set, and $V$ is a valuation on $M(X)$ in the sense of [[Kripke semantics|5GTQ69W47T]].
8. By step 6, $V(p) \cap M(A) = V_1(p)$ and $V(p) \cap M(B) = V_2'(p)$.
9. Claim: for every formula $\chi$ and every $I \in M(A)$, $I \Vdash_V \chi$ iff $I \Vdash_{V_1} \chi$. We prove it by induction on $\chi$.
10. Atoms: by step 8. The cases $\bot$, $\wedge$ and $\vee$: the forcing clauses at $I$ refer only to $I$.
11. Implication: the clause for $\chi_1 \to \chi_2$ at $I$ quantifies over the worlds $J \geq I$. By step 5 these lie in $M(A)$, where the induction hypothesis applies to $\chi_1$ and $\chi_2$.
12. In the same way, forcing under $V$ at worlds of $M(B)$ agrees with forcing under $V_2'$.
13. By steps 1 and 9, $I_1 \nVdash_V \varphi$. By steps 2, 4 and 12, $I_2' \nVdash_V \psi$.
14. The root $X$ lies below every world of $M(X)$, in particular below $I_1$ and $I_2'$.
15. If $X \Vdash_V \varphi$, then $I_1 \Vdash_V \varphi$ by persistence, contradicting step 13. So $X \nVdash_V \varphi$, and in the same way $X \nVdash_V \psi$.
16. By the clause for disjunction, $X \nVdash_V \varphi \vee \psi$.
17. $M(X)$ is isomorphic to $M_{m + n}$, so $\varphi \vee \psi$ is not valid on $M_{m + n}$, and $\varphi \vee \psi \notin \mathsf{ML}$.

This proves the contrapositive, and hence the theorem.

## Idea

Two Medvedev frames fit side by side in a larger one: the subsets of $A$ and the subsets of $B$ are disjoint generated subframes of the subsets of $A \cup B$.
Countermodels for the two disjuncts can therefore be combined on one frame, whose root refutes both.

## Variants

For $\mathsf{IPC}$ the same argument adds a new root below the disjoint union of two models.
Here no new world is needed, because $A \cup B$ is already a root below both parts.
The Kleene slash and cut elimination prove the property from an axiom system.
No recursive axiom system for $\mathsf{ML}$ is known, so the semantic proof is the one used.
