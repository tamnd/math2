## Summary

Fourier optics studies optical systems with the [[Fourier transform|DPVM34ADZV]] in the transverse plane.
Free propagation of light acts on the field as a linear, shift-invariant system, whose transfer function is given by the [[angular spectrum method|WBPGRQ6RJE]].
A thin lens multiplies the field by a quadratic phase.
As a result, the field in its back focal plane is the Fourier transform of the field in its front focal plane.
This makes it possible to filter images optically.
It also describes imaging as a [[convolution|2Z29JN3VES]] with a point spread function, and resolution in terms of spatial frequencies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A picture can be decomposed into stripes of various fineness and direction, its spatial frequencies.
A lens does this decomposition physically: in its focal plane each spot of light corresponds to one stripe pattern of the picture.
Blocking some spots and recombining the light with a second lens removes those stripes from the picture, which is how optical filtering works.
The finest stripes a lens can pass set the smallest details it can show.
