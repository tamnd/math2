## Summary

Local thermodynamic equilibrium is the assumption that a system out of equilibrium is, near each point, in an equilibrium state.
Temperature, pressure and chemical potentials then become fields that vary in space and time.
The equilibrium relations, such as the Gibbs relation between entropy and energy, hold at each point.
This assumption is the starting point of classical [[non-equilibrium thermodynamics|WC6CK8PXPX]] and of continuum mechanics with heat.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A cup of tea cooling on a table is not in equilibrium as a whole: the surface is cooler than the middle.
Yet each tiny drop of tea has a well-defined temperature.
Local thermodynamic equilibrium says that each small piece behaves like a piece of an equilibrium system at its own temperature.
This works when the pieces can be small compared with the scale on which temperature changes, yet large enough to hold very many molecules.
