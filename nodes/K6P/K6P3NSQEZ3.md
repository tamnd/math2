## Summary

Type theory and constructive mathematics study the formal systems of functions and types, and the mathematics that does not use the law of excluded middle.
A type theory is a formal system in which every term has a type, and its rules act both as a logic and as a programming language.
Type theories are the logical base of many proof assistants, and a constructive proof of an existence statement gives a method to compute the object.
Brouwer started intuitionism early in the 20th century, Church introduced the lambda calculus in the 1930s and simple type theory in 1940, and Martin-Löf developed dependent type theory in the 1970s.
This topic collects the type theory and constructive mathematics pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Mathematical logic, computability theory, category theory, and mathematical software and formalization have their own topics.
