## Summary

Plate tectonics is the theory that the outer shell of the Earth, the lithosphere, is broken into a few rigid plates.
The plates move over the weaker mantle below.
Almost all deformation, earthquakes and volcanism are concentrated at the boundaries between plates.
Because the plates are rigid pieces of a spherical shell, the relative motion of two plates is a rotation about an [[Euler pole|6SGQDKV15M]].
The theory explains the distribution of earthquakes, mountain belts, ocean ridges and trenches, and the drift of continents.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of the surface of the Earth as a cracked eggshell whose pieces slide over a soft layer beneath.
The pieces move a few centimetres per year, about as fast as fingernails grow.
Where two pieces pull apart, hot rock rises and makes new ocean floor, as along the middle of the Atlantic.
Where they collide, one dives under the other or they crumple into mountains, as in the Himalaya.
Where they slide past each other, there are long faults such as the San Andreas fault.
