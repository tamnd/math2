## Definition

Let $A$ be a unital, separable, simple, [[nuclear|D2M4VXJ7PA]] [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]].
Let $T(A)$ be its set of tracial states, a compact convex set in the weak* topology.
The *Elliott invariant* of $A$ is
$$\operatorname{Ell}(A) = \big(K_0(A), K_0(A)^+, [1_A], K_1(A), T(A), r_A\big).$$
Here $K_0(A)$ is [[K0|BP86XQRZD2]], with positive cone $K_0(A)^+$ given by classes of projections, and $[1_A]$ is the class of the unit.
The group $K_1(A)$ is [[K1|7NTVTHC7CG]].
The map $r_A \colon T(A) \times K_0(A) \to \R$ is the pairing $r_A(\tau, [p] - [q]) = \tau(p) - \tau(q)$, with $\tau$ extended to matrices by the trace.
An isomorphism of Elliott invariants has three parts, compatible with the pairings.
They are an isomorphism of ordered groups preserving the unit class, an isomorphism of the $K_1$-groups, and an affine homeomorphism of the trace spaces.
A class $\mathcal{C}$ of such algebras is *classified* if, for all $A, B \in \mathcal{C}$, $A \cong B$ if and only if $\operatorname{Ell}(A) \cong \operatorname{Ell}(B)$.
The *Elliott classification program* is the problem of finding classified classes and of determining which invariants occur in them.

## Remarks

Example: the class of unital simple AF algebras is classified by $\operatorname{Ell}$, and there $K_1 = 0$ and the traces are determined by the ordered $K_0$-group, so this is Elliott's AF theorem.
Example: for unital Kirchberg algebras in the UCT class, $T(A)$ is empty and $(K_0(A), [1_A], K_1(A))$ classifies, by the Kirchberg–Phillips theorem.
Non-example: Toms constructed unital simple separable nuclear C*-algebras with the same Elliott invariant that are not isomorphic, so the class of all such algebras is not classified by $\operatorname{Ell}$.
For non-unital algebras one uses a variant of the invariant with traces on the Pedersen ideal or with a scale.
