## Summary

The relativistic Lagrangian of a free particle of mass $m$ is $L = -mc^2\sqrt{1 - \abs{\vec v}^2/c^2}$, where $\vec v$ is its velocity and $c$ the speed of light.
Its action is $-mc^2$ times the [[proper time|YG74WHC3S9]] along the world line, a quantity that all inertial observers agree on.
Its [[Euler–Lagrange equations|PS0VV149ET]] give the relativistic momentum $\gamma m \vec v$ and uniform straight motion.
It is the starting point of [[Lagrangian mechanics|47JMCBM3VJ]] in special relativity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In Newtonian mechanics the Lagrangian of a free particle is its kinetic energy $\frac12 m \abs{\vec v}^2$.
In relativity the natural choice is minus the rest energy times the rate at which the particle's own clock ticks.
So a free particle moves in the way that makes its own elapsed time stationary.
For slow motion, $L \approx -mc^2 + \frac12 m \abs{\vec v}^2$, which is the Newtonian Lagrangian up to a constant that does not change the motion.
