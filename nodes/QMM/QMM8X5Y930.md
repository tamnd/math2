## Summary

The tangent space $T_pM$ of a [[smooth manifold|F45VQ47GPV]] $M$ at a point $p$ is a real vector space, see the [[definition|G4SZMHM8AA]].
Its elements are the directions in which one can move away from $p$ inside $M$.
It has the same dimension as $M$ and is the best linear approximation of $M$ near $p$.
The [[differential of a smooth map|6P9PXQDSEN]] is a linear map between tangent spaces, and the [[tangent bundle|WY6K3P4YCG]] collects all tangent spaces into one manifold.
Vector fields, differential forms and Riemannian metrics are all built from tangent spaces.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine standing at a point on a smooth surface, such as a sphere.
The tangent space there is the flat plane that just touches the surface at that point.
Its elements are arrows, the possible velocities of a curve that passes through the point.
For the unit sphere in $\R^3$ at the north pole $(0, 0, 1)$, the tangent space is the horizontal plane of vectors $(a, b, 0)$.
For a curved space with no surrounding space, the same idea is made precise by derivatives of functions or by velocities of curves.
