## Proof

We turn $V$ into a module over $F[x]$, apply the structure theorem for modules over a principal ideal domain, and translate the cyclic summands back into cyclic subspaces.

*Existence.*

1. Let $V_T$ be the [[$F[x]$-module of $T$|KGVTFBZX59]], in which $f \cdot v = f(T) v$.
   By the [[dictionary between modules and operators|R5X8SNHBGG]], $V_T$ is finitely generated and torsion.
2. The ring $F[x]$ is a [[Euclidean domain|0CHS7RBD39]], so it is a [[principal ideal domain|0QKVE1GD9A]].
3. The invariant factor form of the [[structure theorem over a principal ideal domain|TXMV0YDACG]] gives $s \ge 0$, nonzero non-units $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$ of $F[x]$, and a module isomorphism
$$\psi \colon F[x]^s \oplus F[x]/(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus F[x]/(d_r) \to V_T.$$
4. The units of $F[x]$ are the nonzero constants, so each $d_i$ is nonconstant.
   Dividing $d_i$ by its leading coefficient changes neither the ideal $(d_i)$ nor the divisibility, so we may take each $d_i$ monic.
5. We show that $s = 0$.
   The elements $1, x, x^2, \dots$ of $F[x]$ are [[linearly independent|0CRMGPAH83]] over $F$, so $F[x]^s$ has infinite dimension over $F$ when $s \ge 1$.
   But $\psi$ is $F$-linear and injective into the finite-dimensional space $V$, so $s \ge 1$ is impossible.
6. Let $u_i$ be the element of the direct sum with the class of $1$ in the summand $F[x]/(d_i)$ and $0$ elsewhere, and put $v_i = \psi(u_i)$.
7. The submodule generated by $u_i$ is the $i$-th summand.
   Since $\psi$ is an isomorphism, $V$ is the [[internal direct sum|0XYYT5KNTX]] of the submodules $F[x] v_i$.
8. By the definition of the module structure,
$$F[x] v_i = \{ f(T) v_i : f \in F[x] \} = Z(v_i; T).$$
   With step 7, this proves property 1.
9. For $f \in F[x]$, we have $f(T) v_i = 0$ if and only if $f \cdot u_i = 0$, that is, if and only if $f \in (d_i)$.
   So the annihilator ideal of $v_i$ is $(d_i)$, and the $T$-annihilator of $v_i$ is the monic generator $d_i$.
   It is nonconstant, so $v_i \ne 0$, and this proves property 2.

*The two polynomials.*

10. Let $f \in F[x]$.
    The operator $f(T)$ commutes with every $g(T)$, so $f(T)$ kills $Z(v_i)$ exactly when $f(T) v_i = 0$.
    By step 7, $f(T) = 0$ if and only if $f(T) v_i = 0$ for every $i$.
11. By step 9 this holds if and only if $d_i \mid f$ for every $i$.
    Since each $d_i$ divides $d_r$, this holds if and only if $d_r \mid f$, so $m_T = d_r$.
12. By the [[lemma on cyclic subspaces|5VYBJYPYMW]], the restriction $T|_{Z(v_i)}$ has the companion matrix $C(d_i)$ in the basis $v_i, T v_i, \dots, T^{\deg d_i - 1} v_i$.
13. The union of these bases is a basis of $V$ by step 7.
    Each summand is $T$-invariant, so in this basis $T$ has the block diagonal matrix $C(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus C(d_r)$.
14. By the [[determinant of a block triangular matrix|0J9TVTNB4K]] and induction on $r$, $\chi_T = \prod_i \det(xI - C(d_i))$.
    By the [[theorem on companion matrices|XYR7GPH20K]], each factor is $d_i$, so $\chi_T = d_1 \cdots d_r$.

*Uniqueness.*

15. Let $V = Z(w_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus Z(w_t)$ with nonconstant monic annihilators $e_1 \mid \cdots \mid e_t$.
16. For each $j$, the map $F[x] \to Z(w_j)$, $f \mapsto f(T) w_j$, is a surjective module map with kernel $(e_j)$.
    So $Z(w_j) \cong F[x]/(e_j)$, and
$$V_T \cong F[x]/(e_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus F[x]/(e_t).$$
17. The uniqueness part of the structure theorem gives $t = r$ and $(e_j) = (d_j)$ for each $j$.
    Two monic generators of the same ideal are equal, so $e_j = d_j$, and the proof is complete.

## Idea

The operator $T$ makes $V$ a finitely generated torsion module over the principal ideal domain $F[x]$.
The structure theorem splits such a module into cyclic modules $F[x]/(d_i)$ with $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$.
A cyclic submodule of $V_T$ is the same thing as a cyclic subspace of $T$, so the splitting is already the cyclic decomposition.
The minimal polynomial is the largest annihilator, and the characteristic polynomial is the product of the annihilators, because each summand carries a companion block.

## Variants

Hoffman and Kunze give a proof inside linear algebra, with no modules.
They call an invariant subspace $W$ $T$-admissible when each vector $f(T) v$ that lies in $W$ equals $f(T) w$ for some $w \in W$.
Then they build the vectors $v_i$ one at a time.
Each time they choose a vector whose conductor ideal $\{ f : f(T) v \in W \}$ into the sum $W$ of the earlier summands has a generator of the largest possible degree.
A third route computes the Smith normal form of $xI - A$ by row and column operations over $F[x]$, as in the [[theorem on the characteristic matrix|92VZF0QRM8]].
