## Summary

The quasi-geostrophic equations are a simplified model of large-scale flow in the atmosphere and the ocean.
They are obtained as the leading-order approximation for small [[Rossby number|SCYK4FMY59]], when the flow is close to [[geostrophic balance|CZ1S1P465Y]].
The whole dynamics reduces to the advection of a single scalar, the quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity, by a velocity derived from a stream function.
They filter out fast sound and gravity waves while keeping [[Rossby waves|6N1MQMA9TM]] and baroclinic instability.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The full equations of the atmosphere describe everything from sound waves to storms, which makes them hard to study.
For large, slow weather systems, the wind is almost exactly balanced by the pressure pattern.
The quasi-geostrophic equations keep only the small departure from that balance that makes the weather change.
They say that one quantity, a kind of spin, is carried along with the wind, and all of the flow can be recovered from it.
