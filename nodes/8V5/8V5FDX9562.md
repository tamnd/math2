## Example

Statement: the number $\log_2 3$ is [[irrational|080T2TXVQV]].
Here $\log_2 3$ is the real number $x$ with $2^x = 3$, about $1.585$.
We give a [[proof by contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]].

1. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\log_2 3$ is rational, so $\log_2 3 = a / b$ for integers $a$ and $b$ with $b \ne 0$.
2. Since $2^1 = 2 < 3$ and $t \mapsto 2^t$ is increasing, $\log_2 3 > 1 > 0$.
   So $a$ and $b$ have the same sign, and after changing both signs if necessary we may take $a > 0$ and $b > 0$.
3. By the definition of the logarithm, $2^{a/b} = 3$.
4. Raise both sides to the power $b$:
   $$2^a = 3^b.$$
5. Since $a \ge 1$, the left side $2^a$ is even.
6. The right side $3^b$ is a product of odd factors, so it is odd.
7. No integer is both even and odd, so this is a contradiction.
8. So the assumption in step 1 is false, and $\log_2 3$ is irrational.

## Remarks

The assumption in step 1 hands us the integers $a$ and $b$, and the whole proof works with them.
Without it there would be nothing to compute with, because "$\log_2 3$ is not a fraction" is a negative statement.
Since the statement is a negation, the proof is a refutation: it assumes "rational" and refutes it, with no appeal to the law of excluded middle.

The same proof shows that $\log_m n$ is irrational whenever $m, n \ge 2$ and some prime divides one of $m$ and $n$ but not the other.
Then $m^a = n^b$ with $a, b \ge 1$ is impossible, by unique factorization.
The condition is needed.
The number $\log_4 8$ is $\frac{3}{2}$, because $4^{3/2} = 8$, and here $4$ and $8$ share their only prime factor $2$, with $4^3 = 8^2 = 64$.

The number $\log_2 3$ returns in the example of [[irrational a and b with a^b rational|H7X4FMAHC8]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The question is: to what power must we raise $2$ to get $3$?
The answer is between $1$ and $2$, because $2^1 = 2$ and $2^2 = 4$.
Suppose the answer were a fraction $a / b$.
Then $2$ multiplied by itself $a$ times would equal $3$ multiplied by itself $b$ times.
The first product is even and the second is odd, so they cannot be equal, and the answer is not a fraction.

### L2 human

The same method shows that $\log_{10} 2$, the number behind counting the digits of powers of $2$, is irrational.
It is positive, because $10^0 = 1 < 2$, so suppose $\log_{10} 2 = a / b$ with $a, b > 0$.
Then $10^{a/b} = 2$, and raising to the power $b$ gives
$$10^a = 2^b.$$
The left side $2^a 5^a$ is divisible by $5$, since $a \ge 1$, and the right side has no prime factor except $2$, so the two cannot be equal.
The typical use is the one shown: an equation between powers of different primes has no solution in positive exponents.
Watch out: the main proof needs $a, b > 0$, because with $a = 0$ the left side $2^0 = 1$ is odd and the parity argument fails.
