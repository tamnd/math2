## Summary

Interference is what happens when two or more waves overlap: the fields add, so the intensity is not the sum of the separate intensities.
Where the waves arrive in phase they reinforce each other, and where they arrive in opposite phase they cancel.
The result is a pattern of bright and dark fringes that depends on the phase difference.
Interference underlies interferometers, thin-film colours, [[holography|A97MM8JNKF]] and much of wave optics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Drop two stones into a pond and watch the ripples cross.
In some places two crests meet and the water rises twice as high; in others a crest meets a trough and the water stays flat.
Light does the same, because light is a wave.
Two beams of the same colour that overlap on a screen give light and dark stripes.
The spacing of the stripes tells how the path lengths of the two beams differ.
