## Statement

Let $S^2$ be the set of unit vectors in $\R^3$.
There is no [[probability space|059TJN0NZ1]] $(\Lambda, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ with measurable functions $A(a, \cdot), B(b, \cdot) \colon \Lambda \to \set{-1, 1}$ for $a, b \in S^2$ such that
$$\int_\Lambda A(a, \lambda)\, B(b, \lambda)\, d\mu(\lambda) = -a \cdot b \quad \text{for all } a, b \in S^2.$$
Consider two spin-$\tfrac12$ particles in the singlet state.
The right side is the quantum [[expectation value|JNK7GJ3AKE]] of the product of their spin components along $a$ and $b$.
So no local [[hidden-variable theory|QRW6NE1VY7]] reproduces the quantum predictions for this [[entangled|CHNXT8CCK7]] state.

## Remarks

Suppose such a model exists.
Take $a_0, a_1, b_0, b_1$ in one plane at the angles $0$, $\pi/2$, $\pi/4$ and $-\pi/4$, so that $a_i \cdot b_j$ is the cosine of the angle between them.
With $A_i = A(a_i, \cdot)$ and $B_j = B(b_j, \cdot)$, the model gives $E(A_0B_0) + E(A_0B_1) + E(A_1B_0) - E(A_1B_1) = -4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} = -2\sqrt{2}$.
This contradicts the [[CHSH inequality|Y7W23FTBK5]], which bounds the absolute value of this sum by $2$.
Locality enters as the form of the functions: $A$ depends on $a$ and $\lambda$ only, and $B$ on $b$ and $\lambda$ only.
Measurement independence enters as the use of a single measure $\mu$ for all choices of $a$ and $b$.
If $A$ may depend on $b$ as well, the correlations $-a \cdot b$ can be reproduced, as in de Broglie–Bohm theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Quantum mechanics predicts correlations between distant entangled particles that are stronger than any correlations coming from shared instructions fixed in advance.
Bell's theorem makes this precise: no model in which each particle carries its own answers, and is not influenced by the distant measurement choice, gives the quantum predictions.
Experiments confirm the quantum predictions, so nature does not follow such local models.

### L2 human

John Bell proved a version of the theorem in 1964, in response to the EPR argument.
The proof above uses the CHSH form, which needs only bounded outcomes and four measurement settings.
The theorem does not say that signals travel faster than light; the no-communication theorem forbids that within quantum mechanics.
It is a statement about all possible theories with these locality assumptions, not about quantum mechanics alone, which is why experiments can test it.
Related results without inequalities include the GHZ argument and Hardy's paradox.
