## Summary

An ideal gas is a model of a gas whose particles do not interact except by perfectly elastic collisions and occupy no volume.
Its [[equation of state|YPT3SJSS5T]] is $pV = nRT$, and its internal energy depends only on the temperature.
Real gases behave like an ideal gas at low pressure and high temperature.
The model is the starting point of thermodynamics and of the kinetic theory of gases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a box of tiny balls that fly around, bounce off the walls and each other, and never attract or repel.
That is an ideal gas.
The pressure comes from the balls hitting the walls, and the temperature measures how fast they move.
Air in a room is close to this picture: one mole of it at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and atmospheric pressure fills about $22.4$ litres, as the model predicts.
