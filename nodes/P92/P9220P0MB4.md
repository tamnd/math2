## Definition

Let a [[fluid flow|F2JRWJNYN4]] in a region $\Omega \subseteq \R^3$ be described by a density $\rho(x, t) > 0$, a velocity $\mathbf{u}(x, t)$ and a pressure $p(x, t)$.
The flow is *compressible* when the density is not constant along particle paths, that is, $\frac{D\rho}{Dt} = \partial_t \rho + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla\rho \neq 0$ somewhere.
By conservation of mass, $\partial_t \rho + \nabla \cdot (\rho\mathbf{u}) = 0$, this is the same as $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} \neq 0$ somewhere.
In *compressible flow theory* the density is an unknown.
An equation of state links it to the pressure and the temperature $T$.
An example is the ideal gas law $p = \rho R T$, with $R$ the specific gas constant.

## Remarks

Example: in steady isentropic flow of air, with ratio of specific heats $\gamma = 1.4$, the density at [[Mach number|GFHJ858AN1]] $M$ is $\rho = \rho_0 (1 + 0.2 M^2)^{-2.5}$, where $\rho_0$ is the density at rest.
At $M = 0.3$ this is about $0.956\,\rho_0$, a change of about $4$ percent, so the flow is compressible, though only mildly.
Non-example: [[incompressible flow|2JZ0C15A9C]], where $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$, for instance the flow of water in a pipe at ordinary pressures.
Inviscid compressible flow is governed by the compressible [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]], which form a hyperbolic system of conservation laws.
The size of the density changes is set by the Mach number $M = \abs{\mathbf{u}} / c$, with $c$ the [[speed of sound|SN4J6KP03C]]: for $M \to 0$ the relative density changes are of order $M^2$.
