## Summary

The Plateau–Rayleigh instability is the break-up of a long cylinder of liquid, such as a thin jet, into drops under surface tension.
Axisymmetric bulges whose wavelength exceeds the circumference of the cylinder lower the surface area and therefore grow.
Plateau found the critical wavelength from surface energy, and Rayleigh computed the growth rates for an inviscid jet.
It explains drop formation in jets, ink-jet printing and the beading of thin liquid threads.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Open a tap a little and watch the thin stream of water: a short distance below the tap it breaks into drops.
Surface tension pulls the liquid into the shape with the least surface area for its volume.
A long cylinder is not that shape.
A cylinder that narrows and widens along its length has less surface than a straight one of the same volume, as soon as the bulges are longer than its circumference.
So small bulges grow, the narrow necks pinch off, and the stream becomes a row of drops.
