## Proof

We show that $xI - A$ is a presentation matrix of the module of $A$, so that equivalent characteristic matrices give isomorphic modules, and then we read the invariant factors and the determinantal divisors off the Smith normal form.
Write $R = F[x]$ and $M_A = F^n_A$ for the [[module of $A$|KGVTFBZX59]].

*Part 1.*

1. (a) implies (b).
   If $B = P^{-1} A P$ with $P \in GL_n(F)$, then $xI - B = P^{-1} (xI - A) P$.
   The constant matrices $P$ and $P^{-1}$ are invertible over $R$, so $xI - A$ and $xI - B$ are equivalent.
2. (b) is equivalent to (c).
   By the [[Smith normal form theorem|MV28Z5N9XX]], each matrix over the principal ideal domain $R$ is equivalent to exactly one Smith normal form with monic entries.
   Equivalence is an equivalence relation, so two matrices are equivalent exactly when they have the same Smith normal form.
3. We prove a presentation lemma.
   Define $\pi \colon R^n \to M_A$ by $\pi(\sum_j f_j e_j) = \sum_j f_j(A) e_j$.
   It is $R$-linear by the rules of the [[evaluation map|0FCCDXH97W]], and it is surjective because it maps the constant vectors onto $F^n$.
4. Let $K = (xI - A) R^n$, the submodule spanned by the columns $x e_j - \sum_i a_{ij} e_i$ of $xI - A$.
   Then $\pi(x e_j - \sum_i a_{ij} e_i) = A e_j - \sum_i a_{ij} e_i = 0$, so $K \subseteq \ker \pi$.
5. Modulo $K$ we have $x e_j \equiv \sum_i a_{ij} e_i$, a constant vector.
   By induction on $k$, each $x^k e_j$ is congruent modulo $K$ to a constant vector.
   So $R^n / K$ is spanned over $F$ by the classes of $e_1, \dots, e_n$, and $\dim_F R^n / K \le n$.
6. The map $\pi$ induces a surjective $F$-linear map $R^n / K \to F^n$.
   Its target has [[dimension|03GSZ8RTZJ]] $n$ and its source has dimension at most $n$, so the map is a bijection.
   So $\ker \pi = K$, and $M_A \cong R^n / (xI - A) R^n$ as $R$-modules.
7. (b) implies (a).
   Let $U (xI - A) V = xI - B$ with $U, V$ invertible over $R$.
   Since $V$ is invertible, $V R^n = R^n$, so $(xI - A) V R^n = (xI - A) R^n$.
   So multiplication by $U$ maps $(xI - A) R^n$ onto $U (xI - A) V R^n = (xI - B) R^n$.
8. Multiplication by $U$ is an $R$-module automorphism of $R^n$.
   By step 7 it induces an isomorphism $R^n / (xI - A) R^n \cong R^n / (xI - B) R^n$.
9. By step 6 this means $M_A \cong M_B$.
   By the [[dictionary between modules and operators|R5X8SNHBGG]], isomorphic modules come from similar matrices, so $A$ and $B$ are similar, and part 1 is proved.

*Part 2.*

10. Let $S = U (xI - A) V = \operatorname{diag}(s_1, \dots, s_n)$ be the Smith normal form, with each $s_i$ monic or zero and $s_i \mid s_{i+1}$.
11. Up to a nonzero constant factor, $\det S = \det U \det(xI - A) \det V$, and $\det(xI - A) = \chi_A$ is monic and nonzero.
    So every $s_i$ is nonzero, and $s_1 \cdots s_n = \chi_A$, since both sides are monic.
12. Steps 7 and 8, with $S$ in place of $xI - B$, give
$$M_A \cong R^n / S R^n = R/(s_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(s_n).$$
    A summand with $s_i = 1$ is zero.
13. So $M_A \cong \bigoplus_{s_i \ne 1} R/(s_i)$, with nonconstant monic $s_i$ that divide each other.
    By the uniqueness part of the [[structure theorem|TXMV0YDACG]] and the [[cyclic decomposition theorem|R3Z20J19BR]], these $s_i$ are the invariant factors $d_1, \dots, d_r$, and the other $n - r$ entries are $1$.

*Part 3.*

14. For a matrix $M$ over $R$, let $D_k(M)$ be the monic [[gcd|0C8WYW4KYQ]] of its $k \times k$ minors.
    By the [[Cauchy-Binet formula|08WE2W7DYS]], each $k \times k$ minor of $UM$ or of $MV$ is an $R$-linear combination of $k \times k$ minors of $M$.
    So $D_k(M)$ divides $D_k(UMV)$.
15. Applying step 14 to $U^{-1}$ and $V^{-1}$ gives the opposite divisibility.
    Both gcds are monic, so $D_k(xI - A) = D_k(S)$.
16. For the diagonal matrix $S$ with $s_1 \mid \cdots \mid s_n$, a nonzero $k \times k$ minor is a product $s_{i_1} \cdots s_{i_k}$ with distinct indices.
    Each such product is a multiple of $s_1 \cdots s_k$, which is itself a minor, so $D_k(S) = s_1 \cdots s_k$.
17. Hence $s_k = D_k / D_{k-1}$ for $1 \le k \le n$, with $D_0 = 1$.
    The only $n \times n$ minor is the [[determinant|0C3CFHDN6R]], so $D_n = \det(xI - A) = \chi_A$, and part 3 is proved.

## Idea

The characteristic matrix $xI - A$ presents the module $F^n_A$: its columns are exactly the relations $x e_j = A e_j$ that turn the free module $F[x]^n$ into $F^n_A$.
Equivalent presentation matrices present isomorphic modules, and isomorphic modules come from similar matrices.
The Smith normal form then splits the module into cyclic pieces, so its entries are the invariant factors.
Gcds of minors do not change under invertible row and column operations, and that gives the formula $D_k / D_{k-1}$.

## Variants

Hoffman and Kunze prove part 1 by elementary operations on $xI - A$, in their section on the computation of invariant factors, with no module language.
There is also a classical argument with matrix polynomials for (b) implies (a).
One rewrites the relation as $U (xI - A) = (xI - B) V^{-1}$, divides the polynomial matrices in it by $xI - A$ and by $xI - B$ with remainder, and shows that a constant remainder is an invertible $P$ with $PA = BP$.
