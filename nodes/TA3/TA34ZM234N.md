## Definition

Let $A$ be a [[nonassociative algebra|60W3THKGA7]] over a field $k$, with product $(x, y) \mapsto xy$.
Let $[x, y] = xy - yx$ be the [[commutator|T2HH02QC2M]] of $x$ and $y$.
The algebra $A$ is *Lie-admissible* if the vector space $A$ with the bracket $[\cdot, \cdot]$ is a [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]].
Since $[x, x] = 0$ always holds, this means that
$$[x, [y, z]] + [y, [z, x]] + [z, [x, y]] = 0$$
for all $x, y, z \in A$.
The resulting Lie algebra is written $A^-$.

## Remarks

Example: every associative algebra is Lie-admissible.
Expanding $[x, [y, z]] + [y, [z, x]] + [z, [x, y]]$ gives twelve products of three factors, and by associativity they cancel in pairs.

Non-example: let $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$, and let $A$ have basis $x, y, z$ with $xy = y$, $yx = -y$, $yz = x$, $zy = -x$, and all other products of basis vectors zero.
The product is anticommutative, so $[u, v] = 2uv$.
Then $[x, [y, z]] + [y, [z, x]] + [z, [x, y]] = 4\big(x x + y \cdot 0 + z y\big) = -4x \neq 0$, so $A$ is not Lie-admissible.

An anticommutative algebra is Lie-admissible exactly when it is a Lie algebra, in characteristic not $2$.
