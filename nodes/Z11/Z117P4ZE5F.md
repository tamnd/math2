## Proof

We argue by [[contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]].

1. Let $A$ be an [[infinite set|01MKW4R283]], let $B$ be a [[finite set|0BBTH3YSZA]], and let $f : A \to B$ be a function.
2. For $b \in B$, write $A_b = f^{-1}(b) = \set{a \in A : f(a) = b}$.
3. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $A_b$ is finite for every $b \in B$.
4. The sets $A_b$ for $b \in B$ are pairwise disjoint. Indeed, if $a \in A_b \cap A_{b'}$, then $b = f(a) = b'$.
5. Their union is $A$, because each $a \in A$ lies in $A_{f(a)}$.
6. Since $B$ is finite, steps 3 to 5 show that $A$ is the union of the $|B|$ pairwise disjoint finite sets $A_b$. If $B$ is empty, this union is empty.
7. By the [[rule of sum|0EE2V3CHFK]], a union of finitely many pairwise disjoint finite sets is finite. So $A$ is finite.
8. This contradicts step 1, since an infinite set is by definition not finite.
9. Hence some $b \in B$ has an infinite fibre $f^{-1}(b)$.

## Idea

The fibres of $f$ cover $A$, and there are only finitely many of them.
A finite union of finite sets is finite, so one fibre must be infinite.
The hypothesis that $B$ is finite is used in step 6, and the hypothesis that $A$ is infinite is used in step 8.

## Variants

- A proof by [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $|B|$: the set $B$ is not empty, because $A$ is not empty, and for $|B| = 1$ the only fibre is $A$. For the step, remove one element $b$ of $B$; if $f^{-1}(b)$ is finite, then $A \setminus f^{-1}(b)$ is infinite and maps to the smaller set $B \setminus \set{b}$.
- A proof by [[contrapositive|0BB5TPGQ1N]]: if every fibre is finite, then $A$ is a finite union of finite sets, so $A$ is finite.
