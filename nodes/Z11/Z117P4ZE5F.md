## Proof

We suppose that every fiber is finite and show that $A$ would then be a finite union of finite sets, so finite, which is a [[contradiction|0J0XBKG64S]].

1. Let $A$ be an [[infinite set|01MKW4R283]], let $B$ be a [[finite set|0BBTH3YSZA]], and let $f : A \to B$ be a function.
2. For $b \in B$, write $A_b = f^{-1}(b) = \{ a \in A : f(a) = b \}$.
3. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $A_b$ is finite for every $b \in B$.
4. The sets $A_b$ for $b \in B$ are pairwise disjoint, because $a \in A_b \cap A_{b'}$ gives $b = f(a) = b'$.
5. Their union is $A$, because each $a \in A$ lies in $A_{f(a)}$.
6. Since $B$ is finite, steps 3 to 5 show that $A$ is the union of the $|B|$ pairwise disjoint finite sets $A_b$.
   If $B$ is empty, this union is empty.
7. By the [[rule of sum|0EE2V3CHFK]], a union of finitely many pairwise disjoint finite sets is finite, with
   $$|A| = \sum_{b \in B} |A_b|.$$
   So $A$ is finite.
8. This contradicts step 1, since an infinite set is by definition not finite.
9. Hence some $b \in B$ has an infinite fiber $f^{-1}(b)$.

## Idea

The fibers of $f$ cover $A$, and there are only finitely many of them.
If each fiber were finite, we could add up their sizes and get a finite size for $A$.
So one fiber must be infinite.
The finiteness of $B$ is used in step 6, and the infiniteness of $A$ is used in step 8.

## Variants

A proof by [[induction|0ASJVEFA9J]] on $|B|$ removes one box at a time.
The set $B$ is not empty, because $A$ is not empty and $f$ maps $A$ into $B$, and for $|B| = 1$ the only fiber is $A$.
For the step, fix $b \in B$: if $f^{-1}(b)$ is finite, then $A \setminus f^{-1}(b)$ is infinite and maps into the smaller set $B \setminus \{b\}$, so the induction hypothesis applies.

A proof by [[contrapositive|0BB5TPGQ1N]] says the same thing forward: if every fiber is finite, then $A$ is a finite union of finite sets, so $A$ is finite.
