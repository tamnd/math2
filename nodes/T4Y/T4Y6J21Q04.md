## Summary

A sequential equilibrium is an equilibrium of an [[extensive-form game|4Y8AF9H9HA]] that specifies both strategies and beliefs.
At every information set, the player who moves holds a belief about where she is, and her strategy is optimal given that belief.
The beliefs must be limits of beliefs computed by Bayes' rule from slightly perturbed strategies.
David Kreps and Robert Wilson introduced it in 1982 as a refinement of [[subgame perfect equilibrium|HE8K9M4EXN]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a game with hidden moves, a player sometimes has to act without knowing exactly what happened before.
A sequential equilibrium asks each player to form a sensible guess about the past and to act well given that guess, at every point of the game.
This includes points that are never reached when everyone follows the plan.
For example, an incumbent firm cannot threaten a price war that it would not want to carry out once a rival has entered.
Sequential equilibrium removes such empty threats.
