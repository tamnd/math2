## Summary

This topic is a part of [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]].
It covers what a proof is, the kinds of results that proofs establish, and how mathematical knowledge is built from axioms, examples and conjectures.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]]: a map of the whole area, from the language of statements to the teaching of proof.
2. [[Mathematical proof|0B94TKFQJX]]: a chain of justified steps from axioms, definitions and earlier results, and its formal counterpart.
3. [[Deductive, inductive and abductive reasoning|5V35SGEN1T]]: only deduction proves, while induction and abduction suggest.
4. [[Theorems, lemmas, corollaries and propositions|KK5F0F03ZW]]: what the labels of results signal.
5. [[Hypotheses and conclusion of a theorem|W48XN2MEM9]]: how to read a statement and test each hypothesis.
6. [[Axiomatic method|GD2JBXJV5Z]]: primitive terms, axioms, definitions and theorems, with Euclid and Hilbert as models.
7. [[Conjecture|WMEMMHZ3BH]]: a statement believed but not proved, and how its status can change.
8. [[Functions of proof|ENY0GKRJS8]]: verification, explanation, communication, discovery and systematization.
9. [[Methodology of mathematics|3FJ3XMV9KD]]: how mathematicians form concepts, choose problems and test results.
10. [[Examples in mathematical practice|F3NNR77WKP]]: generic, extreme and boundary examples, and non-examples.
11. [[Abstraction and generalization|976RZZCT1H]]: general notions from cases, and theorems with weaker hypotheses.
12. [[Classification and invariants|9HQ3TADPA8]]: lists of objects up to equivalence, and the invariants that separate them.
13. [[Problem solvers and theory builders|Z29HM45R29]]: how single problems produce general theories, and the reverse.

### Prerequisites

- No earlier topic is needed: this sub-area is the start of the area, and the examples use school algebra and geometry.
- Rows 5, 11 and 12 use a few examples from first-year calculus and algebra, such as continuity, groups and vector spaces.
- [[The language of mathematical statements|WMY13MXY3W]] is optional background on "if", "for all" and "there exists".
- [[Mathematical logic|DYJ0KGTPMR]] gives the formal side of proof, for the reader who wants it.
