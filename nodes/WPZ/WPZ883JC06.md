## Definition

Let a [[heat engine|S7XFW04T3V]] absorb [[heat|CY4GJQGF06]] $Q_H > 0$ and reject heat $Q_C \ge 0$ in each cycle, so that it does net [[work|S8H21RWQ86]] $W = Q_H - Q_C$.
The *thermal efficiency* of the engine is
$$\eta = \frac{W}{Q_H} = 1 - \frac{Q_C}{Q_H}.$$

## Remarks

An engine that takes $1000\,\mathrm{J}$ and rejects $600\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle has $\eta = 400/1000 = 0.4$.
A non-example: the ratio $W/Q_C = 400/600$ for the same engine is not its efficiency, because the work must be divided by the heat absorbed, not the heat rejected.
For a heat engine $0 < \eta \le 1$, and the [[Kelvin–Planck statement|RMNJ00XP8X]] of the second law gives $\eta < 1$.
Between reservoirs at thermodynamic temperatures $T_H > T_C$, Carnot's theorem gives $\eta \le 1 - T_C/T_H$.
Efficiency is often given as a percentage.
