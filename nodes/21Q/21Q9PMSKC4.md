## Summary

Quantum contextuality is the fact that quantum measurement outcomes cannot in general be fixed in advance.
More precisely, they cannot be fixed independently of the compatible measurements performed together with them.
A measurement context is a set of jointly measurable [[observables|DF5RN297GE]], and a noncontextual model would give each observable one value in all contexts.
The Kochen–Specker theorem shows that no such assignment exists in Hilbert spaces of dimension at least $3$.
Contextuality is regarded as a resource in some models of quantum computation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a large table that lists, for every possible measurement, the answer it would give.
In classical physics such a table always exists, and measuring just reads off an entry.
In quantum mechanics, the answers would have to be consistent within every group of measurements that can be made together.
Because one measurement belongs to several such groups, these consistency conditions can contradict each other.
For example, with suitably chosen sets of three orthogonal directions in space, no rule saying "exactly one direction in each set is marked" can be followed.
