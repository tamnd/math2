## Definition

Consider a monatomic gas of identical particles of mass $m > 0$ in a region $\Omega \subseteq \R^3$, and let $k$ be the Boltzmann constant.
A *kinetic description* of the gas is a function $f(t, x, v) \ge 0$ of time $t$, position $x \in \Omega$ and velocity $v \in \R^3$, the one-particle distribution function.
For Borel sets $A \subseteq \Omega$ and $B \subseteq \R^3$, the integral $\int_A \int_B f(t, x, v)\, dv\, dx$ is the number of particles in $A$ with velocity in $B$ at time $t$.
The macroscopic fields are the following moments of $f$ in $v$, where the density $n$ is positive:

1. the number density $n(t, x) = \int f \, dv$;
2. the bulk velocity $u(t, x) = \dfrac{1}{n} \int v f \, dv$;
3. the temperature $T(t, x)$, given by $\dfrac32 n k T = \int \dfrac{m}{2} \abs{v - u}^2 f \, dv$.

The *kinetic theory of gases* is the study of gases through kinetic descriptions and their evolution equations, chiefly the [[Boltzmann equation|MH6CSWD7F0]].

## Remarks

The pressure is $p = \dfrac13 \int m \abs{v - u}^2 f \, dv$, and clause 3 then gives the ideal gas law $p = n k T$.

Example: consider a gas of $N$ particles at rest, uniformly spread in a box of volume $V$.
Let each velocity component be normally distributed with mean $0$ and variance $kT_0/m$.
Then $f = \dfrac{N}{V} \left( \dfrac{m}{2\pi k T_0} \right)^{3/2} e^{-m\abs{v}^2/(2kT_0)}$ in the box.
Its moments are $n = N/V$, $u = 0$ and $T = T_0$, since $\int \tfrac{m}{2}\abs{v}^2 f\, dv = n \cdot \tfrac{m}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot \tfrac{kT_0}{m}$.

Non-example: a dense liquid is not described by a one-particle distribution function alone, because correlations between neighbouring particles matter.
Kinetic theory in this form applies to dilute gases.
