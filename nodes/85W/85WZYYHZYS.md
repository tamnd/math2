## Summary

The acoustic wave equation is the [[wave equation|5EHD2XBQDG]] that governs small pressure disturbances in a fluid at rest.
It comes from the equations of [[compressible flow|FJC2GMVARF]] by linearising about a uniform state of rest.
Its wave speed is the [[speed of sound|SN4J6KP03C]] of the fluid.
It is the basic model of sound in air and in water.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Sound is a tiny ripple of pressure that travels through a fluid.
The fluid hardly moves, but each small squeeze pushes on the next layer of fluid, which squeezes the layer after it.
The acoustic wave equation describes this chain of squeezes.
It says that a pressure ripple keeps its shape and travels at the speed of sound, about $343$ metres per second in air at room temperature.
