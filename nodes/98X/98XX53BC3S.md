## Summary

The Wilson–Cowan model is a system of two [[ordinary differential equations|0NT64JV96A]] for the mean firing activity of an excitatory and an inhibitory population of neurons.
It was introduced by Hugh Wilson and Jack Cowan in 1972.
Each population relaxes towards a sigmoid function of its total input, which comes from both populations and from outside.
The model shows steady states, bistability and [[limit cycles|0AAQ73WRFB]], and it is a basic model of rhythms in cortical tissue.
Its spatial extension is the [[Wilson–Cowan neural field equation|VYB03ZZ8H6]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Instead of following single neurons, the model follows two numbers: how active the excitatory cells are and how active the inhibitory cells are.
Excitatory cells boost both groups; inhibitory cells damp both groups.
Each group responds to its input through an S-shaped curve: little input gives little activity, and the activity saturates for large input.
When excitation drives inhibition and inhibition then shuts excitation down, the two activities can chase each other in a cycle, which models a brain oscillation.
