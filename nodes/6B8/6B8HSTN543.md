## Statement

Let $\mathcal{RH}_\infty$ be the ring of proper real [[rational functions|0XDK9CGYKJ]] of $s$ whose poles all have negative real part.
Let $P$ be a strictly proper real rational [[transfer function|86JKV3TRFD]], and write $P = N/M$ with $N, M \in \mathcal{RH}_\infty$ coprime, so that there are $X, Y \in \mathcal{RH}_\infty$ with $NX + MY = 1$.
A proper real rational controller $C$ *stabilises* $P$ if, in the unity negative [[feedback|7NYES2D0Z4]] loop, the four transfer functions
$$\frac{1}{1 + PC}, \quad \frac{P}{1 + PC}, \quad \frac{C}{1 + PC}, \quad \frac{PC}{1 + PC}$$
all lie in $\mathcal{RH}_\infty$.
Then $C$ stabilises $P$ if and only if
$$C = \frac{X + MQ}{Y - NQ}$$
for some $Q \in \mathcal{RH}_\infty$, and $Q$ is then unique.

## Remarks

For $C = (X + MQ)/(Y - NQ)$, one computes $1 + PC = 1/\big(M(Y - NQ)\big)$ from $NX + MY = 1$.
So the four transfer functions are $M(Y - NQ)$, $N(Y - NQ)$, $M(X + MQ)$ and $N(X + MQ)$, which lie in $\mathcal{RH}_\infty$.
Conversely, a stabilising $C$ has a coprime factorisation $C = U/V$ over $\mathcal{RH}_\infty$ that can be normalised to $NU + MV = 1$, and then $Q = YU - XV$ gives $U = X + MQ$ and $V = Y - NQ$.
Strict properness of $P$ makes $N(\infty) = 0$, hence $Y(\infty) M(\infty) = 1$ and $Y - NQ$ is not zero at infinity, so every $C$ of this form is proper and the loop is well posed.

Example: if $P$ is itself stable, take $N = P$, $M = 1$, $X = 0$, $Y = 1$.
The stabilising controllers are $C = Q/(1 - PQ)$ with $Q \in \mathcal{RH}_\infty$, and the [[closed-loop transfer function|RYTSV821V8]] is $PC/(1 + PC) = PQ$, which is affine in $Q$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Many controllers stabilise a given system, and it is hard to see their set directly.
The theorem gives a formula that lists all of them, each one labelled by a free stable transfer function $Q$.
Every choice of $Q$ gives a stabilising controller, and no stabilising controller is missed.

### L2 human

The point of the parametrisation is that all closed-loop transfer functions become affine in $Q$.
Design goals such as a bound on the [[Hardy space|PNNNFJVF6W]] norm $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ of a closed-loop map then become convex problems in $Q$.
This is the starting point of $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ and $\mathcal{H}_2$ optimal control.
The same statement holds for multivariable plants, with left and right coprime factorisations of matrices over $\mathcal{RH}_\infty$.
It also holds for discrete-time systems, with the unit disc in place of the left half-plane.
