## Summary

Schelling's segregation model is an agent-based model with agents of two types living on a grid.
An agent moves when too few of its neighbours share its type.
Thomas Schelling introduced it in 1971.
Its main lesson is that mild individual preferences can produce strong segregation at the level of the whole population.
It is an early and influential agent-based model in the social sciences.
It is close in spirit to a [[cellular automaton|5QXNC6NTVN]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a chessboard with red and blue counters and some empty squares.
Each counter is content if at least a third of its neighbours are of its own colour; otherwise it moves to an empty square where it would be content.
Nobody in this story wants to live only among its own kind.
Yet after a few rounds of moving, the board usually breaks up into large red and blue patches.
