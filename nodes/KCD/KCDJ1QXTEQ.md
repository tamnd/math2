## Summary

The Jeans length is the critical size above which a region of self-gravitating gas collapses instead of being held up by pressure.
It depends only on the [[sound speed|SN4J6KP03C]] and the density of the gas.
It is the threshold in the [[Jeans instability|S1PZ23YAXQ]] and a basic scale in the theory of star formation and of cosmological structure.
The mass contained in a sphere of this size is called the Jeans mass.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A pressure wave travels across a region in a time equal to its size divided by the speed of sound.
Gravity collapses the region in a time that depends only on its density.
If the region is small, pressure reacts faster than gravity and holds it up.
If the region is larger than the Jeans length, gravity is faster and the region falls in on itself.
Cold gas has a low sound speed and dense gas collapses quickly, so cold dense clouds have short Jeans lengths and fragment easily.
