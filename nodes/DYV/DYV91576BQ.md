## Statement

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an [[abelian category|Y9CZ3MTNF1]] whose isomorphism classes of objects form a set.
Let $\mathcal{P}$ be a full subcategory of $\mathcal{A}$ that contains $0$ and satisfies these conditions.

1. $\mathcal{P}$ is closed under extensions: in a [[short exact sequence|KJKNJMGKZZ]] $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ of $\mathcal{A}$ with $A$ and $C$ in $\mathcal{P}$, also $B$ is in $\mathcal{P}$.
2. $\mathcal{P}$ is closed under kernels of epimorphisms: in such a sequence with $B$ and $C$ in $\mathcal{P}$, also $A$ is in $\mathcal{P}$.
3. Every object $A$ of $\mathcal{A}$ has a finite resolution $0 \to P_m \to \cdots \to P_1 \to P_0 \to A \to 0$ with all $P_i$ in $\mathcal{P}$.

Let $K_0(\mathcal{P})$ be the abelian group generated by the classes $[P]$ of objects of $\mathcal{P}$.
It has a relation $[B] = [A] + [C]$ for each short exact sequence of $\mathcal{A}$ whose three terms lie in $\mathcal{P}$.
Then the inclusion induces an isomorphism $K_0(\mathcal{P}) \to K_0(\mathcal{A})$ onto the [[Grothendieck group|2H7Y1XC93S]] of $\mathcal{A}$, with inverse $[A] \mapsto \sum_{i=0}^{m} (-1)^i [P_i]$.

## Remarks

The main point is that $\sum_i (-1)^i [P_i]$ does not depend on the resolution.
Two resolutions of $A$ can be compared through a third one that maps onto both, and condition 2 makes the kernels that appear lie in $\mathcal{P}$.
Additivity on short exact sequences then follows from the horseshoe construction, and the two maps are inverse to each other.
Quillen proved the same statement for all higher groups $K_n$.

Example: let $R$ be a regular Noetherian ring, that is, a [[Noetherian ring|0670T59HGE]] over which every finitely generated module has a finite projective resolution.
For instance $R = \Z$ or $R = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ over a field $k$.
Take $\mathcal{A}$ the finitely generated modules and $\mathcal{P}$ the finitely generated projective modules.
Short exact sequences of projective modules split, so $K_0(\mathcal{P})$ is [[$K_0(R)$|Q14JS2MA1C]], and the theorem gives the Cartan isomorphism $K_0(R) \cong G_0(R)$ of [[G-theory|YZSR4QKDQQ]].

Condition 3 is necessary.
For $R = k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ over a field $k$, the module $k$ has no finite projective resolution, and indeed $K_0(R) \cong \Z$ maps onto the subgroup $2\Z$ of $G_0(R) \cong \Z$, since $[R] = 2[k]$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A module that is hard to handle can often be replaced by a finite chain of easy modules that resolves it.
The resolution theorem says that, for counting purposes, nothing is lost: the alternating sum of the easy modules stands for the hard one.
So the K-group built from the easy modules equals the K-group built from all modules.

### L2 human

The main use is the comparison between $K$-theory and $G$-theory.
For a regular Noetherian ring, every finitely generated module has a finite projective resolution, and so $K_n(R) \cong G_n(R)$.
This lets one use the good properties of $G$-theory, such as localization sequences and homotopy invariance, to compute $K$-theory of regular rings.
For example, the homotopy invariance $G_n(R[t]) \cong G_n(R)$ for Noetherian $R$ gives $K_n(R[t]) \cong K_n(R)$ for regular $R$.
