## Summary

The (first) Brillouin zone of a crystal is a region of wave vectors around the origin.
It contains the points at least as close to the origin as to any other point of the [[reciprocal lattice|KNAK0TTVTY]].
It is the [[Voronoi cell|V1R1R88GV6]] of the origin in the reciprocal lattice.
Its translates by the reciprocal lattice tile wave-vector space, so every wave vector is equivalent to one in the zone.
Band structures, Fermi surfaces and phonon spectra are drawn over the Brillouin zone.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In a crystal, two wave vectors that differ by a reciprocal lattice vector describe the same physical state.
So one only needs one representative of each class.
The Brillouin zone is the most symmetric choice: the points nearer to the origin than to any other reciprocal lattice point.
For a chain of atoms at spacing $a$ it is the interval $[-\pi/a, \pi/a]$.
For a square grid of spacing $a$ it is the square $[-\pi/a, \pi/a]^2$.
For more complicated lattices it is a polyhedron, such as the truncated octahedron for the face-centred cubic lattice.
