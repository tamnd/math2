## Statement

Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]] of characteristic different from $2$, and let $J$ be a [[Jordan algebra|6C2MCVB8C7]] over $F$ with product $(x, y) \mapsto xy$.
For $x \in J$ let $U_x$ be the map $y \mapsto 2x(xy) - (xx)y$.
Assume that $J$ is prime, meaning that $IK \ne 0$ for all nonzero ideals $I$ and $K$ of $J$.
Assume that $J$ is nondegenerate, meaning that $U_x = 0$ implies $x = 0$.
Let $Z$ be the centre of $J$, the set of $z \in J$ with $(zx)y = z(xy)$ for all $x, y \in J$.
Then at least one of the following holds:

1. $J$ is a [[special Jordan algebra|T4AWF1BRZB]];
2. $Z \ne 0$, and the algebra of fractions $Z^{-1}J$ is an [[Albert algebra|1MSMVDRAVD]] over the field of fractions of $Z$.

## Remarks

Proof idea: the proof by Efim Zelmanov studies the values in $J$ of identities that hold in all special Jordan algebras but not in all Jordan algebras.
Glennie's identities are of this kind.
If $J$ is not special, such values are nonzero, and Zelmanov showed that they lead to nonzero central elements and then to case 2.

The special algebras in case 1 are further described: they are of Hermitian type, built from an associative algebra with involution, or of Clifford type, built from a quadratic form.

Example: a simple Jordan algebra with nonzero product is prime.
So a simple nondegenerate exceptional Jordan algebra is an Albert algebra over its centre, which is then a field.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Before Zelmanov, Jordan algebras were well understood only under finiteness assumptions, such as finite dimension.
His theorem removes these assumptions for the basic class of prime nondegenerate algebras.
It says that such an algebra either comes from an associative algebra, or is essentially the $27$-dimensional Albert algebra.
So there is no exotic infinite-dimensional exceptional Jordan algebra of this kind.

### L2 human

The theorem is the Jordan analogue of the structure theory of prime rings in associative algebra.
It explains why the Albert algebra is the only source of exceptional behaviour among prime nondegenerate Jordan algebras.
