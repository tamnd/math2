## Summary

Planetary migration is the slow change of the orbital radius of a planet.
It is caused by exchange of [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] with the gas disk or the small bodies around it.
It explains why giant planets such as hot Jupiters are found very close to their stars, far from where they could have formed.
It also explains how planets are captured into [[orbital resonances|3K553QG5BZ]] with each other.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A planet is born inside a disk of gas and dust that circles its young star.
The planet pulls on the disk and the disk pulls back, and these small tugs add up over many orbits.
If the disk takes angular momentum away, the planet spirals inward; if it gives some, the planet moves outward.
In the Solar System, Neptune is thought to have moved outward by scattering small icy bodies, which explains why Pluto is caught in a $3{:}2$ resonance with Neptune.
