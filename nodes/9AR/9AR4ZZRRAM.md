## Summary

The hodograph transformation exchanges the roles of position and velocity in a steady planar flow.
Instead of the velocity $(u, v)$ as a function of the position $(x, y)$, one studies $(x, y)$ as a function of $(u, v)$ in the hodograph plane.
For steady irrotational compressible flow this turns the nonlinear equations of motion into linear equations, such as [[Chaplygin's equation|GC17HMC8FN]].
It was a main source of exact solutions in [[transonic flow|20G6N8JGEH]] theory.
See the [[definition|VSQ4CYGYCG]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture each point of a flow carrying a small arrow, its velocity.
The hodograph is the picture obtained by drawing the tips of all these arrows from a common origin.
In gas dynamics the equations become simpler when written in this velocity picture, because their coefficients depend only on the velocity.
For example, a vortex in which the speed is $A/r$ at distance $r$ from the centre becomes, in the hodograph plane, a very simple solution of the transformed equation.
The price is that the flow region must be mapped one to one onto a region of velocities.
