## Statement

Consider a system in [[thermodynamic equilibrium|5EKTEE00FZ]] made of $C$ independent chemical components distributed among $P$ coexisting [[phases|6WJ0NADBT4]].
Assume that each phase is homogeneous, that surface, field and other effects are negligible, and that temperature and pressure are the only external [[intensive variables|H9F9CNPBSN]].
Then the number $F$ of intensive variables that can be varied independently without changing the number of phases is
$$F = C - P + 2.$$

## Remarks

Each phase is described by the temperature, the pressure and $C - 1$ independent mole fractions, so there are $P(C - 1) + 2$ intensive variables.
Equilibrium requires that the [[chemical potential|WVH4PVW64P]] of each component is the same in all phases, which gives $C(P - 1)$ independent equations.
Their difference is $P(C - 1) + 2 - C(P - 1) = C - P + 2$.
For a pure substance, $C = 1$.
One phase has $F = 2$ and fills a region of the [[phase diagram|R6XCQYXKRP]], and two phases have $F = 1$ and lie on a curve.
Three phases have $F = 0$ and meet at an isolated [[triple point|QENKZP7W4Y]].
In particular a pure substance cannot have four phases in equilibrium, since $F$ would be negative.
If the components take part in $R$ independent chemical reactions, $C$ is the number of species minus $R$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The phase rule counts how many knobs you can turn freely while keeping the same phases in balance.
For pure water with only liquid present, you can change both the temperature and the pressure a little.
With liquid and steam together, choosing the temperature fixes the pressure: that is the boiling curve.
With ice, liquid and steam together, nothing can be changed: this happens only at the triple point.

### L2 human

The rule is pure counting of unknowns and equations, so it applies to any system that meets the hypotheses, however complicated the chemistry.
It is the first thing to check when reading a [[phase diagram|R6XCQYXKRP]]: regions, curves and points must match $F = 2, 1, 0$ for one component.
For a binary alloy at fixed pressure, the rule becomes $F = C - P + 1 = 3 - P$, which explains why two-phase regions have fixed compositions at each temperature.
It counts only intensive variables; the amounts of each phase are not fixed by it but by mass balance, as in the lever rule.
