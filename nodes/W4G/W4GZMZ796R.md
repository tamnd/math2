## Summary

The Nambu–Goto action is the classical [[action|T0WAGV4KP4]] of a relativistic string.
Up to sign, it is the tension of the string times the area of the surface it sweeps out in spacetime.
It was proposed independently by Yoichiro Nambu and Tetsuo Goto around 1970.
Its extremals are the surfaces of zero mean curvature in spacetime, the analogue for strings of geodesics for point particles.
Because of the square root in the area, it is hard to quantize directly, and one usually replaces it by the classically equivalent [[Polyakov action|HFEGZQB8SV]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A point particle moving through spacetime traces a curve, and its action is proportional to the length of that curve measured by its own clock.
A string moving through spacetime traces a surface, called its worldsheet.
The Nambu–Goto action is proportional to the area of this surface, so the string moves in a way that makes the area stationary.
It is like a soap film, which takes the shape of least area, except that one of the directions on the surface is time.
