## Summary

A dimensionless number in [[fluid mechanics|H27M44BV4M]] is a product of powers of the physical quantities of a flow whose units cancel.
Each such number usually compares two effects, such as inertia and viscosity in the [[Reynolds number|JH9EJ0HA0R]].
Two flows with the same values of the relevant dimensionless numbers behave alike.
This is the basis of model testing in wind tunnels and water tanks.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A speed of $3$ means nothing until you say $3$ metres per second or $3$ kilometres per hour.
A dimensionless number is a combination of quantities in which all the units cancel, so its value is the same in every system of units.
For example, the speed of a jet divided by the speed of sound is a pure number, the [[Mach number|GFHJ858AN1]].
Such numbers tell which effects dominate a flow: a Mach number well above $1$ means that shock waves will form.
