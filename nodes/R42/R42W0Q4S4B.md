## Example

The [[strong Goldbach conjecture|063JNBSMJ0]] says: each even integer greater than $2$ is a sum of two [[primes|01826KMG2H]].
The weak Goldbach conjecture says: each odd integer greater than $5$ is a sum of three primes.
In both statements, the primes need not be distinct.
We check that the strong conjecture is [[stronger|KCK4AQ469Q]] than the weak one.

1. Suppose the strong conjecture is true, and let $n$ be an odd integer with $n > 5$.
2. The number $n - 3$ is even, because $n$ and $3$ are odd.
3. Since $n \geq 7$, we have $n - 3 \geq 4 > 2$.
4. By the strong conjecture, $n - 3 = p + q$ for some primes $p$ and $q$.
5. Then $n = 3 + p + q$, a sum of three primes. So the weak conjecture is true.
6. A numerical case: for $n = 15$, we get $n - 3 = 12 = 5 + 7$, so $15 = 3 + 5 + 7$.

## Remarks

The argument shows that the strong conjecture implies the weak one.
The argument goes in one direction only: a sum of three primes gives no sum of two primes for the even number $n - 3$.
The weak conjecture has a long history of partial results: [[Vinogradov's theorem|C01Z9QRGZY]] proves it for all sufficiently large odd numbers.
Harald Helfgott announced a proof of the full weak conjecture in 2013, while the strong conjecture is still open.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The strong conjecture is a claim about even numbers and pairs of primes, such as $12 = 5 + 7$.
If it is true, then we get a claim about odd numbers for free: take away $3$, split the even rest into two primes, and put the $3$ back.
So the strong claim carries the weak claim inside it, and that is why the names are "strong" and "weak".

### L2 human

We write the odd numbers from $7$ to $13$ as sums of three primes with the method of the example.

1. $7 - 3 = 4 = 2 + 2$, so $7 = 3 + 2 + 2$.
2. $9 - 3 = 6 = 3 + 3$, so $9 = 3 + 3 + 3$.
3. $11 - 3 = 8 = 3 + 5$, so $11 = 3 + 3 + 5$.
4. $13 - 3 = 10 = 5 + 5$, so $13 = 3 + 5 + 5$.

The typical use of this argument is to show that a proof of the strong conjecture would also settle the weak one.
A trap: a proof of the weak conjecture does not give the strong one, because the implication goes in one direction only.
