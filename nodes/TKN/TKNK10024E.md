## Problem

Let $m \ge 2$ machines $M_1, \dots, M_m$ and $n \ge 1$ jobs be given.
Each job $j$ must be processed on $M_1$, then on $M_2$, and so on up to $M_m$, without preemption.
It needs a time $p_{ij} \ge 0$ on machine $M_i$.
A machine processes at most one job at a time, and a job is on at most one machine at a time.
The *flow-shop scheduling problem* $F_m \| C_{\max}$ asks for a schedule that minimizes the makespan, the time at which the last job leaves $M_m$.

## Remarks

For $m = 2$ the problem is solved in time $O(n \log n)$ by Johnson's rule.
This rule sorts the jobs and uses the same order on both machines.
For $m = 3$ and more the problem is [[NP-hard|MHA48AWBXN]] in the strong sense.
For $m \le 3$ some optimal schedule is a permutation schedule, in which every machine processes the jobs in the same order; for $m \ge 4$ this can fail.
Example: two jobs with times $(p_{1j}, p_{2j}) = (1, 3)$ and $(3, 1)$; the order "first job, then second" has makespan $5$, the reverse order has makespan $7$.
Variants change the objective, for instance to total completion time, or forbid waiting between machines (no-wait flow shop).

## Explanations

### L1 human

Every job goes along the same assembly line: first station one, then station two, and so on.
The only choice is the order of the jobs at each station.
A good order keeps all stations busy and finishes the last job early; a bad order leaves stations idle waiting for work.
