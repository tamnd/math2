## Summary

Quantum computing is computation in which the data are held in [[qubits|E9QTT3XBXS]] and processed by unitary operations and measurements.
The state of $n$ qubits is a unit vector in a space of dimension $2^n$, and interference between amplitudes can be exploited.
Some problems, such as factoring by [[Shor's algorithm|83G4EPJ3EJ]], have quantum algorithms much faster than any known classical algorithm.
The problems solved efficiently by quantum computers form the class [[BQP|VP21SFE7ZA]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An ordinary computer stores bits, each $0$ or $1$.
A quantum computer stores qubits, which can be in superpositions of $0$ and $1$.
A computation applies a sequence of reversible operations, called gates, and ends with a measurement that gives random bits.
A good quantum algorithm arranges the amplitudes so that wrong answers cancel and the right answer is likely.
For example, [[Grover's algorithm|K9QPQH4YKY]] finds a marked item among $N$ items with about $\sqrt N$ queries instead of about $N$.
