## Summary

Fracture mechanics is the part of [[solid mechanics|JRD6BDKT1X]] that studies bodies containing cracks.
It computes the stress and strain fields near a crack, which are singular at the crack tip in [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]].
It gives criteria that decide when a crack grows.
Its central quantities are the [[stress intensity factor|8D619PN75J]], the [[energy release rate|D2D6BRJA5X]] and the [[J-integral|BZ2WFST50T]].
It explains why structures fail at loads far below the strength of the flawless material.
It is the basis of the damage-tolerant design of aircraft, pipelines and pressure vessels.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A sheet of paper is hard to tear by pulling on its edges, but a small cut makes tearing easy.
The cut concentrates the force at its end, so the material there breaks first, and the cut grows.
Fracture mechanics measures this concentration and compares it with the resistance of the material.
For example, it predicts that a plate with a crack twice as long breaks at a stress smaller by a factor of about $\sqrt{2}$.
