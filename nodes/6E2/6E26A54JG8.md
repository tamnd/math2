## Summary

The Monod equation gives the specific growth rate of a microbial population as a saturating function of the concentration of a limiting nutrient.
Jacques Monod proposed it in the 1940s from experiments with bacterial cultures.
It has the same form as the Michaelis–Menten rate law, but it is an empirical law for growth, not derived from a reaction mechanism.
It is the standard growth term in models of chemostats, bioreactors and wastewater treatment.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Bacteria grow faster when they have more food, but only up to a point.
At low food levels the growth rate is roughly proportional to the food; at high levels it levels off at a maximal rate.
The Monod equation is the simple curve with this shape, set by the maximal rate and the food level at which half of it is reached.
For example, if the maximal rate is $1$ per hour and the half-saturation level is $2$ mg/L, then at $2$ mg/L the population grows at $0.5$ per hour.
