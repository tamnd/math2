## Example

A function $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is [[continuous at the point|0KRMJRDRZT]] $a$ if, in the [[epsilon-delta form|0W8YHHGCJG]],
$$\forall \varepsilon > 0 \; \exists \delta > 0 \; \forall x \in \mathbb{R} \; \big( |x - a| < \delta \Rightarrow |f(x) - f(a)| < \varepsilon \big).$$
We [[negate|K930V33BSC]] this statement step by step.
Then we use the negation to show that the [[Heaviside step function|09C4JP7Y0M]] $H$, with $H(x) = 0$ for $x < 0$ and $H(x) = 1$ for $x \geq 0$, is not continuous at $0$.

1. The negation of "$\forall \varepsilon > 0$" is "$\exists \varepsilon > 0$ such that not". The domain $\varepsilon > 0$ stays.
2. The negation of "$\exists \delta > 0$" is "$\forall \delta > 0$, not". The domain $\delta > 0$ stays.
3. The negation of "$\forall x$" is "$\exists x$ such that not".
4. The negation of the conditional "$|x - a| < \delta \Rightarrow |f(x) - f(a)| < \varepsilon$" is "$|x - a| < \delta$ and $|f(x) - f(a)| \geq \varepsilon$".
5. So $f$ is not continuous at $a$ when
$$\exists \varepsilon > 0 \; \forall \delta > 0 \; \exists x \in \mathbb{R} \; \big( |x - a| < \delta \text{ and } |f(x) - f(a)| \geq \varepsilon \big).$$
6. For $H$ at $a = 0$, take $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$. Let $\delta > 0$ be arbitrary, and take $x = -\delta/2$.
7. Then $|x - 0| = \delta/2 < \delta$. Since $x < 0$, $H(x) = 0$, and $|H(x) - H(0)| = |0 - 1| = 1 \geq \tfrac{1}{2}$.
8. The $\delta$ in step 6 was arbitrary, so the negation holds, and $H$ is not continuous at $0$.

## Remarks

The negation has the same three quantifiers in the same order, with each one swapped.
The order matters for the proof: $\varepsilon$ is fixed once, before $\delta$, and the point $x$ may depend on $\delta$.
Here $x = -\delta/2$ depends on $\delta$, and $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$ does not.

Any $\varepsilon$ with $0 < \varepsilon \leq 1$ works in step 6, since the jump of $H$ at $0$ has size $1$.
The value $\varepsilon = 2$ does not work: $|H(x) - H(0)| \leq 1 < 2$ for every $x$.
The conclusion does not depend on the value $H(0)$.
With the convention $H(0) = \tfrac{1}{2}$, the same $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and $x = -\delta/2$ give $|0 - \tfrac{1}{2}| = \tfrac{1}{2} \geq \tfrac{1}{2}$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A function is continuous at a point when its graph does not jump there.
The step function is $0$ to the left of $0$ and $1$ from $0$ on.
Points just left of $0$, such as $-0.1$ or $-0.001$, all have value $0$, but the value at $0$ is $1$.
So however close we look, there are points with a value that differs by $1$.
That is what "not continuous at $0$" means.

### L2 human

We show that $f(x) = \lfloor x \rfloor$, the greatest integer at most $x$, is not continuous at $a = 1$.

1. By step 5 above, we need $\varepsilon > 0$ such that every $\delta > 0$ has a point $x$ with $|x - 1| < \delta$ and $|f(x) - f(1)| \geq \varepsilon$.
2. Take $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$, and let $\delta > 0$ be arbitrary.
3. Take $x = 1 - \min(\delta, 1)/2$. Then $\tfrac{1}{2} \leq x < 1$, and $|x - 1| < \delta$.
4. Since $0 \leq x < 1$, we get $f(x) = 0$, while $f(1) = 1$. So $|f(x) - f(1)| = 1 \geq \tfrac{1}{2}$.

The typical use of the negated definition is in a proof of discontinuity: it tells us to fix one $\varepsilon$ and to answer each $\delta$ with a point.

A trap: a student lets $\varepsilon$ depend on $\delta$, for example $\varepsilon = \delta$.
The negation asks for one $\varepsilon$ that works against every $\delta$, so $\varepsilon$ must be chosen first.
