## Summary

A constitutive equation is a relation, specific to a material, between the [[stress|HXGCGHS070]] in a body and its deformation, rate of deformation, temperature or history.
The balance laws of continuum mechanics hold for all materials and do not determine the motion alone; constitutive equations close the system.
Hooke's law for elastic solids and Newton's law of viscosity for fluids are the classical examples.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Steel, rubber, honey and sand respond very differently to the same push.
The laws of motion are the same for all of them; what differs is how stress is produced by deformation.
A constitutive equation is the rule that captures this behaviour for one material.
For a spring it is "force equals stiffness times extension"; for a solid, a matrix version of the same idea.
