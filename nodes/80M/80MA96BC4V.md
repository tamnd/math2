## Example

The claim is the [[inequality of arithmetic and geometric means|0DRF0F3XC9]] for two numbers: for all real $a \ge 0$ and $b \ge 0$,
$$\frac{a + b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab}.$$
A student hands in the following text, which is a flawed example.

> $\frac{a+b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab} \;\Rightarrow\; a + b \ge 2\sqrt{ab} \;\Rightarrow\; a - 2\sqrt{ab} + b \ge 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (\sqrt a - \sqrt b)^2 \ge 0$, which is true.
> $\blacksquare$

The flaw is the direction of the argument, and we check it against the method of [[scratch work and the final proof|E97XXFW9MV]].

1. The first line is the claim, so the text assumes what it must prove.
2. Each arrow goes from the claim toward a known fact, so the text shows only that the claim implies $(\sqrt a - \sqrt b)^2 \ge 0$.
3. A true conclusion does not make the starting point true, so the text, read as written, proves nothing about the claim.
4. The text is good scratch work, because it finds the fact from which a proof can start.

The final proof reverses the chain and checks that each step goes forward.

> Let $a \ge 0$ and $b \ge 0$.
> Then $\sqrt a$ and $\sqrt b$ are real numbers with $(\sqrt a)^2 = a$ and $(\sqrt b)^2 = b$.
> A square is non-negative, so $(\sqrt a - \sqrt b)^2 \ge 0$.
> Expanding gives $a - 2\sqrt a \sqrt b + b \ge 0$, and $\sqrt a \sqrt b = \sqrt{ab}$ because $a, b \ge 0$.
> Adding $2\sqrt{ab}$ to both sides gives $a + b \ge 2\sqrt{ab}$, and dividing by $2$ gives $\frac{a + b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab}$.
> $\square$

Check: $a = 1$ and $b = 9$ give $\frac{1 + 9}{2} = 5 \ge 3 = \sqrt 9$, and $(\sqrt 1 - \sqrt 9)^2 = 4 \ge 0$.

## Remarks

Here each backward step happens to be reversible, so the reversed chain is a proof.
The student's mathematics is right, but its order is the order of a search, not of a proof.
With a one-way step, the same form can start from a false claim and still reach a true statement; [[squaring both sides|FGS7ZJJYRK]] is the usual example.

The reversed proof uses the hypotheses $a \ge 0$ and $b \ge 0$ twice: for $\sqrt a$ and $\sqrt b$ to exist, and for $\sqrt a \sqrt b = \sqrt{ab}$.
A student who wants to keep the backward order can write "$\iff$" at each step and give the reason why each step is an equivalence.
Then the text is a proof, but the reader must check two directions at every step.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The student starts by writing the claim and then changes it, step by step, into something true.
That is a good way to find a proof, but it is not a proof.
It is like saying "if I am right, then two plus two is four, so I am right".
A true end point does not show that the start was true.
The repair is to start from the true fact and walk forward to the claim.

### L2 human

We test the backward form on a false claim, to see why the form proves nothing.
The false claim is $a \ge b$ for all real $a$ and $b$.
The backward text reads "$a \ge b$, so $0 \cdot a \ge 0 \cdot b$, so $0 \ge 0$, which is true".
Each step is a correct implication, and the last line is true.
But the claim is false, since $a = 0$ and $b = 1$ give $0 \ge 1$.

So the form "claim, arrows, true statement" can reach a true line from a false start.
Use the backward search to find the starting fact of a proof.
Watch out: before you reverse the chain, check each step, because multiplication by $0$ cannot be reversed.
