## Problem

Find a consistent quantum theory of the gravitational field.
It should agree with [[general relativity|7M7M55TKXA]] in the classical limit.
It should agree with [[quantum field theory|W031YNVRE3]] on a fixed background when gravity is weak.
It should give definite predictions at the Planck length $\ell_P = \sqrt{\hbar G / c^3} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-35}$ m.

## Remarks

The problem is open.
Treating the metric perturbatively as a quantum field around flat spacetime gives a theory that is not renormalizable.
't Hooft and Veltman (1974) found one-loop divergences once matter is included.
Goroff and Sagnotti (1986) found a two-loop divergence of pure gravity.
Below the Planck scale, gravity can be treated as an effective field theory, so the problem concerns high energies, black hole singularities and the early universe.
A further difficulty is that general relativity is background independent, while standard [[quantum mechanics|108MYT1R5Z]] uses a fixed time; this leads to the problem of time.
Main approaches include [[string theory|GM58M1HFY0]], [[loop quantum gravity|9C3ECSDRTP]], [[asymptotic safety|J02PA86B38]], [[causal dynamical triangulation|2Y9VNGWFTS]] and [[causal sets|1VDKXSAFRE]].
In 1916 Einstein remarked that quantum effects should require a modification of his theory of gravitation.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Our two best theories of nature, general relativity for gravity and quantum theory for everything else, do not fit together.
Usually this does not matter: gravity is negligible for atoms, and quantum effects are negligible for planets.
Inside black holes and at the Big Bang both matter at once, and there we have no reliable theory.
Quantum gravity is the search for that theory.
