## Summary

A nonassociative algebra over a [[commutative ring|0310REJJY8]] $R$ is an $R$-[[module|0NZ8JA25WK]] with an $R$-bilinear multiplication.
The word "nonassociative" means "not necessarily associative", so [[associative algebras|3CTW1P4HBP]] are a special case.
The main classes are [[Lie algebras|5JJYBJ6A8K]], [[Jordan algebras|6C2MCVB8C7]] and [[alternative algebras|N8K9SSVMRN]] such as the [[octonions|1HRMT3DSX2]].
Each class is cut out by [[identities|TM8ZXES0DJ]] that replace the associative law.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take a set of objects that you can add, scale by numbers and multiply, with the usual distributive laws.
In a nonassociative algebra the brackets in a product of three elements may matter: $(xy)z$ and $x(yz)$ can differ.
The cross product on $\R^3$ is a familiar example.
For it, $(e_1 \times e_1) \times e_2 = 0$, while $e_1 \times (e_1 \times e_2) = -e_2$.
