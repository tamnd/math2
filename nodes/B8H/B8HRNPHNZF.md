## Summary

Radiative forcing is the change in the net flow of radiative energy into the climate system caused by an imposed change.
Examples are more carbon dioxide, volcanic aerosols or a change in the brightness of the Sun.
It is measured before the surface temperature has responded.
It is measured in watts per square metre, averaged over the globe.
It lets very different causes of climate change be compared on one scale.
In simple models the eventual change of the global temperature is roughly proportional to the forcing.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of the Earth's energy budget as a bank account: sunlight is the income and heat radiated to space is the spending.
A forcing is a sudden change in this budget, measured before the planet has had time to warm or cool.
Adding carbon dioxide makes it harder for heat to escape, so the Earth keeps a little more energy each second, a positive forcing.
A large volcanic eruption puts reflective particles in the sky, so less sunlight is absorbed, a negative forcing.
Doubling the carbon dioxide in the air gives a forcing of a few watts on each square metre of the Earth.
