## Summary

Goal programming is a way to handle [[multi-objective optimization|E560RGEKPP]] by setting a target value for each objective and minimizing the shortfalls from the targets.
Each target becomes a linear equation with two extra nonnegative variables that measure how far the target is missed from below and from above.
The weighted sum of the unwanted deviations, or a lexicographic order of them, is then minimized as a [[linear program|VNMXB0J2KA]].
It is widely used in planning problems where several aims compete, such as cost, quality and workload.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A manager wants high profit, low overtime and little waste, but cannot have everything at once.
Goal programming asks for a target for each aim, for example "profit at least 100" and "overtime at most 10 hours".
It then looks for a plan that misses the targets as little as possible, counting each miss with a weight that says how much it matters.
If all targets can be met at once, the plan meets them all; otherwise it finds the best compromise.
