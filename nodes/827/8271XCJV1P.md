## Definition

Let $\rho$ and $J$ be twice continuously differentiable charge and current densities with $\partial_t \rho + \nabla \cdot J = 0$.
Assume that they vanish outside a fixed ball around the origin of $\R^3$.
Let $E$, $B$ be the retarded solution of [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]] with these sources, given by [[Jefimenko's equations|Y4A92XVXP0]].
Let $S = E \times B / \mu_0$ be the [[Poynting vector|P3PK0Q02CZ]] and let $c$ be the speed of light.
For $R > 0$ let $S_R$ be the sphere of radius $R$ about the origin, with outward unit normal $n$.
The *radiated power* of the sources at time $t$ is
$$P(t) = \lim_{R \to \infty} \oint_{S_R} S(x, t + R/c) \cdot n \, dA(x),$$
when the limit exists.
The sources *radiate* at time $t$ if $P(t) \neq 0$.
The *radiation field* is the part of $(E, B)$ that decays like $1/R$ at distance $R$; this part alone determines $P$.

## Remarks

The shift $t + R/c$ follows the energy emitted at time $t$ as it travels out.
Fields decaying like $1/R^2$ give flux of order $R^{-4} \cdot R^2 \to 0$, so only the $1/R$ part contributes.
In Jefimenko's equations this part is formed by the terms with $\partial_t \rho$ and $\partial_t J$.

Example: an electric dipole $p(t) = p_0 \cos(\omega t)$ along a fixed axis radiates, with time-averaged power $\frac{\mu_0 p_0^2 \omega^4}{12 \pi c}$.

Non-example: static charges and steady currents do not radiate: their fields decay like $1/R^2$, so $P = 0$.
A single charge in uniform motion does not radiate either, although it lies outside this definition, since it does not stay in a fixed ball.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Radiation is what is left of the field very far from the sources.
To test whether a source radiates, measure the power flowing through a huge sphere around it, waiting for the travel time of light.
If this power stays nonzero as the sphere grows, energy is really leaving the source.
