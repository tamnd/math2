## Summary

The stagnation pressure, or total pressure, at a point of a flow is a pressure the fluid would reach at rest.
The fluid must be brought to rest reversibly and without heat exchange.
For slow flows it is the static pressure plus the dynamic pressure $\frac{1}{2}\rho u^2$, by [[Bernoulli's principle|7XFWQVWPG1]].
It is constant along streamlines of ideal flow and drops only through losses such as friction and shock waves.
So it measures the mechanical energy of the flow.
Pitot tubes measure it, and the speed of an aircraft is computed from it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put your hand out of a car window and the air piles up against your palm.
At the centre of the palm the air comes to a stop, and the pressure there is higher than in the surrounding air.
That higher pressure is the stagnation pressure.
At $30$ metres per second in air of density $1.2\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ the excess is $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.2 \cdot 30^2 = 540$ pascals.
