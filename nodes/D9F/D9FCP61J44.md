## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be an [[alphabet|SEVFW9PNMC]].
A *string*, or *word*, over $\Sigma$ is a finite sequence $w = a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n$ with $n \ge 0$ and each $a_i \in \Sigma$.
Its *length* is $\abs{w} = n$.
The string with $n = 0$ is the *empty string*, written $\varepsilon$.
The set of all strings over $\Sigma$ is written $\Sigma^*$.
The *concatenation* of $u = a_1 \cdots a_n$ and $v = b_1 \cdots b_m$ is $uv = a_1 \cdots a_n b_1 \cdots b_m$.

## Remarks

Example: over $\Sigma = \set{a, b}$, $w = aba$ is a string of length $3$, and its concatenation with $b$ is $abab$.
Non-example: the infinite sequence $ababab\cdots$ is not a string, since a string has finite length; such sequences are infinite words, studied separately.
Concatenation is associative, $\abs{uv} = \abs{u} + \abs{v}$, and $\varepsilon w = w \varepsilon = w$.
So $\Sigma^*$ with concatenation is the [[free monoid|27ZPTJP9PP]] on $\Sigma$.
For $k \ge 0$, $\Sigma^k$ denotes the set of strings of length $k$, and $\Sigma^+$ the set of nonempty strings.
