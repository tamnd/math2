## Summary

Causal dynamical triangulation (CDT) is an approach to [[quantum gravity|M1VXRT5X56]].
It defines the gravitational [[path integral|1NYTFT5NPE]] as a sum over spacetimes built from flat simplices.
The simplices are glued so that the result has a global notion of time.
It was developed by Jan Ambjørn, Jerzy Jurkiewicz and Renate Loll from the late 1990s.
The causal gluing rule distinguishes it from earlier Euclidean dynamical triangulations.
Computer simulations in four dimensions show an extended four-dimensional universe in one of its phases.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The idea is to build spacetime out of identical small building blocks, like a structure of Lego pieces.
Each piece is a simplex with fixed edge lengths, and the only freedom is how the pieces are glued.
The rule is that spacetime must be layered into slices of constant time, so that cause and effect stay well ordered.
The quantum theory sums over all such ways of gluing, weighted by the discrete Einstein action, and the sum is computed by Monte Carlo simulation.
Then one takes the size of the pieces to zero and asks what kind of smooth spacetime appears.
