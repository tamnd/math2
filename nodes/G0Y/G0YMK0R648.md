## Summary

The Ernst equation is a single nonlinear partial differential equation for one complex function, the Ernst potential.
It is equivalent to the vacuum Einstein equations for stationary axisymmetric spacetimes, written in [[Weyl–Lewis–Papapetrou coordinates|ASWJNB3804]].
It was introduced by Frederick J. Ernst in 1968.
The equation is integrable, and solution-generating techniques based on it produce many exact solutions, including the [[Kerr metric|X3FZC85THY]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Einstein's equations are ten coupled equations, which are hard to solve in general.
For a rotating body that does not change in time, symmetry reduces them a lot, and Ernst found that what remains fits into one equation for one complex-valued function.
The real part of this function measures the gravitational potential, and the imaginary part measures the rotation.
If there is no rotation, the equation becomes the ordinary Laplace equation for a potential, as in Newtonian gravity.
The equation has hidden symmetries that allow one to build new solutions from known ones.
