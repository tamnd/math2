## Summary

A linear response function describes how an observable of a system changes, to first order, when a weak time-dependent force acts on it.
The change at time $t$ is the integral of the force at earlier times against a kernel $\chi$.
Causality makes $\chi$ vanish for negative times.
Response functions give susceptibilities, conductivities and absorption spectra.
In equilibrium statistical mechanics they are computed from correlation functions by the Kubo formula.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Push a system lightly and watch how one of its quantities moves.
If the push is small, the effect is proportional to the push, and a push now affects the system only later.
The response function records how strongly a short kick at one time shows up at a later time.
For a damped spring pushed by a force, the response function is the motion that follows a sharp tap, a decaying oscillation.
Knowing this one function lets one predict the effect of any weak force by adding up the effects of many small taps.
