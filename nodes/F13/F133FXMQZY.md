## Summary

In number theory, a positive integer $k$ is said to be an **Erdős–Woods number** if it has the following property: there exists a positive integer $a$ such that in the [[sequence|0G9EKM0E15]] $(a, a + 1, \ldots, a + k)$ of consecutive integers, each of the elements has a non-trivial common factor with one of the endpoints. In other words, $k$ is an Erdős–Woods number if there exists a positive integer $a$ such that for each integer $i$ between $0$ and $k$, at least one of the [[greatest common divisors|0C8WYW4KYQ]] $\gcd(a, a + i)$ or $\gcd(a + i, a + k)$ is greater than $1$.
