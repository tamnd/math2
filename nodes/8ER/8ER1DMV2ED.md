## Summary

Geometric group theory is the study of infinite groups, most of them finitely generated, through their presentations, their algorithms and the geometry of the spaces on which they act.
A finite generating set makes a group into a metric space with the word metric, and the geometry of this space up to quasi-isometry does not depend on the choice of the finite generating set.
The subject links algebra with topology and geometry, and the fundamental groups of manifolds, the mapping class groups of surfaces and the braid groups are central examples.
Dehn stated the word, conjugacy and isomorphism problems for groups in 1911, and the work of Gromov on growth and on hyperbolic groups in the 1980s made the geometric view a field of its own.
This topic collects the geometric group theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Group theory, Lie groups, representation theory, low-dimensional topology and homological algebra have their own topics.
