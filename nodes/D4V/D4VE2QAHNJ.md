## Definition

*Orbital mechanics*, also called *astrodynamics*, is the part of [[celestial mechanics|RCC89W11XQ]] about artificial satellites, spacecraft and rockets.
It applies [[Newton's laws of motion|KNBP19FG6K]] and [[Newton's law of gravitation|29WRN5J730]] to their motion.
Its objects are trajectories under the gravity of one or more bodies, together with the changes of velocity produced by thrust.

## Remarks

The first approximation treats each phase of a flight as a two-body problem, whose orbits obey [[Kepler's laws|M1A7XV292M]].
Typical tasks are the transfer between two orbits with the least change of velocity, the determination of an orbit from observations, and the rendezvous of two spacecraft.
The cost of a manoeuvre is measured by its total change of velocity, written $\Delta v$.
The study of planets and moons for their own sake belongs to celestial mechanics rather than to orbital mechanics.
