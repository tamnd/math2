## Definition

Consider a ball of uniform density $\rho > 0$ made of pressureless matter, initially at rest, that contracts under its own Newtonian gravity, with gravitational constant $G$.
The *free-fall time* is the time after which every shell of the ball reaches the centre:
$$t_{\text{ff}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{32 \, G \rho}}.$$

## Remarks

Check: a shell starting at radius $r_0$ feels the attraction of the mass $M = \frac{4\pi}{3} r_0^3 \rho$ inside it, which stays constant because shells do not cross.
By [[Newton's law of gravitation|29WRN5J730]] the shell moves on a degenerate Kepler orbit with semi-major axis $r_0/2$, and it reaches the centre after half a period, $\frac{\pi}{2} \sqrt{r_0^3 / (2GM)} = \sqrt{3\pi / (32 G \rho)}$, independent of $r_0$.
Example: for $\rho = 1.4 \times 10^3$ kg per cubic metre, close to the mean density of the Sun, and $G \approx 6.67 \times 10^{-11}$ in SI units, the formula gives about $1.8 \times 10^3$ seconds.
Non-example: a star in [[hydrostatic equilibrium|4FC5XQA2RK]] has pressure, so it does not collapse in the time $t_{\text{ff}}$ or at all.
Still, $t_{\text{ff}}$ is used as its dynamical time scale.
