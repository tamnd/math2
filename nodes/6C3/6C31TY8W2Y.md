## Problem

Let $n \ge 1$, let $a_1, \dots, a_n$ be points of the plane $\R^2$, and let $w_1, \dots, w_n > 0$ be weights.
The *Weber problem* asks for a point $x \in \R^2$ that minimizes
$$f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \norm{x - a_i},$$
where $\norm{\cdot}$ is the [[Euclidean distance|0NPPGHQNXQ]].

## Remarks

The function $f$ is [[convex|07Q7CNM5WF]] and tends to infinity as $\norm{x} \to \infty$, so a minimizer exists.
If the points $a_i$ are not all on one line, $f$ is strictly convex and the minimizer is unique.
At a minimizer $x$ that is not one of the $a_i$, the weighted unit vectors pointing to the $a_i$ sum to zero: $\sum_i w_i (a_i - x)/\norm{a_i - x} = 0$.
A point $a_k$ is a minimizer exactly when $\norm{\sum_{i \ne k} w_i (a_i - a_k)/\norm{a_i - a_k}} \le w_k$.
In general there is no closed formula; Weiszfeld's iteration (1937), $x \mapsto \big(\sum_i w_i a_i / \norm{x - a_i}\big) / \big(\sum_i w_i / \norm{x - a_i}\big)$, is the classical numerical method.
For $n = 3$ and equal weights the minimizer is the [[Fermat point|QM0CGSQ4TF]] of the triangle.
The same problem in $\R^d$ is also studied, and with equal weights its minimizer is called the geometric median.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A company wants to build one warehouse to supply several shops.
Each shop needs a different amount of goods, so the trucks to it weigh differently in the transport cost.
The Weber problem asks where to put the warehouse so that the total weighted travel distance is smallest.
Unlike the average of the points, this best place is pulled less strongly by shops that are far away.
