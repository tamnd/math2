## Summary

The quantum inverse scattering method is an algebraic framework for quantum integrable models in one space dimension.
The Leningrad school of Faddeev developed it in the late 1970s.
It builds a family of commuting operators, the transfer matrices, from a solution of the [[Yang–Baxter equation|663BP8ZHS5]].
The Hamiltonian is one member of this family.
The eigenvectors are created by matrix entries of the monodromy matrix; this is the algebraic form of the [[Bethe ansatz|S92G5M6X41]].
It is the quantum version of the classical [[inverse scattering transform|6GY3X4Y8X5]] and led to the theory of quantum groups.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a chain of quantum spins, one multiplies small matrices, one per site, into one big matrix.
The entries of this big matrix are operators, and the relations between them come from one equation, the Yang–Baxter equation.
Taking the trace gives a whole family of operators that commute with each other and with the energy.
Other entries act as creation operators that build the eigenstates one excitation at a time.
