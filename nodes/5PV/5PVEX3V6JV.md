## Summary

Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet gravity is general relativity with the Gauss–Bonnet term $R^2 - 4R_{ab}R^{ab} + R_{abcd}R^{abcd}$ added to the action.
It is the simplest [[Lovelock theory|AS38SRETPQ]] beyond general relativity and has second-order field equations.
The added term changes the field equations only in dimension five or more.
In four dimensions its integral is a topological invariant, by the [[Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem|GG6NYPKMHX]].
It appears in the low-energy limit of some string theories and gives new black hole solutions in higher dimensions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In four dimensions the Gauss–Bonnet combination of curvatures adds up to a number that depends only on the topology of spacetime.
So it cannot affect the equations of motion.
In five or more dimensions it does affect them, and gives a theory that is still well behaved but differs from Einstein's.
Black holes in this theory, for example, have horizons and thermodynamics that differ from those of general relativity at small sizes.
