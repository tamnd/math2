## Summary

L-theory is the K-theory of quadratic forms over a [[ring with involution|7W52X4N3NJ]].
It assigns to such a ring $R$ abelian groups $L_n(R)$, periodic in $n$ with period $4$.
Wall introduced these groups as the home of obstructions in [[surgery theory|MHHN8386TG]].
Ranicki gave an algebraic theory of them through chain complexes with Poincaré duality.
The L-groups of the integers are $\Z$, $0$, $\Z/2$, $0$ in degrees $0, 1, 2, 3$ modulo $4$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A quadratic form can be cancelled when it is hyperbolic, that is when it looks like $xy$ in suitable coordinates.
The even L-groups record forms over a ring up to adding hyperbolic forms, much like the Witt group of a field.
The odd L-groups record automorphisms of hyperbolic forms, in a similar way.
In topology, the middle-dimensional homology of a manifold carries an intersection form.
L-groups measure whether such forms can be made hyperbolic by surgery, and so whether a map can be improved to a homotopy equivalence of manifolds.
For the integers, $L_0(\Z) \cong \Z$ is detected by the signature divided by $8$, and $L_2(\Z) \cong \Z/2$ by the Arf invariant.
