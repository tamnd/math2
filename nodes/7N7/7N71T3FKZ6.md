## Summary

The nonlinear susceptibilities $\chi^{(2)}, \chi^{(3)}, \dots$ are the tensors of coefficients of the higher powers of the field in the [[polarization density|ASQAH710WN]] of a medium.
They measure how strongly the medium responds in a nonlinear way.
The second-order tensor $\chi^{(2)}$ gives three-wave effects such as second-harmonic generation, and the third-order tensor $\chi^{(3)}$ gives the [[Kerr effect|Y7WQY4568B]] and four-wave mixing.
Symmetry of the material constrains them strongly: $\chi^{(2)}$ vanishes in media with a centre of inversion.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a light field pushes on the electrons of a material, they respond almost in proportion, like a spring that is only slightly stretched.
The nonlinear susceptibilities are the small correction terms, which matter when the push is strong.
The second-order term is like a spring that is stiffer on one side than the other.
Glass has no second-order term, because it looks the same after turning it inside out, but it does have a third-order term.
