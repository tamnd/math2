## Summary

A waveguide is a structure, uniform along one axis, that confines electromagnetic waves in the transverse directions and guides them along the axis.
Typical examples are hollow metal pipes for microwaves, dielectric slabs and optical fibers.
Its guided waves are modes with a definite transverse profile and a propagation constant along the axis.
Each mode of a hollow metal guide propagates only above a cutoff frequency, set by the size of the cross-section.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A waveguide is a pipe for waves.
In free space a wave spreads out and gets weaker, but inside a metal tube it bounces off the walls and keeps going along the tube.
Only certain patterns fit across the tube, much as only certain notes fit on a guitar string.
A rectangular tube about $2.3$ cm wide carries microwaves of around $10$ GHz, as in radar systems.
