## Statement

Let a source with voltage $V \ne 0$ and internal resistance $R_S > 0$ be connected to a load resistor $R_L \ge 0$.
The power absorbed by the load is
$$P(R_L) = \frac{V^2 R_L}{(R_S + R_L)^2}.$$
Among all $R_L \ge 0$, $P$ attains its maximum exactly at $R_L = R_S$, and the maximum value is $V^2 / (4 R_S)$.

## Remarks

The current is $V / (R_S + R_L)$, and the load power is $R_L$ times its square.
The derivative is $P'(R_L) = V^2 (R_S - R_L)/(R_S + R_L)^3$, which is positive for $R_L < R_S$ and negative for $R_L > R_S$.
By [[Thévenin's theorem|FD73GQDXYR]], the result applies to any linear resistive circuit seen from two terminals, with $V = V_{\mathrm{th}}$ and $R_S = R_{\mathrm{th}}$.
At the maximum, the source and the load absorb equal power, so the efficiency is only one half.
The theorem fixes the source and varies the load; if instead the load is fixed, the load power is largest when $R_S = 0$.
For sinusoidal steady state with source impedance $Z_S$ with positive real part, the average load power is largest when the load impedance is the [[complex conjugate|0FTH2E9TAS]] $\overline{Z_S}$.
For example, with $V = 10$ volts and $R_S = 5$ ohms, the best load is $5$ ohms and it receives $5$ watts.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To draw the most power from a source that has some internal resistance, give the load the same resistance as the source.
A smaller load wastes the voltage inside the source, and a larger load lets too little current flow.

### L2 human

The theorem is the reason for impedance matching in amplifiers, antennas and transmission lines.
In the language of [[scattering parameters|EWRNXMPHGM]], a load equal to the real reference impedance reflects nothing back.
It must not be read as a rule for efficiency: power plants are built with source resistance much smaller than the load.
