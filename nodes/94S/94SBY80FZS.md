## Summary

The Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector is a vector that stays constant during the motion in the [[Kepler problem|TYXD1T6FRX]], in addition to the energy and the [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]].
It points from the centre of force to the pericentre of the orbit, and its length is proportional to the eccentricity.
Its conservation explains why Kepler orbits are closed conics that do not precess.
It reflects a hidden symmetry of the inverse-square force, which is also behind the degeneracy of the energy levels of the hydrogen atom.

## Explanations

### L1 human

For a planet around the Sun, the ellipse keeps the same orientation in space forever, in the ideal two-body model.
The Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector is the arrow from the Sun to the closest point of the ellipse, with a length that measures how stretched the ellipse is.
It is a [[first integral|0262JP0NV9]]: it does not change along the motion.
For a circular orbit the vector is zero, since a circle has no special closest point.
