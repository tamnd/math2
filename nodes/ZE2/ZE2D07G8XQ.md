## Example

Fix a prime $p$, and work in the signature of rings $\set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$.
Let $\mathcal{F}_p$ be the class of [[finite fields|08RTDHX1SK]] of characteristic $p$.
We show that the [[algebraic closure|0MCXX29HWY]] $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is the [[Fraïssé limit|HJVRBDQSGG]] of $\mathcal{F}_p$.

1. $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is countable, being a countable union of finite fields.
2. Its finitely generated subrings are finite, since each element is algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p$. A finite subring of a field is a field.
3. Every finite field of characteristic $p$ embeds into $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$, so the age is $\mathcal{F}_p$.
4. For each $n$, $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ has exactly one subfield of order $p^n$, by the theory of [[subfields of a finite field|08YERZDM9X]].
5. So an isomorphism $q \colon A \to B$ of finite subfields has $A = B$, and $q$ is an isomorphism of fields contained in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$.
6. An isomorphism between subfields extends to an automorphism of an algebraic closure, by [[uniqueness of the algebraic closure|01VQ7C4FTB]].
7. So $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is ultrahomogeneous with age $\mathcal{F}_p$, hence it is the limit.

## Remarks

The class $\mathcal{F}_p$ has the [[amalgamation property|XSCSX39GEZ]], as Fraïssé's theorem predicts: embed both fields into $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ compatibly over the base.
The automorphism group of $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is the profinite completion $\widehat{\Z}$, topologically generated by the Frobenius map $x \mapsto x^p$.
The union of all finite fields of all characteristics is not a Fraïssé class, since it fails JEP.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The finite fields of characteristic $p$ form a tower: $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$ sits inside $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ when $m$ divides $n$.
Putting them all together gives one countable field that contains every finite field of characteristic $p$.
Any way of matching two of its finite subfields comes from a symmetry of the whole field.

### L2 human

We look at automorphisms of $\mathbb{F}_4 \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{F}}_2$.

1. $\mathbb{F}_4 = \set{0, 1, \omega, \omega + 1}$ with $\omega^2 = \omega + 1$.
2. The Frobenius map $x \mapsto x^2$ swaps $\omega$ and $\omega + 1$.
3. The Frobenius map on $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_2$ is an automorphism that extends this swap.

The typical use is as an algebraic example of a Fraïssé limit, where the signature has function symbols.
A trap is to include fields of different characteristics, which breaks JEP.
