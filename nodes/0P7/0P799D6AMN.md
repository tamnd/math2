## Definition

Let $F$ be a field and let $V$ be a [[vector space|000AYPA9AJ]] over $F$.
A *bilinear form* on $V$ is a map $B \colon V \times V \to F$ such that, for all $u, v, w \in V$ and $a \in F$:

1. $B(u + v, w) = B(u, w) + B(v, w)$ and $B(au, w) = a B(u, w)$.
2. $B(u, v + w) = B(u, v) + B(u, w)$ and $B(u, aw) = a B(u, w)$.

Condition 1 says that $B$ is linear in the first slot, and condition 2 says that $B$ is linear in the second slot.

## Notation

We write $B(u, v)$, or $\langle u, v \rangle$ when only one form is present.
Mathlib writes `LinearMap.BilinForm F V` for the type of bilinear forms, and `B u v` for the value.

## Remarks

The [[dot product|0S52TNNTH9]] on $F^n$ is a bilinear form.
For each $n \times n$ matrix $M$ over $F$, the map $B(x, y) = x^T M y$ is a bilinear form on $F^n$.
The bilinear forms on $V$ form a vector space over $F$, and if $\dim V = n$, this space has dimension $n^2$.
A bilinear map $V \times W \to F$ on two different spaces is a bilinear pairing.
The concept page [[bilinear form|0SQZMXB6GP]] gives examples and the main properties.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A bilinear form takes two vectors and gives a number.
It is linear in the first vector and also linear in the second vector.
The dot product is the main example, but bilinear forms need not be symmetric or positive.

### L2 human

To check that a map $B$ is bilinear, fix $v$ and check that $u \mapsto B(u, v)$ is linear, then fix $u$ and check that $v \mapsto B(u, v)$ is linear.
For $B(x, y) = x_1 y_2$ on $F^2$, both maps are linear, so $B$ is bilinear.
A trap: $B(x, y) = x_1 y_1 + 1$ fails, because $B(0, y) = 1 \neq 0$.
