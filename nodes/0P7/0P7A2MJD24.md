## Definition

The coefficient matrix of a linear system is the table of its coefficients, with one row for each equation and one column for each unknown.
Let $F$ be a [[field|04C5ZBG1WT]].
Take a [[system of linear equations|0GT4WWGWH2]] over $F$ with $m$ equations in $n$ unknowns, where equation $i$ is $a_{i1} x_1 + \cdots + a_{in} x_n = b_i$.
The *coefficient matrix* of the system is the $m \times n$ [[matrix|0VD9ZSWWJG]] $A = (a_{ij})$.
Its entry in row $i$ and column $j$ is the coefficient of $x_j$ in equation $i$.
When $x_j$ does not appear in equation $i$, the entry $a_{ij}$ is $0$.

## Notation

With $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)^{\mathsf{T}}$ and $b = (b_1, \dots, b_m)^{\mathsf{T}}$, the system is the matrix equation $Ax = b$.
Here $Ax$ is the [[matrix-vector product|0PDVQEP0ZC]], whose entry $i$ is $a_{i1} x_1 + \cdots + a_{in} x_n$.
Some books write $[a_{ij}]$ or $(a_{ij})_{1 \le i \le m,\, 1 \le j \le n}$ for $A$.

## Remarks

The definition takes the equations in the standard form, with all unknowns on the left in the fixed order $x_1, \dots, x_n$.
This is why the column index $j$ always means the same unknown, in every row.
For $x + y = 2x + 1$, $3y = x - 2$, you first rewrite the equations as $-x + y = 1$ and $-x + 3y = -2$, and only then read off $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

The rule for missing unknowns keeps the matrix rectangular.
For example, $x_1 - x_3 = 7$ in the unknowns $x_1, x_2, x_3$ has the $1 \times 3$ coefficient matrix $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$, and without the $0$ the column of $x_3$ would be in the wrong place.

The constants $b_i$ are not part of the coefficient matrix.
For $2x + y = 5$, $x - 3y = 0$ the coefficient matrix is $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$, and the matrix with the extra column $(5, 0)$ is the [[augmented matrix|0T6MWGZSXA]].
The concept page on the [[coefficient matrix|0HQFZAP3ND]] explains what the matrix decides about the solutions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The coefficient matrix keeps only the numbers in front of the unknowns, in a rectangular table.
Each row is one equation, and each column is one unknown.
For $2x + y = 5$ and $x - 3y = 0$, the first row is $2, 1$ and the second row is $1, -3$.

### L2 human

To write $x_1 - 2x_3 = 4$, $3x_2 + x_1 = 0$ as $Ax = b$, put the unknowns in the order $x_1, x_2, x_3$ and fill in zeros:
$$x_1 + 0x_2 - 2x_3 = 4, \qquad x_1 + 3x_2 + 0x_3 = 0.$$
So $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $b = (4, 0)^{\mathsf{T}}$.
Watch out: the second equation lists $x_2$ before $x_1$, but its row must still follow the fixed order, so it is $(1, 3, 0)$ and not $(3, 1, 0)$.

### L3 human

The coefficient matrix is the matrix of the [[linear map|0NRYHJPQG2]] $F^n \to F^m$, $x \mapsto Ax$, in the standard bases.
So existence of solutions is a question about the image of this map, and uniqueness is a question about its kernel.
