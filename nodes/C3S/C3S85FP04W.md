## Definition

Let $c \ge 2$ and $n \ge 1$ be integers, and let $0 < \alpha \le 1$ and $\beta > c/2$ be reals.
Let $G$ be a [[bipartite graph|0ZVQKTRKVQ]] with left vertex set $V = \set{1, \dots, n}$ and right vertex set $U$ such that:

1. every left vertex has exactly $c$ neighbours;
2. every set $S \subseteq V$ with $\abs{S} \le \alpha n$ has at least $\beta \abs{S}$ neighbours in $U$.

The *expander code* of $G$ is the binary [[linear code|E85KREWV60]]
$$C(G) = \set{x \in \F_2^n : \sum_{i \in N(u)} x_i = 0 \text{ for every } u \in U},$$
where $N(u)$ is the set of neighbours of $u$.
In other words, $G$ is the [[Tanner graph|GTDMNAMPD9]] of $C(G)$.

## Remarks

The code has rate at least $1 - \abs{U}/n$.
Its [[minimum distance|M2X88AXM17]] exceeds $\alpha n$.
Indeed, let $x \ne 0$ be a codeword with support $S$ and suppose $\abs{S} \le \alpha n$.
Each neighbour of $S$ must see an even, hence at least $2$, number of edges from $S$.
There are $c \abs{S}$ such edges, so $S$ has at most $c \abs{S}/2 < \beta \abs{S}$ neighbours, against clause 2.
A more general version, due to Tanner, replaces each parity check by a small inner code on the neighbours.

Example: the left vertices are the $6$ edges of the complete graph $K_4$ and the right vertices its $4$ vertices, each edge joined to its two endpoints, so $c = 2$.
One edge has $2$ endpoints and two edges have at least $3$, so clause 2 holds with $\alpha n = 2$ and $\beta = 3/2 > 1$.
The code consists of the edge sets with even degree at every vertex, and its minimum distance $3 > \alpha n$ is the length of a triangle.

Non-example: in the same graph, a triangle is a set of $3$ edges with only $3$ endpoints.
So clause 2 fails for $\abs{S} = 3$ with any $\beta > 1$, and indeed the triangle is a codeword of weight $3$.
