## Summary

The escape velocity at a point is the least speed that a body needs there to move away from a massive body for ever.
No further push is needed after that.
At distance $r$ from the centre of a spherically symmetric body of mass $M$ it equals $\sqrt{2 G M / r}$.
It separates bound elliptic orbits from open parabolic and hyperbolic ones.
See the [[definition|HZJBTDPJAS]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Throw a ball up and it falls back; throw it faster and it rises higher.
Above a certain speed it never comes back: gravity slows it forever but never stops it.
That threshold is the escape velocity.
At the surface of the Earth it is about $11.2$ km/s, whatever the direction of the throw, if air is ignored.
