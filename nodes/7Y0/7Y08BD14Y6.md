## Example

Let $\varphi_1(x) = x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + x_4$ and $\varphi_2(x) = x_1 - x_2$ be [[linear forms|0RT96J4D3H]] on $\R^4$.
Let $U = \ker \varphi_1$ and $W = \ker \varphi_2$ be their [[kernels|02YFME3RGW]].
We compute the [[codimensions|JJCGJ1EGCQ]] of $U$, of $W$ and of $U \cap W$.

1. The form $\varphi_1$ is onto $\R$, since $\varphi_1(t, 0, 0, 0) = t$.
   So $\R^4 / U \cong \R$ by the first isomorphism theorem, and $\operatorname{codim} U = 1$.
   In the same way $\varphi_2(t, 0, 0, 0) = t$, so $\operatorname{codim} W = 1$.
2. The two hyperplanes are different.
   The vector $(1, -1, 0, 0)$ lies in $U$, since $1 - 1 + 0 + 0 = 0$, but $\varphi_2(1, -1, 0, 0) = 2 \ne 0$, so it is not in $W$.
3. So $U + W$ strictly contains $U$.
   Its dimension is more than $3$, so $U + W = \R^4$.
4. The equality case of the [[codimension of an intersection|G7DHT62D9F]] now gives $\operatorname{codim}(U \cap W) = 1 + 1 = 2$.
5. We check this directly with the map $\psi(x) = (\varphi_1(x), \varphi_2(x))$ from $\R^4$ to $\R^2$, whose kernel is $U \cap W$.
   It is onto, because for each $(a, b)$
$$\psi\Bigl(\tfrac{a + b}{2}, \tfrac{a - b}{2}, 0, 0\Bigr) = \Bigl(\tfrac{a + b}{2} + \tfrac{a - b}{2},\ \tfrac{a + b}{2} - \tfrac{a - b}{2}\Bigr) = (a, b).$$
   So $\R^4 / (U \cap W) \cong \R^2$, and the codimension is $2$.
6. Last, the vectors $(1, 1, -2, 0)$ and $(1, 1, 0, -2)$ satisfy both $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + x_4 = 0$ and $x_1 = x_2$, and they are not proportional.
   Every solution has the form $(s, s, t, -2s - t)$, which equals $-\tfrac{t}{2}(1, 1, -2, 0) + \tfrac{2s + t}{2}(1, 1, 0, -2)$.
   So they form a basis of $U \cap W$, and $\dim(U \cap W) = 2 = 4 - 2$.

## Remarks

### What the quotient looks like

The form $\varphi_1$ identifies the [[quotient space|0SVR29SV1V]] $\R^4 / U$ with $\R$.
The coset that corresponds to $c \in \R$ is the parallel hyperplane $\{x : \varphi_1(x) = c\}$.
In the same way, $\psi$ identifies $\R^4 / (U \cap W)$ with $\R^2$, and each coset of $U \cap W$ is the plane where $\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_2$ take two fixed values.

### When the forms are proportional

Replace $\varphi_2$ by $2 \varphi_1$.
Then $W = U$, the sum $U + W$ is only $U$, and the intersection $U \cap W = U$ has codimension $1$, not $2$.
For two forms, the codimensions add exactly when the forms are not proportional, which for two nonzero forms is the same as being independent.

### The same picture in infinite dimension

In $\R[x]$, the forms $p \mapsto p(0)$ and $p \mapsto p(1)$ are not proportional, since the polynomial $x$ gives the values $0$ and $1$.
So their kernels are two different hyperplanes, and $\{p : p(0) = p(1) = 0\}$ has codimension $2$ by the same argument.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Each hyperplane in $\R^4$ is described by one linear equation.
Two equations that really differ cut out a smaller piece that misses two directions.
Here the piece is the set of points with $x_1 = x_2$ whose four coordinates add up to $0$, and it is a plane.

### L2 human

The test in step 2 is the heart of the computation.
Two nonzero forms with the same kernel are proportional, so exhibiting a vector in one kernel and not in the other proves that the forms are independent.
Once the forms are independent, the map $\psi$ is onto $\R^2$ and the codimensions add.
The typical use is to count the dimension of a solution space without solving the system: here $4 - 2 = 2$.
Watch out: the count fails for dependent equations, such as $\varphi_1$ and $2 \varphi_1$.
