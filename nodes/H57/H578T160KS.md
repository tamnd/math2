## Summary

A matrix product state is a vector in the state space of a chain of sites whose coefficients are products of matrices.
There is one matrix for each site and each local basis state.
The size of the matrices, the bond dimension, bounds the entanglement between the two parts of the chain on either side of a cut.
Ground states of gapped one-dimensional Hamiltonians are well approximated by matrix product states of small bond dimension.
This makes them the basis of numerical methods such as the density matrix renormalization group.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A chain of $N$ sites with $d$ states each has $d^N$ coefficients, far too many to store for large $N$.
A matrix product state replaces this list by $N$ small families of matrices, and recovers each coefficient as a product of $N$ matrices.
The size $D$ of the matrices measures how much the two halves of the chain may be entangled.
With $D = 1$ the matrices are numbers, and the state is a product state with no entanglement at all.
The state $\lvert 00\cdots0\rangle + \lvert 11\cdots1\rangle$ already needs $D = 2$.
