## Summary

Quantum chaos studies quantum systems whose classical limit is a chaotic dynamical system.
It asks how the chaos shows up in the spectrum and the eigenfunctions.
One typical question is the distribution of high-energy eigenfunctions, as in [[quantum ergodicity|D9W79NKYZJ]] and the [[quantum unique ergodicity conjecture|F3EB7WZFAC]].
Another is the statistics of the spacings between eigenvalues, which are expected to follow [[random matrix|1HS4RT7HK5]] laws.
Its tools are semiclassical analysis, trace formulas such as the [[Selberg trace formula|BDAWKPBNQP]], and ergodic theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A billiard ball on a stadium-shaped table moves chaotically: two nearby starts soon lead to completely different paths.
What does a quantum particle in the same stadium do?
Quantum mechanics has no trajectories, so the chaos must show up elsewhere: in the patterns of the standing waves and in the spacings of the energy levels.
For chaotic shapes the standing waves tend to spread evenly over the table, and the levels tend to repel each other, like eigenvalues of a random matrix.
