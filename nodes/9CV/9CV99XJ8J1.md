## Example

Let $\tau$ be the formula $(a \land b) \lor \neg (a \land b)$.
We prove it in [[extended Frege|5YSE04SHFH]] over a fixed [[Frege system|J2BAR09TPF]] $\mathcal F$, with one extension variable $q$ as a name for $a \land b$.
Each line below stands for a fixed block of $\mathcal F$-lines.

1. $q \leftrightarrow (a \land b)$, an extension axiom.
   The variable $q$ does not occur in $a \land b$, it occurs in no earlier line, and it does not occur in $\tau$.
2. $q \lor \neg q$.
   This line ends a fixed $\mathcal F$-proof of $p \lor \neg p$, with the [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]] $p \mapsto q$ applied to all its lines.
3. $(q \leftrightarrow (a \land b)) \to ((q \lor \neg q) \to \tau)$.
   The formula $(r \leftrightarrow s) \to ((r \lor \neg r) \to (s \lor \neg s))$ is a tautology, so $\mathcal F$ has a fixed proof of it, and the substitution $r \mapsto q$, $s \mapsto a \land b$ turns it into a proof of line 3.
4. $(q \lor \neg q) \to \tau$, by modus ponens from lines 1 and 3.
5. $\tau$, by modus ponens from lines 2 and 4.

Each block uses a fixed number of $\mathcal F$-lines, so the whole proof has a constant number of lines, and its size is a constant too.
The last line contains no extension variable, as the definition requires.

## Remarks

Why is the proof sound?
Extend any assignment to $a$ and $b$ by $q := a \land b$.
Then line 1 is true, every rule preserves truth, and so all later lines are true.

In this small case the extension saves nothing, because $\tau$ is itself an instance of $p \lor \neg p$ under $p \mapsto a \land b$, and Frege proves it directly.
The saving appears when a long formula is named once and then used in many lines, as in [[the carry example|MKDZSQM3KE]], where a name of size $1$ replaces a formula of size $2^i$ or more.
Watch out for line 3: without it, the proof would end at $q \lor \neg q$, which mentions $q$, and that is not allowed as a last line.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The proof says "let $q$ mean $a$ and $b$".
Then it proves "$q$ or not $q$", which is true whatever $q$ is.
Finally it puts the meaning of $q$ back in and gets the formula $\tau$.

### L2 human

We check the three conditions on $q$ in line 1.

1. The defining formula $a \land b$ has only the variables $a$ and $b$, so $q$ does not occur in it.
2. Line 1 is the first line, so $q$ occurs in no earlier line.
3. The variables of $\tau$ are $a$ and $b$, so $q$ does not occur in $\tau$.

All three hold, so line 1 is an extension axiom.
The typical use of an extension axiom is to name a subformula once and reuse the name, and then to remove the name before the end, here by line 3.
