## Example

Let $\tau$ be the formula $(a \land b) \lor \neg (a \land b)$.
Here is the plan of an [[extended Frege|5YSE04SHFH]] proof of $\tau$ over a fixed [[Frege system|J2BAR09TPF]] $\mathcal F$.

1. $q \leftrightarrow (a \land b)$. This is an extension axiom. The variable $q$ does not occur in $a \land b$, it occurs in no earlier line, and it does not occur in $\tau$.
2. $q \lor \neg q$. This line ends a fixed $\mathcal F$-proof of $p \lor \neg p$, with the [[substitution|NCDZ969V7X]] $p \mapsto q$ applied to all its lines.
3. $(q \leftrightarrow (a \land b)) \to ((q \lor \neg q) \to \tau)$. The formula $(r \leftrightarrow s) \to ((r \lor \neg r) \to (s \lor \neg s))$ is a tautology, so $\mathcal F$ has a fixed proof of it. The substitution $r \mapsto q$, $s \mapsto a \land b$ turns that proof into a proof of line 3.
4. $(q \lor \neg q) \to \tau$, by modus ponens from lines 1 and 3.
5. $\tau$, by modus ponens from lines 2 and 4.

Each part of the plan uses a fixed number of lines, so the whole proof has a constant number of lines.
The final formula $\tau$ contains no extension variable, as the definition requires.

## Remarks

For soundness, extend any assignment to $a$ and $b$ by $q := a \land b$.
Then line 1 is true, and so are all later lines.

In this small case the extension saves nothing, since $\tau$ is itself an instance of $p \lor \neg p$.
The saving appears when a long formula is named once and then used in many lines, as in the [[carry example|MKDZSQM3KE]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The proof says "let $q$ mean $a$ and $b$".
Then it proves "$q$ or not $q$", which is true for every $q$.
Finally it replaces $q$ by its meaning and gets the formula $\tau$.

### L2 human

We check the three conditions on $q$ for line 1.

1. The defining formula $a \land b$ has the variables $a$ and $b$ only, so $q$ does not occur in it.
2. Line 1 is the first line, so $q$ occurs in no earlier line.
3. The variables of $\tau$ are $a$ and $b$, so $q$ does not occur in $\tau$.

The typical use of an extension axiom is to name a subformula once and reuse the name.
A trap: the name $q$ must be eliminated before the last line, here by line 3.
