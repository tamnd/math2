## Summary

The expected utility hypothesis says that a decision maker ranks risky prospects by the [[expected value|0WQNRCNY3M]] of a utility of their outcomes.
The utility is a function on the outcomes, and only its probability-weighted average matters for the choice.
It is the standard model of choice under risk, and its axioms are given by the von Neumann–Morgenstern utility theorem.
The shape of the utility function then describes the attitude to risk.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Imagine a choice between a sure payment and a gamble.
The hypothesis says that the person gives each possible outcome a personal value, weighs these values by their probabilities, and picks the option with the higher total.
For example, with a utility of $\sqrt{x}$ for $x$ euros, a fair coin flip between $0$ and $100$ euros is worth $\tfrac12 \cdot 0 + \tfrac12 \cdot 10 = 5$ units, the same as a sure $25$ euros.
So this person takes a sure $30$ euros over the gamble, even though the gamble has the higher expected payment of $50$ euros.
