## Statement

Let $H$ be a separable infinite-dimensional complex [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]].
Let $\operatorname{Fred}(H)$ be the space of [[Fredholm operators|8HWGKWD6ZM]] on $H$ with the [[operator norm|06EKW1AKXF]] topology.
Let $X$ be a [[compact|0TVJZYB6SH]] Hausdorff space, and let $[X, \operatorname{Fred}(H)]$ be the set of [[homotopy|9YA6S6HD9V]] classes of continuous maps $X \to \operatorname{Fred}(H)$.
For a continuous map $f \colon X \to \operatorname{Fred}(H)$, there is a closed subspace $V \subseteq H$ of finite codimension with $V \cap \ker f(x) = 0$ for all $x \in X$.
The spaces $H / f(x)(V)$ then form a vector bundle $H/f(V)$ over $X$, and the *index bundle* of $f$ is
$$\operatorname{ind}(f) = [X \times (H/V)] - [H/f(V)] \in K^0(X).$$
The class $\operatorname{ind}(f)$ does not depend on $V$, and it depends only on the homotopy class of $f$.
The theorem states that
$$\operatorname{ind} \colon [X, \operatorname{Fred}(H)] \to K^0(X)$$
is a bijection, where $K^0$ is [[complex K-theory|9E4JE4902G]].

## Remarks

For $X$ a point, the index bundle is the [[Fredholm index|2V9Q4KHY1T]] $\dim \ker T - \dim \operatorname{coker} T$.
The theorem then says that the connected components of $\operatorname{Fred}(H)$ are exactly the sets of operators of a given index.

The set $[X, \operatorname{Fred}(H)]$ is a monoid under pointwise composition of operators, and $\operatorname{ind}$ turns composition into addition, so the bijection is an isomorphism of groups.
Surjectivity is proved by realizing a class $[E] - [\C^n]$ through an explicit family of operators.
Injectivity uses [[Kuiper's theorem|9SJ4SC9FQX]], that the group of invertible operators on $H$ is contractible, to deform a family of index zero to a family of invertible operators.

The theorem says that $\operatorname{Fred}(H)$ is a classifying space for complex K-theory, homotopy equivalent to $\Z \times BU$.
It was proved independently by Atiyah and by Jänich.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Fredholm operator is an operator on an infinite-dimensional space that is invertible up to finite-dimensional errors, measured by its kernel and cokernel.
For one operator, the index, the dimension of the kernel minus that of the cokernel, is the only invariant under deformation.
For a family of Fredholm operators over a space, the kernels and cokernels jump around, but their formal difference is a well-defined element of K-theory.
The theorem says that K-theory is exactly what one gets this way.

### L2 human

The index bundle is the natural home for the index of a family of elliptic operators, as in the families index theorem.
The theorem gives an analytic model of K-theory, which makes it possible to define twisted K-theory by twisting bundles of Fredholm operators.
It also explains why index theory and K-theory are so closely linked.
