## Summary

A representative elementary volume, also called a representative volume element, is a sample of a heterogeneous material.
It is the smallest sample whose averaged response stands for the material as a whole.
It must be large compared to the grains, fibres or pores.
It must be small compared to the size of the body and to the scale on which the loads vary.
Effective properties used in [[homogenization|5RSREPYN0F]] and in multiscale simulations are computed on such a volume.
For a periodic microstructure, one period cell plays this role exactly.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A cube of sandstone the size of a sand grain is either all grain or all pore, so its porosity is $1$ or $0$.
A cube a few centimetres wide contains many thousands of grains, and its porosity hardly changes when the cube is moved or enlarged.
That is the size of a representative elementary volume.
Below that size the measured property jumps around; above it the property has settled, and the material can be treated as uniform.
