## Summary

Macaulay's method finds the deflection of a beam with several point loads by writing the [[bending moment|Q6XAPXMGH0]] as one formula valid along the whole beam.
It uses brackets $\langle x - a \rangle^n$ that are zero to the left of $a$ and equal to $(x - a)^n$ to the right.
The [[Euler–Bernoulli beam equation|13FGKM7NQ0]] is then integrated twice as a single equation, with only two constants of integration.
This avoids writing a separate equation on each segment between loads and matching the pieces.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When a beam carries several loads, the formula for the bending moment changes each time one passes a load.
Normally one would solve a separate small problem on each piece and then glue the pieces together.
Macaulay's trick is a bracket that switches a term on only after its load, so one formula covers the whole beam.
Integrating this one formula twice gives the bent shape everywhere at once.
