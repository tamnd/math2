## Summary

Tidal acceleration is the slow exchange of angular momentum between the spin of a planet and the orbit of a satellite.
It is caused by the tides that the satellite raises on the planet.
For the Earth and the Moon it lengthens the day and pushes the Moon outward.
Lunar laser ranging shows that the Moon recedes by about $3.8$ cm per year.
Tides raised on a moon by its planet act in the same way and make many moons always show the same face.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Moon raises a bulge on the Earth, but the Earth spins faster than the Moon goes around it.
Friction drags the bulge a little ahead of the line to the Moon.
The bulge pulls the Moon forward along its orbit, so the Moon slowly climbs to a higher orbit.
In return the Moon pulls back on the bulge, so the Earth's spin slows down and the day gets longer.
The total [[angular momentum|7147M98XNR]] stays the same; it only moves from the Earth's spin to the Moon's orbit.
