## Definition

*Physical cosmology* is the part of astrophysics whose object of study is the universe as a whole, on scales much larger than galaxies.
Its models have three parts.

1. A background spacetime that obeys the [[cosmological principle|Q8MSCRDFAT]]: the [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric|ECJMS4VDTF]], whose scale factor evolves by the [[Friedmann equations|PPHFS2P4G4]] for given matter and energy contents.
2. Small deviations from homogeneity and their growth, described by [[cosmological perturbation theory|XMH5RQCJYT]] and, in the nonlinear regime, by simulations.
3. The physics of the contents, such as nuclear reactions, radiation and atoms, in this expanding background, which predicts observable quantities.

## Remarks

Example: the prediction of the abundance of helium from nuclear reactions in the first minutes is a question of physical cosmology.
It uses all three parts.

Non-example: the structure of a single star does not belong to physical cosmology; it does not depend on the expansion or on the large-scale state of the universe.

The current standard model of physical cosmology is the $\Lambda$CDM model, with a cosmological constant, cold dark matter, ordinary matter and radiation.
