## Summary

A hidden-variable theory is a model of quantum systems with a more detailed physical state, the hidden variable.
Each preparation of a system produces such a hidden variable, which carries more information than the quantum state.
The quantum probabilities arise from averaging over the hidden variable with a probability distribution fixed by the preparation.
No-go theorems, such as Bell's theorem and the Kochen–Specker theorem, show which kinds of hidden-variable theories cannot reproduce quantum mechanics.
De Broglie–Bohm theory is a hidden-variable theory that does reproduce it, at the price of nonlocality.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A coin toss looks random, but its outcome is fixed by details of the throw that we do not track.
A hidden-variable theory proposes the same for quantum mechanics: behind the quantum state there is a fuller description that fixes or biases the outcomes.
Quantum randomness would then come only from our ignorance of these hidden details.
Such theories are possible, but only if they have unusual features such as nonlocality or contextuality.
