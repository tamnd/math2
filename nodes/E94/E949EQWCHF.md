## Summary

Finite strain theory describes deformations of a body with no assumption that displacements or rotations are small.
Its kinematic tools are the [[deformation gradient|HTYRR2WDQ6]], the [[Cauchy–Green tensors|N5G8WNQFC3]] and finite strain measures such as the [[Green–Lagrange tensor|MRANPXATRD]].
It is needed for rubber, soft tissue, metal forming and any problem where shapes change a lot.
It is the kinematic basis of nonlinear elasticity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stretch a rubber band to twice its length: the strain is not small, and formulas made for tiny strains give wrong answers.
Finite strain theory keeps all terms and makes no small-strain shortcuts.
It also distinguishes carefully between the body before and after the deformation, because the two shapes can be very different.
