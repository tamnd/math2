## Summary

Etendue measures how spread out a beam of light is, in area and in angle together.
It is the volume that the beam occupies in the phase space of [[Hamiltonian optics|T56WHM058J]].
In a lossless optical system etendue cannot decrease, and in an ideal system it is conserved.
This limits how far light can be concentrated, and it explains why no lens can make an image brighter than the source.
See the [[definition|DNY1K38NX9]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A lens can squeeze the light of a lamp into a small spot, but then the rays arrive from a wide range of angles.
Etendue is the product of the area and the spread of angles, suitably measured.
Shrinking one makes the other grow, so the product cannot be reduced.
This is why a magnifying glass cannot make a spot hotter than the surface of the Sun.
