## Statement

Let a mechanism consist of $N$ rigid links, one of which is fixed (the ground), joined by $j$ joints.
Joint number $i$ allows $f_i$ [[degrees of freedom|21SZKJ4XCK]] of relative motion between the two links it joins.
The *mobility* of the mechanism is the number
$$M = 6\,(N - 1 - j) + \sum_{i=1}^{j} f_i$$
for a mechanism in space, and
$$M = 3\,(N - 1 - j) + \sum_{i=1}^{j} f_i$$
for a mechanism in the plane.
Suppose the constraint equations of the joints are independent at a configuration, that is, their Jacobian has full rank there.
Then near that configuration the [[configuration space|MGYKGP7PW3]] of the mechanism is a smooth manifold of dimension $M$.

## Remarks

Idea: each moving link has $6$ degrees of freedom in space ($3$ in the plane), giving $6(N - 1)$ in total.
A joint with $f_i$ freedoms imposes $6 - f_i$ scalar equations ($3 - f_i$ in the plane).
Subtracting gives $M$, and the implicit function theorem turns the rank condition into the manifold statement.

Example: a planar four-bar linkage has $N = 4$ links and $j = 4$ revolute joints with $f_i = 1$, so $M = 3(4 - 1 - 4) + 4 = 1$: it moves with one degree of freedom.
A triangle of three links pinned at three joints has $M = 3(3 - 1 - 3) + 3 = 0$ and is rigid.

The independence hypothesis is needed.
In special geometries the constraints become dependent and the true mobility exceeds $M$.
Take a parallelogram four-bar linkage and add a third crank, equal and parallel to the two cranks, joining ground and coupler.
It has $N = 5$ and $j = 6$, so $M = 0$ by the count, yet it moves.
Such mechanisms are called overconstrained.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The criterion counts how many independent ways a mechanism can move.
Start with all the freedom the separate pieces would have, then subtract what each joint takes away.
A hinge in the plane, for example, leaves only one way for two pieces to move relative to each other.
The count is right for typical shapes but can fail for very symmetric ones.

### L2 human

Designers use the formula as a first check: $M = 1$ means one motor can drive the mechanism, $M = 0$ means a structure, and $M < 0$ means a statically indeterminate structure.
The count is a statement about generic configurations, in the same spirit as counting equations and unknowns.
Overconstrained exceptions in space include the Bennett linkage, a four-bar spatial loop with $M = -2$ by the count that still moves.
The finer analysis of such cases uses [[screw theory|NVBD7Y86ES]].
