## Statement

Model a lumped electrical circuit by a finite [[directed graph|0KFYGSHHF5]] $G = (V, E)$.
The vertices are the nodes, and each edge $e$ is a branch with a chosen reference direction from its tail to its head.
Each branch carries a current $i_e \in \R$, measured in the reference direction, and a voltage $v_e \in \R$, the potential drop from tail to head.
The branch currents and voltages satisfy the following two laws.

1. Current law: for every node $x \in V$, $\sum_{e \text{ leaves } x} i_e - \sum_{e \text{ enters } x} i_e = 0$.
2. Voltage law: for every [[cycle|0Z05VCBQ7Y]] of the underlying undirected graph, traversed in one direction, $\sum_{e} \pm v_e = 0$.
   The sign is $+$ if the traversal follows the direction of $e$ and $-$ otherwise.

## Remarks

The current law expresses conservation of charge: no charge piles up at a node.
The voltage law expresses that the voltage comes from a potential.
Let $G$ be connected.
Then the voltage law holds if and only if there is a function $\varphi \colon V \to \R$ with $v_e = \varphi(\text{tail}(e)) - \varphi(\text{head}(e))$ for all $e$.
The function $\varphi$ is unique up to an additive constant.
In matrix form, with the incidence matrix $A$ of $G$, the current law says $A i = 0$ and the voltage law says $v = A^{T} \varphi$.
The laws say nothing about the elements in the branches.
Together with a law for each element, such as Ohm's law $v_e = r_e i_e$, they determine the state of the circuit.
For example, take a single loop of a battery of voltage $E$ and two resistors $r_1, r_2$.
The current law forces one current $i$ around the loop, and the voltage law gives $E = r_1 i + r_2 i$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The current law says that whatever current flows into a junction must flow out again.
The voltage law says that the voltage rises and drops around any closed loop of the circuit add up to zero.
In the same way, a walk around a loop on a hill brings you back to the same height.
These two rules, plus a rule for each component, are all you need to compute the currents in a circuit.

### L2 human

The current law lives on vertices and the voltage law lives on cycles, which is the duality between the cut space and the cycle space of the graph.
Allowed current vectors form the kernel of the incidence matrix, and allowed voltage vectors form the image of its transpose; these two subspaces are orthogonal complements.
This orthogonality is [[Tellegen's theorem|VEFHHSP35P]].
[[Nodal analysis|9NW7KCBSME]] uses the potential $\varphi$ as unknown, and [[mesh analysis|5KKY8RPFB4]] uses loop currents; each builds in one law and imposes the other.
On an [[electrical network|T7KRE7J3B4]] of resistors, the current law at a node without sources says that the potential is a weighted mean of its neighbours.
