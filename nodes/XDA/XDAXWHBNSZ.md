## Summary

A simple Lie algebra is a non-abelian [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] whose only [[ideals|JWMTXQ8A3A]] are $0$ and itself.
Simple Lie algebras are the building blocks of semisimple Lie algebras, which are direct sums of them in characteristic $0$.
Over $\C$ the finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras are classified: four infinite families $A_n$, $B_n$, $C_n$, $D_n$ and five exceptional algebras $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, $F_4$, $G_2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A simple Lie algebra cannot be broken into smaller pieces by dividing out an ideal.
It plays the role that prime numbers play for integers, or simple groups for finite groups.
The smallest example is the three-dimensional algebra of $2 \times 2$ complex matrices with trace zero.
The one-dimensional Lie algebra also has no ideals other than $0$ and itself, but it is commutative, and by convention it is not called simple.
