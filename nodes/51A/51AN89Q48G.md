## Example

The following tree claims to derive $\exists x\, P(x) \to \forall y\, P(y)$ with no open assumptions, that is, "if something has $P$, then everything has $P$".
We list its nodes.

1. $[\exists x\, P(x)]^1$ is an assumption with label 1.
2. $[P(y)]^2$ is an assumption with label 2, of the form $P(x)[y/x]$.
3. $P(y)$ is claimed by ${\exists}\mathrm{E}^2$ from lines 1 and 2, with eigenvariable $y$ and conclusion $\chi = P(y)$.
4. $\forall y\, P(y)$ follows by ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ from line 3, since the only open assumption is line 1 and $y$ is not free in it.
5. $\exists x\, P(x) \to \forall y\, P(y)$ follows by ${\to}\mathrm{I}^1$.

We check that the tree is not a derivation, and that its conclusion is not valid.

1. The eigenvariable condition of ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ in the [[definition of natural deduction|PAQ4ZX2TX1]] requires that $y$ is not free in the conclusion $\chi$.
2. Here $\chi = P(y)$, and $y$ is free in it.
   So line 3 breaks the condition, and the tree is not a derivation.
3. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be the [[structure|983GZBQYT4]] with domain $\{0, 1\}$ in which $P$ holds of $0$ only.
4. $\mathcal{M} \models \exists x\, P(x)$, with the witness $0$, and $\mathcal{M} \not\models \forall y\, P(y)$, because $P$ fails at $1$.
5. So the conclusion is false in $\mathcal{M}$, and by [[soundness|4A84VHDTHV]] no correct derivation has this conclusion.

## Remarks

### What goes wrong

Line 3 lets the name of the witness escape from the ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ step.
Inside that step, $y$ names one particular object with $P$.
Outside it, ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ treats $y$ as an arbitrary object, and the two readings clash.
Lines 4 and 5 are correct steps on their own; the whole error sits in line 3.

### What changes when we change the example

With the conclusion $\chi = \exists z\, P(z)$ in place of $P(y)$, the step ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ is correct, and the tree derives only $\exists x\, P(x) \to \exists z\, P(z)$, which is valid.
In a structure with one element the bad conclusion is true, so a countermodel needs at least two elements.
The same kind of error with ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ in place of ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ derives $P(y) \to \forall x\, P(x)$; there $y$ is free in an open assumption, as shown under [[natural deduction|6KT3BJZ7D1]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

From "somebody in the room is a doctor" we may say "call this doctor Sam".
We may then argue about Sam, but the final conclusion must not mention Sam.
The faulty tree concludes "Sam is a doctor" outside the argument, and then treats Sam as an arbitrary person.
So it ends with "everybody is a doctor", which does not follow.

### L2 human

To check a step of ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ with eigenvariable $y$, make three checks.

1. Check that $y$ is not free in the major premise $\exists x\, \varphi$; here this holds.
2. Check that $y$ is not free in the conclusion $\chi$; here this fails, so the step is wrong.
3. Check that $y$ is not free in an open assumption other than the discharged leaves $\varphi[y/x]$; here this holds.

Watch out: the check for ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ looks only at open assumptions and the conclusion $\forall x\, \varphi$, so a reader who checks ${\exists}\mathrm{E}$ the same way forgets item 2.
