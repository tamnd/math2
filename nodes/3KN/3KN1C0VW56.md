## Definition

Work on Minkowski space $\R^{1,3}$.
Let $G_{\mathrm{SM}} = SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)$, built from the [[special unitary groups|CZCWFPNS4N]] and the [[unitary group|0R9R0Y1HDK]] $U(1)$.
Write an irreducible [[representation|2K8BZRGE8R]] of $G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ as $(a, b)_Y$.
Here $a$ is the dimension of the $SU(3)$ factor, and $\bar 3$ is the dual of the defining representation.
The number $b$ is the dimension of the $SU(2)$ factor.
The hypercharge $Y$ says that $e^{i\theta} \in U(1)$ acts by $e^{iY\theta}$, up to a fixed normalization.
The *Standard Model* is the [[gauge theory|2GKJQNQMVN]] with gauge group $G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ and the following fields.

1. A connection with values in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{u}(1)$: the gluon, $W$ and $B$ fields.
2. Three generations of left-handed [[Weyl spinor fields|W3GY719S7X]], each generation consisting of $Q \in (3, 2)_{1/6}$, $u^c \in (\bar 3, 1)_{-2/3}$, $d^c \in (\bar 3, 1)_{1/3}$, $L \in (1, 2)_{-1/2}$ and $e^c \in (1, 1)_{1}$.
3. A complex scalar field, the Higgs field $H \in (1, 2)_{1/2}$.

The [[Lagrangian density|SANYJGZ311]] is the most general one that is Lorentz invariant, gauge invariant and renormalizable.
That is, it is a polynomial in the fields and their covariant derivatives of mass dimension at most $4$.
It consists of Yang–Mills terms for the three factors and kinetic terms $i \bar\chi \bar\sigma^\mu D_\mu \chi$ for each Weyl field $\chi$.
It also has the term $(D_\mu H)^\dagger D^\mu H - V(H)$ with $V(H) = -\mu^2 H^\dagger H + \lambda (H^\dagger H)^2$, and Yukawa terms that couple $H$ to pairs of fermion fields.

## Remarks

The electric charge of a field component is $Q_{\mathrm{em}} = T_3 + Y$, where $T_3 = \pm\frac12$ is the weak isospin of the component of a doublet.
For example the two components of $L$ have charges $0$ and $-1$, the neutrino and the electron.
The two components of $Q$ have charges $\frac23$ and $-\frac13$, the up and the down quark.
When $\mu^2 > 0$ the potential $V$ has its minima on the sphere $H^\dagger H = \mu^2 / (2\lambda)$.
Then the [[Higgs mechanism|F985ZCF9SZ]] gives masses to the $W$ and $Z$ bosons and, through the Yukawa terms, to the fermions.

Check of the hypercharges: in each generation the sum of $Y^3$ over all left-handed Weyl fields, counted with multiplicity, is $6 \cdot \frac{1}{216} + 3 \cdot (-\frac{8}{27}) + 3 \cdot \frac{1}{27} + 2 \cdot (-\frac18) + 1 = 0$.
This is one of the conditions for the cancellation of gauge [[anomalies|FGASZ29SFV]].

Non-example: a mass term $m \, L \, e^c$ without the Higgs field is not gauge invariant.
Indeed $L$ is an $SU(2)$ doublet and $e^c$ a singlet, and the hypercharges $-\frac12 + 1$ do not add up to $0$.
The gauge-invariant Yukawa term $y \, H^\dagger L \, e^c$ becomes such a mass term when $H$ takes its vacuum value.

The group that acts faithfully on the fields is the quotient of $G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ by a cyclic subgroup of order $6$.
The model contains no right-handed neutrinos; observed neutrino masses require an extension of it.
