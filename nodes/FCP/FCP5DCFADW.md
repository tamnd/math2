## Summary

The Carter constant is a conserved quantity for free motion in the [[Kerr metric|X3FZC85THY]], found by Brandon Carter in 1968.
Together with the energy, the axial angular momentum and the rest mass, it gives four constants of motion for geodesics.
This makes the geodesic equations of the Kerr spacetime completely integrable.
Unlike energy and axial angular momentum, it does not come from a Killing vector field but from a Killing tensor.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Around a non-rotating star, a planet keeps its total angular momentum, so its orbit stays in a fixed plane.
Around a rotating black hole the orbits are not planar, and the total angular momentum is not conserved.
Carter found a replacement: a quantity built from the momentum that stays constant along every free-fall path.
For a non-rotating black hole it reduces to the square of the total angular momentum minus the square of its component along the axis.
Thanks to it, orbits around rotating black holes can be computed by solving ordinary integrals.
