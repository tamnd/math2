## Summary

The Big Bang is the hot, dense initial state from which the universe has been expanding and cooling.
In the [[Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker|ECJMS4VDTF]] models it appears as a past time at which the [[scale factor|J70F32P86W]] tends to zero.
There the density and the curvature blow up, so it is an initial [[gravitational singularity|45AE2YXWT3]].
The Big Bang model explains the expansion of the universe, the cosmic microwave background and the abundances of the light elements.
The singularity itself marks the limit of classical general relativity rather than an observed event.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Run the expansion of the universe backwards like a film.
Galaxies come closer, matter gets denser and hotter, and at a finite time in the past every distance shrinks to zero.
That limit is the Big Bang.
It is not an explosion at a point in space: it happened everywhere at once, and every place in the universe today was part of it.
In the simplest matter-filled model, the Big Bang is $2/(3H_0)$ in the past, where $H_0$ is the Hubble constant.
