## Summary

Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates are coordinates on the maximally extended Schwarzschild spacetime.
They cover the whole spacetime with one chart, and radial light rays are straight lines at $45$ degrees, as in flat spacetime.
The extension they reveal has four regions: two exterior regions, a [[black hole|YHJ0PDNY8T]] interior and a white hole interior.
They are the standard picture of the global structure of the [[Schwarzschild metric|6Y50T68WKY]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

[[Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates|SG9GAYQT4R]] follow either the inward or the outward light rays, but not both.
Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates use both families at once, so all radial light rays become diagonal lines on a flat diagram.
On this diagram the horizon is a pair of crossing diagonal lines, and the singularity $r = 0$ is a pair of hyperbolas, one in the future and one in the past.
The diagram shows a second copy of the outside universe, joined to the first one by a bridge that pinches off too fast for anything to cross.
Every curve of constant Schwarzschild radius $r \ne 2m$ is a hyperbola in these coordinates.
