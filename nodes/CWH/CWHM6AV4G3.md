## Summary

A congruence of geodesics in a spacetime is a family of [[geodesics|NSYMQMHPDN]] such that through each point of a region passes exactly one of them.
It models a cloud of freely falling test particles or a bundle of light rays.
Its local behaviour is described by the expansion, shear and rotation of nearby curves, whose evolution is governed by the Raychaudhuri equation.
Congruences are a key tool in the singularity theorems and in the study of horizons.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a swarm of dust grains, each falling freely under gravity, with no two grains at the same place.
The paths of the grains fill a region of spacetime, one path through each point: this is a congruence of geodesics.
One can ask whether the swarm spreads out, gets squeezed in some directions, or swirls around.
In flat space, grains at rest stay at rest, so the swarm does nothing at all.
Grains flying apart from a common explosion form a congruence that keeps expanding.
