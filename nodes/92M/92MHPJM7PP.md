## Summary

Baryon acoustic oscillations are sound waves that travelled through the plasma of photons and baryons in the early universe and stopped at [[recombination|EH021K5438]].
They left a preferred comoving distance, the sound horizon, of about $150$ Mpc, between regions of higher density.
This distance appears as a bump in the correlation function of galaxies and serves as a standard ruler to measure the expansion history.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Throw a stone into a pond and a ring of waves spreads out.
In the early universe each lump of matter sent out a spherical sound wave in the hot plasma.
When the plasma turned into neutral gas, the waves froze in place, leaving a shell of extra matter at a fixed distance from each lump.
Galaxies formed a little more often at the centres and on these shells.
So, counting pairs of galaxies, one finds slightly more pairs at that distance, which is known well enough to use as a ruler across the universe.
