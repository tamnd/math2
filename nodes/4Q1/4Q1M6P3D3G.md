## Example

Let $N \ge 2$ and place $N$ sites $0, 1, \dots, N - 1$ on a circle.
Each site holds one ball, black or white, and a set $S$ of $m$ of the $N$ edges $\set{i, i + 1}$ (indices modulo $N$) is marked.
At each time step every ball moves from its site $i$ to the site $i + 1$, and it changes colour if the edge $\set{i, i + 1}$ is marked.
This is the *Kac ring*, introduced by Mark Kac.

Check of reversibility and recurrence: the dynamics is a bijection on the $2^N$ colourings.
Running it backward is the same kind of dynamics in the other direction.
After $N$ steps every ball has gone once around the circle and has crossed each of the $m$ marked edges once.
So every colour has changed $m$ times.
Hence after $2N$ steps every ball is back at its site with its original colour, and the motion is periodic.
For example, take $N = 3$, $S = \set{\set{0, 1}}$ and colours (black, white, white) at sites $0, 1, 2$.
The next states are (white, white, white), (white, black, white) and (white, black, black).
The last one is the initial state with all colours switched.

## Remarks

Let $B(t)$ and $W(t)$ be the numbers of black and white balls, $\Delta(t) = B(t) - W(t)$ and $\mu = m/N$.
In the spirit of molecular chaos, assume that a fraction $\mu$ of the black balls, and of the white balls, sit just before a marked edge at every step.
Then $\Delta(t + 1) = (1 - 2\mu) \Delta(t)$, so $\Delta(t) = (1 - 2\mu)^t \Delta(0)$ decays to $0$ monotonically or with alternating signs.
This contradicts the exact periodicity $\Delta(2N) = \Delta(0)$, a toy version of [[Loschmidt's paradox|T9TCS3426N]] and of the objection based on the [[Poincaré recurrence theorem|TVYJPBHD0S]].
The answer: if each edge is marked independently with probability $\mu$, then for every fixed $t$ and fixed initial colours, $\Delta(t)/N - (1 - 2\mu)^t \Delta(0)/N$ tends to $0$ in probability as $N \to \infty$.
So the decay law holds for typical markings and for times small compared with $N$, but not for all times.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Balls ride around a circular track, and some gates on the track repaint every ball that passes through them.
If one assumes that the gates see a fair sample of the balls at each step, the colours mix and settle to half black, half white.
Yet after two full laps every ball is back with its original colour.
The Kac ring shows by hand how approach to equilibrium and exact recurrence can both be true, on different time scales.
