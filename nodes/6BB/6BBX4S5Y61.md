## Definition

Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional [[Lie algebra|5JJYBJ6A8K]] over a field $k$, with [[Killing form|TWVQT18JXE]] $\kappa$.
The Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is a *Killing Lie algebra* if $\kappa$ is [[nondegenerate|01NHFZF2SK]]: if $x \in \mathfrak g$ and $\kappa(x, y) = 0$ for all $y \in \mathfrak g$, then $x = 0$.

## Remarks

Every Killing Lie algebra is [[semisimple|EY9DN7QAX1]], in any characteristic.
Indeed, let $\mathfrak a$ be an abelian ideal, $a \in \mathfrak a$ and $y \in \mathfrak g$.
The map $\operatorname{ad} a \circ \operatorname{ad} y$ sends $\mathfrak g$ into $\mathfrak a$ and $\mathfrak a$ to $0$, so its square is $0$ and its trace $\kappa(a, y)$ is $0$; nondegeneracy gives $a = 0$.
If $k$ has [[characteristic|1ZB13N78XW]] $0$, then by [[Cartan's criterion|EJT0WX8QD6]] a finite-dimensional Lie algebra is a Killing Lie algebra if and only if it is semisimple.
The name and the more general version over a commutative ring come from the Mathlib library.

Example: the [[special linear Lie algebra|1ZAT1Z967A]] $\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)$ with $\operatorname{char} k \ne 2$.
In the basis $e, h, f$ the Gram matrix of $\kappa$ has $\kappa(e, f) = \kappa(f, e) = 4$, $\kappa(h, h) = 8$ and all other entries $0$.
Its determinant is $-128 \ne 0$, so $\kappa$ is nondegenerate.

Non-example: the Heisenberg algebra with basis $x, y, z$, $[x, y] = z$ and $z$ central.
Each $\operatorname{ad} u$ maps $\mathfrak g$ into $kz$ and kills $z$, so every $\operatorname{ad} u \circ \operatorname{ad} v$ is nilpotent and $\kappa = 0$.
