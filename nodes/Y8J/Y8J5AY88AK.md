## Definition

Work in units with $G = c = 1$.
Let $(M, g)$ be a four-dimensional [[Lorentzian manifold|BY4QYYS788]].
*Nordström's theory of gravitation*, in the geometric form of Einstein and Fokker, is the [[alternative to general relativity|384JFR3K6X]] with these conditions:

1. the [[Weyl tensor|SBHY31HESP]] of $g$ vanishes, $C_{abcd} = 0$, so $g$ is locally conformally flat;
2. the [[scalar curvature|ZHX2VYQDZ3]] of $g$ satisfies $R = 24 \pi T$, in the sign conventions of the source; here $T = g^{ab} T_{ab}$ is the trace of the [[stress–energy tensor|REDZ92MKXX]] of matter;
3. freely falling test particles move along timelike [[geodesics|RNRTS8WWNM]] of $g$.

## Remarks

Locally $g = \phi^2 \eta$, where $\eta$ is the Minkowski metric and $\phi > 0$ is a scalar field.
Then condition 2 becomes a wave equation for $\phi$, which recovers Nordström's original scalar theory on flat spacetime.
Example: in vacuum, $T = 0$, the metric $g = \eta$ satisfies the conditions, with $C = 0$ and $R = 0$.
Non-example: the Schwarzschild metric is a vacuum solution of general relativity, but its Weyl tensor is not zero, so it fails clause 1.
Null geodesics are unchanged as curves under conformal changes of the metric, and $g$ is conformal to a flat metric.
So light rays travel on straight lines, and the theory predicts no deflection of light.
It also predicts a perihelion shift of Mercury in the wrong direction.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The theory says that spacetime is flat spacetime with all lengths and times rescaled by a factor that depends on the matter present.
Gravity is then nothing but this varying scale factor.
