## Summary

The Zel'dovich approximation describes the growth of cosmic structure by moving each particle of matter along a straight line in comoving coordinates.
The displacement of each particle is fixed by the initial density field, and its size grows with the linear growth factor $D(t)$.
It extends linear perturbation theory into the mildly nonlinear regime and predicts that matter first collapses into flat sheets.
It is a basic tool of [[structure formation|VXMCTGXRW6]] and is used to set up the initial conditions of cosmological [[N-body simulations|DRCDVJS6PT]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of the matter in the early universe as dust that is almost evenly spread.
Each grain gets a small push, determined by the lumps around it, and keeps moving in the same direction while the push grows in time.
Grains in denser regions are pushed together, so dense regions become denser.
In one dimension, with positions $x = q - D(t) A \sin(kq)/k$, grains near $q = 0$ approach each other, and when $D A = 1$ they pile up at $x = 0$, where the density becomes infinite.
