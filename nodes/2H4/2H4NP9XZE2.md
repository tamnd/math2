## Summary

Drucker stability is a condition on a material, introduced by Daniel Drucker.
It says that an extra load applied by an outside agent does positive work.
It also says that the agent cannot extract net work by applying and then removing the load.
For elastoplastic materials it implies that the [[yield surface|CWWCEXZ8BH]] is convex and that the plastic strain rate is normal to it.
It thus gives a mechanical justification of the [[associated flow rule|80NPDZSKBT]], and it excludes strain softening.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Press a little harder on a stable material and it gives a little more, in the direction you push.
It does not suddenly give way, and you cannot get energy out of it by pushing and releasing.
A material that softens, carrying less load as it is stretched more, fails this test, and in such materials deformation tends to concentrate in narrow bands.
