## Description

Work in the Georgi–Glashow model on $\R^3$, with static fields.
Its fields are an [[$SU(2)$|CZCWFPNS4N]] gauge field $A_i^a$ and a Higgs field $\phi^a$ in the adjoint representation, with $a = 1, 2, 3$.
Let $e > 0$ be the coupling, $v > 0$ and $\lambda \ge 0$.
A static configuration with zero electric field has energy
$$E = \int_{\R^3} \left( \frac{1}{2} B_i^a B_i^a + \frac{1}{2} (D_i\phi)^a (D_i\phi)^a + \frac{\lambda}{4} \left(\phi^a \phi^a - v^2\right)^2 \right) d^3x.$$
Here $B_i^a$ is the magnetic field of $A$ and $D_i$ the covariant derivative in the adjoint representation.
The *'t Hooft–Polyakov monopole* is a static solution of the field equations with finite energy.
It has the spherically symmetric form
$$\phi^a(x) = \frac{x^a}{r} \, h(r), \qquad A_i^a(x) = \epsilon_{aij} \frac{x^j}{e r^2} \left(1 - K(r)\right),$$
with $r = \abs{x}$, $h(r) \to v$ and $K(r) \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$, and with signs as in the usual convention.

## Remarks

At infinity the Higgs field points radially, $\phi^a \approx v \, x^a / r$.
This "hedgehog" map from a large sphere to the sphere $\phi^a \phi^a = v^2$ has [[degree|TMFG2P912R]] $1$, a topological invariant that finite-energy deformations cannot change.
Far away the symmetry is [[spontaneously broken|3498FHKNFK]] from $SU(2)$ to $U(1)$, and the unbroken $U(1)$ field is that of a magnetic charge $4\pi/e$ in these units.
So the monopole is a smooth, finite-energy soliton that looks from far away like a Dirac magnetic monopole.

The energy satisfies the Bogomolny bound $E \ge 4\pi v \abs{n} / e$, where $n$ is the degree of the Higgs field at infinity.
In the limit $\lambda \to 0$, the bound is attained by solutions of the first-order equations $B_i^a = \pm (D_i\phi)^a$.
For $n = 1$ the solution is explicit, the Prasad–Sommerfield solution: with $\xi = e v r$,
$$K = \frac{\xi}{\sinh\xi}, \qquad h = v \left( \coth\xi - \frac{1}{\xi} \right).$$

Gerard 't Hooft and Alexander Polyakov found the solution independently in 1974.
It shows that a gauge theory whose unbroken group is $U(1)$ inside a compact non-abelian group predicts magnetic monopoles.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In Maxwell's theory a magnetic monopole would have a singular field at its centre.
't Hooft and Polyakov found a smooth object in a richer gauge theory that looks exactly like a monopole from far away.
Its Higgs field points outward in every direction, like the spines of a hedgehog, and this pattern cannot be combed flat without infinite energy.
That topological knot is what makes the monopole stable, and its magnetic charge counts how many times the pattern wraps around.
