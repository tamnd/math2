## Example

Let $L = \{ a, g \}$, with a constant symbol $a$ and a binary function symbol $g$.
The [[Herbrand universe|JKP0XNXRPB]] $H(L)$ contains $a$, then $g(a, a)$, then $g(a, g(a, a))$ and $g(g(a, a), a)$, and so on.
Let $N_n$ be the number of closed terms with exactly $n$ occurrences of $g$.
We show that $N_n$ is the $n$-th [[Catalan number|0YWZJEABB2]] $C_n$, defined by
$$C_0 = 1, \qquad C_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} C_k \, C_{n-1-k} \quad (n \geq 1).$$

1. A term with $0$ occurrences of $g$ is the constant $a$, so $N_0 = 1$.
2. Let $n \geq 1$.
   A term with $n$ occurrences of $g$ is not $a$, so it is $g(t_1, t_2)$ for unique closed terms $t_1$ and $t_2$, by the unique readability of terms.
3. If $t_1$ has $k$ occurrences of $g$, then $t_2$ has $n - 1 - k$, because the outer $g$ uses up one occurrence.
   Here $0 \leq k \leq n - 1$.
4. Conversely, every pair $(t_1, t_2)$ with $k$ and $n - 1 - k$ occurrences gives exactly one term $g(t_1, t_2)$ with $n$ occurrences.
5. So $N_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} N_k N_{n-1-k}$, which is the recursion of $C_n$, and $N_0 = C_0$.
6. By strong induction on $n$, $N_n = C_n$ for every $n$.

The first values are $N_0 = 1$, $N_1 = 1$, $N_2 = 2$, $N_3 = 5$ and $N_4 = 14$.
For $n = 2$ the two terms are $g(a, g(a, a))$ and $g(g(a, a), a)$.
For $n = 3$, sort the terms by $k$, the number of $g$ in the left argument:
$$\underbrace{g(a, g(a, g(a, a))), \ g(a, g(g(a, a), a))}_{k = 0}, \quad \underbrace{g(g(a, a), g(a, a))}_{k = 1}, \quad \underbrace{g(g(a, g(a, a)), a), \ g(g(g(a, a), a), a)}_{k = 2}.$$
Check: $2 + 1 + 2 = 5 = C_3$.

## Remarks

The example shows that the closed terms of $L$ are the full [[binary trees|0D7EZ10V2R]]: each $g$ is an inner node with two children, and each $a$ is a leaf.
So even a signature with two symbols has a rich Herbrand universe.

Counting by levels gives a different and much faster growth.
Since $H_{k+1}$ holds $a$ and every $g(t, u)$ with $t, u \in H_k$, we get
$$|H_0| = 1, \qquad |H_{k+1}| = 1 + |H_k|^2,$$
so $|H_1| = 2$, $|H_2| = 5$ and $|H_3| = 26$.

Two different terms, such as $g(a, g(a, a))$ and $g(g(a, a), a)$, are different elements of $H(L)$.
This stays true even when a structure reads $g$ as an associative operation and gives the two terms the same value.

## Explanations

### L1 human

With one name $a$ and one way $g$ of joining two names into a new one, we get names such as $g(a, a)$ and $g(g(a, a), a)$.
Each name is a tree that branches in two at each $g$.
The number of names with $n$ joins follows the Catalan numbers $1, 1, 2, 5, 14$, and so on.

### L2 human

The typical use is to see that a Herbrand universe can be large even for a tiny signature, so a search over ground instances must be organized with care.
Count the terms with $4$ occurrences of $g$ by the split $k$:
$$N_4 = N_0 N_3 + N_1 N_2 + N_2 N_1 + N_3 N_0 = 5 + 2 + 2 + 5 = 14.$$
Watch out: do not mix the count by occurrences of $g$ with the count by levels $H_k$.
The level $H_2$ has $5$ elements, but they have $0$, $1$, $2$, $2$ and $3$ occurrences of $g$.
