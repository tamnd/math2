## Summary

Ray tracing computes the paths of waves through a system by following rays of geometrical optics.
In a medium of varying [[refractive index|9VGK0SP6EQ]] the rays solve the ray equation, and at interfaces they obey [[Snell's law|QDV9SBK6W0]] and the [[law of reflection|JSTZ7M5VEB]].
Along each ray one can also carry the [[optical path length|AQ5ZKPZ88R]], the amplitude and the polarization.
It is used to design lenses, to study the propagation of radio and seismic waves, and to compute images in computer graphics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Send a thin pencil of light into a system of lenses and follow it.
In each piece of glass it goes straight, and at each surface it bends by Snell's law.
Repeating this for many starting rays shows where the light goes and where it focuses.
In air of varying temperature, rays bend gradually, which explains mirages.
