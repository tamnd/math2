## Summary

The input–output model, developed by Wassily Leontief, describes an economy split into sectors.
Each sector uses the outputs of other sectors as inputs in fixed proportions.
Given a demand for final goods, it asks for the gross output of each sector.
The answer solves a linear system $x = A x + d$ with a [[nonnegative matrix|JPQCCFCY8X]] $A$.
The model is used in national accounts and economic planning, and the [[Hawkins–Simon condition|G7V6BQSPMW]] decides when it has meaningful solutions.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Steel mills need electricity, and power plants need steel.
To deliver some steel and some electricity to households, the economy must also produce the steel and electricity that the two industries use from each other.
The input–output model writes this as one linear equation: total output equals what industries use plus what households get.
For example, say each unit of steel needs $0.2$ units of electricity and each unit of electricity needs $0.1$ units of steel.
Then one solves two linear equations to find the gross outputs.
