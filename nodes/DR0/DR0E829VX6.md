## Summary

In statistical physics, a [[Monte Carlo method|HWP11G3HTN]] estimates averages over the [[Boltzmann distribution|GYNMZ9201M]] of a system by sampling configurations at random.
The configuration space is too large to sum over.
So one runs a [[Markov chain|0G48PYM96Q]] whose stationary distribution is the Boltzmann distribution, as in the [[Metropolis–Hastings algorithm|MKHF449E22]].
Time averages along the chain then approximate thermal averages such as the energy or the magnetization.
It is the main numerical tool for lattice models like the [[Ising model|R0KGM32QN6]], for fluids, and for many quantum systems.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A magnet made of a million tiny spins has $2^{1000000}$ possible states, far too many to list.
Instead one starts from some state and repeatedly proposes a small random change, such as flipping one spin.
A change that lowers the energy is accepted; one that raises it is accepted only sometimes, more rarely at low temperature.
After many steps the states visited look like typical states at that temperature.
Averaging, say, the magnetization over these states estimates its thermal average.
