## Summary

Topological K-theory, extended to pairs of spaces and to all degrees, satisfies every [[Eilenberg–Steenrod axiom|YHK51GHK2T]] except the dimension axiom.
So it is a [[generalized cohomology theory|H28PSZR7XG]], with coefficient groups $K^n(\text{point})$ equal to $\Z$ for even $n$ and $0$ for odd $n$.
This point of view gives K-theory the tools of cohomology: long exact sequences, Mayer–Vietoris sequences and spectral sequences.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cohomology theories assign groups to spaces in a way that is unchanged by deformation and that can be computed by cutting spaces into pieces.
K-theory, built from vector bundles, turns out to have all these properties.
The one difference from ordinary cohomology is that a point has nonzero K-groups in infinitely many degrees, one copy of $\Z$ in each even degree.
Thanks to this structure, K-groups can be computed by the same methods as ordinary cohomology groups.
