## Definition

Let $X$ be a finite set with $\abs{X} \ge 2$, the set of taxa.

1. An *unrooted phylogenetic $X$-tree* is a [[tree|0MJ0JS72DZ]] $T$ with no vertex of degree $2$, with a bijection from $X$ to the leaves of $T$.
2. A *rooted phylogenetic $X$-tree* is a [[rooted tree|0C9J6W2A8A]] $T$ in which every non-leaf vertex has at least two children. It comes with a bijection from $X$ to the leaves of $T$.

A phylogenetic tree is *binary* if every non-leaf vertex has exactly two children (rooted case) or degree exactly $3$ (unrooted case).
Two phylogenetic $X$-trees are the same when there is a graph isomorphism between them that preserves the labels and, in the rooted case, the root.

## Remarks

Example: let $X = \set{a, b, c}$, and take the rooted tree with root $\rho$, children $v$ and $c$ of $\rho$, and children $a$ and $b$ of $v$.
Each non-leaf vertex $\rho$, $v$ has two children, so this is a rooted binary phylogenetic $X$-tree, written $((a, b), c)$.
Non-example: adding a new vertex $w$ on the edge from $\rho$ to $c$ gives a vertex with one child, which clause 2 forbids.
Trees often carry edge lengths $\ell(e) \ge 0$; then the distance between two taxa is the sum of the lengths on the path between them.
