## Summary

In an expanding universe there is no single notion of distance to a far galaxy, so cosmology uses several distance measures.
The main ones are the comoving distance, the transverse comoving distance, the angular diameter distance and the luminosity distance.
Each is defined so that a familiar formula of flat static space stays true.
Examples are the size of an object versus its angle on the sky, and the brightness of a source versus its power.
They are all computed from the [[scale factor|J70F32P86W]] as functions of the [[cosmological redshift|8K69WPX4GS]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Light from a distant galaxy left it long ago, when the universe was smaller, and the galaxy has moved away since.
So "how far away is it?" has several reasonable answers: the distance now, the distance then, or the distance that its brightness or its apparent size suggests.
These answers agree for nearby galaxies and differ a lot for distant ones.
For example, in a flat universe the luminosity distance is $(1 + z)^2$ times the angular diameter distance, so at $z = 1$ they differ by a factor of four.
