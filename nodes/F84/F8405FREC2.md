## Proof

We multiply $A + UCV$ by the proposed inverse and expand until the correction terms cancel, and then run the same computation for $K$ to get the converse.

Write $X = A + UCV$, and use $K = C^{-1} + VA^{-1}U$ as in the statement.
All steps use only the distributive and associative laws of the [[matrix product|076JGP6R3T]].

**Part 1, "if".** Let $K$ be invertible, and put $Y = A^{-1} - A^{-1} U K^{-1} V A^{-1}$.

1. Expand: $XY = AA^{-1} + UCVA^{-1} - AA^{-1}UK^{-1}VA^{-1} - UCVA^{-1}UK^{-1}VA^{-1}$.
2. Use $AA^{-1} = I_n$: $XY = I_n + UCVA^{-1} - UK^{-1}VA^{-1} - UCVA^{-1}UK^{-1}VA^{-1}$.
3. Collect the last two terms: $UK^{-1}VA^{-1} + UCVA^{-1}UK^{-1}VA^{-1} = U(I_k + CVA^{-1}U)K^{-1}VA^{-1}$.
4. Factor out $C$: $I_k + CVA^{-1}U = C(C^{-1} + VA^{-1}U) = CK$.
5. So the two terms equal $UCKK^{-1}VA^{-1} = UCVA^{-1}$.
6. By steps 2 and 5, $XY = I_n + UCVA^{-1} - UCVA^{-1} = I_n$.
7. $X$ is square, so $YX = I_n$ as well, by the theorem on [[one-sided inverses|0753Y2X5K6]]. So $X$ is invertible with $X^{-1} = Y$, by the [[definition of the inverse|0TWTS88TWN]].

**Part 1, "only if".** Let $X$ be invertible with inverse $W$, and put $Z = C - CVWUC$.

8. Expand: $KZ = C^{-1}C + VA^{-1}UC - C^{-1}CVWUC - VA^{-1}UCVWUC = I_k + VA^{-1}UC - VWUC - VA^{-1}UCVWUC$.
9. Collect the last two terms: $VWUC + VA^{-1}UCVWUC = VA^{-1}(A + UCV)WUC$.
10. Since $(A + UCV)W = XW = I_n$, these two terms equal $VA^{-1}UC$.
11. So $KZ = I_k + VA^{-1}UC - VA^{-1}UC = I_k$.
12. $K$ is square, so $K$ is invertible with $K^{-1} = Z$, by the theorem on one-sided inverses.

**Part 2.**

13. Take $k = 1$, $C = (1)$, $U = u$ and $V = v^{\mathsf{T}}$. Then $UCV = uv^{\mathsf{T}}$ and $K = (1 + v^{\mathsf{T}}A^{-1}u)$, a $1 \times 1$ matrix.
14. A $1 \times 1$ matrix $(\kappa)$ is invertible if and only if $\kappa \ne 0$, with inverse $(\kappa^{-1})$.
15. Part 1 then gives the condition $1 + v^{\mathsf{T}}A^{-1}u \ne 0$ and the formula, where $A^{-1}uK^{-1}v^{\mathsf{T}}A^{-1}$ is the matrix $A^{-1}uv^{\mathsf{T}}A^{-1}$ divided by the scalar $1 + v^{\mathsf{T}}A^{-1}u$.

## Idea

Multiply $A + UCV$ by the proposed inverse and expand.
Two of the four terms carry the factor $U \cdots K^{-1} V A^{-1}$, and pulling out $C$ turns what sits between them into $C(C^{-1} + VA^{-1}U) = CK$.
So $K$ cancels with $K^{-1}$, and the correction terms cancel the cross term $UCVA^{-1}$.
The same computation with the roles of $A + UCV$ and $K$ exchanged gives the converse.
Only one product needs to be checked, because the matrices are square.
