## Definition

Let $M = \R^{1,3}$ be Minkowski space and let $\mathcal{K}$ be the set of bounded open regions of $M$ (for instance the double cones).
Two regions $O_1, O_2$ are spacelike separated if $x - y$ is a spacelike vector for all $x \in O_1$ and $y \in O_2$.
Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the proper orthochronous [[Poincaré group|8ZQ9TA4Q2Q]].
A *net of local observables satisfying the Haag–Kastler axioms* is an assignment $O \mapsto \mathcal{A}(O)$ of a unital [[C*-algebra|M2WFY1D3SZ]] to each $O \in \mathcal{K}$ with these properties.

1. Isotony: if $O_1 \subseteq O_2$, then $\mathcal{A}(O_1)$ is a unital C*-subalgebra of $\mathcal{A}(O_2)$.
   Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the C*-algebra generated by all $\mathcal{A}(O)$, called the quasilocal algebra.
2. Locality: if $O_1$ and $O_2$ are spacelike separated, then $AB = BA$ for all $A \in \mathcal{A}(O_1)$ and $B \in \mathcal{A}(O_2)$.
3. Covariance: there is a homomorphism $g \mapsto \alpha_g$ from $\mathcal{P}$ to the automorphism group of $\mathcal{A}$ with $\alpha_g(\mathcal{A}(O)) = \mathcal{A}(gO)$ for all $g$ and $O$.
4. Vacuum: there is a state $\omega$ on $\mathcal{A}$ with $\omega \circ \alpha_g = \omega$ for all $g \in \mathcal{P}$.
   In the GNS representation of $\omega$ on a [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] $H$, the automorphisms $\alpha_g$ are implemented by a strongly continuous [[unitary representation|74BPYJNZTM]] $U$ of $\mathcal{P}$.
   The joint spectrum of the generators $P^\mu$ of the translations $U(a) = e^{i P^\mu a_\mu}$ lies in the closed forward light cone $\set{p : p^0 \ge 0, \ p_\mu p^\mu \ge 0}$ (spectrum condition).

## Remarks

Example: take the free Hermitian scalar field $\phi$ of mass $m > 0$ on Fock space.
Let $\mathcal{A}(O)$ be the C*-algebra generated by the Weyl operators $e^{i \phi(f)}$ with $f$ real and supported in $O$.
Isotony is clear.
Locality holds because the commutator $[\phi(f), \phi(g)]$ is a multiple of the identity given by the commutator function.
This function vanishes at spacelike separation, so the Weyl operators of spacelike separated regions commute.
Covariance and the vacuum come from the second quantization of the positive-energy representation of $\mathcal{P}$ of mass $m$.

Non-example: if $\dim H \ge 2$ and one puts $\mathcal{A}(O) = B(H)$ for every $O$, isotony holds but locality fails, since $B(H)$ is not commutative.

Many authors add further axioms, such as the time-slice axiom (the algebra of a neighbourhood of a Cauchy surface is the whole algebra) or additivity.
The algebras $\mathcal{A}(O)$ are often taken to be [[von Neumann algebras|PSFWQVZ0FS]] acting on the vacuum Hilbert space.
