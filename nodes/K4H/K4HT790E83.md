## Summary

The Sérsic profile is a family of functions for the surface brightness of a galaxy.
It describes how the brightness falls off with the projected distance from the centre.
It has the form $I(R) = I_e \exp\left(-b_n \left[ (R/R_e)^{1/n} - 1 \right]\right)$, where the Sérsic index $n$ controls the shape.
The index $n = 1$ gives the exponential profile of galactic disks, and $n = 4$ gives the de Vaucouleurs profile of elliptical galaxies.
It is the standard parametric model in the photometry of galaxies.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Photograph a galaxy and measure how bright it is at each distance from the centre.
The Sérsic profile is a single formula that fits these measurements for most galaxies, with one number $n$ saying how concentrated the light is.
A small $n$ means a galaxy with a flat middle and a quick fade at the edge; a large $n$ means a sharp bright centre and a long faint halo.
Spiral disks have $n$ near $1$ and big elliptical galaxies have $n$ near $4$ or higher.
