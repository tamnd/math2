## Example

Let $S^n$ be the $n$-[[sphere|T62369RFH6]], $n \ge 1$, with a base point.
Its [[reduced complex K-theory|RASZAXBHX0]] is
$$\tilde{K}(S^n) \cong \begin{cases} \Z & n \text{ even}, \\ 0 & n \text{ odd}. \end{cases}$$
Its reduced [[real K-theory|P7MN6MQV7P]] $\widetilde{KO}(S^n)$ depends on $n \bmod 8$: for $n \equiv 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7$ it is $\Z, \Z/2, \Z/2, 0, \Z, 0, 0, 0$.

Check: by the [[clutching construction|3M42BWTNQH]], complex bundles of rank $m$ over $S^n$ correspond to homotopy classes of maps $S^{n-1} \to GL_m(\C)$, and $GL_m(\C)$ deforms onto $U(m)$.
For $m$ large, this gives $\tilde{K}(S^n) \cong \pi_{n-1}(U)$, where $U$ is the infinite [[unitary group|0R9R0Y1HDK]].
Bott periodicity gives $\pi_{n-1}(U) \cong \Z$ when $n - 1$ is odd and $0$ when $n - 1$ is even.
In the same way $\widetilde{KO}(S^n) \cong \pi_{n-1}(O)$, which gives the table above.

For $n = 2$, $\tilde{K}(S^2)$ is generated by $[H] - 1$, where $H$ is the tautological line bundle over $\C P^1 = S^2$, and $([H] - 1)^2 = 0$.
For $n = 1$, $\widetilde{KO}(S^1) \cong \Z/2$ is generated by $[M] - 1$, where $M$ is the Möbius line bundle.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Spheres are the simplest spaces on which vector bundles can twist.
Complex bundles can twist in an essential way only on even-dimensional spheres, and then the twisting is measured by one integer.
Real bundles are richer: their twisting depends on the dimension modulo $8$, and in some dimensions it is measured by a sign rather than an integer.
These computations are the starting point for most uses of K-theory.
