## Summary

The Whitehead group $\mathrm{Wh}(G)$ of a group $G$ is a quotient of [[K1|SXVNJY4XFY]] of the integral [[group ring|4NMTSX0XDS]] $\Z[G]$.
One divides out the classes of the trivial units $\pm g$.
It is the home of [[Whitehead torsion|4QMDFYEK3T]], which decides whether a homotopy equivalence of finite CW complexes is a simple homotopy equivalence.
Through the [[s-cobordism theorem|E1E5ABP89W]], its vanishing for $\pi_1$ is what makes h-cobordisms of high-dimensional manifolds trivial.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Invertible matrices over the group ring $\Z[G]$ always include the $1 \times 1$ matrices $(g)$ and $(-g)$ for $g \in G$.
These are considered trivial, since they come from just relabelling or reorienting cells of a space.
The Whitehead group collects what remains of $K_1(\Z[G])$ after these are removed.
For the trivial group nothing remains, and the same is true for free abelian groups, but finite cyclic groups such as $\Z/5\Z$ have nonzero Whitehead groups.
