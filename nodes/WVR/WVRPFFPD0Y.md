## Summary

The theory of small oscillations describes the motion of a mechanical system near a [[stable equilibrium|KHT3FBB7NZ]] by linearizing its equations of motion.
One keeps the kinetic and the potential energy to second order in the velocities and the displacements.
This gives a linear system $M \ddot x + K x = 0$ with constant symmetric matrices.
When $K$ is positive definite, every solution is a superposition of [[normal modes|JVM27J1FTF]], each a [[simple harmonic motion|8C19QDA99E]].
This is the starting point of the theory of vibrations of molecules, structures and coupled oscillators.
See the [[definition|WKTX3JJHMB]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Near the bottom of a valley, any smooth valley looks like a bowl shaped by a quadratic function.
A system slightly disturbed from a stable rest position therefore behaves like a set of springs.
Its equations become linear, and they can be solved exactly.
For a pendulum, this gives the familiar result that small swings have period $2\pi\sqrt{\ell/g}$.
For several coupled bodies, it gives a few special patterns of motion, the normal modes, from which every small motion is built.
