## Summary

Plate reconstruction is the process of finding the past positions of the tectonic plates.
In [[plate tectonics|AYQQM6PBMR]], each plate moves rigidly, so its past position is obtained from its present position by a rotation about an [[Euler pole|6SGQDKV15M]].
The rotations are fitted to data such as magnetic stripes on the ocean floor, fracture zones and the magnetisation of old rocks.
Reconstructions show how continents assembled into supercontinents such as Pangaea and then broke apart.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The ocean floor is a tape recorder: as new floor forms at a ridge, it records the direction of the Earth's magnetic field, which [[reverses|ZR7WW7HP5W]] now and then.
Stripes of the same age on the two sides of a ridge were once joined.
Rotate one side so that its stripes of a given age lie on top of the matching stripes on the other side.
Then you see the two plates as they were at that age.
Repeat for many ages and many plates, and you get a movie of the Earth's surface through time.
