## Definition

Let $(M, g)$ be a [[Lorentzian manifold|D1SYF8F8RQ]] with signature $(-,+,\dots,+)$, and let $\xi$ be a [[Killing vector field|PT9FSNP9T5]] on $M$.
A *Killing horizon* of $\xi$ is an embedded null hypersurface $H \subseteq M$ with two properties.
The field $\xi$ is nowhere zero on $H$, and $\xi$ is normal to $H$ at each point of $H$.

Since $H$ is null, its normal is tangent to $H$, so $\xi$ is a null vector tangent to the generators of $H$.
The function $g(\xi, \xi)$ vanishes on $H$, so its gradient is normal to $H$.
Hence there is a function $\kappa$ on $H$, the *surface gravity*, with
$$\nabla^a \bigl( \xi^b \xi_b \bigr) = -2 \kappa \, \xi^a \quad \text{on } H.$$

## Remarks

Example: in Minkowski space $\R^{1,1}$ with coordinates $(t, x)$ and metric $-dt^2 + dx^2$, take the boost Killing field $\xi = x \partial_t + t \partial_x$.
On the null line $H = \set{t = x > 0}$ one has $\xi = x(\partial_t + \partial_x)$, which is nonzero, null and normal to $H$.
Here $\xi^b \xi_b = t^2 - x^2$, whose gradient is $(-2t, -2x)$ after raising the index; on $H$ this is $-2\xi$, so $\kappa = 1$.
Non-example: the same line $H$ is not a Killing horizon of the time translation $\partial_t$, because $\partial_t$ is not normal to $H$.
The surface gravity is constant along each generator of $H$.
It is constant on all of $H$ when the horizon is bifurcate.
It is also constant when the Einstein equations hold with matter that satisfies the dominant energy condition; this is the zeroth law of black hole mechanics.
If $\xi$ is rescaled by a constant $c > 0$, then $\kappa$ is multiplied by $c$, so one fixes a normalisation of $\xi$ at infinity.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A Killing horizon is a light-like surface along which a symmetry of spacetime moves at the speed of light.
