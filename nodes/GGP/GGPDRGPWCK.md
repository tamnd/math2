## Summary

Perpetual motion is the running of a hypothetical machine that would deliver work forever in a way that the laws of thermodynamics forbid.
A machine of the first kind would create energy, against the [[first law of thermodynamics|V14W2V4JSR]].
A machine of the second kind would turn heat from a single source fully into work, against the [[second law of thermodynamics|T11MGWEKJ3]].
The impossibility of such machines is a classical way to state the laws of thermodynamics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

People have long tried to build a machine that keeps going and does useful work with no fuel.
A wheel with weights that always pulls harder on one side is a typical design.
Every such design fails, because friction drains energy and no machine can make energy from nothing.
A cleverer machine would draw heat from the sea and turn it all into work, but the second law forbids this too.
