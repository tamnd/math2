## Summary

A triple point of a substance is a temperature and pressure at which three of its phases coexist in equilibrium.
In the [[phase diagram|R6XCQYXKRP]] it is a point where three coexistence curves meet.
By the [[Gibbs phase rule|5AYW64A7J3]] it is isolated, so it is a fixed and reproducible state.
The solid–liquid–vapour triple point of water served to define the kelvin until 2019.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Usually ice, liquid water and water vapour cannot all sit together for long: one of them grows at the expense of the others.
There is exactly one temperature and one pressure where all three are in balance.
For water this is a little above $0$ degrees Celsius and at a pressure of about $0.6$ percent of normal air pressure.
Because nothing can be adjusted there, laboratories use such points to calibrate thermometers.
