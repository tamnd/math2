## Summary

Nonlinear programming is the solution of [[optimization problems|NS0Q42Y3FX]] in finitely many real variables where the objective or some constraint is a nonlinear function.
It covers [[convex optimization|1W1BXJN9ER]] as an important special case, but also nonconvex problems with many local minima.
Its theory centres on optimality conditions such as the [[Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions|HDWEPH4YG4]].
Its algorithms include [[gradient descent|12317R8RTJ]], [[Newton's method|TNQZVR6TFN]], [[penalty methods|AY9Q1VNDJB]] and [[sequential quadratic programming|CPX8Q2GFGD]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Linear programming handles objectives and constraints that are straight-line expressions, such as $3x + 2y$.
Real problems often contain products, squares, square roots or exponentials of the unknowns.
Nonlinear programming is the general toolbox for these.
For example, choosing the side lengths $x, y > 0$ of a rectangle with perimeter $4$ to maximise the area $xy$ is a nonlinear program, with answer $x = y = 1$.
