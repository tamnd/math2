## Summary

An outcome is Pareto efficient, or Pareto optimal, if no other feasible outcome makes some person better off without making anyone worse off.
It is the weakest common standard of efficiency in economics, because it needs no comparison of one person's gains with another's losses.
The set of Pareto efficient outcomes is usually large, and the [[fundamental theorems of welfare economics|J6KA8R1H5Z]] relate it to [[competitive equilibria|YTBPB52K27]].
In optimization with several objectives the same notion gives the Pareto front.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose two friends share a cake and a pizza.
If one friend prefers cake and the other prefers pizza, giving each half of both is wasteful: swapping the cake halves for the pizza halves pleases both.
An outcome is Pareto efficient when all such improvements are used up, so helping one person necessarily hurts someone else.
Giving the whole cake and pizza to one person is also Pareto efficient, so efficiency says nothing about fairness.
