## Proof

We prove $2 \Rightarrow 1 \Rightarrow 5 \Rightarrow 4 \Rightarrow 3 \Rightarrow 2$.
Formulas are formulas of $L$, built with $\neg$, $\to$ and $\forall$, unless we say otherwise.
For a list or set $\Delta$ of formulas, $\neg \Delta$ is the set of the formulas $\neg \delta$ with $\delta$ in $\Delta$.

**From natural deduction to the Hilbert-style calculus ($2 \Rightarrow 1$).**
For a node of a natural deduction derivation, let $\Delta$ be the set of open assumptions of the subtree above it.
We show by induction on the subtree: if the node is a formula $\psi$ of $L$, then $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \psi$; if the node is $\bot$, then $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \chi$ for every formula $\chi$.
Each case uses the [[definition of the calculus|2B00FE6K5W]], [[tautologies|05006AJ7DZ]] and the [[deduction theorem|8M9ZFDR4DH]] for $\mathsf{H}$, which has no restriction.
Fix a derivation of $\varphi$ whose open assumptions lie in $\Gamma$.
No assumption in it is $\bot$: a discharge of $\bot$ by ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ or ${\neg}\mathrm{I}$ would give $\bot \to \chi$ or $\neg \bot$, which are not allowed.
So every set $\Delta$ below is a set of formulas of $L$.

1. Assumption $\psi$. Here $\Delta = \{\psi\}$, and $\{\psi\} \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \psi$ by a one-line deduction.
2. ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ from $\psi$ and $\psi \to \chi$. By induction both are deducible from $\Delta$, and modus ponens gives $\chi$.
3. ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ from $\chi$, with $\psi$ discharged. By induction $\Delta \cup \{\psi\} \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \chi$, and the deduction theorem gives $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \psi \to \chi$.
4. ${\neg}\mathrm{E}$ from $\psi$ and $\neg \psi$. For each $\chi$, the tautology $\psi \to (\neg \psi \to \chi)$ and modus ponens twice give $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \chi$.
5. ${\neg}\mathrm{I}$ from $\bot$, with $\psi$ discharged. By induction, with $\chi = \neg \psi$, we get $\Delta \cup \{\psi\} \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \neg \psi$.
6. The deduction theorem gives $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \psi \to \neg \psi$. The tautology $(\psi \to \neg \psi) \to \neg \psi$ and modus ponens give $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \neg \psi$.
7. RAA from $\bot$, with $\neg \psi$ discharged. By induction, with $\chi = \psi$, we get $\Delta \cup \{\neg \psi\} \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \psi$.
8. The deduction theorem gives $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \neg \psi \to \psi$. The tautology $(\neg \psi \to \psi) \to \psi$ and modus ponens give $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \psi$.
9. ${\bot}\mathrm{E}$ from $\bot$, concluding $\psi$. By induction $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \chi$ for every $\chi$, in particular for $\chi = \psi$.
10. ${\forall}\mathrm{E}$ from $\forall x\, \psi$, concluding $\psi[t/x]$. The axiom $\forall x\, \psi \to \psi[t/x]$ of group 2 and modus ponens give it.
11. ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ from $\psi[y/x]$, concluding $\forall x\, \psi$. By induction $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \psi[y/x]$.
12. The rule requires that $y$ is substitutable for $x$ in $\psi$, and that $y$ is not free in $\Delta$ or in $\forall x\, \psi$.
13. The [[generalization theorem|VKSK35Y52Z]] gives $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \forall y\, \psi[y/x]$. If $y = x$, this is $\forall x\, \psi$, and the case is done.
14. Let $y \neq x$. Then $y$ is not free in $\psi$, because $y$ is not free in $\forall x\, \psi$.
15. So the free occurrences of $y$ in $\psi[y/x]$ are exactly the places of the free $x$ in $\psi$. These places lie in no scope of a quantifier $\forall x$, because $x$ is free there.
16. So $x$ is substitutable for $y$ in $\psi[y/x]$, and $(\psi[y/x])[x/y] = \psi$. Group 2 gives the axiom $\forall y\, \psi[y/x] \to \psi$.
17. So $\{\forall y\, \psi[y/x]\} \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \psi$. The variable $x$ is not free in $\forall y\, \psi[y/x]$, so the generalization theorem gives $\{\forall y\, \psi[y/x]\} \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \forall x\, \psi$.
18. The deduction theorem gives $\vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \forall y\, \psi[y/x] \to \forall x\, \psi$. Modus ponens with step 13 gives $\Delta \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \forall x\, \psi$.
19. The root of the fixed derivation is $\varphi$, and its open assumptions lie in $\Gamma$. So the claim gives $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{H}} \varphi$.

**From the Hilbert-style calculus to consequence ($1 \Rightarrow 5$).**

20. Every structure and assignment that satisfy $\Gamma$ satisfy each line of a deduction from $\Gamma$, by the [[soundness theorem|4A84VHDTHV]] for $\mathsf{H}$. Since $\Gamma$ and $\varphi$ are sentences, $\Gamma \models \varphi$.

**From consequence to tableaux ($5 \Rightarrow 4$).**

21. Assume $\Gamma \models \varphi$. Then $\Gamma \cup \{\neg \varphi\}$ has no model.
22. The language $L$ is countable, so the completeness of systematic tableaux on the page [[semantic tableaux|WGWBBVE8GX]] applies. It gives a closed tableau for $\Gamma \cup \{\neg \varphi\}$.
23. So $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{T}} \varphi$.

**From tableaux to the sequent calculus ($4 \Rightarrow 3$).**
Let $L^{+}$ be $L$ with the parameters of the tableau as new constant symbols, and use LK for $L^{+}$.
We write $\vdash \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ for "the sequent has an LK derivation" and treat the order of formulas as free, by exchange.

24. Lemma A: if $\vdash \rho \Rightarrow \sigma$ and $\vdash \Theta, \sigma \Rightarrow$, and $\rho$ occurs in $\Theta$, then $\vdash \Theta \Rightarrow$. Proof: cut on $\sigma$ gives $\vdash \rho, \Theta \Rightarrow$, and contraction on $\rho$ gives $\vdash \Theta \Rightarrow$.
25. Lemma B: $\vdash \neg \neg \psi \Rightarrow \psi$. From $\psi \Rightarrow \psi$, the rule ${\neg}$R gives $\Rightarrow \psi, \neg \psi$, and ${\neg}$L gives $\neg \neg \psi \Rightarrow \psi$.
26. Lemma C: $\vdash \neg(\psi \to \chi) \Rightarrow \psi$. From $\psi \Rightarrow \psi$: WR gives $\psi \Rightarrow \psi, \chi$, ${\to}$R gives $\Rightarrow \psi, \psi \to \chi$, and ${\neg}$L finishes.
27. Lemma D: $\vdash \neg(\psi \to \chi) \Rightarrow \neg \chi$. From $\chi \Rightarrow \chi$: WL gives $\psi, \chi \Rightarrow \chi$, ${\to}$R gives $\chi \Rightarrow \psi \to \chi$, ${\neg}$L gives $\neg(\psi \to \chi), \chi \Rightarrow$, and ${\neg}$R finishes.
28. Lemma E: $\vdash \forall x\, \psi \Rightarrow \psi[t/x]$ for a closed term $t$. Apply ${\forall}$L to the axiom $\psi[t/x] \Rightarrow \psi[t/x]$.
29. Lemma F: if $\vdash \Theta, \neg \psi \Rightarrow$, then $\vdash \Theta \Rightarrow \psi$. Proof: ${\neg}$R gives $\vdash \Theta \Rightarrow \neg \neg \psi$, and a cut with Lemma B gives $\vdash \Theta \Rightarrow \psi$.
30. Lemma G: let $c$ be a parameter, and $z$ a variable that occurs nowhere in an LK derivation of $\Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$. Replacing $c$ by $z$ everywhere gives an LK derivation of $\Sigma[z/c] \Rightarrow \Pi[z/c]$.
31. Proof of Lemma G: axioms stay axioms, and each rule stays an instance of the same rule. The replacement commutes with substitution, since $z$ is new, and eigenvariables differ from $z$. The check is the one in the [[proof of the generalization theorem|HFQG8BVXKS]], part 2.
32. Fix a closed tableau for $\Gamma \cup \{\neg \varphi\}$. For a node $n$, let $S(n)$ be the list of sentences on the path from the root to $n$.
33. Claim: for each node $n$ there is a finite list $\Gamma'$ of members of $\Gamma \cup \{\neg \varphi\}$ with $\vdash \Gamma', S(n) \Rightarrow$. We prove it by induction on the height of the subtree at $n$.
34. Case $n$ is a leaf. Its branch is closed, so $S(n)$ contains some $\chi$ and $\neg \chi$. From $\chi \Rightarrow \chi$, ${\neg}$L gives $\neg \chi, \chi \Rightarrow$, and weakening gives $S(n) \Rightarrow$.
35. Case the child $m$ of $n$ is a start node or comes from the premise rule, with a sentence $\sigma \in \Gamma \cup \{\neg \varphi\}$. By induction $\vdash \Gamma', S(n), \sigma \Rightarrow$, so the list $\Gamma', \sigma$ works for $n$.
36. Case the child $m$ comes from an $\alpha$ or $\gamma$ rule applied to a sentence $\rho$ in $S(n)$, and adds $\sigma$. By induction $\vdash \Gamma', S(n), \sigma \Rightarrow$.
37. Here $\vdash \rho \Rightarrow \sigma$ by Lemma B, C, D or E: the types are $\neg \neg \psi$, $\neg(\psi \to \chi)$ (two components) and $\forall x\, \psi$. Lemma A gives $\vdash \Gamma', S(n) \Rightarrow$.
38. Case $n$ has two children by the $\beta$ rule for $\psi \to \chi$ in $S(n)$, with $\neg \psi$ and $\chi$. By induction $\vdash \Gamma_1, S(n), \neg \psi \Rightarrow$ and $\vdash \Gamma_2, S(n), \chi \Rightarrow$.
39. Lemma F turns the first sequent into $\vdash \Gamma_1, S(n) \Rightarrow \psi$.
40. The rule ${\to}$L with these two sequents gives $\vdash \psi \to \chi, \Gamma_1, S(n), \Gamma_2, S(n) \Rightarrow$. Contraction gives $\vdash \Gamma_1, \Gamma_2, S(n) \Rightarrow$, because $\psi \to \chi$ is in $S(n)$.
41. Case the child $m$ comes from the $\delta$ rule for $\neg \forall x\, \psi$ in $S(n)$, and adds $\neg \psi[c/x]$ with $c$ new on the branch. By induction $\vdash \Gamma', S(n), \neg \psi[c/x] \Rightarrow$.
42. The parameter $c$ occurs neither in $S(n)$, which contains $\neg \forall x\, \psi$, nor in $\Gamma'$. Lemma G, with $z$ new, gives $\vdash \Gamma', S(n), \neg \psi[z/x] \Rightarrow$.
43. Lemma F gives $\vdash \Gamma', S(n) \Rightarrow \psi[z/x]$. The variable $z$ is substitutable for $x$ in $\psi$, and not free in the sentences $\Gamma'$, $S(n)$ and $\forall x\, \psi$.
44. So ${\forall}$R gives $\vdash \Gamma', S(n) \Rightarrow \forall x\, \psi$, and ${\neg}$L gives $\vdash \neg \forall x\, \psi, \Gamma', S(n) \Rightarrow$. Contraction gives $\vdash \Gamma', S(n) \Rightarrow$.
45. At the root, $S(\mathrm{root})$ is one member of $\Gamma \cup \{\neg \varphi\}$. So the claim gives $\vdash \Lambda \Rightarrow$ for a finite list $\Lambda$ of members of $\Gamma \cup \{\neg \varphi\}$.
46. Contraction and weakening on $\neg \varphi$ give $\vdash \Gamma_0, \neg \varphi \Rightarrow$, where $\Gamma_0$ is a finite list of members of $\Gamma$.
47. Lemma F gives $\vdash \Gamma_0 \Rightarrow \varphi$. No parameter occurs in this sequent, so Lemma G removes each parameter of the derivation and keeps the sequent.
48. So $\Gamma_0 \Rightarrow \varphi$ has a derivation in LK for $L$.

**From the sequent calculus to natural deduction ($3 \Rightarrow 2$).**
Here a derivation of $\bot$ "from $\Theta$" means a natural deduction derivation of $\bot$ whose open assumptions lie in $\Theta$.

49. Claim: if $\Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ has an LK derivation, then $\bot$ has a derivation from $\Sigma \cup \neg \Pi$. We prove it by induction on the LK derivation.
50. Axiom $\psi \Rightarrow \psi$. The rule ${\neg}\mathrm{E}$ applied to the assumptions $\psi$ and $\neg \psi$ gives $\bot$.
51. Weakening, contraction and exchange. The set $\Sigma \cup \neg \Pi$ of the premise is contained in that of the conclusion, so the same derivation works.
52. Cut on $\psi$, from $\Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi, \psi$ and $\psi, \Sigma' \Rightarrow \Pi'$. By induction, $\bot$ has a derivation from $\Sigma \cup \neg \Pi \cup \{\neg \psi\}$ and one from $\{\psi\} \cup \Sigma' \cup \neg \Pi'$.
53. RAA on the first, discharging $\neg \psi$, gives $\psi$. ${\neg}\mathrm{I}$ on the second, discharging $\psi$, gives $\neg \psi$. Then ${\neg}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\bot$.
54. ${\neg}$L, from $\Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi, \psi$ to $\neg \psi, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$. The set $\Sigma \cup \neg \Pi \cup \{\neg \psi\}$ is the same for premise and conclusion.
55. ${\neg}$R, from $\psi, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ to $\Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi, \neg \psi$. ${\neg}\mathrm{I}$ on the given derivation gives $\neg \psi$, and ${\neg}\mathrm{E}$ with the assumption $\neg \neg \psi$ gives $\bot$.
56. ${\to}$R, from $\psi, \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi, \chi$. RAA discharges $\neg \chi$ and gives $\chi$, ${\to}\mathrm{I}$ discharges $\psi$, and ${\neg}\mathrm{E}$ with the assumption $\neg(\psi \to \chi)$ gives $\bot$.
57. ${\to}$L, from $\Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi, \psi$ and $\chi, \Sigma' \Rightarrow \Pi'$. RAA on the first gives $\psi$, and ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ with the assumption $\psi \to \chi$ gives $\chi$.
58. ${\neg}\mathrm{I}$ on the second derivation, discharging $\chi$, gives $\neg \chi$. Then ${\neg}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\bot$.
59. ${\forall}$L, from $\psi[t/x], \Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$. ${\neg}\mathrm{I}$ gives $\neg \psi[t/x]$, ${\forall}\mathrm{E}$ on the assumption $\forall x\, \psi$ gives $\psi[t/x]$, and ${\neg}\mathrm{E}$ gives $\bot$.
60. ${\forall}$R, from $\Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi, \psi[y/x]$, with $y$ not free in $\Sigma$, $\Pi$ or $\forall x\, \psi$. RAA discharges $\neg \psi[y/x]$ and gives $\psi[y/x]$ from $\Sigma \cup \neg \Pi$.
61. The eigenvariable condition of ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ holds, because $y$ is not free in $\Sigma \cup \neg \Pi$ or in $\forall x\, \psi$. So ${\forall}\mathrm{I}$ gives $\forall x\, \psi$.
62. ${\neg}\mathrm{E}$ with the assumption $\neg \forall x\, \psi$ gives $\bot$.
63. Every formula in these derivations is a formula of $L$ or $\bot$, as condition 2 requires.
64. Now let $\Gamma_0 \Rightarrow \varphi$ have an LK derivation. The claim gives a derivation of $\bot$ from $\Gamma_0 \cup \{\neg \varphi\}$.
65. RAA, discharging $\neg \varphi$, gives a derivation of $\varphi$ from $\Gamma_0$. So $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathsf{ND}} \varphi$.

Steps 19, 20, 23, 48 and 65 close the cycle, so the five conditions are equivalent.

## Idea

Three arrows translate proofs by induction on derivations.
Natural deduction goes into $\mathsf{H}$ with the deduction and generalization theorems, and tableaux go into LK when we read the tree from the leaves to the root.
LK goes into natural deduction when we read a sequent $\Sigma \Rightarrow \Pi$ as "$\Sigma$ and $\neg \Pi$ give $\bot$".
One arrow is the soundness of $\mathsf{H}$.
The last arrow, from consequence to tableaux, is the completeness of systematic tableaux, which builds a countermodel from an open branch with [[Hintikka's lemma|2A6QP78XN3]].

## Variants

- Soundness can be proved for each system directly, by induction on derivations, and completeness then only for one system. This is how most textbooks proceed.
- The translation of tableaux into LK can avoid cut: read the closed tableau upside down as a derivation in a one-sided sequent calculus. Then the cycle shows that this cut-free calculus is complete.
- A direct translation from $\mathsf{H}$ into natural deduction derives each axiom schema and uses ${\to}\mathrm{E}$ for modus ponens. The tautologies need the propositional completeness of natural deduction.
- Henkin's proof of [[Gödel's completeness theorem|WQ1ZE255SF]] can replace the tableau step: it builds a model of any consistent set, for one fixed system.
