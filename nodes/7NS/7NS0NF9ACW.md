## Definition

*Axial precession* is the secular motion of the Earth's rotation axis in an inertial frame.
The axis turns westward around the pole of the ecliptic, keeping an angle of about $23.4^\circ$ with it.
It is the [[precession|CFE9SNEHMP]] driven by the torques of the Moon and the Sun on the Earth's equatorial bulge.
The short periodic part of the motion of the axis is called nutation and is not included.

## Remarks

The rate of the general precession in longitude is about $50.3''$ per year, so a full turn takes about $25\,800$ years.
As the celestial pole moves, the celestial equator and the March equinox move with it.
Example: the [[ecliptic longitude|3ND041H1AA]] of a fixed star grows by about $50.3''$ per year, while its ecliptic latitude barely changes.
So [[equatorial coordinates|QBEJJPMWV1]] must be given for an epoch, such as J2000.0.
Non-example: the slow turning of the major axis of the Earth's orbit is apsidal precession, a different phenomenon.
Another name is precession of the equinoxes.
