## Summary

A Stokeslet is the flow that a point force produces in a viscous fluid governed by the Stokes equations.
It is the [[fundamental solution|K2NK9A8M5F]] of the Stokes equations, and its velocity decays like the inverse of the distance to the force.
Every [[Stokes flow|13CB7V2YM4]] can be built by superposing Stokeslets and their derivatives, which is the basis of boundary integral methods and of slender-body theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Push on a tiny point inside honey and the honey around it moves.
The motion is strongest near the point and fades like one over the distance, which is slow, so the effect reaches far.
The fluid ahead and behind moves along with the push twice as strongly as the fluid at the sides.
Since the Stokes equations are linear, the flow around a swimming bacterium or a sinking particle can be pieced together from many such point pushes.
