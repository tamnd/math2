## Summary

Classical propositional logic cannot be strengthened without collapsing.
Try to add the converse law $(p \to q) \to (q \to p)$, with all its substitution instances.
Put any formula $B$ for $p$ and the theorem $r \to r$ for $q$.
The instance says that $B \to (r \to r)$ implies $(r \to r) \to B$, and two uses of modus ponens then [[derive B|BH8WMN52AF]].
So every formula becomes a theorem, and the same happens for every formula that is not already a law.

Let $S$ be a deductive system whose theorems are formulas of a fixed language.
$S$ is *Post consistent*, or *absolutely consistent*, when at least one formula of the language is not a theorem of $S$.
Otherwise $S$ is *Post inconsistent*, or trivial.
For a formula $A$, let $S + A$ be the system that adds every substitution instance of $A$ as a new axiom.
So $A$ is added as an axiom [[schema|NYK5HABF3K]].
$S$ is *Post complete* when, for every formula $A$ that is not a theorem of $S$, the system $S + A$ is Post inconsistent.
In other words, a Post complete system has no proper Post consistent extension by a schema.

The notions need no negation sign, so they apply to every logic.
The main example is that [[classical propositional logic is Post complete|R1122T076S]].

## Motivation

The usual definition of [[consistency|VM8TQ70CVJ]], "no formula $\varphi$ with both $\varphi$ and $\lnot\varphi$ provable", needs a negation.
Emil Post studied systems of very general form, some without a negation sign, and he needed a notion that only counts theorems.
"Not every formula is a theorem" is such a notion.
In classical logic it agrees with the usual one, by [[explosion|TTKZGJEEK7]].

A second question is whether a logic is as strong as it can be.
For a theory of sentences, this means that it decides each sentence.
For a logic with schematic letters, deciding each formula is the wrong goal, since neither $p$ nor $\lnot p$ should be a law of logic.
Post completeness is the right goal.
Every formula that is not a law, once accepted as a law for all its instances, destroys the system.

## Notation

Post consistency is also called absolute consistency, and simple consistency is then the notion with a negation.
Post completeness is also called Hilbert-Post completeness, maximality, or deductive completeness.
Some books call it simply "completeness", which clashes with two other uses of the word.
Here the systems are propositional, with formulas built from the variables $p_1, p_2, \ldots$.
"Schema" means closure under uniform substitution of formulas for variables.
Some authors also require a Post complete system to be Post consistent, and classical propositional logic satisfies both conditions.

## Remarks

### Three meanings of "complete"

A proof system is complete for a semantics when every valid formula is a theorem, as in [[Gödel's completeness theorem|WQ1ZE255SF]].
A theory is negation complete when, for each sentence, it derives the sentence or its negation; see [[complete theory|655K8Y7VS8]].
A system is Post complete when it has no proper Post consistent extension by a schema.

Classical propositional logic is complete for truth tables, and it is Post complete.
It is not negation complete, because neither $p$ nor $\lnot p$ is a theorem.

For theories of first-order sentences, the second and third ideas meet.
Let $T$ be a consistent first-order theory, and consider the property "adding any unprovable sentence gives an inconsistent theory".
If $T$ is negation complete and $T \nvdash \varphi$, then $T \vdash \lnot\varphi$, and $T \cup \{ \varphi \}$ derives everything.
Conversely, if $T \nvdash \varphi$ and $T \cup \{ \varphi \}$ is inconsistent, then $T \vdash \lnot\varphi$ by the deduction theorem.
So for $T$ the property is the same as negation completeness.

### Systems that are Post consistent or Post complete

The [[Hilbert system for classical propositional logic|HZCFTQDRA5]] is Post consistent.
The variable $p_1$ is not a tautology, so by soundness it is not a theorem.

Classical propositional logic is Post complete.
The [[statement and its proof|R1122T076S]] substitute always true and always false formulas into a non-tautology, following a row where it fails.
[[Adding the converse schema|BH8WMN52AF]] $(p \to q) \to (q \to p)$ shows the collapse in ten lines.

In the paraconsistent logic of paradox, [[the theory with the premises p and not p|5D02W0KT45]] is Post consistent, although it derives a formula and its negation.
A negation complete consistent first-order theory, such as the theory of [[dense linear orders|2W4S2H4VYA]] without endpoints, is Post complete as a set of sentences.

### Systems that are not Post complete

[[Intuitionistic propositional logic is not Post complete|EBVDK5GCGV]].
The excluded middle $p \lor \lnot p$ is not a theorem, and adding it as a schema gives classical logic, which is still Post consistent.

The fragment with only the schemas $\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)$ and $(\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)) \to ((\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi))$ and modus ponens is not Post complete.
It does not prove $\lnot\lnot p \to p$.
To see this, read $\lnot$ as the function with constant value true.
Both schemas stay valid under this reading, and $\lnot\lnot p \to p$ fails for $p$ false.
Adding $\lnot\lnot p \to p$ as a schema keeps all theorems tautologies, so $p_1$ stays unprovable.

The trivial system, in which every formula is a theorem, is Post inconsistent.

### Why schemas matter, and what completeness gives

If $S$ has an explosive negation, then $S$ is Post consistent exactly when $S$ is simply consistent, as shown under [[consistency|VM8TQ70CVJ]].

The schema condition is needed.
Add the single formula $p_1$, without its instances, to classical propositional logic.
The new system proves $p_1$ but not $p_2$, because the valuation with $p_1$ true and $p_2$ false makes every axiom true and modus ponens keeps truth.

Post completeness gives completeness for truth tables, under mild conditions.
Let $S$ be closed under modus ponens and substitution, prove only tautologies, and be Post complete.
If $A$ is a tautology, then $S + A$ still proves only tautologies, so it is Post consistent.
Post completeness then forces $A$ to be a theorem of $S$ already.

So a Post complete, Post consistent logic is maximal among the Post consistent logics in its language that are closed under substitution and modus ponens.

### Watch out

Do not confuse Post completeness with negation completeness.
Classical propositional logic is Post complete, but it proves neither $p$ nor $\lnot p$.

Do not forget the substitution instances.
Adding one formula is not the same as adding a schema, as the example with $p_1$ above shows.

Post consistency and simple consistency agree only when negation is explosive.
They differ in paraconsistent logics.

### History

Post's paper of 1921 on the propositional calculus of *Principia Mathematica* proved that every tautology is a theorem of that system, with truth tables as the method.
The same paper proved that the system is consistent and that it is Post complete, and the name "Post completeness" refers to it.

### Try it

Add the schema $\lnot p_1$ to classical propositional logic, and show that the result is trivial.
Answer: the instance $\lnot(p_1 \to p_1)$ is an axiom, and $p_1 \to p_1$ is a theorem.
By explosion every formula follows.

Is the trivial system Post complete?
Answer: yes, vacuously, since it has no non-theorems, but it is not Post consistent.
This is why some authors add Post consistency to the definition.

Does adding the single formula $(p \to q) \to (q \to p)$, without its instances, make classical logic trivial?
Answer: no.
The valuation with $p$ and $q$ true and a third variable $s$ false makes every axiom true, so $s$ is not derivable.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A system of rules is absolutely consistent when it does not prove everything.
A system that proves every statement is useless, like a judge who says "guilty" and "innocent" to every case.
Classical logic of "and", "or", "not" and "if then" has a strong property.
Its laws are exactly the formulas that are true in every row of their truth table.
Suppose we add any other formula as a new law for all its instances, for example "if $p$ implies $q$, then $q$ implies $p$".
Then the system collapses and proves everything.
So classical propositional logic is as strong as a logic in its language can be without collapse.

### L2 human

We show that adding the schema $p \lor q$, a non-tautology, collapses classical propositional logic.

1. The formula $p \lor q$ is false when $p$ and $q$ are both false, so it is not a tautology.
2. Substitute $\lnot(r \to r)$ for $p$ and for $q$.
   The instance is $\lnot(r \to r) \lor \lnot(r \to r)$.
3. Under every valuation, $r \to r$ is true, so $\lnot(r \to r)$ is false, and the instance is false.
4. So the negation of the instance is a tautology, and by the completeness of propositional logic for truth tables it is a theorem.
5. The instance is an axiom of the new system, so the new system proves a formula and its negation, and by explosion every formula.

The typical use of the method is in step 2.
One valuation that falsifies the formula tells which variables get an always true and which get an always false formula.
A trap is to stop after step 1.
One falsifying valuation does not make the new system trivial, and the substitution of step 2 turns it into an instance that is false under every valuation.

### L3 human

The intermediate logics are the propositional logics between intuitionistic and classical logic that are closed under substitution and modus ponens.
They form a lattice with classical logic as its top.
Post completeness says more: every logic in the same language that contains classical logic and is closed under these rules is classical logic or the trivial logic.

Algebraically, a non-tautology fails in the two-element Boolean algebra.
Every nontrivial Boolean algebra contains the two-element algebra, so an equation that fails there fails in all nontrivial Boolean algebras.
Thus the variety of Boolean algebras has no proper subvariety except the trivial one, which is the algebraic form of Post completeness.

In modal logic, Makinson showed that every consistent normal modal logic is contained in one of exactly two Post complete normal modal logics.
They are axiomatized by $\Box p \leftrightarrow p$ and by $\Box \bot$.
The study of such lattices of logics belongs to [[algebraic logic|QYA4H5Y7VR]].
Post consistency is the notion that survives in [[paraconsistent logics|NJB94B39QK]], where the gap between simple and absolute consistency is the main subject.
