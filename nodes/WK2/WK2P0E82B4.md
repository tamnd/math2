## Summary

Let $S$ be a deductive system whose theorems are formulas of a fixed language.
$S$ is *Post consistent*, or *absolutely consistent*, when at least one formula of the language is not a theorem of $S$; otherwise $S$ is *Post inconsistent*, or trivial.
For a formula $A$, let $S + A$ be the system that adds every substitution instance of $A$ as a new axiom, so that $A$ is added as an axiom [[schema|NYK5HABF3K]].
$S$ is *Post complete* when, for every formula $A$ that is not a theorem of $S$, the system $S + A$ is Post inconsistent.
In other words, a Post complete system has no proper Post consistent extension by a schema.
The notions matter because they need no negation sign, so they apply to every logic, and because [[classical propositional logic is Post complete|R1122T076S]]: no new schema can be added to it without collapse.

## Motivation

The usual definition of [[consistency|VM8TQ70CVJ]], "no formula $\varphi$ with both $\varphi$ and $\lnot\varphi$ provable", needs a negation.
Emil Post studied systems of very general form, some without a negation sign, and he needed a notion that only counts theorems.
"Not every formula is a theorem" is such a notion, and in classical logic it agrees with the usual one, by [[explosion|TTKZGJEEK7]].
A second question is whether a logic is as strong as it can be.
For a theory of sentences, this means that it decides each sentence.
For a logic with schematic letters, deciding each formula is the wrong goal: neither $p$ nor $\lnot p$ should be a law of logic.
Post completeness is the right goal: every formula that is not a law, once accepted as a law for all its instances, destroys the system.

## Notation

Post consistency is also called absolute consistency, and simple consistency is then the notion with a negation.
Post completeness is also called Hilbert-Post completeness, maximality, or deductive completeness, and some books call it simply "completeness".
In this page the systems are propositional, with formulas built from the variables $p_1, p_2, \ldots$, and "schema" means closure under uniform substitution of formulas for variables.
Some authors also require a Post complete system to be Post consistent; classical propositional logic satisfies both conditions.

## Remarks

### Three meanings of "complete"

- A proof system is complete for a semantics when every valid formula is a theorem; [[Gödel's completeness theorem|WQ1ZE255SF]] is of this kind.
- A theory is complete when, for each sentence, it derives the sentence or its negation; see [[complete theory|655K8Y7VS8]].
- A system is Post complete when it has no proper Post consistent extension by a schema, as on this page.

Classical propositional logic is complete in the first sense and in the third sense.
It is not complete in the second sense, because neither $p$ nor $\lnot p$ is a theorem.
For a consistent first-order theory $T$ of sentences, consider the property "adding any unprovable sentence gives an inconsistent theory".
This property is the same as completeness in the second sense.
If $T$ decides $\varphi$ and $T \nvdash \varphi$, then $T \vdash \lnot\varphi$, and $T \cup \set{\varphi}$ derives everything.
Conversely, if $T \nvdash \varphi$ and $T \cup \set{\varphi}$ is inconsistent, then $T \vdash \lnot\varphi$ by the deduction theorem.

### Examples

- The [[Hilbert system for classical propositional logic|HZCFTQDRA5]] is Post consistent: the variable $p_1$ is not a tautology, so it is not a theorem, by soundness.
- Classical propositional logic is Post complete; the [[statement and its proof|R1122T076S]] substitute tautologies and contradictions into a non-tautology.
- [[Adding the converse schema|BH8WMN52AF]] $(p \to q) \to (q \to p)$ to classical propositional logic makes every formula a theorem, in ten lines.
- In the paraconsistent logic of paradox, [[the theory with the premises p and not p|5D02W0KT45]] is Post consistent, although it derives a formula and its negation.
- A complete consistent first-order theory, such as the theory of [[dense linear orders|2W4S2H4VYA]] without endpoints, is Post complete as a set of sentences.

### Non-examples

- [[Intuitionistic propositional logic is not Post complete|EBVDK5GCGV]]: the excluded middle $p \lor \lnot p$ is not a theorem, and adding it as a schema gives classical logic, which is still Post consistent.
- The fragment of classical logic with only the schemas $\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)$ and $(\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)) \to ((\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi))$ and modus ponens is not Post complete. It does not prove $\lnot\lnot p \to p$: if $\lnot$ is read as the function with constant value true, both schemas stay valid and $\lnot\lnot p \to p$ fails for $p$ false. Adding that schema keeps all theorems tautologies, so $p_1$ stays unprovable.
- The trivial system, in which every formula is a theorem, is Post inconsistent.

### Basic properties

- If $S$ has an explosive negation, then $S$ is Post consistent exactly when $S$ is simply consistent; the proof is in [[consistency|VM8TQ70CVJ]].
- If $S$ is closed under modus ponens and uniform substitution, all its theorems are tautologies, and $S$ is Post complete, then every tautology is a theorem of $S$. Indeed, adding a tautology $A$ keeps all theorems tautologies, so $S + A$ is Post consistent, and Post completeness forces $A$ to be a theorem already.
- The schema condition is needed. Adding the single formula $p_1$ (and not its instances) to classical propositional logic gives a system that proves $p_1$ but not $p_2$: the valuation with $p_1$ true and $p_2$ false satisfies every theorem of that system.
- Post completeness of a Post consistent logic says that it is maximal among the Post consistent logics in its language that are closed under substitution and modus ponens.

### History

Post's paper of 1921 on the propositional calculus of *Principia Mathematica* proved that every tautology is a theorem of that system, with truth tables as the method.
The same paper proved that the system is consistent and that it is complete in the sense of this page, and the name "Post completeness" refers to it.

### Common errors

- Confusing Post completeness with the completeness of a theory. Classical propositional logic is Post complete, but it does not prove $p$ or $\lnot p$.
- Forgetting the substitution instances. Adding one formula is not the same as adding a schema, as the example with $p_1$ above shows.
- Thinking that Post consistency and simple consistency agree in every logic. They differ in paraconsistent logics.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A system of rules is absolutely consistent when it does not prove everything.
A system that proves every statement is useless, like a judge who says "guilty" and "innocent" to every case.
Classical logic of "and", "or", "not" and "if then" has a strong property.
Its laws are exactly the formulas that are true in every row of their truth table.
If we try to add any other formula as a new law for all its instances, for example "if $p$ implies $q$, then $q$ implies $p$", the system collapses and proves everything.
So classical propositional logic is as strong as a logic in its language can be without collapse.

### L2 human

We show that adding the schema $p \lor q$, a non-tautology, collapses classical propositional logic.

1. The formula $p \lor q$ is false when $p$ and $q$ are both false, so it is not a tautology.
2. Substitute $\lnot(r \to r)$ for $p$ and for $q$. The instance is $\lnot(r \to r) \lor \lnot(r \to r)$.
3. Under every valuation, $r \to r$ is true, so $\lnot(r \to r)$ is false, and the instance is false.
4. So the negation of the instance is a tautology, and by the completeness of propositional logic it is a theorem.
5. The instance is an axiom of the new system, so the new system proves a formula and its negation, and by explosion every formula.

The typical use of the method is in step 2: one valuation that falsifies the formula tells which variables get a "true" and which get a "false" formula.
A trap is to stop after step 1.
One falsifying valuation does not make the new system trivial; the substitution of step 2 turns it into an instance that is false under every valuation.

### L3 human

The propositional logics that contain intuitionistic logic, are contained in classical logic, and are closed under substitution and modus ponens form the lattice of intermediate logics, with classical logic as its top.
Post completeness says more: every logic in the same language that contains classical logic and is closed under these rules is classical logic or the trivial logic.
Algebraically, a non-tautology fails in the two-element Boolean algebra, and every nontrivial Boolean algebra contains the two-element algebra, so an equation that fails there fails in all nontrivial Boolean algebras.
Thus the variety of Boolean algebras has no proper subvariety except the trivial one, which is the algebraic form of Post completeness.
In modal logic, Makinson showed that every consistent normal modal logic is contained in one of exactly two Post complete normal modal logics, axiomatized by $\Box p \leftrightarrow p$ and by $\Box \bot$.
The study of such lattices of logics belongs to [[algebraic logic|QYA4H5Y7VR]].
Post consistency is the notion that survives in [[paraconsistent logics|NJB94B39QK]], where the gap between simple and absolute consistency is the main subject.
