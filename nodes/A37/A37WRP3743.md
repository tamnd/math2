## Definition

Model the Earth's surface as a sphere $S$ of radius $R$ centred at the origin.
*Plate tectonics* is the kinematic model in which:

1. the lithosphere, an outer layer about 100 km thick, is divided into finitely many *plates* $P_1, \dots, P_k \subseteq S$;
   their interiors are disjoint and their union is $S$;
2. each plate moves as a [[rigid body|EZ9P9ERKNE]] on $S$;
   so its motion between two times is an element of the [[special orthogonal group|0CGYNDCWRR]] $SO(3)$;
3. deformation is concentrated on the plate boundaries, which are divergent (plates move apart), convergent (plates move together) or transform (plates slide past each other).

## Remarks

By [[Euler's rotation theorem|2ZVG43GSKA]], the motion of a plate between two times is a rotation about an axis through the centre of the Earth.
Example: the African and South American plates separate along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, a divergent boundary, and the two plates are rigid away from the ridge and its transform faults.
Non-example: a wide zone of distributed deformation, such as the Tibetan plateau, does not fit clause 2 well; such regions are treated as diffuse plate boundaries or many small blocks.
The model was assembled in the late 1960s from continental drift and seafloor spreading.
