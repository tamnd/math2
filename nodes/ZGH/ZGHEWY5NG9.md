## Summary

The Soret effect, or thermal diffusion, is the separation of the components of a mixture caused by a temperature gradient.
A mass flux of a component appears even when the composition is uniform, and in a steady state it is balanced by ordinary diffusion.
It is a cross effect of [[non-equilibrium thermodynamics|WC6CK8PXPX]].
By the [[Onsager reciprocal relations|9YACB2RQ80]] it is paired with the Dufour effect, a heat flux caused by a concentration gradient.
In gases the same effect is called thermal diffusion, and for particles in a fluid it is called thermophoresis.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put a uniform salt solution between a warm plate and a cold plate.
Over time, slightly more salt gathers on one side, often the cold side.
Diffusion pushes back against this, and a steady concentration difference is reached.
The effect is small but measurable, and it is used to separate isotopes and to study liquid mixtures.
