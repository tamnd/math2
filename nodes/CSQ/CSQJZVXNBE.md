## Summary

The quantum Fisher information measures how sensitive a family of quantum states is to a change of a real parameter.
It is the quantum analogue of the classical [[Fisher information|G1Z3WSTE8Q]].
It bounds the precision of any estimate of the parameter through the quantum Cramér–Rao bound.
It is the central quantity of quantum metrology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Suppose a phase or a field strength $\theta$ is imprinted on a quantum state, and one wants to read off $\theta$.
If the state moves fast when $\theta$ changes, small changes of $\theta$ are easy to detect.
The quantum Fisher information measures this speed, using the best possible measurement.
For a pure state rotated by $e^{-i\theta A}$, it is four times the variance of $A$ in that state.
So states with a large spread of $A$, such as some entangled states, give the best precision.
