## Summary

Finding a proof and writing one are different jobs, and this part of [[reasoning and proof|SGSVYFDSYK]] is about the second job and its mirror image, reading.
A student who sees why $8$ divides $n^2 - 1$ for odd $n$ still has to turn that insight into sentences that a stranger can check line by line.
The story starts with the plan: what is given, what is to be shown, and how the logical form of the goal suggests the next step.
It moves on to the words and layouts that carry a proof, from "let" and "suppose" to two-column tables and numbered structured proofs.
Then it asks how much detail a given reader needs, and how sentences, symbols and displays should look on the page.
The same skills, run backward, make a careful reader and a careful referee, and at the largest scale they shape a research paper.
The advice of writers on mathematical style, such as Halmos, Knuth and Lamport, runs through these pages, together with the points where such advice differs.

## Remarks

### Reading order

1. [[Writing proofs|8JQHJK94RZ]] gives the reader the core habits: state what is proved, give a reason for each step, and write for a stated reader.
2. [[Scratch work and the final proof|E97XXFW9MV]] shows why the written proof hides the search for it, and how to turn notes into a clean argument.
3. [[Proof strategies by logical form|872K5XB580]] gives a table of givens and goals that suggests the first move for each form of statement.
4. [[Unpacking definitions|41JZ39GE3F]] teaches the reader to replace each defined term by its definition and see what must be proved.
5. [[The language of proofs|GKMRQCA8CW]] explains what words such as "let", "suppose", "hence" and "we claim" do in a proof.
6. [[Two-column proof|P8PCGFASP8]] shows the school format of statements and reasons, and what it gains and loses against prose.
7. [[Structured and hierarchical proofs|H2W8D7BMSW]] shows how numbered levels of claims make a long proof easy to check.
8. [[Level of detail in proofs|VYGBV5D8Y4]] helps the reader decide which steps to write for which audience, and tell a gap from an acceptable omission.
9. [[Mathematical writing style|D5YPYTQ88G]] collects advice on sentences, symbols and displayed formulas.
10. [[Reading proofs|40GCSM2G56]] teaches active reading: find the reason for each step, test it on examples, and see where each hypothesis is used.
11. [[Checking a proof|4M98ZZNK0E]] turns reading into a verdict, by testing the argument on a case where the conclusion fails.
12. [[Structure of a mathematical paper|XZHACQ74C0]] shows how the same principles shape a whole paper, from the abstract to the references.

### Prerequisites

1. [[Mathematical proof|0B94TKFQJX]] says what a proof is.
2. [[The language of mathematical statements|WMY13MXY3W]] covers connectives, conditionals, quantifiers and negations in mathematical English.
3. [[Basic proof methods|958EF46EYJ]] covers direct proof, contrapositive, contradiction and cases, which the written proofs here use.
4. [[Notation, conventions and jargon|Z2WNQ1DNTX]] covers the symbols, abbreviations and end-of-proof marks.
