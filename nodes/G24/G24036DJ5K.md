## Definition

Let $n \ge 2$ and let $P_n = \set{0, 1, \dots, n - 1}$.
The *$n$-element Post chain* is $\mathbf{P}_n = (P_n, \lor, \land, ', 0, n - 1)$, where:

1. $x \lor y = \max(x, y)$ and $x \land y = \min(x, y)$;
2. $x' = x + 1$ for $x < n - 1$, and $(n - 1)' = 0$;
3. $0$ and $n - 1$ are constants.

A *Post algebra of order $n$* is an algebra in the [[variety|AJNZJD8442]] generated by $\mathbf{P}_n$.

## Notation

We write $\mathbf{Post}_n$ for this variety, and $e_i$ for the term $0$ followed by $i$ primes.
In $\mathbf{P}_n$, $e_i = i$ for $0 \le i \le n - 1$.

## Remarks

1. Example: [[the chain $\mathbf{P}_3$|P7MWSZ837M]].
2. Example: [[the square $\mathbf{P}_3^2$|8YXQWBZJQW]]. A variety is closed under direct products, so every power $\mathbf{P}_n^X$ is a Post algebra of order $n$.
3. Non-example: [[the three-element chain with $x' = 2 - x$|G8JCK81ASR]]. In $\mathbf{P}_3$ the identity $x''' = x$ holds, but here $0''' = 2$.
4. The algebra $\mathbf{P}_n$ is [[primal|XD01Y7B6XW]]: $\max$ and the cyclic negation generate every operation on $P_n$, by a result of Post.
5. For $n = 2$, $\mathbf{P}_2$ is the two-element Boolean algebra with $x' = \neg x$, so the Post algebras of order $2$ are the Boolean algebras.
6. Rosenbloom and Epstein used an equivalent signature: a bounded distributive lattice with constants $e_0 < \dots < e_{n - 1}$ and unary operations. The two signatures have the same term operations on $\mathbf{P}_n$.
7. Post algebras give algebraic semantics for Post's [[many-valued logic|DWYCKAKXZD]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Take the numbers $0$ to $n - 1$ on a ladder.
"Or" takes the higher value, "and" takes the lower value, and "not" moves one step up, with the top going back to $0$.
A Post algebra is an algebra that satisfies every law that this ladder satisfies.

### L2 human

1. Take $n = 4$. The negation is $0 \mapsto 1 \mapsto 2 \mapsto 3 \mapsto 0$.
2. Applying it four times gives back each value, so $x'''' = x$ holds in $\mathbf{P}_4$ and in every Post algebra of order $4$.
3. The law $x \land (y \lor z) = (x \land y) \lor (x \land z)$ holds in every chain, so every Post algebra has a distributive lattice reduct.
4. The law $x \land x' = 0$ fails in $\mathbf{P}_4$ at $x = 1$, since $\min(1, 2) = 1$.

The typical use: to prove an identity in all Post algebras of order $n$, check it in $\mathbf{P}_n$, a finite computation.
The trap: the prime is not a complement; it is a cyclic shift.
