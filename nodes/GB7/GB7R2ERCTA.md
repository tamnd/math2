## Summary

A Riemann invariant of a hyperbolic system is a function of the state that is constant along certain waves.
For a system of two equations, such as one-dimensional isentropic gas dynamics, each Riemann invariant is constant along one family of characteristic curves.
Changing to Riemann invariants as unknowns decouples the system into two transport equations.
Riemann introduced them in 1860 to study sound waves of finite amplitude, and they underlie the [[method of characteristics in gas dynamics|6R71SY5N3Q]].
See the [[definition|PK2XEWNNXC]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A gas in a tube has two unknowns at each place, its velocity and its sound speed.
Riemann found two clever combinations of them, one carried forward with the waves and one carried backward.
Each combination stays unchanged as it travels, as long as the flow is smooth.
For a gas with $\gamma = 3$ the two combinations are simply $u + c$ and $u - c$.
Knowing how these two numbers travel tells the whole story of the smooth flow.
