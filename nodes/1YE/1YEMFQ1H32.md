## Summary

A population model is a mathematical model that describes how the sizes of one or more populations change in time.
It is usually an [[ordinary differential equation|0NT64JV96A]] in continuous time or a [[recurrence relation|0FYX44GP92]] in discrete time, with nonnegative state variables.
Population models are the basic objects of [[population dynamics|GAX3SRT9GD]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A population model is a rule that tells how a population changes from its present size.
The simplest one says that the population grows at a rate proportional to its size, which gives exponential growth.
A better one adds a brake when the population is large, which gives the logistic curve.
For two species, one can let predators eat prey; the model is then a pair of equations, one for each species.
The sizes stay nonnegative, since a population cannot be less than zero.
