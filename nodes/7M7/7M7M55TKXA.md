## Summary

General relativity is Einstein's theory of gravitation, published in 1915.
It describes gravity not as a force but as the curvature of [[spacetime|63ZKC7AFX2]], a four-dimensional Lorentzian manifold.
Matter curves spacetime through the [[Einstein field equations|3MCZ97SW6V]], and freely falling bodies move along [[geodesics|RNRTS8WWNM]] of the curved spacetime.
It explains the perihelion shift of Mercury, the bending of light, gravitational waves and black holes, and it is the basis of modern cosmology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Newton described gravity as a pull between masses.
Einstein's picture is different: mass and energy bend spacetime, and objects move along the straightest possible paths in the bent spacetime.
A common picture is a heavy ball on a stretched rubber sheet, which makes smaller balls roll towards it, though this picture is only a rough analogy.
A satellite in orbit is not pushed by a force; it falls freely along a straightest path, which happens to circle the Earth.
