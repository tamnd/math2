## Definition

Consider a [[pulsation mode|QW9QPV0HHG]] of a star and a layer of the star.
In the layer, the opacity $\kappa$ depends on the density $\rho$ and the temperature $T$.
The *kappa mechanism* is the driving of the mode by that layer when the following hold:

1. During the compression phase of the oscillation, the opacity $\kappa$ of the layer increases.
2. As a result, the layer absorbs radiative heat during compression and loses it during expansion.
3. The net work done by the layer on the oscillation over one cycle is positive.

## Remarks

Example: in the second helium ionisation zone of a Cepheid, part of the compression work goes into ionising helium.
So $T$ rises only a little while $\rho$ rises, the opacity increases on compression, and the three conditions hold.
Non-example: in a layer obeying [[Kramers' opacity law|QK10S7KMF9]], $\kappa \propto \rho T^{-7/2}$, the temperature rises strongly on adiabatic compression and $\kappa$ decreases, so condition 1 fails and the layer damps the mode.
When the driving comes from the low adiabatic exponent of the ionisation zone rather than from the opacity itself, it is often called the $\gamma$ mechanism; the two act together.
