## Summary

The thermoelectric effect is the direct coupling of heat flow and electric current in a conductor.
It has three forms.
In the Seebeck effect, a temperature difference causes a voltage.
In the Peltier effect, an electric current carries a heat flow.
In the Thomson effect, a current along a temperature gradient heats or cools the conductor reversibly.
The three are linked by the [[Kelvin relations|N89246VPKQ]].
It is the basis of thermocouples, thermoelectric generators and Peltier coolers, and a standard example of coupled flows in [[non-equilibrium thermodynamics|WC6CK8PXPX]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Heat a junction of two different metal wires and a small voltage appears between the free ends: this is how a thermocouple measures temperature.
Run a current through such a junction and it gets warmer or cooler, depending on the direction of the current.
Both effects come from the same fact: electrons carry both charge and heat.
A typical metal thermocouple gives some tens of microvolts per kelvin of temperature difference.
