## Summary

A Rayleigh wave is an elastic wave that travels along the free surface of a solid and dies out exponentially with depth.
The particles move in ellipses in the vertical plane through the direction of travel, combining a compressional and a shear part.
On a homogeneous isotropic half-space it travels without dispersion at a speed a little below the [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]] speed.
Lord Rayleigh predicted these waves in 1885.
They carry most of the shaking of an earthquake at the surface, and they are used in surface acoustic wave devices.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Throw a stone into a pond and ripples run along the surface while deep water stays still.
A Rayleigh wave is the solid-earth version: a ripple that hugs the surface of the ground.
Points at the surface roll in small ellipses as the wave passes.
Because its energy stays near the surface instead of spreading into the whole volume, it weakens slowly with distance and does much of the damage in earthquakes.
