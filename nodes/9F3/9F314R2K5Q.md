## Problem

Consider the one-dimensional [[Euler equations|RW0JK9VDZE]] of a polytropic gas with $\gamma = 1.4$ on the interval $0 \le x \le 1$.
At $t = 0$ the gas is at rest, with density $\rho = 1$ and pressure $p = 1$ for $x < 0.5$, and with $\rho = 0.125$ and $p = 0.1$ for $x > 0.5$.
Compute the solution at a later time, before any wave reaches the ends of the interval, and compare a numerical scheme with the exact solution.

## Remarks

This is the Sod shock tube, a standard test for numerical methods for compressible flow, proposed by Gary Sod in a 1978 survey of finite difference schemes.
It is a special case of the [[Riemann problem for gas dynamics|Z94NBKVCRY]], so its exact solution is known.
The solution contains a rarefaction wave moving left, a contact discontinuity and a [[shock wave|FRVV0NHBXT]] moving right.
In the middle region the pressure is about $0.30313$ and the velocity is about $0.92745$.
A good scheme must capture the shock sharply, keep the contact discontinuity from smearing too much, and produce no spurious oscillations; the comparison is often made at $t = 0.2$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A tube is divided by a membrane into dense high-pressure gas on the left and thin low-pressure gas on the right.
When the membrane bursts, a shock runs to the right, a smooth expansion runs to the left, and the boundary between the two gases follows the shock more slowly.
Since the exact answer is known, programmers run their codes on this problem to see how well they handle jumps.
