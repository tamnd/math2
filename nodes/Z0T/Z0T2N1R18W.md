## Proof

We prove the two implications separately.

1. For $n \in \mathbb{N}$, let $s_n = \sup \{a_k : k \ge n\}$ and $i_n = \inf \{a_k : k \ge n\}$. They exist since $(a_n)$ is bounded.
2. By the definitions of the [[limit superior|0P8BA7T7S5]] and the [[limit inferior|0H4R3RESGV]], $s_n \to \limsup a_n$ and $i_n \to \liminf a_n$.
3. The term $a_n$ lies in the set $\{a_k : k \ge n\}$. So $i_n \le a_n \le s_n$ for all $n$.
4. Assume first that $a_n \to L$. Let $\varepsilon > 0$.
5. By the [[epsilon-N definition|09G6KHS6GY]], there is $N$ with $L - \varepsilon < a_k < L + \varepsilon$ for all $k \ge N$.
6. Let $n \ge N$. Then $L + \varepsilon$ is an upper bound of $\{a_k : k \ge n\}$. By the definition of the [[supremum|0BTMQ04556]], $s_n \le L + \varepsilon$.
7. In the same way, $L - \varepsilon$ is a lower bound of this set, so $i_n \ge L - \varepsilon$ by the definition of the [[infimum|04KFTA34C5]].
8. With step 3, $L - \varepsilon \le i_n \le s_n \le L + \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge N$.
9. By [[preservation of non-strict inequalities|0N32V0E3XJ]], compared with constant sequences, $\liminf a_n$ and $\limsup a_n$ both lie in $[L - \varepsilon, L + \varepsilon]$.
10. Let $x$ be $\liminf a_n$ or $\limsup a_n$. Then $|x - L| \le \varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$. If $x \ne L$, the choice $\varepsilon = |x - L|/2$ gives a contradiction. So $x = L$.
11. Hence $\liminf a_n = \limsup a_n = L = \lim a_n$.
12. Conversely, assume $\liminf a_n = \limsup a_n = \ell$. By step 2, $i_n \to \ell$ and $s_n \to \ell$.
13. By step 3 and the [[squeeze theorem|0XMF00T4XQ]], $a_n \to \ell$. So $(a_n)$ is a [[convergent sequence|0AZ3R52593]], with limit equal to the common value.
14. Steps 11 and 13 prove the equivalence, and in both directions the limit is the common value.

## Idea

Each term $a_n$ sits between the infimum and the supremum of the tail that starts at $n$. If the sequence converges, each late tail lies in a small window around the limit, and so do its infimum and supremum. If the two bounds have the same limit, the squeeze theorem traps $a_n$ between them.
