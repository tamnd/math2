## Summary

The impulse of a force over a time interval is the integral of the force with respect to time.
By [[Newton's second law|KNBP19FG6K]], the impulse of the net force on a particle equals the change of its [[momentum|37Q4BTJMXW]].
Impulse is useful for short, strong forces, such as a bat hitting a ball.
There the force itself is hard to measure, but the change of momentum is not.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A small force acting for a long time can change motion as much as a large force acting for a short time.
Impulse is "force times time" and captures exactly this trade-off.
A push of $10$ newtons for $0.5$ seconds gives an impulse of $5$ newton-seconds, enough to start a $1$ kg cart at $5$ m/s from rest on a frictionless track.
Airbags work by stretching the time of a crash, so the same impulse comes with a smaller force.
