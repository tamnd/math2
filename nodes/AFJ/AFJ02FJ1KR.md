## Summary

Genetic drift is the random change of allele frequencies in a finite population, caused by chance in which individuals reproduce.
It has no direction, but over time it removes genetic variation: eventually one allele is fixed and the others are lost.
Its strength is inversely proportional to the [[effective population size|MBW183XCKH]], so it matters most in small populations.
Together with selection, mutation and migration it is one of the basic forces of [[population genetics|CCN582E2C4]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Put $50$ red and $50$ blue marbles in a jar, and form the next jar by drawing $100$ times with replacement.
The new jar will rarely have exactly $50$ red marbles; it may have $46$ or $55$.
Repeat this generation after generation, and the share of red marbles wanders at random.
Sooner or later the jar is all red or all blue, and then it stays so.
This wandering is genetic drift, with marbles in place of gene copies.
