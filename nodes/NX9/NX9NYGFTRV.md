## Summary

Fermi–Walker transport is a way to carry vectors along a timelike curve in [[spacetime|63ZKC7AFX2]].
It keeps the tangent vector of the curve fixed and rotates other vectors as little as possible.
It extends [[parallel transport|TVAGN3D6W5]] from geodesics to accelerated world lines; along a geodesic the two agree.
A Fermi–Walker transported orthonormal frame is the natural non-rotating frame of an accelerated observer.
It models the axes of gyroscopes on which no torque acts.
It is used to build [[Fermi normal coordinates|APH28RGPZK]] and to describe [[Thomas precession|DHN1NV44A3]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

An accelerated observer, such as an astronaut in a firing rocket, wants space axes that do not spin.
Parallel transport does not work, because it would tilt the time axis away from the observer's own time direction.
Fermi–Walker transport adjusts parallel transport by the smallest rotation, a boost in the plane of the velocity and the acceleration, that keeps the time axis along the world line.
Gyroscopes carried by the observer point along Fermi–Walker transported directions.
