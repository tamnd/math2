## Summary

The seismic moment $M_0$ of an earthquake is a product of three factors.
They are the [[shear modulus|J2GWW1NPTC]] of the rock, the area of the fault that slipped, and the average slip.
It measures the size of an earthquake by its physical source, not by the reading of an instrument.
It is the size of the force couples equivalent to the slip, and it is measured in newton metres.
The [[moment magnitude scale|8QWGDVEH46]] is defined from it.

## Explanations

### L1 human

An earthquake is a sudden slip of one block of rock past another along a fault.
A bigger fault, a bigger slip, or stiffer rock all make a bigger earthquake.
The seismic moment multiplies these three together.
For example, a slip of one metre on a fault ten kilometres long and ten kilometres wide in typical crust gives about $3 \times 10^{18}$ newton metres.
The largest recorded earthquakes reach more than $10^{22}$ newton metres.
