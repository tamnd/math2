## Summary

The French flag model is Lewis Wolpert's model, from 1969, of how cells in a tissue learn their position and choose their fate.
A substance called a morphogen forms a concentration gradient across the tissue.
Each cell reads the local concentration and compares it with thresholds, so the tissue splits into bands of different cell types.
It is the basic example of positional information in developmental biology.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Picture a strip of cells with a source of a chemical at the left end.
The chemical spreads and breaks down, so its level is high on the left and low on the right.
Cells where the level is high become blue, cells in the middle range become white, and cells where it is low become red.
The strip then looks like a French flag, and it keeps the same proportions if the strip is cut or grown, as long as the gradient scales with it.
