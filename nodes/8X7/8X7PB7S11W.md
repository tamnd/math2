## Definition

Work in units with $G = c = 1$ and let $\omega$ be a real constant, the *Brans–Dicke coupling constant*.
Let $g$ be a [[Lorentzian metric|BY4QYYS788]] on a four-dimensional manifold, $\phi$ a positive scalar field and $\psi$ the matter fields.
*Brans–Dicke theory* is the [[scalar–tensor theory|MWX9XPYEYY]] with action
$$S = \frac{1}{16\pi} \int \left[ \phi R - \frac{\omega}{\phi} \, g^{ab} \partial_a\phi \, \partial_b\phi \right] \sqrt{-g} \, d^4x + S_m[g, \psi],$$
where $R$ is the [[scalar curvature|ZHX2VYQDZ3]] of $g$ and $S_m$ does not depend on $\phi$.

## Remarks

Varying the action gives the field equations
$$\square \phi = \frac{8\pi}{3 + 2\omega} T, \qquad G_{ab} = \frac{8\pi}{\phi} T_{ab} + \frac{\omega}{\phi^2}\left(\partial_a\phi\,\partial_b\phi - \tfrac12 g_{ab} \, g^{cd}\partial_c\phi\,\partial_d\phi\right) + \frac{1}{\phi}\left(\nabla_a\nabla_b\phi - g_{ab}\square\phi\right),$$
where $T_{ab}$ is the [[stress–energy tensor|REDZ92MKXX]] of matter, $T$ its trace and $G_{ab}$ the [[Einstein tensor|P13KTBC7MH]].
Example: a constant field $\phi = \phi_0$ solves the scalar equation in vacuum, and then the metric equation is Einstein's equation with gravitational constant $1/\phi_0$.
Non-example: adding a potential term $-2U(\phi)$ with $U \ne 0$ to the bracket gives a scalar–tensor theory that is not Brans–Dicke theory, since the action is no longer of the form above.
The scalar equation above requires $\omega \ne -3/2$.
In the [[parameterized post-Newtonian formalism|2TAW8GAMM1]] the theory has $\gamma = (1 + \omega)/(2 + \omega)$, which tends to the general-relativistic value $1$ as $\omega \to \infty$.

## Explanations

### L1 human

In Brans–Dicke theory the number $\phi$ in front of the curvature replaces $1/G$.
Matter is a source for $\phi$, so the strength of gravity can vary, and the constant $\omega$ measures how hard $\phi$ is to change.
