## Definition

Let a [[rigid body|EZ9P9ERKNE]] turn about a fixed point $O$.
Let $e_3$ be a unit vector fixed in the body and $u$ a fixed unit vector in space.
Describe the orientation of the body by [[Euler angles|7WGBNTRQ2S]] $(\varphi, \theta, \psi)$ with respect to $u$.
Here $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ is the angle between $u$ and $e_3$.
The *nutation* of the body is the change of the angle $\theta$ in time.
It is the motion of the axis $e_3$ towards or away from $u$.
Its rate is $\dot\theta$.

## Remarks

Nutation and [[precession|CFE9SNEHMP]] together describe the motion of the axis $e_3$, which is the point of the unit sphere with polar angle $\theta$ and azimuth $\varphi$.

Example: for the heavy symmetric top, $\cos\theta$ oscillates periodically between two roots of a cubic polynomial, so the axis nutates between two cones about the vertical.
Depending on the initial conditions, the tip of the axis then traces a wavy curve, a curve with loops, or a curve with cusps on the sphere.

Non-example: in steady precession $\theta$ is constant, so there is no nutation, although $\varphi$ changes.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Nutation is the nodding of a spinning body's axis: its tilt from the fixed direction goes up and down.
In the language of Euler angles it is the change of the second angle, the tilt angle.
