## Definition

Let a homogeneous elastic medium with density $\rho$ and stiffness tensor $c_{ijkl}$ show [[seismic anisotropy|XXB2TQX6KW]].
Fix a propagation direction $n$ and let $\Gamma_{ik} = c_{ijkl} n_j n_l$ be the Christoffel matrix, a positive definite [[symmetric matrix|0VR671PHE5]].
Suppose two of its [[eigenvalues|0BQAA2BK3Q]] belong to quasi-shear waves, with speeds $v_{S1} > v_{S2}$ and orthonormal eigenvectors $e_1, e_2$, by the [[Christoffel equation|EK3A1GMMFM]].
An incident [[S-wave|GZ7E9GHQYB]] travelling along $n$ with polarization $q$ in the plane of $e_1, e_2$ is decomposed as $q = (q \cdot e_1) e_1 + (q \cdot e_2) e_2$.
After a path of length $L$ the two parts arrive separated by the delay
$$\delta t = L\Big(\frac{1}{v_{S2}} - \frac{1}{v_{S1}}\Big) > 0.$$
This separation is *shear wave splitting*; its *splitting parameters* are the fast polarization direction $e_1$, usually given by an azimuth $\phi$, and the delay $\delta t$.

## Remarks

Example: over a path of $L = 200$ km with $v_{S1} = 4.6$ km/s and $v_{S2} = 4.4$ km/s, the delay is $200(1/4.4 - 1/4.6) \approx 2.0$ s.
Non-example: in an isotropic medium $v_{S1} = v_{S2}$, so $\delta t = 0$ and there is no splitting.
Non-example: if the incident polarization $q$ is parallel to $e_1$ or $e_2$, only one wave is excited.
The record then shows no splitting even though the medium is anisotropic; such a record is called a null measurement.
The two shear polarizations are orthogonal because $\Gamma$ is symmetric.
