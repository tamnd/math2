## Summary

Earth's rotation is the turning of the Earth about its axis.
It is described by the rotation that carries a frame fixed to the crust onto a frame fixed relative to distant objects.
Its rate is about one turn per sidereal day of about $86164$ seconds, an [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]] of about $7.292 \cdot 10^{-5}$ rad/s.
The rotation is not uniform.
The axis moves in space by [[precession|CFE9SNEHMP]] and [[nutation|QP24MG6QFS]], and it moves relative to the crust by [[polar motion|QTRK8GS2Y1]].
Its rate also changes, which shows up as [[length of day variations|BC5JSSJSEW]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Earth spins once a day, which gives day and night.
Measured against the stars, one turn takes about $23$ hours $56$ minutes; the solar day of $24$ hours is a little longer because the Earth also moves along its orbit.
The spin is not perfectly steady.
The axis wobbles slowly in space and also wanders a few metres across the crust near the poles.
The day itself changes by a few thousandths of a second, because winds, ocean currents, tides and the core trade angular momentum with the solid Earth.
