## Summary

Semiclassical gravity is the approximation in which matter is described by [[quantum field theory|W031YNVRE3]] while spacetime stays a classical Lorentzian manifold.
The curvature of spacetime is sourced by the expectation value of the quantum [[stress–energy tensor|REDZ92MKXX]].
It is expected to hold when curvature is small compared to the Planck scale, and it is the setting in which [[Hawking radiation|Z1Y6E4YCTZ]] is derived.
It is an approximation, not a full theory of [[quantum gravity|M1VXRT5X56]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

A full quantum theory of gravity is not known, but quantum effects of matter in curved spacetime can still be studied.
Semiclassical gravity keeps spacetime classical and lets only the matter be quantum.
Matter then pushes on spacetime through its average energy and momentum.
This is like treating a heavy ship as classical while the water around it is treated as many tiny quantum particles.
For example, it predicts that black holes slowly lose mass by emitting thermal radiation.
