## Example

Let $W = \{u, v\}$, $R = \{(u, v)\}$, $V(i) = \{u, v\}$ and $V(p) = \{u\}$.
The frame is irreflexive, but the nominal $i$ is true at two worlds.
We check the condition on nominals of [[hybrid logic|NVPQ9W9DED]], and then two formulas that hold in every hybrid model on this frame.

1. A hybrid valuation gives each nominal exactly one world.
   Here $V(i)$ has two elements, so $(W, R, V)$ is not a hybrid model.
2. Read the truth clause for $i$ literally, so that $i$ holds at $u$ and at $v$.
3. Take $i \to \neg \lozenge i$ at $u$.
   The antecedent holds, and the successor $v$ satisfies $i$, so $\lozenge i$ holds and the formula fails.
4. So the [[pure formula for irreflexivity|RK1RCGPAY6]] fails on an irreflexive frame under this valuation.
5. The clause for $@_i \varphi$ speaks of "the world named $i$", and there is no such single world.
   Read $@_i \varphi$ as "$\varphi$ holds at every world where $i$ holds".
6. Take the Intro axiom $i \wedge p \to @_i p$ at $u$.
   The antecedent holds, since $i$ and $p$ are both true at $u$.
   Under the reading of step 5, $@_i p$ needs $p$ at $v$ as well, which fails, so the axiom fails at $u$.

## Remarks

The uniqueness condition on nominals carries all the strength of the pure axioms.
Without it a nominal is just a propositional variable, and the correspondence of step 4 is lost.

The choice in step 5 does not rescue the structure.
Under the other reading, "$\varphi$ holds at some world where $i$ holds", the self-dual axiom $@_i p \leftrightarrow \neg @_i \neg p$ fails instead.
With $p$ true at $u$ only, both $@_i p$ and $@_i \neg p$ come out true, so $@_i p$ holds while $\neg @_i \neg p$ fails.
If we change $V(i)$ to $\{u\}$, the structure becomes a hybrid model, and both formulas of the example hold at both worlds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A name is supposed to belong to one world only.
Here two worlds share the name $i$.
Then "the world called $i$" makes no sense, and the formulas that rely on names give wrong answers.
For example, $u$ sees a world called $i$, so the formula "I do not see myself" seems to fail, although $u$ does not see itself.

### L2 human

We check the repaired valuation $V(i) = \{u\}$.
At $u$, the only successor $v$ does not satisfy $i$, so $\neg \lozenge i$ holds, and so does $i \to \neg \lozenge i$.
At $v$, the nominal is false, so the same formula holds there.
At $u$, $@_i p$ holds because $p$ holds at $u$, the world named $i$, so $i \wedge p \to @_i p$ holds.
At $v$, the nominal is false, so the Intro axiom holds there too.
The typical use of such a check is to see which axiom of $\mathcal{H}(@)$ expresses which part of the uniqueness condition.
Watch out: a structure can pass some axioms even when the condition fails, so passing one axiom does not show that a valuation is hybrid.
