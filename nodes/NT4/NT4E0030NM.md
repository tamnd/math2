## Example

Let $W = \{u, v\}$, $R = \{(u, v)\}$, $V(i) = \{u, v\}$ and $V(p) = \{u\}$.
The frame is irreflexive.
We check the condition on nominals of [[hybrid logic|NVPQ9W9DED]], and two formulas that are valid in hybrid models on this frame.

1. A hybrid valuation must give each nominal exactly one world. Here $V(i)$ has two elements, so $(W, R, V)$ is not a hybrid model.
2. Read the clauses literally: $i$ holds at $u$ and at $v$.
3. The formula $i \to \neg \lozenge i$ at $u$: the antecedent holds. The successor $v$ satisfies $i$, so $\lozenge i$ holds at $u$, and the formula fails.
4. So the [[pure formula for irreflexivity|RK1RCGPAY6]] fails on an irreflexive frame under this valuation.
5. The truth clause of $@_i \varphi$ refers to "the world named $i$", and there is none. Read $@_i \varphi$ as "$\varphi$ holds at every world where $i$ holds".
6. The Intro axiom $i \wedge p \to @_i p$ at $u$: the antecedent holds, since $u \models i$ and $u \models p$.
7. Under the reading of step 5, $@_i p$ needs $p$ at $u$ and at $v$. It fails at $v$, so the axiom fails at $u$.

## Remarks

The example shows that the uniqueness condition on nominals carries all the strength of the pure axioms.
Without it a nominal is a propositional variable, and the correspondence of step 4 is lost.
Under the other reading of step 5, "$\varphi$ holds at some world where $i$ holds", the self-dual axiom $@_i p \leftrightarrow \neg @_i \neg p$ fails instead: with $p$ true at $u$ only, both $@_i p$ and $@_i \neg p$ hold.
If we change $V(i)$ to $\{u\}$, the structure becomes a hybrid model, and both formulas hold at both worlds.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A name is supposed to belong to one world only.
Here two worlds share the name $i$.
Then "the world called $i$" makes no sense, and the formulas that rely on names give wrong answers.
For example, $u$ sees a world called $i$, so the formula "I do not see myself" seems to fail, although $u$ does not see itself.

### L2 human

We check the last claim of the remarks, with $V(i) = \{u\}$.

1. $i \to \neg \lozenge i$ at $u$: the only successor $v$ does not satisfy $i$, so $\neg \lozenge i$ holds. True.
2. $i \to \neg \lozenge i$ at $v$: $i$ is false at $v$. True.
3. $i \wedge p \to @_i p$ at $u$: $@_i p$ holds, since $p$ holds at $u$, the world named $i$. True.
4. $i \wedge p \to @_i p$ at $v$: $i$ is false at $v$. True.

The typical use of this check is to see which axiom of $\mathcal{H}(@)$ expresses which part of the uniqueness condition.
A trap: a structure can satisfy some of the axioms even when the condition fails, so passing one axiom does not show that a valuation is hybrid.
