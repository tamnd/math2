## Definition

Consider a system of non-interacting identical particles in the [[grand canonical ensemble|1C5S72A9JX]] at temperature $T > 0$ and chemical potential $\mu \in \R$.
Let $k_B$ be the Boltzmann constant and $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$.
The particles obey *Fermi–Dirac statistics* if each single-particle state is occupied by $0$ or $1$ particle (the Pauli exclusion principle).
Then the mean occupation number of a single-particle state of energy $\varepsilon$ is
$$\bar n(\varepsilon) = \frac{1}{e^{\beta (\varepsilon - \mu)} + 1}.$$
The function $f(\varepsilon) = \bar n(\varepsilon)$ is called the Fermi–Dirac distribution.

## Remarks

The formula follows from the grand partition function of one state, $1 + e^{-\beta(\varepsilon - \mu)}$, which has one term for each allowed occupation.
The mean occupation is $e^{-\beta(\varepsilon - \mu)} / (1 + e^{-\beta(\varepsilon - \mu)})$, which equals the formula above.

Example: at $\varepsilon = \mu$, $\bar n = 1/(1 + 1) = 1/2$.
For every $\varepsilon$, $0 < \bar n(\varepsilon) < 1$, consistent with at most one particle per state.
As $T \to 0$, $\bar n(\varepsilon)$ tends to $1$ for $\varepsilon < \mu$ and to $0$ for $\varepsilon > \mu$.

Non-example: [[Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics|B60C1S87XB]] gives $\bar n = e^{-\beta(\varepsilon - \mu)}$, which exceeds $1$ when $\varepsilon < \mu$, so it violates the exclusion principle.
The two agree up to small relative error when $\beta(\varepsilon - \mu) \gg 1$.

Integrals of $f$ against powers of $\varepsilon$ are [[Fermi–Dirac integrals|HHKKPD81J2]].
