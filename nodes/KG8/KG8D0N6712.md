## Summary

The Grothendieck–Witt group $GW(F)$ of a field $F$ is the [[Grothendieck group|5FDMY7PDYY]] of nondegenerate [[quadratic forms|0RBQS1JJCR]] over $F$ under orthogonal sum.
With the tensor product of forms it is a commutative ring.
It keeps both the dimension of a form and its class in the [[Witt group|KFVY0RPGF2]].
The Witt group is the quotient of $GW(F)$ by the multiples of the hyperbolic plane.
It is the group $\pi_0$ of Hermitian K-theory, and it appears in motivic homotopy theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Quadratic forms can be added by putting them side by side, but they cannot be subtracted.
The Grothendieck–Witt group adds formal differences of forms, in the same way that the integers are built from the natural numbers.
Unlike the Witt group, it does not throw away the hyperbolic plane, so it remembers the dimension.
Over the real numbers a form is determined by its numbers of positive and negative squares, and $GW(\R) \cong \Z \oplus \Z$.
Over the complex numbers only the dimension matters, and $GW(\C) \cong \Z$.
