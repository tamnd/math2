## Summary

An optical soliton is a pulse or beam of light whose shape does not change as it propagates.
A nonlinear effect exactly balances dispersion or diffraction.
Temporal solitons are pulses in fibres, where the [[Kerr effect|Y7WQY4568B]] balances group-velocity dispersion.
Spatial solitons are beams that do not spread, where self-focusing balances diffraction.
They are [[solitons|63D4AXYCT4]] of the [[nonlinear Schrödinger equation in optics|BRJ36NF7KX]] and its variants.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A short light pulse in a fibre normally spreads out, because its colours travel at slightly different speeds.
The Kerr effect also shifts the colours of an intense pulse.
In the right kind of fibre the two effects cancel, and the pulse keeps its shape over long distances.
Hasegawa and Tappert predicted such pulses in 1973, and Mollenauer, Stolen and Gordon observed them in 1980.
