## Summary

The multiplicative decomposition writes the [[deformation gradient|HTYRR2WDQ6]] of an elastic–plastic body as a product $F = F^e F^p$ of an elastic part $F^e$ and a plastic part $F^p$.
The plastic part maps the reference configuration to a local, stress-free intermediate configuration, and the elastic part maps this to the current configuration.
It is the standard kinematic basis of finite strain [[plasticity|K8BBMTN0KV]] and of [[crystal plasticity|YV775GKJ3T]], and it is often called the Kröner–Lee decomposition.
At small strains it reduces to the familiar additive split of the strain into elastic and plastic parts.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Bend a paper clip and let go: part of the deformation springs back and part remains.
Think of the deformation as two steps done in order.
First the material is rearranged permanently, without any stress, and then the rearranged material is stretched elastically to its present shape.
Each step is a linear map on small material elements, and doing one after the other multiplies the two matrices.
