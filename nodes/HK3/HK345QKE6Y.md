## Summary

The Parker spiral is the shape of the magnetic field lines of the solar wind.
The Sun rotates while its wind streams out almost radially, and the field lines are frozen into the plasma.
So each field line is wound into an Archimedean spiral in the plane of the solar equator.
Near the Earth the field makes an angle of roughly $45$ degrees with the direction to the Sun.
The same picture applies to the winds of other rotating, magnetized stars.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Think of a rotating garden sprinkler: each drop flies straight out, but the stream of drops forms a spiral.
The Sun is the sprinkler, the solar wind is the water, and the magnetic field lines are carried along the streams of wind.
Because the wind moves at a nearly constant speed, the turns of the spiral are equally spaced.
With a wind of about $400$ kilometres per second and one solar rotation in about $27$ days, the field near the Earth is wound about halfway between radial and sideways.
