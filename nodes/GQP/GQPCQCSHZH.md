## Example

A *tippe top* is a small top made of a truncated sphere with a short stem attached to the flat side.
Its centre of mass lies on the axis of symmetry, between the centre of the spherical part and the round bottom.
Spun fast on its round part, with the stem up, it soon turns upside down and keeps spinning on its stem.

The check that something must supply energy: after the flip the centre of mass is higher than before.
So the [[potential energy|DF7DKHTQ7A]] has increased, and this energy comes from the kinetic energy of the spin.
The only force that can transfer it is the sliding [[friction|4XE2B2PBGC]] at the point of contact with the table.

## Remarks

Seen from above, the top keeps turning in the same direction during the flip, because the angular momentum stays roughly vertical.
Relative to the body, the spin is therefore reversed after the flip.
During the flip the axis performs [[precession|CFE9SNEHMP]] around the vertical while its tilt grows.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The tippe top is a toy that flips itself over while spinning and ends up standing on its stem.
It looks as if it defies gravity, since its centre of mass rises.
The energy for this comes from the spin, and friction with the table is what converts it.
It is a favourite example in rigid body dynamics of a surprising effect of friction.
