## Statement

Let $p$ be a nonzero real number. For each natural number $n$, let $\binom{p}{n} = p(p-1)\cdots(p-n+1)/n!$.

Then $(1+x)^p$ is equal to the [[binomial series|0HB0XKDXHH]] of $p$, that is
$$(1+x)^p = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \binom{p}{n} x^n.$$

## Remarks

The right side is a [[power series|0VP56B4EMX]] in $x$. When $p$ is a positive integer, the coefficients vanish for $n > p$, and the formula is the usual binomial formula.

For a negative integer $p = -m$, it gives the expansion of $1/(1+x)^m$ with infinitely many terms.
