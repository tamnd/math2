## Summary

The Wightman axioms are a mathematically precise list of properties that a relativistic [[quantum field theory|W031YNVRE3]] should have.
They ask for a Hilbert space with a unitary representation of the [[Poincaré group|8ZQ9TA4Q2Q]], positive energy, and a unique invariant vacuum.
The fields are operator-valued [[tempered distributions|AES2T5JPFP]], covariant under the Poincaré group and commuting at spacelike separation.
From them one proves general theorems such as the CPT theorem and the spin–statistics theorem.
The vacuum expectation values of products of fields, the Wightman functions, determine the whole theory.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Physicists compute with quantum fields using many rules of thumb, and it is not clear which assumptions are really needed.
The Wightman axioms, formulated in the 1950s, list the basic ones in a precise form.
Fields cannot be sharp at a single point, so they are averaged against smooth test functions.
Causality becomes the rule that measurements in regions that no light signal can connect do not disturb each other.
The free field satisfies the axioms, and constructing interacting examples is a central problem of mathematical physics.
