## Summary

Chemical graph theory is the study of molecules through [[graphs|00C0BR7D7H]] whose vertices are atoms and whose edges are chemical bonds.
It uses graph invariants, called topological indices or molecular descriptors, to predict physical and chemical properties, such as boiling points, from structure alone.
Typical tools are distance-based indices such as the [[Wiener index|TBWVJXMM45]] and counts of matchings such as the [[Hosoya index|1P57E5E03M]].
Others are degree-based indices such as the [[Randić index|R37X2BTK50]], and the spectrum of the adjacency matrix as in the [[Hückel method|FXW4C6DPNF]].
It also includes the enumeration of isomers and the study of special molecular graphs such as [[fullerene graphs|SCDYDGR153]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Draw a molecule as dots for atoms and lines for bonds, and forget the exact positions and angles.
What is left is a graph, and much chemistry is already visible in it.
For example, the alkanes with formula $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}}$ give two different carbon skeletons, a path of four vertices and a star with three leaves.
These two graphs explain why butane and isobutane are different substances with different boiling points.
