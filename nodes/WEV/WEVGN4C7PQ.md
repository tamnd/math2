## Summary

A Lorentz transformation is a linear map of [[Minkowski space|G49G4R1K04]] that preserves the Minkowski metric.
In physics it converts the coordinates of an event in one inertial frame into its coordinates in another frame moving uniformly relative to the first.
Lorentz transformations form the [[Lorentz group|QWK3BYVJ9W]], the symmetry group of special relativity at a fixed origin.

## Explanations

### L1 human

When you change from one observer to another moving past at constant velocity, you must recompute the times and positions of all events.
In Newtonian physics you only shift positions and keep time; a Lorentz transformation also mixes time with position.
It is like a rotation, but one that preserves $-c^2 t^2 + x^2$ instead of $x^2 + y^2$, so it squeezes along hyperbolas instead of turning along circles.
For example, with $c = 1$ and relative speed $v = 0.6$, the event $(t, x) = (1, 0)$ gets the new coordinates $(1.25, -0.75)$.
