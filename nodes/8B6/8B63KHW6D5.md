## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, and let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over $F$ with finite bases $B = (b_1, \dots, b_n)$ and $C = (c_1, \dots, c_m)$.
Let $T \colon V \to W$ be a [[linear map|0RJF668P1T]], with [[matrix|0YYMY95FWS]] $[T]_{C,B}$.
Then for each $v \in V$,
$$[T(v)]_C = [T]_{C,B}\, [v]_B,$$
where $[\,\cdot\,]_B$ and $[\,\cdot\,]_C$ are [[coordinate vectors|0BMVBHTWV2]] and the right side is a [[matrix-vector product|0PDVQEP0ZC]].
Conversely, $[T]_{C,B}$ is the only $m \times n$ matrix $M$ with $[T(v)]_C = M [v]_B$ for each $v \in V$.

## Remarks

### Why each hypothesis is needed

- $T$ must be linear. The map $f(x) = x + 1$ on $\R$ with $B = C = (1)$ gives the $1 \times 1$ array $(f(1)) = (2)$, but $f(2) = 3 \ne 2 \cdot 2$.
- The bases must be ordered. If the order of $C$ changes, then the rows of $[T]_{C,B}$ and the entries of $[T(v)]_C$ are permuted in the same way.

### Consequences

- The [[matrix of a composition|05Q1B7MH0M]] is the product of the matrices.
- The vector $v$ is in $\ker T$ exactly when $[v]_B$ is in the [[null space|0PHDYNR24D]] of $[T]_{C,B}$.
- The vector $w$ is in $\im T$ exactly when $[w]_C$ is in the [[column space|03AJKXH1AV]] of $[T]_{C,B}$. So $\rank T = \rank [T]_{C,B}$.

### Converse

The uniqueness part is a converse: a matrix that computes coordinates of images for each $v$ is the matrix of $T$.

### Generalizations

The same formula holds for free modules of finite rank over a commutative ring, with the same proof.

## Explanations

### L1 human

To find the coordinates of $T(v)$, multiply the matrix of $T$ by the coordinates of $v$.
So all computation with $T$ becomes computation with one table of numbers.

### L2 human

The proof expands $v$ in the basis $B$ and uses linearity.
Each $T(b_j)$ contributes $x_j$ times column $j$ of the matrix.
The sum of these columns, weighted by the coordinates $x_j$, is the matrix-vector product.
The uniqueness follows by testing on the basis vectors, whose coordinate vectors are the standard unit vectors.

### L3 human

The coordinate maps $\phi_B \colon V \to F^n$ and $\phi_C \colon W \to F^m$ are isomorphisms.
The statement says that the square with $T$ on top, $x \mapsto [T]_{C,B} x$ at the bottom and $\phi_B$, $\phi_C$ on the sides commutes.
So $[T]_{C,B}$ is the matrix of $\phi_C \circ T \circ \phi_B^{-1}$ in the standard bases.
