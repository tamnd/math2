## Definition

Fix a stellar mass $M$ and a chemical composition.
Consider the [[stellar structure|SBKFDNEMFF]] models of mass $M$ that are convective from the centre to the photosphere.
Here convective is meant in the sense of the [[Schwarzschild criterion|85GWQPTJ3W]], and the interior gradient is taken adiabatic.
Let $L$ be the luminosity and $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the [[effective temperature|WETKRE94E0]].
The *Hayashi track* of mass $M$ is the curve traced by these models, as their radius varies, in the plane of $\log T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $\log L$.

## Remarks

Matching an adiabatic interior to a photosphere whose opacity is dominated by negative hydrogen ions makes $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ depend only weakly on $L$, so the track is nearly vertical.
Example: a newly formed star of one solar mass contracts down its Hayashi track, with $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ around $4000 \, \mathrm{K}$, until a radiative core appears.
Non-example: the Sun today is not on its Hayashi track, since its core is radiative; only its outer envelope is convective.
Models to the right of the track, at lower $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$, would need a temperature gradient steeper than adiabatic, which convection removes at once, so such equilibria do not exist.
