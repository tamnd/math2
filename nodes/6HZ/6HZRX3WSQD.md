## Summary

The Coriolis force is the [[fictitious force|ZN2Z2HJY9Z]] that acts on a body moving in a [[rotating reference frame|HNXK6CJ5B2]].
It equals $-2m\,\Omega \times v$, where $m$ is the mass, $v$ the [[velocity|K160D2Z0CS]] in the rotating frame and $\Omega$ the [[angular velocity|2FTZ7D8Z0F]] of the frame.
It is perpendicular to the velocity, so it bends paths without changing speed, and it vanishes for bodies at rest in the rotating frame.
On the rotating Earth it turns winds and ocean currents and shapes the circulation around weather systems.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Roll a ball straight out from the centre of a turning merry-go-round.
Seen from the ground, the ball goes straight; seen from the merry-go-round, its path curves, because the floor turns under it.
The Coriolis force is the sideways push that the rider would need to explain that curve.
On the Earth, which turns once a day, it deflects moving air to the right in the northern hemisphere and to the left in the southern one.
