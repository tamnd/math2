## Summary

Hermitian K-theory is the analogue of [[algebraic K-theory|5GVFZWMWC8]] for modules equipped with nondegenerate [[hermitian forms|48P4A1CX85]] or quadratic forms.
It was developed by Karoubi and others.
Its group in degree zero is the [[Grothendieck–Witt group|KG8D0N6712]].
Its higher groups are built from orthogonal and symplectic groups, as K-theory is built from general linear groups.
It is linked to K-theory by forgetful and hyperbolic maps.
On $\pi_0$, the cokernel of the hyperbolic map is the [[Witt group|KFVY0RPGF2]].

## Explanations

### L1 human

Algebraic K-theory studies modules and their invertible matrices.
Hermitian K-theory studies modules that carry a symmetric or alternating form, and the matrices that preserve the form.
For real vector spaces with inner products, the symmetry groups are orthogonal groups instead of general linear groups.
Over a field, the zeroth Hermitian K-group counts forms up to isometry, with formal differences allowed.
For example, over $\R$ a form is described by its numbers of positive and negative squares, so this group is $\Z \oplus \Z$.
