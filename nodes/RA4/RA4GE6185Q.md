## Statement

Let $\mu_0 > 0$ be the vacuum permeability.
Let $J \colon \R^3 \to \R^3$ be a steady current density, a continuously differentiable [[vector field|0299VCW1TH]] with compact support and $\nabla \cdot J = 0$.
Then the [[magnetic field|XRED3C547P]] produced by $J$ in vacuum is
$$B(x) = \frac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \int_{\R^3} J(y) \times \frac{x - y}{\abs{x - y}^3} \, dy, \qquad x \in \R^3,$$
where $\times$ is the [[cross product|0N5N15HWZ7]].

## Remarks

For a steady current $I$ in a thin closed wire $\gamma$, the formula becomes the [[line integral|0H5MR9B6GT]]
$$B(x) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi} \oint_\gamma \frac{d\ell \times (x - y)}{\abs{x - y}^3}.$$
In the sense of magnetostatics this is the definition of the field of a current, and it is the experimental law of Biot and Savart.
The mathematical content is that $B = \nabla \times A$ with $A(x) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \int J(y) / \abs{x - y} \, dy$, so that $\nabla \cdot B = 0$ and $\nabla \times B = \mu_0 J$ when $\nabla \cdot J = 0$.
Example: take the limit of an infinite straight wire along the third axis with current $I$.
At distance $s$ from the wire, the formula gives a field of size $\mu_0 I / (2 \pi s)$, circling the wire.
The hypothesis $\nabla \cdot J = 0$ is necessary for $\nabla \times B = \mu_0 J$, since the divergence of a curl is zero.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The Biot–Savart law tells how to compute the magnetic field of an electric current.
Each small piece of current makes a small field that circles around it and gets weaker with the square of the distance.
The total field is the sum of all these small contributions.

### L2 human

The Biot–Savart law plays for magnetostatics the role that Coulomb's law plays for electrostatics.
From it one derives Gauss's law for magnetism and Ampère's circuital law, the two magnetostatic [[Maxwell equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]].
It is also used to compute the field of coils, for example the uniform field inside a long solenoid.
