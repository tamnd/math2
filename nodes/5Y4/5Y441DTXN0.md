## Definition

An [[elasticity tensor|SX32X1D537]] $C$ is *isotropic* if $Q_{ip} Q_{jq} Q_{kr} Q_{ls} C_{pqrs} = C_{ijkl}$ for every [[orthogonal matrix|0W02QESPJQ]] $Q$, with summation over repeated indices.
Every isotropic elasticity tensor has the form
$$C_{ijkl} = \lambda \, \delta_{ij} \delta_{kl} + \mu \, (\delta_{ik} \delta_{jl} + \delta_{il} \delta_{jk})$$
for unique real numbers $\lambda$ and $\mu$, where $\delta$ is the Kronecker delta.
These numbers are the *Lamé parameters* of $C$: $\lambda$ is the first Lamé parameter and $\mu$ is the second.
Equivalently, the stress produced by a [[strain|8QS6QMJF5Z]] $\varepsilon$ is the [[stress|KG2XWEMJ30]] $\sigma = \lambda (\tr \varepsilon) I + 2\mu \varepsilon$.

## Remarks

Example: with $\lambda = 1$ and $\mu = 1$ and the pure shear strain $\varepsilon = \gamma (e_1 \otimes e_2 + e_2 \otimes e_1)/2$, the trace is $0$ and $\sigma_{12} = 2\mu \cdot \gamma/2 = \gamma$; so $\mu$ is the ratio of shear stress to engineering shear strain $\gamma$.

Non-example: a cubic crystal has an elasticity tensor with three independent constants, and in general it is not of the form above, so it has no Lamé parameters.

Positivity of the elasticity tensor is equivalent to $\mu > 0$ and $3\lambda + 2\mu > 0$.
In terms of Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$, $\lambda = \frac{E \nu}{(1 + \nu)(1 - 2\nu)}$ and $\mu = \frac{E}{2(1 + \nu)}$.
