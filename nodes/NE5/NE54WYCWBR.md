## Summary

The law of comparative judgment is a model of how people compare two stimuli.
Louis Leon Thurstone published it in 1927.
Each stimulus produces a random internal value, normally distributed around a scale value.
A person judges one stimulus greater than another when its value comes out larger.
The model turns observed proportions of paired judgments into positions on an interval scale, and it is the basis of Thurstonian scaling.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Ask many people which of two drinks tastes sweeter.
If the drinks are very different, almost everyone agrees; if they are close, the answers split.
Thurstone's idea is that each tasting gives a slightly random impression, scattered like a bell curve around the drink's true sweetness.
The share of people preferring one drink then tells how far apart the two bell curves are.
For example, if $50\%$ choose each drink, the two drinks get the same scale value.
