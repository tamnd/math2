## Proof

Take $F = \{X_1, \neg X_1\}$.
It has the refutation with $3$ lines: line $1$ is $X_1$, line $2$ is $\neg X_1$, and line $3$ is their resolvent $\square$.
The proof below writes "$F$ has a refutation with $s$ lines" as a CNF, and for $s = 3$ this CNF is satisfied by the variables that describe these three lines.

We give the structure of the proof of Atserias and Müller, and we cite one step, their width lower bound, as Lemma A.
They prove it by a Prover and Adversary argument.
Let $F$ be a CNF with variables $X_1, \dots, X_n$ and clauses $C_1, \dots, C_m$, and write $X_i^{(1)} = X_i$ and $X_i^{(0)} = \neg X_i$.
A refutation here is a sequence of non-tautological clauses, each a weakening of a clause of $F$ or of a resolvent of two earlier lines, that ends with $\square$.

### The formulas

1. For $s \geq 1$, the CNF $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ has variables for $u, v \leq s$.
   $D[u, i, b]$ says that line $u$ contains $X_i^{(b)}$.
   $V[u, i]$ says that line $u$ is a resolvent on $X_i$, or an axiom when $i = 0$.
   $I[u, j]$ says that line $u$ weakens $C_j$, or is a resolvent when $j = 0$.
   $L[u, v]$ and $R[u, v]$ name the two earlier premises of line $u$.
2. Its clauses say that these variables describe a refutation of $F$ with $s$ lines.
   So $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ is satisfiable if and only if $F$ has a refutation with $s$ lines.
3. Each variable *mentions* one line index, its first index $u$.
   The *index-width* of a clause is the number of line indices that its variables mention, and each clause of $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ has index-width at most $2$.
4. The *relativized* CNF $\mathrm{RREF}(F, s)$ adds variables $P[u]$, "line $u$ is active", in the style of Dantchev and Riis.
   It says that the active lines form a refutation of $F$ and that line $s$ is active.
   The clauses of $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ get extra literals $\neg P[u]$ for the lines they mention, and the indices keep their built-in order $1 < 2 < \dots < s$.

### The width lemma

5. *Lemma A (cited).*
   Let $n, w, s \geq 1$ with $2^n \geq s \geq 6nw$, and let $F$ be unsatisfiable with $n$ variables.
   Then every resolution refutation of $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ has index-width at least $w$.
6. The idea of its proof is that inferences of small index-width cannot tell $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ from $\mathrm{REF}(F, 2^{n+1} - 1)$.
   The second CNF is satisfiable, by the complete decision tree of $F$, which has $2^n$ leaves and $2^n - 1$ inner nodes.

### The lower bound when F is unsatisfiable

7. *Lemma B.*
   For $n$ large and $F$ unsatisfiable with $n$ variables, every resolution refutation $\Pi$ of $\mathrm{RREF}(F, 13n^2)$ has more than $2^{2n/5}$ clauses.
8. Suppose $\Pi$ has $\ell \leq 2^{2n/5}$ clauses, and let $t = 13n^2$.
   Choose a random restriction $\rho$ as follows.
   Set each $P[u]$ to $1$ or $0$ with probability $1/2$, and let $A$ be the set of $u$ with $P[u] = 1$.
   Set $L[u, v] = R[u, v] = 0$ for $u \in A$ and $v \notin A$.
   Set every other variable that mentions some $u \notin A$ to a random bit.
9. A literal that mentions $u$ is true under $\rho$ with probability at least $1/4$, namely when $u \notin A$ and its bit comes out right, and these events are independent for distinct $u$.
   So a clause of index-width at least $n$ survives with probability at most $(3/4)^n$.
10. By the union bound, some clause of $\Pi$ of index-width at least $n$ survives with probability at most $\ell (3/4)^n \leq (2^{2/5} \cdot 3/4)^n$.
    Since $2^{2/5} \cdot 3/4 < 0.99$, this is less than $1/4$ for large $n$.
11. $\lvert A \rvert$ is binomial with mean $13n^2/2$, so by the [[Chernoff bound|014PEBGDYF]] we have $\lvert A \rvert < 6n^2$ with probability less than $1/4$ for large $n$.
    Also $P[t] = 0$ with probability $1/2$.
12. The three bad events have total probability less than $1/4 + 1/4 + 1/2 = 1$.
    So some $\rho$ makes $\Pi$ restricted by $\rho$ of index-width less than $n$, with $\lvert A \rvert \geq 6n^2$ and $t \in A$.
13. For this $\rho$, every clause of $\mathrm{RREF}(F, t)$ becomes true or becomes a clause of a copy of $\mathrm{REF}(F, \lvert A \rvert)$ with index set $A$.
    So $\Pi$ restricted by $\rho$ is a refutation of that copy with index-width less than $n$.
14. Since $2^n \geq 13n^2 \geq \lvert A \rvert \geq 6n \cdot n$ for large $n$, this contradicts Lemma A with $w = n$.

### The upper bound when F is satisfiable

15. *Lemma C.*
    If $F$ is satisfiable, then $\mathrm{RREF}(F, s)$ has a resolution refutation with a number of clauses polynomial in $s$, $n$ and $m$.
16. Fix $\alpha$ that satisfies $F$.
    For $u = 1, 2, \dots, s$ in order, derive $\mathrm{True}(u) = \neg P[u] \vee \bigvee_{i} D[u, i, \alpha(X_i)]$, which says that an active line $u$ has a literal true under $\alpha$.
17. If line $u$ weakens $C_j$, it contains the literal of $C_j$ that is true under $\alpha$.
    If line $u$ is a resolvent on $X_i$, take the premise whose literal on $X_i$ is false under $\alpha$.
    That premise is an earlier active line, so $\mathrm{True}$ for it gives another literal true under $\alpha$, and this literal passes to line $u$.
18. Each $\mathrm{True}(u)$ takes polynomially many resolution steps.
    Then $\mathrm{True}(s)$, the clauses saying that line $s$ is empty, and the clause $P[s]$ give $\square$.

### From the gap to the theorem

19. For a 3-CNF $F$ with $n \geq n_0$ variables let $G(F) = \mathrm{RREF}(F, 13n^2)$, where $n_0$ is large enough for Lemma B.
    For $n < n_0$, $G$ decides $F$ by trying all $2^n$ assignments, and outputs $\{x, \neg x\}$ if $F$ is satisfiable and $\{x\}$ if not.
20. A 3-CNF has $m \leq (2n)^3 = 8n^3$ distinct clauses, so $r(G(F))$ lies between $n^{1/q}$ and $n^q$ for a constant $q$.
    Part 1 of the theorem follows from Lemma C, with $c$ large, and from Lemma B, with $d > q$, since then $r^{1/d} \leq n^{q/d} \leq 2n/5$ for large $n$.
21. For parts 2 to 4, let $t$ be the time bound, non-decreasing and computable within its own bound.
    Given $F$, compute $G(F)$ and run the automating algorithm for $t(n^q + n^{qc})$ steps.
    Answer "satisfiable" if and only if it outputs, in time, a sequence of clauses that is a resolution refutation of $G(F)$, which can be checked in polynomial time.
22. If $F$ is satisfiable, a refutation with fewer than $n^{qc}$ clauses exists, so the algorithm finishes in time.
    If $F$ is unsatisfiable, every refutation has more than $2^{r^{1/d}} \geq 2^{n^{1/(qd)}}$ clauses, which exceeds the budget for large $n$ when $t$ is subexponential.
    So this decides 3-SAT in time $t(\mathrm{poly}(n)) + \mathrm{poly}(n)$.
    $\blacksquare$

## Idea

Encode "$F$ has a short refutation" as a CNF.
If $F$ is satisfiable, the satisfying assignment gives a short refutation of the encoding, line by line.
If $F$ is unsatisfiable, a random restriction turns any short refutation of the relativized encoding into a narrow refutation of the plain encoding, and a width argument rules that out.

## Variants

The relativization is what lets a random restriction turn a bound on length into a bound on width, and the proof gives no length lower bound for $\mathrm{REF}(F, s)$ itself.

Alekhnovich and Razborov used a different reduction, from a parameterized problem.
It gives the weaker conclusion that W[P] collapses to FPT, but it also applies to tree-like resolution.
