## Summary

The Maxwell–Bloch equations couple the classical electromagnetic field, governed by [[Maxwell's equations|9BWWD4QK8Y]], to a medium of two-level quantum systems, governed by the optical Bloch equations.
The field drives the atoms, and the induced atomic [[polarization density|ASQAH710WN]] is the source of the field.
They are the basic semiclassical model of the laser and of coherent light–matter effects such as self-induced transparency.
For a single-mode laser, Haken showed that they are equivalent to the Lorenz equations, so a laser can behave chaotically.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A laser has two partners: light in the cavity and atoms in the gain medium.
The light makes the atoms oscillate, and the oscillating atoms in turn emit light.
The Maxwell–Bloch equations write down this exchange: one equation for the field, one for the oscillation of the atoms, and one for how many atoms are excited.
They are simple enough to study by hand and rich enough to show instability and chaos.
