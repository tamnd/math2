## Summary

A semigroup is left amenable if there is a mean on its bounded functions that is invariant under left translations.
This extends the notion of [[amenable group|CEAT9BESKK]] to [[semigroups|0XMEM406JM]], where left and right versions can differ.
Every commutative semigroup is amenable, and the free semigroup on two generators is not left amenable.
Day developed much of the theory of amenable semigroups, including fixed point characterizations.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A semigroup is like a group in which elements need not have inverses, such as the natural numbers under addition.
Left amenability asks for an averaging rule for bounded functions that does not change when the variable is multiplied on the left by a fixed element.
For the natural numbers, long-run averages over $\set{0, \ldots, n-1}$ lead to such a rule.
In the free semigroup on $a$ and $b$, the words starting with $a$ and those starting with $b$ would each need average $1$, which is impossible.
