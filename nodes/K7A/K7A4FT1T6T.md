## Summary

Flexure of the lithosphere is the bending of the Earth's rigid outer layer under a load.
Typical loads are volcanic islands, mountain belts and ice sheets.
The lithosphere is modelled as a thin elastic plate floating on a fluid mantle, as in [[Kirchhoff–Love plate theory|9E6E0SCEYA]].
The model predicts a moat around the load and a low bulge further out, both seen in seafloor topography and gravity.
Fitting it to data gives the effective elastic thickness of the lithosphere, a measure of its strength.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Stand in the middle of a thin wooden board floating on water: the board bends down under you, and it pushes water aside over a wide area.
The Earth's lithosphere behaves the same way under the weight of a big volcano such as Hawaii.
The ocean floor dips down near the volcano and rises slightly in a ring further away.
How wide the dip is tells us how stiff the plate is.
Unlike simple [[isostasy|7CJTGB6M0V]], where each column floats on its own, a stiff plate spreads the load over a wide region.
