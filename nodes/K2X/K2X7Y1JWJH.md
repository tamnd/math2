## Summary

Algebraic number theory is the study of number fields, of their rings of integers and of their completions.
A number field is a field that contains the rational numbers and has finite dimension as a vector space over them.
In a ring of integers the factorization of elements into irreducibles need not be unique, but each nonzero ideal is a product of prime ideals in exactly one way.
Kummer introduced ideal numbers in his work on Fermat's Last Theorem, and Dedekind and Hilbert built the theory of ideals and of number fields in the late nineteenth century.
This topic collects the algebraic number theory pages of math2.
Its sub-areas are listed below, in reading order.
Fields and Galois theory, arithmetic geometry, and automorphic forms and the Langlands program have their own topics.
