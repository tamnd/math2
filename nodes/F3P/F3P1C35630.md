## Summary

Tajima's D is a test [[statistic|0Q5CG2NEQ8]] of population genetics.
It compares two estimates of the scaled mutation rate $\theta$ from the same sample of DNA sequences.
One estimate uses the mean number of pairwise differences, the other the number of segregating sites, as in the [[Watterson estimator|XD14B1J3EQ]].
Under the neutral model with constant population size both estimate $\theta$.
So a value of $D$ far from $0$ is evidence of selection or of a change in population size.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Rare mutations, seen in only one or two sequences, add to the count of differing positions but add little to the average difference between two sequences.
Tajima's D measures whether a sample has more or fewer rare mutations than neutral evolution predicts.
A negative value means an excess of rare variants, as after a population expansion or a selective sweep.
A positive value means an excess of intermediate variants, as under balancing selection or after a bottleneck.
