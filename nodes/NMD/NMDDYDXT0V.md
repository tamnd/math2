## Summary

Anisotropic elasticity is [[linear elasticity|6W00ZECYT0]] for materials whose elastic response depends on direction.
The [[elasticity tensor|SX32X1D537]] of such a material is not invariant under all rotations.
It can have up to $21$ independent constants instead of the $2$ of an isotropic material.
Crystals, wood, fibre composites and rolled metals are anisotropic, so the theory is needed for most engineering materials with a structure.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A block of wood is much stiffer along the grain than across it.
So the same push produces different stretches depending on its direction.
A material like this is called anisotropic, and its elastic behaviour needs more numbers to describe it than steel or glass do.
An orthotropic material, such as wood, has three mutually perpendicular planes of symmetry and needs $9$ constants.
