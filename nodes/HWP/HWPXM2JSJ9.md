## Summary

A perfect group is a [[group|G5S1M9]] equal to its own [[commutator subgroup|0ASGSTXSBW]].
Equivalently, it has no nontrivial abelian quotient.
Non-abelian simple groups are perfect.
In algebraic K-theory the [[elementary subgroup|E5JXB0E324]] $E(R)$ of any ring is perfect, which is what makes the plus construction work.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The commutator $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ measures how far $a$ and $b$ are from commuting.
A group is perfect when every element is a product of commutators.
Such a group is as far from abelian as possible: every homomorphism to an abelian group is trivial.
The alternating group $A_5$, the rotation group of the icosahedron, is the smallest nontrivial example.
The trivial group is also perfect.
