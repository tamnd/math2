## Summary

Mixing length theory is the standard simple model of convection inside stars.
It pictures convection as blobs of gas that rise or sink over a characteristic distance, the mixing length.
Then the blobs dissolve into their surroundings and give up their excess heat.
The mixing length is taken proportional to the pressure scale height, with a free factor $\alpha$ of order one that is fitted to the Sun.
The model turns the [[Schwarzschild criterion|85GWQPTJ3W]] into a formula for the heat flux carried by convection.
It goes back to the mixing length idea of Prandtl in the theory of turbulence.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Inside the outer layers of the Sun, hot gas rises, cools and sinks, like water boiling in a pot.
Following every swirl is impossible, so the theory uses a cartoon.
A hot blob rises a certain distance, then mixes with its surroundings and hands over its heat.
That distance, the mixing length, is the one knob of the model.
With it, one can compute how much heat convection carries and how steep the temperature profile must be.
