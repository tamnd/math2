## Summary

The fixation probability of an allele is the probability that it eventually becomes the only allele at its locus in the population.
In a finite population, [[genetic drift|AFJ02FJ1KR]] ensures that some allele is eventually fixed.
For a neutral allele the fixation probability equals its initial frequency, and selection raises or lowers it.
It measures how likely a new mutation is to spread, and so it governs the rate of evolution.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Start a population of $100$ gene copies in which one copy carries a new variant.
Chance alone decides whether the variant spreads to all copies or disappears.
If the variant has no effect, its chance of taking over is just $1/100$, its starting share.
A helpful variant has a better chance, but it is still lost most of the time when it starts as a single copy.
