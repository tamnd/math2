## Summary

Seiberg–Witten theory is the exact description of the low-energy physics of four-dimensional $N = 2$ supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory, found by Nathan Seiberg and Edward Witten in 1994.
The vacua of the theory form a complex curve, the Coulomb branch.
Over each vacuum sits an [[elliptic curve|427N6S6QQW]] whose periods give the low-energy coupling constant.
At special vacua, magnetic monopoles become massless, and a dual description in terms of these monopoles takes over.
This picture led Witten to the [[Seiberg–Witten invariants|PRDDMSTR4X]] of four-manifolds, and later to links with integrable systems and enumerative geometry.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Gauge theories are usually hard to solve because their interactions are strong at low energies.
With enough [[supersymmetry|S847N922FY]], Seiberg and Witten found that the low-energy behaviour is controlled by a single holomorphic function.
They determined this function by noticing that it is encoded in a family of elliptic curves, one for each possible vacuum.
Walking around special points of the family exchanges electric and magnetic charges, which shows that monopoles play a central role.
The method gave the first exact results about confinement in a four-dimensional gauge theory.
