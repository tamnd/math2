## Summary

The geodynamo is the process that generates and maintains the [[Earth's magnetic field|Z13HPJFV1F]].
Convection of liquid iron alloy in the outer core, organised by the Earth's rotation, induces electric currents, and these currents produce the field.
It is the main application of [[dynamo theory|AFVDBT0K5C]].
Without it, the field would decay by ohmic dissipation in a few tens of thousands of years.

## Explanations

### L1 human

The outer core is a shell of hot liquid metal about 2,200 km thick.
The liquid stirs because heat and light elements escape from the deep core.
A conductor moving through a magnetic field carries electric currents, and currents make magnetic fields.
If the motion is vigorous and organised enough, the field that the currents make is the same field that drives them, and the loop sustains itself.
This is like a bicycle dynamo, but with no wires and no permanent magnet: everything is fluid.
Computer simulations of this process reproduce a dipole-dominated field and also its occasional [[reversals|ZR7WW7HP5W]].
