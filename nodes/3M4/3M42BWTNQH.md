## Summary

The clutching construction builds a [[vector bundle|P4DZM975BV]] over a sphere from two trivial bundles over the hemispheres.
They are glued along a map from the equator to invertible matrices.
Homotopic gluing maps give isomorphic bundles, and every bundle over a sphere arises in this way.
So vector bundles over spheres are classified by homotopy classes of maps into the general linear group.
This links the [[K-theory of spheres|4NH8D0BDX0]] to homotopy groups of unitary and orthogonal groups.

## Explanations

### L1 human

Cover a sphere by a northern and a southern cap; each cap is a disc, and every vector bundle over a disc is trivial.
To build a bundle on the whole sphere, take a product bundle over each cap and decide how to identify the fibres over the equator.
This choice is a map $f$ from the equator to the invertible $n \times n$ matrices, called the clutching function.
For example, on $S^2$ with equator the unit circle, the map $f(z) = z^m$ into $\C^\times$ gives a complex line bundle for each integer $m$, and different $m$ give different bundles.
