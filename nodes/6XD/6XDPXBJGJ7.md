## Summary

A spin foam is a two-dimensional cell complex whose faces carry spins and whose edges carry intertwiners.
It is a "history" of [[spin networks|B173ESJ4YX]].
Spin foam models define the transition amplitudes of [[loop quantum gravity|9C3ECSDRTP]] as a sum over such histories, a discrete version of the gravitational [[path integral|1NYTFT5NPE]].
The name was introduced by John Baez in 1998; the Ponzano–Regge model of three-dimensional gravity (1968) is the first example.

## Explanations

### L1 human

A spin network is a snapshot of quantum space: a graph with labels.
As time goes on, the graph can change, and each edge sweeps out a surface while each vertex sweeps out a line.
The resulting two-dimensional foam of labelled surfaces records one possible history of quantum space.
A spin foam model assigns a number, an amplitude, to each foam and adds them up to get the probability amplitude for one spin network to evolve into another.
This is like the way Feynman diagrams sum over the histories of particles.
